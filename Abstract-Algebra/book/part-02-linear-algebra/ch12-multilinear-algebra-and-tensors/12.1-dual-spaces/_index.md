# 12.1 Dual Spaces

Given a vector space $V$ over a field $F$, there is a second vector space $V^* = \mathrm{Hom}(V, F)$ lurking in the background — the space of all linear maps from $V$ to $F$. These are called linear functionals, and together they form the dual space of $V$. Elements of $V^*$ might seem at first like a mere formal construction, but they are genuinely ubiquitous: integration is a linear functional on function spaces, gradient vectors are naturally elements of the cotangent space (the dual of the tangent space), and row vectors in matrix algebra are linear functionals on column vectors. The distinction between $V$ and $V^*$ is subtle in finite dimensions but becomes essential in infinite dimensions, and even in finite dimensions the right algebraic framework requires keeping them separate.

When $V$ is finite-dimensional with basis $\{e_1, \ldots, e_n\}$, there is a canonical dual basis $\{e^1, \ldots, e^n\}$ of $V^*$ defined by $e^i(e_j) = \delta^i_j$ (the Kronecker delta, equal to 1 if $i = j$ and 0 otherwise). Every linear functional $f \in V^*$ expands uniquely as $f = \sum_i f(e_i) e^i$, so $\dim V^* = \dim V$. However, the isomorphism $V \cong V^*$ obtained by sending $e_i$ to $e^i$ depends on the choice of basis; no basis-free isomorphism between $V$ and $V^*$ exists in general. This is the distinction between a natural isomorphism and an accidental one, and it is the conceptual starting point of category theory.

The double dual $V^{**} = (V^*)^*$ is the space of linear functionals on $V^*$. For each vector $v \in V$, the evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}_v : V^* \to F$ defined by $\mathrm{ev}_v(f) = f(v)$ is a linear functional on $V^*$, hence an element of $V^{**}$. The remarkable fact is that the map $v \mapsto \mathrm{ev}_v$ is a canonical isomorphism from $V$ to $V^{**}$ — it is natural in the sense that it commutes with every linear map between vector spaces. This identification of $V$ with $V^{**}$ is the simplest example of reflexivity. The dual transpose of a linear map $T : V \to W$ is the map $T^* : W^* \to V^*$ defined by $T^*(f) = f \circ T$; the matrix of $T^*$ in dual bases is the transpose of the matrix of $T$, giving dual spaces a direct connection to matrix transposition.
