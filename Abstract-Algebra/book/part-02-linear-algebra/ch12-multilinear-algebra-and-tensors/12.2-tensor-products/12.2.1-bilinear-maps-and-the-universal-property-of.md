# 12.2.1 Bilinear Maps and the Universal Property of $V \otimes W$

In many mathematical contexts — computing volumes, describing physical states, encoding multilinear data — one encounters functions of two vector arguments that are linear in each argument separately. A function $\varphi: V \times W \to U$ is called **bilinear** if$v \mapsto \varphi(v, w)$ is linear for each fixed$w$, and$w \mapsto \varphi(v, w)$ is linear for each fixed$v$. The central question of this section is: is there a universal object that captures all bilinear maps from$V \times W$ at once?

The answer is the **tensor product** $V \otimes W$, characterized by its universal property.

\begin{definition}[Universal Property of Tensor Product]
The tensor product of $V$ and$W$ is a vector space$V \otimes W$ together with a bilinear map$\otimes: V \times W \to V \otimes W$ such that for any vector space$U$ and any bilinear map$\varphi: V \times W \to U$, there exists a unique linear map$\tilde{\varphi}: V \otimes W \to U$ satisfying$\varphi(v, w) = \tilde{\varphi}(v \otimes w)$ for all$v \in V$,$w \in W$.
\end{definition}

In commutative-diagram language: bilinear maps from $V \times W$ to$U$ correspond bijectively and naturally to linear maps from$V \otimes W$ to$U$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** The standard inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle: \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is bilinear, so it factors through$\mathbb{R}^n \otimes \mathbb{R}^n$: there is a unique linear map$\widetilde{\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle}: \mathbb{R}^n \otimes \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ with$\widetilde{\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle}(e_i \otimes e_j) = \delta_{ij}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Matrix multiplication $\mu: M_{m \times n}(\mathbb{R}) \times M_{n \times p}(\mathbb{R}) \to M_{m \times p}(\mathbb{R})$ sending$(A, B) \mapsto AB$ is bilinear. It factors as a linear map from$M_{m \times n} \otimes M_{n \times p}$ to$M_{m \times p}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The determinant on $2 \times 2$ matrices:$(u, v) \mapsto \det[u | v] = u_1 v_2 - u_2 v_1$ is bilinear from$\mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$. It factors through$\mathbb{R}^2 \otimes \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ (and in fact through the exterior power$\bigwedge^2 \mathbb{R}^2$ since it is alternating).
\end{example}

The universal property should be understood as a **conversion device**: bilinearity in two arguments is harder to work with than linearity in one argument; the tensor product converts the former into the latter. Once a bilinear problem is reformulated as a linear problem on $V \otimes W$, all the standard tools of linear algebra become available.

The universal property also determines $V \otimes W$ **up to unique isomorphism**: if$V \otimes W$ and$(V \otimes W)'$ both satisfy the universal property, then the universal properties induce linear maps$\varphi: V \otimes W \to (V \otimes W)'$ and$\psi: (V \otimes W)' \to V \otimes W$ satisfying$\varphi \psi = \mathrm{id}$ and$\psi \varphi = \mathrm{id}$. This means we need not worry about which explicit construction we use.

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** For $V = W = F$ (one-dimensional), the tensor product$F \otimes F$ must satisfy: bilinear maps$F \times F \to U$ correspond to linear maps$F \otimes F \to U$. A bilinear map out of$F \times F$ is determined by its value on$(1,1)$, which can be any element of$U$. So linear maps$F \otimes F \to U$ are also determined by a single element of$U$, meaning$F \otimes F \cong F$. The pure tensor$1 \otimes 1$ is the generator.
\end{example}
\par
\begin{remark}
The tensor product is the categorical coproduct for bilinear maps, playing the same role for "bilinear algebra" that the direct sum plays for linear algebra. More precisely, it is the **left adjoint** to the hom functor: $\mathrm{Hom}(V \otimes W, U) \cong \mathrm{Bil}(V \times W, U) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathrm{Hom}(W, U))$. This "tensor-hom adjunction" is one of the most frequently used natural isomorphisms in algebra and topology.
\end{remark}
