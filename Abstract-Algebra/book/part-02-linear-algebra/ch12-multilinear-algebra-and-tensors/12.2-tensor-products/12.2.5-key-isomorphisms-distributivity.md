# 12.2.5 Key Isomorphisms: $V^* \otimes W \cong \mathcal{L}(V,W)$, Distributivity

Once we have the tensor product and its universal property, several important isomorphisms follow naturally. These are not merely formal facts — they reveal how tensor products relate to other constructions we already know.

**The central isomorphism.** The most important identifies tensors with linear maps:

\begin{theorem}
$V^* \otimes W \cong \mathcal{L}(V, W)$ (when$V$ is finite-dimensional).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define a bilinear map $V^* \times W \to \mathcal{L}(V, W)$ by$(f, w) \mapsto (v \mapsto f(v) \cdot w)$. By the universal property, this extends to a linear map$\Phi: V^* \otimes W \to \mathcal{L}(V, W)$. In bases:$\Phi(e^i \otimes f_j)(e_k) = e^i(e_k) f_j = \delta^i_k f_j$. So$\Phi(e^i \otimes f_j)$ is the elementary map sending$e_i$ to$f_j$ and all other basis vectors to 0. Since these elementary maps are a basis for$\mathcal{L}(V, W)$ (there are$mn$ of them for$\dim V = m$,$\dim W = n$, and$\dim \mathcal{L}(V,W) = mn$),$\Phi$ is an isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** A pure tensor $e^i \otimes f_j \in V^* \otimes W$ corresponds to the elementary matrix$E_{ji}$ (sending basis vector$e_i$ to$f_j$, others to 0). Every matrix is a sum of elementary matrices, matching the fact that every element of$V^* \otimes W$ is a sum of pure tensors.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** A linear map $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ with matrix$A = \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ corresponds to the tensor$a \cdot e^1 \otimes e_1 + b \cdot e^2 \otimes e_1 + c \cdot e^1 \otimes e_2 + d \cdot e^2 \otimes e_2 \in (\mathbb{R}^2)^* \otimes \mathbb{R}^2$.
\end{example}

**Distributivity.** The tensor product distributes over direct sums:

\begin{proposition}
$(V \oplus U) \otimes W \cong (V \otimes W) \oplus (U \otimes W)$.
\end{proposition}

More generally, $\bigoplus_i V_i \otimes W \cong \bigoplus_i (V_i \otimes W)$. This makes$\otimes$ "bilinear over the category of vector spaces."

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** $\dim(V \otimes W) = mn$ follows from$\mathbb{F}^m \otimes \mathbb{F}^n \cong (\mathbb{F} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{F}) \otimes \mathbb{F}^n \cong (\mathbb{F} \otimes \mathbb{F}^n) \oplus \cdots \oplus (\mathbb{F} \otimes \mathbb{F}^n) \cong \mathbb{F}^n \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{F}^n$ ($m$ summands)$\cong \mathbb{F}^{mn}$.
\end{example}

**Other natural isomorphisms.** Using the universal property:
- $V \otimes W \cong W \otimes V$ (commutativity of tensor product, via$(v,w) \mapsto w \otimes v$).
- $(U \otimes V) \otimes W \cong U \otimes (V \otimes W)$ (associativity).
- $F \otimes V \cong V$ (unit for tensor product, via$c \otimes v \mapsto cv$).
- $V^* \otimes V \cong \mathcal{L}(V, V) = \mathrm{End}(V)$, and contraction gives$\mathrm{tr}: V^* \otimes V \to F$ by$f \otimes v \mapsto f(v)$.

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The trace on $M_n(\mathbb{F}) \cong (\mathbb{F}^n)^* \otimes \mathbb{F}^n \cong V^* \otimes V$: the trace is the contraction map$f \otimes v \mapsto f(v)$. For the elementary matrix$E_{ij} \leftrightarrow e^i \otimes e_j$:$\mathrm{tr}(E_{ij}) = e^i(e_j) = \delta^i_j$. Consistent with the matrix trace$\mathrm{tr}(E_{ij}) = \delta_{ij}$.
\end{example}
\par
\begin{remark}
The isomorphism $V^* \otimes W \cong \mathcal{L}(V,W)$ shows that tensors are not exotic new objects but a unified framework encompassing objects already central to linear algebra. In particular, bilinear forms on$V$ correspond to$V^* \otimes V^*$ (or equivalently$\mathcal{L}(V, V^*)$), inner products correspond to symmetric elements of$V^* \otimes V^*$, and the Riemannian metric tensor in differential geometry is a field of symmetric$\binom{0}{2}$-tensors (smooth sections of$T^*M \otimes T^*M$).
\end{remark}
