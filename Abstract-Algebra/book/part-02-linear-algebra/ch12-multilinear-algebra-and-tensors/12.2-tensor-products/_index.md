# 12.2 Tensor Products

A bilinear map $B : V \times W \to U$ is one that is linear in each argument separately: $B(\lambda v + v', w) = \lambda B(v, w) + B(v', w)$ and similarly in $w$. Bilinear maps arise everywhere — the inner product, matrix multiplication viewed in the right way, the cross product — but they do not fit into the framework of linear algebra as long as they must be studied "one map at a time." The tensor product $V \otimes W$ is the universal solution to this problem: it is a vector space equipped with a bilinear map $\otimes : V \times W \to V \otimes W$ (written $(v, w) \mapsto v \otimes w$) such that every bilinear map out of $V \times W$ factors uniquely through $\otimes$. This universal property is not a theorem about one particular construction; it is the definition of what $V \otimes W$ must be, and it determines $V \otimes W$ up to unique isomorphism.

One concrete construction of $V \otimes W$ takes the free vector space on the set $V \times W$ and quotients by all relations that force bilinearity: $(v + v', w) - (v, w) - (v', w) = 0$, $(\lambda v, w) - \lambda(v, w) = 0$, and the symmetric versions in $w$. The equivalence class of $(v, w)$ in this quotient is written $v \otimes w$; these are the elementary (or pure) tensors. Not every element of $V \otimes W$ is a pure tensor: a general element is a finite sum $\sum_i v_i \otimes w_i$, and this sum may not be reducible to a single $v \otimes w$. When $V$ has basis $\{e_1, \ldots, e_m\}$ and $W$ has basis $\{f_1, \ldots, f_n\}$, the set $\{e_i \otimes f_j : 1 \leq i \leq m, 1 \leq j \leq n\}$ is a basis for $V \otimes W$, giving the dimension formula $\dim(V \otimes W) = \dim V \cdot \dim W$.

Several key isomorphisms follow from the universal property. The most important are $V \otimes F \cong V$, $(V \otimes W) \otimes U \cong V \otimes (W \otimes U)$ (associativity), $V \otimes (W \oplus U) \cong (V \otimes W) \oplus (V \otimes U)$ (distributivity over direct sums), and $V^* \otimes W \cong \mathrm{Hom}(V, W)$ (the identification of linear maps with tensors, made precise). This last isomorphism is especially significant: a tensor $f \otimes w \in V^* \otimes W$ acts as the rank-one linear map $v \mapsto f(v) \cdot w$, and every linear map can be written as a sum of such rank-one maps. The tensor product is thus not just a construction for multilinear algebra — it is the language in which linear maps themselves are naturally expressed when working over multiple spaces at once.
