# 12.3 The Tensor Algebra

The tensor product makes it possible to multiply vectors: given $v, w \in V$, their tensor product $v \otimes w \in V \otimes V$ is a new object in a new space. By iterating, we get $v \otimes w \otimes u \in V \otimes V \otimes V$, and so on for any number of factors. The tensor algebra $T(V)$ assembles all these spaces at once:

$$T(V) = F \oplus V \oplus (V \otimes V) \oplus (V \otimes V \otimes V) \oplus \cdots = \bigoplus_{k=0}^\infty T^k(V),$$

where $T^k(V) = V^{\otimes k}$ is the $k$-fold tensor power of $V$ (with $T^0(V) = F$ by convention). The multiplication in $T(V)$ is tensor product: an element of $T^j(V)$ multiplied by an element of $T^k(V)$ gives an element of $T^{j+k}(V)$. This makes $T(V)$ into a graded algebra — an algebra whose underlying vector space is a direct sum of "homogeneous" pieces, with multiplication respecting the grading.

The tensor algebra satisfies a universal property that identifies it as the free associative algebra on $V$: given any associative $F$-algebra $A$ and any $F$-linear map $f : V \to A$, there is a unique algebra homomorphism $\bar{f} : T(V) \to A$ extending $f$. This means $T(V)$ is the most general associative algebra generated by $V$, with no relations imposed. The polynomial ring $F[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is a quotient of $T(V)$ (where $V = F^n$) obtained by imposing commutativity, and the exterior algebra $\Lambda(V)$ is a quotient obtained by imposing $v \otimes v = 0$. Every associative algebra generated by a vector space can therefore be viewed as $T(V)$ modulo some ideal of relations, and understanding $T(V)$ is the first step toward understanding any such algebra.

The grading of $T(V)$ also introduces the concept of homogeneous components. An element is homogeneous of degree $k$ if it lies in $T^k(V)$; a general element is a finite sum of homogeneous pieces. Algebra maps that respect the grading — graded algebra homomorphisms — are maps sending $T^k(V)$ to the degree-$k$ part of the target. The constructions of the exterior and symmetric algebras in Sections 12.4 and 12.5 are exactly quotients of $T(V)$ by homogeneous ideals, preserving the graded structure and inheriting specific universal properties from the parent algebra.
