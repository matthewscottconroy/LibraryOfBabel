# 12.4.2 The Exterior Powers $\bigwedge^k V$ and Their Bases

The exterior algebra $\bigwedge V = \bigoplus_{k \geq 0} \bigwedge^k V$ inherits the grading from$T(V)$. The **$k$-th exterior power**$\bigwedge^k V$ is the degree-$k$ component: spanned by all$k$-fold wedge products$v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_k$ with$v_i \in V$, subject to anti-commutativity.

\begin{theorem}
Let $\{e_1, \ldots, e_n\}$ be a basis of$V$. A basis of$\bigwedge^k V$ consists of the wedge products
$$e_{i_1} \wedge e_{i_2} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_k} \quad \text{with } 1 \leq i_1 < i_2 < \cdots < i_k \leq n.$$
\end{theorem}

**Spanning.** Any $k$-fold wedge of basis vectors can be reordered to an increasing sequence by transpositions (each introducing a sign), and any wedge of general vectors expands by multilinearity into a linear combination of wedges of basis vectors.

**Linear independence.** For each strictly increasing tuple $(i_1, \ldots, i_k)$, define the alternating multilinear map$\omega_{i_1 \ldots i_k}: V^k \to F$ by$\omega_{i_1 \ldots i_k}(e_{j_1}, \ldots, e_{j_k}) = \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)$ if$\{j_1, \ldots, j_k\} = \{i_1, \ldots, i_k\}$ as sets (with$\sigma$ the permutation), and$0$ otherwise. By the universal property of$\bigwedge^k V$, this extends to a linear map on$\bigwedge^k V$, and it evaluates to$\pm 1$ on$e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_k}$ and$0$ on all other basis elements, confirming independence.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** For $n = 3$:$\bigwedge^2 \mathbb{R}^3$ has basis$\{e_1 \wedge e_2, e_1 \wedge e_3, e_2 \wedge e_3\}$: three elements, dimension 3.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** For $n = 4$:$\bigwedge^2 \mathbb{R}^4$ has basis$\{e_1\wedge e_2, e_1\wedge e_3, e_1\wedge e_4, e_2\wedge e_3, e_2\wedge e_4, e_3\wedge e_4\}$: six elements, dimension 6.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Expand $(e_1 + e_2) \wedge (e_2 + e_3)$ in$\bigwedge^2 \mathbb{R}^3$:
$(e_1 + e_2) \wedge (e_2 + e_3) = e_1 \wedge e_2 + e_1 \wedge e_3 + e_2 \wedge e_2 + e_2 \wedge e_3 = e_1 \wedge e_2 + e_1 \wedge e_3 + 0 + e_2 \wedge e_3$.
\end{example}
\par
**Coordinates.** For $v = \sum_i a_i e_i$ and$w = \sum_j b_j e_j$:
$$v \wedge w = \sum_{i < j} (a_i b_j - a_j b_i)(e_i \wedge e_j).$$
The coefficients $a_i b_j - a_j b_i$ are the$2 \times 2$ minors of the matrix$\begin{pmatrix}a_1 & \cdots & a_n \\ b_1 & \cdots & b_n\end{pmatrix}$. In$\mathbb{R}^3$, the coefficients$(a_2 b_3 - a_3 b_2, a_3 b_1 - a_1 b_3, a_1 b_2 - a_2 b_1)$ are exactly the components of the cross product$v \times w$.
\par
\begin{example}
**Example 4.** For $v = (2, 1, 0)$ and$w = (1, 3, 1)$ in$\mathbb{R}^3$:
$v \wedge w = (2\cdot3 - 1\cdot1)(e_1\wedge e_2) + (2\cdot1 - 0\cdot1)(e_1\wedge e_3) + (1\cdot1 - 0\cdot3)(e_2\wedge e_3) = 5(e_1\wedge e_2) + 2(e_1\wedge e_3) + e_2\wedge e_3$.
The corresponding cross product would be $(5, 2, 1)$ (reading off the coefficients with appropriate signs).
\end{example}
\par
\begin{remark}
The exterior algebra $\bigwedge V$ encodes the theory of determinants, oriented volumes, and differential forms in a single algebraic structure. The basis elements$e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_k}$ for$i_1 < \cdots < i_k$ are in bijection with$k$-element subsets of$\{1, \ldots, n\}$, and the coefficient of$e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_k}$ in an exterior product corresponds to the$k \times k$ minor of the matrix formed by the factor vectors. This connection between wedge products and minors is the basis for the Plücker embedding of Grassmannians in projective space.
\end{remark}
