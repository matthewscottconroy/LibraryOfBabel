# 12.5 The Symmetric Algebra

Parallel to the exterior algebra, the symmetric algebra $\mathrm{Sym}(V)$ is obtained by imposing commutativity on the tensor algebra: it is the quotient of $T(V)$ by the ideal generated by all elements $v \otimes w - w \otimes v$. The image of a tensor $v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_k$ in this quotient is written $v_1 \cdot v_2 \cdots v_k$ (or sometimes $v_1 \vee \cdots \vee v_k$) and called the symmetric product. By definition, $v \cdot w = w \cdot v$ for all $v, w \in V$, so the symmetric algebra is commutative. It is the natural algebraic home for symmetric multilinear maps in the same way that the exterior algebra is the home for alternating ones.

The $k$-th symmetric power $\mathrm{Sym}^k(V)$ has a basis consisting of symmetric products $e_{i_1} \cdot e_{i_2} \cdots e_{i_k}$ with $i_1 \leq i_2 \leq \cdots \leq i_k$, i.e., monomials in the basis vectors allowing repetition. The count of such monomials is $\binom{n+k-1}{k}$, the number of multisets of size $k$ from an alphabet of size $n = \dim V$. In particular, $\mathrm{Sym}^k(V) \cong F[x_1, \ldots, x_n]_k$, the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $k$ in $n$ variables, and $\mathrm{Sym}(V) \cong F[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ as graded algebras (once a basis for $V$ is chosen). The symmetric algebra is therefore the polynomial algebra on $V$, understood in a basis-free way.

The relationship between $\mathrm{Sym}^k(V)$ and multilinear maps is given by polarization: every homogeneous polynomial function of degree $k$ on $V$ corresponds to a unique symmetric $k$-linear map, and the two perspectives are interconvertible by the polarization identity. For example, over a field of characteristic zero, a degree-2 homogeneous polynomial $Q(v)$ corresponds to the symmetric bilinear form $B(u, v) = \frac{1}{2}[Q(u+v) - Q(u) - Q(v)]$. This connection between symmetric algebras and polynomial functions breaks down in characteristic $p$ (when $p \leq k$), which is one reason characteristic-$p$ algebra requires extra care. The symmetric algebra also appears in representation theory as the module on which the symmetric group and general linear group act by natural actions, and its structure as a polynomial ring makes it a fundamental object in invariant theory.
