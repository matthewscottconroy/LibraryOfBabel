# 12.6.3 Contraction and the Trace

**Contraction** is the fundamental operation that reduces a tensor's type by pairing one upper index with one lower index and summing. Given a tensor of type $(p, q)$ with$p \geq 1$ and$q \geq 1$, we can form a tensor of type$(p-1, q-1)$ by selecting one upper index position$r$ and one lower index position$s$, then summing the components when the values of those two indices are set equal. In Einstein notation, contracting the$r$-th upper index with the$s$-th lower index of$T^{i_1 \cdots i_p}{}_{j_1 \cdots j_q}$ gives:
$$C^{i_1 \cdots \hat\imath_r \cdots i_p}{}_{j_1 \cdots \hat\jmath_s \cdots j_q} = T^{i_1 \cdots k \cdots i_p}{}_{j_1 \cdots k \cdots j_q}$$
where $k$ is the summation index (dummy index), and hats indicate omitted slots.

The **trace** is the most familiar example. A linear operator $T: V \to V$ is a$(1,1)$-tensor with components$T^i{}_j$. Contracting the single upper index with the single lower index gives the scalar$T^i{}_i = \sum_{i=1}^n T^i{}_i = \mathrm{tr}(T)$.

\begin{proposition}
The trace $\mathrm{tr}(T) = T^i{}_i$ is independent of the choice of basis.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under the basis change $e_i \mapsto \tilde{e}_i = P^k{}_i e_k$, the components of$T$ transform as$\tilde{T}^i{}_j = (P^{-1})^i{}_k T^k{}_l P^l{}_j$ (one$P^{-1}$ for the upper index, one$P$ for the lower). Contracting:$\tilde{T}^i{}_i = (P^{-1})^i{}_k T^k{}_l P^l{}_i = T^k{}_l \delta^l{}_k = T^k{}_k$ (using$(P^{-1})^i{}_k P^l{}_i = (P \cdot P^{-1})^l{}_k = \delta^l{}_k$). So$\tilde{\mathrm{tr}}(T) = \mathrm{tr}(T)$, confirming basis-independence. Alternatively: this follows from the cyclic property of trace,$\mathrm{tr}(P^{-1}AP) = \mathrm{tr}(A)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
**Example 1 (Contracting a $(2,1)$-tensor).** Let$T^{ij}{}_k$ be a$(2,1)$-tensor. Contracting the second upper index with the lower index gives the$(1,0)$-tensor (vector)$C^i = T^{ij}{}_j = \sum_j T^{ij}{}_j$. Contracting the first upper index with the lower index gives$D^j = T^{ij}{}_i = \sum_i T^{ij}{}_i$. These two contractions generally produce different vectors, so it matters which indices are contracted.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2 (Inner product via contraction).** Given vectors $u, v \in V$ and a metric tensor$g \in V^* \otimes V^*$ (type$(0,2)$), the inner product$\langle u, v \rangle = g_{ij} u^i v^j$ is obtained by two contractions: first contract$g_{ij}$ with$u^i$ on the index$i$ to get a covector$g_{ij} u^i = u_j$ (lowering the index of$u$), then contract$u_j$ with$v^j$ on the index$j$ to get a scalar. The result is the scalar$u_j v^j = g_{ij} u^i v^j$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3 (Ricci tensor from Riemann tensor).** The Riemann curvature tensor $R^i{}_{jkl}$ (a$(1,3)$-tensor) is contracted to form the Ricci tensor$R_{jl} = R^i{}_{jil}$ (a$(0,2)$-tensor) by summing over the first upper index and the middle lower index. Further contracting the Ricci tensor with the inverse metric gives the Ricci scalar$R = g^{jl} R_{jl} = R^i{}_{j}{}^j{}_i$ (a scalar). These are the fundamental invariants appearing in Einstein's field equations$G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\, T_{\mu\nu}$, where$G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4 (Determinant via contraction).** The determinant can be written using the Levi-Civita symbol $\varepsilon_{i_1 \cdots i_n}$ (a totally antisymmetric$(0,n)$-tensor with$\varepsilon_{1 2 \cdots n} = 1$) as:
$$\det(A) = \varepsilon_{i_1 \cdots i_n} A^{i_1}{}_1 A^{i_2}{}_2 \cdots A^{i_n}{}_n,$$
where all $n$ upper indices are contracted with$n$ lower indices of$\varepsilon$. This formula is the Leibniz expansion of the determinant expressed purely in terms of tensorial contractions.
\end{example}

The coordinate-independence of contraction is the key point. Abstractly, contraction is the map induced by the evaluation pairing $V^* \otimes V \to F$ given by$f \otimes v \mapsto f(v)$. This pairing is defined without coordinates, so its output is a genuine scalar. The fact that the trace is basis-independent, or that$g_{ij} u^i v^j$ is the same in all coordinate systems, follows from this abstract observation.

This explains why the trace and determinant are intrinsic invariants of a linear map: they arise from contractions (or iterated contractions), which are basis-independent operations. More generally, all **scalar invariants** of a tensor — the invariants studied in representation theory and differential geometry — arise as iterated contractions. For example, if $M$ is a matrix, then$\mathrm{tr}(M^2) = M^i{}_j M^j{}_i$ is a scalar invariant (a degree-2 polynomial in the entries of$M$); more generally,$\mathrm{tr}(M^k)$ for all$k$ generates all scalar polynomial invariants of$M$ over an algebraically closed field.

\begin{remark}
Contraction has a natural categorical description: it is the **counit** of the adjunction $\mathrm{Hom}(V \otimes W, X) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathrm{Hom}(W, X))$ specialized to$W = V$ and$X = F$, giving the evaluation map$V^* \otimes V \to F$. In the language of monoidal categories, this is the "cap" morphism in a compact closed category. The duality between$V$ and$V^*$ expressed by the evaluation pairing, and its dual "unit"$F \to V \otimes V^*$ (the coevaluation), are the algebraic structures underlying the coordinate-independence of all tensorial operations.
\end{remark}
