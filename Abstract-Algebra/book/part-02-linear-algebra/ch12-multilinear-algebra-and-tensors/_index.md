# Chapter 12 — Multilinear Algebra and Tensors

The previous chapters of Part 2 developed linear algebra: the study of maps that are linear in one argument. This chapter opens up an entire new dimension by studying maps that are linear in each of several arguments simultaneously. A multilinear map $f : V_1 \times V_2 \times \cdots \times V_k \to W$ is one that is linear in each slot when the others are held fixed. Determinants, cross products, scalar triple products, and the metric tensors of Riemannian geometry are all instances of multilinear maps. The machinery built in this chapter — dual spaces, tensor products, exterior algebras, symmetric algebras — provides the algebraic language in which all of differential geometry, theoretical physics, and representation theory is written.

The chapter begins with the dual space $V^* = \mathrm{Hom}(V, F)$, the space of all linear functionals on $V$. In finite dimensions $V^*$ is isomorphic to $V$, but the isomorphism depends on a choice of basis (or, in an inner product space, on the inner product itself) — it is not canonical. The dual basis $\{e^1, \ldots, e^n\}$ satisfies $e^i(e_j) = \delta^i_j$ (the Kronecker delta), and the double dual $V^{**}$ is canonically isomorphic to $V$ via the evaluation map $v \mapsto \mathrm{ev}_v$, where $\mathrm{ev}_v(f) = f(v)$. This canonical identification — which holds for any vector space and requires no additional structure — is the first example of a natural isomorphism, a concept that recurs throughout category theory.

Tensor products make the passage from linear to multilinear systematic. The tensor product $V \otimes W$ is characterized by a universal property: bilinear maps from $V \times W$ to any vector space $U$ correspond naturally to linear maps from $V \otimes W$ to $U$. This is not merely a convenience — it is the correct definition of what $V \otimes W$ should be, and it makes tensor products the categorical "universal multilinear object." The tensor algebra $T(V) = F \oplus V \oplus (V \otimes V) \oplus \cdots$ assembles all tensor powers into a single graded algebra, the free associative algebra on $V$. Quotients of this algebra by specific ideals give the exterior algebra $\Lambda(V)$ (imposing $v \wedge v = 0$) and the symmetric algebra $\mathrm{Sym}(V)$ (imposing $v \otimes w = w \otimes v$), each with its own universal property and dimension formulas.

The exterior algebra is where linear independence and volume live. The wedge product $v_1 \wedge v_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_k$ is the prototype of an alternating multilinear form, and the collection $\Lambda^k(V)$ has dimension $\binom{n}{k}$. The top exterior power $\Lambda^n(V)$ is one-dimensional, and the determinant of a linear operator $T : V \to V$ is defined as the unique scalar $\det(T)$ such that $T$ acts on $\Lambda^n(V)$ by multiplication by $\det(T)$. This coordinate-free definition makes the multiplicativity $\det(ST) = \det(S)\det(T)$ and the alternating property of the determinant completely transparent. The chapter closes with tensors in coordinates, introducing the index notation used in physics and differential geometry, the Einstein summation convention, and the transformation law for tensor components under change of basis. This prepares the reader for the use of tensor algebra in representation theory and Lie theory in Part 3.
