# 14.1.1 Left and Right Cosets; the Coset Partition

Given a subgroup $H$ of a group$G$, we can use$H$ to sort the elements of$G$ into equal-sized, non-overlapping pieces. This sorting is the coset partition, and it is the geometric heart of Lagrange's theorem.

\begin{definition}
For a subgroup $H \leq G$ and an element$g \in G$, the \textbf{left coset} of$H$ containing$g$ is
$$gH = \{gh : h \in H\}.$$
The \textbf{right coset} of $H$ containing$g$ is$Hg = \{hg : h \in H\}$.
\end{definition}

Intuitively, a left coset is the set obtained by translating the entire subgroup $H$ to the left by$g$. In an abelian group,$gH = Hg$ always, but in a nonabelian group the two may differ — a distinction that will become crucial when we ask which subgroups support a quotient group structure.

The key structural fact is that the left cosets of $H$ in$G$ form a **partition** of$G$: every element of$G$ belongs to exactly one left coset, and any two left cosets are either identical or disjoint. To see this, note first that$g \in gH$ (since$e \in H$, so$g = ge \in gH$), ensuring every element is in some coset. For the disjointness: suppose$gH \cap g'H \neq \emptyset$, and let$x$ be a common element. Then$x = gh_1 = g'h_2$ for some$h_1, h_2 \in H$, so$g = g'h_2 h_1^{-1} \in g'H$. From$g \in g'H$ one shows$gH \subseteq g'H$ (multiply on the right by any$h \in H$:$gh = g'(h_2 h_1^{-1} h) \in g'H$), and by symmetry$g'H \subseteq gH$, giving$gH = g'H$. The same argument applies to right cosets.

Every coset $gH$ has exactly$|H|$ elements: the map$H \to gH$ defined by$h \mapsto gh$ is a bijection (injective since left multiplication by$g$ is injective; surjective by definition of$gH$). The partition of$G$ into left cosets therefore partitions$|G|$ elements into pieces of size$|H|$, immediately implying that$|H|$ divides$|G|$ whenever$G$ is finite.

**Example 1.** Let $G = S_3$ and$H = \langle (12) \rangle = \{e, (12)\}$. The left cosets are:
$$eH = \{e, (12)\}, \quad (13)H = \{(13), (132)\}, \quad (23)H = \{(23), (123)\}.$$
These three cosets of size 2 partition $S_3$. The right cosets are$He = \{e,(12)\}$,$H(13) = \{(13),(123)\}$,$H(23) = \{(23),(132)\}$. Note that$(13)H \neq H(13)$, confirming that$\langle (12) \rangle$ is not normal in$S_3$.

**Example 2.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}$ (additive) and$H = 3\mathbb{Z}$. The left (= right, since$\mathbb{Z}$ is abelian) cosets are$0 + 3\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots,-3,0,3,6,\ldots\}$,$1 + 3\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots,-2,1,4,7,\ldots\}$, and$2 + 3\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots,-1,2,5,8,\ldots\}$. These are the three residue classes modulo 3.

**Example 3.** In $G = D_4 = \langle r, s : r^4 = s^2 = e, srs = r^{-1} \rangle$, let$H = \langle r \rangle = \{e, r, r^2, r^3\}$. The two left cosets are$H$ and$sH = \{s, sr, sr^2, sr^3\}$. Since$[D_4 : H] = 2$, this single nontrivial coset is all of$D_4 \setminus H$. Any subgroup of index 2 is automatically normal, so$\langle r \rangle \trianglelefteq D_4$.

**Example 4.** Let $H = SL_2(\mathbb{R})$ inside$G = GL_2(\mathbb{R})$. The left coset of a matrix$A$ with$\det(A) = \lambda$ is$A \cdot SL_2(\mathbb{R}) = \{B : \det(B) = \lambda\}$. The cosets are exactly the level sets of the determinant, and they are indexed by nonzero real numbers. Since$SL_2(\mathbb{R})$ is normal in$GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ (being the kernel of the determinant homomorphism), left and right cosets coincide here.

**Example 5.** In $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ with$H = \langle \bar{4} \rangle = \{\bar{0}, \bar{4}, \bar{8}\}$, the cosets are$\bar{0} + H = \{\bar{0},\bar{4},\bar{8}\}$,$\bar{1} + H = \{\bar{1},\bar{5},\bar{9}\}$,$\bar{2} + H = \{\bar{2},\bar{6},\bar{10}\}$,$\bar{3} + H = \{\bar{3},\bar{7},\bar{11}\}$. Four cosets of size 3 partition the 12 elements of$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, confirming$[G:H] = 4$.

\begin{remark}
The coset partition is a combinatorial object that exists for any subgroup, without any normality assumption. However, for the set of cosets to inherit a group structure — forming a quotient group — normality is essential. The distinction between the coset partition (always available) and the quotient group (available only for normal subgroups) is one of the central themes of Chapter 14.
\end{remark}
