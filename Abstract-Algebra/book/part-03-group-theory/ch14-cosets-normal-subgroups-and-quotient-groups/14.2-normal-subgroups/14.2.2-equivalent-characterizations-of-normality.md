# 14.2.2 Equivalent Characterizations of Normality

In practice, normality is verified using whichever of its several equivalent formulations is most convenient for the problem at hand. Fluency with all of them is therefore essential.

\begin{proposition}
The following conditions on a subgroup $N \leq G$ are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $gNg^{-1} \subseteq N$ for all$g \in G$.
\item $gNg^{-1} = N$ for all$g \in G$.
\item $gN = Ng$ for all$g \in G$ (left and right cosets coincide).
\item $N$ is a union of conjugacy classes of$G$.
\item $N$ is the kernel of some group homomorphism$\varphi: G \to H$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The equivalence of (1) and (2) requires a moment's thought: (1) says $gNg^{-1} \subseteq N$, but applying this also with$g^{-1}$ in place of$g$ gives$g^{-1}Ng \subseteq N$, i.e.,$N \subseteq gNg^{-1}$, upgrading the inclusion to equality. This is a useful technique — when checking normality in practice, it often suffices to verify only the inclusion direction (as long as it holds for all$g$).

The characterization (4) is geometrically transparent. Recall that the conjugacy class of $n \in G$ is$\{gng^{-1} : g \in G\}$. The normality condition$gNg^{-1} = N$ says that conjugation by any$g$ permutes the elements of$N$ among themselves, which is precisely the statement that$N$ is a union of complete conjugacy classes. In an abelian group, every conjugacy class is a single element (since$gng^{-1} = n$ always), so every subgroup is automatically a union of conjugacy classes, and hence every subgroup is normal.

The characterization (5) — that $N$ is normal if and only if it is the kernel of some homomorphism — ties the algebraic and structural perspectives together. Every kernel is normal (proved in Section 15.1.3), and conversely every normal subgroup is the kernel of the projection$G \to G/N$. This gives an inexhaustible supply of examples.

**Example 1.** To check that $A_n \trianglelefteq S_n$ via condition (4): the conjugacy classes of$S_n$ are determined by cycle type, and$A_n$ consists of all permutations of even cycle type (i.e., even permutations). The cycle type of$\sigma$ and$\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}$ are the same (conjugation preserves cycle structure), so$A_n$ is a union of conjugacy classes, hence normal.

**Example 2.** Consider $G = D_4 = \langle r, s : r^4 = s^2 = e, srs^{-1} = r^{-1} \rangle$ and$N = \langle r^2 \rangle = \{e, r^2\}$. To verify normality via condition (1):$r(r^2)r^{-1} = r^2 \in N$, and$s(r^2)s^{-1} = sr^2s^{-1}$. Since$srs^{-1} = r^{-1}$, we get$sr^2s^{-1} = (srs^{-1})^2 = r^{-2} = r^2 \in N$. So$gNg^{-1} \subseteq N$ for generators$r$ and$s$, hence for all$g \in D_4$.

**Example 3.** The subgroup $SL_n(\mathbb{F}) \trianglelefteq GL_n(\mathbb{F})$ via condition (5): it is the kernel of the determinant homomorphism$\det: GL_n(\mathbb{F}) \to \mathbb{F}^*$.

**Example 4.** In $S_4$, the Klein four-group$V = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$ is normal. Check via condition (4): in$S_4$ the conjugacy class of$(12)(34)$ consists of all products of two disjoint transpositions, which are$(12)(34)$,$(13)(24)$,$(14)(23)$ — exactly the nonidentity elements of$V$. So$V$ is a union of conjugacy classes$\{e\}$ and$\{(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}$.

**Example 5.** In $Q_8$, the center$Z(Q_8) = \{1, -1\}$ is normal. Check: it has order 2 in a group of order 8, so$[Q_8 : Z(Q_8)] = 4$. But more directly:$-1$ commutes with every element of$Q_8$ (it is the central element$i^2 = j^2 = k^2$), so$\{1,-1\}$ is the kernel of the map$Q_8 \to Q_8/\{1,-1\}$, or alternatively it is a union of conjugacy classes$\{1\}$ and$\{-1\}$.

\begin{remark}
Condition (1) — checking only $gNg^{-1} \subseteq N$ rather than equality — is the most efficient in practice when$G$ is generated by a set$S$. Normality of$N$ is equivalent to$sNs^{-1} \subseteq N$ and$s^{-1}Ns \subseteq N$ for each generator$s \in S$. In the example of$D_4$, it sufficed to check conjugation by$r$ and$s$ since these generate$D_4$.
\end{remark}
