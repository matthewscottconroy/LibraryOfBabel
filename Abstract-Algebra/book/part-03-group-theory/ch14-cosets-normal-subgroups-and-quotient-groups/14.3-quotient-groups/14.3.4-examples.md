# 14.3.4 Examples: $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$,$S_3/A_3$,$GL_n/SL_n$

The quotient group construction acquires meaning through examples, and the three most instructive cases reveal the range of structures that quotients can produce. Each also provides a model computation that can be imitated in less familiar settings.

The first and most familiar example is $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, which is the quotient of the additive group$\mathbb{Z}$ by the normal subgroup$n\mathbb{Z} = \{nk : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. The cosets are the residue classes$\bar{0}, \bar{1}, \ldots, \overline{n-1}$, and the group operation is addition modulo$n$. The order is$[\mathbb{Z} : n\mathbb{Z}] = n$. This recovers the cyclic group$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ as a quotient group, confirming that the abstract construction generalizes modular arithmetic exactly as intended.

**Example 1.** $S_3/A_3$. Since$A_3$ has order 3 in a group of order 6, the quotient has order$6/3 = 2$. A group of order 2 is necessarily cyclic and isomorphic to$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The two elements of the quotient are$A_3 = \{e,(123),(132)\}$ (the identity of$S_3/A_3$) and the coset$(12)A_3 = \{(12),(23),(13)\}$ of odd permutations. Multiplication:$(12)A_3 \cdot (12)A_3 = (12)^2 A_3 = eA_3 = A_3$ (since$(12)^2 = e$). Under the isomorphism$S_3/A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, the projection$S_3 \to S_3/A_3 \cong \{0,1\}$ is the sign homomorphism. The quotient "remembers" only the parity of each permutation, discarding all other information.

**Example 2.** $GL_n(\mathbb{F})/SL_n(\mathbb{F})$. The determinant$\det: GL_n(\mathbb{F}) \to \mathbb{F}^*$ is a surjective homomorphism with kernel$SL_n(\mathbb{F})$. The quotient$GL_n(\mathbb{F})/SL_n(\mathbb{F})$ is therefore isomorphic to$(\mathbb{F}^*, \cdot)$ — the multiplicative group of nonzero field elements — via the induced isomorphism$\bar{\det}(A \cdot SL_n(\mathbb{F})) = \det(A)$. This is the first isomorphism theorem in action: a surjective homomorphism with kernel$N$ realizes the codomain as a quotient of the domain by$N$.

**Example 3.** $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. The quotient of$(\mathbb{R},+)$ by the subgroup$\mathbb{Z}$ is the **circle group**$\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, whose elements are real numbers modulo 1 (fractional parts). It is isomorphic to the group of complex numbers of absolute value 1 under multiplication, via$t + \mathbb{Z} \mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$. Every element of$\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ has finite order if and only if$t$ is rational:$t + \mathbb{Z}$ has order$q$ iff$qt \in \mathbb{Z}$ iff$t = p/q$ for some integer$p$.

**Example 4.** $D_n/\langle r \rangle$. The rotation subgroup$\langle r \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is normal in$D_n$ (index 2). The quotient$D_n/\langle r \rangle$ has order 2, with elements$\langle r \rangle$ (rotations) and$s\langle r \rangle$ (reflections). Multiplication:$(s\langle r \rangle)^2 = s^2 \langle r \rangle = \langle r \rangle$ (identity). So$D_n/\langle r \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 5.** $Q_8/\langle -1 \rangle$. The center$\langle -1 \rangle = \{1,-1\}$ is normal in$Q_8$ (it lies in$Z(Q_8)$). The four cosets are$\{1,-1\}$,$\{i,-i\}$,$\{j,-j\}$,$\{k,-k\}$. Multiplication table (denoting cosets by their positive representative):

|$\cdot$|$1$|$i$|$j$|$k$|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|$1$|$1$|$i$|$j$|$k$|
|$i$|$i$|$1$|$k$|$j$|
|$j$|$j$|$k$|$1$|$i$|
|$k$|$k$|$j$|$i$|$1$|

Every nonidentity element has order 2 and any two distinct nonidentity elements multiply to the third. This is the multiplication table of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, also known as the Klein four-group$V_4$.

These examples illustrate the deeper point: quotient groups extract specific "invariants" from complicated groups by collapsing everything except the invariant we care about — parity in the case of $S_3/A_3$, determinant in the case of$GL_n/SL_n$, fractional part in the case of$\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. The isomorphism theorems in Chapter 15 provide the general framework for this kind of analysis.
