# 15.2.2 Second Isomorphism Theorem: $H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$

The First Isomorphism Theorem tells us what a quotient by a normal subgroup looks like from inside the group. The Second Isomorphism Theorem addresses a different situation: given a subgroup $H$ and a normal subgroup$N$ of$G$, how does their interaction produce a quotient?

\begin{theorem}[Second Isomorphism Theorem]
Let $H \leq G$ and$N \trianglelefteq G$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $HN = \{hn : h \in H, n \in N\}$ is a subgroup of$G$.
\item $H \cap N \trianglelefteq H$.
\item $H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) $HN$ is nonempty (contains$e = ee$). For closure:$(h_1 n_1)(h_2 n_2) = h_1(n_1 h_2)n_2$. Since$N \trianglelefteq G$,$h_2^{-1}n_1 h_2 \in N$, so$n_1 h_2 = h_2(h_2^{-1}n_1 h_2) \in h_2 N$. Write$n_1 h_2 = h_2 n_3$ for$n_3 \in N$. Then$(h_1 n_1)(h_2 n_2) = (h_1 h_2)(n_3 n_2) \in HN$. For inverses:$(hn)^{-1} = n^{-1}h^{-1} = h^{-1}(hn^{-1}h^{-1}) \in HN$ since$hn^{-1}h^{-1} \in N$ by normality.

(2) For any $h \in H$ and$x \in H \cap N$:$hxh^{-1} \in H$ (since$H$ is a subgroup) and$hxh^{-1} \in N$ (since$N$ is normal in$G$ and$h \in G$). So$hxh^{-1} \in H \cap N$.

(3) Consider $\pi|_H: H \to G/N$, the restriction to$H$ of the natural projection$\pi: G \to G/N$. This is a homomorphism with$\mathrm{im}(\pi|_H) = HN/N$ (since$\pi(h) = hN$ and$hN \in HN/N$ for each$h \in H$, and every coset$hnN = hN$ in$HN/N$ is in the image). The kernel is$\{h \in H : hN = N\} = \{h \in H : h \in N\} = H \cap N$. The First Isomorphism Theorem gives$H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$.
\end{proof}

This theorem is sometimes called the **diamond theorem** because the four subgroups $HN$,$H$,$N$, and$H \cap N$ form a diamond shape in the subgroup lattice. The two sides of the diamond yield isomorphic quotients:$[HN : N] = [H : H \cap N]$.

**Example 1.** In $S_4$, let$H = \langle (12)(34), (13)(24) \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (the Klein four-group$V$) and$N = A_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$. Then$H \cap N = H$ (since$H \subseteq A_4$), so the theorem gives$H/(H \cap H) = H/H \cong HN/N = A_4/A_4$, both trivial — a degenerate case.

**Example 2.** In $\mathbb{Z}$, let$H = m\mathbb{Z}$ and$N = n\mathbb{Z}$ for positive integers$m, n$. Then$HN = \gcd(m,n)\mathbb{Z}$ and$H \cap N = \mathrm{lcm}(m,n)\mathbb{Z}$. The theorem gives$m\mathbb{Z}/\mathrm{lcm}(m,n)\mathbb{Z} \cong \gcd(m,n)\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. Taking$m=6, n=4$:$H \cap N = 12\mathbb{Z}$,$HN = 2\mathbb{Z}$. The theorem:$6\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \cong 2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$. Both have order 2, consistent.

**Example 3.** In $D_6 = \langle r, s \rangle$ (order 12), let$H = \langle s \rangle = \{e, s\}$ and$N = \langle r^2 \rangle = \{e, r^2, r^4\}$ (normal, of order 3). Then$H \cap N = \{e\}$ (orders 2 and 3 are coprime), and$HN$ has order$|H||N|/|H \cap N| = 2 \cdot 3 / 1 = 6$. The Second Isomorphism Theorem:$H/\{e\} \cong HN/N$, i.e.,$H \cong HN/N$, so$HN/N$ has order 2.

**Example 4.** Let $G = GL_3(\mathbb{F})$,$N = SL_3(\mathbb{F})$,$H = \{ \mathrm{diag}(\lambda, 1, 1) : \lambda \in \mathbb{F}^* \}$ (diagonal matrices with first entry free, rest 1). Then$H \cap N = \{I\}$ (only$\lambda = 1$ gives determinant 1),$HN$ consists of matrices with$(2,2)$ and$(3,3)$ entries 1 (roughly), and$HN/N \cong H/(H \cap N) \cong H \cong \mathbb{F}^*$.

**Example 5.** In $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, let$H = \langle \bar{4} \rangle = \{0,4,8\}$ and$N = \langle \bar{6} \rangle = \{0,6\}$. Then$H \cap N = \{0\}$ (only common element),$HN = H + N = \langle \bar{2} \rangle = \{0,2,4,6,8,10\}$ (order 6). The theorem:$H/\{0\} \cong HN/N$, i.e.,$\langle\bar{4}\rangle \cong \langle\bar{2}\rangle/\langle\bar{6}\rangle$. Both sides have order 3.

\begin{remark}
When the Sylow theorems produce two subgroups $H$ and$N$ with$N$ normal, the Second Isomorphism Theorem is frequently the tool that establishes the key counting argument. In particular, if$|H|$ and$|N|$ are coprime, then$|H \cap N| = 1$ and$|HN| = |H||N|$, so$HN$ is a subgroup of order$|H||N|$ with$HN/N \cong H$.
\end{remark}
