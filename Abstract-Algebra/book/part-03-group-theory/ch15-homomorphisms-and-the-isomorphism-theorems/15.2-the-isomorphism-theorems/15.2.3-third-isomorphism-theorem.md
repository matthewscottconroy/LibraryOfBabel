# 15.2.3 Third Isomorphism Theorem: $(G/N)/(M/N) \cong G/M$

When we have a chain of normal subgroups $N \trianglelefteq M \trianglelefteq G$, we can form quotient groups in two ways: we can pass directly from$G$ to$G/M$, or we can first pass to$G/N$ and then further quotient by the image of$M$. The Third Isomorphism Theorem says these two paths yield the same result.

\begin{theorem}[Third Isomorphism Theorem]
Let $N \trianglelefteq G$ and$M \trianglelefteq G$ with$N \subseteq M$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M/N \trianglelefteq G/N$.
\item $(G/N)/(M/N) \cong G/M$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) For any $gN \in G/N$ and$mN \in M/N$:$(gN)(mN)(gN)^{-1} = (gmg^{-1})N$. Since$M \trianglelefteq G$,$gmg^{-1} \in M$, so$(gmg^{-1})N \in M/N$. Thus$M/N \trianglelefteq G/N$.

(2) Define $\psi: G/N \to G/M$ by$\psi(gN) = gM$. \textbf{Well-defined}: if$gN = g'N$ then$g'^{-1}g \in N \subseteq M$, so$gM = g'M$. \textbf{Homomorphism}:$\psi((gN)(g'N)) = \psi((gg')N) = (gg')M = (gM)(g'M) = \psi(gN)\psi(g'N)$. \textbf{Surjective}: every$gM \in G/M$ is$\psi(gN)$. \textbf{Kernel}:$\psi(gN) = M$ iff$gM = M$ iff$g \in M$ iff$gN \in M/N$. So$\ker\psi = M/N$. The First Isomorphism Theorem gives$(G/N)/(M/N) \cong G/M$.
\end{proof}

The intuition is best captured by analogy with fractions: just as $(a/b)/(c/b) = a/c$, canceling the common factor$N$ from both the numerator$G/N$ and the denominator$M/N$ yields$G/M$. This analogy becomes precise in the language of exact sequences: the Third Isomorphism Theorem expresses the compatibility of a chain of quotients.

**Example 1.** Take $G = \mathbb{Z}$,$M = m\mathbb{Z}$,$N = n\mathbb{Z}$ with$m \mid n$ (so$N \subseteq M$). Then$(G/N)/(M/N) = (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})/(m\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} = G/M$. Concretely:$(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})/(4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, where$4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} = \{\bar{0}, \bar{4}, \bar{8}\}$ is the subgroup of order 3 in$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 2.** In $S_4$, take$G = S_4$,$M = A_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$,$N = V = \{e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\} \trianglelefteq A_4$ (and$V \trianglelefteq S_4$ as well). The theorem:$(S_4/V)/(A_4/V) \cong S_4/A_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Here$|S_4/V| = 6$,$|A_4/V| = 3$, so$(S_4/V)/(A_4/V)$ has order 2, consistent.

**Example 3.** For $G = \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$,$M = \langle \bar{2} \rangle = \{0,2,4,6,8,10\}$ (order 6),$N = \langle \bar{4} \rangle = \{0,4,8\}$ (order 3, contained in$M$). The theorem:$(G/N)/(M/N) \cong G/M$. We have$G/N \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ (order 4),$M/N$ is a subgroup of order$6/3 = 2$ inside$G/N$, and$(G/N)/(M/N)$ has order$4/2 = 2$. And$G/M \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (order 2). Consistent.

**Example 4.** Let $G = D_6$,$M = \langle r \rangle$ (rotations, order 6, normal),$N = \langle r^2 \rangle$ (order 3, normal). The theorem:$(D_6/N)/(\langle r \rangle/N) \cong D_6/\langle r \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Here$D_6/N$ has order 4, and$\langle r \rangle/N$ is a subgroup of order 2, so the quotient has order 2.

**Example 5.** If $G$ is abelian and$N \subseteq M$ are both subgroups (automatically normal), the Third Isomorphism Theorem gives a short exact sequence$0 \to M/N \to G/N \to G/M \to 0$. This is the basis for the snake lemma and diagram chasing in homological algebra.

\begin{remark}
The Third Isomorphism Theorem will be used when studying composition series and the Jordan–Hölder theorem. A composition series $\{e\} = G_0 \trianglelefteq G_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq G_n = G$ gives rise to successive quotients$G_{i+1}/G_i$, called composition factors, and the Third Isomorphism Theorem governs the relationship between different refinements of such series.
\end{remark}
