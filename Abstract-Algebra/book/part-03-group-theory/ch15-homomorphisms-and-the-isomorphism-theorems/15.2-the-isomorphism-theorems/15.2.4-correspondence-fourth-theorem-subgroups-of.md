# 15.2.4 Correspondence (Fourth) Theorem: Subgroups of $G/N$

When we pass to a quotient $G/N$, we collapse a normal subgroup$N$ to a point and ask what the resulting group looks like. It is natural to wonder whether the subgroup structure of$G/N$ can be read off from the subgroup structure of$G$. The Correspondence Theorem gives a complete and satisfying answer.

\begin{theorem}[Correspondence Theorem / Fourth Isomorphism Theorem]
Let $N \trianglelefteq G$, and let$\pi: G \to G/N$ be the natural projection. There is a bijection
$$\Psi: \{H \leq G : N \subseteq H\} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \{\bar{H} \leq G/N\}$$
given by $\Psi(H) = H/N$ and$\Psi^{-1}(\bar{H}) = \pi^{-1}(\bar{H})$. This bijection satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $H_1 \subseteq H_2 \iff H_1/N \subseteq H_2/N$ (order-preserving).
\item $[G : H] = [G/N : H/N]$ (index is preserved).
\item $H \trianglelefteq G \iff H/N \trianglelefteq G/N$ (normality is preserved).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\Psi$ is well-defined: if$H \leq G$ contains$N$, then$H/N$ is a subgroup of$G/N$.$\Psi^{-1}$ is well-defined: if$\bar{H} \leq G/N$, then$\pi^{-1}(\bar{H})$ is a subgroup of$G$ containing$N$ (since$N = \pi^{-1}(\{N\}) \subseteq \pi^{-1}(\bar{H})$). One checks$\Psi$ and$\Psi^{-1}$ are mutual inverses. Properties (1)--(3) follow from direct computation.
\end{proof}

The bijection is not merely set-theoretic: it preserves the full subgroup lattice structure. The portion of $G$'s subgroup lattice lying above$N$ is faithfully replicated in the subgroup lattice of$G/N$.

**Example 1.** In $G = \mathbb{Z}$ with$N = 12\mathbb{Z}$, the subgroups containing$12\mathbb{Z}$ are exactly$d\mathbb{Z}$ for$d \mid 12$:$\mathbb{Z}$,$2\mathbb{Z}$,$3\mathbb{Z}$,$4\mathbb{Z}$,$6\mathbb{Z}$,$12\mathbb{Z}$. These correspond to the 6 subgroups of$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$:$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$,$\langle\bar{2}\rangle$,$\langle\bar{3}\rangle$,$\langle\bar{4}\rangle$,$\langle\bar{6}\rangle$,$\{\bar{0}\}$ — one for each divisor of 12. The lattice of containments is the same.

**Example 2.** In $G = S_4$ with$N = V = \{e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}$, the subgroups of$G/N \cong S_3$ correspond to subgroups of$S_4$ containing$V$:$V$ itself (trivial subgroup of$S_3$), the three Sylow 2-subgroups of order 8 (the three subgroups isomorphic to$D_4$ in$S_4$ that contain$V$, corresponding to order-2 subgroups of$S_3$),$A_4$ (order-3 subgroup of$S_3$, corresponding to$A_4$ in$S_4$), and$S_4$ ($S_3$ itself). The lattice structure matches.

**Example 3.** In $G = D_4$ with$N = \langle r^2 \rangle = \{e, r^2\}$, the quotient$G/N \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ has 5 subgroups (the trivial, the whole group, and three of order 2). The corresponding subgroups of$D_4$ containing$\langle r^2 \rangle$ are:$\langle r^2 \rangle$ itself,$D_4$, and the three subgroups$\langle r \rangle$,$\langle r^2, s \rangle$,$\langle r^2, rs \rangle$ of order 4.

**Example 4.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/30\mathbb{Z}$ and$N = \langle \bar{6} \rangle = \{0,6,12,18,24\}$ (order 5). The quotient$G/N \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ has 4 subgroups (of orders 1,2,3,6). The corresponding subgroups of$\mathbb{Z}/30\mathbb{Z}$ containing$\langle\bar{6}\rangle$ are:$\langle\bar{6}\rangle$ (order 5),$\langle\bar{2}\rangle$ (order 15),$\langle\bar{3}\rangle$ (order 10), and$\mathbb{Z}/30\mathbb{Z}$ (order 30).

**Example 5.** The Correspondence Theorem implies that $G/N$ is simple if and only if$N$ is a maximal normal subgroup of$G$ — that is, there is no normal subgroup$M$ with$N \subsetneq M \subsetneq G$. This is because the normal subgroups of$G/N$ correspond exactly to normal subgroups of$G$ containing$N$, and$G/N$ is simple iff the only such subgroups are$N$ and$G$ themselves.

\begin{remark}
The Correspondence Theorem is indispensable whenever one works with quotient groups: to understand the normal subgroups of $G/N$, one identifies them with normal subgroups of$G$ containing$N$. We will rely on this correspondence repeatedly in the proof of the Jordan--Hölder theorem, in the analysis of solvable and nilpotent groups, and in the Galois correspondence of field theory — where the lattice of subgroups of the Galois group corresponds (in reversed order) to the lattice of intermediate fields.
\end{remark}
