# 15.2 The Isomorphism Theorems

The isomorphism theorems are the central results of homomorphism theory, formalizing the relationship between subgroups, normal subgroups, quotient groups, and homomorphisms. Emmy Noether stated them in their modern abstract form in the 1920s, recognizing that they hold simultaneously for groups, rings, modules, and other algebraic structures. In group theory, there are three principal theorems, each describing a different way in which quotient groups appear as isomorphic images.

The First Isomorphism Theorem is the most used result in all of group theory: if $\phi: G \to H$ is a group homomorphism, then there is an isomorphism $\bar{\phi}: G/\ker(\phi) \to \text{im}(\phi)$ defined by $\bar{\phi}(g\ker(\phi)) = \phi(g)$. To apply it, one identifies a homomorphism $\phi$, determines its kernel and image, and concludes that the quotient by the kernel is isomorphic to the image. The sign homomorphism $\text{sgn}: S_n \to \{\pm 1\}$ yields $S_n/A_n \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$; the determinant homomorphism $\det: \text{GL}_n(F) \to F^*$ yields $\text{GL}_n(F)/\text{SL}_n(F) \cong F^*$. The theorem reduces questions about quotient groups to questions about images of homomorphisms, and vice versa.

The Second Isomorphism Theorem describes the interaction between a subgroup $H$ and a normal subgroup $N$. When $N \trianglelefteq G$ and $H \leq G$, the set $HN = \{hn : h \in H, n \in N\}$ is a subgroup of $G$, the intersection $H \cap N$ is normal in $H$, and there is an isomorphism $H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$. The subgroup lattice diagram showing $G$, $HN$, $H$, $N$, $H \cap N$ in a diamond shape gives the theorem its informal name. The Third Isomorphism Theorem handles the case of nested normal subgroups: if $N \trianglelefteq G$ and $N \leq K \leq G$ with $K \trianglelefteq G$, then $K/N \trianglelefteq G/N$ and $(G/N)/(K/N) \cong G/K$. The Correspondence Theorem (sometimes called the Fourth Isomorphism Theorem) completes the picture: there is an order-preserving bijection between subgroups of $G/N$ and subgroups of $G$ containing $N$, with normal subgroups corresponding to normal subgroups. Together, these four results give a complete description of the subgroup structure of any quotient group.
