# 15.3.1 External and Internal Direct Products

Having studied how to decompose groups via quotients, we now turn to how groups can be *built* from simpler pieces. The direct product is the most basic construction, allowing us to assemble two (or more) groups into a single larger one in the most straightforward way possible.

\begin{definition}
The \textbf{external direct product} $G \times H$ of two groups$G$ and$H$ is the Cartesian product$G \times H$ equipped with componentwise multiplication:
$$(g_1, h_1)(g_2, h_2) = (g_1 g_2, h_1 h_2).$$
The identity is $(e_G, e_H)$ and inverses are$(g,h)^{-1} = (g^{-1}, h^{-1})$.
\end{definition}

When $G$ and$H$ are finite,$|G \times H| = |G| \cdot |H|$. The construction is symmetric up to isomorphism:$G \times H \cong H \times G$ via$(g,h) \mapsto (h,g)$. It is also associative up to isomorphism, so we can speak unambiguously of products$G_1 \times \cdots \times G_n$.

The **internal direct product** arises when we look at a group $G$ already in hand and ask whether it decomposes into a product of its own subgroups. We say$G$ is the **internal direct product** of subgroups$H$ and$K$ if$H \trianglelefteq G$,$K \trianglelefteq G$,$H \cap K = \{e\}$, and$HK = G$. The first three conditions together imply that elements of$H$ and$K$ commute: the commutator$hkh^{-1}k^{-1}$ lies in both$H$ and$K$ (by normality), hence equals$e$. This gives$hk = kh$ for all$h \in H$,$k \in K$.

\begin{proposition}
If $G = HK$ with$H \trianglelefteq G$,$K \trianglelefteq G$,$H \cap K = \{e\}$, then the map$(h, k) \mapsto hk$ is a group isomorphism$H \times K \to G$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Well-defined homomorphism}: $(h_1,k_1)(h_2,k_2) = (h_1h_2, k_1k_2) \mapsto h_1h_2k_1k_2$. We need$h_1k_1h_2k_2 = h_1h_2k_1k_2$, i.e.,$k_1h_2 = h_2k_1$. As argued above, elements of$K$ and$H$ commute, so this holds. \textbf{Surjective}:$HK = G$ by assumption. \textbf{Injective}: if$hk = e$ then$h = k^{-1} \in H \cap K = \{e\}$, so$h = k = e$.
\end{proof}

**Example 1.** $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. The subgroups$H = \langle \bar{3} \rangle = \{\bar{0},\bar{3}\}$ (order 2) and$K = \langle \bar{2} \rangle = \{\bar{0},\bar{2},\bar{4}\}$ (order 3) satisfy$H \cap K = \{\bar{0}\}$ and$H + K = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. The isomorphism is$\bar{k} \mapsto (\bar{k} \bmod 2, \bar{k} \bmod 3)$.

**Example 2.** $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is not isomorphic to$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Though$|\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}| = 4$, it cannot be written as an internal direct product because it has a unique subgroup of order 2 ($\langle\bar{2}\rangle$), and$\langle\bar{2}\rangle \cap \langle\bar{2}\rangle \neq \{0\}$.

**Example 3.** In $S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, the two factors are$S_3 \times \{0\}$ and$\{e\} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and the group has order 12. This is isomorphic to$D_6$ (the dihedral group of the hexagon), since both are the unique non-abelian group of order 12 with a cyclic subgroup of index 2... though this requires more work to confirm.

**Example 4.** $Q_8$ cannot be written as a direct product of two nontrivial subgroups. If$Q_8 = H \times K$ with$|H|, |K| > 1$, then since$|Q_8| = 8$ we'd need$|H| = |K| = 4$ with$H \cap K = \{1\}$. But all subgroups of order 4 in$Q_8$ contain$-1$, so$H \cap K \supseteq \{1,-1\} \neq \{1\}$. Hence no internal direct product decomposition exists.

**Example 5.** $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/mn\mathbb{Z}$ if and only if$\gcd(m,n) = 1$. When$\gcd(m,n)=1$, the element$(\bar{1},\bar{1})$ generates the product (by the Chinese Remainder Theorem). When$\gcd(m,n)>1$, the maximum order of any element is$\mathrm{lcm}(m,n) < mn$, so the product has no element of order$mn$ and is not cyclic.

\begin{remark}
The internal and external perspectives are simply two ways of describing the same situation. We freely identify $G \cong H \times K$ when the internal decomposition conditions hold. The distinction matters mainly when$G$ is presented abstractly: in practice, internal direct products are detected by verifying the three conditions (two normal subgroups with trivial intersection spanning$G$), while external direct products are used to construct new groups from scratch.
\end{remark}
