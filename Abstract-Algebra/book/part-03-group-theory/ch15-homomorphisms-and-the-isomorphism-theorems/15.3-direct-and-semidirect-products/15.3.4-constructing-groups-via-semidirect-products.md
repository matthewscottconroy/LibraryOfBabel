# 15.3.4 Constructing Groups via Semidirect Products

The semidirect product is the primary tool for constructing nonabelian groups from abelian building blocks. Its power lies in the flexibility of the twisting homomorphism $\phi$: by varying$\phi: H \to \mathrm{Aut}(N)$, one can produce a family of distinct groups all having the same "components"$N$ and$H$.

The dihedral group $D_n$ is the canonical example:$D_n \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, where the generator of$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ acts on$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ by inversion$k \mapsto -k$. This single automorphism — negation — is what distinguishes$D_n$ from the cyclic group$\mathbb{Z}/2n\mathbb{Z}$. The direct product$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ would arise from the trivial$\phi$, but that gives a group in which the reflection commutes with every rotation, which is not what happens geometrically.

The construction generalizes far beyond dihedral groups. The method for classifying groups of order $pq$ (for primes$p < q$) illustrates the full power of the semidirect product approach:

- If $p \nmid (q-1)$: the only group of order$pq$ is$\mathbb{Z}/pq\mathbb{Z}$ (cyclic), arising from the trivial$\phi$.
- If $p \mid (q-1)$: there is exactly one nonabelian group of order$pq$, namely$\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z} \rtimes_\phi \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, where$\phi$ sends the generator of$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ to an automorphism of$\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ of order$p$ (which exists since$p \mid |\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})| = q-1$).

**Example 1.** Groups of order 6. Here $p=2$,$q=3$, and$2 \mid (3-1) = 2$. So there are two groups:$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (cyclic) and$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (nonabelian). The latter is$S_3 \cong D_3$, with the generator of$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ acting on$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ by inversion.

**Example 2.** Groups of order 21. $p=3$,$q=7$, and$3 \mid (7-1) = 6$. The automorphism group$\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. The element of order 3 in$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ is multiplication by$2$ (since$2^3 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$). The nontrivial group is$\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ with multiplication$(a_1,b_1)(a_2,b_2) = (a_1 + 2^{b_1}a_2, b_1+b_2)$. This is the unique nonabelian group of order 21.

**Example 3.** Groups of order 10. $p=2$,$q=5$,$2 \mid 4$. There is$\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ and$D_5 = \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (dihedral group of order 10). The nontrivial automorphism of$\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$ of order 2 is inversion$k \mapsto -k \equiv 4k \pmod 5$.

**Example 4.** Consider $N = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ and$H = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ for a prime$p$. Then$\mathrm{Aut}(N) \cong GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ (invertible$2\times 2$ matrices over$\mathbb{F}_p$). Any element of order$p$ in$GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ gives a nontrivial semidirect product. For$p=2$:$N = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$H = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$GL_2(\mathbb{F}_2) \cong S_3$. Elements of order 2 in$S_3$ give semidirect products of order 8: the two nonabelian groups of order 8 are$D_4$ and$Q_8$, and$D_4 \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ for an appropriate$\phi$.

**Example 5.** The Frobenius group $F_{20} = \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ arises from the order-4 automorphism$k \mapsto 2k$ of$\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$ (since$2^4 = 16 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ and$2^2 = 4 \not\equiv 1$, the order is 4). This group of order 20 is the Frobenius group$F_{20}$, which appears as the Galois group of the splitting field of a generic degree-5 polynomial over certain fields.

Notice that the same pair $(N, H)$ with different choices of$\phi$ can produce non-isomorphic groups. For instance,$\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ admits a nontrivial$\phi$ (since$3 \mid 6$) and the trivial$\phi$, yielding a nonabelian group of order 21 in the first case and$\mathbb{Z}/21\mathbb{Z}$ in the second. This construction reappears in the analysis of Sylow structure, in the Levi decomposition of Lie algebras, and whenever one needs to assemble a group whose normal structure is understood but whose commutativity is not.

\begin{remark}
The classification of groups of order $p^3$ requires semidirect products with$N = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ and$H = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, as well as more subtle constructions. For$p=2$ this gives the classification of groups of order 8:$\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$D_4$, and$Q_8$ — five groups in total. Semidirect products account for$D_4$ but not$Q_8$, which requires a different construction and is the first example of a group that cannot be written as a semidirect product of two proper subgroups.
\end{remark}
