# 15.3 Direct and Semidirect Products

The isomorphism theorems tell us how to extract a quotient from a group; direct and semidirect products go in the other direction, assembling larger groups from smaller ones. The external direct product $G \times H$ of two groups is the Cartesian product with componentwise multiplication: $(g_1, h_1)(g_2, h_2) = (g_1 g_2, h_1 h_2)$. It is a group of order $|G| \cdot |H|$, and it contains copies of $G$ and $H$ as normal subgroups via the embeddings $g \mapsto (g, e_H)$ and $h \mapsto (e_G, h)$. The recognition theorem for internal direct products asks when an abstract group $G$ is (up to isomorphism) a direct product of two of its subgroups: $G \cong H \times K$ if and only if $H, K \trianglelefteq G$, $H \cap K = \{e\}$, and $HK = G$. The last condition can be replaced by $|HK| = |G|$ in the finite case.

Direct products are abelian when both factors are abelian, but many non-abelian groups arise from a more flexible construction: the semidirect product. If $H$ acts on $N$ by automorphisms via a homomorphism $\phi: H \to \text{Aut}(N)$, the semidirect product $N \rtimes_\phi H$ is the set $N \times H$ with the twisted multiplication $(n_1, h_1)(n_2, h_2) = (n_1 \phi(h_1)(n_2),\, h_1 h_2)$. When $\phi$ is trivial (every $h$ acts as the identity), this reduces to the direct product. The defining property of the semidirect product is that $N \trianglelefteq N \rtimes_\phi H$, $H \leq N \rtimes_\phi H$, $N \cap H = \{e\}$, and $NH = G$ — the same conditions as for the direct product, except that $H$ need not be normal.

The dihedral group $D_n$ is the canonical example: $D_n \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, where the non-trivial element of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ acts on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ by inversion $k \mapsto -k$. The group $S_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ with the same action. In Chapter 17, the Sylow theorems will frequently force a group to be a semidirect product by producing a normal Sylow subgroup and a complementary subgroup with trivial intersection. Different choices of the action $\phi$ can yield non-isomorphic groups even with the same factors $N$ and $H$, so classifying semidirect products is equivalent to classifying the orbits of the relevant action of $H$ on $\text{Hom}(H, \text{Aut}(N))$. This is the key step in classifying groups of small order.
