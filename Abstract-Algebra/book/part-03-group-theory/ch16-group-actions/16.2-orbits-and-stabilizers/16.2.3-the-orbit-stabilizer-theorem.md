# 16.2.3 The Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem: $|G| = |G \cdot x| \cdot |G_x|$

The Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem is the quantitative heart of the theory of group actions. It connects the size of an orbit, the size of a stabilizer, and the order of the group in a single elegant formula.

\begin{theorem}[Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem]
Let $G$ be a group acting on a set$X$, and let$x \in X$. Then there is a bijection
$$G/G_x \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} G \cdot x, \quad gG_x \mapsto g \cdot x.$$
In particular, if $G$ is finite,$|G \cdot x| = [G : G_x] = |G|/|G_x|$, so
$$|G| = |G \cdot x| \cdot |G_x|.$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define $\Phi: G/G_x \to G \cdot x$ by$\Phi(gG_x) = g \cdot x$. \textbf{Well-defined}: if$gG_x = g'G_x$ then$g'^{-1}g \in G_x$, so$(g'^{-1}g) \cdot x = x$, giving$g \cdot x = g' \cdot x$. \textbf{Surjective}: every element of$G \cdot x$ is$g \cdot x = \Phi(gG_x)$ for some$g$. \textbf{Injective}: if$\Phi(gG_x) = \Phi(g'G_x)$ then$g \cdot x = g' \cdot x$, so$g'^{-1}g \cdot x = x$, meaning$g'^{-1}g \in G_x$, i.e.,$gG_x = g'G_x$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a generalization of Lagrange's theorem: the orbit size $|G \cdot x| = [G:G_x]$ divides$|G|$. In the special case of the conjugation action, the orbit of$x$ is its conjugacy class and the stabilizer is the centralizer$C_G(x)$, giving$|\mathrm{cl}(x)| = [G : C_G(x)]$. This is the key ingredient in the class equation.

**Example 1.** $S_4$ acting on$\{1,2,3,4\}$. Orbit of 1: all of$\{1,2,3,4\}$, size 4. Stabilizer of 1:$S_{\{2,3,4\}} \cong S_3$, order 6. Check:$|S_4| = 24 = 4 \cdot 6$.

**Example 2.** $D_n$ acting on vertices of a regular$n$-gon. Orbit of any vertex: all$n$ vertices. Stabilizer of a vertex: the reflection across the axis through that vertex, so$|G_v| = 2$. Check:$|D_n| = 2n = n \cdot 2$.

**Example 3.** $SO(3)$ (rotation group of the sphere) acting on the set of faces of a regular dodecahedron (12 faces). The orbit of any face is all 12 faces (transitivity). The stabilizer of a face consists of rotations preserving that face — the rotations by$0°, 72°, 144°, 216°, 288°$ (5-fold rotational symmetry of the pentagonal face). So$|G_f| = 5$, and$|SO(3)\text{ restricted}| = 12 \cdot 5 = 60 = |A_5|$, the rotation symmetry group of the dodecahedron.

**Example 4.** $GL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$ acting on the set of nonzero vectors in$\mathbb{F}_q^n$. The action is transitive (for any$\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w} \neq 0$, extend each to a basis and change basis). Number of nonzero vectors:$q^n - 1$. Stabilizer of$\mathbf{e}_1$: matrices with first column$\mathbf{e}_1$ and invertible remaining block. Its order is$q^{n-1} \cdot |GL_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_q)|$. By orbit-stabilizer:$|GL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)| = (q^n-1) \cdot q^{n-1} \cdot |GL_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_q)|$. This gives a recursion:$|GL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)| = (q^n-1)(q^n-q)(q^n-q^2)\cdots(q^n-q^{n-1})$.

**Example 5.** Counting colorings via orbit-stabilizer. Let $G = D_4$ act on the colorings of the 4 edges of a square by 2 colors. The set$X$ has$2^4 = 16$ elements. The orbit-stabilizer theorem helps compute orbits: for each element$g \in D_4$, the number of fixed colorings$|X^g|$ can be counted, and Burnside's lemma (Section 16.3.3) then gives the number of distinct colorings up to symmetry.

\begin{remark}
The Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem is one of the most broadly applicable formulas in combinatorial group theory. It can be used to count the symmetries of geometric objects, to determine the sizes of conjugacy classes, and to prove divisibility conditions on group orders. The formula $|G| = |G \cdot x| \cdot |G_x|$ encodes the fundamental trade-off: the more of$G$ that fixes$x$ (large stabilizer), the fewer places$x$ can go (small orbit), and vice versa.
\end{remark}
