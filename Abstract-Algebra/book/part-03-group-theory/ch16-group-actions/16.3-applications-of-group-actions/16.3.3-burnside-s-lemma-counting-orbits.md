# 16.3.3 Burnside's Lemma: Counting Orbits

A natural question about any group action is: how many orbits are there? Burnside's lemma provides a surprisingly clean answer in terms of fixed-point counting, transforming what might seem like a global problem into a sum of local data.

\begin{theorem}[Burnside's Lemma]
Let $G$ be a finite group acting on a finite set$X$. For each$g \in G$, let$X^g = \{x \in X : g \cdot x = x\}$ denote the set of points fixed by$g$. Then the number of orbits is
$$|\text{orbits}| = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} |X^g|.$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Count the set of pairs $S = \{(g, x) \in G \times X : g \cdot x = x\}$ in two ways. Summing over$g$:$|S| = \sum_{g \in G} |X^g|$. Summing over$x$:$|S| = \sum_{x \in X} |G_x|$. By the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem,$|G_x| = |G|/|G \cdot x|$. Group$X$ by orbits$O_1, \ldots, O_k$:$\sum_{x \in X} |G_x| = \sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{x \in O_j} \frac{|G|}{|O_j|} = \sum_{j=1}^k |O_j| \cdot \frac{|G|}{|O_j|} = k \cdot |G|$. Equating the two expressions and dividing by$|G|$ gives$k = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} |X^g|$.
\end{proof}

**Example 1.** Count distinct necklaces with $n = 4$ beads and$k = 2$ colors (red/blue), where two necklaces are the same if one is a rotation of the other. Group:$G = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ acting on the$2^4 = 16$ colorings by cyclic rotation. Fixed points:
- $\bar{0}$ (identity): all 16 colorings fixed.
- $\bar{1}$ (rotate 90°): fixed iff all beads same color — 2 fixed colorings.
- $\bar{2}$ (rotate 180°): fixed iff bead 1 = bead 3 and bead 2 = bead 4 —$2^2 = 4$ fixed colorings.
- $\bar{3}$ (rotate 270°): same as$\bar{1}$ by symmetry — 2 fixed.

Burnside: orbits $= (16 + 2 + 4 + 2)/4 = 24/4 = 6$. There are 6 distinct necklaces.

**Example 2.** Count distinct necklaces with $n=3$ beads, 3 colors, including reflections. Group:$G = D_3$ (order 6) on$3^3 = 27$ colorings.
- $e$: 27 fixed.
- $(123)$,$(132)$ (rotations by$120°$,$240°$): fixed iff all 3 beads same color —$3$ each.
- $(12), (13), (23)$ (reflections): each fixes colorings where beads 1 and 2 are same (or 1,3 same, etc.) —$3^2 = 9$ each.

Burnside: orbits $= (27 + 3 + 3 + 9 + 9 + 9)/6 = 60/6 = 10$.

**Example 3.** Coloring the faces of a cube with 3 colors, up to rotation. Group: $G =$ rotation group of the cube (order 24) acting on$3^6 = 729$ face colorings. By type of rotation:
- 1 identity: 729 fixed.
- 6 face rotations by $\pm 90°$:$3^3 = 27$ fixed each.
- 3 face rotations by $180°$:$3^4 = 81$ fixed each.
- 8 vertex rotations by $\pm 120°$:$3^2 = 9$ fixed each.
- 6 edge rotations by $180°$:$3^3 = 27$ fixed each.

Burnside: $\frac{1}{24}(729 + 6 \cdot 27 + 3 \cdot 81 + 8 \cdot 9 + 6 \cdot 27) = \frac{1}{24}(729 + 162 + 243 + 72 + 162) = \frac{1368}{24} = 57$.

**Example 4.** $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ acting on$k$-colorings of the vertices of a regular$n$-gon. The rotation by$j$ positions fixes a coloring iff the coloring has period$\gcd(j,n)$, i.e., there are$k^{\gcd(j,n)}$ such colorings. Burnside: number of distinct colorings$= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} k^{\gcd(j,n)}$.

**Example 5.** $G = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ acting on$2$-colorings of a hexagon's vertices.$|X^{\bar{j}}| = 2^{\gcd(j,6)}$:$j=0$:$2^6=64$;$j=1$:$2^1=2$;$j=2$:$2^2=4$;$j=3$:$2^3=8$;$j=4$:$2^2=4$;$j=5$:$2^1=2$. Burnside:$(64+2+4+8+4+2)/6 = 84/6 = 14$.

\begin{remark}
The key insight of Burnside's lemma is that fixed-point counting is almost always more tractable than orbit counting directly. For a rotation group, the number of fixed colorings under a given rotation has a simple formula (it depends only on the cycle structure of the rotation), while the direct enumeration of orbits would require listing all symmetry classes explicitly. Burnside's lemma is the standard tool in combinatorial enumeration problems with symmetry, and it generalizes to Pólya enumeration, which gives a generating function for the orbit count weighted by the colors used.
\end{remark}
