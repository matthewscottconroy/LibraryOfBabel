# 16.3.4 The Action of $G$ on Left Cosets of$H$; Kernel of This Action

The action of a group $G$ on the left cosets of a subgroup$H$ is one of the most powerful tools for deducing information about the internal structure of$G$. It produces, almost for free, a homomorphism from$G$ into a symmetric group, and the kernel of this homomorphism is a normal subgroup that can be computed explicitly.

Recall the action: $G$ acts on$G/H = \{aH : a \in G\}$ by left multiplication,$g \cdot (aH) = (ga)H$. This is a well-defined action that is automatically transitive, since any coset$aH$ can be reached from$H$ by the element$a$. The associated homomorphism$\rho: G \to S_{[G:H]}$ sends each$g$ to the permutation of$G/H$ induced by left multiplication by$g$.

\begin{theorem}
The kernel of $\rho: G \to S_{[G:H]}$ is$\mathrm{Core}_G(H) = \bigcap_{g \in G} gHg^{-1}$, the largest normal subgroup of$G$ contained in$H$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$g \in \ker\rho$ iff$g$ fixes every coset:$g \cdot (aH) = aH$ for all$a \in G$, i.e.,$gaH = aH$, i.e.,$a^{-1}ga \in H$ for all$a$. This is equivalent to$g \in aHa^{-1}$ for all$a$, i.e.,$g \in \bigcap_{a \in G} aHa^{-1}$. For normality of the core: any normal subgroup$N \trianglelefteq G$ with$N \leq H$ satisfies$N = gNg^{-1} \leq gHg^{-1}$ for all$g$, so$N \leq \bigcap_g gHg^{-1} = \mathrm{Core}_G(H)$.
\end{proof}

By the First Isomorphism Theorem, $G/\mathrm{Core}_G(H)$ embeds into$S_{[G:H]}$. This gives a concrete upper bound: the order of$G/\mathrm{Core}_G(H)$ divides$[G:H]!$.

\begin{corollary}
If $[G:H] = n$, then$|G/\mathrm{Core}_G(H)|$ divides$n!$. In particular, if$|G|$ does not divide$n!$, then$H$ must contain a nontrivial normal subgroup of$G$.
\end{corollary}

**Example 1.** Let $G = S_3$ and$H = \langle (12) \rangle$ (order 2, index 3). The core$\mathrm{Core}_{S_3}(H) = \bigcap_{g} g\langle(12)\rangle g^{-1}$. Conjugates of$(12)$:$(12), (13), (23)$, giving conjugates of$H$:$\langle(12)\rangle, \langle(13)\rangle, \langle(23)\rangle$. Their intersection is$\{e\}$. So the action$S_3 \to S_3$ (on 3 cosets) has trivial kernel, and$S_3$ embeds in$S_3$ — a tautology, but it shows the action is faithful.

**Example 2.** Let $G = A_4$ (order 12) and$H$ any subgroup of order 3 (index 4). The core: by the corollary,$|A_4/\mathrm{Core}|$ divides$4! = 24$. Also,$|A_4|/|\mathrm{Core}|$ divides 24, so$|\mathrm{Core}|$ must be at least$12/24 = 1/2$, hence$|\mathrm{Core}| \geq 1$. Since$A_4$ has no normal subgroup of order 3 (the Sylow 3-subgroups are not normal in$A_4$), the core must be trivial. So the action$A_4 \to S_4$ (on 4 cosets) is faithful, giving an embedding$A_4 \hookrightarrow S_4$. This is essentially the natural inclusion.

**Example 3.** Let $G = A_4$ and$H = V = \{e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}$ (normal, index 3). The core$\mathrm{Core}_{A_4}(V) \supseteq V$ since$V \trianglelefteq A_4$. Since$V$ is itself a normal subgroup of$A_4$ contained in$V$, the core equals$V$. The action$A_4 \to S_3$ (on 3 cosets of$V$) has kernel$V$, giving$A_4/V \hookrightarrow S_3$. Since$|A_4/V| = 3$ and$|S_3| = 6$, this embeds$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ into$S_3$.

**Example 4.** Suppose $G$ has a subgroup$H$ of index 4 and$|G| > 24$. Then$|G/\mathrm{Core}|$ divides$4! = 24 < |G|$, so the core$\mathrm{Core}_G(H)$ is nontrivial. This shows$G$ has a proper nontrivial normal subgroup and hence is not simple. For instance,$G = A_5$ has order 60; if it had a subgroup of index 4 (order 15), then the core would divide$4! = 24$ but also divide 60, so$|\mathrm{Core}|$ would divide$\gcd(24, 60) = 12$... actually the argument is that$60 \nmid 4! = 24$, so any subgroup of index 4 in$A_5$ has nontrivial core. But$A_5$ is simple, so$A_5$ has no subgroup of index 4.

**Example 5.** Groups of order $pq$ with$p < q$. Suppose$G$ has a Sylow$q$-subgroup$Q$ of order$q$. If$Q$ is not normal, then$[G:N_G(Q)]$ is the number of Sylow$q$-subgroups. The action of$G$ on$G/N_G(Q)$ gives a homomorphism$G \to S_m$ where$m = [G:N_G(Q)]$. If$m < p < q$, then$|G| = pq$ does not divide$m!$ (since$q > m$ means$q \nmid m!$), so the kernel is nontrivial —$Q$ itself is normal.

\begin{remark}
The corollary — that $|G|$ not dividing$n!$ forces$H$ to contain a nontrivial normal subgroup — is a powerful simplicity obstruction. It is used to prove simplicity of$A_5$ by showing that$A_5$ has no subgroup of index less than 5 (other than$\{e\}$ and$A_5$), and to show that groups of specific orders cannot be simple. More generally, this approach of embedding$G$ into$S_n$ via the coset action and studying the kernel is one of the fundamental methods of finite group theory.
\end{remark}
