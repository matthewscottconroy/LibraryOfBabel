# 18.1.2 Composition Series; Composition Factors

The prime factorization of an integer $n$ breaks$n$ into atoms — the primes — in a way that is both complete (every integer factors) and canonical (the factorization is unique up to order). Group theory has an exact analogue: the decomposition of a finite group into a chain of subgroups, each as simple as possible. The formalism that makes this precise is the notion of a composition series.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{composition series} for a group $G$ is a chain of subgroups
$$G = G_0 \supset G_1 \supset G_2 \supset \cdots \supset G_k = \{e\}$$
such that each $G_{i+1}$ is normal in$G_i$ (not necessarily in$G$ itself) and each quotient$G_i / G_{i+1}$ is a nontrivial simple group. The quotients$G_i / G_{i+1}$ are the \emph{composition factors}, and$k$ is the \emph{length} of the series.
\end{definition}

Several clarifications are in order. First, $G_{i+1}$ need only be normal in$G_i$, not in the full group$G$. This weaker condition (called *subnormality*) is exactly what is needed to form the quotient$G_i / G_{i+1}$, and it allows the chain to "zigzag" through the subgroup lattice. Second, the requirement that$G_i / G_{i+1}$ be simple means there is no room to refine the chain further at that step — inserting any subgroup between$G_{i+1}$ and$G_i$ would produce a non-simple intermediate quotient. A composition series is, in this sense, a *maximally refined* subnormal series.

Every finite group has a composition series. The proof is constructive: if $G$ is simple, the series$G \supset \{e\}$ is a composition series of length$1$. Otherwise,$G$ has a maximal normal subgroup$G_1$ (which exists by finiteness), and$G/G_1$ is simple by maximality of$G_1$. Applying the same construction to$G_1$ produces$G_2, G_3, \ldots$, and since the groups are strictly decreasing in order, the chain must terminate at$\{e\}$ in finitely many steps.

The composition factors are the "prime factors" of the group. The analogy with integer factorization is precise: just as $12 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$ decomposes into primes (with repetition), a group of order$12$ will have composition factors that are simple groups whose orders multiply to$12$. For$G = \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, one composition series is$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \supset \langle \bar{4} \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \supset \{0\}$ wait — let us be more careful: the series$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \supset \langle \bar{2} \rangle \supset \langle \bar{4} \rangle \supset \langle \bar{6} \rangle \supset \{0\}$, but these quotients need checking.

Let us work this out cleanly for $S_3$. The composition series$S_3 \supset A_3 \supset \{e\}$ has factors$S_3/A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and$A_3/\{e\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, both simple. This series has length$2$ and composition factors$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. The Jordan–Hölder theorem (next subsection) guarantees these are the only possible composition factors for$S_3$, in any order.

It is important to note that a group can have multiple composition series. For $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, there are three composition series: inserting any of the three subgroups of order$2$ as the middle term$G_1$. For instance,$G \supset \{(0, 1), (0, 0)\} \supset \{(0, 0)\}$ gives factors$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. All three series have the same length and the same factors (both$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$), consistent with Jordan–Hölder.

For a group like $A_4$: one composition series is$A_4 \supset V_4 \supset \langle (12)(34) \rangle \supset \{e\}$, where$V_4 = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$. The factors are$A_4/V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$,$V_4/\langle (12)(34) \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and$\langle (12)(34) \rangle / \{e\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The composition factors of$A_4$ are$\{\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\}$.

**Example 1.** $G = \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$. A composition series:$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \supset 2\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \supset 4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \supset 0$. The factors:$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} / 2\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$2\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} / 4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} / 0 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. So the composition factors are$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ — matching the prime factorization$12 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$.

**Example 2.** $G = S_4$. A composition series:$S_4 \supset A_4 \supset V_4 \supset \langle (12)(34) \rangle \supset \{e\}$. Factors:$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Length$4$, factors matching$24 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 2$.

**Example 3.** $G = A_5$. Since$A_5$ is simple, the only composition series is$A_5 \supset \{e\}$, with the single composition factor$A_5$ itself. Length$1$. This is the sense in which simple groups are "atoms": their composition series cannot be refined.

**Example 4.** $G = \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ for a prime$p$. The composition series is$\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z} \supset p\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z} \supset p^2\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z} \supset \cdots \supset p^{n-1}\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z} \supset 0$. Each factor is$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. The composition factors are$n$ copies of$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, consistent with$|G| = p^n$.

**Example 5.** Composition series need not be unique even at the level of the chain, only at the level of factors. For $G = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$, there are two composition series:$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \supset 2\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \supset 0$ (factors$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$) and$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \supset 3\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \supset 0$ (factors$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$). The factors are the same multiset$\{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\}$, just appearing in different orders. Jordan–Hölder guarantees this match.
