# 18.1 Simple Groups and Composition Series

A group $G$ is simple if it has no proper non-trivial normal subgroups — the only normal subgroups are $\{e\}$ and $G$ itself. Simple groups are the atoms of group theory: they cannot be "factored" into smaller pieces by taking quotients. The abelian simple groups are exactly the cyclic groups $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ of prime order (any proper non-trivial subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ would have order dividing $p$, forcing it to be trivial). The first non-abelian simple group is the alternating group $A_5$ of order 60, and proving $A_5$ is simple — by showing any non-trivial normal subgroup must have order equal to the sizes of one or more conjugacy classes, none of which add up to a proper divisor of 60 other than the class $\{e\}$ — is a beautiful exercise. The classification of all finite simple groups, completed in the 1980s after a century of work, reveals that every finite simple group is either cyclic of prime order, an alternating group $A_n$ for $n \geq 5$, one of 16 infinite families of groups of Lie type, or one of 26 sporadic groups.

A composition series for $G$ is a chain $G = G_0 \supset G_1 \supset G_2 \supset \cdots \supset G_r = \{e\}$ where each $G_{i+1} \trianglelefteq G_i$ and each quotient $G_i/G_{i+1}$ is simple. The quotients $G_i/G_{i+1}$ are the composition factors. Not every group has a finite composition series: infinite groups typically do not. Finite groups always do: start with $G_0 = G$; if $G_0$ is not simple, find a maximal proper normal subgroup $G_1 \trianglelefteq G_0$ (so $G_0/G_1$ is simple); continue. The process terminates because $|G_i|$ strictly decreases.

The Jordan–Hölder theorem guarantees the uniqueness of composition factors: any two composition series of a finite group $G$ have the same length $r$ and the same multiset of composition factors up to isomorphism. The proof is by induction, using the Zassenhaus butterfly lemma to compare adjacent steps of two series. This theorem is the precise group-theoretic analogue of the fundamental theorem of arithmetic — the composition factors are the "prime factors" of the group. Two groups with the same composition factors need not be isomorphic (the extension problem is hard), but the factors themselves are an invariant of the group that constrains its structure substantially.
