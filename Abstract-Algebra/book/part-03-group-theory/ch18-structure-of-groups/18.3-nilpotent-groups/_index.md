# 18.3 Nilpotent Groups

Nilpotent groups are a strictly smaller class than solvable groups, satisfying a more controlled version of the "built from abelian pieces" condition. A group $G$ is nilpotent if its lower central series $G_0 = G$, $G_1 = [G, G_0]$, $G_2 = [G, G_1]$, $\ldots$, defined by $G_{i+1} = [G, G_i]$ (the subgroup generated by all $[g, h]$ with $g \in G$ and $h \in G_i$), eventually reaches the trivial group. Equivalently, $G$ is nilpotent if and only if the upper central series $Z_0 = \{e\}$, $Z_1 = Z(G)$, $Z_2/Z_1 = Z(G/Z_1)$, $\ldots$ eventually reaches all of $G$. The smallest $c$ for which $G_c = \{e\}$ (equivalently $Z_c = G$) is the nilpotency class of $G$.

Every abelian group is nilpotent of class at most 1 (class exactly 1 if non-trivial), and every nilpotent group is solvable. The simplest non-abelian nilpotent groups have class 2: this means $[G,G] \subseteq Z(G)$, so all commutators are central. The Heisenberg group of upper triangular $3 \times 3$ matrices with 1s on the diagonal and entries in $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is the prototype; it has order $p^3$ and nilpotency class 2. More generally, every $p$-group is nilpotent. This is proved by induction: every non-trivial $p$-group has a non-trivial center, the quotient $G/Z(G)$ is a $p$-group of smaller order, and by induction $G/Z(G)$ is nilpotent, which implies $G$ is nilpotent.

The most elegant characterization of finite nilpotent groups is in terms of their Sylow subgroups. A finite group is nilpotent if and only if all of its Sylow subgroups are normal, which in turn is equivalent to saying $G$ is isomorphic to the direct product of its Sylow subgroups. This result places $p$-groups at the absolute center of finite nilpotency theory and gives a complete structural decomposition: a finite nilpotent group is simply a direct product of $p$-groups, one for each prime dividing the group order. The hierarchy $\text{abelian} \subsetneq \text{nilpotent} \subsetneq \text{solvable}$ is strict, and understanding where a given group falls in this hierarchy is one of the basic tasks of finite group theory.
