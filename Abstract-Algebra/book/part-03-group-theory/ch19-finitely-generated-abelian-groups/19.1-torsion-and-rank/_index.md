# 19.1 Torsion and Rank

To classify finitely generated abelian groups, we must first understand the two fundamentally different kinds of structure they can contain. An element $a$ of an abelian group $A$ is a torsion element if $na = 0$ for some positive integer $n$ (using additive notation throughout, since all groups in this chapter are abelian). The smallest such $n$ is the order of $a$. The set of all torsion elements $T(A) = \{a \in A : na = 0 \text{ for some } n \geq 1\}$ is a subgroup — the torsion subgroup — because if $ma = 0$ and $nb = 0$ then $mn(a+b) = 0$. An abelian group is a torsion group if $T(A) = A$ (every element has finite order), and torsion-free if $T(A) = \{0\}$ (no non-zero element has finite order).

A free abelian group is one isomorphic to a direct sum $\mathbb{Z}^r = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}$ ($r$ copies) for some cardinal $r$. Free abelian groups are torsion-free, and the rank $r$ is the size of any maximal linearly independent subset (in the sense that no non-trivial integer linear combination is zero). The rank is a complete invariant among free abelian groups: two free abelian groups are isomorphic if and only if they have the same rank. The integers $\mathbb{Z}$ are free of rank 1; $\mathbb{Z}^2$ is free of rank 2; the direct sum of $r$ copies of $\mathbb{Z}$ is free of rank $r$.

For a finitely generated abelian group $A$, the structure theorem will assert that $A \cong T(A) \oplus \mathbb{Z}^r$, where $r$ is the rank of $A$ (the rank of the free part $A/T(A)$). This section establishes the two components of this decomposition. The rank $r$ is a non-negative integer, well-defined as the rank of the free abelian group $A/T(A)$; it measures the "continuous" or "infinite" part of $A$. The torsion subgroup $T(A)$ measures the "finite" part. For a finite abelian group, $T(A) = A$ and $r = 0$; for $\mathbb{Z}^r$ itself, $T(A) = 0$ and the rank is $r$. The interplay between torsion and rank is what makes the classification theorem both necessary and informative: knowing $r$ and $T(A)$ separately, and in what way they combine, completely determines $A$.
