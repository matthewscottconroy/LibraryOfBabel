# 19.2 The Structure Theorem

The Structure Theorem for Finitely Generated Abelian Groups is the complete classification: every finitely generated abelian group $A$ is isomorphic to a direct sum $\mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_k\mathbb{Z}$, where $r \geq 0$ and $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$ with each $d_i \geq 2$. Moreover, this decomposition is unique: the rank $r$ and the sequence of invariant factors $d_1, \ldots, d_k$ are uniquely determined by $A$. There is an equivalent primary decomposition form, in which the torsion part is further decomposed at each prime into cyclic $p$-groups; the two forms carry identical information and are interconverted using the Chinese Remainder Theorem.

The proof proceeds via Smith normal form. A finitely generated abelian group with $n$ generators and $m$ relations is presented as the cokernel of a matrix $M \in M_{m \times n}(\mathbb{Z})$: $A \cong \mathbb{Z}^n / \text{im}(M)$. Row operations on $M$ correspond to changing the relations (replacing one relation by its combination with another), and column operations correspond to changing the generating set (replacing one generator by its combination with others). Both types of operations are invertible over $\mathbb{Z}$, so they preserve the isomorphism type of the cokernel. By a sequence of such operations — always dividing the current pivot into the other entries in its row and column — one can reduce $M$ to diagonal form $\text{diag}(d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_k, 0, \ldots, 0)$ with $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$. This is Smith normal form, and the cokernel of the diagonal matrix is immediately $\mathbb{Z}/d_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_k\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}^{n-k}$.

Uniqueness of the invariant factors is proved by showing that the product $d_1 d_2 \cdots d_j$ equals the GCD of all $j \times j$ minors of $M$ — an invariant of the matrix independent of how row and column operations were performed. Alternatively, uniqueness can be proved by analyzing the $p$-primary parts for each prime separately and comparing the ranks of the subgroups $\{a \in A : pa = 0\}$ for varying primes $p$. The theorem is the group-theoretic special case of the far more general Structure Theorem for Finitely Generated Modules over a Principal Ideal Domain: replacing $\mathbb{Z}$ by a PID $R$ and $\mathbb{Z}^n$ by $R^n$ gives the classification of finitely generated $R$-modules. For $R = k[x]$ (a polynomial ring over a field), the structure theorem produces the rational canonical form and Jordan normal form of a linear operator.
