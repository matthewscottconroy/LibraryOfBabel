# 19.3.2 Computing the Group of Units $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$

The group of units $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ — the multiplicative group of integers coprime to$n$ — is a finite abelian group of order$\phi(n)$ (Euler's totient function). The structure theorem gives us its complete invariant factor decomposition, which has both theoretical content and practical applications in number theory and cryptography.

## Reduction to Prime Powers

The computation begins with the Chinese Remainder Theorem: if $n = p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_s^{a_s}$, then$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/p_1^{a_1}\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p_s^{a_s}\mathbb{Z}$ as rings, so
$$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^* \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p_1^{a_1}\mathbb{Z})^* \times \cdots \times (\mathbb{Z}/p_s^{a_s}\mathbb{Z})^*.$$
It suffices to determine $(\mathbb{Z}/p^a\mathbb{Z})^*$ for each prime power$p^a$.

\begin{theorem}[Structure of $(\mathbb{Z}/p^a\mathbb{Z})^*$]
\begin{enumerate}
\item For an odd prime $p$ and$a \geq 1$:$(\mathbb{Z}/p^a\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^{a-1}(p-1)\mathbb{Z}$, a cyclic group of order$\phi(p^a) = p^{a-1}(p-1)$.
\item $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^* = \{1\}$ (trivial).
\item $(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
\item For $a \geq 3$:$(\mathbb{Z}/2^a\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2^{a-2}\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

For odd $p$: a **primitive root** modulo$p^a$ is an element of order$p^{a-1}(p-1)$; its existence shows the group is cyclic. One proves that any primitive root$g$ modulo$p$ lifts to a primitive root modulo$p^a$ (either$g$ itself works, or$g + p$ does).

For $p = 2$ and$a \geq 3$: the element$-1$ has order$2$ and$5$ has order$2^{a-2}$ modulo$2^a$ (verifiable by checking$5^{2^{a-3}} \not\equiv 1$ but$5^{2^{a-2}} \equiv 1 \pmod{2^a}$). These generate a subgroup isomorphic to$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2^{a-2}\mathbb{Z}$, which has the right order$\phi(2^a) = 2^{a-1}$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Compute $(\mathbb{Z}/35\mathbb{Z})^*$. Here$35 = 5 \cdot 7$, so$(\mathbb{Z}/35\mathbb{Z})^* \cong (\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})^* \times (\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^*$. Both factors are cyclic of orders$4$ and$6$ respectively. So$(\mathbb{Z}/35\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. The invariant factors: elementary divisors$4, 6$ (we need to find$\gcd(4,6)=2$ and$\mathrm{lcm}(4,6)=12$), giving invariant factors$2 \mid 12$. So$(\mathbb{Z}/35\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, which is not cyclic (since$\phi(35) = 24$ and$12 < 24$).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Compute $(\mathbb{Z}/72\mathbb{Z})^*$. Here$72 = 8 \cdot 9 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2$. So$(\mathbb{Z}/72\mathbb{Z})^* \cong (\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^* \times (\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z})^*$. By the theorem:$(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and$(\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. Hence$(\mathbb{Z}/72\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. Elementary divisors:$\{2, 2, 2, 3\}$. Invariant factors (Frobenius):$\mathrm{lcm}(2,3)=6$ and$\mathrm{lcm}(2,1)=2$ and$\mathrm{lcm}(2,1) = 2$, so factors$2 \mid 2 \mid 6$. Hence$(\mathbb{Z}/72\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** When is $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ cyclic? The group is cyclic iff$n = 1, 2, 4, p^a$, or$2p^a$ for an odd prime$p$. These are exactly the$n$ for which a **primitive root modulo$n$** exists. For instance,$(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^* \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^* \times (\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \{1\} \times \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, cyclic. But$(\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ (since$(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and$(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$), which has invariant factors$2 \mid 4$ and is not cyclic.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The structure of $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$ for small primes:$(\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (generators:$3$ and$5$),$(\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ (generator:$2$),$(\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ (generator:$2$). For$p = 7$:$3^1 = 3$,$3^2 = 2$,$3^3 = 6$,$3^4 = 4$,$3^5 = 5$,$3^6 = 1$, confirming$3$ is a primitive root mod$7$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The structure of $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ has applications in number theory (Dirichlet characters are group homomorphisms$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^* \to \mathbb{C}^*$, and their structure depends on the group structure), in cryptography (the RSA and discrete logarithm cryptosystems depend on the cyclic structure of$(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$), and in algebraic number theory (via the structure of the multiplicative group of a finite field, which generalizes$(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$).
\end{remark}
