# 21.3 Prime and Maximal Ideals

Among all ideals in a commutative ring, the prime ideals and maximal ideals are the most important, and the distinction between them is one of the central themes of ring theory. A proper ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime if whenever $ab \in \mathfrak{p}$ it follows that $a \in \mathfrak{p}$ or $b \in \mathfrak{p}$; this is the abstract distillation of the defining property of prime numbers in $\mathbb{Z}$. A proper ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ is maximal if there is no proper ideal of $R$ that strictly contains $\mathfrak{m}$. The connection to the quotient construction is clean and decisive: $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime if and only if $R/\mathfrak{p}$ is an integral domain, and $\mathfrak{m}$ is maximal if and only if $R/\mathfrak{m}$ is a field. Since every field is an integral domain, every maximal ideal is prime, but the converse fails in general.

The existence of maximal ideals requires proof, because it is a statement about infinite ascending chains: starting from any proper ideal, one asserts the existence of a maximal element in the partially ordered set of proper ideals containing it. Zorn's lemma is the standard tool. The conclusion is that every proper ideal in a nonzero unital ring is contained in at least one maximal ideal, a fact as fundamental as it is nonconstructive. A local ring is a ring with a unique maximal ideal; it is one of the most important classes of rings for both algebraic geometry and algebraic number theory. Localizations of rings at prime ideals, constructed in Chapter 24, are always local rings.

The collection of all prime ideals of a commutative ring $R$ forms a topological space called the prime spectrum $\text{Spec}(R)$, with the Zariski topology: the closed sets are the sets $V(I) = \{\mathfrak{p} \in \text{Spec}(R) : I \subseteq \mathfrak{p}\}$ for ideals $I \subseteq R$. This topology is generally not Hausdorff — points need not be closed — but it encodes the containment relations among prime ideals and recovers topological information about geometric spaces from purely algebraic data. For $R = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ over an algebraically closed field $k$, the maximal ideals of $R$ correspond to points of affine $n$-space, and $\text{Spec}(R)$ adds the prime ideals corresponding to irreducible subvarieties. The spectral perspective is a preview of the language of schemes and of the Nullstellensatz, developed fully in Chapter 24.
