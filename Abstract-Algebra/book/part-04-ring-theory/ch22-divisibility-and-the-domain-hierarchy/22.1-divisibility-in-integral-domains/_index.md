# 22.1 Divisibility in Integral Domains

Divisibility is the most primitive relation in arithmetic, and its study in abstract integral domains reveals how much of elementary number theory depends on special properties of $\mathbb{Z}$ that are not shared by all domains. In an integral domain $R$, we say $a$ divides $b$ (written $a \mid b$) if $b = ac$ for some $c \in R$. Two elements are associates if each divides the other, which happens exactly when they differ by a unit: $a$ and $b$ are associates if $a = ub$ for some unit $u \in R^\times$. Divisibility is reflexive and transitive, making it a preorder on $R$; passing to equivalence classes of associates makes it a partial order on $R / R^\times$.

An element $p \in R$ is irreducible if it is nonzero, not a unit, and cannot be written as a product of two non-units. An element $p$ is prime if it is nonzero, not a unit, and whenever $p \mid ab$, it follows that $p \mid a$ or $p \mid b$. In $\mathbb{Z}$, these two notions coincide, and the fact is essentially the content of Euclid's lemma. In a general domain they diverge: every prime is irreducible (if $p = ab$ and $p \mid ab$, then $p \mid a$ or $p \mid b$, forcing one factor to be a unit), but irreducibles need not be prime. The failure of this implication is precisely the obstruction to unique factorization, and constructing a domain where irreducibles are not prime — such as $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ — is the key example that motivates the rest of the chapter.

Greatest common divisors in a domain need not exist in general, and when they do, they are only unique up to associates. In $\mathbb{Z}$, the gcd of any two elements exists by the Euclidean algorithm; in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, the gcd of $6$ and $2(1+\sqrt{-5})$ fails to exist as an element of the ring (the obstruction being the failure of unique factorization). The existence of gcd's for all pairs of elements is equivalent to the ring being a GCD domain, a class that includes all UFDs. Establishing these foundational distinctions between the notions of divisibility — units, associates, irreducibles, primes, GCDs — is the work of this section, preparing the way for the hierarchy of domains that the rest of Chapter 22 erects.
