# 22.5 Failure of Unique Factorization

The failure of unique factorization in rings of algebraic integers was one of the great surprises of nineteenth-century mathematics. Kummer, working with cyclotomic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$, discovered that the rings $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_p]$ did not always have unique factorization into elements, which threatened to invalidate proofs of Fermat's Last Theorem and other results that implicitly assumed it. His response — inventing "ideal numbers" that could be formally multiplied and that restored unique factorization — was the conceptual breakthrough that Dedekind later placed on rigorous foundations by replacing ideal numbers with actual subsets of rings satisfying the absorption property.

The canonical example is $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, where $6 = 2 \cdot 3 = (1 + \sqrt{-5})(1 - \sqrt{-5})$. The norm $N(a + b\sqrt{-5}) = a^2 + 5b^2$ shows that all four factors are irreducible: if $2 = \alpha\beta$ with $N(\alpha), N(\beta) > 1$, then $4 = N(\alpha)N(\beta)$ forces $N(\alpha) = N(\beta) = 2$, but $a^2 + 5b^2 = 2$ has no integer solutions. However, $2$ is not prime: $2 \mid (1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})$ but $2 \nmid (1 \pm \sqrt{-5})$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$. The presence of irreducibles that are not prime is the precise algebraic signature of the failure of UFD.

Dedekind's resolution is that in rings of algebraic integers — Dedekind domains — unique factorization holds at the level of ideals rather than elements. A Dedekind domain is an integrally closed Noetherian domain of Krull dimension one. In any Dedekind domain, every nonzero proper ideal factors uniquely as a product of prime ideals. For $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, the element $2$ generates an ideal $(2)$ that factors as a product of two prime ideals: $(2) = \mathfrak{p}^2$ where $\mathfrak{p} = (2, 1+\sqrt{-5})$. The ideal class group, defined as the group of fractional ideals modulo principal ideals, measures the obstruction to every ideal being principal. When the class group is trivial, the domain is a PID and element-level unique factorization is restored. For $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, the class group has order two, encoding the single "defect" in unique factorization. Computing class groups is a central problem of algebraic number theory, and the ideal-theoretic perspective introduced here pervades all of Chapter 24.
