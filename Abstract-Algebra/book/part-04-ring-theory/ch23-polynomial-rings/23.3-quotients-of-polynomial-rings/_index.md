# 23.3 Quotients of Polynomial Rings

The quotient ring $F[x]/(f)$ for a field $F$ and a polynomial $f \in F[x]$ is one of the most important constructions in algebra, and its structure depends entirely on the factorization of $f$. When $f$ is irreducible, the ideal $(f)$ is maximal (since $F[x]$ is a PID and $f$ is prime, hence $(f)$ is a prime ideal, and in a PID prime ideals other than $(0)$ are maximal), so $F[x]/(f)$ is a field. The image of $x$ in this field is a root of $f$: writing $\alpha = x + (f)$, we have $f(\alpha) = f(x) + (f) = 0$ in the quotient. This is the Kronecker construction, and it produces a field extension of $F$ of degree $n = \deg f$ in which $f$ acquires a root, without any assertion about the "existence" of that root outside the construction.

The most vivid instance is $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1) \cong \mathbb{C}$: the polynomial $x^2+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$, the quotient is a field of dimension 2 over $\mathbb{R}$, and the element $i = x + (x^2+1)$ satisfies $i^2 = -1$. Every element of the quotient can be written uniquely as $a + bi$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$. Similarly, $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2-2) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, and $\mathbb{F}_2[x]/(x^2+x+1)$ is the field $\mathbb{F}_4$ with four elements. The Kronecker construction shows that field extensions are not mysterious — they are simply quotients of polynomial rings.

When $f$ is reducible over $F$, the Chinese Remainder Theorem provides the correct description of $F[x]/(f)$. If $f = f_1^{e_1} \cdots f_k^{e_k}$ is the irreducible factorization and the $f_i$ are pairwise coprime (the squarefree case $e_i = 1$), then $F[x]/(f) \cong F[x]/(f_1) \times \cdots \times F[x]/(f_k)$. For instance, $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2-1) \cong \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}$ since $x^2-1 = (x-1)(x+1)$. When $f$ has repeated factors, the structure is more complex — the quotient is not semisimple — and understanding this connects to the theory of modules and to the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over PIDs, which will appear in the module theory chapters.
