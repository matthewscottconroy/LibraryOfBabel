# 24.1 Noetherian Rings and Modules

A ring $R$ is Noetherian if it satisfies the ascending chain condition (ACC) on ideals: every ascending chain $I_1 \subseteq I_2 \subseteq I_3 \subseteq \cdots$ of ideals eventually stabilizes, meaning $I_n = I_{n+1} = \cdots$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Equivalently, $R$ is Noetherian if every ideal is finitely generated, or if every nonempty collection of ideals has a maximal element under inclusion. The equivalence of these three conditions is a standard argument using the axiom of choice (in the form of Zorn's lemma or the well-ordering principle), and each formulation is useful in different proofs. The Noetherian condition is a finiteness hypothesis, and it is strong enough to guarantee a remarkable range of structural results while being satisfied by nearly every ring that arises in practice.

Hilbert's basis theorem states that if $R$ is Noetherian, then the polynomial ring $R[x]$ is Noetherian. The proof is a beautiful piece of algebra: given an ideal $I \subseteq R[x]$, one considers the ideal $J$ of all leading coefficients of polynomials in $I$; since $R$ is Noetherian, $J$ is finitely generated by leading coefficients of some polynomials $f_1, \ldots, f_k \in I$; one then shows that $I$ is generated by $f_1, \ldots, f_k$ together with finitely many generators of $I \cap R$ (the constant polynomials in $I$, using Noetherianness of $R$ again). Iterating the theorem shows that $R[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is Noetherian for any $n$, giving finite generation of all ideals in the polynomial rings central to algebraic geometry.

The module-theoretic extension is important: a module $M$ over a ring $R$ is Noetherian if every ascending chain of submodules stabilizes, or equivalently if every submodule of $M$ is finitely generated. Over a Noetherian ring, every finitely generated module is Noetherian, and in particular every submodule of a finitely generated module is itself finitely generated. This chain condition is what makes primary decomposition of submodules possible, gives the theory of finitely generated modules its clean structure, and ensures that the algebraic geometry carried out over Noetherian rings has the finiteness properties needed for dimension theory, intersection theory, and cohomology.
