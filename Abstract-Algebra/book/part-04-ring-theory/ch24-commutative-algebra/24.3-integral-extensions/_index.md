# 24.3 Integral Extensions

An element $s$ of a ring $S$ is integral over a subring $R \subseteq S$ if it satisfies a monic polynomial equation $s^n + a_{n-1} s^{n-1} + \cdots + a_1 s + a_0 = 0$ with coefficients $a_i \in R$. The insistence on monic is essential: without it, the condition would be trivially satisfied by any element (multiply any relation by the right power of the leading coefficient), but the monic condition gives integrality its particular geometric content. The integral closure of $R$ in $S$ is the set of all elements of $S$ integral over $R$; this set is a subring of $S$ (the sum and product of integral elements are integral, though the proof requires a module-theoretic argument). When the integral closure of $R$ in its fraction field equals $R$ itself, $R$ is called integrally closed. All UFDs are integrally closed, and the integrally closed domains of Krull dimension one are precisely the Dedekind domains.

The prototypical example is the inclusion $\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$: every element $a + b\sqrt{2}$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ since it satisfies $x^2 - 2ax + (a^2 - 2b^2) = 0$. More generally, the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$ of a number field $K$ is defined as the integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $K$, and it is always a Dedekind domain. The algebraic integers — complex numbers integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ — form a ring whose local structure at each prime ideal determines the arithmetic of number fields. In algebraic geometry, integral extensions correspond to finite maps between varieties: a ring map $R \to S$ is integral if and only if the corresponding map of varieties is finite (all fibers are finite sets), connecting the algebra to geometric intuition.

The going-up and going-down theorems describe how prime ideals interact with integral extensions and are the algebraic counterparts of the geometric statement that finite maps preserve dimension. Going-up: if $R \subseteq S$ is integral, $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq R$ is prime, and $\mathfrak{P}_0 \subseteq S$ lies over a prime $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$ of $R$, then there exists a prime $\mathfrak{P} \supseteq \mathfrak{P}_0$ in $S$ with $\mathfrak{P} \cap R = \mathfrak{p}$. Going-down holds when $R$ is integrally closed and $S$ is a domain integral over $R$: one can descend from a prime of $S$ to a prime of $R$ contained in a given prime. These theorems are used to prove that integral extensions preserve Krull dimension, a fact that feeds directly into the dimension theory of Section 24.4.
