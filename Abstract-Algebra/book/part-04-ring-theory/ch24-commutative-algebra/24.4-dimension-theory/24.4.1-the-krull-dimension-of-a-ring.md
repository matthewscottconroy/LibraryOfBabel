# 24.4.1 The Krull Dimension of a Ring

Algebra has its own notion of dimension, one that captures the same geometric intuition as the topological or linear-algebraic notions while being defined purely in terms of prime ideals. The **Krull dimension** $\dim R$ of a commutative ring$R$ is the supremum of the lengths of strictly increasing chains of prime ideals:
$$P_0 \subsetneq P_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq P_n,$$
where the length of such a chain is $n$ (the number of strict inclusions). The Krull dimension measures how many "levels" of prime ideals a ring has, which corresponds geometrically to the dimension of the associated scheme$\mathrm{Spec}(R)$.

\begin{definition}
The **Krull dimension** of a commutative ring $R$, written$\dim R$ or$\mathrm{Kdim}(R)$, is
$$\dim R = \sup\{n \geq 0 : \exists \text{ chain } P_0 \subsetneq P_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq P_n \text{ of prime ideals in } R\}.$$
If no such chain exists (i.e., $R$ has no prime ideal), then$\dim R = -\infty$ by convention (this only occurs for the zero ring). If arbitrarily long chains exist,$\dim R = \infty$.
\end{definition}

The basic examples confirm that the definition captures the right notion. A field has Krull dimension 0: its only prime ideal is $(0)$, and no proper chain of primes can have positive length. The integers$\mathbb{Z}$ have Krull dimension 1: the longest chains of primes are$(0) \subsetneq (p)$ for a prime$p$, and no longer chain exists since$(p)$ is maximal. The polynomial ring$F[x]$ over a field also has dimension 1, consistent with the picture of$\mathbb{A}^1_F$ as a curve.

The Krull dimension of a local ring $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ is the length of the longest chain of primes ending at$\mathfrak{m}$. For a Noetherian local ring, the dimension equals the minimal number of generators of$\mathfrak{m}$ needed to reduce the ring to dimension 0 (i.e., to make the quotient Artinian). This is the dimension-theoretic content of the **Krull dimension theorem** (or **principal ideal theorem**): in a Noetherian ring, if$f$ is a non-unit, the prime ideals minimal over$(f)$ have height at most 1.

The **height** of a prime ideal $P$ is$\mathrm{ht}(P) = \dim R_P$ — the Krull dimension of the localization at$P$, which equals the length of the longest chain of primes in$R$ contained in$P$. Then$\dim R = \sup_P (\mathrm{ht}(P) + \dim R/P)$ (where the sup is over all primes$P$), and for a domain,$\dim R = \mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{m})$ for any maximal ideal$\mathfrak{m}$.

For Noetherian rings, the Krull dimension is always the "right" dimension in that it matches geometric intuition about varieties. Non-Noetherian rings can behave pathologically — Nagata constructed a Noetherian ring with infinite Krull dimension, though this requires an elaborate construction — and for the commutative rings arising in practice, Krull dimension accurately reflects geometric dimension.

**Example 1.** A field $k$:$\dim k = 0$. The only prime ideal is$(0)$, and there is no chain of length$\geq 1$.

**Example 2.** $\mathbb{Z}$:$\dim \mathbb{Z} = 1$. Chains:$(0) \subsetneq (p)$ for prime$p$. The prime$(p)$ is maximal, so no extension is possible.

**Example 3.** $\mathbb{Z}[x]$:$\dim \mathbb{Z}[x] = 2$. A chain of length 2:$(0) \subsetneq (p) \subsetneq (p, x-a)$ for a prime$p$ and integer$a$. The ideal$(p)$ is prime (quotient$\mathbb{Z}[x]/(p) \cong \mathbb{F}_p[x]$, a domain), and$(p, x-a)$ is maximal (quotient$\cong \mathbb{F}_p$). No chain of length 3 exists, since any prime properly containing a maximal ideal would be the whole ring.

**Example 4.** $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$:$\dim = n$. Lower bound:$(0) \subsetneq (x_1) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq (x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ gives a chain of length$n$. Upper bound: no chain of primes can have length$> n$ (proved using integral extensions and the going-up theorem). Details in §24.4.2.

**Example 5.** A local Artinian ring $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ with$\mathfrak{m}^n = 0$:$\dim R = 0$. A ring of Krull dimension 0 is called Artinian if it is also Noetherian. Such rings have finitely many maximal ideals, all of which are also minimal primes, and the ring decomposes as a finite product of local Artinian rings. Example:$k[x]/(x^n)$ has unique prime$(x)/(x^n)$ and dimension 0.
