# 24.4 Dimension Theory

The Krull dimension of a commutative ring $R$ is the supremum of the lengths of chains of prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_n$ in $R$; a chain of length $n$ has $n+1$ terms. Fields have Krull dimension $0$ (their only prime ideal is $(0)$). The integers $\mathbb{Z}$ and the polynomial ring $k[x]$ in one variable over a field have Krull dimension $1$: the prime ideals are $(0)$ and the maximal ideals, and chains have length at most one. The polynomial ring $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ has Krull dimension $n$, witnessed by the chain $(0) \subsetneq (x_1) \subsetneq (x_1, x_2) \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq (x_1, \ldots, x_n)$. This algebraic dimension agrees perfectly with the geometric dimension of affine $n$-space, and the proof that no longer chain exists uses the Noether normalization lemma.

Noether normalization is a foundational lemma: every finitely generated algebra over a field $k$ contains a polynomial subring over which it is integral. More precisely, if $A = k[a_1, \ldots, a_m]$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra, there exist algebraically independent elements $y_1, \ldots, y_d \in A$ such that $A$ is integral over $k[y_1, \ldots, y_d]$. Here $d$ is the Krull dimension of $A$, also equal to the transcendence degree of the fraction field of $A/\mathfrak{p}$ for any minimal prime $\mathfrak{p}$. The lemma reduces dimension theory to the well-understood case of polynomial rings, and the going-up theorem for integral extensions then controls the prime spectrum of $A$.

Hilbert's Nullstellensatz is the culminating theorem, establishing the dictionary between commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. The weak form states that if $I$ is a proper ideal of $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ and $k$ is algebraically closed, then $V(I)$ — the set of common zeros of all $f \in I$ — is nonempty. The strong form states that for any ideal $I$, the ideal $I(V(I))$ of polynomials vanishing on $V(I)$ equals the radical $\sqrt{I} = \{f : f^n \in I \text{ for some } n \geq 1\}$. This gives a bijection between radical ideals in $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ and algebraic subsets of $k^n$, with prime ideals corresponding to irreducible varieties and maximal ideals corresponding to points. Under this bijection, containment of ideals reverses to containment of varieties, and the Krull dimension of the coordinate ring of a variety equals the geometric dimension of the variety. The Nullstellensatz is thus the theorem that makes algebraic geometry possible as a rigorous discipline, and the commutative algebra of this entire chapter is the technical foundation on which it rests.
