# 25.3 Module Homomorphisms

A module homomorphism — also called an $R$-linear map — is a function $f : M \to N$ between $R$-modules that respects both the additive group structure and the scalar action: $f(m + m') = f(m) + f(m')$ and $f(r \cdot m) = r \cdot f(m)$ for all $r \in R$ and $m, m' \in M$. The condition $f(rm) = rf(m)$ is what distinguishes a module homomorphism from a mere group homomorphism: it demands that $f$ intertwines the two $R$-actions. Over a field, this is exactly the condition for a linear map in the sense of linear algebra; over $\mathbb{Z}$, the scalar condition is automatic (since the $\mathbb{Z}$-action is determined by the group structure), so every group homomorphism between abelian groups is a $\mathbb{Z}$-module homomorphism.

The set $\text{Hom}_R(M, N)$ of all $R$-module homomorphisms from $M$ to $N$ is itself an abelian group under pointwise addition, and when $R$ is commutative it becomes an $R$-module in its own right via $(r \cdot f)(m) := r \cdot f(m)$. When $M = N$, the set $\text{End}_R(M) = \text{Hom}_R(M, M)$ is a ring under pointwise addition and composition of maps, called the endomorphism ring of $M$. This ring is generally noncommutative and encodes the internal symmetry of $M$: for instance, $\text{End}_R(R^n) \cong M_n(R)$ (the ring of $n \times n$ matrices over $R$), which is why matrices appear as soon as one works with free modules of rank $n$. When $M$ is a simple module, Schur's Lemma implies that $\text{End}_R(M)$ is a division ring — a result that plays a central role in representation theory and the theory of semisimple rings.

The kernel and image of a module homomorphism are submodules of $M$ and $N$ respectively, so the entire submodule machinery of Section 25.2 applies. A homomorphism is injective if and only if its kernel is zero, and surjective if and only if its image is all of $N$. A bijective module homomorphism is an isomorphism, and the inverse of a module isomorphism is again a module homomorphism. The First Isomorphism Theorem gives the canonical factorization $M \twoheadrightarrow M/\ker f \xrightarrow{\sim} \text{im}\, f \hookrightarrow N$, which decomposes any homomorphism into a surjection followed by an isomorphism followed by an inclusion. This factorization will be used systematically in Chapter 27 to analyze the structure of finitely generated modules by presenting them as cokernels of explicit maps between free modules.
