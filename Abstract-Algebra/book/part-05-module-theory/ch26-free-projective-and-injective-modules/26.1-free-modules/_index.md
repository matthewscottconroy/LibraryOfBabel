# 26.1 Free Modules

A free module is the most structured kind of module: one that possesses a basis in the same sense that a vector space does. Formally, a subset $B \subseteq M$ is a basis for an $R$-module $M$ if $B$ generates $M$ and every element of $M$ has a unique expression as a finite $R$-linear combination of elements of $B$. When such a basis exists, $M$ is called a free module of rank $|B|$. The canonical example is $R^n$, the direct sum of $n$ copies of $R$ viewed as a left module over itself; the standard basis vectors $e_i$ form a basis, and every element is uniquely a tuple $(r_1, \ldots, r_n)$ with $r_i \in R$.

The universal property of free modules captures their essential role: any function $f : B \to M'$ from a basis $B$ into any $R$-module $M'$ extends uniquely to an $R$-module homomorphism $\tilde{f} : F(B) \to M'$. This is the module-theoretic analogue of defining a linear map by specifying where basis vectors go. The universal property has an immediate and important consequence: every $R$-module is a quotient of a free module. Given any module $M$ with generating set $S$, the map $F(S) \to M$ sending each generator to the corresponding element of $M$ is a surjective homomorphism, so $M \cong F(S)/K$ for the kernel $K$. This representation of $M$ as a cokernel of a map between free modules is called a presentation of $M$, and it is the starting point for all homological calculations.

Over a field, every module (vector space) is free, but over a general ring the rank of a free module may not be well-defined. For commutative rings and for $\mathbb{Z}$, the rank is indeed well-defined — this is the content of the invariant basis number (IBN) property — but there exist noncommutative rings admitting $R^m \cong R^n$ for $m \neq n$. For a PID such as $\mathbb{Z}$ or $F[x]$, free modules are extremely well-behaved: every submodule of a free module of rank $n$ is free of rank at most $n$. This submodule theorem for PIDs is a key input to the proof of the structure theorem in Chapter 27, where one needs to know that the kernel of a presentation map between free modules is itself free.
