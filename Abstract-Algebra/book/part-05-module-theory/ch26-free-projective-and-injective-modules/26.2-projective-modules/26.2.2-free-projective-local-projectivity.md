# 26.2.2 Free $\Rightarrow$ Projective; Local Projectivity

Every free module is projective: to lift a map $f: F \to N$ through a surjection$g: M \to N$, one need only choose a preimage in$M$ for each basis element's image in$N$ and then extend linearly. This construction works because basis elements are "independent" — there are no relations that could obstruct the lift. Since projective modules are summands of free modules, and summands of projective modules are projective, the implication free$\Rightarrow$ projective follows immediately.

The implication cannot be reversed in general: there exist projective modules that are not free. However, the gap between "projective" and "free" narrows dramatically over special rings. Over a local ring (a ring $R$ with a unique maximal ideal$\mathfrak{m}$), every finitely generated projective module is free. The key tool is Nakayama's lemma: if$P$ is a finitely generated projective module over a local ring, one lifts a basis of$P/\mathfrak{m}P$ (a vector space over$R/\mathfrak{m}$) to generators of$P$, and uses the local hypothesis to show these generators form a basis. Over a field, all modules are free (they are vector spaces), so all modules are projective — a special case of the local result.

The projectivity condition is about the module's ability to "split out" of any sequence that surjects onto it: $P$ is projective if and only if every short exact sequence$0 \to M' \to M \to P \to 0$ splits (i.e.,$M \cong M' \oplus P$). Making projective modules "locally free" is therefore the statement that over local rings, every finitely generated projective has this splitting property with a free complement.

One should internalize the following mental model: projective modules are "vector bundles" over the spectrum of the ring. Over a field (a single point), all bundles are trivial — free. Over a local ring (a "fat point"), all bundles are still trivial. Nonfree projectives arise when the base space has nontrivial topology — e.g., over the ring of continuous functions $C(S^1)$, the Möbius band gives a rank-1 projective that is not free, corresponding to the nontrivial line bundle over the circle.

\begin{theorem}[Kaplansky]
Every projective module over a local ring is free.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Nakayama's Lemma, Projective Form]
Let $R$ be a local ring with maximal ideal$\mathfrak{m}$ and$P$ a finitely generated projective$R$-module. If$\{p_1, \ldots, p_n\}$ are elements of$P$ whose images in$P/\mathfrak{m}P$ form a$k$-basis (where$k = R/\mathfrak{m}$), then$\{p_1, \ldots, p_n\}$ is a basis for$P$ as an$R$-module.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Over a field $k$, every module is a vector space, hence free, hence projective. This is the trivial case where free, projective, and flat all coincide. The unique simple module$k$ is free of rank 1.

**Example 2.** Over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \{a/b \in \mathbb{Q} : p \nmid b\}$ (the localization of$\mathbb{Z}$ at$p$), which is a local ring with maximal ideal$(p)$, every finitely generated projective module is free. Indeed,$\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ is a PID (a DVR), so free and projective already coincide by Section 26.2.3.

**Example 3.** The module $P = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ over$R = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ is projective (Example 2 of 26.2.1). But$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ is not a local ring (it has two maximal ideals:$(2)$ and$(3)$). After localizing at$(2)$:$R_{(2)} \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$... wait, let us instead localize$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ at the prime$2$. Applying the Chinese Remainder Theorem,$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}_{(2)} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and$P_{(2)} = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is free of rank 1 over$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Locally at every prime,$P$ becomes free — this is the local-global principle for projectivity.

**Example 4.** The tangent bundle of the 2-sphere $S^2$ (viewed as a rank-2 projective module over$C^\infty(S^2)$) is not free by the hairy ball theorem: there is no nonvanishing vector field on$S^2$, so no global frame. The algebraic analogue: the module of global sections of a nontrivial vector bundle is projective but not free.

**Example 5.** Let $R = k[x, y]/(x^2 + y^2 - 1)$ be the coordinate ring of the unit circle over$\mathbb{R}$. The module$P$ corresponding to the Möbius band — sections of the nontrivial line bundle over$S^1$ — is projective of rank 1 but not free: there is no global nowhere-vanishing section. This is a concrete algebraic example of projective$\not\Rightarrow$ free over a non-local ring.
