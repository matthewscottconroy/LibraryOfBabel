# 26.2.4 Projective Modules in Algebraic K-Theory

The set of isomorphism classes of finitely generated projective modules over $R$ carries a natural monoid structure under direct sum:$[P] + [Q] = [P \oplus Q]$. The *Grothendieck group*$K_0(R)$ is the group completion of this monoid — it is the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes$[P]$ of finitely generated projective$R$-modules, subject to the relation$[P] = [P'] + [P'']$ whenever$P \cong P' \oplus P''$.

The group $K_0(R)$ measures the "shape" of the collection of projective modules. For$R = F$ a field, every projective module is free (a vector space), and two vector spaces are isomorphic if and only if they have the same dimension. Thus$K_0(F) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the class$[F]$ of the rank-1 free module. For$R = \mathbb{Z}$ or more generally any PID, projective modules are free by Section 26.2.3, so$K_0(R) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ again. The departure from$\mathbb{Z}$ occurs precisely when nonfree projectives exist: for the ring of integers of a number field,$K_0(R) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathrm{Cl}(R)$, where$\mathrm{Cl}(R)$ is the ideal class group, whose nontriviality reflects the failure of unique factorization.

The group $K_0(R)$ is the first in a series of algebraic K-groups$K_0, K_1, K_2, \ldots$, each encoding progressively deeper algebraic information about$R$. Algebraic K-theory connects ring theory to topology (via vector bundles over spaces, which are the geometric counterpart of projective modules over rings), number theory (via class groups and zeta functions), and even to the deeper structure of manifolds. The humble projective module thus serves as the entry point to an extremely rich part of modern mathematics.

More explicitly: $K_0(R)$ is constructed via the Grothendieck group (also called group completion or$K$-group) of the abelian monoid$\mathrm{Proj}(R)$ of isomorphism classes. The elements of$K_0(R)$ are formal differences$[P] - [Q]$, subject to the relation that$[P] - [Q] = [P'] - [Q']$ if and only if$P \oplus Q' \oplus R^n \cong P' \oplus Q \oplus R^n$ for some$n \geq 0$ (stable isomorphism). This "stable" comparison is what makes$K_0(R)$ a group, since$[P] + (- [P]) = [P \oplus P'] - [P \oplus P'] = 0$ for any complement$P'$ of$P$ in a free module.

\begin{definition}
The \textit{Grothendieck group} $K_0(R)$ is the abelian group with one generator$[P]$ for each isomorphism class of finitely generated projective$R$-modules, subject to the relation$[P \oplus Q] = [P] + [Q]$.
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** $K_0(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$: generated by$[\mathbb{Z}]$, the rank-1 free module. The rank map$[P] \mapsto \mathrm{rk}(P)$ is an isomorphism since all projectives are free.

**Example 2.** $K_0(\mathbb{Z}[i]) \cong \mathbb{Z}$: the Gaussian integers form a PID (since$\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is a Euclidean domain), so all finitely generated projectives are free. The rank map is again an isomorphism.

**Example 3.** $K_0(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$: the ideal class group of$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ is$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (generated by the class of$(2, 1+\sqrt{-5})$). The free summand$\mathbb{Z}$ accounts for the rank, and the$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ detects the nonfree projectives. An explicit isomorphism:$[R^n \oplus I^{\oplus m}] \mapsto (n + m, m \bmod 2)$ where$I$ is the nontrivial ideal class.

**Example 4.** $K_0(k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by the Quillen–Suslin theorem: all finitely generated projectives over$k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ are free. More broadly,$K_0(R) = K_0(R[x])$ (homotopy invariance of$K_0$) for many rings$R$, reflecting the algebro-geometric principle that$\mathrm{Spec}(R[x]) = \mathrm{Spec}(R) \times \mathbb{A}^1$ is homotopy equivalent to$\mathrm{Spec}(R)$.

**Example 5.** For the complex group algebra $R = \mathbb{C}[G]$ of a finite group$G$, the Artin–Wedderburn theorem gives$R \cong \prod_i M_{n_i}(\mathbb{C})$, and$K_0(R) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$ where$r$ is the number of irreducible complex representations of$G$. The projective indecomposable modules are the summands of$R$ (one for each irreducible), and their classes form a$\mathbb{Z}$-basis of$K_0(R)$.
