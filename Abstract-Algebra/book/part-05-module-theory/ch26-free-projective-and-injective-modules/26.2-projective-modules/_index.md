# 26.2 Projective Modules

Projective modules generalize free modules by retaining the most important property of free modules from the homological point of view: the ability to lift maps. A module $P$ is projective if, for every surjective homomorphism $\pi : M \twoheadrightarrow N$ and every homomorphism $f : P \to N$, there exists a homomorphism $\tilde{f} : P \to M$ such that $\pi \circ \tilde{f} = f$. Geometrically, one thinks of $\pi$ as a projection or covering, and projectivity as the ability to "pull back" any map from $P$ through such a covering. Free modules are projective because a map from a free module is determined by where the basis goes, and one can always lift basis images through a surjection by choosing preimages.

There are three equivalent characterizations of projectivity that illuminate different aspects of the concept. First, $P$ is projective if every short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to P \to 0$ splits — that is, $B \cong A \oplus P$. Second, $P$ is projective if and only if it is a direct summand of a free module: there exists $Q$ with $P \oplus Q \cong R^n$ (or more generally $P \oplus Q$ free for infinite-rank cases). Third, the functor $\text{Hom}_R(P, -)$ is exact. The equivalence of these three characterizations is a theorem, and each one is useful in different contexts: the splitting property is convenient for checking specific sequences; the summand property provides the most concrete handle on projective modules; and the exactness of Hom is the form used in homological algebra to define projective resolutions.

Over a PID, every projective module is free — a clean and elegant result that partially explains why the structure theory over PIDs is so complete. The proof uses the submodule theorem for free modules and an inductive argument. Over more general rings, projectivity and freeness genuinely differ, and this discrepancy becomes a rich source of algebraic geometry and $K$-theory. The Serre–Quillen–Suslin theorem, one of the deep results of twentieth-century algebra, says that finitely generated projective modules over $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ are free when $k$ is a field — a fact that is far from obvious and whose proof requires sophisticated techniques. Over local rings, the simpler Nakayama-based argument shows that finitely generated projective modules are free, which is the algebraic analogue of the topological statement that every vector bundle over a contractible space is trivial.
