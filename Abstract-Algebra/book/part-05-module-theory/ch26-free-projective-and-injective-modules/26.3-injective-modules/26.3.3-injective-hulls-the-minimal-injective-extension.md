# 26.3.3 Injective Hulls: The Minimal Injective Extension

Every module embeds into an injective module — this is a theorem analogous to "every abelian group embeds into a divisible abelian group" — but among all such embeddings there is a canonical minimal one called the *injective hull* or *injective envelope*.

The injective hull $E(M)$ of a module$M$ is an injective module containing$M$ as a submodule, with the additional property that the embedding$M \hookrightarrow E(M)$ is *essential*: every nonzero submodule of$E(M)$ has nontrivial intersection with$M$. Intuitively,$E(M)$ is the smallest injective module containing$M$, in the sense that$M$ is "dense" in$E(M)$ — no information about$M$ is lost when we pass to$E(M)$, and$E(M)$ contains no "wasted" injective part that is disjoint from$M$.

Injective hulls are unique up to isomorphism: if $E$ and$E'$ are two injective hulls of$M$, there is an isomorphism$E \to E'$ fixing$M$ pointwise. This uniqueness makes$E(M)$ a genuine invariant of the module$M$. Over$\mathbb{Z}$, the injective hull of$\mathbb{Z}$ is$\mathbb{Q}$ (which is the rational completion, an injective$\mathbb{Z}$-module containing$\mathbb{Z}$ essentially), and the injective hull of$\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ is the Prüfer$p$-group$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$. Injective hulls play the role in module theory that algebraic closures play in field theory and completions play in metric space theory: they are the "minimal completion" of the given object into a category of particularly well-behaved objects. In homological algebra, injective hulls provide the building blocks for *injective resolutions*, which are the right tool for computing the$\mathrm{Ext}$ functors.

The construction of $E(M)$ proceeds in two steps. First, one shows that$M$ embeds into some injective module$Q$: embed$M$ into a free module (which is always possible as a set, by mapping$M$ into a product of copies of an injective cogenerator), then observe that$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ serves as an injective cogenerator for$\mathbb{Z}$-modules (every abelian group embeds into a product of copies of$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$). Second, one finds the smallest injective submodule of$Q$ containing$M$ by taking the intersection of all injective submodules of$Q$ containing$M$ (which is again injective since injective modules are closed under intersections within an ambient injective module).

\begin{definition}
An embedding $M \hookrightarrow E$ is \textit{essential} if every nonzero submodule$N \leq E$ satisfies$N \cap M \neq 0$. The \textit{injective hull}$E(M)$ of$M$ is an injective module containing$M$ via an essential embedding.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
Every $R$-module$M$ has an injective hull$E(M)$, unique up to isomorphism over$M$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** $E(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Q}$ over$\mathbb{Z}$. The embedding$\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}$ is essential: every nonzero subgroup of$\mathbb{Q}$ contains a nonzero rational, which when multiplied by a suitable integer lands in$\mathbb{Z}$. And$\mathbb{Q}$ is injective (divisible). No proper injective submodule of$\mathbb{Q}$ contains$\mathbb{Z}$ (since$\mathbb{Q}$ is already the "minimal" divisible group containing$\mathbb{Z}$).

**Example 2.** $E(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ over$\mathbb{Z}$. The Prüfer$p$-group contains$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ as the elements of order$p$, and the embedding is essential: every nonzero subgroup of$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ contains an element of order$p$ (since$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) = \bigcup_{n} \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ and any nonzero subgroup must contain$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$).

**Example 3.** $E(\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ for any$k \geq 1$. The module$\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ embeds into$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ via$1 \mapsto p^{-k} + \mathbb{Z}$, and this embedding is essential for the same reason as above.

**Example 4.** Over a field $k$,$E(M) = M$ for every$k$-module$M$ (since every vector space is already injective). The injective hull of a vector space is the vector space itself.

**Example 5.** Over $R = k[x]$ and$M = k$ (where$x$ acts as 0), the injective hull is$E(k) = k(x)/k[x]$ — the "fraction field modulo the polynomial ring," or equivalently the$k[x]$-module of formal Laurent series with only finitely many negative powers, modulo polynomials. More concretely,$E(k) \cong k[x, x^{-1}]/k[x]$ as$k[x]$-modules, which is the$p$-primary injective corresponding to the prime$p = x$.
