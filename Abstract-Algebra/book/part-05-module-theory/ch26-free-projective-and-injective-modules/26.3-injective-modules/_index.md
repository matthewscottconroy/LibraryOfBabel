# 26.3 Injective Modules

Injective modules are the categorical dual of projective modules, but they are considerably harder to construct and recognize explicitly. A module $Q$ is injective if, for every injective homomorphism $\iota : A \hookrightarrow B$ and every homomorphism $f : A \to Q$, there exists a homomorphism $g : B \to Q$ extending $f$: $g \circ \iota = f$. Dualizing the diagram for projectivity, one replaces surjections with injections and lifts with extensions, and reverses all arrows. The injective modules are precisely those that allow any partial map into them to be extended, making them "maximally extendable" targets. Over $\mathbb{Z}$, the injective modules are the divisible abelian groups: $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, the Prüfer groups $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) = \mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]/\mathbb{Z}$, and direct sums of these.

Baer's criterion, proved by Reinhold Baer in 1940, is the key tool for verifying injectivity without examining all possible injections. It states: $Q$ is injective if and only if every homomorphism $f : I \to Q$ from a left ideal $I \subseteq R$ extends to a homomorphism $R \to Q$. The criterion reduces an a priori infinite-dimensional verification — checking all possible injections $A \hookrightarrow B$ — to checking only the inclusions of ideals into $R$, which is a much more tractable family. In practice, one uses Baer's criterion by observing that a surjective image of a divisible group is divisible (establishing closures under quotients) and that $\mathbb{Q}$ is injective because $\mathbb{Z}$ is a PID and every fractional extension is realized inside $\mathbb{Q}$.

Every module embeds into an injective module — this is the dual of the fact that every module is a quotient of a free module — and among all injective modules containing $M$ there is a smallest one, called the injective hull or injective envelope of $M$, denoted $E(M)$. The injective hull is characterized by being an essential extension of $M$: every nonzero submodule of $E(M)$ has nonzero intersection with $M$. The existence and uniqueness of $E(M)$ follow from Zorn's lemma, and the injective hull is a fundamental invariant of the module. Injective resolutions, built by iterating the injective hull construction, are used to compute the right derived functors of $\text{Hom}_R(M, -)$, which are the Ext groups $\text{Ext}^n_R(M, N)$ measuring the failure of $\text{Hom}$ to be exact in the second variable.
