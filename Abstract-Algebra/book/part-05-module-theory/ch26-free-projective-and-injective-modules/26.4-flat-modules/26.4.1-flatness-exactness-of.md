# 26.4.1 Flatness: Exactness of $- \otimes_R M$

The tensor product functor $- \otimes_R M$ is always right-exact: it takes surjections to surjections and preserves exact sequences of the form$A \to B \to C \to 0$. But it does not in general preserve injections, and the failure to do so is a central source of difficulty in module theory. A module$M$ for which tensor product is fully exact — that is, also preserves injections — is called *flat*.

Precisely, a right $R$-module$M$ is *flat* if the functor$- \otimes_R M$ is exact: for every short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ of left$R$-modules, the tensored sequence$0 \to A \otimes_R M \to B \otimes_R M \to C \otimes_R M \to 0$ is also exact. Since right exactness is automatic, the content is entirely that$A \otimes_R M \hookrightarrow B \otimes_R M$ is injective whenever$A \hookrightarrow B$ is.

Flatness is a strictly weaker condition than projectivity. Every projective module is flat, but flat modules that are not projective exist in abundance — most prominently, $\mathbb{Q}$ as a$\mathbb{Z}$-module is flat (because$\mathbb{Z}$ is a domain and localization is always flat) but is not projective. Geometrically, flatness captures the idea of a "continuously varying" family: a ring map$R \to S$ makes$S$ into a flat$R$-module when the fibers of the corresponding geometric map vary without "jumping," which is why flatness is one of the fundamental conditions in algebraic geometry. The derived functor measuring the failure of flatness is$\mathrm{Tor}$, introduced in Chapter 40.

Flatness can also be characterized in terms of relations: a module $M$ is flat if and only if whenever$\sum_i r_i m_i = 0$ (a relation in$M$ with$r_i \in R$,$m_i \in M$), this relation is a consequence of "trivial" relations coming from$R$ — in the sense that there exist$m'_j \in M$ and$a_{ij} \in R$ such that$m_i = \sum_j a_{ij} m'_j$ and$\sum_i r_i a_{ij} = 0$ in$R$. This "equational criterion" for flatness (due to Lazard) is the most explicit way to verify flatness in many concrete situations.

\begin{definition}
A right $R$-module$M$ is \textit{flat} if the functor$- \otimes_R M: \mathrm{Mod}_R \to \mathrm{Ab}$ is exact, i.e., preserves all short exact sequences.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lazard]
A module $M$ is flat if and only if every finitely presented relation among elements of$M$ follows from relations in$R$. Equivalently,$M$ is a filtered colimit of finitely generated free modules.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** $\mathbb{Q}$ is a flat$\mathbb{Z}$-module. Given an injection$f: A \hookrightarrow B$ of abelian groups, we must show$A \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow B \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$. But$A \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} = A[\text{inverted by all integers}]$ is the rational localization, and injectivity is preserved under localization: if$a/n = 0$ in$B \otimes \mathbb{Q}$, then$ma = 0$ in$B$ for some$m$, so$ma = 0$ in$A$ (since$f$ is injective), so$a/n = 0$ in$A \otimes \mathbb{Q}$.

**Example 2.** $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \{a/b : b \text{ not divisible by } p\}$ is a flat$\mathbb{Z}$-module: it is the localization of$\mathbb{Z}$ at$S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus p\mathbb{Z}$, and localizations are always flat.

**Example 3.** Any free module $R^n$ is flat. Tensoring$0 \to A \to B$ with$R^n$ gives$0 \to A^n \to B^n$, which is exact since the injection$A \to B$ is preserved componentwise.

**Example 4.** $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is NOT a flat$\mathbb{Z}$-module. Tensor the injection$\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}$ with$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$: we get$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which is the zero map. An injection became a zero map, violating flatness.

**Example 5.** A direct limit (filtered colimit) of flat modules is flat. In particular, $\mathbb{Q} = \varinjlim_n \frac{1}{n!}\mathbb{Z}$ is a filtered colimit of free$\mathbb{Z}$-modules, confirming its flatness via Lazard's theorem.
