# 26.4.2 Free $\Rightarrow$ Projective$\Rightarrow$ Flat; Counterexamples to Reversal

The three conditions — free, projective, flat — form a strict hierarchy: every free module is projective, and every projective module is flat. Understanding where the implications fail to reverse is essential for knowing which tools apply in which contexts.

That free implies projective is immediate from the definition: a free module $F$ is a summand of itself ($F = F \oplus 0$), satisfying the summand characterization of projectivity. The implication projective$\Rightarrow$ flat follows from the tensor product characterization: a projective module$P$ is a summand of a free module$F = P \oplus Q$, and tensoring a short exact sequence with$F$ is exact (since it just tensors component-wise with each copy of$R$), so tensoring with$P$ is also exact.

The reverse implications both fail. The module $\mathbb{Q}$ over$\mathbb{Z}$ shows that flat does not imply projective:$\mathbb{Q}$ is flat because$\mathbb{Z}$ is a domain and localization at any multiplicative set is flat (we can see this because$\mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}]$ where$S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$), but$\mathbb{Q}$ is not projective because it is not a summand of any free$\mathbb{Z}$-module. The ideal$(2, 1 + \sqrt{-5})$ in$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ shows that projective does not imply free (as discussed in Section 26.2.3). The hierarchy therefore genuinely has three distinct levels, and characterizing when the implications reverse — over Noetherian local rings, over PIDs, over fields — is a recurring theme in commutative algebra.

To verify that $\mathbb{Q}$ is not projective over$\mathbb{Z}$: if$\mathbb{Q}$ were projective, it would be a direct summand of a free$\mathbb{Z}$-module$F = \bigoplus_\alpha \mathbb{Z}$. But any map$\mathbb{Q} \to \bigoplus_\alpha \mathbb{Z}$ must be zero (since$\mathbb{Q}$ is divisible and torsion-free, any map$\mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is zero: if$f(1) = n$, then$f(1) = f(2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}) = 2f(\frac{1}{2})$, so$n = 2f(\frac{1}{2})$, meaning$n$ is divisible by all powers of 2, hence$n = 0$). So no map$\mathbb{Q} \to F$ can be a section of a surjection$F \to \mathbb{Q}$, and$\mathbb{Q}$ is not projective.

The summary table is:

| Condition | Free | Projective | Flat |
|-----------|------|------------|------|
| Over a field | all | all | all |
| Over a PID | = projective | = free | = torsion-free |
| Over a local ring | = projective (f.g.) | = free (f.g.) | more general |
| In general | strongest | middle | weakest |

\begin{theorem}
Free $\Rightarrow$ projective$\Rightarrow$ flat. Neither implication reverses in general. Over a PID, projective$\Leftrightarrow$ free. Over a local ring (finitely generated), projective$\Leftrightarrow$ free.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Free $\Rightarrow$ projective:$\mathbb{Z}^2$ is free, hence projective over$\mathbb{Z}$. The lifting property holds because we can lift images of the two basis vectors independently.

**Example 2.** Projective $\not\Rightarrow$ free (when not over a PID): the ideal$I = (2, 1+\sqrt{-5})$ in$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ is projective (summand of the free module$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]^2$) but not free (it is not principal). Verified:$I \oplus I \cong \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]^2$ as$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$-modules.

**Example 3.** Projective $\Rightarrow$ flat: Let$P$ be a projective$\mathbb{Z}$-module, so$P$ is free (by 26.2.3). Any free module is flat (tensoring with$\mathbb{Z}^n$ preserves injections). Hence$P$ is flat.

**Example 4.** Flat $\not\Rightarrow$ projective:$\mathbb{Q}$ is flat over$\mathbb{Z}$ (proved above) but not projective (proved above). More generally, any localization$S^{-1}R$ of a domain$R$ is flat but not projective unless$S = R \setminus \{0\}$ and$R$ itself is a field.

**Example 5.** Over $k[x]$: every flat finitely generated module is projective (since$k[x]$ is a PID and finitely generated flat modules over Noetherian rings are projective). But$k(x)$ (the field of rational functions) is flat over$k[x]$ and not finitely generated, hence not projective.
