# 26.4.4 Flat Base Change in Commutative Algebra

One of the most powerful applications of flatness is the principle of *flat base change*: flat modules allow exact sequences to be transported along ring maps without distortion. This principle is used constantly in both commutative algebra and algebraic geometry.

The precise statement is as follows. Let $M$ be a flat right$R$-module. If$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ is an exact sequence of left$R$-modules, then$0 \to A \otimes_R M \to B \otimes_R M \to C \otimes_R M \to 0$ is also exact. In other words, tensoring with a flat module preserves short exact sequences. This is the definition of flatness restated as a concrete tool.

The archetypal example of flat base change is *localization*. If $S \subseteq R$ is a multiplicative set, the localization$S^{-1}R$ is a flat$R$-module. Consequently, localizing a short exact sequence of$R$-modules always yields a short exact sequence of$S^{-1}R$-modules. This is the foundation of the local-global principles in commutative algebra: a sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ is exact if and only if$0 \to A_{\mathfrak{p}} \to B_{\mathfrak{p}} \to C_{\mathfrak{p}} \to 0$ is exact for every prime ideal$\mathfrak{p} \subseteq R$ (where$M_{\mathfrak{p}} = M \otimes_R R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ denotes localization at$\mathfrak{p}$).

More broadly, a ring map $\phi: R \to S$ is called *flat* if$S$ is flat as an$R$-module; flat ring maps in algebraic geometry correspond to "nice" morphisms of schemes — those for which fibers vary continuously. The fundamental examples are: open immersions (corresponding to localizations$R \to S^{-1}R$), étale morphisms (local isomorphisms in the algebraic sense), and smooth morphisms. The lesson is that flatness is not merely a technical condition but the correct algebraic notion of "continuity" for families of modules, making it indispensable wherever geometry and algebra interact.

Flat base change for cohomology is especially powerful: if $\phi: R \to S$ is a flat ring map and$M$ is an$R$-module, then$\mathrm{Tor}_i^R(M, N) \otimes_R S \cong \mathrm{Tor}_i^S(M \otimes_R S, N \otimes_R S)$. This "base change theorem" allows one to compute Tor after extending scalars, and is a key tool in algebraic geometry for comparing cohomology groups along flat morphisms.

\begin{theorem}[Flat Base Change]
Let $\phi: R \to S$ be a flat ring map, and let$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ be an exact sequence of$R$-modules. Then$0 \to A \otimes_R S \to B \otimes_R S \to C \otimes_R S \to 0$ is an exact sequence of$S$-modules.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Localization at a prime: $R = \mathbb{Z}$,$S = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$. The exact sequence$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ base-changes to$0 \to \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} \to \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}/n\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} \to 0$. If$\gcd(n, p) = 1$, then$n$ is invertible in$\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ and$\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}/n\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = 0$.

**Example 2.** Completion: the inclusion $\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \hat{\mathbb{Z}}_p$ (p-adic completion) is flat. Therefore, any exact sequence of finitely generated$\mathbb{Z}$-modules remains exact after tensoring with$\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_p$.

**Example 3.** Polynomial extension: $R \to R[x]$ is flat (since$R[x]$ is free as an$R$-module with basis$\{1, x, x^2, \ldots\}$). Base changing any exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ along$R \to R[x]$ gives$0 \to A[x] \to B[x] \to C[x] \to 0$, which is exact since$-[x]$ is free.

**Example 4.** The local-global principle: to check that a map $f: M \to N$ of finitely generated$R$-modules (for Noetherian$R$) is an isomorphism, it suffices to check that$f_{\mathfrak{m}}: M_{\mathfrak{m}} \to N_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is an isomorphism for every maximal ideal$\mathfrak{m}$. This uses the flatness of all localizations$R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ over$R$.

**Example 5.** Faithfully flat descent: if $R \to S$ is a *faithfully flat* ring map (flat and$M \otimes_R S = 0 \Rightarrow M = 0$), then properties of$S$-modules descend to$R$-modules. For instance, if$M \otimes_R S$ is a projective$S$-module and$R \to S$ is faithfully flat, then$M$ is a projective$R$-module. This descent principle is fundamental in the construction of vector bundles in algebraic geometry via patching over a flat cover.
