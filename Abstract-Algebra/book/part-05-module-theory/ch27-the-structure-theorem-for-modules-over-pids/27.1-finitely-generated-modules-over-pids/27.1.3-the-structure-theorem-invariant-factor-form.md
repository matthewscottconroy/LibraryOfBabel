# 27.1.3 The Structure Theorem: Invariant Factor Form

With Smith normal form in hand, we can now read off the isomorphism type of any finitely generated module over a PID. Reducing the presentation matrix $A$ to Smith normal form$\mathrm{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_r, 0, \ldots, 0)$ by invertible row and column operations corresponds to choosing new bases for$R^n$ and$K$. In these new bases, the quotient$M = R^n / K$ decomposes as a direct sum of quotients of$R$ by the diagonal entries.

The *Structure Theorem for Finitely Generated Modules over a PID* states: every finitely generated $R$-module$M$ is isomorphic to a direct sum
$$M \cong R^{\oplus r} \oplus R/(d_1) \oplus R/(d_2) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(d_k),$$
where $r \geq 0$ is the *free rank* and$d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_k$ are nonzero non-units in$R$ satisfying the divisibility chain$d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$. The elements$d_1, \ldots, d_k$ are called the *invariant factors* of$M$. The free rank$r$ corresponds to the number of zero diagonal entries in the Smith normal form, while the invariant factors come from the nonzero diagonal entries.

Two finitely generated $R$-modules are isomorphic if and only if they have the same free rank and the same list of invariant factors (up to associates in$R$). In the case$R = \mathbb{Z}$, the invariant factors are positive integers$d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$, and the theorem reduces to the Fundamental Theorem of Finitely Generated Abelian Groups. In the case$R = F[x]$, the invariant factors are monic polynomials, and the theorem gives the rational canonical form of a linear operator.

To see how the Smith normal form gives the decomposition: if $PAQ = \mathrm{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_r, 0, \ldots, 0)$, then in the new bases$\{e'_1, \ldots, e'_n\} = \{Pe_1, \ldots, Pe_n\}$ for$R^n$ and$\{f'_1, \ldots, f'_m\} = \{Qf_1, \ldots, Qf_m\}$ for$K$, the inclusion$K \hookrightarrow R^n$ sends$f'_i \mapsto d_i e'_i$ (for$i \leq r$) and$f'_i \mapsto 0$ (for$i > r$). Therefore
$$M = R^n/K \cong \frac{Re'_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus Re'_n}{Rd_1e'_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus Rd_re'_r} \cong \frac{R}{(d_1)} \oplus \cdots \oplus \frac{R}{(d_r)} \oplus R^{n-r}.$$
Removing any unit invariant factors $d_i$ (since$R/(d_i) = 0$ when$d_i$ is a unit) and setting$k$ to the number of non-unit invariant factors gives the invariant factor form.

\begin{theorem}[Structure Theorem — Invariant Factor Form]
Let $R$ be a PID and$M$ a finitely generated$R$-module. Then$M \cong R^r \oplus R/(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(d_k)$ with$d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$, where$r$ and$d_1, \ldots, d_k$ (up to associates) are uniquely determined by$M$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Find the invariant factor form of $M = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ over$\mathbb{Z}$. The divisibility chain$2 \mid 6$ holds, so the invariant factors are already$d_1 = 2$,$d_2 = 6$ and$r = 0$. The module is$\mathbb{Z}/(2) \oplus \mathbb{Z}/(6)$.

**Example 2.** Find the invariant factor form of $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. We need$d_1 \mid d_2$, but$4 \nmid 6$ and$6 \nmid 4$. The Smith normal form of the presentation matrix$\mathrm{diag}(4, 6)$ is computed by noting that$\gcd(4, 6) = 2$ and$\mathrm{lcm}(4, 6) = 12$: the invariant factors are$d_1 = 2$ and$d_2 = 12$. So$\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 3.** Over $\mathbb{Z}$:$M = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$. The presentation matrix is$\mathrm{diag}(2, 2, 4)$. Chain:$2 \mid 2 \mid 4$? Yes! So the invariant factors are$d_1 = 2$,$d_2 = 2$,$d_3 = 4$.

**Example 4.** Over $F[x]$ (for field$F$): Let$M = F[x]/(x^2) \oplus F[x]/(x^3)$. The divisibility chain$(x^2) \mid (x^3)$? We have$x^2 \mid x^3$, yes. So invariant factors are$d_1 = x^2$,$d_2 = x^3$.

**Example 5.** Construct the module with invariant factors $d_1 = 2$,$d_2 = 6$, free rank$r = 1$ over$\mathbb{Z}$:$M = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. This has$|M_{\mathrm{tors}}| = 2 \cdot 6 = 12$ and free rank 1. The presentation matrix (after extracting the torsion part) is$\mathrm{diag}(2, 6)$.
