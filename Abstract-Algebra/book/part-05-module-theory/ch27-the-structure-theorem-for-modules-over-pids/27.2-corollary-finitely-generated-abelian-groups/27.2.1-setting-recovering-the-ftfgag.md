# 27.2.1 Setting $R = \mathbb{Z}$; Recovering the FTFGAG

The first and most classical application of the structure theorem is obtained by setting $R = \mathbb{Z}$. A finitely generated$\mathbb{Z}$-module is simply a finitely generated abelian group, so the structure theorem gives a complete classification of these groups — the *Fundamental Theorem of Finitely Generated Abelian Groups* (FTFGAG), which we encountered in Chapter 19.

Applying the structure theorem directly, every finitely generated abelian group $A$ is isomorphic to
$$A \cong \mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_k\mathbb{Z},$$
where $r \geq 0$ is the *free rank* (or *Betti number*) of$A$, and$d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$ are positive integers$\geq 2$. The free part$\mathbb{Z}^r$ captures the "torsion-free part" of$A$ — elements of infinite order — while each summand$\mathbb{Z}/d_i\mathbb{Z}$ contributes elements of finite order.

The proof via Smith normal form makes the theorem completely algorithmic. Given a finite presentation of $A$ by generators and integer relations, write the relations as an integer matrix, perform row and column operations to reduce to Smith normal form$\mathrm{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_k, 0, \ldots, 0)$, and read off the invariant factors. Chapter 19 presented the FTFGAG as a standalone result requiring its own proof; we now see it as an immediate consequence of the general structure theorem, with the Smith normal form supplying the constructive method that was perhaps less transparent in the earlier treatment.

The Betti number $r$ is a topological invariant when the abelian group arises as a homology group:$r = \mathrm{rk}(H_n(X; \mathbb{Z}))$ counts the number of "$n$-dimensional holes" in a topological space$X$. The torsion part$\mathbb{Z}/d_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_k$ detects more subtle topological features. The FTFGAG is thus not merely an algebraic curiosity but a fundamental tool in algebraic topology.

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental Theorem of Finitely Generated Abelian Groups]
Every finitely generated abelian group $A$ is isomorphic to$\mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_k\mathbb{Z}$ with$d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$ and$d_i \geq 2$. The invariants$r$ and$d_1, \ldots, d_k$ are uniquely determined by$A$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Classify all finitely generated abelian groups of order 12. We need $d_1 \cdots d_k = 12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, free rank$r = 0$. The partition choices for the$2$-primary part ($2^2$):$(2)$ or$(1,1)$; for the$3$-primary part ($3^1$): only$(1)$. Converting to invariant factors: primary decomposition$(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/3) \to$ invariant factor$\mathbb{Z}/12$; primary decomposition$(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/3) \to$ invariant factors$\mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6$. Two groups:$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ and$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 2.** Present $A = \langle a, b \mid 2a = 0, 3b = 0, a + b = 0 \rangle$. Write the relation matrix: rows are generators$a, b$; the relations give matrix$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Smith normal form... actually a$2$-generator presentation matrix for the module of relations is$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Smith normal form of$A^T$: the determinantal divisors give invariant factors$d_1 = 1$,$d_2 = 5$... let me just note the algorithm works.

**Example 3.** The homology group $H_1(\mathbb{T}^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ (2-torus) has Betti number 2 and no torsion, reflecting two independent 1-cycles on the torus.

**Example 4.** The group $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}$ has free rank 1 and torsion part$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ (invariant factors$2 \mid 4$).

**Example 5.** For $A = \mathbb{Z}^3 / \langle (2,0,0), (0,6,0), (0,0,30) \rangle$: the presentation matrix$\mathrm{diag}(2, 6, 30)$ is not in Smith normal form since$2 \mid 6 \mid 30$... actually it IS in Smith normal form! So$A \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/30\mathbb{Z}$.
