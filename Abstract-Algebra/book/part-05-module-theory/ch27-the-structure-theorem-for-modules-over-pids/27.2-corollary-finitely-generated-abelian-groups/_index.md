# 27.2 Corollary: Finitely Generated Abelian Groups

Setting $R = \mathbb{Z}$ in the structure theorem immediately yields the Fundamental Theorem of Finitely Generated Abelian Groups: every finitely generated abelian group $G$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/n_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/n_k\mathbb{Z}$ for a unique rank $r \geq 0$ and unique integers $n_1 \mid n_2 \mid \cdots \mid n_k \geq 2$. This result, first proved in its finite form by Kronecker and completed in generality by Frobenius and Stickelberger, now appears as a special case of a single theorem about modules. The torsion subgroup of $G$ is $\mathbb{Z}/n_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/n_k\mathbb{Z}$, the free part is $\mathbb{Z}^r$, and the two parts fit together as a direct sum — not just an extension, but a split extension — because the free part is projective.

The elementary divisor form of the same classification rewrites each cyclic piece $\mathbb{Z}/n_i\mathbb{Z}$ as a product of cyclic groups of prime power order using the Chinese Remainder Theorem. So a finite abelian group of order $p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_s^{a_s}$ decomposes as $G \cong G_{p_1} \oplus \cdots \oplus G_{p_s}$, where each $G_{p_j}$ is the $p_j$-primary component of $G$ — a direct sum of cyclic groups $\mathbb{Z}/p_j^{a_{j,i}}\mathbb{Z}$. The classification of finite abelian groups is therefore equivalent to the classification, for each prime $p$, of the possible partitions of the $p$-power component. A partition $a_{j,1} \geq a_{j,2} \geq \cdots \geq a_{j,l} \geq 1$ with $\sum a_{j,i} = a_j$ describes the $p_j$-primary component. The number of nonisomorphic abelian groups of a given order $n$ is the product over all primes $p \mid n$ of the number of partitions of $v_p(n)$ (the $p$-adic valuation).

The practical algorithm for applying this corollary begins with a presentation: write $G = \mathbb{Z}^n / \text{im}(A)$ for an integer matrix $A$, compute its Smith normal form $\text{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_r, 0, \ldots, 0)$, and read off the group $\mathbb{Z}/d_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_r\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}^{n-r}$. This reduces the classification of any finitely presented abelian group to a computation with integer matrices — a fully algorithmic procedure. The classification result proved here will be applied in Chapter 33 to understand the structure of the class group and unit group of a number ring, and again in Part VI when homology groups of spaces are computed as finitely generated abelian groups.
