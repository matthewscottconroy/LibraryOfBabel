# 27.3.1 The $F[x]$-Module Structure on a Vector Space with Operator

The second major application of the structure theorem comes from the polynomial ring $F[x]$, where$F$ is a field. Recall from Section 25.1.3 that a finite-dimensional$F$-vector space$V$ equipped with a linear operator$T: V \to V$ becomes an$F[x]$-module by defining$f \cdot v = f(T)(v)$: the polynomial$f$ acts by applying$f$ evaluated at the operator$T$. Two pairs$(V, T)$ and$(V', T')$ give isomorphic$F[x]$-modules if and only if there is an invertible$F$-linear map$\phi: V \to V'$ with$\phi \circ T = T' \circ \phi$ — that is, if and only if$T$ and$T'$ are *similar* (conjugate in the group$\mathrm{GL}(V)$).

Since $F[x]$ is a PID and$V$ is finitely generated as an$F[x]$-module (any basis of$V$ as an$F$-vector space generates it as an$F[x]$-module), the structure theorem applies. We conclude that$V \cong F[x]/(f_1) \oplus F[x]/(f_2) \oplus \cdots \oplus F[x]/(f_k)$ as$F[x]$-modules, where$f_1 \mid f_2 \mid \cdots \mid f_k$ are monic polynomials in$F[x]$ — the *invariant factors* of$T$. Notice that the free rank must be zero:$F[x]$ itself is infinite-dimensional over$F$, but$V$ is finite-dimensional, so no free summands$F[x]$ can appear.

The largest invariant factor $f_k$ is called the *minimal polynomial* of$T$, and the product$f_1 f_2 \cdots f_k$ is the *characteristic polynomial* of$T$ (up to sign). Both are intrinsic invariants of the similarity class of$T$, and their factorizations encode the eigenvalue and Jordan block structure of$T$. The module-theoretic framework thus reveals that the minimal polynomial and characteristic polynomial are not ad hoc constructions but natural invariants arising from the structure theorem.

Why must the free rank be zero? If $V \cong F[x]^r \oplus \bigoplus_i F[x]/(f_i)$, then the$F$-dimension of$V$ is$\mathrm{dim}_F(V)$. The dimension of$F[x]$ over$F$ is infinite (basis$\{1, x, x^2, \ldots\}$), so any free summand would make$V$ infinite-dimensional. Since$V$ is finite-dimensional,$r = 0$ necessarily.

The passage from $(V, T)$ to the$F[x]$-module$V$ is functorial: a$T$-equivariant map$V \to V'$ (i.e.,$\phi T = T' \phi$) is precisely an$F[x]$-module map. Thus the category of finite-dimensional$F$-vector spaces with an operator is equivalent to the category of finitely generated$F[x]$-modules with no free part — a beautiful categorical translation that makes the structure theorem directly applicable.

\begin{theorem}
A finite-dimensional $F$-vector space$V$ with a linear operator$T$ corresponds to a finitely generated torsion$F[x]$-module. The isomorphism type of this module (i.e., the invariant factors) classifies$T$ up to similarity.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Let $V = \mathbb{R}^2$ and$T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (rotation by$90°$). The minimal polynomial of$T$ is$x^2 + 1$ (since$T^2 = -I$, so$(T^2 + I)v = 0$ for all$v$, and no degree-1 polynomial annihilates$T$). Since$\dim V = 2 = \deg(x^2 + 1)$, there is one invariant factor$f_1 = x^2 + 1$, and$V \cong \mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1)$ as$\mathbb{R}[x]$-modules.

**Example 2.** Let $V = F^3$ and$T$ have minimal polynomial$x^2 - 1$ and characteristic polynomial$(x-1)^2(x+1)$. Since$x^2 - 1 = (x-1)(x+1)$ divides the characteristic polynomial and the characteristic polynomial has degree 3, the invariant factors satisfy$f_1 f_2 = (x-1)^2(x+1)$ and$f_1 \mid f_2$,$f_2 = x^2 - 1$. Then$f_1 = x - 1$ (since$f_1 \mid f_2 = x^2-1$ and$f_1 f_2 = (x-1)^2(x+1)$ gives$f_1 = (x-1)^2(x+1)/(x^2-1) = x-1$). So$V \cong F[x]/(x-1) \oplus F[x]/(x^2-1)$.

**Example 3.** Let $T: F^4 \to F^4$ have characteristic polynomial$(x-\lambda)^4$ and minimal polynomial$(x-\lambda)^2$. The invariant factors are polynomials dividing$(x-\lambda)^2$ whose product is$(x-\lambda)^4$: we need$f_1 f_2 = (x-\lambda)^4$ and$f_1 \mid f_2 \mid (x-\lambda)^2$. So$f_1 = (x-\lambda)^2$ and$f_2 = (x-\lambda)^2$, giving$V \cong F[x]/((x-\lambda)^2) \oplus F[x]/((x-\lambda)^2)$.

**Example 4.** For $T = I_n$ (the identity), the minimal polynomial is$x - 1$ (since$(T - I)v = 0$ for all$v$) and the characteristic polynomial is$(x-1)^n$. The invariant factors are all$f_i = x - 1$:$V \cong F[x]/(x-1) \oplus \cdots \oplus F[x]/(x-1)$ ($n$ copies). This corresponds to$n$ Jordan blocks of size 1 with eigenvalue 1.

**Example 5.** For the nilpotent operator $N: F^3 \to F^3$ given by$Ne_1 = e_2$,$Ne_2 = e_3$,$Ne_3 = 0$ (a single Jordan block), the minimal polynomial equals the characteristic polynomial:$x^3$. So there is one invariant factor$f_1 = x^3$, and$V \cong F[x]/(x^3)$ as$F[x]$-modules.
