# 27.3 Corollary: Rational Canonical Form

The second great application of the structure theorem comes from setting $R = F[x]$ for a field $F$. An $F[x]$-module structure on a finite-dimensional $F$-vector space $V$ amounts to a choice of linear operator $T : V \to V$ — the action of $x$ on $V$ — with the rest of the $F[x]$-action determined by $f(x) \cdot v = f(T)(v)$. Since $F[x]$ is a PID, the structure theorem applies: $V$ decomposes as an $F[x]$-module into a direct sum of cyclic pieces $F[x]/(f_1(x)) \oplus \cdots \oplus F[x]/(f_k(x))$, with $f_1 \mid f_2 \mid \cdots \mid f_k$ monic polynomials in $F[x]$. The summand $F[x]/(f_i(x))$ is a vector space of dimension $\deg f_i$ over $F$, and on it $T$ acts by the companion matrix $C(f_i)$ — the matrix with $1$s on the subdiagonal and the negated coefficients of $f_i$ in the last column. The resulting block diagonal matrix $\text{diag}(C(f_1), \ldots, C(f_k))$ is the rational canonical form of $T$.

The polynomial $f_k$ is the minimal polynomial of $T$ (since $F[x]/(f_k)$ contains the cyclic summand on which $T$ acts with the largest invariant factor), and the product $f_1 f_2 \cdots f_k$ is the characteristic polynomial (since the determinant of $xI - T$ equals the product of the determinants $xI - C(f_i)$, each of which is $f_i$). The rational canonical form is "rational" in the sense that it is defined over $F$ itself, without requiring any field extension: the companion matrices are built entirely from the coefficients of the $f_i$, which are polynomials over $F$. Two operators $T$ and $T'$ on $V$ are similar — conjugate under $\text{GL}(V)$ — if and only if they have the same rational canonical form, which happens if and only if their $F[x]$-module structures are isomorphic, which by the structure theorem is equivalent to having the same invariant factors.

When $F$ is algebraically closed (or more generally, when the minimal polynomial of $T$ splits completely over $F$), each invariant factor $f_i$ factors into linear pieces $(x - \lambda)^{a_{i,j}}$, and the primary decomposition of the $F[x]$-module $V$ gives the Jordan decomposition: $V \cong \bigoplus_\lambda \bigoplus_j F[x]/((x-\lambda)^{a_{i,j}})$, where each $F[x]/((x-\lambda)^a)$ is a Jordan block of size $a$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$. Jordan normal form is therefore not a separate theorem but the primary decomposition form of the structure theorem applied to $F[x]$-modules over a splitting field. The structure theorem unifies rational canonical form and Jordan form into a single classification principle, revealing that the theory of canonical forms in linear algebra is a chapter in the theory of modules over PIDs.
