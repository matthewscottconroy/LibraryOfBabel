## Examples, Applications, and Thought Experiments

- **Rational canonical form via $F[x]$-modules** — give a vector space $V$ the structure of an $F[x]$-module by $f(x) \cdot v := f(T)(v)$ for a linear operator $T$; the structure theorem decomposes $V$ into cyclic $F[x]$-modules; the minimal polynomial of each cyclic summand gives the invariant factors; this is the rational canonical form
- **Jordan normal form as primary decomposition** — over a field where the characteristic polynomial factors completely, the primary decomposition $(x-\lambda)^k$ gives the Jordan blocks; the structure theorem over $F[x]/(x-\lambda)^k$ (a PID) classifies these
- **Smith normal form in action** — the integer matrix with rows $(4, 2)$ and $(2, 4)$: Smith normal form gives $\text{diag}(2, 6)$; the $\mathbb{Z}$-module presented by this matrix is $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$; this is a concrete computation of a finitely generated abelian group from a presentation matrix
- **The universality of the theorem** — all classification theorems in the chapters on abelian groups and linear algebra are the same theorem in different disguises; the "one ring to rule them all" is the PID, and the structure theorem is its master result; once you understand it for $R = \mathbb{Z}$, you understand Jordan forms for free

## Exercises

1. Use the structure theorem to classify all finitely generated abelian groups of order $360 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$. List all isomorphism classes in both invariant factor form and elementary divisor form. How many distinct groups are there?

2. Let $A$ be the integer matrix with rows $(6, 4)$ and $(3, 3)$. Compute the Smith normal form of $A$ by performing row and column operations over $\mathbb{Z}$. Identify the invariant factors, and write down the finitely generated abelian group presented by $A$ (i.e., $\mathbb{Z}^2 / \text{im}(A)$) as a direct sum of cyclic groups.

3. A finitely generated abelian group $G$ has invariant factor form $G \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$. Verify that the divisibility condition $2 \mid 6 \mid 12$ holds. Convert to elementary divisor form, and determine the number of elements of order $4$ in $G$.

4. Let $T : \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^4$ be a linear operator with characteristic polynomial $c_T(x) = (x-2)^2(x+1)^2$. If the minimal polynomial of $T$ is $m_T(x) = (x-2)(x+1)$, determine the rational canonical form of $T$ and write down the invariant factors of the $\mathbb{R}[x]$-module $\mathbb{R}^4$.

5. Let $T : \mathbb{C}^4 \to \mathbb{C}^4$ have characteristic polynomial $(x - 3)^4$ and minimal polynomial $(x-3)^2$. Determine all possible Jordan forms for $T$. For each Jordan form, write down the corresponding invariant factors and the elementary divisors of the $\mathbb{C}[x]$-module structure on $\mathbb{C}^4$.

6. Two $n \times n$ matrices $A$ and $B$ over a field $F$ are similar if and only if they define isomorphic $F[x]$-modules. Use this principle to show that the matrix $A$ with rows $(0, -1)$ and $(1, 2)$ is similar over $\mathbb{Q}$ to the matrix $B$ with rows $(1, 1)$ and $(0, 1)$, by computing the invariant factors of $xI - A$ and $xI - B$ via Smith normal form over $\mathbb{Q}[x]$.

7. Prove uniqueness of the invariant factor decomposition: if $M \cong R/(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(d_k) \cong R/(e_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(e_l)$ with $d_i \mid d_{i+1}$ and $e_j \mid e_{j+1}$, then $k = l$ and $d_i$ and $e_i$ are associates for each $i$. The key tool is the fact that the $i$-th Fitting ideal (generated by the $(n-i+1) \times (n-i+1)$ minors of a presentation matrix) is an isomorphism invariant.

8. (Challenge) A finitely generated module $M$ over a PID $R$ is torsion-free if and only if it is free. Prove one direction: every finitely generated torsion-free module over a PID is free. (Hint: use induction on the number of generators, and at each step find a generator whose annihilator is trivial, then show the complementary summand is also torsion-free and apply the inductive hypothesis.)
