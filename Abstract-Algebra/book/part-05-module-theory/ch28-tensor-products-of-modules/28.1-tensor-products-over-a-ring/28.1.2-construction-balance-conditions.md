# 28.1.2 Construction; Balance Conditions

The universal property characterizes $M \otimes_R N$ uniquely up to isomorphism, but existence requires an explicit construction. We build$M \otimes_R N$ as a quotient of a free abelian group.

Let $F(M \times N)$ be the free abelian group on the set$M \times N$: its generators are formal symbols$(m, n)$ for each pair$(m, n) \in M \times N$, with no relations yet imposed. Let$K$ be the subgroup of$F(M \times N)$ generated by the following elements, which encode bilinearity and balance:

- $(m + m', n) - (m, n) - (m', n)$ for all$m, m' \in M$,$n \in N$;
- $(m, n + n') - (m, n) - (m, n')$ for all$m \in M$,$n, n' \in N$;
- $(mr, n) - (m, rn)$ for all$m \in M$,$n \in N$,$r \in R$.

The tensor product is then $M \otimes_R N = F(M \times N)/K$, and the image of$(m, n)$ in this quotient is written$m \otimes n$.

The first two families of relations impose bilinearity: $(m + m') \otimes n = m \otimes n + m' \otimes n$ and$m \otimes (n + n') = m \otimes n + m \otimes n'$. The third family is the *balance condition*:$mr \otimes n = m \otimes rn$. This last relation is what distinguishes the tensor product over$R$ from the tensor product over$\mathbb{Z}$: over$\mathbb{Z}$, there is no ring action to balance, so all$(m, n)$ with their bilinear relations suffice. Over$R$, the balance condition "identifies" the two ways of incorporating the scalar$r$, ensuring that the ring structure is respected.

One can verify directly that any $R$-balanced map$f: M \times N \to A$ factors uniquely through the quotient: define$\bar{f}: F(M \times N) \to A$ by$\bar{f}((m, n)) = f(m, n)$ on generators and extend linearly. The relations defining$K$ are exactly the conditions that$\bar{f}$ vanishes on$K$, since$f$ is$R$-balanced. Hence$\bar{f}$ descends to a well-defined map on the quotient$M \otimes_R N$, confirming the universal property.

The balance condition also implies: $0 \otimes n = 0$ (from$0 \otimes n = (0 + 0) \otimes n - 0 \otimes n$ implies$0 \otimes n = 0$), and$m \otimes 0 = 0$. Also,$(-m) \otimes n = -(m \otimes n)$ and$m \otimes (-n) = -(m \otimes n)$. These follow from the additive group structure.

\begin{construction}
$M \otimes_R N = F(M \times N) / K$ where$K$ is generated by the bilinearity and balance relations listed above.
\end{construction}

**Example 1.** $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. By balance:$1 \otimes 1 = 1 \otimes (1 + 1 + 1) - 1 \otimes (1 + 1) = 3(1 \otimes 1) - 2(1 \otimes 1) = 1 \otimes 1$; also$2(1 \otimes 1) = (1+1) \otimes 1 = 0 \otimes 1 = 0$ (since$2 = 0$ in$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$), and$3(1 \otimes 1) = 1 \otimes 3 = 1 \otimes 0 = 0$ (since$3 = 0$ in$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$). From$2(1\otimes 1) = 0$ and$3(1 \otimes 1) = 0$:$\gcd(2,3) = 1$ divides$1$, so$1 \otimes 1 = 0$. The tensor product is$0$.

**Example 2.** $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. By the formula$\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$:$\gcd(4,6) = 2$, so the tensor product is$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The generator is$1 \otimes 1$, with relation$2(1 \otimes 1) = 2 \otimes 1 = 0$ (since$2 \cdot 2 = 4 = 0$ in$\mathbb{Z}/4$).

**Example 3.** $\mathbb{Q} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$. Any pure tensor$\frac{a}{b} \otimes \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd} \otimes 1$ (by balance). So every element is of the form$q \otimes 1$ for$q \in \mathbb{Q}$, and the map$q \mapsto q \otimes 1$ is an isomorphism$\mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Q} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$.

**Example 4.** $R \otimes_R M \cong M$ for any left$R$-module$M$. The balanced map$R \times M \to M$,$(r, m) \mapsto rm$, is$R$-balanced ($(r \cdot s) \times m \mapsto rsm = r \cdot (sm)$). By the universal property, this factors uniquely through$R \otimes_R M \to M$. Conversely, the map$m \mapsto 1 \otimes m$ is an inverse. So$1 \otimes m \leftrightarrow m$.

**Example 5.** $k[x] \otimes_{k} k[y] \cong k[x, y]$ (for a field$k$). The balanced map$k[x] \times k[y] \to k[x,y]$ sending$(f, g) \mapsto f(x) g(y)$ is$k$-bilinear. By the universal property, it induces a map$k[x] \otimes_k k[y] \to k[x,y]$; the inverse sends$x^i y^j \mapsto x^i \otimes y^j$. The monomials$\{x^i \otimes y^j\}$ form a basis for the tensor product, confirming the isomorphism.
