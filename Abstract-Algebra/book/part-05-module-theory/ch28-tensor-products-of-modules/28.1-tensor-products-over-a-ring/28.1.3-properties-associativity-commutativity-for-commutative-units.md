# 28.1.3 Properties: Associativity, Commutativity (for Commutative $R$), Units

Having constructed the tensor product, we now identify the structural properties that make it behave like a product operation in the category of modules.

For a commutative ring $R$ (in which left and right modules coincide), the tensor product satisfies three fundamental properties. First, *commutativity*:$M \otimes_R N \cong N \otimes_R M$ via the map$m \otimes n \mapsto n \otimes m$. This is an isomorphism of$R$-modules, not merely abelian groups, when$R$ is commutative. Second, *associativity*:$(M \otimes_R N) \otimes_R P \cong M \otimes_R (N \otimes_R P)$ naturally in all three modules. Third, *unitality*:$R \otimes_R M \cong M$ and$M \otimes_R R \cong M$ via$r \otimes m \mapsto rm$ and$m \otimes r \mapsto mr$ respectively.

These three isomorphisms together say that the category $\mathrm{Mod}_R$ of$R$-modules, equipped with the tensor product$\otimes_R$ and the unit object$R$, is a *symmetric monoidal category*. This is a categorical structure with wide-ranging applications: the theory of Hopf algebras, quantum groups, and braided monoidal categories all arise by asking what additional structure a symmetric monoidal category can carry.

For non-commutative $R$, commutativity fails:$M \otimes_R N$ and$N \otimes_R M$ may not be isomorphic, and may not even be defined as modules over the same ring (since$M \otimes_R N$ is an abelian group, or a module over the center of$R$, but not in general a left or right$R$-module in a natural way). The careful bookkeeping of left and right module structures is one of the hallmarks of non-commutative algebra, and the tensor product is where this care is most essential.

Additional important properties: tensor distributes over direct sums, $M \otimes_R (\bigoplus_\alpha N_\alpha) \cong \bigoplus_\alpha (M \otimes_R N_\alpha)$, since$\otimes$ is a left adjoint functor (Section 28.3) and left adjoints preserve colimits, in particular coproducts. This distribution law is used constantly in computations: to compute a tensor product, decompose one factor into cyclic modules and tensor each piece separately.

\begin{proposition}
For $R$ commutative and$R$-modules$M$,$N$,$P$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Commutativity) $M \otimes_R N \cong N \otimes_R M$.
\item (Associativity) $(M \otimes_R N) \otimes_R P \cong M \otimes_R (N \otimes_R P)$.
\item (Unit) $R \otimes_R M \cong M \cong M \otimes_R R$.
\item (Distribution) $M \otimes_R (N \oplus P) \cong (M \otimes_R N) \oplus (M \otimes_R P)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

**Example 1.** Commutativity: $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m$. The isomorphism sends$a \otimes b \mapsto b \otimes a$ (well-defined on the quotient).

**Example 2.** Associativity: $(\mathbb{Z}/4 \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/2) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(2,3) = 0$. Alternatively,$\mathbb{Z}/4 \otimes (\mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}/3) = \mathbb{Z}/4 \otimes 0 = 0$. Same result.

**Example 3.** Unit: $\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} M \cong M$ for any abelian group$M$: the map$n \otimes m \mapsto nm$ is an isomorphism.

**Example 4.** Distribution: $M \otimes_R (R \oplus N) \cong (M \otimes_R R) \oplus (M \otimes_R N) \cong M \oplus (M \otimes_R N)$.

**Example 5.** Tensor product of algebras: if $A$ and$B$ are commutative$R$-algebras, then$A \otimes_R B$ is again an$R$-algebra via$(a \otimes b)(a' \otimes b') = aa' \otimes bb'$. For example,$k[x] \otimes_k k[y] \cong k[x,y]$ as$k$-algebras. More generally,$R[x] \otimes_R R[y] \cong R[x,y]$, showing that polynomial ring in two variables is the "tensor product" of polynomial rings in one variable each.
