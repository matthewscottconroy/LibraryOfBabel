# 28.1.4 Base Change: $M \otimes_R S$ for a Ring Map$R \to S$

One of the most important uses of the tensor product is *base change* or *extension of scalars*: starting from an $R$-module$M$ and a ring map$\phi: R \to S$, we produce an$S$-module$M \otimes_R S$ by "replacing the ground ring$R$ with$S$."

The construction is as follows. The ring map $\phi$ makes$S$ into an$(R, S)$-bimodule: the right$S$-action is multiplication in$S$, and the left$R$-action is$r \cdot s = \phi(r)s$. The tensor product$M \otimes_R S$ then inherits a right$S$-module structure via$(m \otimes s) \cdot s' = m \otimes (ss')$, making it a left$S$-module when$S$ is commutative. The natural map$M \to M \otimes_R S$ sending$m \mapsto m \otimes 1_S$ is$R$-linear and provides the canonical passage from$M$ to its scalar extension.

The key examples illustrate how powerfully base change transforms modules. Tensoring $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with$\mathbb{Q}$ over$\mathbb{Z}$ gives$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} = 0$, because the torsion element$1 \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ satisfies$1 \otimes q = 1 \otimes \frac{1}{n}(nq) = \frac{n \cdot 1}{n} \otimes q = \frac{0}{n} \otimes q = 0$; torsion vanishes over$\mathbb{Q}$. Complexification gives$\mathbb{Z}[i] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R} \cong \mathbb{C}$: the Gaussian integers become the complex numbers upon tensoring with$\mathbb{R}$. Localization fits into this framework as well: the localization$M_P = M \otimes_R R_P$ at a prime$P$ is a base change along the ring map$R \to R_P$.

Base change is functorial: if $f: M \to N$ is an$R$-linear map and$\phi: R \to S$ is a ring map, then$f \otimes 1_S: M \otimes_R S \to N \otimes_R S$ is$S$-linear. This makes$- \otimes_R S$ into a functor from$R$-modules to$S$-modules, called the *extension of scalars* functor. It has a right adjoint, the *restriction of scalars* functor that regards an$S$-module as an$R$-module via$\phi$.

\begin{proposition}
Extension of scalars $- \otimes_R S: \mathrm{Mod}_R \to \mathrm{Mod}_S$ is left adjoint to restriction of scalars$\mathrm{Res}^S_R: \mathrm{Mod}_S \to \mathrm{Mod}_R$.
\end{proposition}

**Example 1.** Torsion vanishes over $\mathbb{Q}$: for any$n \geq 1$,$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} = 0$. Proof:$\bar{1} \otimes q = \bar{1} \otimes \frac{1}{n}(nq) = n\bar{1} \otimes \frac{q}{n} = 0 \otimes \frac{q}{n} = 0$. Geometrically: localization kills torsion sheaves.

**Example 2.** Complexification: $\mathbb{R}^n \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{C}^n$. The map$v \otimes z \mapsto zv$ (where$v$ is viewed in$\mathbb{C}^n$) is an isomorphism of$\mathbb{C}$-modules.

**Example 3.** Gaussian integers: $\mathbb{Z}[i] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R} \cong \mathbb{C}$. The map$\alpha \otimes r \mapsto r\alpha$ is an$\mathbb{R}$-algebra isomorphism from$\mathbb{Z}[i] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R}$ to$\mathbb{C}$ (since$\mathbb{Z}[i]$ generates$\mathbb{C}$ as an$\mathbb{R}$-algebra via$i \mapsto i$).

**Example 4.** Localization as base change: $M_p = M \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ for a$\mathbb{Z}$-module$M$. For$M = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$:$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}_{(2)} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (the$2$-torsion part, since$3$ becomes invertible in$\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}$ and$3 \cdot \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$-relations force the$3$-torsion to vanish).

**Example 5.** Splitting a polynomial: $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2 - 2) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})[x]/(x^2-2) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \times \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ (since$x^2 - 2 = (x - \sqrt{2})(x + \sqrt{2})$ splits over$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, and CRT gives the product). Base change to a splitting field splits the algebra.
