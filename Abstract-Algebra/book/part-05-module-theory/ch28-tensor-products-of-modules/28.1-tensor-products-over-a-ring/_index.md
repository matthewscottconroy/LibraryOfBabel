# 28.1 Tensor Products over a Ring

The central question of this section is: how does one linearize a bilinear map? Given a right $R$-module $M$ and a left $R$-module $N$, a bilinear map (or balanced map) $b : M \times N \to P$ is a function into an abelian group $P$ that is additive in each variable and satisfies the balance condition $b(mr, n) = b(m, rn)$. This condition ensures compatibility with the $R$-structure, collapsing the two ways to move $r$ across the pair $(m, n)$. The tensor product $M \otimes_R N$ is the universal recipient of such bilinear maps: there is a bilinear map $\otimes : M \times N \to M \otimes_R N$ sending $(m, n)$ to the pure tensor $m \otimes n$, and every bilinear map $b : M \times N \to P$ factors uniquely as $b = \tilde{b} \circ \otimes$ for a unique group homomorphism $\tilde{b} : M \otimes_R N \to P$. This universal property defines $M \otimes_R N$ up to isomorphism; the concrete construction as a quotient of the free abelian group $\mathbb{Z}[M \times N]$ by the relations encoding bilinearity and balance proves existence.

Pure tensors $m \otimes n$ generate $M \otimes_R N$ as an abelian group, but not every element is a pure tensor — a general element is a finite sum $\sum_i m_i \otimes n_i$. This means one cannot define a map from $M \otimes_R N$ by saying what it does on pure tensors alone without verifying well-definedness against the relations; the universal property is the correct tool, which requires specifying a bilinear map on $M \times N$ and checking the balance condition. Key computational rules follow directly from the relations: $m \otimes (n + n') = m \otimes n + m \otimes n'$, $(m + m') \otimes n = m \otimes n + m' \otimes n$, and $mr \otimes n = m \otimes rn$. These relations mean, for instance, that $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$, since $m \cdot (a \otimes b) = ma \otimes b = 0 \otimes b = 0$ and symmetrically $n \cdot (a \otimes b) = 0$.

When $R$ is commutative, $M \otimes_R N$ inherits an $R$-module structure from either factor (both give the same result because of commutativity), and the tensor product becomes a symmetric monoidal operation on the category of $R$-modules: there are natural isomorphisms $M \otimes_R N \cong N \otimes_R M$, $(M \otimes_R N) \otimes_R P \cong M \otimes_R (N \otimes_R P)$, and $R \otimes_R M \cong M$. The operation of base change — given a ring map $R \to S$, the $S$-module $S \otimes_R M$ — is one of the most important applications: it extends scalars from $R$ to $S$ and is the algebraic analogue of pulling back a bundle along a map of base spaces. These structural properties make the tensor product a fundamental operation in algebraic geometry, commutative algebra, and representation theory.
