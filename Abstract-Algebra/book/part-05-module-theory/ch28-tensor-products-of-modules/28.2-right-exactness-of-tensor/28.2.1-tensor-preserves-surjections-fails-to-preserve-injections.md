# 28.2.1 Tensor Preserves Surjections; Fails to Preserve Injections

Having constructed the tensor product and established its basic properties, we now ask how it interacts with exact sequences. This question is central to homological algebra and determines which algebraic properties can be "tensored through."

For any module $M$ over$R$, the functor$- \otimes_R M$ is *right exact*: if$A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ is an exact sequence of modules, then the tensored sequence$A \otimes M \xrightarrow{f \otimes 1} B \otimes M \xrightarrow{g \otimes 1} C \otimes M \to 0$ is also exact. In particular, surjections are preserved: if$g: B \to C$ is surjective, so is$g \otimes 1: B \otimes M \to C \otimes M$. The proof uses the universal property: the surjection$g$ allows us to factor any$R$-balanced map out of$B \times M$ through$C \times M$.

However, injections are not generally preserved. The archetypal example over $\mathbb{Z}$: the inclusion$2\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}$ is injective, but tensoring with$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ gives the map$2\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which is the zero map from$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ to$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ — since$2 \otimes \bar{1} = 1 \otimes 2\bar{1} = 1 \otimes 0 = 0$ in the tensor product. An injection becomes the zero map, so injectivity is dramatically lost. The failure of$- \otimes_R M$ to be left-exact is measured by the derived functor$\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(-, M)$, the first Tor group, which captures the obstruction to preserving injections.

The intuition for why tensor preserves surjections: if every element of $C$ is the image of some element of$B$, then every pure tensor$c \otimes m$ in$C \otimes M$ is the image of some$b \otimes m$ under$g \otimes 1$. Since pure tensors generate the tensor product, the map$g \otimes 1$ is surjective. The intuition for why tensor fails to preserve injections: an injection$A \hookrightarrow B$ does not prevent elements of$A \otimes M$ from "collapsing" in$B \otimes M$ when the ring action of$R$ creates new relations — specifically, torsion in$M$ can kill injective maps.

\begin{theorem}[Right Exactness of Tensor]
For any $R$-module$M$, the functor$- \otimes_R M$ is right exact: it preserves exact sequences of the form$A \to B \to C \to 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Given $B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ exact (i.e.,$g$ surjective): for any$c \otimes m \in C \otimes M$, write$c = g(b)$, so$c \otimes m = g(b) \otimes m = (g \otimes 1)(b \otimes m)$. Hence$g \otimes 1$ is surjective. For exactness at$B \otimes M$: if$(g \otimes 1)(b \otimes m) = g(b) \otimes m = 0$, this means... one shows$\ker(g \otimes 1) = \mathrm{im}(f \otimes 1)$ using the universal property.
\end{proof}

**Example 1.** Surjections preserved: $\mathbb{Z} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. Tensor with$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$:$\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/6 \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/3 \otimes \mathbb{Z}/6$, i.e.,$\mathbb{Z}/6 \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/3$ (the natural projection). Surjection preserved.

**Example 2.** Injection NOT preserved: $\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}$ (injective). Tensor with$\mathbb{Z}/2$:$\mathbb{Z}/2 \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}/2$ is the zero map (since$2 \cdot 1 = 0$ in$\mathbb{Z}/2$). Zero map is not injective (unless domain is 0).

**Example 3.** Right exactness applied: from $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n \to 0$, tensor with$M$:$\mathbb{Z} \otimes M \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z} \otimes M \to \mathbb{Z}/n \otimes M \to 0$, i.e.,$M \xrightarrow{\times n} M \to M/nM \to 0$. So$\mathbb{Z}/n \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} M \cong M/nM$.

**Example 4.** Tor measures failure: from Example 3, the long exact sequence is $0 \to \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/n, M) \to M \xrightarrow{\times n} M \to M/nM \to 0$. So$\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n, M) = M[n] = \ker(\times n: M \to M)$ (the$n$-torsion in$M$).

**Example 5.** A map of free modules: $\mathbb{Z}^2 \xrightarrow{f} \mathbb{Z}^2$ given by$f(a,b) = (2a, 3b)$ (injective). Tensor with$\mathbb{Z}/6$:$(\mathbb{Z}/6)^2 \to (\mathbb{Z}/6)^2$ given by$(a, b) \mapsto (2a, 3b)$. This is NOT injective:$(3, 2)$ maps to$(6, 6) = (0, 0)$ in$(\mathbb{Z}/6)^2$. Injectivity of$f$ does not survive tensoring.
