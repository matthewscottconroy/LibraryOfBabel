# 28.2.2 Examples of Non-Flatness: $\mathbb{Z}/n \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m$

The computation of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ is a fundamental exercise that simultaneously illustrates the failure of left-exactness and provides a formula used constantly in topology and algebra. The answer —$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(n,m)\mathbb{Z}$ — is elegant, and the derivation via right-exactness and presentations is a template for computing tensor products in a wide range of situations.

The strategy is to use a free presentation of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, apply the right-exact tensor functor, and read off the result from the resulting exact sequence. The starting point is the short exact sequence of$\mathbb{Z}$-modules
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\;\times n\;} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;} \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to 0,$$
where the first map is multiplication by $n$ and$\pi$ is the quotient map. This is the canonical free presentation of the cyclic module$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$: it expresses$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ as the cokernel of the map$\times n$ on the free module$\mathbb{Z}$.

Applying the functor $- \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ and using right-exactness, we obtain the exact sequence
$$\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\;\times n \otimes 1\;} \mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\;\pi \otimes 1\;} \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
Using the canonical isomorphism $\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} N \cong N$ (which sends$k \otimes x \mapsto kx$), this simplifies to the exact sequence
$$\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\;\times n\;} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
The map $\times n: \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ sends$\bar{x} \mapsto n\bar{x}$. Its image is$n \cdot \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} = \{n\bar{x} : \bar{x} \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\}$. Since$n\bar{x}$ ranges over all multiples of$\gcd(n,m)$ in$\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$, the image is the subgroup$\gcd(n,m) \cdot (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})$, which is isomorphic to$\mathbb{Z}/(m/\gcd(n,m))\mathbb{Z}$ and has index$\gcd(n,m)$ in$\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$.

By exactness, $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ is the cokernel of the map$\times n$ on$\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$:
$$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \;\cong\; \frac{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}{\gcd(n,m) \cdot (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(n,m)\mathbb{Z}.$$

\begin{theorem}
For any integers $n, m \geq 1$,
$$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(n,m)\mathbb{Z}.$$
\end{theorem}

The formula has a conceptual explanation that bypasses the calculation. The group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ is cyclic, generated by$\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1}$, since every elementary tensor$\bar{a} \otimes \bar{b} = ab \cdot (\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1})$ (using bilinearity). The order of$\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1}$ must divide both$n$ (from$n\bar{1} = \bar{0}$ in$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$) and$m$ (from$m\bar{1} = \bar{0}$ in$\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$), hence divides$\gcd(n,m)$. The order is exactly$\gcd(n,m)$ because Bezout's identity gives integers$s, t$ with$sn + tm = \gcd(n,m)$, and the order is at least$\gcd(n,m)$ by careful analysis of the cokernel.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(6,4)\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Let us verify: the map$\times 6: \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is the same as$\times 2: \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ (since$6 \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$). Its image is$\{0, 2\} = 2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The cokernel is$(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})/(2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Thus$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, as predicted.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z} \cong 0$ for distinct primes$p \neq q$, since$\gcd(p,q) = 1$. Directly:$\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1}$ generates the group, and$p \cdot (\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1}) = p\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1} = \bar{0} \otimes \bar{1} = 0$, so the generator has order dividing$p$. But also$q \cdot (\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1}) = 0$, so the order divides$q$. Since$\gcd(p,q) = 1$, the order is$1$, meaning$\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1} = 0$ and the entire module is trivial.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** $\mathbb{Z}/p^a\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/p^b\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^{\min(a,b)}\mathbb{Z}$ for a prime$p$ and integers$a, b \geq 1$, since$\gcd(p^a, p^b) = p^{\min(a,b)}$. For instance,$\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** *Using CRT to compute $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$.* By the Chinese remainder theorem,$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ and$\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}$. Since tensor distributes over direct sums:
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z} &\cong (\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}) \otimes (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}) \\
&\cong (\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) \oplus (\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}) \oplus (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) \oplus (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}) \\
&\cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus 0 \oplus 0 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \\
&\cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.
\end{align*}
This matches the formula: $\gcd(12, 18) = 6$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 5.** *Non-flatness via the kernel computation.* Let $n = m = 2$. Consider the injection$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}$. Tensoring with$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, we obtain the map$\times 2: \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which sends$\bar{x} \mapsto 2\bar{x} = \bar{0}$ for all$\bar{x}$. This is the zero map. Thus the injection$\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}$ has been converted by tensoring into the zero map — meaning the element$\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1} \in \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ maps to zero in$\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, even though$\bar{1} \otimes \bar{1} \neq 0$ (since$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$). This proves$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is not flat: a perfectly good injection has been destroyed. The kernel of the tensored map is exactly$\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The failure of $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ to be flat reflects a general principle: a finitely generated module over a Noetherian domain is flat if and only if it is torsion-free, and over a PID this is equivalent to being free. Thus the only flat finitely generated$\mathbb{Z}$-modules are the free ones$\mathbb{Z}^n$. Any module with a nonzero torsion element fails flatness because the multiplication-by-that-torsion-order map, which is an injection$\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}$, becomes zero upon tensoring. More precisely,$\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(n,m)\mathbb{Z}$, which is nonzero whenever$\gcd(n,m) > 1$ — precisely measuring the failure of flatness of$\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{remark}
