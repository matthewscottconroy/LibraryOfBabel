# 28.2.3 The Exact Triangle: Right Exactness and the Cokernel

Right exactness gives us a precise description of what happens when we tensor a short exact sequence. Starting from $0 \to A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ and tensoring with$M$, we always obtain an exact sequence at the right end:$A \otimes M \xrightarrow{f \otimes 1} B \otimes M \xrightarrow{g \otimes 1} C \otimes M \to 0$. The map$f \otimes 1$ lands in the kernel of$g \otimes 1$, but$f \otimes 1$ itself may not be injective — there may be elements of$A \otimes M$ that become zero in$B \otimes M$.

The failure of $f \otimes 1$ to be injective is measured by the first Tor group. Associated to the short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$, there is a long exact sequence:
$$\cdots \to \mathrm{Tor}_1^R(B, M) \to \mathrm{Tor}_1^R(C, M) \to A \otimes_R M \to B \otimes_R M \to C \otimes_R M \to 0.$$
The connecting homomorphism $\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(C, M) \to A \otimes M$ captures exactly the "leftover" failure of injectivity. When$M$ is flat, all the Tor terms vanish and the long exact sequence collapses to the short exact sequence$0 \to A \otimes M \to B \otimes M \to C \otimes M \to 0$, consistent with the definition of flatness.

The key point is that right exactness is the best general statement: without a flatness hypothesis, we cannot extend the sequence to the left. The Tor groups, defined as derived functors of $\otimes$ in Chapter 40, are precisely the additional terms that restore exactness in the longer sequence. Right exactness of tensor and left exactness of Hom are the two fundamental half-exactness properties from which all of homological algebra is built.

There is an immediate application of right exactness that is used constantly: if $M$ is an$R$-module presented as$M = R/I$ (for an ideal$I$), then for any$N$ we have an exact sequence$I \otimes_R N \to R \otimes_R N \to (R/I) \otimes_R N \to 0$, which gives$I \otimes_R N \to N \to N/IN \to 0$. Hence$(R/I) \otimes_R N \cong N/IN$ as abelian groups (and as$R/I$-modules). This is the "base change" formula: tensoring with$R/I$ replaces$N$ by its quotient$N/IN$.

\begin{corollary}
For any ideal $I \subseteq R$ and any$R$-module$N$:
$$R/I \otimes_R N \cong N/IN.$$
\end{corollary}

**Example 1.** $\mathbb{Z}/n \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} N \cong N/nN$ for any abelian group$N$. For$N = \mathbb{Z}$:$\mathbb{Z}/n \otimes \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ (consistent). For$N = \mathbb{Z}/m$:$\mathbb{Z}/n \otimes \mathbb{Z}/m \cong \mathbb{Z}/m / n\mathbb{Z}/m \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(n,m)$ (consistent with our earlier formula).

**Example 2.** $k[x]/(f) \otimes_{k[x]} M \cong M/fM$ for any$k[x]$-module$M$. For$M = k[x]/(g)$:$(k[x]/(f)) \otimes (k[x]/(g)) \cong k[x]/(\gcd(f,g) \cdot (k[x]/(g)))$... more precisely,$\cong k[x]/(\gcd(f,g))$ when$f, g$ generate ideals whose intersection... actually$M/fM = k[x]/(g) / (f \cdot k[x]/(g)) = k[x]/(g + fk[x]) = k[x]/(f, g) = k[x]/(\gcd(f,g))$.

**Example 3.** The sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$ tensored with$\mathbb{Z}/4$:$\mathbb{Z}/4 \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$. The kernel of$\times 2$ on$\mathbb{Z}/4$ is$\{0, 2\} = \mathbb{Z}/2$. So$\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/4) = \mathbb{Z}/2$. The long exact sequence at the Tor level:$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$.

**Example 4.** Exactness of $A \otimes M \to B \otimes M$: consider$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$ (the natural inclusion and projection). Tensor with$\mathbb{Z}/2$:$\mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$. The first map$\mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ is induced by$\mathbb{Z}/2 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2$, which is multiplication by 2 (zero map). Not injective.

**Example 5.** Right exactness is "the best we can do" in general: there is no way to extend $A \otimes M \to B \otimes M \to C \otimes M \to 0$ to a short exact sequence without further assumptions. The Tor functor (Chapter 40) is precisely the universal repair:$\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(C, M)$ is the "correction term" that must be added to the left to restore exactness.
