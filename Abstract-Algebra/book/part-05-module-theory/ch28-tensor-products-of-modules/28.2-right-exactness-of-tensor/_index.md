# 28.2 Right Exactness of Tensor

One of the most important properties of the tensor product functor — and one that distinguishes it sharply from the Hom functor — is that it is right exact but not in general left exact. Given a short exact sequence $0 \to A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ of left $R$-modules and any right $R$-module $M$, the tensored sequence $M \otimes_R A \xrightarrow{1 \otimes f} M \otimes_R B \xrightarrow{1 \otimes g} M \otimes_R C \to 0$ is exact, but the map $1 \otimes f$ need not be injective. The right exactness — exactness at the last two terms — follows from the universal property of tensor products and the fact that $g$ is surjective. The failure at the left is a genuine phenomenon, not an artifact of the construction.

The canonical counterexample demonstrating failure of left exactness is the injection $f : \mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}$ defined by multiplication by $2$, sitting in the short exact sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. Tensoring with $M = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ gives $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and multiplication by $2$ on $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is the zero map — so the injective map $f$ becomes the zero map after tensoring, demonstrating that $1 \otimes f$ is not injective. This example shows that $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is not flat as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module. More generally, any torsion element in a module over an integral domain witnesses non-flatness by a similar argument.

The derived functor measuring the failure of left exactness is $\text{Tor}_1^R(M, N)$. This is defined by taking a projective (or free) resolution $\cdots \to P_1 \to P_0 \to N \to 0$ of $N$, tensoring with $M$ to get a chain complex $\cdots \to M \otimes P_1 \to M \otimes P_0 \to 0$, and defining $\text{Tor}_1^R(M, N)$ as the homology of this complex at position $1$ — it measures exactly the kernel of $1 \otimes (P_1 \to P_0)$ modulo boundaries. The long exact sequence in Tor, running $\cdots \to \text{Tor}_1(M, C) \to M \otimes A \to M \otimes B \to M \otimes C \to 0$, is the fundamental tool for computing tensor products of non-free modules and for understanding how exactness fails. The group $\text{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ is a standard computation that appears throughout algebraic topology in the Universal Coefficient Theorem.
