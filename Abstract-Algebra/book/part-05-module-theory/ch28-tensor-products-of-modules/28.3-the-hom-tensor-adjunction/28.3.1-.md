# 28.3.1 $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N,P))$

Among all the identities involving tensor products and Hom, none is more fundamental than the tensor-hom adjunction. It says that a linear map out of a tensor product is the same as a "curried" linear map — one that takes the first argument and produces a linear map that then takes the second argument.

Precisely, for $R$-modules$M$,$N$, and$P$ (with appropriate left/right module structures), there is a natural isomorphism of abelian groups:
$$\mathrm{Hom}_R(M \otimes_R N,\, P) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}_R\!\left(M,\, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)\right).$$
An $R$-linear map$f: M \otimes_R N \to P$ corresponds, under this isomorphism, to the map$\tilde{f}: M \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)$ defined by$\tilde{f}(m)(n) = f(m \otimes n)$. Conversely, given$g: M \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)$, the corresponding map out of the tensor product is$(m \otimes n) \mapsto g(m)(n)$, which is$R$-balanced precisely because$g$ is$R$-linear and$g(m)$ is$R$-linear.

To see why this isomorphism is natural — meaning it is compatible with maps in $M$,$N$, and$P$ — observe that the passage from$f$ to$\tilde{f}$ is given by "currying" in the sense of functional programming: a function of two variables is converted to a function of one variable that returns a function of the remaining variable. This adjunction is one instance of the general principle that left adjoints (here$- \otimes_R N$) and right adjoints (here$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$) are related by this kind of currying identity, and it is the algebraic foundation for a great deal of the structural theory developed in Chapters 40–41.

The naturality of the isomorphism means: for any $R$-linear maps$\alpha: M' \to M$,$\beta: N' \to N$,$\gamma: P \to P'$, the bijection$f \leftrightarrow \tilde{f}$ is compatible with precomposition by$\alpha \otimes \beta$ on the left and postcomposition by$\gamma$ on the right. Concretely:
$$(\widetilde{\gamma \circ f \circ (\alpha \otimes \beta)})(m')(n') = \gamma(f(\alpha(m') \otimes \beta(n'))),$$
which is exactly $(\gamma_* \circ \tilde{f} \circ \alpha)$... applied to$m'$ then$n'$. This naturality is what makes the adjunction a genuine categorical adjunction, not merely a set-theoretic bijection.

\begin{theorem}[Tensor-Hom Adjunction]
For a right $R$-module$M$, left$R$-module$N$, and abelian group$P$ (or$R$-module when$R$ is commutative), there is a natural isomorphism:
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, P)).$$
When all modules are over a commutative ring $R$:
$$\mathrm{Hom}_R(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)).$$
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^m \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}^n, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^m, \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^n, \mathbb{Z}))$. Left side:$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{mn}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{mn}$. Right side:$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^m, \mathbb{Z}^n) \cong M_{n \times m}(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{mn}$. Consistent.

**Example 2.** Currying for bilinear maps: a bilinear map $f: \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ (a matrix) corresponds to a linear map$\mathbb{R}^m \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}) = (\mathbb{R}^n)^*$. The matrix$A$ corresponds to the linear map$v \mapsto (w \mapsto v^T A w)$.

**Example 3.** $\mathrm{Hom}_R(R \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(R, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)$ (using$R \otimes N \cong N$ and$\mathrm{Hom}(R, -) \cong \mathrm{id}$). This shows the adjunction reduces to the identity when$M = R$.

**Example 4.** $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} A, B) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A, B))$. Left side:$\mathrm{Hom}(A/nA, B)$ (using$\mathbb{Z}/n \otimes A \cong A/nA$). Right side:$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathrm{Hom}(A, B)) \cong \{f: A \to B : nf = 0\} = \mathrm{Hom}(A/nA, B)$ (maps that kill$nA$). Consistent.

**Example 5.** The adjunction for free modules: $\mathrm{Hom}_R(R^m \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(R^m, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)^m$. And$R^m \otimes_R N \cong N^m$, so$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N^m, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)^m$ — a consistent identity.
