# 28.3.2 This Is an Adjunction: $- \otimes_R N \dashv \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$

The isomorphism $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P))$ is not merely an identity between hom-sets for fixed modules; it is *natural* in all three variables$M$,$N$, and$P$. This naturality is what elevates the identity to an *adjunction* between functors: the functor$- \otimes_R N$ (tensoring with$N$) is *left adjoint* to the functor$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ (taking hom from$N$).

Recall that an adjunction between functors $F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ and$G: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{C}$ consists of natural bijections$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X), Y) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X, G(Y))$. In our case,$F = - \otimes_R N$ and$G = \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$, both functors on the category of$R$-modules (or between categories of left and right modules, depending on the non-commutativity of$R$). The tensor-hom adjunction is thus one of the most important adjunctions in algebra, alongside the free-forgetful adjunction.

The adjunction has profound structural implications. Because $\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ is a right adjoint, it preserves all limits; in particular it preserves kernels and hence is *left exact*. Because$- \otimes_R N$ is a left adjoint, it preserves all colimits; in particular it preserves cokernels and hence is *right exact*. Both exactness properties, which we verified directly in Section 28.2, now emerge as consequences of the adjunction, without the need for ad hoc calculations. More deeply, the failure of$- \otimes_R N$ to be left-exact and the failure of$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ to be right-exact are the primary subjects of homological algebra: the derived functors that measure these failures are Tor and Ext respectively.

An adjunction can also be expressed via *unit* and *counit* natural transformations. The unit $\eta: \mathrm{id} \Rightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, - \otimes_R N)$ is the natural map$M \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, M \otimes_R N)$ sending$m \mapsto (n \mapsto m \otimes n)$. The counit$\varepsilon: \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -) \otimes_R N \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ is the *evaluation map* sending$f \otimes n \mapsto f(n)$. The unit-counit equations$(\varepsilon_{-\otimes N}) \circ (\eta_M \otimes N) = \mathrm{id}$ and$(\mathrm{Hom}(N, \varepsilon)) \circ (\eta_{\mathrm{Hom}(N,-)}) = \mathrm{id}$ encode the adjunction in a coordinate-free way.

\begin{theorem}[Tensor-Hom Adjunction as an Adjunction of Functors]
For a fixed $R$-module$N$, there is an adjunction
$$- \otimes_R N \;\dashv\; \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$$
between endofunctors of $\mathrm{Mod}_R$ (for commutative$R$). The unit is$\eta_M: M \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, M \otimes_R N)$,$m \mapsto (n \mapsto m \otimes n)$. The counit is$\varepsilon_P: \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P) \otimes_R N \to P$,$f \otimes n \mapsto f(n)$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Take $N = R$. The adjunction$- \otimes_R R \dashv \mathrm{Hom}_R(R, -)$ is trivial:$M \otimes_R R \cong M$ (unit$R$) and$\mathrm{Hom}_R(R, P) \cong P$. The unit$\eta_M: M \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(R, M \otimes R) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(R, M) \cong M$ is the identity.

**Example 2.** Take $N = k$ (field) and$R = k$. The adjunction$- \otimes_k k \dashv \mathrm{Hom}_k(k, -)$ is again trivial (both sides are the identity functor). More interesting: take$N = k^n$. The adjunction$- \otimes_k k^n \dashv \mathrm{Hom}_k(k^n, -)$ says: linear maps from$V \otimes k^n \cong V^n$ to$W$ are the same as linear maps from$V$ to$\mathrm{Hom}(k^n, W) \cong W^n$ — both are$n$-tuples of linear maps from$V$ to$W$.

**Example 3.** Extension and restriction of scalars: for $\phi: R \to S$ and the adjunction$- \otimes_R S \dashv \mathrm{Res}^S_R(-)$, the unit is the map$M \to \mathrm{Res}(M \otimes_R S)$ sending$m \mapsto m \otimes 1_S$, and the counit is$\mathrm{Res}(N) \otimes_R S \to N$ sending$n \otimes s \mapsto sn$. These satisfy the triangle identities of an adjunction.

**Example 4.** The adjunction $- \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \dashv \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Q}, -)$: a map$M \otimes \mathbb{Q} \to P$ is the same as a map$M \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Q}, P)$. If$M = \mathbb{Z}$: a map$\mathbb{Q} \to P$ is the same as a map$\mathbb{Z} \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Q}, P)$, i.e., an element of$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Q}, P)$.

**Example 5.** The evaluation counit $\varepsilon_P: \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P) \otimes_R N \to P$,$f \otimes n \mapsto f(n)$, is surjective when$N$ is a generator of the module category (when every module is a quotient of a direct sum of copies of$N$). For$N = R$, this is always surjective:$\mathrm{Hom}(R, P) \otimes R \cong P \otimes R \cong P \to P$ is the identity.
