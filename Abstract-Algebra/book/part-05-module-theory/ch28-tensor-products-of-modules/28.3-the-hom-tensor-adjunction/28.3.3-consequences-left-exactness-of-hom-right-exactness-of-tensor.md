# 28.3.3 Consequences: Left Exactness of Hom, Right Exactness of Tensor

The tensor-hom adjunction unifies the two fundamental half-exactness properties of module theory under a single categorical principle. Rather than proving left-exactness of Hom and right-exactness of tensor by separate direct calculations, both follow from one structural fact: right adjoints preserve limits, and left adjoints preserve colimits.

Applied to Hom: since $\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ is a right adjoint (to$- \otimes_R N$), it preserves all limits. In the abelian category of modules, kernels are limits, so$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ preserves kernels. This is precisely left-exactness: if$0 \to A \to B \to C$ is exact (meaning$A \to B$ has the correct kernel), then$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(N, A) \to \mathrm{Hom}(N, B) \to \mathrm{Hom}(N, C)$ is exact. Applied to tensor: since$- \otimes_R N$ is a left adjoint (to$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$), it preserves all colimits. In the abelian category of modules, cokernels are colimits, so$- \otimes_R N$ preserves cokernels, i.e., it is right-exact.

The failure of Hom to be right-exact — that $\mathrm{Hom}(N, B) \to \mathrm{Hom}(N, C)$ need not be surjective even when$B \to C$ is surjective — and the failure of tensor to be left-exact are the core phenomena of homological algebra. The derived functors that measure these failures are$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(N, -)$ (derived from$\mathrm{Hom}$) and$\mathrm{Tor}_n^R(-, N)$ (derived from$\otimes$), developed in Chapter 40. Thus the tensor-hom adjunction is not merely a convenient identity but the organizing principle from which all of the deeper homological theory flows.

The two failures are dual in a precise sense: $\mathrm{Hom}(N, -)$ fails to be right-exact because it cannot "lift" maps through surjections (the obstruction being$\mathrm{Ext}^1$), while$- \otimes N$ fails to be left-exact because it cannot "inject" through injections (the obstruction being$\mathrm{Tor}_1$). Both$\mathrm{Ext}$ and$\mathrm{Tor}$ can be computed using either free resolutions or injective resolutions, and the tensor-hom adjunction shows that the two computations give the same answer — a deep consistency theorem whose proof uses the adjunction in an essential way.

\begin{theorem}[Left Exactness of Hom]
For any $R$-module$N$ and any short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$, the induced sequence
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, A) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, B) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, C)$$
is exact. (The last map may not be surjective in general.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Right Exactness of Tensor]
For any $R$-module$N$ and any short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$, the induced sequence
$$A \otimes_R N \to B \otimes_R N \to C \otimes_R N \to 0$$
is exact. (The first map may not be injective in general.)
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Left-exactness of Hom: take $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$ and apply$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-, \mathbb{Z}/2)$ (contravariant). The resulting sequence is$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2) \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2)$, i.e.,$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \xrightarrow{0} \mathbb{Z}/2$. The last map$\times 2$ is zero, and the sequence is left-exact. Hom is NOT right-exact here: the map$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2)$ has image$\cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ surjective... wait, let us redo.

Apply $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, -)$ (covariant) to$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$:$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2)$. The first two terms are 0 (no nonzero maps$\mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}$), giving$0 \to 0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2$. The map$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2)$ is from 0 to$\mathbb{Z}/2$, not surjective! Hom is left-exact but not right-exact.

**Example 2.** Right-exactness of tensor: apply $- \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2$ to$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$:$\mathbb{Z}/2 \xrightarrow{0} \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$. This is right-exact (the last two terms are exact), but the first map is not injective (it is the zero map). Right-exact, not left-exact.

**Example 3.** When Hom IS right-exact: $\mathrm{Hom}(P, -)$ is right-exact if and only if$P$ is projective. For$P = \mathbb{Z}^n$ (free),$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^n, -)$ is right-exact (actually exact). For$P = \mathbb{Z}/n$, Hom is not right-exact (as shown above).

**Example 4.** When tensor IS left-exact: $- \otimes_R M$ is left-exact if and only if$M$ is flat. For$M = \mathbb{Q}$ (flat over$\mathbb{Z}$):$- \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$ is exact (it localizes, which preserves injections). For$M = \mathbb{Z}/n$ (not flat):$- \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n$ is not left-exact.

**Example 5.** The long exact sequences from the adjunction: from $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ and a module$N$, we get long exact sequences:
$$\cdots \to \mathrm{Tor}_1(C, N) \to A \otimes N \to B \otimes N \to C \otimes N \to 0$$
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(C, N) \to \mathrm{Hom}(B, N) \to \mathrm{Hom}(A, N) \to \mathrm{Ext}^1(C, N) \to \cdots$$
Both long exact sequences arise from the adjunction: Tor is the derived functor of $\otimes$, Ext is the derived functor of Hom, and the connecting homomorphisms in both sequences are constructed via the adjunction isomorphism.
