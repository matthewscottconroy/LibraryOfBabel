# 28.3 The Hom–Tensor Adjunction

The Hom–Tensor adjunction is the structural theorem that ties together the tensor product and the Hom functor at the deepest level. For a right $R$-module $M$, a left $R$-module $N$, and an abelian group $P$, there is a natural isomorphism $\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \text{Hom}_R(M, \text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, P))$. In categorical language, this says that the functor $- \otimes_R N$ is left adjoint to $\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, -)$. The adjunction is not a coincidence but a reflection of the universal property of tensor products: a homomorphism $M \otimes_R N \to P$ is the same data as a bilinear map $M \times N \to P$, which is the same data as a family of maps $M \to \text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, P)$ parameterized by $M$ and linear in the $M$-variable. The natural bijection between hom-sets in the two positions is precisely this three-way identification.

From the adjunction, the exactness properties of both functors follow formally. Left adjoints preserve colimits and in particular preserve cokernels, which means $- \otimes_R N$ preserves right exactness — recovering the right exactness theorem of Section 28.2 as a consequence of the adjunction rather than a separate argument. Right adjoints preserve limits and in particular preserve kernels, which means $\text{Hom}_R(N, -)$ is left exact: for any short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$, the sequence $0 \to \text{Hom}(N, A) \to \text{Hom}(N, B) \to \text{Hom}(N, C)$ is exact. This left exactness of Hom — which fails to be right exact in general — is the mirror image of the failure of left exactness of tensor. The two failures are measured by Ext and Tor respectively, and the Hom–Tensor adjunction explains their relationship.

The deeper significance of the adjunction appears when one passes to derived categories. The derived tensor product $M \otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_R N$ and the derived Hom $\mathbf{R}\text{Hom}_R(M, N)$ are the correct derived-categorical analogues of tensor and Hom, and the Hom–Tensor adjunction lifts to a derived adjunction $\mathbf{R}\text{Hom}(M \otimes^{\mathbf{L}} N, P) \cong \mathbf{R}\text{Hom}(M, \mathbf{R}\text{Hom}(N, P))$. The homology groups of $M \otimes^{\mathbf{L}} N$ are the Tor groups $\text{Tor}_i^R(M, N)$, and the cohomology groups of $\mathbf{R}\text{Hom}(M, N)$ are the Ext groups $\text{Ext}^i_R(M, N)$. The Universal Coefficient Theorem in algebraic topology is a consequence of this adjunction over $\mathbb{Z}$, and the derived category framework where the adjunction lives is the natural home for much of modern homological algebra and algebraic geometry.
