# 29.2 Algebraic Extensions

The most important question one can ask about an element $\alpha$ in a field extension $K/F$ is whether $\alpha$ is algebraic over $F$: does it satisfy some nonzero polynomial equation with coefficients in $F$? If so, there is a unique monic polynomial of least degree doing so, called the minimal polynomial $m_\alpha \in F[x]$, and it is irreducible. The degree of $m_\alpha$ equals $[F(\alpha):F]$, and $F(\alpha) \cong F[x]/(m_\alpha(x))$, so the minimal polynomial completely determines the simple extension generated by $\alpha$. This tight relationship between an element and its minimal polynomial is one of the most useful facts in all of field theory.

If $\alpha$ is not algebraic over $F$, it is called transcendental, and the simple extension $F(\alpha)$ is isomorphic to the field of rational functions $F(t)$, with no polynomial relations among powers of $\alpha$. The distinction between algebraic and transcendental elements runs deep: almost all real numbers are transcendental (by a cardinality argument), yet proving any specific number transcendental is notoriously difficult. Liouville constructed the first transcendental numbers artificially in 1844; Hermite proved $e$ transcendental in 1873; Lindemann proved $\pi$ transcendental in 1882, settling the ancient problem of squaring the circle.

An extension $K/F$ is algebraic if every element of $K$ is algebraic over $F$. Every finite extension is algebraic — if $[K:F] = n$, then for any $\alpha \in K$, the $n+1$ elements $1, \alpha, \alpha^2, \ldots, \alpha^n$ are linearly dependent over $F$, giving a polynomial relation — but algebraic extensions need not be finite. The field $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ of all algebraic numbers is an infinite algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$. A key closure property: the algebraic elements form a field. If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are both algebraic over $F$, then so are $\alpha \pm \beta$, $\alpha\beta$, and $\alpha/\beta$ (when $\beta \neq 0$). This is proved via the Tower Law: $F(\alpha, \beta)$ is a finite extension of $F$, and every element of a finite extension is algebraic.
