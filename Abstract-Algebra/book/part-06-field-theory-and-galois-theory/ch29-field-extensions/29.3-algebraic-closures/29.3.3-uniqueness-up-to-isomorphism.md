# 29.3.3 Uniqueness up to Isomorphism

Once existence is established, the next question is whether the algebraic closure is in any sense unique. If we follow Artin's construction starting from two different choices of maximal ideals, or if we embed $\mathbb{Q}$ into two different algebraically closed ambient fields, do we get the same algebraic closure? The answer is yes, up to isomorphism: any two algebraic closures of$F$ are isomorphic as$F$-algebras. This means we can speak of "the" algebraic closure$\bar{F}$, understanding this as shorthand for "any algebraic closure, which is unique up to$F$-algebra isomorphism."

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of Algebraic Closures]
Let $\bar{F}$ and$\bar{F}'$ be two algebraic closures of$F$. Then there exists an$F$-algebra isomorphism$\bar{F} \to \bar{F}'$.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses a Zorn's Lemma argument that extends $F$-algebra isomorphisms step by step. Consider the set of all partial$F$-algebra isomorphisms: pairs$(E, \phi)$ where$F \subseteq E \subseteq \bar{F}$ and$\phi: E \to E' \subseteq \bar{F}'$ is an$F$-algebra isomorphism onto some subfield$E' \subseteq \bar{F}'$. We partially order these pairs by extension:$(E_1, \phi_1) \leq (E_2, \phi_2)$ if$E_1 \subseteq E_2$ and$\phi_2|_{E_1} = \phi_1$. The union of any chain of such partial isomorphisms is again a partial isomorphism (the maps agree on overlaps, so the union is well-defined), so Zorn's Lemma applies.

Take a maximal partial isomorphism $(E, \phi)$. We claim$E = \bar{F}$. Suppose$E \subsetneq \bar{F}$; pick$\alpha \in \bar{F} \setminus E$. The element$\alpha$ is algebraic over$F$, hence over$E$; let$f = \mathrm{min}_{E,\alpha} \in E[x]$. The polynomial$\phi(f) \in E'[x]$ (obtained by applying$\phi$ to the coefficients of$f$) is irreducible over$E'$. Since$\bar{F}'$ is algebraically closed,$\phi(f)$ has a root$\beta \in \bar{F}'$. Then$\phi$ extends to an isomorphism$E(\alpha) \to E'(\beta)$ by$\alpha \mapsto \beta$, contradicting the maximality of$(E, \phi)$. Therefore$E = \bar{F}$.

A similar argument shows the map $\phi: \bar{F} \to \bar{F}'$ is surjective: the image is an algebraically closed subfield of$\bar{F}'$ that contains$F$, and since$\bar{F}'$ is algebraic over$F$, every element of$\bar{F}'$ is algebraic over$F$, hence lies in any algebraically closed extension of$F$ — in particular in$\phi(\bar{F})$. So$\phi$ is surjective, hence an isomorphism.

The surprise here is the gap between uniqueness-up-to-isomorphism and canonicity. The isomorphism $\bar{F} \cong \bar{F}'$ is far from unique. The automorphism group$\mathrm{Aut}(\bar{F}/F)$ — the group of all$F$-algebra automorphisms of$\bar{F}$ — measures the non-uniqueness of the isomorphisms. For$F = \mathbb{R}$, the algebraic closure is$\mathbb{C} = \bar{\mathbb{R}}$, and$\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}) = \{id, \text{complex conjugation}\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ — there are two isomorphisms$\mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ over$\mathbb{R}$, reflecting the two conjugate copies of$\mathbb{C}$ over$\mathbb{R}$.

**Example 1.** For $F = \mathbb{R}$, both of the following are algebraic closures:$\mathbb{C}$ as constructed by adjoining$i$ (a root of$x^2 + 1$), and$\mathbb{C}$ as constructed by adjoining$-i$ (the other root). Both are isomorphic to$\mathbb{C}$, but they correspond to different embeddings of$\mathbb{C}$: the identity and complex conjugation. These are the two$\mathbb{R}$-algebra isomorphisms of$\mathbb{C}$ with itself.

**Example 2.** For $F = \mathbb{Q}$, the field$\bar{\mathbb{Q}} \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ is one algebraic closure. Another is$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^{\sigma} = \{\sigma(\alpha) : \alpha \in \bar{\mathbb{Q}}\}$ where$\sigma: \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ is any field automorphism. For any$\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q})$ (these are the automorphisms fixing$\mathbb{Q}$),$\sigma(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}) = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, so there is only one algebraic closure inside$\mathbb{C}$. Different algebraic closures would arise from different embeddings of$\mathbb{Q}$ into algebraically closed fields.

**Example 3.** The absolute Galois group $G_{\mathbb{Q}} = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ is the group of all$\mathbb{Q}$-algebra automorphisms of$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. It is an enormous profinite group (the inverse limit of all finite Galois groups$\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ as$K$ ranges over finite Galois extensions of$\mathbb{Q}$). The uniqueness of$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ up to isomorphism says that any two algebraic closures of$\mathbb{Q}$ are isomorphic, but$G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ measures how many ways this isomorphism can be chosen. One of the deepest open problems in mathematics concerns the structure of$G_{\mathbb{Q}}$.

**Example 4.** For $F = \mathbb{F}_p$, the algebraic closure is$\overline{\mathbb{F}_p} = \bigcup_{n} \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$. Any automorphism of$\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$ must permute the roots of$x^{p^n} - x$ for each$n$, hence restrict to a$\mathbb{F}_p$-automorphism of$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$. The automorphism group$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is the profinite completion$\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$, the inverse limit of the groups$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, topologically generated by the Frobenius$x \mapsto x^p$.

**Example 5.** Consider two explicit algebraic closures of $\mathbb{Q}$ living in different ambient spaces:$\bar{\mathbb{Q}} \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ and$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}' \subseteq \tilde{\mathbb{C}}$ where$\tilde{\mathbb{C}}$ is some other algebraically closed field of characteristic$0$ and cardinality$|\mathbb{C}|$. These two algebraic closures are isomorphic as$\mathbb{Q}$-algebras by the theorem, even though they live in different universes. The isomorphism, however, is not unique and cannot be made canonical without additional choices (such as choosing where to send$i$, then where to send$\sqrt[3]{2}$, etc.).

The uniqueness theorem licenses the notation $\bar{F}$ for the algebraic closure of$F$ and allows one to speak of the Galois group$\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{F}/F)$ without specifying which algebraic closure. It also justifies the use of$\bar{F}$ as the universal domain for all algebraic extensions of$F$: every algebraic extension$E/F$ embeds into$\bar{F}$ (the embedding is constructed by the same Zorn's Lemma argument), so$\bar{F}$ contains all algebraic extensions of$F$ up to isomorphism. This is the correct universal property of the algebraic closure.
