# Chapter 29 — Exercises

## Important Figures

- **Carl Friedrich Gauss (1777–1855)** — theory of cyclotomic fields; constructibility via ruler and compass; first studied specific field extensions in depth
- **Évariste Galois (1811–1832)** — field extensions implicit in his work on polynomial equations; the notion of "splitting field" is central to his theory
- **Ernst Steinitz (1871–1928)** — *Algebraische Theorie der Körper* (1910): first abstract axiomatic treatment of fields; introduced algebraic closure, transcendence degree, and characteristic

## References and Primary Sources

- **E. Steinitz, "Algebraische Theorie der Körper" (1910)** — *J. Reine Angew. Math.* 137 — founding of abstract field theory
- **S. Lang, *Algebra* (rev. 3rd ed., Springer, 2002)**, Chs. V–VI — field extensions and Galois theory
- **I. Stewart, *Galois Theory* (4th ed., CRC Press, 2015)** — readable and historically motivated introduction

## Examples, Applications, and Thought Experiments

- **$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ over $\mathbb{Q}$** — degree-2 extension; minimal polynomial $x^2 - 2$; basis $\{1, \sqrt{2}\}$; every element is $a + b\sqrt{2}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}$; this is the prototype for a simple algebraic extension
- **The tower law** — $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})] \cdot [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 2 \cdot 2 = 4$; $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ has degree 4 over $\mathbb{Q}$; use the tower law to constrain what extensions are possible
- **Algebraic vs. transcendental** — $\sqrt{2}$ is algebraic (root of $x^2-2$); $\pi$ is transcendental (Lindemann, 1882); the algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ contains all algebraic numbers; $\mathbb{C}$ contains transcendentals; the algebraic numbers form a countable algebraically closed field sitting inside $\mathbb{C}$
- **$\mathbb{C}$ as algebraic closure of $\mathbb{R}$** — every polynomial in $\mathbb{C}[x]$ has a root (Fundamental Theorem of Algebra); $[\mathbb{C} : \mathbb{R}] = 2$; the only algebraic extension of $\mathbb{R}$ is $\mathbb{C}$; a very "thin" algebraic closure

## Exercises

1. Find the minimal polynomial of $\alpha = \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Verify that it is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, and use it to compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}]$. Conclude that $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3})$.

2. Use the Tower Law to compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{5}) : \mathbb{Q}]$. You may assume each successive square root is not in the field generated by the previous ones; justify this assumption by showing that $\sqrt{5} \notin \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3})$.

3. Let $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ be a primitive cube root of unity. Find the minimal polynomial of $\omega$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\omega) : \mathbb{Q}]$. Then compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega) : \mathbb{Q}]$ using a tower.

4. Let $F$ be a field and $f \in F[x]$ an irreducible polynomial of degree $n$. Prove that $F[x]/(f(x))$ is a field extension of $F$ of degree $n$, and that the coset $\alpha = x + (f(x))$ satisfies $f(\alpha) = 0$.

5. Prove that if $[K : F] = p$ for a prime $p$, then there are no intermediate fields $E$ with $F \subsetneq E \subsetneq K$.

6. An element $\alpha$ in an extension $K/F$ is algebraic over $F$ if and only if $F(\alpha)$ is a finite extension of $F$. Prove both directions of this equivalence carefully.

7. Let $\alpha = 2^{1/4}$ (the positive real fourth root of 2). Compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) : \mathbb{Q}]$, find an explicit $\mathbb{Q}$-basis for $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$, and determine whether $i \in \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$.

8. (Challenge) Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ where $\alpha$ is a root of $f(x) = x^4 - 10x^2 + 1$. Show that $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (you may use the rational root theorem and reduction mod a prime), compute $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$, and then show that $K$ contains $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$. Conclude that $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$.
