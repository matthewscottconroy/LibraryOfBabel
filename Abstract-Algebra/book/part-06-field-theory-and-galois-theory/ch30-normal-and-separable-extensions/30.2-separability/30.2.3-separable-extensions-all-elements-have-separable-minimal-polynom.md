# 30.2.3 Separable Extensions: All Elements Have Separable Minimal Polynomials

Having defined separability for polynomials, we lift the concept to extensions. An algebraic extension $E/F$ is *separable* if every element$\alpha \in E$ has a separable minimal polynomial over$F$ — that is,$\mathrm{min}_{F,\alpha}$ has no repeated roots in$\bar{F}$. This is a condition on every element, not just on a generating set. The extension fails to be separable if even one element in$E$ has an inseparable minimal polynomial over$F$.

\begin{definition}
Let $E/F$ be an algebraic extension. An element$\alpha \in E$ is *separable over$F$* if its minimal polynomial$\mathrm{min}_{F,\alpha} \in F[x]$ is separable, meaning it has no repeated roots in$\bar{F}$. The extension$E/F$ is *separable* if every element of$E$ is separable over$F$.
\end{definition}

The condition on every element, rather than just on generators, might seem stricter than necessary. However, one can show that if $E = F(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_k)$ and each$\alpha_i$ has a separable minimal polynomial over the appropriate intermediate field, then every element of$E$ is separable. More precisely, separability is stable under composition: if$E/K$ is separable and$K/F$ is separable, then$E/F$ is separable. The proof runs by noting that for any$\alpha \in E$, the minimal polynomial of$\alpha$ over$F$ divides the minimal polynomial of$\alpha$ over$K$ in$K[x]$ (which is separable over$K$). Any divisor of a separable polynomial is itself separable, so$\mathrm{min}_{F,\alpha}$ is separable as well.

\begin{proposition}
Let $F \subseteq K \subseteq E$ be a tower of algebraic extensions. If$E/K$ and$K/F$ are both separable, then$E/F$ is separable. Moreover, if$E/F$ is separable, then every intermediate extension$K/F$ is separable.
\end{proposition}

The number of distinct $F$-embeddings of$E$ into$\bar{F}$ is a fundamental invariant of a finite algebraic extension, sometimes called the *separable degree*$[E:F]_{s}$. For a simple extension$E = F(\alpha)$, there are exactly$[F(\alpha):F]_{s}$ distinct$F$-embeddings of$F(\alpha)$ into$\bar{F}$, corresponding to the distinct roots of$\mathrm{min}_{F,\alpha}$. The extension is separable if and only if$[E:F]_{s} = [E:F]$: the separable degree equals the vector-space degree. In the inseparable case,$[E:F]_{s} < [E:F]$ — there are strictly fewer embeddings than the degree would suggest, and the missing embeddings are the symptom of the repeated roots.

\begin{theorem}
Let $E/F$ be a finite algebraic extension. Then$E/F$ is separable if and only if the number of$F$-embeddings$E \hookrightarrow \bar{F}$ equals$[E:F]$.
\end{theorem}

Separability is the condition that allows the Galois group to be as large as possible. For a finite Galois extension $E/F$, the group$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ has order$[E:F]$; this equality depends on separability. The Galois correspondence requires a perfect matching between the degree of a sub-extension and the index of the corresponding subgroup, and separability is precisely what ensures this matching holds at every intermediate field. If an extension is normal but not separable, the automorphism group is smaller than the degree and the correspondence breaks down.

Every algebraic extension in characteristic $0$ is automatically separable, so for most classical applications over$\mathbb{Q}$, the separability condition is invisible — it is always satisfied. In positive characteristic, however, separability is a genuine and non-trivial condition. The standard source of inseparable extensions is the adjunction of$p$th roots in characteristic$p$: the extension$\mathbb{F}_p(t^{1/p})/\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ is purely inseparable, with$[\mathbb{F}_p(t^{1/p}) : \mathbb{F}_p(t)] = p$ but$[\mathbb{F}_p(t^{1/p}) : \mathbb{F}_p(t)]_{s} = 1$, because the only embedding of$\mathbb{F}_p(t^{1/p})$ into the algebraic closure over$\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ is the inclusion itself.

**Example 1.** The extension $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is separable. The minimal polynomial of$\sqrt[3]{2}$ is$x^3 - 2$, which has distinct roots$\sqrt[3]{2},\, \omega\sqrt[3]{2},\, \omega^2\sqrt[3]{2}$ in$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ (where$\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$). Since$\mathbb{Q}$ has characteristic$0$, all irreducible polynomials over$\mathbb{Q}$ are separable, and the extension is separable. There are exactly$3 = [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}):\mathbb{Q}]$ distinct$\mathbb{Q}$-embeddings into$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, one for each root.

**Example 2.** Let $F = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ and$E = F(t^{1/p})$. The element$\alpha = t^{1/p}$ has minimal polynomial$x^p - t$ over$F$. As computed in §30.2.2, this polynomial is inseparable — its only root in$\bar{F}$ is$\alpha$ with multiplicity$p$. Therefore$E/F$ is not separable; it is a purely inseparable extension of degree$p$ with separable degree$1$.

**Example 3.** The extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ is separable. Every element$\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ satisfies$\alpha^{p^n} = \alpha$, hence is a root of$x^{p^n} - x$. The derivative of$x^{p^n} - x$ is$-1 \neq 0$, so this polynomial is separable and all its roots are distinct. Since the minimal polynomial of$\alpha$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$ divides the separable polynomial$x^{p^n} - x$, the minimal polynomial is itself separable, and$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ is a separable (in fact Galois) extension of degree$n$.

**Example 4.** Let $F = \mathbb{F}_p(s, t)$ (two independent variables) and$E = F(s^{1/p}, t^{1/p})$. Both$s^{1/p}$ and$t^{1/p}$ have inseparable minimal polynomials$x^p - s$ and$x^p - t$ respectively over$F$. So$E/F$ is not separable:$[E:F] = p^2$ but$[E:F]_{s} = 1$. The only$F$-embedding of$E$ into$\bar{F}$ is the inclusion itself, illustrating that a purely inseparable extension of degree$p^2$ has trivial automorphism group and no non-trivial embeddings.

**Example 5.** Consider the tower $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt[3]{3})$. Each step is separable over$\mathbb{Q}$ and over the intermediate field, respectively:$\sqrt{2}$ has minimal polynomial$x^2 - 2$ over$\mathbb{Q}$ (two distinct roots), and$\sqrt[3]{3}$ has minimal polynomial$x^3 - 3$ over$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ (three distinct roots in$\mathbb{C}$). By the tower property of separability, the full extension$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt[3]{3})/\mathbb{Q}$ is separable of degree$6$, and there are exactly$6$ distinct$\mathbb{Q}$-embeddings into$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$.

\begin{remark}
The separability of a finite extension $E/F$ is equivalent, in the language of commutative algebra, to the condition that$E \otimes_{F} \bar{F} \cong \bar{F}^n$ as$\bar{F}$-algebras, where$n = [E:F]$. This tensor product characterization clarifies the geometric meaning: a separable extension is one whose "scheme of points" over an algebraically closed field is reduced. This perspective underlies the theory of étale algebras and smooth morphisms in algebraic geometry, where separability plays the role of the smoothness hypothesis.
\end{remark}
