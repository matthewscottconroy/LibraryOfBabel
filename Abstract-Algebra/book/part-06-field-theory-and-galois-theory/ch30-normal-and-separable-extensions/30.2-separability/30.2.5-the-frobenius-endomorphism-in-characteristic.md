# 30.2.5 The Frobenius Endomorphism in Characteristic $p$

In characteristic $p$, the map$\phi: a \mapsto a^p$ is not just a curiosity — it is a ring endomorphism of every commutative ring of characteristic$p$, and it is the fundamental structural map of the positive-characteristic world. The key calculation is that$\phi(a + b) = (a + b)^p = a^p + b^p$: the cross terms in the binomial expansion are$\binom{p}{k} a^k b^{p-k}$ for$0 < k < p$, and the binomial coefficient$\binom{p}{k}$ is divisible by$p$ for all such$k$, hence vanishes in characteristic$p$. Multiplicativity$\phi(ab) = (ab)^p = a^p b^p$ is immediate. This map$\phi$ is called the *Frobenius endomorphism*. The observation that$(a+b)^p = a^p + b^p$ in characteristic$p$ is sometimes called the "freshman's dream."

\begin{definition}
Let $F$ be a field (or commutative ring) of characteristic$p > 0$. The *Frobenius endomorphism* is the map$\phi: F \to F$ defined by$\phi(a) = a^p$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
The Frobenius $\phi: F \to F$ is a ring homomorphism. For a field$F$ of characteristic$p$,$\phi$ is injective. If$F$ is finite,$\phi$ is an automorphism.
\end{proposition}

*Proof.* Additivity follows from the binomial theorem as above. Multiplicativity is clear. If $a^p = 0$ in a field, then$a = 0$ (no zero divisors), so$\phi$ is injective. A finite injective map is bijective.$\square$

For a field $F$ of characteristic$p$, the image$F^p = \{a^p : a \in F\}$ is a subfield of$F$ isomorphic to$F$ via$\phi$. Whether$\phi$ is surjective — equivalently, whether$F = F^p$ — depends on the field. For the finite field$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$, the Frobenius is a field automorphism and generates the entire Galois group$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

The Frobenius connects directly to separability and inseparability. The inseparable polynomial $x^p - a$ (for$a \notin F^p$) is inseparable precisely because$a$ is not in the image of the Frobenius on$F$: there is no element of$F$ whose$p$th power equals$a$. An extension$F \subseteq F(a^{1/p})$ is a *purely inseparable extension* generated by a$p$th root that does not exist in$F$. Its Galois group is trivial: the only$F$-automorphism of$F(a^{1/p})$ is the identity, because$a^{1/p}$ has no conjugates — its minimal polynomial$x^p - a$ has only one distinct root in$\bar{F}$. The Frobenius thus measures precisely the "deficit" of$F$ — how far$F$ is from being closed under$p$th roots — and this deficit is the source of all inseparability.

**Example 1.** Over $\mathbb{F}_4 = \mathbb{F}_2(\alpha)$ where$\alpha^2 + \alpha + 1 = 0$: the Frobenius$\phi: a \mapsto a^2$ sends$\alpha \mapsto \alpha^2 = \alpha + 1$ (since$\alpha^2 = -\alpha - 1 = \alpha + 1$ in characteristic$2$). Checking:$\phi^2(\alpha) = \phi(\alpha + 1) = (\alpha+1)^2 = \alpha^2 + 1 = \alpha + 1 + 1 = \alpha$. So$\phi$ has order$2$ and is the nontrivial element of$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_4/\mathbb{F}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, confirming that the Frobenius generates the Galois group.

**Example 2.** Let $F = \mathbb{F}_3(t)$. The Frobenius$\phi: F \to F$ sends$\phi(t) = t^3$ and$\phi(f(t)/g(t)) = f(t^3)/g(t^3)$. The image$F^3 = \mathbb{F}_3(t^3)$, a proper subfield of$F$. The element$t \in F$ is not a cube in$F$, so$F \neq F^3$ and$F$ is not perfect. The extension$F(t^{1/3})/F$ is purely inseparable of degree$3$, with trivial Galois group:$\mathrm{Gal}(F(t^{1/3})/F) = \{\mathrm{id}\}$.

**Example 3.** In $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$, the Frobenius$\phi$ has order exactly$n$:$\phi^n = \mathrm{id}$ (since every$a \in \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ satisfies$a^{p^n} = a$) and$\phi^k \neq \mathrm{id}$ for$0 < k < n$ (since$\phi^k = \mathrm{id}$ would confine$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ inside$\mathbb{F}_{p^k}$, contradicting$[\mathbb{F}_{p^n}:\mathbb{F}_p] = n > k$). This confirms$\phi$ generates the cyclic group$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 4.** Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, consider$f(x) = x^4 + x + 1$. This is irreducible over$\mathbb{F}_2$ (no roots in$\mathbb{F}_2$ or$\mathbb{F}_4$). Let$\alpha$ be a root in$E = \mathbb{F}_{16}$. The Frobenius sends$\alpha \mapsto \alpha^2$. Since$\alpha^4 = \alpha + 1$ (the minimal polynomial relation), we can track:$\alpha^2 \mapsto \alpha^4 = \alpha + 1 \mapsto (\alpha+1)^2 = \alpha^2 + 1 \mapsto (\alpha^2+1)^2 = \alpha^4 + 1 = \alpha$. So the Frobenius permutes the four roots cyclically as$(\alpha, \alpha^2, \alpha+1, \alpha^2+1)$, a$4$-cycle in$S_4$. This confirms$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{16}/\mathbb{F}_2) = \langle \phi \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 5.** The iterated Frobenius $\phi^d$ fixes exactly$\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ inside$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ for each$d \mid n$. For$\mathbb{F}_{p^{12}}$:$\phi^1$ fixes$\mathbb{F}_p$,$\phi^2$ fixes$\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$,$\phi^3$ fixes$\mathbb{F}_{p^3}$,$\phi^4$ fixes$\mathbb{F}_{p^4}$,$\phi^6$ fixes$\mathbb{F}_{p^6}$,$\phi^{12} = \mathrm{id}$ fixes$\mathbb{F}_{p^{12}}$. The correspondence$d \mid 12 \leftrightarrow \mathbb{F}_{p^d} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^{12}}$ is the Galois correspondence for the cyclic group$\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, which has subgroups of orders$1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$ corresponding to divisors$12, 6, 4, 3, 2, 1$ (reversed by the anti-isomorphism).

The Frobenius also plays a central role in algebraic geometry and number theory beyond the elementary theory of fields. In the theory of elliptic curves over finite fields, the Frobenius endomorphism acts on the $\ell$-adic Tate module, and its characteristic polynomial encodes the number of rational points over every extension of$\mathbb{F}_p$. In$\ell$-adic cohomology and the Weil conjectures, proved by Deligne in 1974, the Frobenius is the key operator whose eigenvalues are algebraic integers of specified absolute value. The elementary map$\phi: a \mapsto a^p$ is thus the prototype for a structure that permeates modern arithmetic geometry.

\begin{theorem}
Let $p$ be prime and$n \geq 1$. The Frobenius$\phi: x \mapsto x^p$ is an element of$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ of order$n$, and$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) = \langle \phi \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The Frobenius is the unique lift to arithmetic of the intuition that "raising to the $p$th power is the characteristic-$p$ analogue of the identity." In characteristic$0$, the identity map fixes everything; in characteristic$p$, the Frobenius$x \mapsto x^p$ is the map that "acts like the identity on$\mathbb{F}_p$" (by Fermat's little theorem) and reveals the structure of extensions through its orbits on the elements of the extension field.
\end{remark}
