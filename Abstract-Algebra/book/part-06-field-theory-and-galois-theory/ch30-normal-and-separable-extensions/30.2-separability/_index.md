# 30.2 Separability

A polynomial $f \in F[x]$ is separable if it has no repeated roots in its splitting field, or equivalently if $\gcd(f, f') = 1$ in $F[x]$, where $f'$ is the formal derivative. For polynomials over fields of characteristic zero, this condition is automatically satisfied by irreducible polynomials: an irreducible $f$ of degree $n \geq 1$ has $f'$ of degree $n-1$, so $f' \neq 0$ and $f' \nmid f$, forcing $\gcd(f, f') = 1$. In characteristic zero, inseparability simply cannot arise for irreducible polynomials. The interesting and genuinely new phenomena all occur in characteristic $p$.

In characteristic $p$, the formal derivative of $x^p$ is $px^{p-1} = 0$, and more generally any polynomial in $x^p$ has zero derivative. The polynomial $x^p - a \in F[x]$, when irreducible, has derivative identically zero and factors in its splitting field as $(x - \alpha)^p$ for any root $\alpha$ — a single root of multiplicity $p$. Such a polynomial is perfectly irreducible over $F$ but completely inseparable. The Frobenius endomorphism $\phi: x \mapsto x^p$ is the mechanism behind this: it is always an injective ring homomorphism of $F$ into itself, and its failure to be surjective in certain fields of characteristic $p$ is exactly what produces inseparability. A field $F$ of characteristic $p$ is perfect — meaning all its algebraic extensions are separable — if and only if $\phi$ is surjective, i.e., every element of $F$ is a $p$-th power.

An algebraic extension $K/F$ is separable if every element of $K$ has a separable minimal polynomial over $F$. Separable extensions have a clean structure theory: they are precisely the extensions that behave like extensions in characteristic zero, with the full count of automorphisms one expects. Inseparable extensions, by contrast, are "deficient" in automorphisms — the fixed field of the full automorphism group is larger than $F$, and the Galois correspondence breaks down. Recognizing this failure and isolating exactly where separability is needed is indispensable preparation for the rigorous statement of the fundamental theorem in Chapter 31.
