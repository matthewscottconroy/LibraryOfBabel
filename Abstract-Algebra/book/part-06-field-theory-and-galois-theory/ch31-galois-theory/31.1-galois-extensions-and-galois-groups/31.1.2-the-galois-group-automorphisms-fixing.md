# 31.1.2 The Galois Group $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$: Automorphisms Fixing$F$

Given a field extension $E/F$, one can ask: in how many ways can the elements of$E$ be rearranged while leaving all of$F$ unchanged and preserving all the algebraic structure? The answer is the *Galois group*$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$, defined as the group of all field automorphisms$\sigma: E \to E$ satisfying$\sigma(a) = a$ for every$a \in F$. The group operation is composition. The elements of$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ are precisely the symmetries of$E$ that$F$ cannot distinguish.

\begin{definition}
Let $E/F$ be a field extension. The *Galois group* of$E$ over$F$ is
$$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) = \{\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(E) : \sigma(a) = a \text{ for all } a \in F\},$$
the group of field automorphisms of $E$ that fix$F$ pointwise, under composition.
\end{definition}

The connection to polynomial roots gives the Galois group its computational teeth. When $E/F$ is the splitting field of some polynomial$f \in F[x]$ with roots$\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$, every automorphism$\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ must send each$\alpha_i$ to another root of$f$. This is because$\sigma$ fixes$F$ and therefore respects any polynomial equation with coefficients in$F$: if$f(\alpha_i) = 0$ then$f(\sigma(\alpha_i)) = \sigma(f(\alpha_i)) = \sigma(0) = 0$. The automorphism$\sigma$ is thus determined entirely by where it sends the roots, giving an injective homomorphism$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) \hookrightarrow S_n$ where$n = \deg f$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $E$ be the splitting field of$f \in F[x]$ with roots$\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$. The map$\sigma \mapsto (\sigma(\alpha_1), \ldots, \sigma(\alpha_n))$ defines an injective group homomorphism$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) \hookrightarrow S_n$, and every element of$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ is completely determined by its action on the roots.
\end{proposition}

This embedding is one of the central tools for computing Galois groups. The image of $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ in$S_n$ is constrained by the algebraic relations among the roots: not every permutation of the roots necessarily extends to a field automorphism. Identifying which permutations do extend — which depends on the irreducibility of$f$ and the algebraic dependencies among its roots — is the core computational problem in Galois theory. The embedding also makes clear that$|\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)| \leq n!$, with equality only in very special cases.

An automorphism $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ must preserve not only the polynomial equations of individual elements but all algebraic structure: it is an$F$-algebra homomorphism from$E$ to itself. Since$E/F$ is algebraic and$\sigma$ is injective (field homomorphisms are always injective),$\sigma$ is also surjective, hence a field automorphism. The identity automorphism always lies in$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$, composition of$F$-fixing automorphisms is$F$-fixing, and the inverse of an$F$-fixing automorphism is$F$-fixing. So$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ is indeed a group.

**Example 1.** For $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$,$F = \mathbb{Q}$. An automorphism$\sigma: E \to E$ fixing$\mathbb{Q}$ is determined by$\sigma(\sqrt{2})$, which must be a root of the minimal polynomial$x^2 - 2$. The roots are$\pm\sqrt{2}$. So$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}) = \{\mathrm{id}, \sigma\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, where$\sigma: \sqrt{2} \mapsto -\sqrt{2}$ extends to$\sigma(a + b\sqrt{2}) = a - b\sqrt{2}$.

**Example 2.** For $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega)$ (splitting field of$x^3 - 2$, where$\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$), the Galois group$G = \mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ has order$[E:\mathbb{Q}] = 6$. Each$\sigma \in G$ is determined by$(\sigma(\sqrt[3]{2}), \sigma(\omega))$. The three roots of$x^3-2$ are$\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega^2\sqrt[3]{2}$, and$\omega$ must map to$\omega$ or$\omega^2$ (the two primitive cube roots of unity). This gives$3 \times 2 = 6$ automorphisms:
$$\sigma_1: \sqrt[3]{2}\mapsto\sqrt[3]{2},\ \omega\mapsto\omega; \quad \sigma_2: \sqrt[3]{2}\mapsto\omega\sqrt[3]{2},\ \omega\mapsto\omega; \quad \sigma_3: \sqrt[3]{2}\mapsto\omega^2\sqrt[3]{2},\ \omega\mapsto\omega$$
$$\tau_1: \sqrt[3]{2}\mapsto\sqrt[3]{2},\ \omega\mapsto\omega^2; \quad \tau_2: \sqrt[3]{2}\mapsto\omega\sqrt[3]{2},\ \omega\mapsto\omega^2; \quad \tau_3: \sqrt[3]{2}\mapsto\omega^2\sqrt[3]{2},\ \omega\mapsto\omega^2$$
These form $G \cong S_3$. The element$\sigma_2$ has order$3$ (cycling the three roots of$x^3-2$) and$\tau_1$ has order$2$ (complex conjugation restricted to$E$).

**Example 3.** For $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$,$F = \mathbb{Q}$. The automorphisms are determined by the images of$\sqrt{2}$ and$\sqrt{3}$, each of which can be sent to$\pm$ itself independently:
\begin{align*}
\sigma_1: &\sqrt{2} \mapsto \sqrt{2},\quad \sqrt{3} \mapsto \sqrt{3} \quad (\text{identity})\\
\sigma_2: &\sqrt{2} \mapsto -\sqrt{2},\quad \sqrt{3} \mapsto \sqrt{3}\\
\sigma_3: &\sqrt{2} \mapsto \sqrt{2},\quad \sqrt{3} \mapsto -\sqrt{3}\\
\sigma_4: &\sqrt{2} \mapsto -\sqrt{2},\quad \sqrt{3} \mapsto -\sqrt{3}
\end{align*}
These form $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (the Klein four-group$V_4$).

**Example 4.** For $\mathbb{F}_{16}/\mathbb{F}_2$, every automorphism must fix$\mathbb{F}_2$ pointwise. The Galois group is$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{16}/\mathbb{F}_2) = \{\mathrm{id}, \phi, \phi^2, \phi^3\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, where$\phi: x \mapsto x^2$ is the Frobenius. The embedding into$S_{15}$ (permutations of$\mathbb{F}_{16}^*$) can be made explicit, but the key point is that each automorphism is a power of the Frobenius.

**Example 5.** For $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)/\mathbb{Q}$ where$\zeta_5 = e^{2\pi i/5}$: the Galois group has order$\phi(5) = 4$. Any automorphism$\sigma_k: \zeta_5 \mapsto \zeta_5^k$ is valid for$\gcd(k,5) = 1$, i.e.,$k \in \{1,2,3,4\}$. The four automorphisms$\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3, \sigma_4$ form$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$. For example,$\sigma_2 \circ \sigma_3 = \sigma_6 = \sigma_1$ (since$6 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$), confirming the group law is multiplication mod$5$.

The embedding $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) \hookrightarrow S_n$ is one of the central tools for computing Galois groups. The image is constrained by algebraic relations among the roots, and identifying which permutations extend to automorphisms — which depends on the irreducibility of$f$ and the structure of the splitting field — is the core problem in computing a specific Galois group. The group can range from cyclic (for cyclotomic and finite field extensions) to the full symmetric group$S_n$ (for "generic" polynomials with no special symmetry).

\begin{remark}
The Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ can be nonabelian, and this is the generic case for extensions of degree$\geq 3$. The simplest nonabelian example is$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2},\omega)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$ of order$6$. The abelian extensions of$\mathbb{Q}$ are precisely those contained in cyclotomic fields (by the Kronecker–Weber theorem), and their classification is part of class field theory. For general fields, the inverse Galois problem — which groups arise as$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ for some Galois extension$E/F$? — remains one of the central open problems in algebra.
\end{remark}
