# 31.1 Galois Extensions and Galois Groups

A Galois extension is a field extension $K/F$ that is simultaneously normal and separable. These two conditions, developed at length in Chapter 30, together ensure that the group of automorphisms of $K$ fixing $F$ is as large as possible: the Galois group $\text{Gal}(K/F)$, consisting of all field automorphisms $\sigma: K \to K$ with $\sigma(a) = a$ for all $a \in F$, has order exactly $[K:F]$. This equality between the degree of the extension and the order of the group is the numerical backbone of the entire theory — it confirms that the group is exactly rich enough to encode the extension completely.

The Galois group acts on $K$ faithfully: different automorphisms do different things to elements of $K$. Each $\sigma \in \text{Gal}(K/F)$ permutes the roots of any irreducible polynomial $f \in F[x]$ that splits over $K$, because $\sigma$ sends roots of $f$ to roots of $f$ (the polynomial has coefficients in $F$ which $\sigma$ fixes). This permutation action is the bridge between abstract automorphisms and the concrete combinatorics of roots, and it is what makes the Galois group computable in examples. For an irreducible polynomial of degree $n$, the action on the $n$ roots embeds $\text{Gal}(K/F)$ as a transitive subgroup of $S_n$.

The fixed field of a subgroup $H \leq \text{Gal}(K/F)$ is the subfield $K^H = \{\alpha \in K : \sigma(\alpha) = \alpha \text{ for all } \sigma \in H\}$. Artin's theorem — that $[K : K^H] = |H|$ and $K/K^H$ is Galois with group $H$ — is the pivotal result. It says that every subgroup of $\text{Gal}(K/F)$ arises as the Galois group of $K$ over some intermediate field, and that intermediate field is exactly the fixed field. Artin's proof builds the fixed field carefully and uses the linear independence of characters — the fact that distinct field homomorphisms are linearly independent — to count dimensions precisely. This count is the hinge on which the fundamental theorem of Section 31.2 turns.
