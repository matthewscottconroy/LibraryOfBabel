# 31.2.4 Quotient Groups as Galois Groups: The Full Galois Correspondence

The Galois correspondence between subgroups of $\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)$ and intermediate fields between $F$ and $K$ is more than a bijection of sets — it is a correspondence that translates group-theoretic properties into field-theoretic ones with perfect fidelity. Among the most powerful clauses of the fundamental theorem is its treatment of normality: a subgroup $H \leq \mathrm{Gal}(K/F)$ is normal if and only if the corresponding fixed field $K^H$ is Galois over $F$ (not just over $K^H$), and in that case the quotient group $\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)/H$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Gal}(K^H/F)$.

## The Full Statement

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory]
Let $K/F$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/F)$. There is an inclusion-reversing bijection:
$$\left\{\text{subgroups } H \leq G\right\} \longleftrightarrow \left\{\text{intermediate fields } F \subseteq E \subseteq K\right\}$$
given by $H \mapsto K^H$ (the fixed field of $H$) and $E \mapsto \mathrm{Gal}(K/E)$ (the automorphisms fixing $E$). Under this correspondence:

1. $[K : K^H] = |H|$ and $[K^H : F] = [G : H]$.
2. $H$ is normal in $G$ if and only if $K^H/F$ is Galois.
3. When $H \trianglelefteq G$, the restriction map $\sigma \mapsto \sigma|_{K^H}$ gives an isomorphism $G/H \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Gal}(K^H/F)$.
\end{theorem}

The third clause is the one we develop here in depth.

## Proof of the Quotient Isomorphism

Suppose $H \trianglelefteq G$ and let $E = K^H$. We need to show that $E/F$ is Galois and that $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) \cong G/H$.

**Step 1: $E/F$ is Galois.** Being Galois means being normal and separable. $K/F$ is separable (it's Galois), so $E/F$ is separable as an intermediate extension. For normality: since $H \trianglelefteq G$, every $\sigma \in G$ satisfies $\sigma H \sigma^{-1} = H$, which means $\sigma(K^H) = K^H = E$ — i.e., $E$ is stable under every element of $G$. Since $K/F$ is normal (as a Galois extension), every embedding $E \hookrightarrow \bar{F}$ over $F$ extends to an automorphism of $K$, and that automorphism maps $E$ to $E$, so $E/F$ is normal.

**Step 2: Define the restriction map.** Consider $\phi: G \to \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ given by $\phi(\sigma) = \sigma|_E$. This is well-defined since $\sigma(E) = E$ (from Step 1). It is a group homomorphism since $(\sigma\tau)|_E = \sigma|_E \circ \tau|_E$.

**Step 3: Compute the kernel.** $\phi(\sigma) = \mathrm{id}_E$ iff $\sigma$ fixes every element of $E = K^H$ iff $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(K/K^H) = H$ (using the fundamental theorem's bijection, since $\mathrm{Gal}(K/K^H) = H$ exactly). So $\ker \phi = H$.

**Step 4: $\phi$ is surjective.** Every automorphism $\tau$ of $E$ over $F$ extends to an automorphism $\sigma$ of $K$ over $F$ (since $K/F$ is normal). Then $\phi(\sigma) = \tau$. Hence $\phi$ is surjective.

**Step 5: First isomorphism theorem.** $G/\ker\phi = G/H \cong \mathrm{im}(\phi) = \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$. $\square$

## Worked Examples

**Example 1: Splitting field of $x^4 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.**

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$, $F = \mathbb{Q}$. Then $[K:F] = 8$ and $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4$ (dihedral of order 8). Label generators: let $\sigma(\sqrt[4]{2}) = i\sqrt[4]{2}$, $\sigma(i) = i$, and $\tau(\sqrt[4]{2}) = \sqrt[4]{2}$, $\tau(i) = -i$. Then $G = \langle \sigma, \tau \mid \sigma^4 = \tau^2 = e, \tau\sigma\tau^{-1} = \sigma^{-1} \rangle$.

The center of $D_4$ is $Z(G) = \{\mathrm{id}, \sigma^2\}$, which is normal in $G$. The corresponding fixed field is:
$$K^{Z(G)} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, i)$$
since $\sigma^2(\sqrt[4]{2}) = -\sqrt[4]{2}$ and $\sigma^2(i) = i$, so $\sigma^2$ fixes $\sqrt{2} = (\sqrt[4]{2})^2$ and $i$, but moves $\sqrt[4]{2}$. The quotient $G/Z(G) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and indeed $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},i)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$, generated by $\sqrt{2} \mapsto -\sqrt{2}$ and $i \mapsto -i$ independently.

Consider instead $H = \langle \sigma \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$. This is normal in $G$ (it has index 2, so all index-2 subgroups are normal). The fixed field is:
$$K^H = \mathbb{Q}(i)$$
since $\sigma$ moves $\sqrt[4]{2}$ but fixes $i$. The quotient $G/\langle\sigma\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by complex conjugation.

**Example 2: Cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ for prime $p$.**

Here $G = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$, a cyclic group of order $p-1$. Since $G$ is abelian, every subgroup is normal. For each divisor $d$ of $p-1$, there is a unique subgroup $H_d$ of order $d$ in $G$, giving a unique intermediate field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^{H_d}$ with $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^{H_d}:\mathbb{Q}] = (p-1)/d$.

For $p = 7$: $G \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. The subgroups are $\{0\}, \langle 2 \rangle, \langle 3 \rangle, G$ of orders $1, 2, 3, 6$. The lattice of intermediate fields between $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)$ has four levels; the unique quadratic subfield is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$ (corresponding to the index-2 subgroup of $G$). The quotient $G/H_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, and the cubic subfield $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7 + \zeta_7^{-1}) = \mathbb{Q}(\cos(2\pi/7))$ has Galois group $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, as predicted.

**Example 3: Composite extensions and the tower.**

Let $K/F$ Galois with $G = S_3$, and $H = A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ (the alternating subgroup). Then $H \trianglelefteq S_3$ (since it has index 2), and $K^H/F$ is Galois with group $S_3/A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Thus $K^H/F$ is a degree-2 Galois extension, and the quadratic subfield sits at the bottom of the tower:
$$F \subseteq K^H \subseteq K, \quad [K^H:F] = 2, \quad [K:K^H] = 3.$$
The non-normal subgroup $H' = \langle (12) \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ gives a fixed field $K^{H'}$ with $[K^{H'}:F] = [G:H'] = 3$, but $K^{H'}/F$ is not Galois (since $H'$ is not normal in $S_3$), and there are three conjugate copies of $H'$, corresponding to three conjugate (non-Galois) cubic subfields.

## The Lattice Isomorphism

The Galois correspondence is an anti-isomorphism of lattices: $H_1 \subseteq H_2$ in $G$ iff $K^{H_1} \supseteq K^{H_2}$ in the field lattice. Meets and joins swap: $K^{H_1 \cap H_2} = K^{H_1} \cdot K^{H_2}$ (the compositum) and $K^{\langle H_1, H_2 \rangle} = K^{H_1} \cap K^{H_2}$. This lattice anti-isomorphism is one of the most striking features of the correspondence, and it means: the more elements a subgroup contains, the fewer elements its fixed field contains.

Normality in the group lattice corresponds to being Galois over $F$ in the field lattice. The normal closure of a subgroup corresponds to the Galois closure of the corresponding extension. This translation between group theory and field theory is the core mechanism behind the Galois-theoretic proofs of results like the Abel-Ruffini theorem (insolvability of the quintic) and the characterisation of constructible numbers.
