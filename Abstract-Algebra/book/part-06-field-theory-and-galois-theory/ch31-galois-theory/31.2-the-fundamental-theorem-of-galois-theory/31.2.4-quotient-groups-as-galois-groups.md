# 31.2.4 Quotient Groups as Galois Groups: $\mathrm{Gal}(E^H/F) \cong G/H$

When $H \trianglelefteq G$, the preceding subsection guarantees that$E^H/F$ is a Galois extension. Its Galois group is identified by the theorem$\mathrm{Gal}(E^H/F) \cong G/H$. This is one of the most striking consequences of the entire Galois correspondence: a quotient group — an abstract algebraic object — appears concretely as the symmetry group of an intermediate field extension. Quotients of the Galois group encode the Galois symmetry of sub-extensions.

\begin{theorem}
Let $E/F$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group$G$, and let$H \trianglelefteq G$ be a normal subgroup with fixed field$K = E^H$. Then$K/F$ is Galois and$\mathrm{Gal}(K/F) \cong G/H$.
\end{theorem}

The mechanism is the restriction map. Every automorphism $\sigma \in G = \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ sends$E^H$ to itself (by the normality of$H$, proved in §31.2.3), so the restriction$\sigma|_{E^H}$ is a well-defined element of$\mathrm{Aut}(E^H/F)$. The map$\rho: G \to \mathrm{Gal}(E^H/F)$ defined by$\rho(\sigma) = \sigma|_{E^H}$ is a group homomorphism. Its kernel consists of those$\sigma \in G$ that fix every element of$E^H$, which is exactly$\mathrm{Gal}(E/E^H) = H$ (by Artin's theorem). That$\rho$ is surjective — every automorphism of$E^H/F$ extends to an automorphism of$E/F$ — follows from the Galois condition on$E/F$: any$F$-embedding of a subfield of$E$ into$\bar{F}$ extends to an automorphism of$E$. The first isomorphism theorem then delivers:
$$G/H = G/\ker\rho \cong \mathrm{Im}(\rho) = \mathrm{Gal}(E^H/F).$$

The surjectivity of $\rho$ is worth dwelling on. It says that every symmetry of$E^H/F$ can be "lifted" to a symmetry of$E/F$. This is not obvious: an automorphism of$E^H$ fixing$F$ need not, a priori, extend to an automorphism of the bigger field$E$. That it does is a special property of Galois extensions, following from the fact that$E$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial and every$F$-embedding of$E$ into$\bar{F}$ is automatically an automorphism of$E$.

As $H$ varies over all normal subgroups of$G$, the quotients$G/H$ account for all the Galois sub-extensions$E^H/F$. The richer the normal subgroup structure of$G$ — for instance, if$G$ is abelian, then every subgroup is normal — the more Galois structure the lattice of extensions exhibits. This connects to the theory of solvable extensions (§32.1): a solvable group has a composition series with abelian (in fact cyclic) quotients, and the corresponding tower of fixed fields is a tower of Galois extensions with abelian Galois groups, which is the algebraic structure underlying solvability by radicals.

**Example 1.** For $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2},\omega)$,$G = S_3$,$H = A_3 \trianglelefteq S_3$. Then$K = E^{A_3} = \mathbb{Q}(\omega)$ and$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\omega)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3/A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Indeed,$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\omega)/\mathbb{Q}) = \{\mathrm{id}, \sigma\}$ where$\sigma: \omega \mapsto \omega^2$ (complex conjugation restricted to$\mathbb{Q}(\omega)$). The restriction map$\rho: S_3 \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is the sign homomorphism: even permutations restrict to the identity on$\mathbb{Q}(\omega)$, odd permutations restrict to$\sigma$.

**Example 2.** For $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)/\mathbb{Q}$ with$G = (\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$: take$H = \{1, 3\} \leq G$ (the subgroup$\{\sigma_1, \sigma_3\}$ where$\sigma_3: \zeta_8 \mapsto \zeta_8^3$). Since$G$ is abelian,$H \trianglelefteq G$ and$G/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The fixed field:$\zeta_8 + \zeta_8^3 = e^{i\pi/4} + e^{3i\pi/4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(1+i) + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(-1+i) = i\sqrt{2}$. So$E^H = \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{2})$, a quadratic extension of$\mathbb{Q}$. The Galois group$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong G/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by$i\sqrt{2} \mapsto -i\sqrt{2}$ (complex conjugation).

**Example 3.** For $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)/\mathbb{Q}$ with$G = (\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$: the unique subgroup of order$3$ is$H = \{1, 2, 4\}$ (the quadratic residues mod$7$; these are the squares:$1^2=1, 2^2=4, 3^2=2$ mod$7$). Then$H \trianglelefteq G$ (since$G$ is abelian) and$G/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The fixed field$K = E^H$ is the unique quadratic subfield of$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)$. One computes: the Gauss sum$\tau = \sum_{k=1}^{6} \left(\frac{k}{7}\right) \zeta_7^k$ satisfies$\tau^2 = -7$ (or$7$, depending on sign conventions), so$K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$. The Galois group$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong G/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 4.** For $G = D_4 \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2},i)/\mathbb{Q})$ and$H = \langle r^2 \rangle = \{e, r^2\}$ (the center$Z(D_4)$, which is normal):$G/H \cong D_4/Z(D_4) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The fixed field$K = E^H$ is the degree-$4$ sub-extension fixed by$r^2$, where$r: \sqrt[4]{2} \mapsto i\sqrt[4]{2}$,$i \mapsto i$. Then$r^2: \sqrt[4]{2} \mapsto -\sqrt[4]{2}$,$i \mapsto i$, and$E^H = \mathbb{Q}(i, \sqrt{2})$. The Galois group$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, consistent with$G/H$.

**Example 5.** For $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ with$G = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and$H$ the unique subgroup of order$d$ (for$d \mid n$): then$G/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/(n/d)\mathbb{Z}$ and$E^H = \mathbb{F}_{p^{n/d}}$. The Galois group$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{n/d}}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/(n/d)\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the Frobenius$x \mapsto x^p$ on$\mathbb{F}_{p^{n/d}}$. The formula$\mathrm{Gal}(E^H/F) \cong G/H$ specializes cleanly:$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})/(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/(n/d)\mathbb{Z}$.

\begin{remark}
The identification $\mathrm{Gal}(E^H/F) \cong G/H$ is the Galois-theoretic version of the "quotient corresponds to subcover" principle in covering space theory. In the covering space formulation, a connected covering$p: \tilde{X} \to X$ with deck transformation group$H$ (a normal subgroup of$\pi_1(X)$) gives rise to a further covering$\tilde{X}/H \to X$ with deck transformation group$\pi_1(X)/H$. The Galois analogue is: the sub-extension$E^H/F$ has Galois group$G/H$. This analogy, made precise by Grothendieck's theory of the algebraic fundamental group, shows that Galois theory is a special case of the general theory of covering spaces in algebraic geometry.
\end{remark}
