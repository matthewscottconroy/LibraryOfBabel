# 31.3.1 Galois Groups as Permutation Groups on Roots

The abstract definition of $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ as a group of field automorphisms provides the theoretical framework, but for explicit computation one needs a more concrete handle. When$E$ is the splitting field of a polynomial$f \in F[x]$ with roots$\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$ in$E$, every automorphism$\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$ is completely determined by its action on these roots. Since$\sigma$ fixes$F$ and$E = F(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n)$, knowing$\sigma(\alpha_i)$ for each$i$ determines$\sigma$ everywhere. Moreover,$\sigma$ sends roots of$f$ to roots of$f$, so it permutes$\{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n\}$. This gives an injective homomorphism$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) \hookrightarrow S_n$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $E$ be the splitting field of$f \in F[x]$ with roots$\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$. The map$\sigma \mapsto (\text{the permutation of } \{1,\ldots,n\} \text{ induced by } \sigma)$ is an injective group homomorphism$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) \hookrightarrow S_n$.
\end{proposition}

The image of this embedding is determined by which permutations of the roots are compatible with all algebraic relations among them. A permutation $\pi \in S_n$ lies in the image if and only if$\pi$ preserves every polynomial relation among the$\alpha_i$ with coefficients in$F$: for any$g \in F[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ with$g(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n) = 0$, one needs$g(\alpha_{\pi(1)}, \ldots, \alpha_{\pi(n)}) = 0$. Identifying which permutations preserve all such relations is the core problem in computing Galois groups.

A key structural fact governs the irreducible case: if $f$ is irreducible over$F$, then$\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$, viewed as a subgroup of$S_n$, acts transitively on the roots. This is because any two roots of an irreducible polynomial are conjugate over$F$ — there is an$F$-isomorphism sending one to the other — and the Galois extension allows this isomorphism to extend to a full automorphism of$E$. Transitivity restricts the possibilities severely: only transitive subgroups of$S_n$ can arise as Galois groups of irreducible polynomials.

\begin{theorem}
If $f \in F[x]$ is separable and irreducible of degree$n$, then$\mathrm{Gal}(f/F) \leq S_n$ acts transitively on the$n$ roots of$f$. In particular,$n \mid |\mathrm{Gal}(f/F)|$.
\end{theorem}

The stabilizer $\mathrm{Stab}(\alpha_1) = \{\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/F) : \sigma(\alpha_1) = \alpha_1\} = \mathrm{Gal}(E/F(\alpha_1))$. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem:$|\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)| = n \cdot |\mathrm{Gal}(E/F(\alpha_1))| = n \cdot [E:F(\alpha_1)] = [F(\alpha_1):F] \cdot [E:F(\alpha_1)] = [E:F]$, consistent with the Order Theorem.

**Example 1.** $f(x) = x^2 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, roots$\alpha_1 = \sqrt{2}$,$\alpha_2 = -\sqrt{2}$. The splitting field is$E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$. The nontrivial automorphism$\sigma: \sqrt{2} \mapsto -\sqrt{2}$ maps to the transposition$(12) \in S_2$. The image is all of$S_2$, so$\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 2.** $f(x) = x^3 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, roots$\alpha_1 = \sqrt[3]{2}$,$\alpha_2 = \omega\sqrt[3]{2}$,$\alpha_3 = \omega^2\sqrt[3]{2}$ (where$\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$). The splitting field$E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2},\omega)$ has degree$6$. Since$|S_3| = 6$, the embedding$\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \hookrightarrow S_3$ must be an isomorphism:$\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$. Explicitly:$\sigma: \sqrt[3]{2} \mapsto \omega\sqrt[3]{2},\, \omega \mapsto \omega$ corresponds to the$3$-cycle$(123)$;$\tau: \sqrt[3]{2} \mapsto \sqrt[3]{2},\, \omega \mapsto \omega^2$ corresponds to the transposition$(23)$ (it fixes$\alpha_1$ and swaps$\alpha_2 \leftrightarrow \alpha_3$).

**Example 3.** $f(x) = x^4 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, roots$\sqrt[4]{2},\, i\sqrt[4]{2},\, -\sqrt[4]{2},\, -i\sqrt[4]{2}$. The splitting field is$E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}, i)$ of degree$8$. The Galois group embeds in$S_4$ as a transitive subgroup of order$8$. The transitive subgroups of$S_4$ of order$8$ are conjugate to the dihedral group$D_4$. Explicitly:$\sigma: \sqrt[4]{2} \mapsto i\sqrt[4]{2},\, i \mapsto i$ has order$4$ (it cycles the four roots:$\sqrt[4]{2} \to i\sqrt[4]{2} \to -\sqrt[4]{2} \to -i\sqrt[4]{2} \to \sqrt[4]{2}$), and$\tau: \sqrt[4]{2} \mapsto \sqrt[4]{2},\, i \mapsto -i$ has order$2$. These generate$D_4$ with$\tau\sigma\tau^{-1} = \sigma^{-1}$, giving$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2},i)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4$.

**Example 4.** $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 1 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$. The three roots$\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$ satisfy$\alpha_i = 2\cos(2\pi k/9)$ for$k = 1, 2, 4$. The discriminant of$f$ is$\Delta = -4(-3)^3 - 27(1)^2 = 108 - 27 = 81 = 9^2$, a perfect square. By the discriminant test (§31.3.3),$\mathrm{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q}) \subseteq A_3$. Since$f$ is irreducible (no rational roots:$f(0) = 1$,$f(1) = -1$,$f(-1) = 3$, no integer roots, check rational root theorem),$\mathrm{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q})$ acts transitively, so$|\mathrm{Gal}| \geq 3$. The only transitive subgroup of$S_3$ contained in$A_3$ is$A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. So$\mathrm{Gal}(x^3 - 3x + 1/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 5.** The possible transitive subgroups of $S_n$ for small$n$ are restricted:
- $n = 2$: only$S_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
- $n = 3$:$A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ or$S_3$.
- $n = 4$: five conjugacy classes —$V_4 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$,$D_4$,$A_4$,$S_4$.
- $n = 5$: five classes —$\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$,$D_5$,$\mathrm{Fr}(20) = \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$,$A_5$,$S_5$.

For $f(x) = x^4 - 5 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$: roots$\pm 5^{1/4}$ and$\pm i 5^{1/4}$. Splitting field$E = \mathbb{Q}(5^{1/4}, i)$,$[E:\mathbb{Q}] = 8$. The only order-$8$ transitive subgroup of$S_4$ (up to conjugacy) is$D_4$, so$\mathrm{Gal}(x^4-5/\mathbb{Q}) \cong D_4$.

\begin{remark}
The problem of determining the Galois group of a given polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ is algorithmically solvable (van der Waerden, Stauduhar), but computationally demanding for large degrees. The method combines transitivity constraints, the discriminant, the resolvent cubic (for degree$4$), and for higher degrees, factorization patterns modulo primes (the Frobenius density theorem: the cycle types of Frobenius elements at unramified primes sample the conjugacy classes of$\mathrm{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q})$ with frequencies proportional to class sizes). This last method, which reduces Galois group computation to factoring polynomials over finite fields, is the basis of the Schönhage-Strassen and PARI/GP algorithms for Galois group computation.
\end{remark}
