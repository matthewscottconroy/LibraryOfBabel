# 31.3.2 Cyclotomic Fields: $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$

Among all Galois extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$, the cyclotomic fields are the most explicitly computable and the most fundamental. Let$\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i/n}$ be a primitive$n$th root of unity. The field$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ is called the$n$th *cyclotomic field*; it is the splitting field of$x^n - 1$ over$\mathbb{Q}$, or equivalently of the$n$th cyclotomic polynomial
$$\Phi_n(x) = \prod_{\substack{1 \leq k \leq n \\ \gcd(k,n)=1}} (x - \zeta_n^k),$$
which is the minimal polynomial of $\zeta_n$ over$\mathbb{Q}$, is irreducible, and has degree$\phi(n) = |(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*|$.

\begin{theorem}
The extension $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois of degree$\phi(n)$, and there is an isomorphism
$$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\sim} (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*, \qquad \sigma_k \mapsto [k],$$
where $\sigma_k: \zeta_n \mapsto \zeta_n^k$.
\end{theorem}

The Galois group is determined by where $\zeta_n$ can be sent. Any automorphism$\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q})$ must send$\zeta_n$ to another primitive$n$th root of unity, i.e., to$\zeta_n^k$ for some$k$ with$\gcd(k,n) = 1$. Since$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n^k)$ for any such$k$, this mapping extends to a valid automorphism. Distinct values of$k$ modulo$n$ give distinct automorphisms (since they differ on$\zeta_n$), and there are exactly$\phi(n)$ such values. The group operation:$\sigma_j \circ \sigma_k(\zeta_n) = \sigma_j(\zeta_n^k) = \zeta_n^{jk}$, so$\sigma_j \circ \sigma_k = \sigma_{jk}$, matching multiplication in$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$.

Cyclotomic fields are *abelian extensions* of $\mathbb{Q}$ — their Galois groups are abelian because$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ is abelian. This makes the Galois correspondence for cyclotomic fields particularly tractable: every subgroup of$(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ is normal, so every intermediate field between$\mathbb{Q}$ and$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ is Galois over$\mathbb{Q}$.

**Example 1.** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)/\mathbb{Q}$:$n = 3$,$\phi(3) = 2$,$(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^* = \{1, 2\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The two automorphisms are$\sigma_1 = \mathrm{id}$ and$\sigma_2: \zeta_3 \mapsto \zeta_3^2 = \bar{\zeta}_3$. The cyclotomic polynomial is$\Phi_3(x) = x^2 + x + 1$. The Galois group is$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by complex conjugation.

**Example 2.** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)/\mathbb{Q}$:$n = 5$,$\phi(5) = 4$,$(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})^* = \{1, 2, 3, 4\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$. The four automorphisms$\sigma_k: \zeta_5 \mapsto \zeta_5^k$ satisfy:$\sigma_2$ has order$4$ (since$2^4 = 16 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ and$2^2 = 4 \not\equiv 1$), so$\sigma_2$ generates the cyclic group. The unique subgroup of order$2$ is$\{1, 4\}$ (since$\sigma_4: \zeta_5 \mapsto \zeta_5^4 = \zeta_5^{-1}$ has order$2$). Its fixed field is$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5 + \zeta_5^{-1}) = \mathbb{Q}(2\cos(2\pi/5))$, a quadratic subfield:$2\cos(2\pi/5) = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}$, giving$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ as the unique quadratic subfield of$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$.

**Example 3.** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)/\mathbb{Q}$:$n = 8$,$\phi(8) = 4$,$(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^* = \{1, 3, 5, 7\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Check:$3^2 = 9 \equiv 1$,$5^2 = 25 \equiv 1$,$7^2 = 49 \equiv 1$ mod$8$, so each non-identity element has order$2$, confirming the Klein four-group structure. The three subgroups of order$2$ are$\{1,3\}$,$\{1,5\}$,$\{1,7\}$, corresponding to the three quadratic subfields:
- $E^{\{1,3\}}$: fixed by$\sigma_3: \zeta_8 \mapsto \zeta_8^3$. We have$\zeta_8 = e^{i\pi/4}$ and$\zeta_8^3 = e^{3i\pi/4}$. The element$\zeta_8 + \zeta_8^3 = e^{i\pi/4} + e^{3i\pi/4} = i\sqrt{2}$ is fixed, so$E^{\{1,3\}} = \mathbb{Q}(i\sqrt{2}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$.
- $E^{\{1,5\}}$: fixed by$\sigma_5: \zeta_8 \mapsto \zeta_8^5 = -\zeta_8$. The element$\zeta_8^2 = e^{i\pi/2} = i$ is fixed by$\sigma_5$ (since$\sigma_5(\zeta_8^2) = \zeta_8^{10} = \zeta_8^2$), so$E^{\{1,5\}} = \mathbb{Q}(i)$.
- $E^{\{1,7\}}$: fixed by$\sigma_7: \zeta_8 \mapsto \zeta_8^7 = \bar{\zeta}_8$. The element$\zeta_8 + \bar{\zeta}_8 = 2\cos(\pi/4) = \sqrt{2}$ is fixed, so$E^{\{1,7\}} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$.

**Example 4.** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})/\mathbb{Q}$:$n = 12$,$\phi(12) = 4$,$(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})^* = \{1, 5, 7, 11\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Note$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12}) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3, \zeta_4) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}, i)$. The three quadratic subfields correspond to the three subgroups of order$2$:$\{1,5\}$,$\{1,7\}$,$\{1,11\}$, giving$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$,$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$, and$\mathbb{Q}(i)$.

**Example 5.** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)/\mathbb{Q}$:$n = 7$,$\phi(7) = 6$,$(\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^* = \{1,2,3,4,5,6\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (cyclic:$3$ is a primitive root mod$7$ since$3^1=3, 3^2=2, 3^3=6, 3^4=4, 3^5=5, 3^6=1$ mod$7$). The subgroup lattice of$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$: subgroups of orders$1, 2, 3, 6$. The unique subgroup of order$2$ is$\{1,6\}$ (since$6 \equiv -1$ has order$2$), corresponding to the fixed field$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7 + \zeta_7^{-1}) = \mathbb{Q}(2\cos(2\pi/7))$, a totally real cubic subfield of degree$3$ over$\mathbb{Q}$. The unique subgroup of order$3$ is$\{1,2,4\}$ (the quadratic residues mod$7$), corresponding to the unique quadratic subfield$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$.

The Kronecker–Weber theorem establishes the remarkable converse: every abelian extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ is contained in some cyclotomic field. This classifies all abelian extensions of$\mathbb{Q}$: they are exactly the subfields of cyclotomic fields. Class field theory generalizes this to all number fields.

\begin{remark}
The structure $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ is the prototype for one of the deepest phenomena in number theory: the Artin reciprocity map. For a number field$K$ and an abelian extension$L/K$, the Artin map sends prime ideals of$K$ (unramified in$L$) to elements of$\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ via the Frobenius at that prime. For cyclotomic fields, the Artin map sends a prime$p \nmid n$ to the Frobenius$\sigma_p: \zeta_n \mapsto \zeta_n^p$, which is exactly the element$[p] \in (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$. The Artin reciprocity law, which states that the Artin map factors through a quotient of an idele class group, generalizes Gauss's quadratic reciprocity to all abelian extensions.
\end{remark}
