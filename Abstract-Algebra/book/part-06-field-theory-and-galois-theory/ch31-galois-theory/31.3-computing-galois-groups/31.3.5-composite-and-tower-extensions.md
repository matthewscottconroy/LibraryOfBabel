# 31.3.5 Composite and Tower Extensions

In practice, the fields that arise in number theory and geometry are rarely obtained by a single adjunction; they are built as towers or composita of simpler extensions. Understanding how Galois groups behave under these operations allows one to reduce the computation of a complex Galois group to that of its constituents, and it reveals the internal structure of the Galois group in terms of subgroups and quotients.

For a tower $F \subseteq K \subseteq E$ with$E/F$ Galois and Galois group$G$, the Galois correspondence immediately yields the tools needed. The intermediate field$K$ corresponds to the subgroup$H = \mathrm{Gal}(E/K) \leq G$, and$[K:F] = [G:H]$. Whether or not$K/F$ is itself Galois depends on whether$H$ is normal in$G$: if$H \trianglelefteq G$, then$K/F$ is Galois with$\mathrm{Gal}(K/F) \cong G/H$. The extension$E/K$ is always Galois with$\mathrm{Gal}(E/K) = H$. The tower structure of fields mirrors the subnormal series structure of groups.

For the compositum $E_1 E_2$ of two Galois extensions$E_1/F$ and$E_2/F$ inside a common algebraic closure, there is a natural diagonal embedding
$$\rho: \mathrm{Gal}(E_1 E_2 / F) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Gal}(E_1/F) \times \mathrm{Gal}(E_2/F), \quad \sigma \mapsto (\sigma|_{E_1}, \sigma|_{E_2}).$$

\begin{theorem}[Compositum Formula]
If $E_1/F$ and$E_2/F$ are finite Galois extensions with$E_1 \cap E_2 = F$, then$E_1 E_2/F$ is Galois and
$$\mathrm{Gal}(E_1 E_2 / F) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(E_1/F) \times \mathrm{Gal}(E_2/F).$$
If $E_1 \cap E_2 \supsetneq F$, then$\mathrm{Gal}(E_1 E_2/F)$ is isomorphic to the fiber product$\mathrm{Gal}(E_1/F) \times_{\mathrm{Gal}((E_1\cap E_2)/F)} \mathrm{Gal}(E_2/F)$.
\end{theorem}

*Proof sketch for the direct product case.* The map $\rho$ is injective (an automorphism trivial on both$E_1$ and$E_2$ is trivial on$E_1 E_2$). To show$\rho$ is surjective onto$G_1 \times G_2$: given$\sigma_1 \in G_1$ and$\sigma_2 \in G_2$, one needs to find$\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E_1 E_2/F)$ restricting to$\sigma_1$ and$\sigma_2$. This uses the fact that any automorphism of$E_i$ extends to an automorphism of$E_1 E_2$ (possible because$E_1 E_2$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial), together with the condition$E_1 \cap E_2 = F$ ensuring no compatibility obstruction.$\square$

**Example 1.** $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})/\mathbb{Q})$: take$E_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$,$E_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$. Claim:$E_1 \cap E_2 = \mathbb{Q}$. If$\sqrt{2} \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$, then$\sqrt{2} = a + b\sqrt{3}$ for$a, b \in \mathbb{Q}$, giving$2 = a^2 + 3b^2 + 2ab\sqrt{3}$. This forces$2ab = 0$ (comparing irrational parts), so$a = 0$ or$b = 0$. If$a = 0$:$\sqrt{2} = b\sqrt{3}$, so$2/3 = b^2 \in \mathbb{Q}$, but$\sqrt{2/3}$ is irrational. If$b = 0$:$\sqrt{2} = a \in \mathbb{Q}$, impossible. So$E_1 \cap E_2 = \mathbb{Q}$, and$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 2.** $E_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$ and$E_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$. Since$E_1 \cap E_2 \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ and$E_1 = \mathbb{Q}(i) \not\subset \mathbb{R}$, we have$E_1 \cap E_2 = \mathbb{Q}$. The compositum$E_1 E_2 = \mathbb{Q}(i, \sqrt{2})$ has$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by$\sigma: i \mapsto -i$ (complex conjugation) and$\tau: \sqrt{2} \mapsto -\sqrt{2}$.

**Example 3.** Nontrivial intersection: $E_1 = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3, \sqrt[3]{2})$ and$E_2 = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3, \sqrt[3]{3})$ both contain$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$, so$E_1 \cap E_2 \supseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3) \supsetneq \mathbb{Q}$. The Galois group of$E_1 E_2 / \mathbb{Q}$ is the fiber product$\mathrm{Gal}(E_1/\mathbb{Q}) \times_{\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)/\mathbb{Q})} \mathrm{Gal}(E_2/\mathbb{Q})$, which forces compatibility of the two automorphisms on the shared subfield$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$.

**Example 4.** Tower structure for $\mathbb{Q} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\omega) \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega)$ with$G = S_3$: the lower layer$\mathbb{Q}(\omega)/\mathbb{Q}$ has$H = A_3 \trianglelefteq S_3$ and$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\omega)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3/A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The upper layer$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2},\omega)/\mathbb{Q}(\omega)$ has Galois group$A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. The group extension$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \to S_3 \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 1$ is non-split (i.e.,$S_3 \ncong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$; rather$S_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, a semidirect product), reflecting that the bottom layer$\mathbb{Q}(\omega)/\mathbb{Q}$ is not a split quotient in any sense that would give a direct product for$G$.

**Example 5.** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{15}) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3) \cdot \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$: since$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3) \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5) = \mathbb{Q}$ (the intersection of cyclotomic fields$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)$ and$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ is$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\gcd(m,n)})$, and$\gcd(3,5) = 1$), the compositum formula gives:
$$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{15})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)/\mathbb{Q}) \times \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z})^*.$$
Check: $(\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z})^* \cong (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^* \times (\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, and$\phi(15) = \phi(3)\phi(5) = 2 \cdot 4 = 8$. The Galois group has order$8$, as expected.

\begin{remark}
The compositum formula and the tower behavior of Galois groups are central to the structure theory of abelian extensions. In class field theory, the abelian extensions of a number field $K$ are organized by their Galois groups, and the compositum formula ensures that the set of abelian extensions is closed under compositum: the compositum of two abelian extensions is abelian (since a subgroup of a product of abelian groups is abelian). The Galois group of the compositum is determined by the fiber product construction, and this is what gives the set of abelian extensions of$K$ the structure of a lattice, organized by the class field theory correspondence with the idele class group of$K$.
\end{remark}
