# 32.3.2 $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ as the Splitting Field of$x^{p^n} - x$

The identification of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ as the splitting field of$x^{p^n} - x$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$ is more than a construction — it is the key fact that makes the Galois theory of finite fields available. To apply the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, one needs$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ to be a Galois extension, and this follows immediately from the splitting field characterization together with the separability of$x^{p^n} - x$.

\begin{proposition}
The extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ is Galois of degree$n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The extension is normal by construction: $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is the splitting field of$x^{p^n} - x$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$, and splitting fields are always normal. It is separable because$x^{p^n} - x$ is separable: the formal derivative of$x^{p^n} - x$ is$p^n x^{p^n - 1} - 1 = -1$ (since$p^n \equiv 0 \pmod p$ in characteristic$p$), which is nonzero everywhere, so$\gcd(x^{p^n} - x, -1) = 1$ and the polynomial has no repeated roots. Together, normality and separability make$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ Galois. The degree is$[\mathbb{F}_{p^n}:\mathbb{F}_p] = n$, since$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is an$n$-dimensional vector space over$\mathbb{F}_p$.$\square$
\end{proof}

The Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ therefore has order$n$ by the Order Theorem ($|\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)| = [E:F]$ for Galois extensions). Its structure is computed in §32.3.4: it is cyclic, generated by the Frobenius automorphism$\phi: x \mapsto x^p$.

The elements of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ are precisely the$p^n$ roots of$x^{p^n} - x$, and the fact that these roots form a field — that the root set is closed under addition and multiplication — rests on the Frobenius endomorphism. In characteristic$p$, the map$\phi: x \mapsto x^p$ is a field endomorphism:$\phi(\alpha + \beta) = (\alpha + \beta)^p = \alpha^p + \beta^p$ by the freshman's dream (the binomial coefficients$\binom{p}{k}$ for$0 < k < p$ are all divisible by$p$), and$\phi(\alpha\beta) = (\alpha\beta)^p = \alpha^p \beta^p$. Applying$\phi$ exactly$n$ times gives the map$\phi^n: x \mapsto x^{p^n}$, and$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is exactly the fixed-point set of$\phi^n$:
$$\mathbb{F}_{p^n} = \{\alpha \in \overline{\mathbb{F}_p} : \phi^n(\alpha) = \alpha\} = \{\alpha \in \overline{\mathbb{F}_p} : \alpha^{p^n} = \alpha\}.$$
The field structure of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is a direct consequence of the fact that the fixed-point set of a field endomorphism is a subfield.

\begin{remark}
The polynomial $x^{p^n} - x$ factors over$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ as a product of all linear factors:
$$x^{p^n} - x = \prod_{\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_{p^n}} (x - \alpha).$$
This elegant identity has no analog over infinite fields: it says that every element of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is a root of$x^{p^n} - x$, and there are exactly$p^n$ elements and exactly$p^n$ roots. The polynomial$x^{p^n} - x$ is therefore the "universal polynomial" for$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ — it splits completely over$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$, has distinct roots, and its roots are exactly the elements of the field. This identity also gives a direct proof that the product of all nonzero elements of$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ equals$(-1)^{p^n}$: it is the constant term of$x^{p^n} - x$ divided by the leading coefficient, with sign.
\end{remark}

**Example 1.** $\mathbb{F}_4 = \mathbb{F}_{2^2}$: the polynomial$x^4 - x = x^{2^2} - x$ over$\mathbb{F}_2$. The derivative is$-1 = 1$ (in characteristic 2), which is nonzero, confirming separability. Over$\mathbb{F}_4$, the polynomial factors as$x(x+1)(x+\alpha)(x+\alpha+1)$ where$\alpha$ is a root of$x^2+x+1$ and$\mathbb{F}_4 = \{0, 1, \alpha, \alpha+1\}$. The extension$\mathbb{F}_4/\mathbb{F}_2$ is Galois of degree 2.

**Example 2.** $\mathbb{F}_{p^1} = \mathbb{F}_p$: for$n=1$, the polynomial is$x^p - x = x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-(p-1))$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$ (by Fermat's little theorem, every$a \in \mathbb{F}_p$ satisfies$a^p = a$). The extension$\mathbb{F}_p/\mathbb{F}_p$ is trivially Galois of degree 1, with trivial Galois group.

**Example 3.** $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}/\mathbb{F}_p$: the polynomial$x^{p^2} - x$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$. This factors over$\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ into all$p^2$ linear factors. The extension is Galois of degree 2 with Galois group$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by the Frobenius$\phi: x \mapsto x^p$. The single non-trivial automorphism$\phi$ sends$\alpha \mapsto \alpha^p$ for every$\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_{p^2}$; for example, if$\alpha$ is a root of an irreducible quadratic$x^2 + bx + c$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$, then$\phi(\alpha) = \alpha^p$ is the other root (the "conjugate") of the same polynomial.

**Example 4.** Over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the polynomial$x^8 - x = x^{2^3} - x$ factors as the product of all irreducible polynomials over$\mathbb{F}_2$ whose degree divides 3: the linear factors$x$ and$x+1$ (degree 1, corresponding to$\mathbb{F}_2 \subseteq \mathbb{F}_8$), and the irreducible cubics$x^3+x+1$ and$x^3+x^2+1$ over$\mathbb{F}_2$ (there are exactly two irreducible cubics over$\mathbb{F}_2$). Indeed,$x^8 - x = x(x+1)(x^3+x+1)(x^3+x^2+1)$. This illustrates the general fact that$x^{p^n} - x$ is the product of all monic irreducible polynomials over$\mathbb{F}_p$ of degree dividing$n$.

**Example 5.** The Galois extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^6}/\mathbb{F}_p$ has degree 6 and Galois group$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. By the Galois correspondence, its intermediate fields correspond to subgroups of$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$: the subgroup$\mathbb{Z}/1\mathbb{Z} = \{0\}$ (fixed field$\mathbb{F}_{p^6}$),$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (fixed field$\mathbb{F}_{p^3}$),$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ (fixed field$\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$), and$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (fixed field$\mathbb{F}_p$). The intermediate fields of$\mathbb{F}_{p^6}/\mathbb{F}_p$ are exactly$\mathbb{F}_p \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^2} \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^6}$ and$\mathbb{F}_p \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^3} \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^6}$.
