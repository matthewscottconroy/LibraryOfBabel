# 32.3.5 Subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$:$\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ for$d \mid n$

The Galois correspondence, applied to the cyclic Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, gives a completely explicit and elegant description of the subfield structure of$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$. The subgroups of the cyclic group$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ are in bijection with the divisors of$n$: for each$d \mid n$, there is a unique subgroup of order$d$, namely$\langle \phi^{n/d} \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}$, and no other subgroups exist. The Galois correspondence (inclusion-reversing) then gives a unique intermediate field for each divisor of$n$.

\begin{theorem}[Subfield Structure of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$]
The subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ are exactly the fields$\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ for each divisor$d$ of$n$, and each such subfield is unique. The inclusion$\mathbb{F}_{p^d} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^e}$ holds if and only if$d \mid e$. In particular, the lattice of subfields of$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ (ordered by inclusion) is isomorphic to the lattice of divisors of$n$ (ordered by divisibility).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory applied to the Galois extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ with cyclic Galois group$G = \langle \phi \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$: intermediate fields$\mathbb{F}_p \subseteq K \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ correspond bijectively to subgroups$H \leq G$. The subgroups of$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ are exactly the groups$\langle \phi^d \rangle$ for each divisor$d$ of$n$, and these have order$n/d$. The fixed field of$\langle \phi^d \rangle$ is the set of$x \in \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ with$\phi^d(x) = x$, i.e.,$x^{p^d} = x$. The solutions form the field$\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ (the unique field of order$p^d$ inside$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$). As$d$ ranges over divisors of$n$, we get all the subfields. Inclusion:$\mathbb{F}_{p^d} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^e}$ iff the fixed field of$\langle \phi^d \rangle$ is contained in the fixed field of$\langle \phi^e \rangle$ iff$\langle \phi^e \rangle \subseteq \langle \phi^d \rangle$ (inclusion reverses) iff$d \mid e$ (the subgroup$\langle \phi^e \rangle$ is contained in$\langle \phi^d \rangle$ iff$n/e$ divides$n/d$, i.e.,$d \mid e$).$\square$
\end{proof}

The theorem gives a complete structural picture: $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ contains precisely as many subfields as$n$ has divisors, and the inclusion pattern among subfields exactly mirrors the divisibility pattern among the divisors of$n$. This is arguably the most complete classification theorem in algebra: the theory of finite fields is entirely governed by divisibility of integers.

\begin{remark}
The same result can be stated in terms of containment directly: $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$ is isomorphic to a subfield of$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ if and only if$m \mid n$. This is because$\mathbb{F}_{p^m}$ consists of the roots of$x^{p^m} - x$, and these are all roots of$x^{p^n} - x$ iff$x^{p^m} - x \mid x^{p^n} - x$ iff$p^m - 1 \mid p^n - 1$ iff$m \mid n$ (a standard divisibility result:$p^m - 1 \mid p^n - 1$ iff$m \mid n$ for any prime power$p$). This gives an intrinsic characterization of the subfield containment relation without reference to the Galois correspondence.
\end{remark}

**Example 1.** Subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{p^{12}}$: the divisors of$12$ are$1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$. The subfields are:
$$\mathbb{F}_p \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^2},\ \mathbb{F}_{p^3} \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^4},\ \mathbb{F}_{p^6} \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^{12}}.$$
The inclusion lattice mirrors the divisibility lattice of $\{1,2,3,4,6,12\}$:$1 \mid 2 \mid 4 \mid 12$,$1 \mid 2 \mid 6 \mid 12$,$1 \mid 3 \mid 6 \mid 12$,$1 \mid 3 \mid 12$, and$\gcd(4,3) = 1$ so$\mathbb{F}_{p^4}$ and$\mathbb{F}_{p^3}$ meet only in$\mathbb{F}_p$.

**Example 2.** Subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{p^p}$ (where$p$ is prime): the divisors of$p$ are$1$ and$p$ (since$p$ is prime). The only subfields are$\mathbb{F}_p$ and$\mathbb{F}_{p^p}$ itself. There are no intermediate fields. This is consistent with the Galois group$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ being a simple group (cyclic of prime order), which has no proper non-trivial subgroups by Lagrange's theorem.

**Example 3.** Subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{2^6} = \mathbb{F}_{64}$: divisors of$6$ are$1, 2, 3, 6$. The subfields are$\mathbb{F}_2, \mathbb{F}_4, \mathbb{F}_8, \mathbb{F}_{64}$. The inclusions are$\mathbb{F}_2 \subset \mathbb{F}_4 \subset \mathbb{F}_{64}$ and$\mathbb{F}_2 \subset \mathbb{F}_8 \subset \mathbb{F}_{64}$, with$\mathbb{F}_4 \cap \mathbb{F}_8 = \mathbb{F}_2$ (since$\gcd(2,3) = 1$). So$\mathbb{F}_{64}$ has a "diamond" subfield lattice with$\mathbb{F}_4$ and$\mathbb{F}_8$ as the two "middle" elements.

**Example 4.** The Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{64}/\mathbb{F}_2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$, generated by$\phi: x \mapsto x^2$. The subgroup of order 2,$\langle \phi^3 \rangle = \{1, \phi^3\}$ (where$\phi^3(x) = x^8$), has fixed field$\mathbb{F}_8 = \{x : x^8 = x\} = \{x : x^{2^3} = x\}$. The subgroup of order 3,$\langle \phi^2 \rangle = \{1, \phi^2, \phi^4\}$ (where$\phi^2(x) = x^4$), has fixed field$\mathbb{F}_4 = \{x : x^4 = x\}$. The full group has fixed field$\mathbb{F}_2$, and the trivial subgroup has fixed field$\mathbb{F}_{64}$ — consistent with the Galois correspondence.

**Example 5.** Counting elements: the number of elements of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ that lie in$\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ (for$d \mid n$) but in no proper subfield of$\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ — i.e., elements of$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ of "exact degree$d$" over$\mathbb{F}_p$ — is$|\mathbb{F}_{p^d} \setminus \bigcup_{d' \mid d,\ d'<d} \mathbb{F}_{p^{d'}}|$. By inclusion-exclusion (Möbius inversion on the divisor lattice), this count equals$\sum_{d' \mid d} \mu(d/d') p^{d'}$, where$\mu$ is the Möbius function. For$n = 6$,$p = 2$: elements of exact degree 6 number$\sum_{d \mid 6} \mu(6/d) 2^d = \mu(6)\cdot 2 + \mu(3)\cdot 4 + \mu(2)\cdot 8 + \mu(1)\cdot 64 = 1\cdot 2 + (-1)\cdot 4 + (-1)\cdot 8 + 1\cdot 64 = 2 - 4 - 8 + 64 = 54$. These 54 elements are exactly the primitive elements of$\mathbb{F}_{64}/\mathbb{F}_2$ (elements whose minimal polynomial over$\mathbb{F}_2$ has degree 6).
