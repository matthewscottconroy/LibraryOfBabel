# Chapter 32 — Exercises

## Important Figures

- **Niels Henrik Abel (1802–1829)** — proved the general quintic is not solvable by radicals (1824–1826); the "Abel–Ruffini theorem"
- **Évariste Galois (1811–1832)** — complete criterion: a polynomial is solvable by radicals iff its Galois group is a solvable group; this subsumed Abel's result
- **Carl Friedrich Gauss (1777–1855)** — constructibility of the regular 17-gon (1796) using the fact that 17 is a Fermat prime; *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*

## References and Primary Sources

- **N.H. Abel, "Beweis der Unmöglichkeit, algebraische Gleichungen von höheren Graden als dem vierten allgemein aufzulösen" (1824)** — insolubility of the quintic

## Examples, Applications, and Thought Experiments

- **Insolubility of the general quintic** — a "general" degree-5 polynomial has Galois group $S_5$; $S_5$ is not solvable (since $A_5$ is simple and non-abelian, so $S_5^{(2)} = A_5 = A_5^{(1)}$ never reaches $\{e\}$); therefore no formula involving only $+,-,\times,\div,\sqrt[n]{\cdot}$ solves the general quintic
- **Impossibility of trisecting $60°$** — $\cos(20°)$ is a root of $8x^3 - 6x - 1$, which is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$; a ruler-and-compass construction of $\cos(20°)$ would produce an element of degree a power of 2 over $\mathbb{Q}$, but $\deg = 3$ is not a power of 2; trisection is impossible
- **Finite fields $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$** — for each prime $p$ and $n \geq 1$, there is a unique field of order $p^n$ (up to isomorphism); $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ generated by the Frobenius automorphism $x \mapsto x^p$; every finite field is a Galois extension of its prime subfield
- **Constructibility of the regular 17-gon** — $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{17})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/17\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}$; a cyclic group of order $2^4$; Galois extensions with group a 2-power correspond to tower of degree-2 extensions; degree-2 extensions are constructible; Gauss was 18 years old when he discovered this

## Exercises

1. Prove that the specific polynomial $f(x) = x^5 - 4x + 2$ is not solvable by radicals over $\mathbb{Q}$. (Hint: show that $f$ is irreducible, count its real roots to show the Galois group contains a transposition and a 5-cycle, and conclude that $\text{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_5$. Then show $S_5$ is not solvable by exhibiting a composition factor isomorphic to $A_5$.)

2. Show that doubling the cube is impossible by ruler and compass. That is, prove there is no ruler-and-compass construction that, given a segment of length 1, produces a segment of length $\sqrt[3]{2}$. Your proof should reduce the problem to showing $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}):\mathbb{Q}]$ is not a power of 2.

3. Prove that it is impossible to trisect the angle $60°$ using ruler and compass alone. Show that any construction of $\cos(20°)$ would place it in a degree-$2^k$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, and that $\cos(20°)$ satisfies an irreducible cubic, yielding a contradiction.

4. For each $n \in \{3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12\}$, determine whether the regular $n$-gon is constructible by ruler and compass. Justify each answer using either the Fermat prime criterion for $n$ a prime power or the multiplicativity of degrees for composite $n$.

5. Let $p$ be prime. Prove that there is a unique field of order $p^n$ for each $n \geq 1$, by showing that any two fields of order $p^n$ are both splitting fields of $x^{p^n} - x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and hence isomorphic.

6. Let $\phi: x \mapsto x^p$ be the Frobenius automorphism of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Prove that $\phi$ generates $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p)$, and use the Galois correspondence to show that the subfields of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ are exactly $\mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ for each divisor $d$ of $n$.

7. The multiplicative group $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}^*$ is cyclic of order $p^n - 1$. Accepting this fact, determine the number of elements of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ that are primitive — that is, that generate the multiplicative group. Express your answer using Euler's totient function $\phi(p^n - 1)$.

8. (Challenge) A regular $n$-gon is constructible if and only if $n = 2^k p_1 p_2 \cdots p_r$ where $p_1, \ldots, p_r$ are distinct Fermat primes (primes of the form $2^{2^m}+1$). Assuming the characterization of constructible numbers as those lying in a $2$-power degree extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, and the fact that $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$, prove the "if" direction: if $n$ has this form, then $[Q(\zeta_n) : \mathbb{Q}]$ is a power of 2, and therefore $\zeta_n$ is constructible.
