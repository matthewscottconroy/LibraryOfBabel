# 33.1.5 Opposite (Dual) Categories

Every category carries within it a mirror image of itself, obtained by formally reversing all the arrows. The *opposite category* (or *dual category*) $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ has the same objects as$\mathcal{C}$, but its hom-sets are defined by$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(A, B) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(B, A)$. A morphism that went from$B$ to$A$ in$\mathcal{C}$ is reinterpreted as going from$A$ to$B$ in$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$. Composition in$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the reverse of composition in$\mathcal{C}$: if$f \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(A, B)$ and$g \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(B, C)$ correspond to morphisms$f' \colon B \to A$ and$g' \colon C \to B$ in$\mathcal{C}$, then$g \circ_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}} f$ corresponds to$f' \circ_{\mathcal{C}} g' \colon C \to A$ in$\mathcal{C}$. One verifies that$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ satisfies the category axioms (associativity and unit laws follow immediately from those of$\mathcal{C}$ by reversal), and that$(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}} = \mathcal{C}$ exactly.

The key insight is the *duality principle*: any theorem about categories, stated in purely categorical language, yields a dual theorem obtained by replacing every category $\mathcal{C}$ with$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ throughout. Products become coproducts, limits become colimits, monomorphisms become epimorphisms, initial objects become terminal objects, and so on. Every categorical theorem comes for free with a companion dual theorem, obtained by reversing all arrows in the statement. In practice, this halves the work: one proves one version and records the dual without a separate proof. The duality principle is not a vague analogy but a precise theorem: if$P$ is a property that every category satisfies, then$P$ is self-dual (equivalent to its own dual), and if$P(\mathcal{C})$ holds, then$P^{\mathrm{op}}(\mathcal{C})$ holds where$P^{\mathrm{op}}$ is$P$ with all arrows reversed.

The opposite category also has direct algebraic content beyond its role in duality arguments. A *contravariant functor* $F \colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ (Section 33.3.2) is equivalently a covariant functor$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{D}$, so the opposite category is the natural domain for contravariant phenomena. The *presheaf category*$[\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$, whose objects are contravariant set-valued functors on$\mathcal{C}$, arises centrally in the Yoneda lemma (Section 34.2). In algebra, the opposite of$R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ (left$R$-modules) is equivalent to the category of right$R^{\mathrm{op}}$-modules, where$R^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the ring with the multiplication order reversed:$r \cdot_{\mathrm{op}} s = sr$. This algebraic duality reflects the categorical duality at a structural level.

\begin{definition}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category. The \emph{opposite category}$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ has the same objects as$\mathcal{C}$, with$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(A, B) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(B, A)$ and composition defined by$g \circ_{\mathrm{op}} f = f \circ g$ (where$\circ$ is composition in$\mathcal{C}$). The identity morphism at$A$ in$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the same as$\mathrm{id}_{A}$ in$\mathcal{C}$.
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** In the poset $(\mathbb{N}, \leq)$, the opposite category is$(\mathbb{N}, \geq)$: a morphism from$m$ to$n$ in$\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{op}}$ exists if and only if$m \geq n$. Limits in the original poset correspond to greatest lower bounds; limits in the opposite correspond to least upper bounds. The duality between "sup" and "inf" in order theory is the categorical duality between limits and colimits in the poset case.

**Example 2.** The opposite of $\mathbf{Set}$ is$\mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}}$, which has sets as objects but with arrows reversed. This is not equivalent to$\mathbf{Set}$ itself — there is no obvious algebraic category equivalent to$\mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}}$ — but it arises naturally in the study of Boolean algebras (Stone duality provides an equivalence$\mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}} \simeq \mathbf{CABool}$, the category of complete atomic Boolean algebras). The Stone duality theorem is an instance of the duality principle: the theory of sets is "dual" to the theory of complete atomic Boolean algebras.

**Example 3.** For a ring $R$, the opposite ring$R^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the ring with the same underlying set and additive structure, but with multiplication$r \cdot_{R^{\mathrm{op}}} s = sr$. The category$R^{\mathrm{op}}\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ of left$R^{\mathrm{op}}$-modules is equivalent to the category$\mathbf{Mod}\text{-}R$ of right$R$-modules. Under the correspondence$\mathcal{C} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$, left modules become right modules. When$R$ is commutative,$R \cong R^{\mathrm{op}}$, so the distinction between left and right modules disappears.

**Example 4.** Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an abelian category. The opposite category$\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}$ is also abelian. A short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ in$\mathcal{A}$ corresponds (by reversing arrows) to a short exact sequence$0 \to C \to B \to A \to 0$ in$\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}$. Injective objects in$\mathcal{A}$ correspond to projective objects in$\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}$, and vice versa. The duality between projective and injective resolutions in homological algebra is a direct consequence of the duality of abelian categories.

**Example 5.** For a group $G$ viewed as a one-object category$\mathbf{B}G$, the opposite category$(\mathbf{B}G)^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the category$\mathbf{B}G^{\mathrm{op}}$ where$G^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the group$G$ with multiplication reversed:$g \cdot_{\mathrm{op}} h = hg$. When$G$ is abelian,$G^{\mathrm{op}} \cong G$, so$(\mathbf{B}G)^{\mathrm{op}} \cong \mathbf{B}G$. A left$G$-action on a set$X$ corresponds to a functor$\mathbf{B}G \to \mathbf{Set}$; a right$G$-action corresponds to a functor$(\mathbf{B}G)^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}$, which is equivalently a functor$\mathbf{B}G^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}$. The duality between left and right group actions is categorical duality.

The opposite category construction is in some sense the simplest non-trivial operation one can perform on a category, yet it has far-reaching consequences. The presheaf category $[\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ built from the opposite of$\mathcal{C}$ is the setting for the Yoneda embedding, which shows that$\mathcal{C}$ embeds into its own presheaf category. The duality principle, applied systematically, means that every result about limits has a companion result about colimits, every result about products has a companion about coproducts, and so on — a remarkable economy in the organization of mathematical knowledge.
