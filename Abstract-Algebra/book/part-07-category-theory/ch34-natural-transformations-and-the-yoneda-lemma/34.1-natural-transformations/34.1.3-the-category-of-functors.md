# 34.1.3 The Category $[\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}]$ of Functors

Natural transformations are not merely a convenient language for comparing functors — they are the morphisms in a category of functors. This observation elevates the entire discussion: functors themselves become objects in a new category, and natural transformations become morphisms between those objects.

\begin{definition}
For a small category $\mathcal{C}$ and any category$\mathcal{D}$, the *functor category*$[\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}]$ (also written$\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}$) has:
- **Objects**: functors $F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$,
- **Morphisms**: natural transformations $\eta: F \Rightarrow G$,
- **Composition** (*vertical composition*): given $\eta: F \Rightarrow G$ and$\mu: G \Rightarrow H$, the composite$\mu \circ \eta: F \Rightarrow H$ has components$(\mu \circ \eta)_A = \mu_A \circ \eta_A$,
- **Identities**: the identity natural transformation $\mathrm{id}_F: F \Rightarrow F$ has components$(\mathrm{id}_F)_A = \mathrm{id}_{F(A)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
Vertical composition is well-defined: if $\eta: F \Rightarrow G$ and$\mu: G \Rightarrow H$ are natural transformations, then$\mu \circ \eta: F \Rightarrow H$ is a natural transformation.
\end{proposition}

*Proof.* For any morphism $f: A \to B$ in$\mathcal{C}$, we need$(\mu \circ \eta)_B \circ F(f) = H(f) \circ (\mu \circ \eta)_A$. Expanding:$(\mu_B \circ \eta_B) \circ F(f) = \mu_B \circ (\eta_B \circ F(f)) = \mu_B \circ (G(f) \circ \eta_A) = (\mu_B \circ G(f)) \circ \eta_A = (H(f) \circ \mu_A) \circ \eta_A = H(f) \circ (\mu_A \circ \eta_A)$, using naturality of$\eta$ then naturality of$\mu$.$\square$

The smallness hypothesis on $\mathcal{C}$ is essential: a natural transformation$\eta: F \Rightarrow G$ is a family of morphisms indexed by the objects of$\mathcal{C}$, and for this family to form a set (rather than a proper class), the objects of$\mathcal{C}$ must constitute a set. Without smallness,$[\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}]$ may not be a legitimate category, and one must invoke universe-theoretic caution.

**Example 1.** Let $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{1}$ be the category with a single object$*$ and only its identity morphism. A functor$F: \mathbf{1} \to \mathcal{D}$ is just a choice of object$F(*) \in \mathcal{D}$, and a natural transformation$\eta: F \Rightarrow G$ is just a morphism$\eta_*: F(*) \to G(*)$ in$\mathcal{D}$ (with no other conditions since there are no non-identity morphisms). Thus$[\mathbf{1}, \mathcal{D}] \cong \mathcal{D}$: the functor category from the one-object category is isomorphic to$\mathcal{D}$ itself.

**Example 2.** Let $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{2} = \{0 \to 1\}$ be the category with two objects and one non-identity morphism. A functor$F: \mathbf{2} \to \mathcal{D}$ is a choice of morphism$F(0 \to 1): F(0) \to F(1)$ in$\mathcal{D}$. A natural transformation$\eta: F \Rightarrow G$ between two such functors (two morphisms$f: A \to B$ and$g: C \to D$) consists of$\eta_0: A \to C$ and$\eta_1: B \to D$ such that$g \circ \eta_0 = \eta_1 \circ f$. This is exactly a commutative square in$\mathcal{D}$. So$[\mathbf{2}, \mathcal{D}]$ is the category of morphisms (arrows) in$\mathcal{D}$, with commutative squares as morphisms.

Beyond vertical composition, there is also *horizontal composition* of natural transformations: given $\eta: F \Rightarrow G$ between functors$\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ and$\mu: H \Rightarrow K$ between functors$\mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{E}$, one forms$\mu * \eta: H \circ F \Rightarrow K \circ G$ by$(\mu * \eta)_A = \mu_{G(A)} \circ H(\eta_A) = K(\eta_A) \circ \mu_{F(A)}$ (these two definitions agree by the naturality of$\mu$).

\begin{theorem}[Interchange Law]
Vertical and horizontal composition satisfy the *interchange law*: $((\mu' \circ \mu) * (\eta' \circ \eta)) = (\mu' * \eta') \circ (\mu * \eta)$ whenever all composites are defined. This makes$\mathbf{Cat}$ into a 2-category.
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** In $[\mathbf{Grp}, \mathbf{Ab}]$, the functor category of functors from groups to abelian groups, the abelianization functors$\mathrm{Ab}: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Ab}$ and the forgetful-then-abelianize composite all live as objects. Natural transformations between them encode uniform operations on abelianizations. This functor category is a natural setting for studying operations on homology functors.

**Example 4.** For $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ (taking the opposite), the functor category$[\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ is the *presheaf category* on$\mathcal{C}$. Its objects are contravariant set-valued functors on$\mathcal{C}$, called *presheaves*. The Yoneda embedding (Section 34.2.4) embeds$\mathcal{C}$ fully and faithfully into its presheaf category, which serves as the natural "completion" of$\mathcal{C}$ and is the ambient universe for sheaf theory and topos theory.

\begin{remark}
The functor categories $[\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Set}]$ and$[\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ are of particular importance. They are always complete and cocomplete (have all small limits and colimits), and every small category embeds into its presheaf category via the Yoneda embedding. Representation theory can be formulated as the study of functor categories: a representation of a group$G$ is a functor from$G$ (viewed as a one-object category) to$\mathbf{Vect}_k$, and the category of representations is a functor category.
\end{remark}
\par
The functor category construction is functorial in both variables: for fixed $\mathcal{D}$, a functor$F: \mathcal{C}' \to \mathcal{C}$ induces a "restriction" functor$F^*: [\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}] \to [\mathcal{C}', \mathcal{D}]$ by precomposition. The existence of left and right adjoints to$F^*$ — left and right Kan extensions — is one of the deepest theorems in category theory, and it generalizes many classical constructions in algebra and topology.
