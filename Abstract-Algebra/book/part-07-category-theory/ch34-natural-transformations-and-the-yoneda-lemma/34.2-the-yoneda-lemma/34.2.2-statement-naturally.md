# 34.2.2 Statement: $\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F) \cong F(A)$, Naturally

The Yoneda Lemma is one of the most important results in all of mathematics, and it is simultaneously one of the simplest to state. Its importance comes not from any technical difficulty but from its conceptual reach: it says that an object is completely determined by the functor of maps out of it.

\begin{theorem}[Yoneda Lemma]
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a locally small category, let$A \in \mathcal{C}$ be an object, and let$F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}$ be any functor. Then there is a bijection
$$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F) \cong F(A),$$
where $\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F)$ denotes the set of natural transformations from$\mathrm{h}^A = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, -)$ to$F$. Moreover, this bijection is natural in both$A$ (functorial in$A \in \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$) and$F$ (functorial in$F \in [\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Set}]$).
\end{theorem}

The content of the bijection is this: a natural transformation $\eta: \mathrm{h}^A \Rightarrow F$ is completely determined by where it sends the identity morphism$\mathrm{id}_A \in \mathrm{Hom}(A, A) = \mathrm{h}^A(A)$. Given the value$\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A) \in F(A)$, the naturality condition forces the value of every component$\eta_B$ at every morphism$f: A \to B$: one must have$\eta_B(f) = F(f)(\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A))$. The single element$\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A) \in F(A)$ determines the entire natural transformation.

**Example 1.** Take $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{Set}$,$A = \{*\}$ (a one-element set), and$F = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{Set}}$. The Yoneda Lemma gives$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{Hom}(\{*\}, -), \mathrm{id}) \cong \mathrm{id}(\{*\}) = \{*\}$. Indeed,$\mathrm{Hom}(\{*\}, B) \cong B$ naturally (a map from$\{*\}$ to$B$ is just a choice of element of$B$), and a natural transformation$\eta: \mathrm{Hom}(\{*\}, -) \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ consists of maps$\eta_B: B \to B$ that commute with all functions. The only such family is the identity family$\eta_B = \mathrm{id}_B$, and this corresponds to the unique element of$\{*\}$. There is exactly one natural transformation from$\mathrm{Hom}(\{*\}, -)$ to the identity, just as the Yoneda Lemma predicts.

**Example 2.** Take $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{Grp}$,$A = \mathbb{Z}$,$F = U$ (the forgetful functor to$\mathbf{Set}$). Then$\mathrm{h}^{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(\mathbb{Z}, -)$ and$\mathrm{h}^{\mathbb{Z}}(G) \cong U(G)$ naturally (a homomorphism$\mathbb{Z} \to G$ is determined by the image of$1$). The Yoneda Lemma gives$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^{\mathbb{Z}}, U) \cong U(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$. Indeed, a natural transformation$\eta: \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, -) \Rightarrow U$ is determined by$\eta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Z}}) \in \mathbb{Z}$. Given$n \in \mathbb{Z}$, the corresponding natural transformation has component$\eta_G(f) = f(n)$ — evaluate the homomorphism$f: \mathbb{Z} \to G$ at$n$. There is exactly one such natural transformation for each$n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and the set of natural transformations is in bijection with$\mathbb{Z}$, as predicted.

**Example 3.** Take $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{Ring}$,$A = \mathbb{Z}[x]$,$F = U$ (underlying set). Then$\mathrm{h}^{\mathbb{Z}[x]}(R) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ring}}(\mathbb{Z}[x], R) \cong U(R)$ since a ring map from$\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is determined by the image of$x$. The Yoneda Lemma gives$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^{\mathbb{Z}[x]}, U) \cong U(\mathbb{Z}[x]) = \mathbb{Z}[x]$ (as a set, the underlying set of the polynomial ring). Natural transformations$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}[x], -) \Rightarrow U$ correspond to elements of$\mathbb{Z}[x]$, i.e., to polynomials$p(x) \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$. The corresponding natural transformation sends a ring homomorphism$\phi: \mathbb{Z}[x] \to R$ to$\phi(p(x)) = p(\phi(x)) \in R$.

The naturality of the bijection in $A$ and$F$ means: as$A$ varies along a morphism$g: A' \to A$ in$\mathcal{C}$, the bijections commute with the induced maps on natural transformations and on$F(A)$; and as$F$ varies along a natural transformation$\tau: F \Rightarrow G$, the bijections commute with the induced maps as well. Explicitly, for$g: A' \to A$, the bijection$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F) \cong F(A)$ is compatible with the bijection$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^{A'}, F) \cong F(A')$ via the map$F(g): F(A) \to F(A')$ and the precomposition map$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F) \to \mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^{A'}, F)$ given by$\eta \mapsto \eta \circ (g_*)$ where$g_*: \mathrm{h}^{A'} \Rightarrow \mathrm{h}^A$ is post-composition by$g$.

\begin{remark}
The surprise of the Yoneda Lemma is the size of what it claims. A natural transformation $\eta: \mathrm{h}^A \Rightarrow F$ is, a priori, an indexed family of functions$\eta_B: \mathrm{Hom}(A, B) \to F(B)$ for all objects$B$ in$\mathcal{C}$, satisfying infinitely many commutativity conditions (one for each morphism in$\mathcal{C}$). Yet the entire structure is captured by the single element$\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A) \in F(A)$. The naturality constraints are so rigid that they determine everything from this one value.
\end{remark}

**Example 4.** The Yoneda lemma for the "evaluation" natural transformation. In $\mathbf{Vect}_k$, fix$V$ and let$F = \mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{Vect}_k}$. The Yoneda bijection$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{Hom}(V, -), \mathrm{id}) \cong V$ sends each natural transformation$\eta$ to$\eta_V(\mathrm{id}_V) \in V$. The natural transformation corresponding to$v \in V$ has component$\eta_B(f) = f(v)$ for$f: V \to B$ — "evaluate at$v$." These are all the natural transformations, parametrized by elements of$V$. This is consistent with the general principle: natural endomorphisms of the identity functor on a "simple" category (one with essentially one generating object) are scalar-like.

The naturality of the bijection in $A$ means: as$A$ varies, the bijections assemble into a natural isomorphism of functors$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^{(-)}, F) \cong F(-)$ from$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ to$\mathbf{Set}$. This is the statement that the Yoneda embedding$\mathbf{y}: \mathcal{C} \to [\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ is fully faithful — a consequence that is developed in Section 34.2.4 and which shows that$\mathcal{C}$ can be completely recovered from its presheaf category via the Yoneda embedding.
