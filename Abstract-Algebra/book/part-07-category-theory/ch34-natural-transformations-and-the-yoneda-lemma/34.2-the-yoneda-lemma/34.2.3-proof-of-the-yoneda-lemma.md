# 34.2.3 Proof of the Yoneda Lemma

The proof of the Yoneda Lemma is a study in how far the naturality condition forces one's hand. It is, as Saunders Mac Lane observed, one of the "purest" proofs in mathematics: at every step, there is essentially only one thing to write down, and the proof writes itself. We present it in full, emphasizing at each step the logical necessity of each choice.

**Setup.** We construct the bijection $\Phi: \mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F) \xrightarrow{\sim} F(A)$ and its inverse$\Psi: F(A) \to \mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F)$.

*The map $\Phi$:* Given a natural transformation$\eta: \mathrm{h}^A \Rightarrow F$, define$\Phi(\eta) = \eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A) \in F(A)$. We evaluate the component at$A$ on the identity morphism.

*The map $\Psi$:* Given an element$x \in F(A)$, define a natural transformation$\eta^x: \mathrm{h}^A \Rightarrow F$ by setting$\eta^x_B(f) = F(f)(x)$ for each object$B$ and each morphism$f \in \mathrm{Hom}(A, B) = \mathrm{h}^A(B)$. The component$\eta^x_B$ applies the action of$F$ on the morphism$f$ to the starting element$x$.

**Verification that $\eta^x$ is natural.** Take any morphism$g: B \to C$ in$\mathcal{C}$. We must check that$\eta^x_C \circ \mathrm{h}^A(g) = F(g) \circ \eta^x_B$, i.e., that both sides agree on any$f \in \mathrm{Hom}(A, B)$:
- Left side: $\eta^x_C(\mathrm{h}^A(g)(f)) = \eta^x_C(g \circ f) = F(g \circ f)(x) = (F(g) \circ F(f))(x)$, using functoriality of$F$.
- Right side: $F(g)(\eta^x_B(f)) = F(g)(F(f)(x)) = (F(g) \circ F(f))(x)$.
These agree, so $\eta^x$ is natural.$\checkmark$

**Verification that $\Phi \circ \Psi = \mathrm{id}_{F(A)}$.** For$x \in F(A)$:$\Phi(\eta^x) = \eta^x_A(\mathrm{id}_A) = F(\mathrm{id}_A)(x) = \mathrm{id}_{F(A)}(x) = x$.$\checkmark$

**Verification that $\Psi \circ \Phi = \mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Nat}}$.** For a natural transformation$\eta: \mathrm{h}^A \Rightarrow F$, we must show$\eta^{\Phi(\eta)} = \eta$, i.e., that$\eta^{\Phi(\eta)}_B(f) = \eta_B(f)$ for all$B$ and$f: A \to B$. We compute:
$$\eta^{\Phi(\eta)}_B(f) = F(f)(\Phi(\eta)) = F(f)(\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A)).$$
Now apply the naturality of $\eta$ to the morphism$f: A \to B$: the naturality square gives$\eta_B \circ \mathrm{h}^A(f) = F(f) \circ \eta_A$. Evaluating both sides at$\mathrm{id}_A \in \mathrm{Hom}(A, A)$:$\eta_B(\mathrm{h}^A(f)(\mathrm{id}_A)) = F(f)(\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A))$, i.e.,$\eta_B(f \circ \mathrm{id}_A) = F(f)(\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A))$, i.e.,$\eta_B(f) = F(f)(\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A)) = \eta^{\Phi(\eta)}_B(f)$.$\checkmark$

This completes the proof that $\Phi$ and$\Psi$ are mutual inverses, establishing the bijection$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F) \cong F(A)$.

**Naturality in $F$.** For a natural transformation$\tau: F \Rightarrow G$, the diagram
$$\begin{array}{ccc} \mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F) & \xrightarrow{\Phi_F} & F(A) \\ \downarrow \tau_* && \downarrow \tau_A \\ \mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, G) & \xrightarrow{\Phi_G} & G(A) \end{array}$$
must commute. For $\eta \in \mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F)$: the right-then-down path gives$\tau_A(\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A))$; the down-then-right path gives$\Phi_G(\tau \circ \eta) = (\tau \circ \eta)_A(\mathrm{id}_A) = \tau_A(\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A))$. These agree.$\checkmark$

**Naturality in $A$.** For a morphism$g: A' \to A$ in$\mathcal{C}$, the diagram involving the induced maps on natural transformations (precomposition by$g_*: \mathrm{h}^{A'} \Rightarrow \mathrm{h}^A$) and$F(g): F(A) \to F(A')$ commutes by a similar calculation.

**Example 1.** Let us trace through the proof for $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{Vect}_k$,$A = k$ (the ground field, a 1-dimensional space),$F = \mathrm{id}$. The Yoneda bijection gives$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{Hom}(k, -), \mathrm{id}) \cong k$. A natural transformation$\eta: \mathrm{Hom}(k, -) \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ is determined by$\eta_k(\mathrm{id}_k) \in k$. Given$\lambda \in k$, the corresponding transformation has component$\eta_V(f) = f(\eta_k(\mathrm{id}_k)) = f(\lambda)$ for$f: k \to V$ (so$f$ is determined by$f(1) \in V$ and$\eta_V(f) = \lambda \cdot f(1)$). Each scalar$\lambda$ gives a distinct natural transformation, confirming the bijection.

**Example 2.** The proof for $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{Set}$,$A = \{0,1\}$,$F = \mathcal{P}$ (power set). Yoneda gives$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{Hom}(\{0,1\}, -), \mathcal{P}) \cong \mathcal{P}(\{0,1\}) = \{\emptyset, \{0\}, \{1\}, \{0,1\}\}$. A natural transformation$\eta: \mathrm{Hom}(\{0,1\}, -) \Rightarrow \mathcal{P}$ is determined by$\eta_{\{0,1\}}(\mathrm{id}) \in \mathcal{P}(\{0,1\})$, which is one of four subsets. Each choice of subset gives a unique natural family of maps$\eta_B: B^2 \to \mathcal{P}(B)$ (where$B^2 = \mathrm{Hom}(\{0,1\}, B)$), and naturality forces the formula.

\begin{remark}
The proof reveals an important meta-principle: whenever one works with a natural transformation out of a representable functor, the data reduces to a single element. This is why universal properties, which are statements of representability, always give clean and complete characterizations of objects.
\end{remark}

The purity of this proof — its self-writing quality — has deep philosophical significance. The naturality squares, which seem to impose an infinite number of commutativity conditions, are so rigid that they collectively reduce to a single condition: the value at the identity. This reflects the fact that the identity morphism is the universal element of the representable functor $\mathrm{h}^A$ at$A$: every morphism$f: A \to B$ is the image of$\mathrm{id}_A$ under the action of$\mathrm{h}^A(f) = f_*$. The Yoneda lemma is the precise statement that this "universal" quality of the identity propagates to determine all natural transformations.

**Example 3.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a small category and$F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}$ any functor. For$A \in \mathcal{C}$ and the representable functor$\mathrm{h}^A$, the Yoneda bijection$\Phi: \mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, F) \cong F(A)$ is explicitly: given$\eta$, evaluate at$A$ on$\mathrm{id}_A$. Given$x \in F(A)$, the natural transformation$\eta^x$ has components$\eta^x_B(f) = F(f)(x)$. When$F = \mathrm{h}^C$ for another object$C$, the bijection gives$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}^A, \mathrm{h}^C) \cong \mathrm{h}^C(A) = \mathrm{Hom}(A, C)$: natural transformations between representable functors are in bijection with morphisms between the representing objects. This is the full faithfulness of the Yoneda embedding, obtained as a special case of the Yoneda lemma itself.
