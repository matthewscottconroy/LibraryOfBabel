# 34.2.4 The Yoneda Embedding: $\mathcal{C} \hookrightarrow [\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$

The Yoneda Lemma has an immediate structural consequence: every category $\mathcal{C}$ embeds fully and faithfully into a category of set-valued functors. This embedding — the Yoneda embedding — is the categorical analogue of Cayley's theorem in group theory, which embeds every group into a symmetric group (by having the group act on itself by left multiplication).

\begin{definition}
The *Yoneda embedding* is the functor $\mathbf{y}: \mathcal{C} \to [\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ defined by:
- On objects: $\mathbf{y}(A) = \mathrm{h}_A = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, A)$, the contravariant representable functor at$A$,
- On morphisms: for $f: A \to B$ in$\mathcal{C}$, the natural transformation$\mathbf{y}(f): \mathrm{h}_A \Rightarrow \mathrm{h}_B$ has components$(\mathbf{y}(f))_C(g) = f \circ g$ for$g: C \to A$.
\par
The target category $[\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ is the *presheaf category* on$\mathcal{C}$, whose objects are contravariant set-valued functors (presheaves) and whose morphisms are natural transformations.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
The Yoneda embedding $\mathbf{y}: \mathcal{C} \to [\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ is fully faithful.
\end{theorem}

*Proof.* Full faithfulness means that for any two objects $A, B \in \mathcal{C}$, the map$\mathbf{y}_{A,B}: \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, B) \to \mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}_A, \mathrm{h}_B)$ is a bijection. Apply the Yoneda Lemma to the contravariant functor$\mathrm{h}_B = \mathrm{Hom}(-, B): \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}$, with representing object$B$ (viewed in$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$). The Yoneda Lemma gives$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{h}_A, \mathrm{h}_B) \cong \mathrm{h}_B(A) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, B)$. One checks that the bijection is exactly the map$\mathbf{y}_{A,B}$.$\square$

Full faithfulness says: $\mathcal{C}$ embeds into its presheaf category without losing or gaining morphisms. Two objects of$\mathcal{C}$ are isomorphic if and only if their images under$\mathbf{y}$ are isomorphic in$[\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$.

**Example 1.** Consider $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{Set}$. The Yoneda embedding sends each set$A$ to the functor$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Set}}(-, A): \mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}$. The full faithfulness says: natural transformations between$\mathrm{Hom}(-, A)$ and$\mathrm{Hom}(-, B)$ are in bijection with functions$A \to B$. This is a non-trivial statement: to specify a natural transformation between these two hom-functors is exactly the same data as specifying a single function$A \to B$.

**Example 2.** For a group $G$ viewed as a one-object category$\mathbf{B}G$, the Yoneda embedding$\mathbf{B}G \hookrightarrow [(\mathbf{B}G)^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ sends the unique object$*$ to the contravariant representable functor, which is$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}G}(*, *) = G$ with the right$G$-action. The presheaf category$[(\mathbf{B}G)^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ is the category of right$G$-sets. The Yoneda embedding embeds the single object$*$ into the$G$-set$G$ (with right multiplication), which is Cayley's theorem: every group embeds into the symmetric group on its own underlying set, here realized as the$G$-set of right translations.

**Example 3.** For a poset $(P, \leq)$ viewed as a category, the Yoneda embedding sends$x \in P$ to the *down-set functor* (or *principal down-set*)${\downarrow}x = \{y \in P : y \leq x\}$. The presheaf category is the category of order-preserving functions$P^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}$, i.e., the category of down-sets of$P$ (ordered by inclusion). The Yoneda embedding identifies$x$ with${\downarrow}x$, and the full faithfulness says:$x \leq y$ in$P$ if and only if${\downarrow}x \subseteq {\downarrow}y$.

**Example 4.** For the category $\mathbf{Ring}$, the Yoneda embedding sends a ring$R$ to the functor$\mathrm{Spec}(R) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ring}}(R, -)$ (the functor of ring maps out of$R$). Grothendieck's insight in algebraic geometry was to work in the opposite direction: instead of studying$R$ directly, study the functor$\mathrm{Spec}(R): \mathbf{CRing} \to \mathbf{Set}$ sending each$S$ to$\mathrm{Hom}(R, S)$, the "points" of the spectrum with values in$S$. The Yoneda embedding guarantees that$R$ is completely recovered from this functor.

\begin{proposition}
An object $F \in [\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ lies in the essential image of the Yoneda embedding (i.e.,$F \cong \mathrm{h}_A$ for some$A$) if and only if$F$ is representable.
\end{proposition}

The presheaf category $[\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set}]$ is the "free cocompletion" of$\mathcal{C}$: it is the smallest cocomplete category containing$\mathcal{C}$ (via the Yoneda embedding). Every presheaf is a colimit of representable presheaves, and the Yoneda embedding is the canonical inclusion. This parallels the way a metric space embeds into its completion: the completion is the smallest complete space containing the original.

\begin{remark}
The Yoneda embedding has a dual: the contravariant Yoneda embedding $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \hookrightarrow [\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Set}]$ sends$A$ to$\mathrm{h}^A = \mathrm{Hom}(A, -)$. Both embeddings are fully faithful, and together they reflect the fundamental duality of category theory: every object can be studied either through maps into it or through maps out of it.
\end{remark}
