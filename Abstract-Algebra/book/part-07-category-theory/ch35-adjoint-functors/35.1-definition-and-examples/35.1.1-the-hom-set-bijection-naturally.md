# 35.1.1 The Hom-Set Bijection: $\mathrm{Hom}(FA, B) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(A, GB)$, Naturally

The concept of an adjunction is, according to many category theorists, the single most important concept in all of category theory. It abstracts a relationship between functors that appears throughout mathematics with striking regularity — in algebra, topology, logic, and geometry — and it provides a unified explanation for why free objects, tensor products, direct images, and completions all behave the way they do.

\begin{definition}
Functors $F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ and$G: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{C}$ form an *adjunction*, written$F \dashv G$ ("$F$ is left adjoint to$G$," "$G$ is right adjoint to$F$"), if there is a natural bijection
$$\varphi_{A,B}: \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(FA, B) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, GB)$$
for all objects $A \in \mathcal{C}$ and$B \in \mathcal{D}$. Naturality means that for any$g: A' \to A$ in$\mathcal{C}$ and$h: B \to B'$ in$\mathcal{D}$, the diagram
$$\begin{array}{ccc} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(FA, B) & \xrightarrow{\varphi_{A,B}} & \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, GB) \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(FA', B') & \xrightarrow{\varphi_{A',B'}} & \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A', GB') \end{array}$$
commutes, where the vertical maps are the functorial ones induced by $Fg: FA' \to FA$,$h: B \to B'$,$g: A' \to A$, and$Gh: GB \to GB'$.
\end{definition}

The naturality condition is what gives the adjunction its power. It is not enough that there happen to be bijections between individual hom-sets — the bijections must be assembled coherently across all objects and morphisms. This coherence is what distinguishes a true adjunction from a mere collection of set-theoretic coincidences.

**Example 1.** The free group adjunction. Let $U: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Set}$ be the forgetful functor and$F: \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Grp}$ the free group functor. The adjunction$F \dashv U$ asserts:$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(F(S), G) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Set}}(S, U(G))$ naturally in$S$ and$G$. A group homomorphism from the free group$F(S)$ is completely determined by its values on generators, which can be any function$S \to U(G)$. The bijection is: given$f: F(S) \to G$, associate the restriction$f|_S: S \to U(G)$ (where$S \subseteq U(F(S))$ via the generator inclusion). Conversely, given$\phi: S \to U(G)$, extend uniquely to a group homomorphism$\tilde{\phi}: F(S) \to G$. These are mutually inverse. To check naturality: if$h: G \to G'$ is a group homomorphism and$\psi: S' \to S$ is a function, then post-composing with$h$ or pre-composing with$F\psi$ and$\psi$ commutes with the bijection, as can be verified directly.

**Example 2.** The tensor-hom adjunction. Fix a commutative ring $R$ and an$R$-module$N$. The functor$- \otimes_R N: R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ is left adjoint to$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -): R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$. The adjunction bijection is$\mathrm{Hom}_R(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P))$: an$R$-module map from$M \otimes N$ to$P$ corresponds to an$R$-module map from$M$ to the internal hom$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)$. Explicitly, an$R$-linear map$f: M \otimes N \to P$ corresponds to$\tilde{f}: M \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P)$ defined by$\tilde{f}(m)(n) = f(m \otimes n)$. Naturality is a direct calculation.

**Example 3.** The abelianization adjunction. Let $\mathrm{Ab}: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Ab}$ send$G \mapsto G/[G,G]$ and let$\iota: \mathbf{Ab} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Grp}$ be the inclusion. The adjunction$\mathrm{Ab} \dashv \iota$ gives$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}}(G^{\mathrm{ab}}, A) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(G, \iota A)$: any group homomorphism$G \to A$ into an abelian group$A$ factors uniquely through the abelianization$G \twoheadrightarrow G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, since$A$ being abelian forces$[G,G]$ into the kernel.

The left adjoint $F$ and right adjoint$G$ play markedly asymmetric roles: morphisms out of a "free" or "constructed" object$FA$ in$\mathcal{D}$ correspond to morphisms out of$A$ in$\mathcal{C}$ into the "underlying" structure$GB$. This asymmetry captures the intuition of "building" versus "forgetting."

**Example 4.** In $\mathbf{Top}$, the product functor$- \times X: \mathbf{Top} \to \mathbf{Top}$ (for a fixed space$X$) is left adjoint to the function space functor$X^{(-)} = \mathrm{Map}(X, -): \mathbf{Top} \to \mathbf{Top}$, where$\mathrm{Map}(X, Y)$ carries the compact-open topology. The adjunction gives$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Top}}(Z \times X, Y) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Top}}(Z, \mathrm{Map}(X, Y))$: a continuous map$Z \times X \to Y$ (a "homotopy") corresponds to a continuous map$Z \to \mathrm{Map}(X, Y)$ (a "path of maps from$X$ to$Y$"). This exponential adjunction is fundamental in algebraic topology.

**Example 5.** In $\mathbf{Ab}$, the forgetful functor$U: \mathbf{Ab} \to \mathbf{Set}$ is right adjoint to the free abelian group functor$\mathbb{Z}[-]: \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Ab}$. The adjunction bijection$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}}(\mathbb{Z}[S], A) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Set}}(S, U(A))$ says: to give an abelian group homomorphism from the free abelian group on$S$ to$A$, it suffices to specify the images of the generators$S$ in$A$. This is the universal property of the free abelian group, rephrased as an adjunction.

\begin{remark}
The naturality of the bijection $\varphi_{A,B}$ is equivalent to requiring that$\varphi$ is a natural isomorphism of bifunctors$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F-, -) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, G-)$, viewed as functors$\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \mathcal{D} \to \mathbf{Set}$. This formulation makes the relationship to the Yoneda lemma especially transparent: both sides are representable functors of the pair$(A, B)$, and the adjunction is an isomorphism of these representations.
\end{remark}
