# 35.1.3 Unit and Counit of an Adjunction

The hom-set bijection $\mathrm{Hom}(FA, B) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(A, GB)$ contains hidden within it two canonical natural transformations — the unit and counit — that provide an equivalent and often more geometric way of thinking about adjunctions.

\begin{definition}
Let $F \dashv G$ be an adjunction with bijection$\varphi_{A,B}: \mathrm{Hom}(FA, B) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Hom}(A, GB)$.
\par
The *unit* $\eta: \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}} \Rightarrow GF$ is the natural transformation whose component at$A$ is$\eta_A = \varphi_{A,FA}(\mathrm{id}_{FA}) \in \mathrm{Hom}(A, GFA)$ — the image of the identity on$FA$ under the bijection.
\par
The *counit* $\varepsilon: FG \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}$ is the natural transformation whose component at$B$ is$\varepsilon_B = \varphi_{GB,B}^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{GB}) \in \mathrm{Hom}(FGB, B)$ — the preimage of the identity on$GB$ under the bijection.
\end{definition}

These are natural transformations going in opposite directions — the unit maps "into the round trip $GF$" and the counit maps "out of the round trip$FG$." The unit$\eta_A: A \to GFA$ is the "canonical map of$A$ into the free$\mathcal{D}$-structure generated by$A$," while the counit$\varepsilon_B: FGB \to B$ is the "canonical evaluation map from the$\mathcal{D}$-object freely generated by the underlying structure of$B$."

\begin{theorem}[Triangle Identities]
The unit $\eta$ and counit$\varepsilon$ of an adjunction$F \dashv G$ satisfy:
$$(\varepsilon F) \circ (F\eta) = \mathrm{id}_F \quad \text{and} \quad (G\varepsilon) \circ (\eta G) = \mathrm{id}_G,$$
where $\varepsilon F$ denotes the natural transformation with components$\varepsilon_{FA}: FG(FA) \to FA$, and similarly for the others. Conversely, given functors$F, G$ and natural transformations$\eta: \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}} \Rightarrow GF$ and$\varepsilon: FG \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}$ satisfying these triangle identities, the pair$(F, G)$ forms an adjunction with unit$\eta$ and counit$\varepsilon$.
\end{theorem}

The triangle identities are the key constraint: the unit and counit are not independent but are connected by these coherence equations. They can be visualized as triangles that commute when one "unrolls" the unit-counit round trips.

**Example 1.** Free-forgetful adjunction $F \dashv U$ between$\mathbf{Set}$ and$\mathbf{Grp}$. The unit$\eta_S: S \to UF(S)$ is the inclusion of the set$S$ into the underlying set of the free group$F(S)$ as the set of generators. The counit$\varepsilon_G: FU(G) \to G$ is the group homomorphism from the free group on the underlying set of$G$ to$G$, sending each generator (i.e., each element of$G$ viewed as a generator) to itself in$G$. Let us verify the first triangle identity$(\varepsilon F) \circ (F\eta) = \mathrm{id}_F$: for a set$S$, we need$\varepsilon_{F(S)} \circ F(\eta_S) = \mathrm{id}_{F(S)}$. Here$F(\eta_S): F(S) \to FUF(S)$ is the free group map induced by the inclusion$\eta_S: S \to UF(S)$, and$\varepsilon_{F(S)}: FUF(S) \to F(S)$ is the evaluation map. Their composite: a generator$s \in S$ maps under$F(\eta_S)$ to the generator$[s]$ in$FUF(S)$, and then$\varepsilon_{F(S)}$ sends$[s]$ back to$s$ in$F(S)$. So the composite is the identity on generators, hence the identity on$F(S)$.$\checkmark$

**Example 2.** Abelianization adjunction $\mathrm{Ab} \dashv \iota$. The unit$\eta_G: G \to \iota(\mathrm{Ab}(G)) = G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the quotient map$q_G: G \to G/[G,G]$. The counit$\varepsilon_A: \mathrm{Ab}(\iota(A)) \to A$ for$A \in \mathbf{Ab}$ is the identity: since$A$ is already abelian,$\iota(A)^{\mathrm{ab}} = \iota(A)/[\iota(A), \iota(A)] = A/\{0\} = A$. The triangle identity$(G\varepsilon) \circ (\eta G) = \mathrm{id}_G$: for$A \in \mathbf{Ab}$, we need$\iota(\varepsilon_A) \circ \eta_{\iota(A)} = \mathrm{id}_{\iota(A)}$. Since$\varepsilon_A = \mathrm{id}_A$ and$\eta_{\iota(A)}: \iota(A) \to \iota(A)^{\mathrm{ab}} = \iota(A)$ is also the identity (as$\iota(A)$ is abelian), the composite is the identity.$\checkmark$

**Example 3.** Tensor-hom adjunction. For $R$-modules with$F = - \otimes_R N$ and$G = \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$:
- The unit at $M$ is$\eta_M: M \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, M \otimes_R N)$, defined by$\eta_M(m)(n) = m \otimes n$.
- The counit at $P$ is$\varepsilon_P: \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P) \otimes_R N \to P$, defined by$\varepsilon_P(f \otimes n) = f(n)$ (the evaluation map).

The triangle identities can be verified directly: $(\varepsilon F) \circ (F\eta) = \mathrm{id}_F$ says that for any$m \otimes n \in M \otimes N$, the composite$m \otimes n \mapsto (n' \mapsto m \otimes n') \otimes n \mapsto m \otimes n$ is the identity, which holds by the definition of evaluation.

**Example 4.** Discrete-forgetful-indiscrete adjoint triple for $\mathbf{Top}$. The unit of$D \dashv U$ at$S \in \mathbf{Set}$ is the identity$\mathrm{id}_S: S \to U(D(S)) = S$ (viewed as a function from$S$ to$S$), since the forgetful functor applied to the discrete space gives back the set. The counit is the unique continuous map$D(U(X)) \to X$ (from the discrete version of$X$ to$X$ itself), which is the identity on underlying sets and continuous because every map out of a discrete space is continuous. The counit$\varepsilon_X: D(U(X)) \to X$ being the identity function is what "evaluation of a free structure" means in this context.

\begin{remark}
An adjunction can be equivalently specified by:
1. A natural bijection $\varphi_{A,B}: \mathrm{Hom}(FA, B) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(A, GB)$, or
2. Natural transformations $\eta: \mathrm{id} \Rightarrow GF$ and$\varepsilon: FG \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ satisfying the triangle identities.
\par
The equivalence of these two descriptions is a non-trivial theorem. The unit-counit formulation is often more geometric and easier to work with when verifying that a specific pair of functors forms an adjunction without an explicit hom-set bijection.
\end{remark}
