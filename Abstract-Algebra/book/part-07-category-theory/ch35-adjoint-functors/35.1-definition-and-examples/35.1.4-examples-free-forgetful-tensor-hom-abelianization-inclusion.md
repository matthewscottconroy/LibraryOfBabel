# 35.1.4 Examples: Free–Forgetful, Tensor–Hom, Abelianization–Inclusion

The definition of an adjunction crystallizes when seen through its major examples, each of which encodes a fundamental algebraic construction. The common thread is that every universal construction in algebra is an adjunction, and recognizing an adjunction gives immediate access to powerful structural theorems.

**Example 1. The free–forgetful adjunction** is the prototype. Let $U: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Set}$ be the forgetful functor and$F: \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Grp}$ the free group functor. The adjunction$F \dashv U$ asserts a natural bijection$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(F(S), G) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Set}}(S, U(G))$: to give a group homomorphism out of the free group$F(S)$ is exactly to give a function from the generating set$S$ to the underlying set of$G$. The unit$\eta_S: S \to UF(S)$ is the inclusion of generators, and the counit$\varepsilon_G: FU(G) \to G$ is the homomorphism sending each generator to itself.

To verify this is genuine naturality: for a group homomorphism $\phi: G \to H$ and a function$\psi: S' \to S$, the two ways of composing around the naturality square both send a function$f: S \to U(G)$ to the function$U(\phi) \circ f \circ \psi: S' \to U(H)$, in correspondence with the group homomorphism$\phi \circ \tilde{f} \circ F(\psi): F(S') \to H$.

The same free-forgetful pattern — left adjoint $F$ free, right adjoint$U$ forgetful — appears for:
- Free abelian groups: $\mathbb{Z}[-] \dashv U$ between$\mathbf{Set}$ and$\mathbf{Ab}$,
- Free $R$-modules:$R^{(-)} \dashv U$ between$\mathbf{Set}$ and$R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$,
- Polynomial rings: $k[-] \dashv U$ between$\mathbf{Set}$ and$k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}$,
- Free monoids: $(-)*$ (Kleene star, or list functor)$\dashv U$ between$\mathbf{Set}$ and$\mathbf{Mon}$.

In each case, the structure of the free object is completely determined by its generators, and a structured map out of a free object is determined by where the generators go.

**Example 2. The tensor–hom adjunction** is the algebraic heart of homological algebra. For a ring $R$ and a fixed$R$-$S$-bimodule$N$, the tensor product functor$- \otimes_R N: R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to S\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ is left adjoint to the hom functor$\mathrm{Hom}_S(N, -): S\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$, yielding the natural bijection
$$\mathrm{Hom}_S(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \mathrm{Hom}_S(N, P)).$$
For the special case $R = S = \mathbb{Z}$, this recovers the familiar adjunction between tensor product and internal hom for abelian groups.

The structural consequences are immediate:
- $- \otimes_R N$ is a left adjoint, so it preserves all colimits and in particular is right-exact: if$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ is exact, then$A \otimes N \to B \otimes N \to C \otimes N \to 0$ is exact.
- $\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ is a right adjoint, so it preserves all limits and in particular is left-exact: if$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ is exact, then$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(N, A) \to \mathrm{Hom}(N, B) \to \mathrm{Hom}(N, C)$ is exact.
Both facts, which are usually proved separately, are now immediate consequences of adjointness and the RAPL/LAPC theorem (Section 35.2.2).

**Example 3. The abelianization–inclusion adjunction.** The abelianization functor $\mathrm{Ab}: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Ab}$ sending$G \mapsto G/[G,G]$ is left adjoint to the inclusion$\iota: \mathbf{Ab} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Grp}$. The bijection$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}}(G^{\mathrm{ab}}, A) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(G, \iota(A))$ says that abelian group homomorphisms out of$G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ correspond to group homomorphisms from$G$ into abelian groups. This is exactly the universal property of abelianization: every group homomorphism from$G$ to an abelian group factors uniquely through$G \to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$.

Concretely: the symmetric group $S_3$ has abelianization$S_3^{\mathrm{ab}} = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (the sign map is the only nontrivial homomorphism to an abelian group). The adjunction bijection says$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, A) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}(S_3, A)$ for any abelian group$A$. For$A = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$: the left side has 2 elements (the zero map and the identity), and the right side also has exactly 2 elements (the zero map and the sign homomorphism). The bijection matches them.

**Example 4. Scalar extension and restriction.** For a ring map $\phi: R \to S$, the *restriction of scalars* functor$\phi^*: S\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ (view an$S$-module as an$R$-module via$\phi$) has a left adjoint *extension of scalars*$\phi_! = S \otimes_R -: R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to S\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$, giving$\mathrm{Hom}_S(S \otimes_R M, N) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \phi^* N)$. For$R = \mathbb{Z}$ and$S = k$ a field, extension of scalars sends an abelian group$M$ to the$k$-vector space$k \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} M$.

**Example 5. Limits and colimits as adjunctions.** The diagonal functor $\Delta: \mathcal{C} \to [\mathcal{J}, \mathcal{C}]$ (sending each object to the constant diagram) has both a left and right adjoint when$\mathcal{C}$ has all$\mathcal{J}$-shaped colimits and limits respectively: the left adjoint is$\varinjlim: [\mathcal{J}, \mathcal{C}] \to \mathcal{C}$ (colimit) and the right adjoint is$\varprojlim: [\mathcal{J}, \mathcal{C}] \to \mathcal{C}$ (limit). The adjunction bijection for limits is$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, \varprojlim D) \cong \mathrm{Nat}(\Delta A, D)$ (a map from$A$ to the limit corresponds to a compatible family of maps from$A$ to the diagram), which is exactly the universal property of limits.

These three examples — free-forgetful, tensor-hom, abelianization-inclusion — are instances of a pervasive organizing principle: every universal construction in algebra is an adjunction, and recognizing this unifies the structural properties of these constructions under a single theorem.
