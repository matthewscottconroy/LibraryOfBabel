# 35.2.2 Right Adjoints Preserve Limits; Left Adjoints Preserve Colimits

Among all the properties of adjoint functors, the preservation of limits and colimits is perhaps the most computationally important. The theorem goes by the mnemonics RAPL (Right Adjoints Preserve Limits) and LAPC (Left Adjoints Preserve Colimits), and it unifies dozens of otherwise-separate results from throughout algebra into a single principle.

\begin{theorem}[RAPL and LAPC]
Let $F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ and$G: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{C}$ be functors with$F \dashv G$.
- $G$ preserves all limits that exist in$\mathcal{D}$: if$D: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{D}$ has a limit, then$G(\varprojlim D) \cong \varprojlim G \circ D$.
- $F$ preserves all colimits that exist in$\mathcal{C}$: if$D: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C}$ has a colimit, then$F(\varinjlim D) \cong \varinjlim F \circ D$.
\end{theorem}

*Proof of RAPL.* For any diagram $D: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{D}$ with limit$\varprojlim D$ in$\mathcal{D}$, and any test object$A \in \mathcal{C}$:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, G(\varprojlim D)) &\cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(FA, \varprojlim D) \\
&\cong \varprojlim_{j \in \mathcal{J}} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(FA, D(j)) \\
&\cong \varprojlim_{j \in \mathcal{J}} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, G(D(j))) \\
&\cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, \varprojlim_{j \in \mathcal{J}} G(D(j))).
\end{align*}
The first and third isomorphisms use the adjunction bijection, the second uses the fact that representable functors preserve limits (Section 36.4.3), and the fourth uses the representability of limits in $\mathcal{C}$. Since$\mathrm{Hom}(A, G(\varprojlim D)) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(A, \varprojlim G \circ D)$ naturally in$A$, the Yoneda Lemma gives$G(\varprojlim D) \cong \varprojlim G \circ D$.$\square$ LAPC is proved dually.

**Example 1.** The forgetful functor $U: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Set}$. Since$U$ is right adjoint to the free group functor$F$, RAPL implies$U$ preserves all limits. Concretely:
- *Products*: $U(G \times H) = U(G) \times U(H)$ — the underlying set of a product of groups is the Cartesian product of the underlying sets.
- *Equalizers*: $U(\ker(f)) = U(\{g \in G : f(g) = e\}) = \{g \in G : f(g) = e\}$ — the underlying set of the kernel is the set-theoretic equalizer.
- *Limits in general*: Limits of diagrams of groups are computed on underlying sets (with the group structure inherited from the components), and $U$ sends these to the corresponding limits in$\mathbf{Set}$.

**Example 2.** The tensor product functor $- \otimes_R N: R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to \text{Ab}$. Since this is left adjoint to$\mathrm{Hom}(N, -)$, LAPC implies it preserves all colimits:
- *Cokernels*: if $f: M \to M'$ is a module map,$(M' \to \mathrm{coker}(f)) \otimes N$ is a cokernel of$f \otimes \mathrm{id}_N$. This is the right-exactness of tensor product.
- *Direct sums*: $(M \oplus M') \otimes_R N \cong (M \otimes_R N) \oplus (M' \otimes_R N)$ — tensor product distributes over direct sums.
- *More generally*: $(\varinjlim M_i) \otimes_R N \cong \varinjlim (M_i \otimes_R N)$ — tensor commutes with all colimits.

**Example 3.** The free group functor $F: \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Grp}$. Since$F$ is left adjoint to$U$, LAPC implies$F$ preserves all colimits:
- *Coproducts*: $F(S \sqcup T) \cong F(S) * F(T)$ — the free group on a disjoint union of sets is the free product of free groups.
- *Coequalizers*: $F$ sends coequalizers in$\mathbf{Set}$ (i.e., quotient sets) to coequalizers in$\mathbf{Grp}$ (i.e., quotient groups by normal closures).
- *All colimits*: the free group construction commutes with arbitrary colimits of sets.

**Example 4.** Hom functor $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M, -): R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to \mathbf{Ab}$. This is right adjoint to the tensor product$- \otimes_R M$ (by the tensor-hom adjunction), so RAPL gives:
- *Products*: $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \prod_i N_i) \cong \prod_i \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, N_i)$ — Hom into a product is a product of Homs.
- *Equalizers*: $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \ker(f)) = \ker(\mathrm{Hom}(M, f))$ — Hom preserves kernels.
- *Left-exactness*: Hom preserves the left-exact part of a short exact sequence.

**Example 5.** The abelianization functor. Since $\mathrm{Ab}: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Ab}$ is left adjoint to the inclusion$\iota$, LAPC gives:
- *Coproducts in $\mathbf{Ab}$*:$\mathrm{Ab}(G * H) \cong G^{\mathrm{ab}} \oplus H^{\mathrm{ab}}$ — the abelianization of a free product is the direct sum of abelianizations.
- *Pushouts*: the abelianization of an amalgamated free product $G *_K H$ is the pushout in$\mathbf{Ab}$ of$G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and$H^{\mathrm{ab}}$ over$K^{\mathrm{ab}}$.

\begin{remark}
RAPL and LAPC subsume and explain the following classical facts that were previously proved separately: tensor product is right-exact, Hom is left-exact, free group on a disjoint union is the free product, restriction of scalars preserves products, and so on. Every such result that was previously proved by hand is an instance of RAPL or LAPC applied to the appropriate adjunction.
\end{remark}
