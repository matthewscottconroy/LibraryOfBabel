# 35.2.3 Reflective Subcategories; Localizations

A particularly important class of adjunctions arises when the right adjoint is the inclusion of a subcategory. A full subcategory $\iota: \mathcal{D} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}$ is called *reflective* if the inclusion functor$\iota$ has a left adjoint$L: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$, called the *reflector* or *reflection functor*. The adjunction$L \dashv \iota$ gives, for each object$A \in \mathcal{C}$ and$D \in \mathcal{D}$, a natural bijection$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(LA, D) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, \iota D)$.

\begin{definition}
A full subcategory $\mathcal{D} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}$ is *reflective* if the inclusion has a left adjoint$L: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$, called the *reflector*. The unit$\eta_A: A \to \iota(LA)$ of the adjunction is the *reflection* of$A$ into$\mathcal{D}$ — the "closest" object of$\mathcal{D}$ to$A$, in the sense that every morphism from$A$ into an object of$\mathcal{D}$ factors uniquely through$\eta_A$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
In a reflective subcategory, the unit $\eta_A: A \to \iota(LA)$ is the universal morphism from$A$ to an object of$\mathcal{D}$: for any$D \in \mathcal{D}$ and morphism$f: A \to \iota D$ in$\mathcal{C}$, there exists a unique morphism$\bar{f}: LA \to D$ in$\mathcal{D}$ such that$\iota(\bar{f}) \circ \eta_A = f$.
\end{proposition}

**Example 1.** Abelianization. The category $\mathbf{Ab}$ of abelian groups is a reflective subcategory of$\mathbf{Grp}$: the inclusion$\iota: \mathbf{Ab} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Grp}$ has left adjoint$G \mapsto G^{\mathrm{ab}} = G/[G,G]$. The reflection map$\eta_G: G \to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the canonical quotient. Every group homomorphism from$G$ into an abelian group factors uniquely through$G \to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$: this is the universal property of abelianization, which is precisely the adjunction bijection. The reflector$\mathrm{Ab}$ is idempotent in the sense that$\mathrm{Ab}(\mathrm{Ab}(G)) = \mathrm{Ab}(G)$ (since$\mathrm{Ab}(G)$ is already abelian, its abelianization is itself).

**Example 2.** Sheafification. The category $\mathbf{Sh}(X, \mathbf{Ab})$ of sheaves of abelian groups on a topological space$X$ is a reflective subcategory of the category$\mathbf{PSh}(X, \mathbf{Ab})$ of presheaves. The inclusion$\iota: \mathbf{Sh} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{PSh}$ has left adjoint the *sheafification functor*$F \mapsto F^+$ (applied twice if needed). The reflection$\eta_F: F \to F^+$ is the universal map from a presheaf into a sheaf: every presheaf map from$F$ to a sheaf$\mathcal{G}$ factors uniquely through$F^+$. Sheafification is the fundamental localization in algebraic geometry and sheaf theory.

**Example 3.** Localization of rings and modules. The category of $S^{-1}R$-modules (for a multiplicative set$S \subset R$) is a reflective subcategory of$R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$: the reflector is the localization functor$M \mapsto S^{-1}M = S^{-1}R \otimes_R M$. The reflection map$\eta_M: M \to S^{-1}M$ sends$m$ to$m/1$, and every$R$-module map from$M$ to an$S^{-1}R$-module factors uniquely through$S^{-1}M$.

**Example 4.** Compact Hausdorff spaces. The category of compact Hausdorff spaces $\mathbf{CHaus}$ is a reflective subcategory of$\mathbf{Top}$: the reflector is the *Stone–Čech compactification*$\beta: \mathbf{Top} \to \mathbf{CHaus}$. For any topological space$X$, the reflection$\eta_X: X \to \beta X$ has the universal property that every continuous map from$X$ to a compact Hausdorff space$K$ extends uniquely to a continuous map$\beta X \to K$.

**Example 5.** The category of torsion-free abelian groups. $\mathbf{TF} \subset \mathbf{Ab}$ (torsion-free abelian groups) is not a reflective subcategory in general, but the category of divisible abelian groups is a coreflective subcategory: the right adjoint from$\mathbf{Ab}$ to divisible abelian groups is the *divisible hull*$M \mapsto \mathbb{Q} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} M$ (for torsion-free$M$).

\begin{proposition}
If $\mathcal{D}$ is a reflective subcategory of$\mathcal{C}$ with reflector$L$, then$L \circ \iota \cong \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}$ (the reflector of an object already in$\mathcal{D}$ is naturally isomorphic to itself), i.e., the counit$\varepsilon: L \circ \iota \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}$ is a natural isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

*Proof.* For $D \in \mathcal{D}$, the counit$\varepsilon_D: L(\iota D) \to D$ is an isomorphism by the triangle identity and the fact that$\eta_{\iota D}: \iota D \to \iota(L(\iota D))$ combined with$\iota(\varepsilon_D) \circ \eta_{\iota D} = \mathrm{id}_{\iota D}$ shows$\iota(\varepsilon_D)$ is a split epimorphism with a section, and similarly a split monomorphism.$\square$

Reflective subcategories are the categorical abstraction of *localization*: passing from $\mathcal{C}$ to a reflective subcategory$\mathcal{D}$ is formally inverting a class of morphisms (those sent to isomorphisms by$L$). The category$\mathcal{D}$ is the "localization" of$\mathcal{C}$ at these morphisms, and the reflector$L$ is the localization functor. This provides a common framework for abelianization, sheafification, and algebraic localization, revealing these as instances of a single categorical pattern.
