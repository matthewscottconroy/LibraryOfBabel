# 36.2 Specific Limits

The general definition of a limit becomes fully concrete when one works through the classical examples. Each corresponds to a specific choice of index category $\mathcal{J}$, and the universal property of the limit specializes to a familiar characterization.

*Products* are limits over a discrete index category — one with objects but no non-identity morphisms. The limit of the discrete diagram $\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ is the product $\prod_{i \in I} A_i$ with projection morphisms $\pi_i: \prod A_i \to A_i$. The universal property says that maps into a product correspond bijectively to families of maps into each factor. In $\mathbf{Set}$ this is the Cartesian product; in $\mathbf{Ab}$ it is the direct product; in $\mathbf{Mod}_R$ it is likewise. The universal property is precisely the one that characterizes these constructions and distinguishes them from the direct sum (which is the coproduct, not the product, for infinite families).

*Equalizers* are limits over the index category with two objects and two parallel arrows $\bullet \rightrightarrows \bullet$. The equalizer of $f, g: A \rightrightarrows B$ is an object $E$ with a morphism $e: E \to A$ satisfying $f \circ e = g \circ e$, universal with this property. In concrete categories, the equalizer is the subset $\{a \in A : f(a) = g(a)\}$ with the inclusion map. Kernels in $\mathbf{Ab}$ and $\mathbf{Mod}_R$ are the special case where $g$ is the zero morphism: the kernel of $f: A \to B$ is the equalizer of $f$ and $0$. This identification shows that kernels are a categorical concept, definable in any category with a zero object.

*Pullbacks* (fiber products) are limits over the cospan $\bullet \to \bullet \leftarrow \bullet$. Given morphisms $f: A \to C$ and $g: B \to C$, the pullback $A \times_C B$ is the limit of this diagram: it consists of pairs $(a, b)$ with $f(a) = g(b)$, equipped with projections to $A$ and $B$. In $\mathbf{Grp}$ the pullback is a subgroup of $A \times B$; in algebraic geometry the fiber product of schemes over a base scheme is a pullback in the category of schemes. The pullback is the categorical notion of "intersection over a common base," and it is ubiquitous in geometry and algebra.

*Inverse (projective) limits* are limits over a directed poset, viewed as a category. The $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p = \varprojlim_n \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ are the canonical algebraic example: compatible sequences $(a_n)$ with $a_n \in \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ and $a_{n+1} \equiv a_n \pmod{p^n}$ form the limit, which is a complete discrete valuation ring capturing all finite approximations simultaneously. Any category with finite products and equalizers has all finite limits, and any category with products and equalizers has all small limits — showing that products and equalizers together generate all limits.
