# 36.3.1 Cocones; Colimits as Initial Cocones

Category theory is built on the principle of duality: every concept has a dual obtained by reversing all arrows. The dual of a limit is a *colimit*, and the dual of a cone is a *cocone*.

\begin{definition}
A *cocone* under a diagram $D: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C}$ with apex$N \in \mathcal{C}$ is a family of morphisms$\{\psi_j: D(j) \to N\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}}$, one for each object$j \in \mathcal{J}$, such that for every morphism$f: j \to k$ in$\mathcal{J}$, the triangle
$$D(j) \xrightarrow{D(f)} D(k) \xrightarrow{\psi_k} N$$
commutes with $D(j) \xrightarrow{\psi_j} N$, i.e.,$\psi_k \circ D(f) = \psi_j$.
\par
Equivalently, a cocone is a natural transformation $\psi: D \Rightarrow \Delta N$.
\par
A *colimit* of $D$ is an initial cocone: a cocone$(\varinjlim D, \{\iota_j\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}})$ such that for any other cocone$(N, \{\phi_j\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}})$, there exists a unique morphism$u: \varinjlim D \to N$ with$u \circ \iota_j = \phi_j$ for all$j \in \mathcal{J}$.
\end{definition}

Where a cone maps from an apex into the diagram, a cocone maps from the diagram out into a single apex. The colimit is the "best" object receiving maps from the entire diagram.

**Example 1.** Colimit of the empty diagram. Every object is trivially a cocone; the initial cocone is an *initial object* $I$ — an object with a unique morphism to every other object. This is dual to the terminal object. In$\mathbf{Set}$: the empty set$\emptyset$. In$\mathbf{Grp}$ (and$\mathbf{Ab}$,$R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$): the trivial group$\{e\}$ (or zero module), which is both initial and terminal (a "zero object"). In$\mathbf{Ring}$: the integers$\mathbb{Z}$, since there is a unique ring homomorphism$\mathbb{Z} \to R$ for any ring$R$.

**Example 2.** Cocones over a discrete two-object diagram. A cocone with apex $N$ over$D: \{0, 1\} \to \mathcal{C}$ with$D(0) = A$ and$D(1) = B$ is a pair of morphisms$\iota_A: A \to N$ and$\iota_B: B \to N$. The initial such cocone is the *coproduct*$A \sqcup B$ (or$A \oplus B$ in additive categories) with inclusions, to be discussed in Section 36.3.2.

**Example 3.** Cocone over a directed system. For a directed system $\{A_i, \phi_{ij}\}$, a cocone with apex$N$ consists of morphisms$\psi_i: A_i \to N$ with$\psi_j = \psi_i \circ \phi_{ij}$ for$i \leq j$. The initial such cocone is the direct limit$\varinjlim A_i$ (Section 36.3.3).

**Example 4.** Cocone over a span. For a span $A \xleftarrow{f} C \xrightarrow{g} B$, a cocone with apex$N$ consists of morphisms$\iota_A: A \to N$ and$\iota_B: B \to N$ such that$\iota_A \circ f = \iota_B \circ g$. The initial such cocone is the *pushout*$A \sqcup_C B$ (the amalgamated coproduct), to be discussed in Section 36.3.2.

\begin{remark}
Conceptually, while a limit assembles a compatible system of data into a single object by taking the "most refined common refinement" (a generalized intersection), a colimit assembles data by "identifying and gluing" (a generalized union). This is why colimits are often more "explosive" or combinatorial in character: the coproduct in $\mathbf{Grp}$ is the free product (typically much larger than the direct product), and quotient constructions are colimits rather than limits. The colimit is initial in the category of cocones, and the Yoneda lemma guarantees uniqueness.
\end{remark}

**Example 5.** Colimit in $\mathbf{Ring}$: tensor product. The coproduct of two commutative rings$R$ and$S$ in$\mathbf{CRing}$ is their tensor product$R \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} S$: the cocone maps$\iota_R: R \to R \otimes S$ and$\iota_S: S \to R \otimes S$ (given by$r \mapsto r \otimes 1$ and$s \mapsto 1 \otimes s$) satisfy the universal property that any two ring maps$f: R \to T$ and$g: S \to T$ into a commutative ring$T$ factor uniquely through a ring map$R \otimes S \to T$. This is the colimit characterization of the tensor product of commutative rings.

The category of cocones $\mathrm{Cocone}(D)$ is defined dually to$\mathrm{Cone}(D)$: objects are cocones, and morphisms are maps of apices compatible with all legs. The colimit$\varinjlim D$ is a terminal object of$\mathrm{Cone}(D^{\mathrm{op}}) \cong \mathrm{Cocone}(D)$. A category is *cocomplete* if every small diagram has a colimit, and the dual theorem to the products-plus-equalizers theorem states that cocompleteness is equivalent to having all coproducts and all coequalizers.
