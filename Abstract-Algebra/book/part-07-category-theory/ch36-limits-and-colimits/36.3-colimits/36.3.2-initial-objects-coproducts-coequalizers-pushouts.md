# 36.3.2 Initial Objects; Coproducts; Coequalizers; Pushouts

Just as the fundamental limit types arise from specific choices of index category, so do their duals. These four colimit types — initial objects, coproducts, coequalizers, and pushouts — are sufficient to generate all small colimits.

**Initial objects** are colimits over the empty diagram.

\begin{definition}
An *initial object* $I$ in$\mathcal{C}$ is an object such that for every$A \in \mathcal{C}$, there exists a unique morphism$I \to A$.
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** Initial objects in standard categories:
- In $\mathbf{Set}$: the empty set$\emptyset$ (only one function from$\emptyset$ to any set).
- In $\mathbf{Grp}$ and$\mathbf{Ab}$: the trivial group$\{e\}$ (both initial and terminal — a zero object).
- In $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$: the zero module$0$.
- In $\mathbf{CRing}$: the ring$\mathbb{Z}$ (unique ring homomorphism$\mathbb{Z} \to R$ for any commutative ring$R$).
- In $k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}$ (commutative$k$-algebras): the base field$k$.
- In a poset: the minimum element $\bot$ (if it exists).

**Coproducts** are colimits of discrete diagrams.

\begin{definition}
The *coproduct* $\coprod_{i \in I} A_i$ is an object equipped with inclusion morphisms$\iota_j: A_j \to \coprod_i A_i$, such that for any object$N$ and morphisms$\{f_i: A_i \to N\}$, there exists a unique morphism$[f_i]: \coprod_i A_i \to N$ with$[f_i] \circ \iota_j = f_j$ for all$j$.
\end{definition}

**Example 2.** Coproducts in various categories — this is where the categories diverge most dramatically from one another:
- In $\mathbf{Set}$:$\coprod_i A_i = \bigsqcup_i A_i$ (disjoint union), with$\iota_j(a) = (j, a)$.
- In $\mathbf{Grp}$:$G \sqcup H = G * H$ (free product), which is vastly larger than$G \times H$ for non-trivial groups. The free product$\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}$ is the free group on two generators, while$\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ is merely a free abelian group on two generators.
- In $\mathbf{Ab}$ (and$R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ for finite families):$A \sqcup B = A \oplus B$ (direct sum), coinciding with the product. For infinite families, the coproduct is$\bigoplus_i A_i$ (only finitely many nonzero components), while the product is$\prod_i A_i$.
- In $\mathbf{CRing}$:$R \sqcup S = R \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} S$ (tensor product over$\mathbb{Z}$).
- In $\mathbf{Top}$:$\bigsqcup_i X_i$ (disjoint union with the disjoint union topology).

**Coequalizers** are dual to equalizers.

\begin{definition}
Given morphisms $f, g: A \to B$, the *coequalizer*$\mathrm{Coeq}(f, g)$ is an object$Q$ equipped with a morphism$q: B \to Q$ such that$q \circ f = q \circ g$, and with the universal property: for any$N$ and$h: B \to N$ with$h \circ f = h \circ g$, there exists a unique$u: Q \to N$ with$u \circ q = h$.
\end{definition}

**Example 3.** Coequalizers in various categories:
- In $\mathbf{Set}$:$\mathrm{Coeq}(f, g) = B / {\sim}$ where$\sim$ is the equivalence relation generated by$f(a) \sim g(a)$ for all$a \in A$.
- In $\mathbf{Ab}$:$\mathrm{Coeq}(f, g) = B / \mathrm{im}(f - g)$ (quotient by the image of the difference).
- In $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$:$\mathrm{Coeq}(f, g) = \mathrm{coker}(f - g) = B / (f - g)(A)$.
- Kernels (as equalizers) are dual to cokernels (as coequalizers): the cokernel of $f: A \to B$ is the coequalizer of$f$ and the zero morphism.

**Pushouts** are duals to pullbacks: the colimit of a span $A \xleftarrow{f} C \xrightarrow{g} B$.

\begin{definition}
The *pushout* $A \sqcup_C B$ is an object with morphisms$j_A: A \to A \sqcup_C B$ and$j_B: B \to A \sqcup_C B$ such that$j_A \circ f = j_B \circ g$, with the universal property that any$h_A: A \to N$ and$h_B: B \to N$ with$h_A \circ f = h_B \circ g$ factor uniquely through$A \sqcup_C B$.
\end{definition}

**Example 4.** Pushouts in various categories:
- In $\mathbf{Set}$:$A \sqcup_C B = (A \sqcup B) / \{f(c) \sim g(c) : c \in C\}$ (glue$A$ and$B$ together by identifying$f(c)$ with$g(c)$). For instance, if$f, g: \{*\} \to \{a, b\}$ with$f(*) = a$ and$g(*) = b$, the pushout is$\{a = b\}$ (a one-element set), i.e., the set where$a$ and$b$ are identified.
- In $\mathbf{Grp}$: the pushout is the *amalgamated free product*$A *_C B$, the quotient of the free product$A * B$ by the normal subgroup generated by$\{f(c)g(c)^{-1} : c \in C\}$. This is the fundamental construction in Bass–Serre theory of groups acting on trees: every group acting on a tree is built from free products with amalgamation.
- In $\mathbf{Ab}$:$A \sqcup_C B = (A \oplus B) / \langle (f(c), -g(c)) : c \in C \rangle$.
- In $\mathbf{Top}$: the pushout along$f: Z \hookrightarrow X$ and$g: Z \hookrightarrow Y$ gives the "gluing"$X \cup_Z Y$ — the standard construction for attaching cells, forming CW-complexes, and constructing spaces by gluing.

**Example 5.** Mayer–Vietoris from pushouts. The topological fact that a space $X = U \cup V$ (where$U, V$ are open) is a pushout$U \sqcup_{U \cap V} V$ in$\mathbf{Top}$ underlies the Mayer–Vietoris sequence in homology. Taking the homology of a pushout diagram gives a long exact sequence, reflecting the categorical behavior of the homology functor applied to colimits.

\begin{theorem}
A category $\mathcal{C}$ that has all coproducts and all coequalizers has all small colimits.
\end{theorem}

These four types — initial objects, coproducts, coequalizers, and pushouts — thus generate all small colimits, in the same way that terminal objects, products, and equalizers generate all limits.
