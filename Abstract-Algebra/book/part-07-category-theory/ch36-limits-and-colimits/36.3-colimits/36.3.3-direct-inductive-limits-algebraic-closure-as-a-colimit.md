# 36.3.3 Direct (Inductive) Limits; Algebraic Closure as a Colimit

The dual of an inverse limit is a *direct* (or *inductive*) *limit*, the colimit of a directed system. While inverse limits assemble compatible approximations into a single coherent object "from above," direct limits assemble growing approximations "from below."

\begin{definition}
A *directed system* in $\mathcal{C}$ consists of objects$\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ indexed by a directed poset$(I, \leq)$ and morphisms$\phi_{ij}: A_i \to A_j$ for each$i \leq j$, satisfying$\phi_{ii} = \mathrm{id}_{A_i}$ and$\phi_{jk} \circ \phi_{ij} = \phi_{ik}$ for all$i \leq j \leq k$.
\par
The *direct limit* $\varinjlim_i A_i$ is the colimit of this directed system (viewed as a functor from the directed poset to$\mathcal{C}$).
\end{definition}

In $\mathbf{Set}$,$\varinjlim_i A_i = \bigsqcup_i A_i / {\sim}$, where$a_i \sim \phi_{ij}(a_i)$ for all$i \leq j$ and$a_i \in A_i$. An element of the direct limit is an equivalence class$[a_i]$ of an element$a_i$ in some$A_i$, where two elements$a_i \in A_i$ and$a_j \in A_j$ are identified if they eventually agree:$\phi_{ik}(a_i) = \phi_{jk}(a_j)$ for some$k \geq i, j$.

**Example 1.** Union of a chain. If $A_1 \subseteq A_2 \subseteq A_3 \subseteq \cdots$ is an ascending chain of subsets (groups, rings, etc.) with inclusion maps$\phi_{ij}: A_i \hookrightarrow A_j$, then$\varinjlim A_i = \bigcup_i A_i$ (the actual union). Every element eventually lies in$A_i$ for large$i$, so the direct limit is the union. For example,$\bigcup_{n \geq 0} \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ under the natural inclusions gives a Prüfer group$\mathbb{Z}[p^{-\infty}]/\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 2.** Algebraic closure as a direct limit. The algebraic closure $\bar{F}$ of a field$F$ can be described as the direct limit$\varinjlim E$ over the directed system of all finite extensions$E/F$, ordered by inclusion: whenever$E \subseteq E'$ are finite extensions of$F$, the inclusion$E \hookrightarrow E'$ is a structure map. The direct limit is a field containing all finite extensions simultaneously. It is algebraically closed: any polynomial over$F$ (or over$\bar{F}$) splits in some finite extension, hence splits in the direct limit. The universal property:$\bar{F}$ receives a unique embedding from every algebraic extension of$F$.

More carefully, one must work up to isomorphism: the directed system consists of isomorphism classes of finite extensions, and one must use Zorn's lemma to ensure the system is well-defined. But the categorical structure — a directed system of field extensions with inclusions — is the conceptual content.

**Example 3.** Localization of modules. For a multiplicative set $S \subseteq R$, the localization$S^{-1}M$ of an$R$-module$M$ is the direct limit of the system$\{M \xrightarrow{s \cdot} M\}_{s \in S}$ (multiplication by elements of$S$, ordered by divisibility). More precisely,$S^{-1}M = \varinjlim_{s \in S} M$ where the maps are multiplication by$s$. An element$m/s \in S^{-1}M$ is represented by an element$m \in M$ with denominator$s$, and$m/s = m'/s'$ iff$s''(s'm - sm') = 0$ for some$s'' \in S$.

**Example 4.** Polynomial rings as a direct limit. Fix a set $X$ of variables and let$I$ be the directed poset of finite subsets of$X$ ordered by inclusion. The polynomial rings$k[F]$ (for$F \in I$ a finite subset of$X$) form a directed system with inclusion maps$k[F] \hookrightarrow k[F']$ for$F \subseteq F'$. The direct limit is$k[X] = \varinjlim_{F \subseteq X, |F| < \infty} k[F]$ — the polynomial ring in all variables of$X$ is the direct limit of all finitely-generated polynomial subrings.

**Example 5.** Stalk of a sheaf. If $\mathcal{F}$ is a sheaf (or presheaf) of abelian groups on a topological space$X$ and$x \in X$ is a point, the *stalk* at$x$ is the direct limit$\mathcal{F}_x = \varinjlim_{U \ni x} \mathcal{F}(U)$ over the directed system of open neighborhoods$U$ of$x$ (directed by reverse inclusion). An element of the stalk is a "germ" — an equivalence class of sections$s \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ where two sections are identified if they agree on some smaller neighborhood. Stalks are the fundamental local data of a sheaf.

\begin{remark}
In $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$, direct limits are exact: the direct limit of a directed system of exact sequences is exact. This is a key property distinguishing direct limits from general colimits and from inverse limits (which may not preserve exactness). The exactness of filtered colimits in module categories is the algebraic content of the fact that "compactly supported" or "finite" phenomena can be detected at finite stages.
\end{remark}
