# 36.4.2 Right Adjoints Are Continuous; Left Adjoints Are Cocontinuous

One of the most striking theorems in category theory — and one of the most practically useful — is that adjointness implies preservation of limits: right adjoints preserve all limits (RAPL), and left adjoints preserve all colimits (LAPC).

\begin{theorem}[RAPL]
If $F \dashv G$ (with$F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ and$G: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{C}$), then$G$ is continuous: it preserves all small limits. If$D: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{D}$ is any small diagram that has a limit in$\mathcal{D}$, then$G(\varprojlim D) \cong \varprojlim G \circ D$.
\end{theorem}

*Proof.* Using the adjunction $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A, GB) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(FA, B)$ and the fact that$\mathrm{Hom}(FA, -)$ preserves limits (Section 36.4.3), one computes for any test object$A$:
$$\mathrm{Hom}(A, G(\varprojlim D)) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(FA, \varprojlim D) \cong \varprojlim \mathrm{Hom}(FA, D(-)) \cong \varprojlim \mathrm{Hom}(A, GD(-)) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(A, \varprojlim GD(-)).$$
By the Yoneda Lemma, $G(\varprojlim D) \cong \varprojlim G \circ D$.$\square$

By duality, left adjoints preserve colimits.

The practical consequences pervade algebra. Rather than listing abstract applications, let us trace through specific computations.

**Example 1.** The forgetful functor $U: R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to \mathbf{Ab}$ is right adjoint to$R \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} -$, so$U$ preserves limits:
- *Products*: $U(\prod_i M_i) = \prod_i U(M_i)$ — the underlying abelian group of a product of$R$-modules is the product of the underlying abelian groups.
- *Equalizers (kernels)*: $U(\ker f) = \ker(Uf)$ — the underlying abelian group of the kernel of a module map is the kernel of the underlying abelian group map.
- In fact, $U$ creates limits (Section 36.4.1): the$R$-module structure on limits is inherited term-wise.

**Example 2.** The tensor functor $- \otimes_R N: R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to \text{Ab}$ is left adjoint to$\mathrm{Hom}(N, -)$, so it preserves colimits:
- *Coproducts (direct sums)*: $(M \oplus M') \otimes N \cong (M \otimes N) \oplus (M' \otimes N)$ — tensor distributes over direct sums.
- *Coequalizers (cokernels, right-exactness)*: Given exact $M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$, the sequence$M' \otimes N \to M \otimes N \to M'' \otimes N \to 0$ is exact.
- *All colimits*: $(\varinjlim M_i) \otimes N \cong \varinjlim (M_i \otimes N)$ — tensor commutes with all colimits.

**Example 3.** The free group functor $F: \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Grp}$ is left adjoint to$U$, so$F$ preserves all colimits:
- *Coproducts*: $F(S \sqcup T) \cong F(S) * F(T)$ — the free group on a disjoint union is the free product.
- *Coequalizers*: $F$ sends quotient sets to the corresponding quotient groups (via the normal closure of the image of the relation).
- *Filtered colimits*: $F(\varinjlim S_i) \cong \varinjlim F(S_i)$ — the free group on a filtered direct limit of sets is the filtered direct limit of the free groups.

**Example 4.** The abelianization functor $\mathrm{Ab}: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Ab}$ is left adjoint to the inclusion$\iota$, so$\mathrm{Ab}$ preserves all colimits:
- *Coproducts in $\mathbf{Ab}$*:$\mathrm{Ab}(G * H) \cong G^{\mathrm{ab}} \oplus H^{\mathrm{ab}}$ — the abelianization of a free product is the direct sum of abelianizations.
- *Pushouts*: if $G$ and$H$ are pushed out along$K \to G$ and$K \to H$, the abelianization of the pushout is the pushout in$\mathbf{Ab}$ of the abelianizations along the abelianized maps.
- *Coequalizers*: $\mathrm{Ab}(G/N) \cong G^{\mathrm{ab}} / \overline{N}$ (abelianization commutes with quotients).

**Example 5.** The constant sheaf functor $\underline{A}: \mathbf{Ab} \to \mathbf{Sh}(X, \mathbf{Ab})$ is left adjoint to$\Gamma(X, -)$ (global sections), so$\underline{(-)}$ preserves colimits:
- $\underline{A \oplus B} \cong \underline{A} \oplus \underline{B}$ — constant sheaf of a direct sum is the direct sum of constant sheaves.
- $\underline{A/B} \cong \underline{A}/\underline{B}$ — constant sheaf of a quotient is the quotient of constant sheaves.
The global sections functor $\Gamma$ is right adjoint and preserves limits:$\Gamma(X, \prod_i \mathcal{F}_i) \cong \prod_i \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F}_i)$.

\begin{remark}
The theorem converts the problem of checking preservation of limits or colimits into the problem of identifying adjoint pairs — often a much more tractable task. If one wants to know whether a specific functor preserves products, the quickest approach is often to determine whether it is a right adjoint (in which case RAPL gives products and all other limits for free) rather than to check products directly. This is why adjunctions are so powerful: they provide "global" information (preservation of all limits or colimits) from "local" data (the hom-set bijection).
\end{remark}
