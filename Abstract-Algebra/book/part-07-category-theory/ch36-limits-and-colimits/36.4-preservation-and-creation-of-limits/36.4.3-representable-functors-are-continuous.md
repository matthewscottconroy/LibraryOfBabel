# 36.4.3 Representable Functors Are Continuous

The preservation of limits by representable functors is both a fundamental theorem in its own right and the key step in the proof of RAPL. It expresses a deep connection between the universal property of limits and the universal property of hom-functors.

\begin{theorem}
For any object $A$ in a locally small category$\mathcal{C}$, the representable functor$\mathrm{Hom}(A, -): \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}$ preserves all limits: there is a natural isomorphism
$$\mathrm{Hom}(A, \varprojlim_j D_j) \cong \varprojlim_j \mathrm{Hom}(A, D_j).$$
\end{theorem}

*Proof.* This is a direct consequence of the universal property of limits. By the universal property of $\varprojlim D$, a morphism$A \to \varprojlim D$ is exactly the same data as a compatible family of morphisms$\{f_j: A \to D(j)\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}}$ — i.e., a cone from$A$ to$D$. The set of such cones is$\varprojlim_j \mathrm{Hom}(A, D(j))$ (the limit of the sets$\mathrm{Hom}(A, D(j))$ in$\mathbf{Set}$, computed as a compatible family). So$\mathrm{Hom}(A, \varprojlim D) \cong \varprojlim_j \mathrm{Hom}(A, D(j))$, and the isomorphism is natural in$A$ and in the diagram$D$.$\square$

**Example 1.** Products. For $D$ the discrete two-object diagram with$D(0) = B$ and$D(1) = C$:
$$\mathrm{Hom}(A, B \times C) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(A, B) \times \mathrm{Hom}(A, C).$$
This is the basic fact used constantly in algebra: to give a map from $A$ into a product is the same as giving a pair of maps into the factors. In$\mathbf{Set}$: a function$A \to B \times C$ is a pair of functions$A \to B$ and$A \to C$. In$R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$: a module homomorphism$M \to N \times P$ is a pair of homomorphisms$M \to N$ and$M \to P$.

**Example 2.** Equalizers. For $D$ the parallel pair$f, g: B \to C$:
$$\mathrm{Hom}(A, \mathrm{Eq}(f,g)) \cong \{h \in \mathrm{Hom}(A, B) : f \circ h = g \circ h\}.$$
Maps from $A$ into the equalizer correspond to maps$A \to B$ on which$f$ and$g$ agree. This is used in the following way: to check that a morphism$h: A \to B$ factors through the equalizer, one only needs to verify$f \circ h = g \circ h$ — the factorization is then automatic and unique.

**Example 3.** Kernels. In $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$,$\mathrm{Hom}(M, \ker f) \cong \{g \in \mathrm{Hom}(M, N) : f \circ g = 0\}$. This says: maps into the kernel are exactly the maps that kill everything under$f$. This is the defining property of the kernel as a subobject, and it follows from the general fact that representable functors preserve limits.

**Example 4.** Pullbacks. For a pullback $A \times_C B$ (limit of the cospan$A \to C \leftarrow B$):
$$\mathrm{Hom}(N, A \times_C B) \cong \{(f, g) \in \mathrm{Hom}(N, A) \times \mathrm{Hom}(N, B) : \pi_A \circ f = \pi_B \circ g\}.$$
Maps from $N$ into the pullback correspond to compatible pairs of maps from$N$ into$A$ and$B$.

**Example 5.** The contravariant case. Dually, the contravariant representable functor $\mathrm{Hom}(-, A): \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}$ converts colimits in$\mathcal{C}$ to limits in$\mathbf{Set}$:
$$\mathrm{Hom}(\varinjlim D, A) \cong \varprojlim_j \mathrm{Hom}(D(j), A).$$
In $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$:$\mathrm{Hom}(\bigoplus_i M_i, N) \cong \prod_i \mathrm{Hom}(M_i, N)$ — maps out of a direct sum are given by a family of maps out of each summand. This is used constantly: specifying a module map from a direct sum is the same as specifying maps from each summand.

\begin{remark}
The connection to the Yoneda lemma and to RAPL is as follows. The theorem "$\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)$ preserves limits" is the same as saying that$\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)$ is a right adjoint (to the copower functor, when it exists) or, more directly, that the limit construction represents the functor of cones from$A$. The RAPL theorem then says: for any right adjoint$G: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{C}$, the composite$\mathrm{Hom}(A, G(-)) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(FA, -)$ is a representable functor of the second argument, hence preserves limits, and by Yoneda,$G$ itself must preserve limits.
\end{remark}

The contravariant version also connects to LAPC: $\mathrm{Hom}(-, A)$ converts colimits to limits, which is related to the fact that colimit-preserving functors (left adjoints) can be detected by their behavior on hom-sets with$\mathrm{Hom}(-, A)$. Explicitly, a functor$F$ preserves a colimit$\varinjlim D$ if and only if$\mathrm{Hom}(F(\varinjlim D), A) \cong \varprojlim \mathrm{Hom}(F \circ D, A)$ for all$A$, which by Yoneda reduces to the colimit condition. This gives yet another perspective on the deep connection between representability, adjoints, and limit/colimit preservation.
