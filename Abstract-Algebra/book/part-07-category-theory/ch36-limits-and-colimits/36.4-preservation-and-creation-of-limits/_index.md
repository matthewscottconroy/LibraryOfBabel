# 36.4 Preservation and Creation of Limits

A functor $F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ *preserves* a limit if it sends limit cones to limit cones: whenever $(\lim D, \{\lambda_j\})$ is the limit of $D: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C}$, the image $(F(\lim D), \{F(\lambda_j)\})$ is the limit of $F \circ D: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{D}$. A functor that preserves all small limits is called *continuous* (in the categorical sense); one that preserves all small colimits is *cocontinuous*. These are extremely strong conditions — not every functor satisfies them — but the adjoint functor theorem tells us exactly which functors do: right adjoints are continuous and left adjoints are cocontinuous. This is one of the central organizing theorems of category theory.

The proof that right adjoints preserve limits is conceptually clean. If $G: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{C}$ is right adjoint to $F$, and $L = \lim D$ in $\mathcal{D}$, then for any object $A \in \mathcal{C}$ the natural bijections give $\text{Hom}(A, G(L)) \cong \text{Hom}(F(A), L) \cong \lim_j \text{Hom}(F(A), D(j)) \cong \lim_j \text{Hom}(A, G(D(j)))$. The Yoneda lemma then identifies $G(L)$ with the limit of $G \circ D$. The key step — that $\text{Hom}(F(A), -)$ preserves limits — follows from the fact that hom-sets preserve limits (maps into a limit are compatible families of maps into the diagram). The argument is a model of how the Yoneda lemma, the hom-set definition of limits, and the adjunction bijection interact.

*Creation* of limits is a stronger condition than preservation. A functor $G$ *creates* a limit for a diagram $D$ if: whenever $G \circ D$ has a limit in $\mathcal{D}$, the diagram $D$ itself has a limit in $\mathcal{C}$, and $G$ sends this limit to the given limit of $G \circ D$. The forgetful functor $U: \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Set}$ creates all limits: the limit of a diagram of groups, computed as a subset of the product of their underlying sets, automatically carries a group structure. This is the categorical explanation for why limits of algebraic structures can always be computed "on the underlying sets" and then equipped with the appropriate structure.

*Representable functors* $\text{Hom}(A, -): \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}$ are always continuous: they preserve all limits that exist in $\mathcal{C}$. This is the categorical form of the statement that $\text{Hom}(A, -)$ is left exact in the abelian category sense — it converts exact sequences of the form $0 \to B' \to B \to B''$ into exact sequences of sets. The failure of $\text{Hom}(A, -)$ to preserve colimits (it does not, in general, convert coproducts to products) is measured by the Ext functors, making the limit-preservation property of representable functors the starting point for homological algebra. This section is thus the bridge between the categorical theory of limits and the homological algebra developed in subsequent parts of the text.
