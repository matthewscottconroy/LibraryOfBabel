# 37.1.2 Biproducts: Products = Coproducts

In a general category, products and coproducts are very different constructions. In $\mathbf{Set}$, the product$A \times B$ and the coproduct$A \sqcup B$ (disjoint union) are entirely different; in$\mathbf{Grp}$, the product$G \times H$ is the direct product while the coproduct$G * H$ is the vastly larger free product. One of the most striking features of additive categories is that finite products and finite coproducts coincide.

\begin{definition}
In an additive category, the *biproduct* $A \oplus B$ of two objects$A$ and$B$ is an object equipped with:
- Projection morphisms $\pi_A: A \oplus B \to A$ and$\pi_B: A \oplus B \to B$,
- Inclusion morphisms $\iota_A: A \to A \oplus B$ and$\iota_B: B \to A \oplus B$,
satisfying the relations:
$$\pi_A \circ \iota_A = \mathrm{id}_A, \quad \pi_B \circ \iota_B = \mathrm{id}_B, \quad \pi_A \circ \iota_B = 0, \quad \pi_B \circ \iota_A = 0,$$
and the *completeness relation*: $\iota_A \circ \pi_A + \iota_B \circ \pi_B = \mathrm{id}_{A \oplus B}$.
\end{definition}

The last relation — that the identity on $A \oplus B$ decomposes as the sum of two "projection-inclusion" endomorphisms — is what crucially uses the additive structure on hom-sets and distinguishes biproducts from mere products.

\begin{proposition}
In an additive category, a biproduct $A \oplus B$ is simultaneously a product (with projections$\pi_A, \pi_B$) and a coproduct (with inclusions$\iota_A, \iota_B$).
\end{proposition}

*Proof.* For the product universal property: given $f: C \to A$ and$g: C \to B$, define$h: C \to A \oplus B$ by$h = \iota_A \circ f + \iota_B \circ g$. Then$\pi_A \circ h = \pi_A \circ (\iota_A \circ f + \iota_B \circ g) = f + 0 = f$, and similarly$\pi_B \circ h = g$. Uniqueness: if$h': C \to A \oplus B$ with$\pi_A \circ h' = f$ and$\pi_B \circ h' = g$, then$h' = \mathrm{id} \circ h' = (\iota_A \pi_A + \iota_B \pi_B) \circ h' = \iota_A f + \iota_B g = h$. For the coproduct: given$f: A \to D$ and$g: B \to D$, define$k: A \oplus B \to D$ by$k = f \circ \pi_A + g \circ \pi_B$. Then$k \circ \iota_A = f \circ \pi_A \circ \iota_A + g \circ \pi_B \circ \iota_A = f + 0 = f$, and similarly$k \circ \iota_B = g$.$\square$

**Example 1.** In $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$, the biproduct is the direct sum$M \oplus N = M \times N$ (as an abelian group). Elements are pairs$(m, n)$, with$\iota_M(m) = (m, 0)$,$\pi_M(m, n) = m$,$\iota_N(n) = (0, n)$,$\pi_N(m, n) = n$. The completeness relation:$\iota_M(\pi_M(m,n)) + \iota_N(\pi_N(m,n)) = (m,0) + (0,n) = (m,n) = \mathrm{id}(m,n)$.$\checkmark$

**Example 2.** In $\mathbf{Ab}$, the biproduct of$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and$\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ is$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (by CRT). The product and coproduct coincide, and the direct sum has exactly 6 elements.

**Example 3.** For finite families, the biproduct in $\mathbf{Ab}$ coincides with the product:$\prod_{i=1}^n A_i = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n A_i$. For infinite families, this fails: the product$\prod_{n \geq 1} \mathbb{Z}$ (all integer sequences) is strictly larger than the direct sum$\bigoplus_{n \geq 1} \mathbb{Z}$ (finitely-supported sequences). Biproducts are only guaranteed for finite families in additive categories.

**Example 4.** The biproduct in $\mathrm{Ch}(R)$ (chain complexes):$(C_\bullet \oplus D_\bullet)_n = C_n \oplus D_n$ with the differential acting component-wise. The projections and inclusions act on each degree. This is both the product and the coproduct in$\mathrm{Ch}(R)$ for finite families.

**Example 5.** Idempotent splitting. If $e: A \to A$ is an idempotent ($e^2 = e$) in an additive category, the "splitting" of$e$ (if it exists) is a biproduct decomposition$A \cong \mathrm{im}(e) \oplus \mathrm{im}(1-e)$. In$R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$, every idempotent splits:$M = eM \oplus (1-e)M$. Additive categories in which every idempotent splits are called *Karoubian* (or *pseudo-abelian*), and the Karoubi envelope of an additive category is its completion to a Karoubian category.

\begin{remark}
The coincidence of finite products and finite coproducts is a characteristic property of additive categories: it fails in all the standard non-additive examples ($\mathbf{Set}$,$\mathbf{Grp}$,$\mathbf{Top}$), and its presence is a signal that the category is "linear" in a deep sense. The biproduct is also the bridge between the "additive" structure of homomorphism groups and the "multiplicative" (composition) structure of the category: the formula$\iota_A \pi_A + \iota_B \pi_B = \mathrm{id}$ uses addition of morphisms in an essential way and cannot be stated in a non-additive category.
\end{remark}
