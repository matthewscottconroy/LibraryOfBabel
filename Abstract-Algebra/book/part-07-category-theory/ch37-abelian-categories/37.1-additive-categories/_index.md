# 37.1 Additive Categories

Before one can speak of exact sequences or kernels in a categorical setting, the category must have enough algebraic structure to support these concepts. The first layer of this structure is captured by the notion of a *preadditive category*: one in which every hom-set $\text{Hom}(A, B)$ carries the structure of an abelian group, and in which composition is bilinear — that is, $h \circ (f + g) = h \circ f + h \circ g$ and $(f + g) \circ h = f \circ h + g \circ h$. A preadditive category is, in effect, a category whose morphisms can be added as well as composed. Every ring $R$ defines a preadditive category with one object, with the ring elements as morphisms and multiplication as composition — making preadditive categories a direct generalization of rings.

An *additive category* is a preadditive category that also has a *zero object* (an object that is simultaneously initial and terminal) and *finite biproducts*. A *biproduct* of objects $A$ and $B$ is an object $A \oplus B$ equipped with morphisms $i_1: A \to A \oplus B$, $i_2: B \to A \oplus B$, $p_1: A \oplus B \to A$, $p_2: A \oplus B \to B$ satisfying $p_j \circ i_j = \text{id}$ and $p_j \circ i_k = 0$ for $j \neq k$, along with $i_1 \circ p_1 + i_2 \circ p_2 = \text{id}_{A \oplus B}$. The remarkable feature of the biproduct is that it satisfies simultaneously the universal properties of both the product and the coproduct. In $\mathbf{Ab}$ and $\mathbf{Mod}_R$, the direct sum $A \oplus B$ is the biproduct, and the coincidence of products and coproducts for finite collections is a hallmark of additive structure.

The zero object $0$ — satisfying $\text{Hom}(A, 0) = \{0\}$ and $\text{Hom}(0, A) = \{0\}$ for all $A$ — provides a *zero morphism* $0_{A,B}: A \to B$ in every hom-set, defined as the composite $A \to 0 \to B$. In a preadditive category, the zero morphism in the group $\text{Hom}(A, B)$ is exactly this composite. The existence of zero morphisms allows one to define kernels and cokernels: the kernel of $f: A \to B$ is the equalizer of $f$ and $0_{A,B}$, and the cokernel is the coequalizer. These definitions require only that kernels and cokernels exist — which is not guaranteed by the additive structure alone — and the question of their existence and behavior is what distinguishes additive categories from preabelian and abelian ones.

The additive structure is preserved by *additive functors* — functors between additive categories that are group homomorphisms on each hom-set. The forgetful functor from $R\text{-Mod}$ to $\mathbf{Ab}$ and the hom-functor $\text{Hom}_R(M, -)$ for a fixed module $M$ are both additive. Non-additive functors between additive categories exist but are unusual; most naturally occurring functors between module categories are additive. The condition of additivity is the categorical analog of linearity, and it is the first condition imposed when building toward the full axioms of an abelian category.
