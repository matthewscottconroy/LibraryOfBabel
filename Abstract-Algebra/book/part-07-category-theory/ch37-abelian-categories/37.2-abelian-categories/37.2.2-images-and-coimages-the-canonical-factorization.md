# 37.2.2 Images and Coimages; the Canonical Factorization

In the category of sets or modules, every function $f: A \to B$ factors as a surjection$A \twoheadrightarrow f(A)$ followed by an injection$f(A) \hookrightarrow B$. The first isomorphism theorem sharpens this: there is a canonical isomorphism$A/\ker(f) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{im}(f)$. In an abelian category, both halves of this factorization have purely categorical definitions, and the isomorphism between them is guaranteed by the axioms.

\begin{definition}
For a morphism $f: A \to B$ in an abelian category:
- The *image* of $f$ is$\mathrm{im}(f) = \ker(\mathrm{coker}(f))$: the kernel of the cokernel map$B \to \mathrm{coker}(f)$.
- The *coimage* of $f$ is$\mathrm{coim}(f) = \mathrm{coker}(\ker(f))$: the cokernel of the kernel inclusion$\ker(f) \to A$.
\end{definition}

The image $\mathrm{im}(f)$ is the "subobject of$B$ generated by the image of$f$" — the smallest subobject through which$f$ factors. The coimage$\mathrm{coim}(f)$ is the "quotient of$A$ by the kernel" — the largest quotient on which$f$ is injective.

\begin{proposition}
There is a canonical morphism $\bar{f}: \mathrm{coim}(f) \to \mathrm{im}(f)$ fitting into the factorization$A \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{coim}(f) \xrightarrow{\bar{f}} \mathrm{im}(f) \hookrightarrow B$, and$\bar{f}$ is always a monomorphism.
\end{proposition}

*Proof sketch.* The composite $A \xrightarrow{f} B \to \mathrm{coker}(f)$ is zero (by definition of cokernel), so$f$ factors through$\ker(\mathrm{coker}(f)) = \mathrm{im}(f)$: there is a morphism$A \to \mathrm{im}(f)$. Since$\ker(f) \to A \to \mathrm{im}(f)$ is zero (because$f \circ \ker(f) = 0$ and$\mathrm{im}(f) \hookrightarrow B$ is monic), the map$A \to \mathrm{im}(f)$ factors through$\mathrm{coim}(f) = \mathrm{coker}(\ker(f))$, giving the canonical map$\bar{f}: \mathrm{coim}(f) \to \mathrm{im}(f)$. That$\bar{f}$ is monic follows from the definition of image and the universal properties involved.$\square$

\begin{theorem}[First Isomorphism Theorem in Abelian Categories]
In an abelian category, the canonical map $\bar{f}: \mathrm{coim}(f) \to \mathrm{im}(f)$ is an isomorphism for every morphism$f$. Consequently, every morphism$f: A \to B$ factors canonically as
$$A \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{coim}(f) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{im}(f) \hookrightarrow B,$$
where the first map is an epimorphism and the last is a monomorphism.
\end{theorem}

This isomorphism is precisely the abelian category axiom "every monomorphism is the kernel of its cokernel" applied to $\mathrm{im}(f) \hookrightarrow B$: the image, being defined as$\ker(\mathrm{coker}(f))$, is a kernel, hence a monomorphism; and the coimage, being a cokernel, is an epimorphism.

**Example 1.** In $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$:$\ker(f) = f^{-1}(0) \subseteq M$,$\mathrm{coker}(f) = N/f(M)$,$\mathrm{coim}(f) = M/\ker(f)$,$\mathrm{im}(f) = f(M) \subseteq N$. The canonical map$\bar{f}: M/\ker(f) \to f(M)$ sends$m + \ker(f) \mapsto f(m)$, which is the isomorphism of the standard first isomorphism theorem for modules. The factorization$M \twoheadrightarrow M/\ker(f) \xrightarrow{\sim} f(M) \hookrightarrow N$ is the standard one.

**Example 2.** In $\mathbf{Ab}$, the factorization of$f: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (reduction mod 6). Here$\ker(f) = 6\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathrm{coim}(f) = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathrm{im}(f) = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (since$f$ is surjective), and$\mathrm{coker}(f) = 0$. The factorization$\mathbb{Z} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}} \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ is$\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ (surjective, isomorphism of coimage with image, identity inclusion).

**Example 3.** For a non-surjective, non-injective map: $f: \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ given by$f(n) = 2n$. Then$\ker(f) = \{0, 3\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathrm{coim}(f) = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} / \{0,3\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathrm{im}(f) = \{0, 2, 4\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, and$\mathrm{coker}(f) = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}/\{0,2,4\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The canonical isomorphism$\bar{f}: \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ is the identity (matching$1 \mapsto 2$ via the indexing), and the factorization is$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 4.** In $\mathrm{Sh}(X, \mathbf{Ab})$: the image of a sheaf map$f: \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}$ is the sub-sheaf of$\mathcal{G}$ generated by the local sections in the image of$f$. Since image in sheaf theory requires sheafification (the presheaf image need not be a sheaf), the categorical definition (image = kernel of cokernel) automatically builds in the necessary sheafification. The canonical factorization is$\mathcal{F} \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{F}/\ker(f) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{im}(f) \hookrightarrow \mathcal{G}$.

\begin{remark}
The image-coimage isomorphism is the engine that makes diagram-chasing arguments work uniformly across all abelian categories. In $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$, diagram chasing uses elements directly; in a general abelian category, one uses the image-coimage isomorphism to reduce to the case of module categories via the Freyd–Mitchell embedding (Section 37.2.4). The canonical factorization$A \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{coim}(f) \cong \mathrm{im}(f) \hookrightarrow B$ is also the starting point for the definition of exact sequences (Section 37.3.1): a sequence$A \to B \to C$ is exact at$B$ precisely when$\mathrm{im}(A \to B) = \ker(B \to C)$.
\end{remark}
