# 37.2 Abelian Categories

An *abelian category* is an additive category in which every morphism has both a kernel and a cokernel, and in which a specific coherence condition holds: the canonical morphism from the coimage to the image of every morphism is an isomorphism. To unpack this, given a morphism $f: A \to B$, its kernel is $\ker(f): K \to A$ (the equalizer of $f$ and $0$), its cokernel is $\text{coker}(f): B \to C$ (the coequalizer of $f$ and $0$), its *image* is $\text{im}(f) = \ker(\text{coker}(f))$ (the kernel of the cokernel, sitting inside $B$), and its *coimage* is $\text{coim}(f) = \text{coker}(\ker(f))$ (the cokernel of the kernel, a quotient of $A$). There is always a canonical morphism $\text{coim}(f) \to \text{im}(f)$; the abelian category axiom demands that this be an isomorphism.

This axiom is the categorical form of the first isomorphism theorem: $A/\ker(f) \cong \text{im}(f)$. In $R\text{-Mod}$ it holds because the first isomorphism theorem holds for modules. The axiom ensures that every morphism factors as an epimorphism (coimage) followed by a monomorphism (image): $A \twoheadrightarrow \text{coim}(f) \cong \text{im}(f) \hookrightarrow B$. This *epi-mono factorization* is the structural feature that makes homological algebra work — it is what allows one to define exact sequences and talk about images and kernels in a coherent way.

The main examples beyond $R\text{-Mod}$ and $\mathbf{Ab}$ are the categories of sheaves of abelian groups on a topological space or site: $\text{Sh}(X, \mathbf{Ab})$ is abelian, with kernels and cokernels computed pointwise on stalks. The category $[\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Ab}]$ of functors from any small category $\mathcal{C}$ to abelian groups is abelian, computed pointwise. The category of representations of a finite group $G$ over a field $k$ is abelian (it is a full subcategory of $kG\text{-Mod}$). The category of coherent sheaves on a projective variety is abelian. The diversity of these examples is exactly the point: abelian categories provide a single framework for homological algebra across all of them.

The *Freyd–Mitchell embedding theorem* completes the justification for element-chasing proofs in abelian categories. It asserts that every small abelian category $\mathcal{A}$ admits a full, exact embedding into $R\text{-Mod}$ for some ring $R$ — a functor that is fully faithful and preserves and reflects all exact sequences. As a consequence, any statement about abelian categories that can be proved in $R\text{-Mod}$ by element-chasing (provided the argument uses only the first-order language of abelian categories) is valid in every abelian category. This makes it legitimate to write proofs of the snake lemma, the five lemma, or the nine lemma by chasing elements as if objects had elements, even when working in a sheaf category where objects are sheaves and "elements" are only a fiction.
