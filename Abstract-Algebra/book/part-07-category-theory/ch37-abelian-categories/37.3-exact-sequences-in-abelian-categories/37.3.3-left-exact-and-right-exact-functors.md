# 37.3.3 Left Exact and Right Exact Functors

Not every functor between abelian categories preserves exact sequences. This failure of preservation is not a defect to be corrected but rather a source of information — the failure is systematic, and measuring it gives rise to derived functors. The fundamental classification distinguishes left exact, right exact, and exact functors.

\begin{definition}
A functor $F: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ between abelian categories is:
- *Left exact* if for every short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$, the induced sequence$0 \to F(A) \to F(B) \to F(C)$ is exact.
- *Right exact* if $F(A) \to F(B) \to F(C) \to 0$ is exact for every short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$.
- *Exact* if $0 \to F(A) \to F(B) \to F(C) \to 0$ is exact for every short exact sequence — i.e., both left and right exact.
\end{definition}

Left exact means: $F$ preserves kernels (monomorphisms) and the image-kernel condition at$B$ from the left. Right exact means:$F$ preserves cokernels (epimorphisms) and the image-kernel condition at$B$ from the right.

**Example 1.** $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M, -)$ is left exact. For a fixed$R$-module$M$ and a short exact sequence$0 \to A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$, the induced sequence$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(M, A) \xrightarrow{f_*} \mathrm{Hom}(M, B) \xrightarrow{g_*} \mathrm{Hom}(M, C)$ is exact. Exactness at$\mathrm{Hom}(M, A)$: if$\phi: M \to A$ with$f \circ \phi = 0$, then since$f$ is injective,$\phi = 0$. Exactness at$\mathrm{Hom}(M, B)$:$\ker(g_*) = \{\phi : g \circ \phi = 0\} = \{\phi : \mathrm{im}(\phi) \subseteq \ker(g) = \mathrm{im}(f)\} = \mathrm{im}(f_*)$.

The last map $g_*: \mathrm{Hom}(M, B) \to \mathrm{Hom}(M, C)$ need not be surjective: the obstruction is that a map$M \to C$ may not lift to$M \to B$ unless$M$ is projective. The failure of surjectivity is measured by$\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, A)$.

**Example 2.** $\mathrm{Hom}_R(-, M)$ is left exact (contravariantly). For a short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$, the induced sequence$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(C, M) \to \mathrm{Hom}(B, M) \to \mathrm{Hom}(A, M)$ is exact. The last map need not be surjective: the obstruction is that a map$A \to M$ may not extend to$B \to M$ unless$M$ is injective.

**Example 3.** $- \otimes_R N$ is right exact. For a short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ and a fixed module$N$, the sequence$A \otimes N \to B \otimes N \to C \otimes N \to 0$ is exact. The first map$A \otimes N \to B \otimes N$ need not be injective: the obstruction is$\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(C, N)$. For example,$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\cdot 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ tensored with$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$:$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{0} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$, where the first map is zero (not injective), reflecting$\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \neq 0$.

**Example 4.** Exact functors. The following functors are exact (preserve all short exact sequences):
- *Localization*: $M \mapsto S^{-1}M$ for a multiplicative set$S$ in a commutative ring$R$ is exact (both left and right) when applied to short exact sequences of$R$-modules.
- *Restriction of scalars*: $\phi^*: S\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ along a ring map$\phi: R \to S$ is exact (as it is both left and right adjoint to... no, it is a right adjoint, so left exact; but actually restriction of scalars is exact because it's both left and right exact, reflecting the fact that exactness in$S\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ is determined stalk-wise on underlying$R$-modules).
- *Flat modules*: $- \otimes_R M$ is exact iff$M$ is flat. For a field$k$,$- \otimes_k V$ is always exact.

**Example 5.** Additivity of exact functors. Any additive functor $F: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ that is exact also preserves longer exact sequences: if$A_0 \to A_1 \to \cdots \to A_n$ is exact, then$F(A_0) \to F(A_1) \to \cdots \to F(A_n)$ is exact. This follows from the fact that long exact sequences can be broken into short exact sequences, and exactness is preserved at each step.

\begin{remark}
Left exactness of $\mathrm{Hom}(M, -)$ and right exactness of$- \otimes N$ are the starting points for the definitions of the derived functors$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, -)$ and$\mathrm{Tor}_n^R(-, N)$: the derived functors measure the failure of these functors to be exact, and they are constructed by replacing modules with projective (or injective) resolutions and applying the functor to the resolution. The systematic study of these failures — homological algebra — constitutes Part VIII of this text, and the abelian category framework is what allows the entire theory to be developed in full generality.
\end{remark}
