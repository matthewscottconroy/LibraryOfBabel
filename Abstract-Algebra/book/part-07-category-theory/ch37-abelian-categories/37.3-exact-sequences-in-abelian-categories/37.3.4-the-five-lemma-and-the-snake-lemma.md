# 37.3.4 The Five Lemma and the Snake Lemma

Two of the most important tools in homological algebra are not theorems about individual exact sequences but about *maps between* exact sequences — commutative diagrams with exact rows. These results, the Five Lemma and the Snake Lemma, are the prototype for all the comparison and connecting arguments that recur throughout the subject.

## The Five Lemma

\begin{theorem}[Five Lemma]
Consider a commutative diagram with exact rows in an abelian category:
$$\begin{array}{ccccccccc} A_1 & \xrightarrow{} & A_2 & \xrightarrow{} & A_3 & \xrightarrow{} & A_4 & \xrightarrow{} & A_5 \\ \downarrow{\alpha_1} && \downarrow{\alpha_2} && \downarrow{\alpha_3} && \downarrow{\alpha_4} && \downarrow{\alpha_5} \\ B_1 & \xrightarrow{} & B_2 & \xrightarrow{} & B_3 & \xrightarrow{} & B_4 & \xrightarrow{} & B_5 \end{array}$$
If $\alpha_2$ and$\alpha_4$ are isomorphisms,$\alpha_1$ is an epimorphism, and$\alpha_5$ is a monomorphism, then$\alpha_3$ is an isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

*Proof (diagram chase, justified by Freyd–Mitchell).* We show $\alpha_3$ is monic and epic. To show$\alpha_3$ is monic: suppose$a_3 \in A_3$ with$\alpha_3(a_3) = 0$. Map$a_3$ to$A_4$ via the top row:$a_4 = (a_3 \to A_4)$. Then$\alpha_4(a_4) = (B_3 \to B_4)(\alpha_3(a_3)) = 0$ by commutativity and$\alpha_3(a_3) = 0$. Since$\alpha_4$ is an isomorphism,$a_4 = 0$. By exactness of the top row at$A_4$,$a_3 \in \ker(A_3 \to A_4) = \mathrm{im}(A_2 \to A_3)$, so$a_3 = (a_2 \to A_3)$ for some$a_2 \in A_2$. Then$\alpha_2(a_2)$ maps to$(B_3)$ as$\alpha_3(a_3) = 0$, so$\alpha_2(a_2) \in \ker(B_2 \to B_3) = \mathrm{im}(B_1 \to B_2)$. Since$\alpha_2$ is an isomorphism,$\alpha_2$ gives a bijection on the relevant hom-sets, and$a_2 \in \mathrm{im}(A_1 \to A_2)$ (using the surjectivity of$\alpha_1$ at the top and injectivity in the chain). Therefore$a_3 = 0$ by exactness at$A_2$.$\checkmark$ The proof that$\alpha_3$ is epic is dual.$\square$

**Example 1.** The Five Lemma in $\mathbf{Ab}$. Suppose$f: G \to H$ is a group homomorphism between two groups each fitting in exact sequences$0 \to A \to G \to C \to 0$ and$0 \to A \to H \to C \to 0$, with$f$ restricting to the identity on$A$ and inducing the identity on$C$. Then the Five Lemma (applied to the five-term sequences with trivial outer terms) shows$f$ is an isomorphism. This is the proof technique behind many uniqueness arguments in group theory.

**Example 2.** Uniqueness of products. If two objects $P$ and$P'$ both serve as products of$A \times B$, with projection maps, then the unique comparison map$P \to P'$ (from the universal property of$P'$) fits into a five-lemma diagram, and one concludes it is an isomorphism.

## The Snake Lemma

\begin{theorem}[Snake Lemma]
Given a commutative diagram with exact rows in an abelian category:
$$\begin{array}{ccccccc} & A & \xrightarrow{f} & B & \xrightarrow{g} & C & \to 0 \\ & \downarrow{a} && \downarrow{b} && \downarrow{c} & \\ 0 \to & A' & \xrightarrow{f'} & B' & \xrightarrow{g'} & C' & \end{array}$$
there exists a natural *connecting homomorphism* $\partial: \ker(c) \to \mathrm{coker}(a)$, and the sequence
$$\ker(a) \to \ker(b) \to \ker(c) \xrightarrow{\partial} \mathrm{coker}(a) \to \mathrm{coker}(b) \to \mathrm{coker}(c)$$
is exact. Moreover, if $f$ is a monomorphism,$0 \to \ker(a) \to \ker(b) \to \ker(c)$ is exact on the left; if$g'$ is an epimorphism,$\mathrm{coker}(a) \to \mathrm{coker}(b) \to \mathrm{coker}(c) \to 0$ is exact on the right.
\end{theorem}

*Construction of $\partial$ (in modules, by element-chasing; valid in any abelian category by Freyd–Mitchell).* Given$x \in \ker(c)$:
1. Since $g$ is surjective (top row is exact at$C$), lift$x$ to$b \in B$ with$g(b) = x$.
2. Apply $b$: compute$b(b) \in B'$.
3. Since $g'(b(b)) = c(g(b)) = c(x) = 0$ (by commutativity and$x \in \ker(c)$), we have$b(b) \in \ker(g') = \mathrm{im}(f')$.
4. Since $f'$ is injective, there is a unique$a' \in A'$ with$f'(a') = b(b)$.
5. Define $\partial(x) = a' + \mathrm{im}(a) \in \mathrm{coker}(a)$.

One must verify: (i) $a'$ is independent of the choice of lift$b$, and (ii)$\partial$ is a group homomorphism, and (iii) the full sequence is exact. All three follow from the exactness of the rows and the commutativity of the diagram.

**Example 3.** Snake lemma in $\mathbf{Ab}$: long exact sequence from a short exact sequence of complexes. Given short exact sequences of$R$-modules$0 \to A_n \to B_n \to C_n \to 0$ for each$n$, compatible with differentials$d$ (a short exact sequence of chain complexes$0 \to A_\bullet \to B_\bullet \to C_\bullet \to 0$), the snake lemma applied to the diagram of differentials gives the long exact sequence in homology:$\cdots \to H_n(A) \to H_n(B) \to H_n(C) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{n-1}(A) \to H_{n-1}(B) \to \cdots$. The connecting homomorphism$\partial$ is exactly the snake-lemma connecting map.

**Example 4.** The long exact sequence for Tor. The short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ and the right-exact functor$- \otimes_R N$ give the beginning of the long exact Tor sequence:$\cdots \to \mathrm{Tor}_1^R(C, N) \to A \otimes N \to B \otimes N \to C \otimes N \to 0$, where the connecting map is the snake-lemma map applied to a projective resolution.

**Example 5.** Snake lemma in $\mathrm{Sh}(X, \mathbf{Ab})$. A short exact sequence of sheaves$0 \to \mathcal{F}' \to \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}'' \to 0$ and a morphism of such sequences give, via the snake lemma (justified by Freyd–Mitchell applied to the relevant small subcategory), a six-term exact sequence of sheaves. Taking global sections and applying the snake lemma to the (left-exact) global sections functor produces the beginning of the long exact sheaf cohomology sequence.

\begin{remark}
The Snake Lemma is the prototype for all long exact sequences in homological algebra: the long exact sequence of a pair in topology, the long exact Tor and Ext sequences, and the Mayer–Vietoris sequence all arise by the same mechanism applied to appropriate short exact sequences. The Freyd–Mitchell theorem is what validates the element-chasing proof in an arbitrary abelian category, making all these results available in the full generality of abstract abelian categories.
\end{remark}
