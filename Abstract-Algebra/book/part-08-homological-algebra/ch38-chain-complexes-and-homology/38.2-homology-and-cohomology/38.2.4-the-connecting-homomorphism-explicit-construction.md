# 38.2.4 The Connecting Homomorphism $\partial_n$: Explicit Construction

The connecting homomorphism $\partial_n: H_n(C) \to H_{n-1}(A)$ is the heart of the long exact sequence, and its construction via diagram chasing is a model for a style of argument that appears repeatedly throughout homological algebra. The idea is to trace an element through a commutative diagram, using exactness at each stage to justify each move. Every step is forced, which is what guarantees the construction is well-defined and natural. Learning to perform this construction fluidly — including checking well-definedness — is one of the essential skills in homological algebra.

The setting is a short exact sequence of chain complexes $0 \to A_\bullet \xrightarrow{i} B_\bullet \xrightarrow{p} C_\bullet \to 0$. The key diagram is the "double column" formed by the exact sequences in degrees$n$ and$n-1$:

\begin{align*}
0 \to A_n &\xrightarrow{i_n} B_n \xrightarrow{p_n} C_n \to 0 \\
0 \to A_{n-1} &\xrightarrow{i_{n-1}} B_{n-1} \xrightarrow{p_{n-1}} C_{n-1} \to 0
\end{align*}

with the vertical differentials $d^A_n: A_n \to A_{n-1}$,$d^B_n: B_n \to B_{n-1}$,$d^C_n: C_n \to C_{n-1}$, all satisfying the chain map commutativity conditions.

\begin{construction}
Given a class $[c] \in H_n(C)$ represented by a cycle$c \in Z_n(C) = \ker(d^C_n)$, the connecting homomorphism$\partial_n([c]) \in H_{n-1}(A)$ is constructed as follows:

\textbf{Step 1.} Since $p_n: B_n \to C_n$ is surjective, choose any lift$b \in B_n$ with$p_n(b) = c$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Compute $d^B_n(b) \in B_{n-1}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Show that $d^B_n(b) \in \ker(p_{n-1})$. Indeed,$p_{n-1}(d^B_n(b)) = d^C_n(p_n(b)) = d^C_n(c) = 0$ (using chain map commutativity and the fact that$c$ is a cycle).

\textbf{Step 4.} Since $\ker(p_{n-1}) = \mathrm{im}(i_{n-1})$ by exactness, there exists a unique$a \in A_{n-1}$ with$i_{n-1}(a) = d^B_n(b)$. (Uniqueness follows from the injectivity of$i_{n-1}$.)

\textbf{Step 5.} Show that $a$ is a cycle in$A_{n-1}$. Apply$i_{n-2}$:$i_{n-2}(d^A_{n-1}(a)) = d^B_{n-1}(i_{n-1}(a)) = d^B_{n-1}(d^B_n(b)) = 0$ (using chain map commutativity and$d^2 = 0$). Since$i_{n-2}$ is injective,$d^A_{n-1}(a) = 0$.

\textbf{Define} $\partial_n([c]) = [a] \in H_{n-1}(A)$.
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}
The connecting homomorphism $\partial_n$ is well-defined (independent of the choice of lift$b$ and representative cycle$c$) and$R$-linear.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $b'$ is a different lift of$c$, so$p_n(b') = c = p_n(b)$. Then$b - b' \in \ker(p_n) = \mathrm{im}(i_n)$, say$b - b' = i_n(a')$ for some$a' \in A_n$. The element$a$ from Step 4 with the new lift is$a - d^A_n(a')$ (since$i_{n-1}(a - d^A_n(a')) = d^B_n(b) - d^B_n(i_n(a')) = d^B_n(b) - i_{n-1}(d^A_n(a'))$). Wait — more carefully:$d^B_n(b') = d^B_n(b) - d^B_n(i_n(a')) = d^B_n(b) - i_{n-1}(d^A_n(a'))$. So the new$a'_{\mathrm{step 4}}$ satisfies$i_{n-1}(a'_{\mathrm{step 4}}) = d^B_n(b')$, giving$a'_{\mathrm{step 4}} = a - d^A_n(a')$. Hence$[a'_{\mathrm{step 4}}] = [a] - [d^A_n(a')] = [a]$ in$H_{n-1}(A)$. So$\partial_n$ is independent of the lift.

If instead $c' = c + d^C_{n+1}(c'')$ is a different representative of$[c]$, lift$c''$ to some$b'' \in B_{n+1}$; then$b + d^B_{n+1}(b'')$ is a lift of$c'$, and the boundary at degree$n$ is$d^B_n(b + d^B_{n+1}(b'')) = d^B_n(b) + d^B_n(d^B_{n+1}(b'')) = d^B_n(b)$. So the same$a$ results, confirming independence of the representative.
\end{proof}

The verification that the long exact sequence is exact at every term — particularly at $H_n(A)$ and$H_n(C)$ — amounts to careful diagram chases of the same type. We sketch the exactness at$H_n(B)$ (i.e.,$\ker(p_*) = \mathrm{im}(i_*)$):

- $p_* \circ i_* = 0$: For any$[a] \in H_n(A)$,$p_*(i_*([a])) = [p_n(i_n(a))] = [0]$ since$p_n \circ i_n = 0$ by exactness of$0 \to A_n \to B_n \to C_n \to 0$.
- $\ker(p_*) \subseteq \mathrm{im}(i_*)$: If$[b] \in H_n(B)$ with$p_*([b]) = 0$, then$p_n(b) = d^C_{n+1}(c')$ for some$c'$. Lift$c'$ to$b' \in B_{n+1}$; then$b - d^B_{n+1}(b')$ has$p_n(b - d^B_{n+1}(b')) = 0$, so it lies in$\mathrm{im}(i_n)$ by exactness, say$b - d^B_{n+1}(b') = i_n(a)$. Show$a$ is a cycle and$[b] = i_*([a])$.

\begin{example}
Take $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$ as a short exact sequence of complexes in degree$0$. The long exact sequence is$\cdots \to 0 \to H_0(\mathbb{Z}) \xrightarrow{\times 2} H_0(\mathbb{Z}) \xrightarrow{\pi_*} H_0(\mathbb{Z}/2) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{-1}(\mathbb{Z}) \to \cdots$, which gives$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$. The connecting homomorphism vanishes because there are no degrees below$0$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For a non-trivial $\partial$, consider the complexes$A_\bullet = (0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{0} \mathbb{Z} \to 0)$ in degrees$1$ and$0$,$C_\bullet = (0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{0} \mathbb{Z} \to 0)$ the same, and$B_\bullet$ a complex that mixes them. Take$B_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$,$B_0 = \mathbb{Z}^2$ with$d^B_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$,$i$ and$p$ the inclusions and projections on first and second components. Then$H_1(A) = \mathbb{Z}$,$H_1(B) = \mathbb{Z}$ (kernel is$\mathbb{Z} \times \{0\}$, image is$0$),$H_1(C) = \mathbb{Z}$, and the long exact sequence has a non-trivial$\partial$ threading these together.
\end{example}

The reader who carries out the full verification of exactness at all six positions once will find the pattern recurring throughout the subject. The snake lemma, the five lemma, the nine lemma — all are diagram chases of the same essential type, and mastering the connecting homomorphism construction is the gateway to all of them.
