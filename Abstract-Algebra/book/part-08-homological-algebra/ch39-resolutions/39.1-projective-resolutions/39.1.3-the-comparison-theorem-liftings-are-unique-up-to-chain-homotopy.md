# 39.1.3 The Comparison Theorem: Liftings Are Unique up to Chain Homotopy

Projective resolutions are not unique: different choices of free modules and surjections at each step produce different resolutions of the same module. If homological invariants are to be well-defined, one must know that the choice of resolution does not affect the answer. The Comparison Theorem provides exactly this: any two projective resolutions of modules related by an $R$-linear map are connected by a chain map, and that chain map is unique up to chain homotopy.

More precisely, suppose $P_\bullet \xrightarrow{\varepsilon} M$ and$Q_\bullet \xrightarrow{\eta} N$ are projective resolutions, and$f: M \to N$ is an$R$-linear map. Then there exists a chain map$\tilde{f}: P_\bullet \to Q_\bullet$ such that$\eta \circ \tilde{f}_0 = f \circ \varepsilon$ — the chain map "lifts"$f$ in the sense that it makes the augmentation square commute. The proof is an inductive construction that uses the projectivity of the$P_n$'s at each step: at degree$0$, the map$f \circ \varepsilon: P_0 \to N$ must factor through$\eta: Q_0 \to N$ (which is surjective), and projectivity of$P_0$ supplies the lift$\tilde{f}_0: P_0 \to Q_0$. At degree$1$, the map$\tilde{f}_0 \circ d_1^P: P_1 \to Q_0$ maps into$\ker(\eta) = \mathrm{im}(d_1^Q)$ (by the commutativity already established and exactness of$Q_\bullet$), so one factors$\tilde{f}_0 \circ d_1^P$ through$d_1^Q: Q_1 \to Q_0$; projectivity of$P_1$ supplies the lift$\tilde{f}_1: P_1 \to Q_1$. The pattern continues by induction.

The uniqueness up to chain homotopy is proved by an analogous inductive argument applied to the difference of two lifts. If $\tilde{f}, \tilde{f}': P_\bullet \to Q_\bullet$ are two chain maps over$f$, set$h_n = \tilde{f}_n - \tilde{f}_n'$. The difference$h: P_\bullet \to Q_\bullet$ is a chain map over$0: M \to N$. One constructs a chain homotopy$s: P_\bullet \to Q_{\bullet + 1}$ satisfying$d^Q s + s d^P = h$ by the same projectivity-lifting argument: at each degree, the relevant expression maps into the image of the next differential of$Q_\bullet$, and projectivity of$P_n$ provides a lift. The result is$\tilde{f} - \tilde{f}' = ds + sd$, i.e.,$\tilde{f}$ and$\tilde{f}'$ are chain homotopic.

The key consequence is that derived functors are well-defined. If $F$ is any additive functor from$R$-modules to abelian groups (or another module category), one defines the left derived functors$L_n F(M)$ by choosing a projective resolution$P_\bullet \to M$, applying$F$ to get a complex$F(P_\bullet)$, and taking the$n$th homology. The Comparison Theorem guarantees that any other projective resolution gives a chain-homotopy-equivalent complex$F(Q_\bullet)$, hence the same homology: since additive functors preserve chain homotopies ($F(ds + sd) = F(d)F(s) + F(s)F(d)$), a chain homotopy equivalence$P_\bullet \simeq Q_\bullet$ becomes a chain homotopy equivalence$F(P_\bullet) \simeq F(Q_\bullet)$, and chain homotopy equivalent complexes have the same homology. The invariant$L_n F(M)$ is therefore independent of the choice of resolution, up to canonical isomorphism, and defines a well-behaved functor in$M$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Let $M = N = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ and$f = \mathrm{id}$. Two free resolutions are:
$$P_\bullet: \quad 0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 6} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \to 0,$$
$$Q_\bullet: \quad 0 \to \mathbb{Z}^2 \xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}2 & 0\\0 & 3\end{pmatrix}} \mathbb{Z}^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \to 0$$
(where the augmentation of $Q_\bullet$ sends$(a, b) \mapsto 3\bar{a} + 2\bar{b}$ using the CRT isomorphism$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$). A chain map$P_\bullet \to Q_\bullet$ lifting the identity exists by the theorem; it sends the generator$1 \in P_0 = \mathbb{Z}$ to some element of$Q_0 = \mathbb{Z}^2$ mapping to$\bar{1} \in \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. After applying$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-, A)$ to both resolutions (for any$A$), the resulting cochain complexes are chain homotopy equivalent, so they yield the same cohomology groups$\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}, A)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Let $R = k[x]$ and$M = k = R/(x)$. One resolution is$P_\bullet: 0 \to R \xrightarrow{\times x} R \to k \to 0$. Another is$Q_\bullet: 0 \to R \xrightarrow{\times x} R \to k \to 0$ (the same complex). The identity map$\mathrm{id}: k \to k$ lifts uniquely (up to homotopy) to the identity chain map$P_\bullet \to Q_\bullet$. Any other lift$\tilde{f}$ of$\mathrm{id}$ differs from this by a chain homotopy:$\tilde{f}_0(1) = 1 + g(x) \cdot x$ for some polynomial$g$, and the homotopy$s_0: R \to R$ with$s_0(1) = g$ satisfies$d^Q s_0 - s_0 d^P = \tilde{f}_0 - \mathrm{id}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The Comparison Theorem applies to maps $f: M \to N$ that are not isomorphisms. Let$f: \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ be the injection$\bar{1} \mapsto \bar{2}$. With$P_\bullet: 0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ and$Q_\bullet: 0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 4} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to 0$, the lifting$\tilde{f}_0: P_0 = \mathbb{Z} \to Q_0 = \mathbb{Z}$ must satisfy$\pi_4 \circ \tilde{f}_0 = f \circ \pi_2$, where$\pi_n$ is reduction mod$n$. Thus$\tilde{f}_0(1) \equiv 2 \pmod{4}$, and we take$\tilde{f}_0 = \times 2$. Then$\tilde{f}_0 \circ (\times 2) = \times 4 = (\times 4) \circ \tilde{f}_1$ forces$\tilde{f}_1 = \mathrm{id}$. The chain map$\tilde{f}$ is then the pair$(\times 2, \mathrm{id})$ on the$\mathbb{Z}$-terms.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Comparison Theorem]
Let $P_\bullet \xrightarrow{\varepsilon} M$ be a projective resolution and$Q_\bullet \xrightarrow{\eta} N$ be any resolution (not necessarily projective) of$N$ by acyclic modules. Given any$R$-linear map$f: M \to N$, there exists a chain map$\tilde{f}: P_\bullet \to Q_\bullet$ with$\eta \circ \tilde{f}_0 = f \circ \varepsilon$. Any two such chain maps are chain homotopic. In particular, taking$M = N$ and$f = \mathrm{id}$, two projective resolutions of$M$ are chain homotopy equivalent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The Comparison Theorem is the homological algebra analogue of the fact that a linear map $f: V \to W$ lifts uniquely (up to homotopy) to a chain map between resolutions. Its proof uses nothing beyond the definitions of projectivity and chain homotopy — it is a diagram chase, but a canonical and powerful one. The deeper import is that it places homological algebra on firm foundations: all the machinery of derived functors, Ext, Tor, and group cohomology produces genuine invariants, not artifacts of choices, precisely because of this theorem.
\end{remark}
