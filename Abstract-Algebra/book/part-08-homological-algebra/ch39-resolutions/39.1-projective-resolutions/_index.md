# 39.1 Projective Resolutions

A projective module is one for which every surjection onto it splits — equivalently, one that is a direct summand of a free module. Free modules are the simplest examples, but the class of projective modules is strictly larger and enjoys better categorical properties: projectivity is preserved under direct sums, and over many rings of interest (Dedekind domains, local rings after localization) the projective modules can be classified explicitly. The key property for homological algebra is the lifting property: given a surjection $B \twoheadrightarrow C$ and any homomorphism $P \to C$ with $P$ projective, the map lifts to $P \to B$. This lifting is precisely what makes projective resolutions well-behaved.

A projective resolution of an $R$-module $M$ is an exact sequence $\cdots \to P_2 \xrightarrow{\partial_2} P_1 \xrightarrow{\partial_1} P_0 \xrightarrow{\varepsilon} M \to 0$ in which each $P_n$ is a projective $R$-module. The augmentation map $\varepsilon: P_0 \to M$ is part of the data. Such resolutions always exist: take $P_0$ to be any free module surjecting onto $M$ (which is always possible since every module is a quotient of a free module), let $M_1 = \ker \varepsilon$, take $P_1$ to be a free module surjecting onto $M_1$, and iterate. The module $M_1 = \ker(P_0 \to M)$ is called the first syzygy module of $M$, and the iterates $M_n = \ker(P_{n-1} \to M_{n-2})$ are the higher syzygy modules. The resolution records the successive obstructions to lifting the module to a free module in each degree.

The projective dimension $\operatorname{pd}_R(M)$ of $M$ is the smallest $n$ for which there exists a projective resolution of length $n$ (i.e., with $P_k = 0$ for $k > n$), or $\infty$ if no finite resolution exists. A module has projective dimension 0 if and only if it is projective; projective dimension 1 means $M$ is the cokernel of an injective map between projective modules; and so on. The projective dimension measures homological complexity. The Comparison Theorem — that any two projective resolutions of $M$ are chain homotopy equivalent — guarantees that derived functors computed from projective resolutions are independent of all choices, which is the crucial well-definedness result underpinning the entire theory of Ext and Tor.
