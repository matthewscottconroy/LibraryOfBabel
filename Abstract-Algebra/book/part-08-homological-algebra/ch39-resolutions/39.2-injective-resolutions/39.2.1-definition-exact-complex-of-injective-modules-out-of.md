# 39.2.1 Definition: Exact Complex of Injective Modules Out of $M$

Projective resolutions build a module $M$ from projective modules by mapping onto$M$ from above. Injective resolutions do the opposite: they embed$M$ into injective modules, resolving$M$ by mapping it into a sequence of larger and larger injective modules. The two constructions are formally dual — one is the other with all arrows reversed — and this duality is one of the organizing principles of homological algebra.

An *injective resolution* of an $R$-module$M$ is an exact complex
$$0 \to M \xrightarrow{\eta} I^0 \xrightarrow{d^0} I^1 \xrightarrow{d^1} I^2 \to \cdots$$
where each $I^n$ is an injective$R$-module. The cohomological indexing (superscripts, increasing) is used here because injective resolutions are the natural input for right derived functors. The map$\eta: M \to I^0$ is the *augmentation*, and it must be injective; exactness at$I^n$ for$n \geq 1$ means$\ker(d^n) = \mathrm{im}(d^{n-1})$. The module$M$ appears only at the initial position, embedded into the resolution as a submodule of$I^0$.

It is instructive to unpack what exactness says. Exactness at $I^0$ means$\ker(d^0) = \mathrm{im}(\eta) \cong M$, so the image of$M$ inside$I^0$ is exactly the kernel of$d^0$; equivalently, the induced map$I^0/M \to I^1$ is injective with image$\mathrm{im}(d^0)$. Thus$I^0/M$ embeds into$I^1$ as a submodule, and exactness at$I^1$ means$I^1/(I^0/M)$ embeds into$I^2$, and so on. The module$M$ is successively "approximated from outside" by the injective modules in the resolution, with the cokernel of each step being the input to the next.

Injective resolutions are used to compute right derived functors, particularly $\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(N, M)$ computed from the right by resolving$M$ injectively. When the left-exact functor$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ is applied to the injective resolution$I^\bullet$, the resulting cochain complex$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, I^0) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, I^1) \to \cdots$ is not necessarily exact (since$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ does not preserve all cokernels), and its cohomology defines$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(N, M)$. This is the "right-hand" computation of$\mathrm{Ext}$, dual to the "left-hand" computation using a projective resolution of$N$; both give the same answer, which is the content of the balancing theorem for$\mathrm{Ext}$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Over $R = \mathbb{Z}$, the injective modules are the *divisible* abelian groups:$\mathbb{Q}$,$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$,$\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]/\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$, and their direct sums. For$M = \mathbb{Z}$, the injective hull is$I^0 = \mathbb{Q}$, with$\eta: \mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}$ the standard inclusion. The cokernel$I^0/M = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is itself injective (as a direct sum of$p$-Prüfer groups$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$), so the resolution terminates:
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
The injective dimension of $\mathbb{Z}$ is thus$1$, consistent with global dimension$1$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** For $M = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, the injective hull is$I^0 = \mathbb{Z}(n^\infty)$ (the Prüfer group for$n$, or more precisely$\bigoplus_p \mathbb{Z}(p^{v_p(n)\infty})$, but for concreteness take$n = p$ prime so$I^0 = \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) = \varinjlim \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$). The embedding$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ sends$\bar{1} \mapsto 1/p \pmod{\mathbb{Z}}$. The cokernel is$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)/(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ again (since$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ is divisible and hence the cokernel of any subgroup is also a$p$-divisible group). Thus the injective resolution of$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) \to \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) \to 0,$$
a length-1 injective resolution.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Over a field $k$, every$k$-module is a$k$-vector space, and every$k$-vector space is both projective and injective. The injective resolution of any$k$-module$V$ is$0 \to V \to V \to 0$ (length$0$), confirming$\mathrm{id}_k(V) = 0$ for all$V$, and$\mathrm{gl.dim}(k) = 0$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** For a finite group $G$ and$R = k[G]$ with$k$ a field of characteristic$p$ dividing$|G|$, the situation is more complex. The group algebra$k[G]$ is a self-injective ring (it is a Frobenius algebra), meaning$k[G]$ is injective as a module over itself. This implies that projective$k[G]$-modules are the same as injective$k[G]$-modules. The trivial module$k$ with trivial$G$-action has a periodic injective resolution, reflecting the periodicity of group cohomology in this setting.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The dual Comparison Theorem holds for injective resolutions: given injective resolutions $I^\bullet$ and$J^\bullet$ of$M$ and$N$, and a map$f: M \to N$, there exists a chain map$\tilde{f}: I^\bullet \to J^\bullet$ over$f$, unique up to chain homotopy. The proof uses the injectivity of the$J^n$'s rather than the projectivity of the source. This dual theorem guarantees that derived functors computed from injective resolutions are well-defined, by the same argument as in the projective case.
\end{remark}
