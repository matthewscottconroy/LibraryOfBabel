# 39.2.3 Injective Dimension; Global Dimension of a Ring

Having defined both projective and injective dimensions, a natural question is whether they give different measures of the same ring, or whether they are genuinely independent invariants. The answer is that they agree globally: the global dimension of a ring $R$ can be computed equally well from projective or from injective resolutions.

The *injective dimension* $\mathrm{id}(M)$ of an$R$-module$M$ is the smallest$n$ such that$M$ has an injective resolution of length$n$, or$\infty$ if no such finite resolution exists. A module has$\mathrm{id}(M) = 0$ if and only if$M$ is itself injective. Just as$\mathrm{pd}(M) \leq n$ is characterized by the vanishing of$\mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_R(M, -)$, the injective dimension satisfies$\mathrm{id}(M) \leq n$ if and only if$\mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_R(-, M) = 0$ for all$R$-modules. This dual characterization makes the connection between projective and injective dimension transparent at the level of Ext groups: projective dimension measures the vanishing in the left variable of$\mathrm{Ext}$, while injective dimension measures it in the right variable.

The global dimension of $R$ is the common value$\mathrm{gl.dim}(R) = \sup_M \mathrm{pd}(M) = \sup_M \mathrm{id}(M)$ — the two suprema agree. This equality is not obvious but follows from the balancing of$\mathrm{Ext}$:$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N)$ can be computed using either a projective resolution of$M$ or an injective resolution of$N$, and the vanishing of all such groups for$n$ large enough constrains both dimensions simultaneously. Over a Noetherian ring, the global dimension is often computable from explicit structural theorems: the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula relates projective dimension to depth for finitely generated modules over local Noetherian rings, and Hilbert's Syzygy Theorem gives an exact formula for polynomial rings over fields.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** For $R = \mathbb{Z}$, the global dimension is 1. Every$\mathbb{Z}$-module has projective dimension at most 1 (Section 39.1.4). For injective dimension: given any$\mathbb{Z}$-module$M$, one has the injective resolution$0 \to M \to E(M) \to E(M)/M \to 0$. The quotient$E(M)/M$ is a submodule of a divisible group modulo a subgroup, which is divisible (hence injective), so the resolution has length 1. Thus$\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M) \leq 1$ for all$M$, confirming$\mathrm{gl.dim}(\mathbb{Z}) = 1$ from both sides.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** For $R = k$ a field, both projective and injective dimensions of any module are$0$ (every module is free/projective/injective), so$\mathrm{gl.dim}(k) = 0$. Such rings are called *semisimple*; by the Artin–Wedderburn theorem, they are finite products of matrix algebras over division rings.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** For $R = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, Hilbert's Syzygy Theorem gives$\mathrm{gl.dim}(R) = n$. The residue field$k = R/(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ achieves the maximum: its Koszul resolution has length$n$, and$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(k, k) \cong k \neq 0$ shows this bound is sharp. For injective dimension, the same ring satisfies$\mathrm{id}_R(k) = n$; the injective hull of$k$ as an$R$-module is the module of inverse polynomials (the Matlis dual of$R$), and the injective resolution of$k$ mirrors the projective one.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** For $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2)$ (dual numbers), the global dimension is infinite:$M = \mathbb{Z}$ has$\mathrm{pd}_R(M) = \infty$ as shown in Section 39.1.2. The same module has$\mathrm{id}_R(M) = \infty$ by the same periodic argument. Thus rings of infinite global dimension exist, and they are precisely those for which there exist modules with infinite projective (equivalently, infinite injective) dimension.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Global dimension via Ext]
Let $R$ be a ring. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathrm{gl.dim}(R) \leq n$,
\item $\mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_R(M, N) = 0$ for all$R$-modules$M, N$,
\item $\mathrm{pd}(M) \leq n$ for all$R$-modules$M$,
\item $\mathrm{id}(N) \leq n$ for all$R$-modules$N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Over a Noetherian ring $R$, the global dimension can also be computed from finitely generated modules:$\mathrm{gl.dim}(R) = \sup\{\mathrm{pd}(M) : M \text{ finitely generated}\}$. This is not true for arbitrary rings. For local Noetherian rings, there is a sharper result:$\mathrm{gl.dim}(R) = \mathrm{pd}_R(R/\mathfrak{m})$ where$\mathfrak{m}$ is the maximal ideal — the global dimension is determined by the single most difficult module, the residue field. The Auslander–Buchsbaum–Serre theorem states that a local Noetherian ring$R$ has finite global dimension if and only if it is regular, in which case$\mathrm{gl.dim}(R) = \dim(R)$ (the Krull dimension). This deep theorem connects homological algebra to algebraic geometry through the notion of regularity.
\end{remark}
