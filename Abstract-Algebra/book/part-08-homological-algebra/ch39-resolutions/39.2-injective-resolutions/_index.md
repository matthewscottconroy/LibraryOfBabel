# 39.2 Injective Resolutions

Injective modules are dual to projective modules in the categorical sense: an $R$-module $I$ is injective if every injective homomorphism $A \hookrightarrow B$ and every map $A \to I$ extends to a map $B \to I$. Projective modules split surjections; injective modules extend across injections. Over the integers, the injective $\mathbb{Z}$-modules are exactly the divisible abelian groups — $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]/\mathbb{Z}$, and their direct sums. Over a field, every module is both projective and injective. The structure of injective modules is in general more subtle than that of projective ones, and their existence in sufficient supply is a nontrivial theorem.

An injective resolution of $M$ is an exact sequence $0 \to M \xrightarrow{\eta} I^0 \xrightarrow{d^0} I^1 \xrightarrow{d^1} I^2 \to \cdots$ with each $I^n$ injective. The direction of the resolution has reversed: $M$ maps into $I^0$, which maps to $I^1$, and so on. Existence of injective resolutions depends on the Injective Hull (or Essential Extension) theorem: every module $M$ embeds into a minimal injective module $E(M)$, its injective hull. One takes $I^0 = E(M)$, then $I^1 = E(I^0/M)$, and iterates. The injective hull is unique up to isomorphism, and the injective resolution constructed this way is called the minimal injective resolution; its terms are the canonical injective modules associated to $M$.

The injective dimension $\operatorname{id}_R(M)$ is defined symmetrically to projective dimension: it is the length of the shortest injective resolution. The global dimension of $R$ is $\sup_M \operatorname{pd}_R(M) = \sup_M \operatorname{id}_R(M)$ — a remarkable equality that says the projective and injective measures of complexity for a ring coincide. Serre's theorem characterizes regular local rings as those of finite global dimension, connecting the homological definition to the geometric notion of smoothness. Right derived functors — including $\text{Ext}$ computed from the second argument — are defined using injective resolutions, and the well-definedness argument is dual to the projective case: the Comparison Theorem for injective resolutions guarantees that any two injective resolutions of $M$ are chain homotopy equivalent.
