# 39.3.1 Free Resolutions; Examples over $\mathbb{Z}$ and$F[x]$

Free resolutions are the workhorses of explicit homological computation. While projective resolutions suffice for theoretical purposes, in practice one almost always builds resolutions using free modules, and the specific structure of those free modules — their ranks and the matrices describing the differentials — carries rich information about the module being resolved.

A *free resolution* of $M$ is a projective resolution$\cdots \to P_1 \to P_0 \to M \to 0$ in which every$P_n$ is a free$R$-module. Since free modules are projective, free resolutions are special cases of projective resolutions; but the converse fails in general (there exist projective modules that are not free, for example over rings that are not local). For practical computation, free resolutions are preferred because the differentials can be described by matrices, and the exactness conditions translate into linear-algebraic statements about those matrices: exactness at$P_n$ means the column space of the matrix for$d_n$ equals the null space of the matrix for$d_{n-1}$.

Over $\mathbb{Z}$, the simplest example is the cyclic group$\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. The resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ is a free resolution of length$1$, reflecting the fact that$\mathbb{Z}$ has global dimension$1$. Applying$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-, \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})$ and taking cohomology immediately gives the groups$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)\mathbb{Z}$. Over$F[x]$ for a field$F$, the module$F$ with trivial$x$-action (that is,$x$ acts as$0$) has the analogous resolution$0 \to F[x] \xrightarrow{\times x} F[x] \to F \to 0$. These two examples, though small, are paradigmatic: they illustrate how a short free resolution encodes all$\mathrm{Ext}$ and$\mathrm{Tor}$ groups, and they will appear as building blocks whenever one resolves cyclic modules over principal ideal domains.

For finitely generated abelian groups the story is especially clean, because every finitely generated abelian group $M$ has a free resolution of length$\leq 1$. Writing$M \cong \mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_k\mathbb{Z}$ in Smith normal form, one takes$P_0 = \mathbb{Z}^{r+k}$ surjecting onto$M$ via the obvious quotient, and the kernel$P_1 = \mathbb{Z}^k$ maps in via multiplication by$d_1, \ldots, d_k$ in the appropriate components. The differential$d_1: P_1 \to P_0$ is the diagonal matrix$\mathrm{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_k, 0, \ldots, 0)$ (with$r$ trailing zeros for the free part). This matrix description makes it routine to apply Hom or tensor product and read off all$\mathrm{Ext}$ and$\mathrm{Tor}$ groups.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Free resolution of $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$:
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 6} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
Applying $- \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ gives$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 6} \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. Since multiplication by$6$ on$\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is the same as multiplication by$2$ (as$6 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$), one gets$\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}) = \ker(\times 2: \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}) = \{0, 2\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(6,4)\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Free resolution of $F = k$ over$R = k[x]$:
$$0 \to k[x] \xrightarrow{\times x} k[x] \to k \to 0.$$
Applying $\mathrm{Hom}_{k[x]}(-, k)$ gives$0 \to k \xrightarrow{0} k \to 0$ (since any$k[x]$-linear map$k[x] \to k$ must send$x \cdot f$ to$x \cdot (\text{its value at } 1) = 0$ since$x$ acts as zero on$k$, so the induced map$\mathrm{Hom}(k[x], k) \to \mathrm{Hom}(k[x], k)$ is the zero map). Thus$\mathrm{Ext}^0_{k[x]}(k, k) = k$ and$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{k[x]}(k, k) = k$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Free resolution of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. Noting$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (CRT), the resolution from Example 1 applies. Alternatively, the direct sum resolution:
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z}^2 \xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}} \mathbb{Z}^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
Applying $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-, \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})$ gives$0 \to (\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})^2 \xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}} (\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})^2 \to 0$. The kernel consists of$(a,b)$ with$2a \equiv 0, 3b \equiv 0 \pmod 6$, giving$a \in \{0, 3\}$ and$b \in \{0, 2, 4\}$, so$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The Koszul complex over $k[x, y]$ resolves$k$:
$$0 \to k[x,y] \xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix} y \\ -x \end{pmatrix}} k[x,y]^2 \xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix} x & y \end{pmatrix}} k[x,y] \to k \to 0.$$
This is a free resolution of length 2. Applying $\mathrm{Hom}_{k[x,y]}(-, k)$ gives the complex$0 \to k \to k^2 \to k \to 0$ with all maps zero (since$x$ and$y$ act trivially on$k$), so$\mathrm{Ext}^0 = k$,$\mathrm{Ext}^1 = k^2$,$\mathrm{Ext}^2 = k$. The appearance of$k$ in degree 2 is the signature of$\mathrm{gl.dim}(k[x,y]) = 2$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
In practice, computing a free resolution amounts to finding a presentation matrix (generators and relations) for $M$, then finding generators and relations for the module of relations, and iterating. Over$\mathbb{Z}$ and$F[x]$, this terminates in one step since these are PIDs. Over polynomial rings in more variables, Gröbner basis algorithms (Section 39.3.2) automate this process. The matrices appearing in the resolution carry geometric meaning: over$k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$, the resolution of a module associated to a subvariety$V$ encodes the algebraic geometry of$V$, and the Hilbert function, Betti numbers, and regularity of$V$ are all readable from the free resolution.
\end{remark}
