# 39.3 Free Resolutions and Applications

Free modules are the most concrete projective modules: they have explicit bases, their elements can be written as finite formal sums, and maps out of them are determined by where the basis goes. A free resolution of $M$ is a projective resolution in which every term is free, and over many rings — polynomial rings, group rings, Noetherian domains — one can always arrange this. The advantage of free resolutions over merely projective ones is computational: the matrices of the differential maps can be written down explicitly, and the ranks of the free modules carry information about the minimal number of generators and relations at each stage.

The classical example is the free resolution of a cyclic module. Over $\mathbb{Z}$, the module $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ has the resolution $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to 0$, which is minimal: any shorter resolution would make $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ free, which it is not for $n > 1$. The first syzygy module — the kernel of $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ — is $n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$, which is free, so the resolution terminates. Over polynomial rings, the situation is more complex: the module $k[x,y]/(x,y)$ has a free resolution $0 \to k[x,y] \xrightarrow{(-y, x)} k[x,y]^2 \xrightarrow{(x, y)} k[x,y] \to k \to 0$, the Koszul complex in two variables. This example generalizes to the Koszul complex $K_\bullet(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ on $n$ elements, which is a free resolution of length $n$ of the residue field $k$ over $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$.

Hilbert's syzygy theorem asserts that the global dimension of $k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ is exactly $n$: every finitely generated module has a finite free resolution of length at most $n$, and the bound is sharp. The proof uses the fact that each syzygy module of a finitely generated module is again finitely generated (a consequence of Noetherianness), and a degree argument shows the process must terminate. The depth analogue — the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula $\operatorname{pd}(M) + \operatorname{depth}(M) = \operatorname{depth}(R)$ for finitely generated modules over a regular local ring — connects the length of the minimal free resolution to the ring-theoretic depth of the module, forging a link between homological algebra and the commutative algebraist's notion of how "nicely" a module sits inside the ring.
