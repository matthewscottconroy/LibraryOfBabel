# 40.1.1 Right Derived Functors via Injective Resolutions

The central problem that derived functors solve is this: many naturally occurring functors between abelian categories fail to be exact. A left-exact functor $F: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ preserves the left part of every short exact sequence, but it can fail to preserve exactness on the right. The question is whether this failure is itself functorial — whether we can measure, in a coherent way, by how much$F$ fails to be exact. The right derived functors are the answer: they encode the obstruction to exactness as a sequence of new functors$R^1 F, R^2 F, \ldots$, each capturing a successively deeper layer of the failure.

The construction begins with the observation that left-exact functors behave perfectly on injective objects. If $I$ is injective, then every short exact sequence$0 \to I \to B \to C \to 0$ splits (by definition of injectivity), and$F$ preserves direct sum decompositions, so$F$ is exact on sequences involving injective modules. This suggests the strategy: replace$M$ by a complex of injectives, apply$F$, and measure what cohomology results. Concretely, choose an injective resolution$0 \to M \to I^0 \to I^1 \to I^2 \to \cdots$ — a long exact sequence beginning at$M$ with each$I^n$ injective. Applying$F$ gives a cochain complex
$$F(I^0) \to F(I^1) \to F(I^2) \to \cdots$$
which is no longer necessarily exact, since $F$ need not preserve the exactness among the$I^n$. The$n$th right derived functor is then defined as
$$R^n F(M) = H^n(F(I^\bullet)).$$

The zeroth derived functor recovers $F$ itself: since$F$ is left-exact, the sequence$0 \to F(M) \to F(I^0) \to F(I^1)$ is exact, so$H^0(F(I^\bullet)) = \ker(F(I^0) \to F(I^1)) \cong F(M)$, confirming$R^0 F \cong F$. The higher derived functors$R^1 F, R^2 F, \ldots$ then record the failure of$F$ to be exact in progressively higher degrees. The Comparison Theorem, to be addressed in Section 40.1.3, guarantees that this construction is independent of the choice of injective resolution, so the groups$R^n F(M)$ are genuine invariants of$M$.

The most important example is $F = \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ for a fixed$R$-module$N$. This functor is left-exact, and its right derived functors$R^n \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -) = \mathrm{Ext}^n_R(N, -)$ are the Ext groups. Applying$\mathrm{Hom}_R(N, -)$ to an injective resolution$0 \to M \to I^0 \to I^1 \to \cdots$ gives the cochain complex
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, I^0) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, I^1) \to \cdots$$
whose $n$th cohomology is$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(N, M)$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}$ and$F = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, -)$. Apply this to the injective resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. We get$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}) \to 0$. Since$\mathbb{Q}$ is torsion-free,$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}) = 0$. And$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (maps send$\bar{1}$ to$1/2 + \mathbb{Z}$). Thus$R^1 F(\mathbb{Z}) = H^1 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ and$R^0 F(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$, consistent with$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Let $F = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}, -)$ applied to the injective resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) \to \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) \to 0$ (from Section 39.2.1). Then
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)) \to 0.$$
Since $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$, this gives$R^0 F(\mathbb{Z}/p) = \ker(\mathbb{Z}/p \to \mathbb{Z}/p) = \mathbb{Z}/p$ (consistently with$\mathrm{Hom} = \mathrm{Ext}^0$) and$R^1 F(\mathbb{Z}/p) = \mathrm{coker}(\mathbb{Z}/p \to \mathbb{Z}/p) = \mathbb{Z}/p$, giving$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The functor $F = \Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}$ (local cohomology with support in an ideal$\mathfrak{a} \leq R$) is a left-exact functor from$R$-modules to$R$-modules;$\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M) = \{m \in M : \mathfrak{a}^n m = 0 \text{ for some } n\}$ is the submodule of$\mathfrak{a}$-torsion elements. Its right derived functors$R^i \Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M) = H^i_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)$ are the *local cohomology modules*, which play a central role in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. They can be computed from an injective resolution of$M$ by applying$\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}$ to each term.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Over $R = \mathbb{Z}[G]$ for a group$G$, the functor$F = (-)^G$ (taking$G$-invariants) is left-exact. Its right derived functors are group cohomology:$R^n (-)^G = H^n(G, -)$. Applied to an injective resolution$0 \to M \to I^0 \to I^1 \to \cdots$ of the$G$-module$M$, the formula gives$H^n(G, M) = H^n((I^\bullet)^G)$. This is the "injective resolution" definition of group cohomology, dual to the "bar complex" definition using a projective resolution of the trivial module (Section 40.4.2).
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The language "right derived functor" reflects both the direction of the derivation (taking cohomology of injective resolutions, which go to the right in the cochain sense) and the handedness of the exactness: left-exact functors, resolved from the right (using injective resolutions), yield right derived functors. Dually, right-exact functors, resolved from the left (using projective resolutions), yield left derived functors (Section 40.1.2). The two constructions are precisely dual to each other in the sense of category theory.
\end{remark}
