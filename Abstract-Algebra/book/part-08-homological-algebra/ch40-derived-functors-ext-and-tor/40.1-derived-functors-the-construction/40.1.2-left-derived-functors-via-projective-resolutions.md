# 40.1.2 Left Derived Functors via Projective Resolutions

Where right derived functors address the failure of left-exact functors, left derived functors address the dual situation. A right-exact functor $F: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ preserves short exact sequences on the right — it maps$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ to an exact sequence$F(A) \to F(B) \to F(C) \to 0$ — but may fail to be exact on the left. The tensor product$M \otimes_R -$ is the canonical example: it is right-exact but not left-exact in general, and the failure to preserve injectivity on the left is precisely what Tor will measure.

The construction mirrors the injective resolution construction, but now one works on the other side. Projective objects are the "nice" objects for right-exact functors: $F$ is exact on complexes of projectives by a dual argument to the injective case (every surjection$P \to C$ with$P$ projective splits as an abelian group, and$F$ preserves splittings since it is additive and the splitting is given by maps). Given a module$M$, choose a projective resolution
$$\cdots \to P_2 \to P_1 \to P_0 \to M \to 0$$
— a long exact sequence ending at $M$ with each$P_n$ projective. Applying$F$ gives a chain complex$F(P_0) \leftarrow F(P_1) \leftarrow F(P_2) \leftarrow \cdots$ (no longer exact in general). The$n$th left derived functor is defined as
$$L_n F(M) = H_n(F(P_\bullet)).$$

The zeroth derived functor again recovers $F$: since$F$ is right-exact, the sequence$F(P_1) \to F(P_0) \to F(M) \to 0$ is exact, so$H_0(F(P_\bullet)) \cong F(M)$, giving$L_0 F \cong F$. The higher groups$L_1 F, L_2 F, \ldots$ measure the failure of$F$ to be left-exact — in each degree, one sees the "torsion" that$F$ introduces when the input complex fails to be exact on the left. As in the right derived case, the Comparison Theorem ensures independence of the chosen projective resolution.

The most important example is $F = M \otimes_R -$ for a fixed$R$-module$M$. This functor is right-exact, and its left derived functors$L_n(M \otimes_R -) = \mathrm{Tor}_n^R(M, -)$ are the Tor groups. Given a projective resolution$\cdots \to P_1 \to P_0 \to N \to 0$ of$N$, the complex
$$\cdots \to M \otimes_R P_1 \to M \otimes_R P_0 \to 0$$
(with $N$ deleted) has$n$th homology$\mathrm{Tor}_n^R(M, N)$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Let $F = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} -$ and apply to$N = \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. Use the projective resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 3} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. Applying$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes -$ gives the complex
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 3} \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
Multiplication by $3$ on$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is the same as multiplication by$1$ (since$3 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$), which is an isomorphism. Thus$L_0 = \mathrm{coker} = 0$ (consistently,$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(2,3)\mathbb{Z} = 0$) and$L_1 = \ker = 0$. All Tor groups vanish since$\gcd(2,3) = 1$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Let $F = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} -$ applied to$N = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Use$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. Tensoring with$\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ gives$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. Then$L_0 = \mathrm{coker}(\times 2: \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}/(2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (consistently,$\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$) and$L_1 = \ker(\times 2: \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}) = \{0, 2\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** For the global sections functor $F = H^0(X, -)$ on an algebraic variety$X$, the left derived functors are the higher cohomology groups$H^i(X, -)$ in the sense of sheaf cohomology — but this is actually a right derived functor situation (global sections is left-exact). To get a genuine left-derived example: for$F = -_L \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{F}$ (derived tensor product), the left derived functors are the Tor sheaves$\mathcal{T}\! or_i(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})$ relevant in intersection theory.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Suppose $R = k[G]$ for a group$G$ and$F = - \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]} \mathbb{Z}$ (the co-invariants functor, sending a$G$-module$M$ to$M_G = M/\langle gm - m\rangle$). This is right-exact, and its left derived functors are group homology:$L_n(- \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]} \mathbb{Z}) = H_n(G, -)$. Using the bar resolution$B_\bullet \to \mathbb{Z}$ (from Section 40.4.4) as a projective$\mathbb{Z}[G]$-resolution of$\mathbb{Z}$, one computes$H_n(G, M) = H_n(B_\bullet \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]} M)$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The symmetry between left and right derived functors is not a coincidence: it reflects a genuine duality in homological algebra. In an abelian category with enough injectives and enough projectives, the right derived functors of $F$ and the left derived functors of$F^{\mathrm{op}}$ (the opposite functor in the opposite category) are dual constructions. The category of$R$-modules has both enough projectives (all modules are quotients of free modules) and enough injectives (all modules embed into injective modules), which is why both constructions are available and yield the same Ext and Tor groups from both sides.
\end{remark}
