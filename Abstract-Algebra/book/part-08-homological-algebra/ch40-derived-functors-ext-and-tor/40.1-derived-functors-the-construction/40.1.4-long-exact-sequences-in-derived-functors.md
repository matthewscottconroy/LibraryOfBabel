# 40.1.4 Long Exact Sequences in Derived Functors

Perhaps the most important formal property of derived functors is that they convert short exact sequences into long exact sequences. Given a short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ in$\mathcal{A}$ and a left-exact functor$F$, applying$F$ yields an exact sequence$0 \to F(A) \to F(B) \to F(C)$ that may fail to be exact on the right. The right derived functors repair this failure by extending the sequence:
$$0 \to F(A) \to F(B) \to F(C) \to R^1 F(A) \to R^1 F(B) \to R^1 F(C) \to R^2 F(A) \to \cdots$$
The "broken" short exact sequence is thus completed into an infinite exact sequence by the derived functors. A dual statement holds for left derived functors of right-exact functors: a short exact sequence gives a long exact sequence extending to the left.

The construction of this long exact sequence is itself a substantial argument, relying on the Snake Lemma and the Horseshoe Lemma. The *Horseshoe Lemma* provides a way to assemble a projective (or injective) resolution of $B$ from resolutions of$A$ and$C$: given projective resolutions$P_\bullet \to A$ and$Q_\bullet \to C$, one builds a resolution$R_\bullet = P_\bullet \oplus Q_\bullet \to B$ by using the given maps and the projectivity of$Q_n$ to lift the map$B \to C$ at each level. The differentials on$R_n = P_n \oplus Q_n$ have an upper triangular form:$d^R = \begin{pmatrix} d^P & h_n \\ 0 & d^Q \end{pmatrix}$ where$h_n: Q_n \to P_{n-1}$ is a "mixing" map supplied by the Horseshoe construction. Applying$F$ to the resulting short exact sequence of complexes$0 \to F(P_\bullet) \to F(R_\bullet) \to F(Q_\bullet) \to 0$ and invoking the long exact sequence of homology (the algebraic analogue of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence) yields the desired long exact sequence of derived functors.

This result is the central computational tool in homological algebra. Every long exact sequence that arises in algebra, topology, and arithmetic — the long exact sequence in group cohomology, in sheaf cohomology, in $K$-theory — is an instance of this construction. Knowing the derived functor groups of two terms in a short exact sequence constrains, and often determines, the third; induction on short exact sequences is therefore the primary method for computing derived functors.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Apply $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, -)$ to the short exact sequence$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. The long exact sequence gives:
\begin{align*}
0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}) \\
\to \mathrm{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}) \to 0.
\end{align*}
Since $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ and$\mathrm{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$, this simplifies to:
$$0 \to 0 \to \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n) \to \mathbb{Z}/n \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathrm{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}/m) \to 0.$$
The map $\mathbb{Z}/n \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z}/n$ has image$m\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and cokernel$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$, so$\mathrm{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}/m) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ — consistent with the direct computation.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Apply $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} -$ to$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. The long exact Tor sequence gives:
$$\cdots \to \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0.$$
Since $\mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ and multiplication by$2$ on$\mathbb{Z}/2$ is zero, the sequence gives$\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \ker(\times 2: \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$, recovering the direct computation.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** For group cohomology, let $G = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ and consider$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times p} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ as$G$-modules with trivial action. The long exact sequence in$H^*(G, -)$ gives:
$$H^n(G, \mathbb{Z}) \xrightarrow{\times p} H^n(G, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^n(G, \mathbb{Z}/p) \to H^{n+1}(G, \mathbb{Z}) \xrightarrow{\times p} \cdots$$
Since $H^n(G, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$ for$n$ even and$n > 0$, and$0$ for$n$ odd, this gives$H^n(G, \mathbb{Z}/p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$ for all$n \geq 0$, the well-known cohomology of a cyclic group.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The Horseshoe Lemma in action: given $0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ (a non-split extension), the Horseshoe Lemma assembles a projective resolution of$\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ from the standard resolutions of the two$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ factors:
\begin{align*}
&P_\bullet(A): \quad \cdots \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0,\\
&P_\bullet(C): \quad \cdots \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0.
\end{align*}
The Horseshoe gives $P_\bullet(B) = P_\bullet(A) \oplus P_\bullet(C)$ with differential modified by mixing maps; this yields the known resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 4} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ (after a change of basis in each degree).
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The long exact sequence in derived functors is the homological avatar of a much more general principle: triangulated categories. In the derived category $D(\mathcal{A})$, a short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ gives rise to a *distinguished triangle*$A \to B \to C \to A[1]$ (where$[1]$ denotes the shift functor). Applying any exact functor to a distinguished triangle yields another distinguished triangle, and in the derived category this expresses the long exact sequence. The language of triangulated categories unifies the long exact sequences of derived functors, Mayer–Vietoris sequences in topology, and the localization sequences in$K$-theory into a single framework.
\end{remark}
