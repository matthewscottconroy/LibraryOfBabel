# 40.1 Derived Functors: The Construction

The failure of an exact functor to preserve exactness is, from a categorical perspective, a source of information rather than a defect. When the functor $F$ is left exact — preserving only the left portion of exact sequences — there are higher obstructions lurking in the cokernel at each degree, and derived functors are the tool that systematically extracts them. The construction is conceptually simple: replace the input module by a resolution consisting of modules on which $F$ is exact (injective modules for right derived functors, projective modules for left derived functors), apply $F$ term by term, and read off the homology. The resolution acts as a "scaffolding" that forces the complex to be amenable to $F$, and the resulting homology groups measure the deviation from exactness.

For right derived functors, the setup is as follows. Let $F: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ be a left-exact functor between abelian categories where $\mathcal{A}$ has enough injectives. For an object $M$, choose an injective resolution $0 \to M \to I^0 \to I^1 \to I^2 \to \cdots$, apply $F$ to the truncated complex $0 \to F(I^0) \to F(I^1) \to F(I^2) \to \cdots$, and define $R^n F(M) = H^n(F(I^\bullet))$. The zeroth derived functor $R^0 F$ recovers $F$ itself, since left exactness ensures $F(I^0) \to F(I^1)$ has kernel $F(M)$. The higher derived functors $R^n F$ for $n \geq 1$ measure the failure of $F$ to be exact at the $n$th level. The construction is independent of the choice of injective resolution by the Comparison Theorem for injective resolutions.

For left derived functors of a right-exact functor $F$, projective resolutions replace injective ones. Given $\cdots \to P_1 \to P_0 \to M \to 0$, apply $F$ to the truncated complex and set $L_n F(M) = H_n(F(P_\bullet))$. The most important examples are $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M, N) = L_n(M \otimes_R -)(N)$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(M, N) = R^n \operatorname{Hom}_R(M, -)(N)$. A short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ yields long exact sequences connecting the derived functors: $\cdots \to L_n F(A) \to L_n F(B) \to L_n F(C) \to L_{n-1} F(A) \to \cdots$ (for left derived functors) and $\cdots \to R^n F(A) \to R^n F(B) \to R^n F(C) \to R^{n+1} F(A) \to \cdots$ (for right derived functors). These long exact sequences are the primary computational tool.
