# 40.2.1 $\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M,N)$: Derived Functor of$\mathrm{Hom}_R(M,-)$

The functor $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M, -)$ is left-exact: given a short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$, one obtains$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, A) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, B) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, C)$, but the last map need not be surjective. The Ext groups are the right derived functors that fill in this gap. The definition is$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N) = R^n \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, -)(N)$: choose an injective resolution$0 \to N \to I^0 \to I^1 \to \cdots$, apply$\mathrm{Hom}_R(M, -)$, and take the$n$th cohomology of the resulting complex. The zeroth group$\mathrm{Ext}^0_R(M, N) = \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, N)$ recovers the original functor.

A striking feature of Ext is that it can also be computed from the other variable. One has $\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N) \cong R^n \mathrm{Hom}_R(-, N)(M)$ — that is, Ext is also the right derived functor of the contravariant functor$\mathrm{Hom}_R(-, N)$, computed using a projective resolution of$M$. The two methods give isomorphic groups, which is a non-trivial theorem typically proved using a double complex argument: build a bigraded complex$\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_p, I^q)$ from a projective resolution$P_\bullet \to M$ and an injective resolution$I^\bullet \to N$, and show that both spectral sequences associated to the double complex degenerate to give$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N)$. This "balancing" theorem is what makes Ext a genuine bifunctor in both variables.

The higher Ext groups $\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N)$ for$n \geq 1$ measure the "derived failure" of Hom to be exact. More precisely, a short exact sequence$0 \to N' \to N \to N'' \to 0$ gives a long exact sequence
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, N') \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, N) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, N'') \to \mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, N') \to \mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, N) \to \mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, N'') \to \cdots$$
so $\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, N'')$ is exactly the obstruction to lifting maps$M \to N''$ through the surjection$N \to N''$. There is also a long exact sequence in the first variable from a short exact sequence$0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0$:
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M'', N) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, N) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(M', N) \to \mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M'', N) \to \cdots$$

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, N)$ for any$N$. Since$\mathbb{Z}$ is projective (free), the projective resolution is$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0$ (concentrated in degree 0). Applying$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, -)$ gives$0 \to N \to 0$, so$\mathrm{Ext}^0_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, N) = N$ and$\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, N) = 0$ for$n \geq 1$. This is the statement that$\mathbb{Z}$ has projective dimension 0 — i.e., is projective.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z})$. Use the injective resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. Apply$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, -)$:
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Q}) \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}) \to 0.$$
Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is torsion-free,$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Q}) = 0$. And$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/m$ (a map$\mathbb{Z}/m \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is determined by the image of$\bar{1}$, which must satisfy$m \cdot \text{image} = 0$; the solutions are$0, 1/m, 2/m, \ldots, (m-1)/m$ in$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$). So$\mathrm{Ext}^0 = 0$ (consistently,$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$) and$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/m$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** $\mathrm{Ext}^n_{k[x]}(k, k)$ where$k = k[x]/(x)$. Use the projective resolution$0 \to k[x] \xrightarrow{\times x} k[x] \to k \to 0$. Apply$\mathrm{Hom}_{k[x]}(-, k)$:
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(k[x], k) \xrightarrow{(\times x)^*} \mathrm{Hom}(k[x], k) \to 0.$$
Since $\mathrm{Hom}_{k[x]}(k[x], k) \cong k$ (evaluation at 1), and the map$(\times x)^*$ sends$f \mapsto f \circ (\times x)$, which maps$\phi \in \mathrm{Hom}(k[x], k)$ to$\phi(\cdot x)$. Since$x$ acts as zero on$k$, we have$\phi(fx) = f(0) \cdot \phi(x) = 0$ (as$\phi$ is a$k[x]$-module map,$\phi(fx) = f \cdot \phi(x)$, and$x \cdot \phi(x) = 0$ in$k$). Thus$(\times x)^*$ is the zero map, and$\mathrm{Ext}^0 = k$,$\mathrm{Ext}^1 = k$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The bifunctor structure: $\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ can be computed from either variable. From the right: use the injective resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}(n^\infty) \to \mathbb{Z}(n^\infty) \to 0$ and apply$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, -)$. The map$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}(n^\infty)) \xrightarrow{d^0} \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}(n^\infty))$ has kernel and cokernel both isomorphic to$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$. From the left: use$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m \to 0$ and apply$\mathrm{Hom}(-, \mathbb{Z}/n)$; one computes$\mathrm{coker}(\times m: \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}/n) = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$. Both give$\mathrm{Ext}^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The notation $\mathrm{Ext}^n$ comes from "extension":$\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(C, A)$ classifies extensions$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ (Section 40.2.3), while$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(C, A)$ for$n \geq 2$ classifies "$n$-fold extensions" — exact sequences$0 \to A \to E_n \to \cdots \to E_1 \to C \to 0$ of length$n$, up to an equivalence relation known as Yoneda equivalence. The Yoneda product gives$\mathrm{Ext}^p_R(M, N) \times \mathrm{Ext}^q_R(N, L) \to \mathrm{Ext}^{p+q}_R(M, L)$ a ring-like composition, making the graded group$\bigoplus_n \mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, M)$ into a ring — the *Yoneda Ext ring* — which encodes deep structure of the module category.
\end{remark}
