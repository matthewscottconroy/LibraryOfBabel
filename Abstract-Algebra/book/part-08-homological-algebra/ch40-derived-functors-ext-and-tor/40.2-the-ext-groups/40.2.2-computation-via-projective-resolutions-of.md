# 40.2.2 Computation via Projective Resolutions of $M$

While the definition of $\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N)$ uses an injective resolution of$N$, the most common method in practice is to work with a projective resolution of$M$ instead. The two approaches yield the same groups, but projective resolutions are typically more explicit and easier to write down — for modules over$\mathbb{Z}$ or over polynomial rings, free resolutions are standard and well-understood, while injective resolutions of finitely generated modules can be large and unwieldy.

Given a projective resolution
$$\cdots \to P_2 \xrightarrow{d_2} P_1 \xrightarrow{d_1} P_0 \xrightarrow{\varepsilon} M \to 0,$$
one applies the contravariant functor $\mathrm{Hom}_R(-, N)$ to the deleted complex$P_\bullet$ (the resolution with$M$ removed). This yields a cochain complex
$$\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_0, N) \xrightarrow{d_1^*} \mathrm{Hom}_R(P_1, N) \xrightarrow{d_2^*} \mathrm{Hom}_R(P_2, N) \to \cdots$$
where $d_n^* = \mathrm{Hom}(d_n, N)$ is precomposition with$d_n$: for$f \in \mathrm{Hom}(P_{n-1}, N)$, one has$(d_n^* f)(x) = f(d_n(x))$ for$x \in P_n$. The Ext groups are then the cohomology groups of this complex:
$$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N) = H^n(\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_\bullet, N)) = \ker(d_{n+1}^*) / \mathrm{im}(d_n^*).$$

The key insight is that this complex is not exact precisely because $\mathrm{Hom}_R(-, N)$ is not left-exact: if it were, the complex$\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_\bullet, N)$ would be exact (since$P_\bullet$ is exact), and all Ext groups in positive degrees would vanish. The cohomology of$\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_\bullet, N)$ is therefore a concrete measure of how far$\mathrm{Hom}_R(-, N)$ departs from exactness at each level. In practice, computing Ext amounts to writing down an explicit projective resolution and performing linear algebra (or arithmetic) on the resulting Hom groups.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/4)$. The projective resolution of$\mathbb{Z}/6$ is$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 6} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/6 \to 0$. Deleting$\mathbb{Z}/6$ and applying$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-, \mathbb{Z}/4)$:
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/4) \xrightarrow{(\times 6)^*} \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/4) \to 0.$$
Each Hom group is $\mathbb{Z}/4$, and$(\times 6)^*(f)(n) = f(6n)$. Since$f$ is determined by$f(1)$, and$f(6n) = 6f(1)n$, the map$(\times 6)^*$ corresponds to multiplication by 6 on$\mathbb{Z}/4$, i.e., multiplication by 2 (since$6 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$). Thus$\mathrm{Ext}^0 = \ker(\times 2: \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/4) = \{0, 2\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ (consistent with$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/4) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(6,4) = \mathbb{Z}/2$) and$\mathrm{Ext}^1 = \mathrm{coker}(\times 2) = \mathbb{Z}/4/(2\mathbb{Z}/4) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(6,4)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{k[x]}(k[x]/(x^2), k)$ where$k$ is a field. The projective resolution of$k[x]/(x^2)$ is$0 \to k[x] \xrightarrow{\times x^2} k[x] \to k[x]/(x^2) \to 0$. Applying$\mathrm{Hom}_{k[x]}(-, k)$ (where$k = k[x]/(x)$ with$x$ acting as 0):
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(k[x], k) \xrightarrow{(\times x^2)^*} \mathrm{Hom}(k[x], k) \to 0.$$
Since $\mathrm{Hom}_{k[x]}(k[x], k) \cong k$ and$(\times x^2)^*$ corresponds to multiplication by$x^2 = 0$ in$k$, the map is zero. Thus$\mathrm{Ext}^0 = k$ (consistent with$\mathrm{Hom}(k[x]/(x^2), k) \cong k$: maps must send$1$ to an element killed by$x^2$, i.e., any element of$k$) and$\mathrm{Ext}^1 = k$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ for a prime$p$. Since the projective resolution of$\mathbb{Z}/p \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p$ is$0 \to \mathbb{Z}^2 \xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\0&p\end{pmatrix}} \mathbb{Z}^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p \to 0$, applying$\mathrm{Hom}(-, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ gives$0 \to (\mathbb{Z}/p)^2 \xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\0&p\end{pmatrix}} (\mathbb{Z}/p)^2 \to 0$ where the matrix acts as zero. Thus$\mathrm{Ext}^0 = (\mathbb{Z}/p)^2$ and$\mathrm{Ext}^1 = (\mathbb{Z}/p)^2$. Consistent with$\mathrm{Ext}$ being additive in both variables.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** To illustrate the contravariance: $\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/2)$. The projective resolution of$\mathbb{Z}/4$ is$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times 4} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to 0$. Apply$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-, \mathbb{Z}/2)$: we get$\mathbb{Z}/2 \xrightarrow{(\times 4)^*} \mathbb{Z}/2$. Since$(\times 4)^*$ corresponds to$\times 4 = \times 0$ on$\mathbb{Z}/2$, the map is zero. So$\mathrm{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathrm{coker}(0) = \mathbb{Z}/2$. This says there is one non-trivial extension$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to ? \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to 0$ up to isomorphism — but that extension would have to be$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/8 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to 0$. Indeed$\mathbb{Z}/8$ is the non-split extension, and$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(4,2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ confirms there is exactly one such isomorphism class.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
An important point about the cochain complex $\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_\bullet, N)$: the maps go in the *opposite* direction from$P_\bullet$, because$\mathrm{Hom}(-, N)$ is contravariant. An element of$\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_n, N)$ is an "$n$-cochain," and the coboundary$d_{n+1}^*: \mathrm{Hom}(P_n, N) \to \mathrm{Hom}(P_{n+1}, N)$ is precomposition with$d_{n+1}$. The cohomological nature of Ext (upper indices, increasing differentials) is thus a direct consequence of applying a contravariant functor to a chain complex: contravariance reverses arrows, turning chain complexes into cochain complexes.
\end{remark}
