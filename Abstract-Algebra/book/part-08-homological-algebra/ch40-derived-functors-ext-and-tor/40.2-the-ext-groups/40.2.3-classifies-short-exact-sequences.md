# 40.2.3 $\mathrm{Ext}^0 = \mathrm{Hom}$;$\mathrm{Ext}^1$ Classifies Short Exact Sequences

The fact that $\mathrm{Ext}^0_R(M, N) = \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, N)$ is immediate from the definition:$H^0$ of the complex$\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_\bullet, N)$ is the kernel of the first coboundary map$d_1^*: \mathrm{Hom}_R(P_0, N) \to \mathrm{Hom}_R(P_1, N)$, and using the exactness of the resolution one verifies this is precisely$\mathrm{Hom}_R(M, N)$ (a map$P_0 \to N$ lies in the kernel of$d_1^*$ iff it kills$\mathrm{im}(d_1) = \ker(\varepsilon)$, hence factors through$P_0/\ker(\varepsilon) \cong M$). The surprise and the deeper result is the interpretation of$\mathrm{Ext}^1$.

An *extension* of $C$ by$A$ is a short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$. Two such extensions are *equivalent* if there is a commutative diagram with identity maps on$A$ and$C$:
$$\begin{array}{ccccccc}
0 \to & A & \to & B & \to & C & \to 0 \\
& \| & & \downarrow{\cong} & & \| & \\
0 \to & A & \to & B' & \to & C & \to 0
\end{array}$$
The split exact sequence $0 \to A \to A \oplus C \to C \to 0$ is the "trivial" extension. The theorem — due essentially to Baer — states that equivalence classes of extensions of$C$ by$A$ are in natural bijection with elements of$\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(C, A)$, with the trivial (split) extension corresponding to the zero element. This bijection respects an abelian group structure on both sides, a point developed in Section 40.2.4.

To see why this correspondence holds, start with a projective resolution $0 \to K \xrightarrow{i} P \xrightarrow{\varepsilon} C \to 0$ (using a single projective module$P$ surjecting onto$C$ with kernel$K$). An element of$\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(C, A) = H^1(\mathrm{Hom}_R(P_\bullet, A)) = \mathrm{Hom}_R(K, A) / \{f \circ i : f \in \mathrm{Hom}_R(P, A)\}$ is a homomorphism$\phi: K \to A$ that does not extend to$P \to A$, modulo those that do. Given such$\phi: K \to A$, one constructs an extension by forming the *pushout* of$K \xrightarrow{i} P$ along$\phi$: the resulting module is$B = (P \oplus A) / \{(i(k), -\phi(k)) : k \in K\}$, and the maps$A \to B$ (sending$a \mapsto (0, a)$) and$B \to C$ (sending$(p, a) \mapsto \varepsilon(p)$) fit into an exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Extensions of $C = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ by$A = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. We have$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, so there are two equivalence classes of extensions. The trivial one is$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$ (Klein four group). The non-trivial one is$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$. The non-trivial extension corresponds to the generator of$\mathrm{Ext}^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$; the map$\phi: K = 2\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ sending$2 \mapsto \bar{1}$ does not extend to$\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ (since if it did,$1 \mapsto x$ and$2 \cdot x = \bar{1}$ in$\mathbb{Z}/2$ would force$0 = \bar{1}$, a contradiction), and the pushout construction gives$\mathbb{Z}/4$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Extensions of $C = \mathbb{Z}/p$ by$A = \mathbb{Z}/p$ for a prime$p$. We have$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$, so there are$p$ equivalence classes. The trivial class is$\mathbb{Z}/p \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p$. The non-trivial classes correspond to maps$\phi: p\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ sending$p \mapsto \bar{k}$ for$k = 1, 2, \ldots, p-1$. For$k = 1$, the pushout construction gives$\mathbb{Z}/p^2$. For general$k$, it gives$\mathbb{Z}/p^2$ again (all$p-1$ non-trivial extensions are isomorphic as groups, just with different embeddings$\mathbb{Z}/p \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/p^2$).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Extensions over a non-commutative ring: let $R = k[G]$ for a group$G$ and consider extensions of modules. An element of$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{k[G]}(k, k)$ classifies short exact sequences$0 \to k \to E \to k \to 0$ of$G$-modules. If$G = \mathbb{Z}/2 = \{1, g\}$ acts trivially on$k$, then any extension is determined by a crossed homomorphism$G \to k$, and$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{k[G]}(k, k) = H^1(G, k) = \mathrm{Hom}(G, k)$ (since$G$ acts trivially). For$k = \mathbb{F}_2$,$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{F}_2) \cong \mathbb{F}_2$, giving one non-trivial extension (corresponding to the non-trivial group homomorphism$g \mapsto 1$).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Vanishing of $\mathrm{Ext}^1$ and splitting:$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Q}, A) = 0$ for any$A$, since$\mathbb{Q}$ is a projective (in fact flat)$\mathbb{Z}$-module — wait, actually$\mathbb{Q}$ is flat but not projective over$\mathbb{Z}$. However,$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Q}, A) = 0$ for all finitely generated$A$ by a direct computation: any extension$0 \to A \to B \to \mathbb{Q} \to 0$ splits when$A$ is finitely generated, since$\mathbb{Q}$ is divisible and the sequence splits by a divisibility argument. Thus every short exact sequence$0 \to A \to B \to \mathbb{Q} \to 0$ with$A$ finitely generated is split.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The Baer classification theorem extends to higher Ext: $\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(C, A)$ classifies *Yoneda$n$-extensions* — exact sequences$0 \to A \to E_n \to E_{n-1} \to \cdots \to E_1 \to C \to 0$ — up to Yoneda equivalence (a relation generated by morphisms of exact sequences that are the identity on$A$ and$C$). The Yoneda composition$\mathrm{Ext}^m(B, C) \times \mathrm{Ext}^n(A, B) \to \mathrm{Ext}^{m+n}(A, C)$ is defined by splicing together a$m$-extension of$B$ by$C$ and an$n$-extension of$A$ by$B$. This makes$\bigoplus_n \mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, M)$ into a graded ring, the Yoneda Ext algebra, which plays an important role in representation theory.
\end{remark}
