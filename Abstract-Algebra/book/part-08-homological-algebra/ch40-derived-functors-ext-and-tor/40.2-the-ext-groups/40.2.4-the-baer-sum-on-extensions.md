# 40.2.4 The Baer Sum on Extensions

The identification of $\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(C, A)$ with extension classes is not merely a bijection of sets — it is an isomorphism of abelian groups, and the group structure on extension classes is given by the Baer sum. Understanding this sum requires a concrete construction that shows the group operation is intrinsic to the geometry of extensions, not just an artifact of the identification with a cohomology group.

Given two extensions $\xi: 0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ and$\xi': 0 \to A \to B' \to C \to 0$, the Baer sum$\xi + \xi'$ is constructed in two steps. First, form the *fiber product* (pullback) of$B \oplus B' \to C \oplus C$ over the diagonal map$\Delta: C \to C \oplus C$ sending$c \mapsto (c, c)$; this gives a module
$$E = \{(b, b') \in B \oplus B' : \bar{b} = \bar{b}' \text{ in } C\}$$
which maps surjectively onto $C$ via$(b, b') \mapsto \bar{b} = \bar{b}'$, with kernel$A \oplus A$ (the pairs$(a, 0)$ and$(0, a')$ for$a, a' \in A$). Second, push out along the addition map$\nabla: A \oplus A \to A$ sending$(a, a') \mapsto a + a'$; this quotients$E$ by the submodule$\{(a, -a) : a \in A\}$ (the "anti-diagonal" copy of$A$), yielding
$$B'' = E / \{(a, -a) : a \in A\}.$$
The composition $A \to E \to B''$ (sending$a \mapsto (a, 0) \mapsto [(a, 0)]$) and$B'' \to C$ make$0 \to A \to B'' \to C \to 0$ the Baer sum.

The Baer sum is the group operation on the set of extension classes making $\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(C, A)$ into an abelian group. The zero element is the class of the split extension$0 \to A \to A \oplus C \to C \to 0$. The inverse of$[\xi]$ is the extension obtained by negating the copy of$A$ in$\xi$: replace the inclusion$A \hookrightarrow B$ by$a \mapsto -a$ (or equivalently, replace the extension class$[\phi] \in \mathrm{Hom}(K, A)/\mathrm{im}$ by$[-\phi]$). The commutativity and associativity of the Baer sum follow from the functorial properties of pullbacks and pushouts, and ultimately from the commutativity and associativity of addition in$A$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Baer sum of two copies of $0 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to \mathbb{Z}/p^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to 0$. Each copy represents the generator$[\xi]$ of$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$. The Baer sum should represent$2[\xi]$. Explicitly:$E = \{(a, b) \in \mathbb{Z}/p^2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p^2 : a \equiv b \pmod{p}\}$. Elements of$E$ are pairs$(a, b)$ with$a - b \in p\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}$. The quotient by the anti-diagonal$\{(k, -k) : k \in p\mathbb{Z}/p^2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/p\}$ gives$B'' = E/\{(k, -k)\}$. One can verify that if$p \geq 3$, then$B'' \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^2$ (the non-trivial extension), consistently with$2[\xi] \neq 0$ in$\mathbb{Z}/p$. If$p = 2$, then$2[\xi] = 0$ in$\mathbb{Z}/2$, and$B'' \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$ (the split extension).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Baer inverse: the inverse of $0 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to \mathbb{Z}/p^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to 0$ is the extension where$\mathbb{Z}/p$ embeds into$\mathbb{Z}/p^2$ via$\bar{1} \mapsto -\overline{p}$ (i.e., the map$k \mapsto -kp$ into$\mathbb{Z}/p^2$). The Baer sum of this extension with the original should give the split extension. Indeed: the construction produces$B''$ such that$B'' \cong \mathbb{Z}/p \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p$, which is the split extension and represents the zero class.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** For $A = \mathbb{Z}$ and$C = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, we have$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$ (as computed in Section 40.2.1). The generator corresponds to the extension$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n \to 0$, where$\mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and the map from$\mathbb{Z}$ into$B = \mathbb{Z}$ is multiplication by$n$. The Baer sum of$k$ copies of this generator gives$k$ times the generator in$\mathbb{Z}/n$; the Baer sum of$n$ copies gives the zero class (the split extension).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** In terms of cocycles: if $\xi$ corresponds to the cocycle$\phi: K \to A$ (where$0 \to K \to P \to C \to 0$ is a projective cover) and$\xi'$ corresponds to$\phi': K \to A$, then their Baer sum corresponds to$\phi + \phi': K \to A$. This makes the group law on extension classes entirely transparent from the cocycle perspective: the Baer sum is simply the pointwise sum of the maps$K \to A$, modulo coboundaries. The Baer sum construction is thus the geometric realization of the group law in the cohomology group$\mathrm{Ext}^1 = H^1(\mathrm{Hom}(P_\bullet, A))$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The Baer sum construction generalizes to a *Yoneda composition* on higher Ext groups. If $\alpha \in \mathrm{Ext}^m_R(B, C)$ and$\beta \in \mathrm{Ext}^n_R(A, B)$, their Yoneda composition$\alpha \circ \beta \in \mathrm{Ext}^{m+n}_R(A, C)$ is defined by splicing the$m$-extension of$B$ by$C$ and the$n$-extension of$A$ by$B$ end-to-end, yielding an$(m+n)$-extension of$A$ by$C$. This composition makes$\bigoplus_n \mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, M)$ into a graded algebra (the Yoneda Ext algebra), which is an important object in representation theory and non-commutative algebra — its Hilbert series encodes the complexity of the module$M$.
\end{remark}
