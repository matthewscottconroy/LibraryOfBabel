# 40.2.5 $\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$: Explicit Computation

A worked example anchors the abstract machinery in concrete arithmetic. To compute $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$, one exploits the fact that$\mathbb{Z}$ is a principal ideal domain of global dimension 1, meaning every$\mathbb{Z}$-module has a projective resolution of length at most 1. The standard free resolution of$\mathbb{Z}/m$ is
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathbb{Z}/m \to 0,$$
where $\pi$ is reduction mod$m$. Applying$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ to the deleted complex$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \to 0$ gives the cochain complex
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/n) \xrightarrow{(\times m)^*} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/n) \to 0,$$
which simplifies via $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$ to the two-term complex$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/n \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z}/n \to 0$.

Computing the cohomology of this complex is now a matter of elementary number theory. The group $\mathrm{Ext}^0_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) = \ker(\times m: \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}/n)$ consists of those$x \in \mathbb{Z}/n$ with$mx \equiv 0 \pmod{n}$, which is the cyclic group of order$\gcd(m, n)$. This is consistent with$\mathrm{Ext}^0 = \mathrm{Hom}$ and the standard fact$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$. The group$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) = \mathrm{coker}(\times m: \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}/n) = \mathbb{Z}/n / (m \cdot \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$. Since the projective resolution has length 1, all higher Ext groups vanish:$\mathrm{Ext}^k_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) = 0$ for$k \geq 2$.

The coincidence $\mathrm{Ext}^0 \cong \mathrm{Ext}^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ is a feature of the integral setting. The result has a direct module-theoretic interpretation: the non-vanishing of$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ confirms that there exist non-split extensions of$\mathbb{Z}/m$ by$\mathbb{Z}/n$, and the group$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ classifies them all. For instance, when$m = n = p$ is prime,$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$, corresponding to the non-trivial extensions$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to \mathbb{Z}/p^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to 0$ (and their scalar multiples).

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/10)$. Here$\gcd(6, 10) = 2$. The complex is$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/10 \xrightarrow{\times 6} \mathbb{Z}/10 \to 0$. Multiplication by 6 on$\mathbb{Z}/10$: since$\gcd(6, 10) = 2$, the image is$\{0, 2, 4, 6, 8\} = 2\mathbb{Z}/10 \cong \mathbb{Z}/5$. The kernel:$6x \equiv 0 \pmod{10}$ iff$3x \equiv 0 \pmod 5$ iff$x \equiv 0 \pmod 5$ (since$\gcd(3,5) = 1$), giving kernel$= \{0, 5\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. Thus$\mathrm{Ext}^0 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ and$\mathrm{Ext}^1 = \mathbb{Z}/10 / 2\mathbb{Z}/10 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, both equal to$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(6,10)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/6)$. Here$\gcd(4,6) = 2$. The complex is$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/6 \xrightarrow{\times 4} \mathbb{Z}/6 \to 0$. Multiplication by 4 on$\mathbb{Z}/6$:$4x \equiv 0 \pmod 6$ iff$2x \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ iff$x \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, so kernel$= \{0, 3\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. Image of$\times 4$:$\{0, 4, 2\} = 2\mathbb{Z}/6 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$. Cokernel:$\mathbb{Z}/6 / 2\mathbb{Z}/6 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. So$\mathrm{Ext}^0 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ and$\mathrm{Ext}^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(4,6)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p^2)$ for a prime$p$. Here$\gcd(p, p^2) = p$. The complex is$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/p^2 \xrightarrow{\times p} \mathbb{Z}/p^2 \to 0$. The kernel of$\times p$ on$\mathbb{Z}/p^2$ is$\{0, p, 2p, \ldots, (p-1)p\} = p\mathbb{Z}/p^2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$. The cokernel:$\mathbb{Z}/p^2 / p\mathbb{Z}/p^2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$. Thus$\mathrm{Ext}^0 \cong \mathbb{Z}/p \cong \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p^2)$ (maps must send$\bar{1}$ to an element of order dividing$p$ in$\mathbb{Z}/p^2$, i.e., a multiple of$p$:$\{0, p, 2p, \ldots\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$) and$\mathrm{Ext}^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** $\mathrm{Ext}^*_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ for coprime$m, n$. When$\gcd(m,n) = 1$, all Ext groups vanish:$\mathrm{Ext}^0 = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) = 0$ (no non-trivial homomorphisms between groups of coprime order) and$\mathrm{Ext}^1 = 0$. This means every short exact sequence$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/n \to B \to \mathbb{Z}/m \to 0$ splits when$\gcd(m,n) = 1$. Indeed, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem,$B \cong \mathbb{Z}/m \oplus \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/mn$ (which is split), since a finite abelian group of order$mn$ with$\gcd(m,n) = 1$ decomposes as a direct product.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
These computations over $\mathbb{Z}$ are the foundational examples of Ext, and they should be committed to memory. The formula$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ has an immediate generalization: for any finitely generated abelian groups$A$ and$B$, one decomposes into cyclic summands and applies the formula for each pair of summands using the additivity$\mathrm{Ext}^1(A \oplus A', B) \cong \mathrm{Ext}^1(A, B) \oplus \mathrm{Ext}^1(A', B)$ and$\mathrm{Ext}^1(A, B \oplus B') \cong \mathrm{Ext}^1(A, B) \oplus \mathrm{Ext}^1(A, B')$.
\end{remark}
