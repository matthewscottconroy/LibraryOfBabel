# 40.2.6 Vanishing of Ext and Projectivity/Injectivity

The deepest structural result about Ext groups is that their vanishing characterizes projectivity and injectivity exactly. The statement is clean and powerful: $\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N) = 0$ for all$N$ and all$n \geq 1$ if and only if$M$ is projective; and$\mathrm{Ext}^n_R(M, N) = 0$ for all$M$ and all$n \geq 1$ if and only if$N$ is injective. These vanishing criteria connect the derived functor directly to the structure theory of the module category.

The forward directions are straightforward. If $M$ is projective, then one can take$M$ itself as a projective resolution concentrated in degree zero:$0 \to M \to M \to 0$ (with the identity map as the augmentation). Applying$\mathrm{Hom}_R(-, N)$ to the deleted complex gives the trivial complex$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}(M, N) \to 0$, with cohomology$\mathrm{Hom}(M, N)$ in degree 0 and zero in all other degrees. If$N$ is injective, then$N$ is its own injective resolution (concentrated in degree zero), so applying$\mathrm{Hom}_R(M, -)$ to the deleted resolution$0 \to N \to 0$ gives zero in all positive degrees.

The reverse directions are more subtle: if $\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, N) = 0$ for all$N$, then every extension$0 \to N \to B \to M \to 0$ splits, which (taking$N$ arbitrary) implies$M$ is projective by one of the standard characterizations of projective modules. Specifically, taking$N = \ker(f)$ for any surjection$f: P \to M$ with$P$ free, the fact that$0 \to \ker f \to P \to M \to 0$ splits means there exists a right inverse to$f$, i.e., a lifting of$\mathrm{id}_M$ through$f$ — exactly the universal lifting property of projective modules.

The practical significance of these criteria is considerable. They give a homological test for structural properties: to check whether $M$ is projective, one can compute$\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, R/I)$ for ideals$I$ and look for vanishing. More broadly, the *projective dimension*$\mathrm{pd}(M)$ is defined as the smallest$n$ such that$\mathrm{Ext}^k_R(M, -) = 0$ for all$k > n$; the global dimension of the ring$R$ is the supremum of projective dimensions over all modules. Rings of global dimension zero are exactly semisimple rings (where every module is projective); rings of global dimension one are hereditary rings (such as$\mathbb{Z}$ and polynomial rings in one variable over a field).

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $\mathbb{Z}$ is projective over$\mathbb{Z}$ (it is free), so$\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, N) = 0$ for all$n \geq 1$ and all$N$. Conversely,$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is not projective, and indeed$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \neq 0$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is injective over$\mathbb{Z}$ (as a divisible group), so$\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for all$n \geq 1$ and all$M$. This can be verified: the injective resolution of$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is$0 \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ (length 0), so applying$\mathrm{Hom}(M, -)$ gives the zero complex in positive degrees.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Hereditary rings. A ring $R$ is *hereditary* if every submodule of a projective module is projective, equivalently$\mathrm{gl.dim}(R) \leq 1$. The ring$\mathbb{Z}$ is hereditary: every subgroup of a free abelian group is free, hence projective. This implies$\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, N) = 0$ for all$n \geq 2$ and all$M, N$ — a strong vanishing theorem that makes homological computations over$\mathbb{Z}$ particularly tractable.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** A ring $R$ is *von Neumann regular* if every module over$R$ is flat (but not necessarily projective). For such rings,$\mathrm{Tor}_n^R(M, N) = 0$ for all$n \geq 1$. However,$\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, N)$ may still be nonzero, showing that the Tor-vanishing characterization (flatness) is weaker than the Ext-vanishing characterization (projectivity). This distinction matters over non-Noetherian rings where flat modules need not be projective.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}
Let $R$ be a ring and$M$ an$R$-module. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is projective if and only if$\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(M, N) = 0$ for all$R$-modules$N$.
\item $M$ is injective if and only if$\mathrm{Ext}^1_R(N, M) = 0$ for all$R$-modules$N$.
\item $\mathrm{pd}(M) \leq n$ if and only if$\mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_R(M, N) = 0$ for all$N$.
\item $\mathrm{id}(M) \leq n$ if and only if$\mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_R(N, M) = 0$ for all$N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The vanishing criterion for flatness takes a different form. A module $N$ is flat if and only if$\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(M, N) = 0$ for all$M$. The interplay between the three conditions — projective, flat, injective — and the corresponding vanishing of Ext and Tor organizes much of the theory of module categories. The following strict implications hold in general: projective$\Rightarrow$ flat$\Rightarrow$ (torsion-free over$\mathbb{Z}$); over Noetherian rings, for finitely generated modules, flat$\Leftrightarrow$ projective (by a theorem of Serre). The homological algebra thus forces us to distinguish these conditions carefully, and Ext and Tor are the natural tools for doing so.
\end{remark}
