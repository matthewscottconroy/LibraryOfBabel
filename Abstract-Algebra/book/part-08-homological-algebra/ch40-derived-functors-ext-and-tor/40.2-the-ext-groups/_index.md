# 40.2 The Ext Groups

The Ext groups $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(M, N)$ are the right derived functors of $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M, -)$, and they encode deep information about the relationship between the modules $M$ and $N$. The functor $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M, -)$ is left exact, meaning it preserves kernels but may fail to preserve cokernels, and the Ext groups measure this failure systematically at each homological degree. There are two ways to compute: resolve $M$ projectively and apply $\operatorname{Hom}(-, N)$, or resolve $N$ injectively and apply $\operatorname{Hom}(M, -)$. That both methods give the same answer is the balance theorem for Ext, and its proof via a double complex argument is itself a prototype for the spectral sequence techniques of Chapter 41.

The vanishing and non-vanishing of Ext groups carries important structural information. $\operatorname{Ext}^0_R(M, N) = \operatorname{Hom}_R(M, N)$ always. $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(M, N) = 0$ for all $N$ and all $n \geq 1$ if and only if $M$ is projective — this is a clean characterization of projectivity in homological terms. More subtly, the projective dimension of $M$ is the largest $n$ for which $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(M, N) \neq 0$ for some $N$. The explicit computation $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ follows by taking the resolution $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m \to 0$ and applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ to the non-augmented part, yielding the sequence $0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/n) \xrightarrow{\times m} \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/n) \to 0$ whose cokernel is $\mathbb{Z}/n / m(\mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$.

Perhaps the most striking interpretation of $\operatorname{Ext}^1$ is the Baer correspondence, which identifies $\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(M, N)$ with the set of isomorphism classes of short exact sequences $0 \to N \to E \to M \to 0$. Two such extensions are considered isomorphic if there is a commutative ladder between them fixing $N$ and $M$. The Baer sum turns this set into a group (with the split extension as the identity), and the resulting group structure matches exactly the group structure on $\operatorname{Ext}^1$. This geometric interpretation, which predates the formal definition of derived functors, reveals Ext as the answer to a natural classification problem. Higher $\operatorname{Ext}^n$ groups classify $n$-fold extensions — Yoneda's long exact sequences — extending the picture to all degrees.
