# 40.3.4 $\mathrm{Tor}_n^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$: Explicit Computation

The computation of $\mathrm{Tor}_n^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ parallels the Ext computation of Section 40.2.5 and reinforces the structural parallel between the two families of derived functors. One again uses the standard free resolution of$\mathbb{Z}/m$:
$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m \to 0.$$
This time, instead of applying Hom, one applies $- \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/n$ to the deleted complex$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \to 0$. Using$\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$, this gives the two-term chain complex$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/n \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z}/n \to 0$.

Computing the homology: $\mathrm{Tor}_0^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) = H_0 = \mathrm{coker}(\times m: \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}/n) = \mathbb{Z}/n / (m \cdot \mathbb{Z}/n)$. This cokernel is$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$, consistent with$\mathrm{Tor}_0 = \otimes$ and the standard computation$\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$. And$\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) = H_1 = \ker(\times m: \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}/n)$, which consists of$x \in \mathbb{Z}/n$ with$mx \equiv 0 \pmod{n}$, giving$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$. Since the projective resolution has length 1, all higher Tor groups vanish.

The result $\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ illustrates the torsion-detecting nature of Tor. When$\gcd(m,n) = 1$, both modules are "coprime" and Tor vanishes — the tensor product is exact and$\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n = 0$. When$\gcd(m,n) > 1$, the common torsion is recorded in$\mathrm{Tor}_1$. Comparing with Section 40.2.5, one notices that$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ — a coincidence that is specific to modules over$\mathbb{Z}$ and reflects a deep duality.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $\mathrm{Tor}_*^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/12, \mathbb{Z}/18)$. Here$\gcd(12, 18) = 6$. The chain complex is$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/18 \xrightarrow{\times 12} \mathbb{Z}/18 \to 0$. Kernel of$\times 12$ on$\mathbb{Z}/18$:$12x \equiv 0 \pmod{18}$ iff$2x \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ iff$x \equiv 0 \pmod 3$. So kernel$= \{0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/6$. Cokernel: the image of$\times 12$ on$\mathbb{Z}/18$ is$12\mathbb{Z}/18 = \{0, 12, 6\} = 6\mathbb{Z}/18 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$, so cokernel$\cong \mathbb{Z}/18/(6\mathbb{Z}/18) \cong \mathbb{Z}/6$. Thus$\mathrm{Tor}_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}/6$ and$\mathrm{Tor}_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/6 = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(12, 18)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $\mathrm{Tor}_*^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p^a, \mathbb{Z}/p^b)$ for a prime$p$ and$a \leq b$. Here$\gcd(p^a, p^b) = p^a$. The chain complex is$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/p^b \xrightarrow{\times p^a} \mathbb{Z}/p^b \to 0$. Kernel:$p^a x \equiv 0 \pmod{p^b}$ iff$x \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{b-a}}$, giving kernel$\cong \mathbb{Z}/p^a$. Cokernel:$\mathbb{Z}/p^b / (p^a \mathbb{Z}/p^b) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^a$ (since$|p^a \mathbb{Z}/p^b| = p^{b-a}$ and$|\mathbb{Z}/p^b| = p^b$, so$|\mathrm{coker}| = p^b/p^{b-a} = p^a$). Both$\mathrm{Tor}_0$ and$\mathrm{Tor}_1$ are$\cong \mathbb{Z}/p^a = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(p^a, p^b)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Tor for direct sums: $\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3)$. By additivity in the second variable:
$$\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3) \cong \mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/2) \oplus \mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/3).$$
Now $\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(6,2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ and$\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}/3) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(6,3) = \mathbb{Z}/3$. So$\mathrm{Tor}_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/6$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** $\mathrm{Tor}_n^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) = 0$ for$n \geq 2$. This follows from the fact that$\mathbb{Z}$ has global dimension 1: every$\mathbb{Z}$-module has a projective resolution of length$\leq 1$, so$\mathrm{Tor}_n^{\mathbb{Z}}(A, B) = 0$ for all abelian groups$A, B$ and$n \geq 2$. Concretely, the resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m \to 0$ has length 1, so the chain complex$M \otimes P_\bullet$ is concentrated in degrees 0 and 1, giving zero homology in degrees$\geq 2$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The universal coefficient theorem in algebraic topology states: for a chain complex $C_\bullet$ of free abelian groups and an abelian group$G$, there is a short exact sequence
$$0 \to H_n(C) \otimes G \to H_n(C; G) \to \mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(H_{n-1}(C), G) \to 0.$$
The formula $\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, G) \cong G[m]$ (the$m$-torsion of$G$) then gives an explicit description of the Tor correction term in the universal coefficient theorem. For instance, if$H_{n-1}(C) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (e.g., from$\mathbb{RP}^n$) and$G = \mathbb{Z}/2$, the correction term is$\mathbb{Z}/2$, which is why$H_n(\mathbb{RP}^n; \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ for all$0 \leq n$.
\par
The parallel between the Tor and Ext computations over $\mathbb{Z}$ is not accidental. It reflects a fundamental duality built into the structure of$\mathbb{Z}$ as a self-dual ring: the Pontryagin duality for finite abelian groups exchanges$\mathbb{Z}/m$ with its Pontryagin dual$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/m$, and under this duality$\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(A, B) \cong \mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(A, B)$ for finite abelian groups$A$ and$B$. This self-duality is special to$\mathbb{Z}$; over a general ring,$\mathrm{Ext}^1$ and$\mathrm{Tor}_1$ measure genuinely different phenomena and need not coincide.
\end{remark}
