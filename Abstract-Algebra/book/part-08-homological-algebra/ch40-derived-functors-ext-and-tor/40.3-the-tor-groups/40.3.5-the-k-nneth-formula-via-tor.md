# 40.3.5 The Künneth Formula via Tor

The Künneth formula is one of the most beautiful applications of Tor, bridging homological algebra and topology. The question it answers is: given two chain complexes $C_\bullet$ and$D_\bullet$ (say, of free abelian groups), what is the homology of their tensor product complex$C_\bullet \otimes D_\bullet$? Naively one might hope$H_n(C \otimes D) \cong \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H_i(C) \otimes H_j(D)$, but this would require the tensor product to commute with homology in a strong sense, which it fails to do in the presence of torsion.

The correct statement involves a correction term built from Tor. When $C_\bullet$ and$D_\bullet$ are complexes of flat (in particular, free) modules, the Künneth formula gives a short exact sequence
$$0 \to \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H_i(C) \otimes H_j(D) \to H_n(C \otimes D) \to \bigoplus_{i+j=n-1} \mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(H_i(C), H_j(D)) \to 0,$$
and this sequence is moreover split (though not naturally). The left term is the "expected" answer; the right term is the Tor correction accounting for torsion in the homology groups. The sequence does not split naturally, so the extension between the two terms may be non-trivial over $\mathbb{Z}$, but in practice one often works over a field or in situations where all relevant Tor groups vanish, making the formula simply$H_n(C \otimes D) \cong \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H_i(C) \otimes H_j(D)$.

In topology, the Künneth formula computes the singular homology of a product space. If $X$ and$Y$ are topological spaces, one sets$C_\bullet = C_\bullet(X)$ and$D_\bullet = C_\bullet(Y)$ (singular chain complexes), and the Eilenberg–Zilber theorem provides a quasi-isomorphism$C_\bullet(X) \otimes C_\bullet(Y) \simeq C_\bullet(X \times Y)$. The Künneth formula then gives
$$0 \to \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H_i(X) \otimes H_j(Y) \to H_n(X \times Y) \to \bigoplus_{i+j=n-1} \mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(H_i(X), H_j(Y)) \to 0.$$

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $H_*(S^1 \times S^1; \mathbb{Z})$. The singular homology of$S^1$ is$H_0 = \mathbb{Z}$,$H_1 = \mathbb{Z}$,$H_n = 0$ for$n \geq 2$. Since all groups are free, all Tor groups vanish. The Künneth formula gives:
\begin{align*}
H_0(S^1 \times S^1) &\cong H_0(S^1) \otimes H_0(S^1) = \mathbb{Z},\\
H_1(S^1 \times S^1) &\cong (H_0 \otimes H_1) \oplus (H_1 \otimes H_0) = \mathbb{Z}^2,\\
H_2(S^1 \times S^1) &\cong H_1 \otimes H_1 = \mathbb{Z},\\
H_n &= 0 \text{ for } n \geq 3.
\end{align*}
This is the correct answer: $S^1 \times S^1 = T^2$ (the torus) has homology$\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}^2, \mathbb{Z}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $H_*(\mathbb{RP}^2 \times S^1; \mathbb{Z})$. Recall$H_0(\mathbb{RP}^2) = \mathbb{Z}$,$H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$,$H_n(\mathbb{RP}^2) = 0$ for$n \geq 2$. The Künneth formula gives:
\begin{align*}
H_0 &\cong \mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z},\\
H_1 &\cong (\mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}) \oplus (\mathbb{Z} \otimes \mathbb{Z}) \oplus \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z},\\
H_2 &\cong (\mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}) \oplus \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus 0 = \mathbb{Z}/2,\\
H_3 &\cong \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) = 0.
\end{align*}
Wait — let me be more careful. The formula is $H_n \supseteq \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H_i(\mathbb{RP}^2) \otimes H_j(S^1)$ with a Tor correction$\bigoplus_{i+j=n-1} \mathrm{Tor}_1(H_i(\mathbb{RP}^2), H_j(S^1))$. Since all homology of$S^1$ is free,$\mathrm{Tor}_1(H_i(\mathbb{RP}^2), H_j(S^1)) = 0$ for all$i, j$ (Tor vanishes when one argument is free). Thus the formula simplifies and$H_n(\mathbb{RP}^2 \times S^1) \cong \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H_i(\mathbb{RP}^2) \otimes H_j(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2, 0, \ldots$
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** A case with non-trivial Tor: $H_*(\mathbb{RP}^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z})$. Since$H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ and$\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$, the Künneth formula gives non-trivial Tor corrections. For$n = 2$:
$$0 \to (H_0 \otimes H_2 + H_1 \otimes H_1 + H_2 \otimes H_0) \to H_2 \to \mathrm{Tor}_1(H_0, H_1) + \mathrm{Tor}_1(H_1, H_0) \to 0,$$
where $H_0 = \mathbb{Z}$,$H_1 = \mathbb{Z}/2$,$H_2 = 0$ for$\mathbb{RP}^2$. This gives$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}/2 \to H_2 \to \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2) \oplus \mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to 0$, i.e.,$0 \to 0 \to H_2 \to 0$, so$H_2(\mathbb{RP}^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^2) = 0$. The Tor term appears in degree 2 from the$n-1 = 1$ anti-diagonal:$\mathrm{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$, contributing to$H_2$ — so$H_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. A careful accounting gives$H_*(\mathbb{RP}^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2, 0, \ldots$
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Over a field $k$: if$C_\bullet$ and$D_\bullet$ are complexes of$k$-vector spaces, then all Tor groups vanish and the Künneth formula simplifies to$H_n(C \otimes_k D) \cong \bigoplus_{i+j=n} H_i(C) \otimes_k H_j(D)$. This is the "Künneth formula over a field," which says that the Poincaré series (or Hilbert series) of$H_*(C \otimes D)$ is the product of the Poincaré series of$H_*(C)$ and$H_*(D)$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The proof of the Künneth formula uses a careful analysis of the short exact sequence $0 \to Z_n(C) \to C_n \to B_{n-1}(C) \to 0$ (where$Z_n$ denotes cycles and$B_{n-1}$ denotes boundaries), the fact that$B_{n-1}(C)$ is a submodule of the free module$C_{n-1}$, and a direct sum decomposition argument. The splitting of the Künneth sequence (though non-natural) comes from the fact that$B_n(C)$ is free (as a submodule of a free abelian group), which allows the short exact sequence to split at each stage. Over a general ring, the formula requires more hypotheses and the splitting may fail.
\end{remark}
