# 40.3 The Tor Groups

The tensor product functor $M \otimes_R -$ is right exact: given a short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$, the sequence $M \otimes A \to M \otimes B \to M \otimes C \to 0$ is exact, but the first map may fail to be injective. The failure of injectivity is measured by the Tor groups: $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M, N)$ is the $n$th left derived functor of $M \otimes_R -$, computed by taking a projective resolution $P_\bullet \to N$, applying $M \otimes_R -$, and extracting homology. As with Ext, there is a balance theorem: the same groups arise if one instead resolves $M$ projectively and applies $- \otimes_R N$. This symmetry — that $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M, N) \cong \operatorname{Tor}_n^R(N, M)$ — is not at all obvious from the definition in terms of one argument, and its proof uses the same double complex technique as the balance of Ext.

The connection between Tor and torsion — the etymology is not accidental — is made precise by the computation over $\mathbb{Z}$. For $M = \mathbb{Z}/m$ and $N = \mathbb{Z}/n$, the resolution $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times m} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m \to 0$ yields $\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ and $\operatorname{Tor}_n = 0$ for $n \geq 2$. The nonzero $\operatorname{Tor}_1$ reflects the fact that the tensor product $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n$ has torsion that comes from the interaction of the two cyclic structures. More generally, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(M, N)$ vanishes for all $N$ if and only if $M$ is flat over $R$. This characterizes flatness in homological terms: flat modules are exactly those that kill Tor, just as projective modules kill Ext.

The Künneth formula, one of the principal computational results in algebraic topology, expresses the homology of a product space in terms of the homologies of its factors, with a correction term involving Tor. Specifically, if $C_\bullet$ and $D_\bullet$ are chain complexes of free abelian groups, the Künneth theorem gives a short exact sequence $0 \to \bigoplus_{p+q=n} H_p(C) \otimes H_q(D) \to H_n(C \otimes D) \to \bigoplus_{p+q=n-1} \operatorname{Tor}_1(H_p(C), H_q(D)) \to 0$. The Tor term is the "error term" that arises precisely when the homology groups have torsion; when all homology is torsion-free, the Tor term vanishes and the formula simplifies. This is Tor's most classical geometric application, connecting the algebraic theory directly to topology.
