# 40.4.2 $H^n(G,M) = \mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$

The identification $H^n(G, M) = \mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$ is both a definition and a theorem: it is the definition that makes group cohomology a special case of derived functor theory, and it has the theorem as a consequence that any projective resolution of$\mathbb{Z}$ as a$\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module can be used to compute group cohomology. Here$\mathbb{Z}$ is the *trivial$G$-module*, meaning$G$ acts by the identity:$g \cdot n = n$ for all$g \in G$ and$n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

To compute $H^n(G, M)$, one chooses a projective resolution$P_\bullet \to \mathbb{Z}$ of the trivial module as a$\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module, applies$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(-, M)$ to get a cochain complex, and takes cohomology. The standard and most explicit choice is the *bar resolution* (described in Section 40.4.4), whose terms are free$\mathbb{Z}[G]$-modules with basis$\{[g_1 | \cdots | g_n] : g_i \in G\}$. But the key point is that any projective resolution gives the same answer, so one is free to choose the most convenient one for a given group$G$ and module$M$.

This identification has several immediate consequences. First, the long exact sequence in group cohomology follows immediately from the general theory: a short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ of$G$-modules gives
$$0 \to H^0(G,A) \to H^0(G,B) \to H^0(G,C) \to H^1(G,A) \to H^1(G,B) \to H^1(G,C) \to H^2(G,A) \to \cdots$$
Second, the functoriality in $G$ — restriction maps$\mathrm{res}: H^n(G, M) \to H^n(H, M)$ for subgroups$H \leq G$, and inflation maps$\mathrm{inf}: H^n(G/N, M^N) \to H^n(G, M)$ for normal subgroups$N$ — is a formal consequence of the Ext interpretation, relating group cohomology at different levels of the group-theoretic structure.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $H^0(G, M) = M^G$. The Ext definition gives$H^0(G, M) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$. A$\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module map$f: \mathbb{Z} \to M$ is determined by$f(1) \in M$, and the equivariance condition$f(g \cdot 1) = g \cdot f(1)$ becomes$f(1) = g \cdot f(1)$ (since$g$ acts trivially on$\mathbb{Z}$). So$f(1)$ must be$G$-fixed, giving$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M) \cong M^G$. Consistent with$H^0 =$ invariants.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $H^n(\{e\}, M) = 0$ for$n \geq 1$. The trivial group has$\mathbb{Z}[\{e\}] = \mathbb{Z}$, and the trivial module$\mathbb{Z}$ over$\mathbb{Z}$ has the projective resolution$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0$ (length 0). So$\mathrm{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, M) = 0$ for$n \geq 1$, since$\mathbb{Z}$ is projective over itself.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** $H^n(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ for$n \geq 0$ and a prime$p$. Here$G = \mathbb{Z}/p = \langle \sigma \rangle$ and$\mathbb{Z}[G] = \mathbb{Z}[\sigma]/(\sigma^p - 1)$. The trivial module$\mathbb{Z}/p$ has a periodic free resolution over$\mathbb{Z}[G]$ (or rather over$\mathbb{F}_p[G]$ for the mod-$p$ computation):$\cdots \to \mathbb{F}_p[G] \xrightarrow{N} \mathbb{F}_p[G] \xrightarrow{\sigma - 1} \mathbb{F}_p[G] \xrightarrow{N} \mathbb{F}_p[G] \xrightarrow{\sigma - 1} \mathbb{F}_p[G] \to \mathbb{F}_p \to 0$, where$N = 1 + \sigma + \cdots + \sigma^{p-1}$ is the norm. Applying$\mathrm{Hom}(-, \mathbb{F}_p)$ gives$H^n(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{F}_p$ for all$n \geq 0$ — the cohomology is periodic of period 2 (with trivial coefficients).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The augmented resolution approach: the augmentation map $\varepsilon: \mathbb{Z}[G] \to \mathbb{Z}$ sending$\sum a_g g \mapsto \sum a_g$ is a$\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module surjection, and$\ker(\varepsilon) = I_G$ (the augmentation ideal, generated by$\{g - 1 : g \in G\}$). The short exact sequence$0 \to I_G \to \mathbb{Z}[G] \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0$ begins a projective resolution of$\mathbb{Z}$: since$\mathbb{Z}[G]$ is free over itself, applying$\mathrm{Hom}(-, M)$ gives$H^1(G, M) = \mathrm{Ext}^1(I_G \text{ part})$, and one computes$H^1(G, M) \cong \mathrm{Der}(G, M) / \mathrm{PDer}(G, M)$ (derivations modulo principal derivations), recovering the classical description.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
There is also group *homology* $H_n(G, M) = \mathrm{Tor}_n^{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$, the left derived functors of the co-invariants functor$M \mapsto M_G = M / \langle gm - m \rangle$. Group homology and cohomology are related by universal coefficient theorems and, for finite groups, by Tate duality. The Tate cohomology$\hat{H}^n(G, M)$ for finite groups combines homology and cohomology into a single$\mathbb{Z}$-graded theory that is periodic in the case of cyclic groups — a phenomenon with deep connections to class field theory and the theory of number fields.
\end{remark}
