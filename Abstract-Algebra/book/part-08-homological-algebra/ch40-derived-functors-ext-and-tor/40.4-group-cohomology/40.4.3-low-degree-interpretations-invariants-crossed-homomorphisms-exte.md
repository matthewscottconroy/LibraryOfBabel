# 40.4.3 Low-Degree Interpretations: $H^0$ = Invariants,$H^1$ = Crossed Homomorphisms,$H^2$ = Extensions

One test of any cohomological machine is whether it recovers the "classical" constructions in low degrees. Group cohomology passes this test: the groups $H^0$,$H^1$, and$H^2$ have direct interpretations that were known long before the general derived functor framework, and the derived functor definition shows these are not separate ad hoc constructions but the first three terms of a unified theory.

The group $H^0(G, M) = M^G$ is the group of$G$-invariant elements of$M$: those$m$ with$g \cdot m = m$ for all$g \in G$. This is immediate from the identification$H^0(G, M) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$, since a$\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module map$\mathbb{Z} \to M$ is determined by the image of$1$, and this image must be fixed by all$g$. For$H^1$, a *crossed homomorphism* (or$1$-cocycle) is a function$f: G \to M$ satisfying the cocycle condition$f(gh) = f(g) + g \cdot f(h)$ for all$g, h \in G$. A *principal crossed homomorphism* (or$1$-coboundary) is a function of the form$f(g) = g \cdot m - m$ for some fixed$m \in M$. Then$H^1(G, M) = Z^1(G,M)/B^1(G,M)$ is the quotient. When$G$ acts trivially on$M$, crossed homomorphisms are just group homomorphisms$G \to M$, and$H^1(G, M) = \mathrm{Hom}(G, M)$.

The group $H^2(G, M)$ classifies extensions of$G$ by$M$: these are short exact sequences of groups$1 \to M \to E \to G \to 1$ where$M$ is an abelian normal subgroup and$G$ acts on$M$ by conjugation in$E$. The zero element corresponds to the semidirect product$M \rtimes G$ (determined by the action alone); non-trivial elements of$H^2(G, M)$ correspond to non-split extensions.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $H^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z})$ with trivial action. Here$G = \mathbb{Z}/n = \langle \sigma \rangle$ acts trivially on$\mathbb{Z}$. A crossed homomorphism$f: G \to \mathbb{Z}$ satisfies$f(\sigma^{a+b}) = f(\sigma^a) + f(\sigma^b)$, i.e.,$f$ is a group homomorphism. So$Z^1 = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ (there are no non-trivial homomorphisms from$\mathbb{Z}/n$ to$\mathbb{Z}$ since$\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion-free). Thus$H^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $H^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ with trivial action. Crossed homomorphisms$f: \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}/n$ (with trivial action) are group homomorphisms.$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$ (generated by the identity map). Principal ones:$f(g) = g \cdot m - m = 0$ for all$g$ (since the action is trivial). So$B^1 = 0$ and$H^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** $H^2(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ with trivial action classifies central extensions$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/n \to E \to \mathbb{Z}/m \to 1$. By the computation from Section 40.2.5,$H^2(G, M) \cong \mathrm{Ext}^2_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$... actually for trivial action,$H^2(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ with trivial coefficients is$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m, n)$ (via the low-degree terms of a spectral sequence, or directly from the bar complex). The generator corresponds to the cyclic extension$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/n \to \mathbb{Z}/mn \to \mathbb{Z}/m \to 1$ (when$\gcd(m,n) = n$, this is a central extension; more generally it corresponds to the image of the generator of$\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** $H^1(G, G^{\mathrm{ab}})$ and outer automorphisms. When$G$ acts on$M = G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ (the abelianization) by conjugation,$H^1(G, G^{\mathrm{ab}})$ is related to the module of derivations of$G$. In the case$G$ acts trivially on$G^{\mathrm{ab}}$,$H^1(G, G^{\mathrm{ab}}) = \mathrm{Hom}(G, G^{\mathrm{ab}})$, which is the set of group homomorphisms from$G$ to its abelianization — recovering the natural projection$G \to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ as the universal such map. The crossed homomorphism perspective thus unifies the abelianization with the first cohomology of$G$ with coefficients in its own abelianization.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The interpretation of $H^2(G, M)$ as extensions of groups (rather than modules) is a group-theoretic fact that predates derived functors. The bijection between$2$-cocycles$c: G \times G \to M$ satisfying$c(g, h) + c(gh, k) = g \cdot c(h, k) + c(g, hk)$ (the cocycle condition) and group extensions is classical. The derived functor framework shows this is not a coincidence: it is the instance for$n = 2$ of the Baer classification of$n$-fold extensions by$\mathrm{Ext}^n$. The group-theoretic cocycle condition is precisely the condition that the bar complex boundary$\partial_2$ applied to$c$ gives zero — the cocycle condition is the abstract boundary condition in the bar complex.
\end{remark}
