# 40.4 Group Cohomology

Group cohomology is the derived functor theory applied to the representation theory of groups, and it is one of the richest illustrations of the power of homological methods. Given a group $G$ and a $G$-module $A$ (an abelian group on which $G$ acts by group homomorphisms), the group cohomology $H^n(G, A)$ is defined as $\operatorname{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, A)$, where $\mathbb{Z}$ carries the trivial $G$-action. Equivalently, it is the $n$th right derived functor of the fixed-point functor $A \mapsto A^G = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, A)$. The group homology $H_n(G, A)$ is dual: it is $\operatorname{Tor}_n^{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, A)$, the left derived functor of $A \mapsto A_G = \mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]} A$.

The low-degree groups have concrete interpretations that motivated the subject before the abstract machinery was in place. $H^0(G, A) = A^G$ is the module of $G$-fixed points. $H^1(G, A)$ classifies "crossed homomorphisms" $f: G \to A$ satisfying $f(gh) = f(g) + g \cdot f(h)$, modulo "principal" ones of the form $f(g) = ga - a$; when $A$ is a trivial $G$-module, this reduces to ordinary group homomorphisms $G \to A$, so $H^1(G, A) = \operatorname{Hom}(G, A)$ in the trivial case. The group $H^2(G, A)$ classifies group extensions $1 \to A \to E \to G \to 1$ with $A$ abelian and the conjugation action on $A$ induced by the $G$-module structure. The cocycle condition that characterizes $H^2$ cocycles is precisely the associativity condition for the extension, and the coboundary condition corresponds to the equivalence of extensions.

The bar complex provides an explicit projective resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Z}[G]$. In degree $n$, it is the free $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module on the set of $n$-tuples $(g_1, \ldots, g_n) \in G^n$, with differential given by the standard face maps of the classifying space $BG$. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(-, A)$ to the bar complex yields the explicit cochain complex whose cohomology is $H^*(G, A)$, and the low-degree cocycles and coboundaries match the concrete descriptions above. The normalized bar complex — where all entries are nonidentity elements — yields the same cohomology and is more compact. The bar complex is the algebraic realization of the singular chains on the classifying space $BG$, and the identification $H^n(G, A) = H^n(BG; A)$ (when $A$ is a trivial module) connects group cohomology to the cohomology of a topological space.
