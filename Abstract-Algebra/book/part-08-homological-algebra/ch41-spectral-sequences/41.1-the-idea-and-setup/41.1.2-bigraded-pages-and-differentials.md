# 41.1.2 Bigraded Pages $E_r^{p,q}$ and Differentials$d_r$

The formal structure of a spectral sequence encodes the iterative process of Section 41.1.1 in a precise algebraic framework. The data consists of a sequence of pages, each a bigraded abelian group (or module), connected by differentials whose bidegrees change as the pages advance. This bigrading is not arbitrary: it reflects the two "coordinate directions" of the filtration, and the varying bidegree of the differential captures the fact that each successive correction reaches further across the grid.

A *first-quadrant spectral sequence* (the most common setting) consists of: bigraded modules $\{E_r^{p,q}\}$ indexed by integers$p, q \geq 0$ and page number$r \geq 2$ (sometimes$r \geq 0$ or$r \geq 1$); differentials$d_r: E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p-r, q+r-1}$ satisfying$d_r \circ d_r = 0$; and isomorphisms$E_{r+1}^{p,q} \cong H(E_r^{p,q}, d_r)$ identifying the next page with the cohomology of the current one. The bidegree$(-r, r-1)$ of$d_r$ is the key structural fact: at the$E_2$ page,$d_2$ moves two steps left and one step up; at the$E_3$ page,$d_3$ moves three left and two up; and so on. The differential thus "reaches further" on each successive page.

The sign convention varies in the literature. In the cohomological convention (standard for spectral sequences converging to cohomology), $d_r: E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}$ (moving right and down with increasing$r$). In the homological convention (standard for spectral sequences converging to homology),$d_r: E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p-r, q+r-1}$. The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence and most spectral sequences in algebra use the cohomological convention.

The visual picture is a grid of abelian groups $E_r^{p,q}$ in the first quadrant of the$(p,q)$-plane, with differentials drawn as arrows of increasing length and fixed slope. On the$E_2$ page, the arrows$d_2$ are short (they move 2 in one direction and 1 in the other); on the$E_r$ page, they stretch across$r$ columns (or rows). For fixed$(p,q)$ in the first quadrant, all but finitely many differentials$d_r$ leaving or entering$(p,q)$ must be zero (since eventually the source or target exits the first quadrant), so$E_r^{p,q}$ stabilizes for$r$ large enough. This stabilized value is denoted$E_\infty^{p,q}$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** The $E_2$ page of the LHS spectral sequence: a 5x5 grid with$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Q, H^q(N, M))$. The$d_2$ differentials go from$E_2^{p,q}$ to$E_2^{p+2, q-1}$ (in the cohomological convention). Since the first quadrant starts at$p, q \geq 0$, any$d_2$ from row$q = 0$ goes to row$q - 1 = -1$, which is outside the first quadrant — so$d_2 = 0$ on the bottom row$q = 0$. Similarly,$d_2$ into column$p = 0$ comes from column$p - 2 < 0$, so it is zero. The first non-trivial$d_2$ goes from$E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2, 0}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** A spectral sequence with only two non-zero rows: suppose $E_2^{p,q} = 0$ except for$q = 0$ and$q = m$. Then$d_2, d_3, \ldots, d_m$ all have trivial differential (since they go from row$q$ to row$q - r + 1$, but if$r < m$ then from row$m$ they go to row$m - r + 1 > 0$, which may be non-zero only if it equals 0 or$m$; for$1 < r < m$ this is neither, so$d_r = 0$). Only$d_{m+1}$ can go from row$m$ to row$0$, and any such differential is the only potentially non-trivial one.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The $E_\infty$ stabilization: for a fixed position$(p, q)$, the groups$E_r^{p,q}$ form a non-increasing sequence of subquotients:$E_2^{p,q} \supseteq E_3^{p,q} \supseteq \cdots \supseteq E_\infty^{p,q}$. Each successive group is the cohomology of the previous one with respect to$d_r$. Since each step can only kill elements (boundaries become trivial in the quotient), the sequence is "monotone decreasing" in size. Stabilization occurs when$d_r = 0$ both entering and leaving$(p,q)$, which happens for$r > \max(p, q+1)$ in the first quadrant (since beyond this point, either the source$E_r^{p-r, q+r-1}$ has$p - r < 0$, or the target$E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}$ has$q - r + 1 < 0$).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Homological vs cohomological conventions: in the Serre spectral sequence for a fibration $F \to E \to B$ of simply connected spaces, the$E_2$ page in the cohomological convention is$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(B; H^q(F; k))$ with differentials$d_r: E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}$. The *transgression*$d_r: E_r^{0, r-1} \to E_r^{r, 0}$ maps from the left column to the bottom row, and represents the "transgressing" of a cohomology class of the fiber to a cohomology class of the base. In the example of the loop fibration$\Omega S^n \to PS^n \to S^n$ (where$PS^n$ is contractible), the transgression$d_n: E_n^{0, n-1} \to E_n^{n, 0}$ is an isomorphism, which determines the cohomology ring of$\Omega S^n$ inductively.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
A spectral sequence of modules (rather than abelian groups) carries additional structure: the differentials $d_r$ are module maps, the isomorphisms$E_{r+1} \cong H(E_r)$ are module isomorphisms, and the target$H^*$ carries a compatible module structure. If the base ring is a graded ring$A$, then often the$E_2$ page is a bigraded$A$-algebra (with a product structure), and the differentials are derivations with respect to this product. This additional structure — the multiplicative structure on the spectral sequence — is a powerful tool for computing differentials and establishing degeneration, since a differential must be a ring homomorphism (hence zero on units), and degree considerations often force the differential to be zero.
\end{remark}
