# 41.1.3 The $(r+1)$-st Page Is the Homology of the$r$-th

The defining recurrence of a spectral sequence is that each page is the cohomology of the previous page with respect to its differential. Explicitly, the $(r+1)$-st page is:
$$E_{r+1}^{p,q} = \frac{\ker(d_r: E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r,q-r+1})}{\mathrm{im}(d_r: E_r^{p-r, q+r-1} \to E_r^{p,q})}.$$
This formula defines how to pass from one page to the next. Each step kills some of the previous page — those classes that become boundaries — and retains only the "permanently surviving" cycles. After enough steps, the groups stabilize to $E_\infty^{p,q}$.

The difficulty of spectral sequence computations lies precisely here. Computing $E_{r+1}$ from$E_r$ requires knowing the differential$d_r$, and determining$d_r$ is in general the hard part. At the$E_2$ page, the differential$d_2$ often has a geometric or algebraic interpretation; but$d_3, d_4, \ldots$ become progressively more abstract, and explicit formulas for them are available in only a limited number of situations. In practice, one looks for reasons why differentials must vanish — degree arguments, rank constraints, comparison with known computations — rather than computing them directly. When all differentials above$d_r$ are zero for structural reasons, the spectral sequence collapses at$E_r$, and the computation reduces to that one page.

The recurrence also implies a useful monotonicity: each page $E_{r+1}^{p,q}$ is a subquotient of$E_r^{p,q}$, hence is "no larger" than the previous page. The$E_\infty$ page is thus a subquotient of$E_2$ (and of every intermediate page). In the first-quadrant setting, the stabilization at$E_\infty$ occurs for$r > \max(p, q+1)$ at each position$(p,q)$, since beyond this point both the source and target of any$d_r$ leaving or entering$(p,q)$ lie outside the first quadrant.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** A spectral sequence concentrating on the $E_2$ page. Suppose$E_2^{p,q}$ is non-zero only for$p = 0$ (the left edge). Then$d_2: E_2^{p,q} \to E_2^{p+2, q-1}$ is automatically zero (since$E_2^{p+2, q-1} = 0$ for$p + 2 > 0$). Similarly all higher$d_r = 0$. So$E_2 = E_\infty$ and the spectral sequence degenerates at$E_2$. The target$H^n$ has associated graded$E_\infty^{0, n}$, so$H^n$ is a (possibly non-trivial) extension of$E_\infty^{0, n}$ by$0$, i.e.,$H^n \cong E_2^{0, n}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** The $E_3$ page from an explicit$d_2$. Suppose$E_2^{p,q} \cong k$ (a field) for$(p,q) \in \{(0,2), (2,1), (4,0)\}$ and$0$ elsewhere. The$d_2$ differentials are:
- $d_2: E_2^{0,2} \to E_2^{2,1}$: a map$k \to k$, either$0$ or an isomorphism.
- $d_2: E_2^{2,1} \to E_2^{4,0}$: a map$k \to k$, either$0$ or an isomorphism.
If both $d_2$'s are isomorphisms, then$E_3^{0,2} = E_3^{2,1} = E_3^{4,0} = 0$, and$E_3 = E_\infty = 0$ (in those positions). If one$d_2$ is zero and the other is an isomorphism, then one position survives to$E_\infty$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Stabilization: consider the LHS spectral sequence for $1 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n \to 1$ with$M = \mathbb{Z}$. The$E_2$ page has$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/n, H^q(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}))$. Since$\mathbb{Z}$ is a PID,$H^q(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for$q \geq 2$ and$H^0(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$,$H^1(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$ (by explicit computation). So the$E_2$ page has non-zero entries only in rows$q = 0$ and$q = 1$. The$d_2$ differential is the only potentially non-trivial one, and after$E_3 = H(E_2, d_2)$, all further differentials are zero (since they would have to go from row 1 to row 0 in a step of 3 or more, which is impossible if non-zero entries are only in rows 0 and 1).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Passing from $E_3$ to$E_4$: suppose on the$E_3$ page, the differential$d_3: E_3^{0,2} \to E_3^{3,0}$ is non-zero. Then$E_4^{0,2} = \ker(d_3)$ and$E_4^{3,0} = E_3^{3,0}/\mathrm{im}(d_3)$. If$d_3$ is an isomorphism between$k$-vector spaces of dimension 1, then$E_4^{0,2} = 0$ and$E_4^{3,0} = 0$. If$d_3$ is zero, then$E_4^{0,2} = E_3^{0,2}$ and$E_4^{3,0} = E_3^{3,0}$. The stabilization then occurs at some later page when all remaining differentials are zero.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The recurrence $E_{r+1} = H(E_r, d_r)$ defines the spectral sequence *algebraically*, but understanding it geometrically requires knowing where$d_r$ comes from. In the case of a filtered complex,$d_r$ is the "shadow" of the original differential$d: C \to C$ acting across$r$ filtration levels. A cycle$x \in F^p C_n$ (i.e.,$dx = 0$) represents a class in$E_1^{p, n-p}$, but if$dx \neq 0$ in$F^{p+r} C_{n-1}$ (i.e.,$dx$ is non-zero in filtration level$p+r$ but zero in higher levels), then the class$[x]$ is killed by$d_r$ in$E_r$. The successive differentials thus detect the "depth" at which a cycle fails to be a permanent cycle in$H_*(C)$.
\end{remark}
