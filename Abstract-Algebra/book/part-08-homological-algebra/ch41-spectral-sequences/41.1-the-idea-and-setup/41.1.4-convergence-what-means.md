# 41.1.4 Convergence: What $E_r \Rightarrow H^*$ Means

The notation $E_2^{p,q} \Rightarrow H^{p+q}$ — read "the$E_2$ page converges to$H^*$" — summarizes the relationship between a spectral sequence and the graded group it is computing. Understanding what this notation means precisely requires understanding both the stabilization of pages and the filtration structure of the target.

A spectral sequence *converges* to $H^*$ if: (1) for each$(p,q)$, the sequence of groups$E_r^{p,q}$ stabilizes for$r$ large enough, defining$E_\infty^{p,q}$; and (2) there is a filtration$H^n = F^0 H^n \supseteq F^1 H^n \supseteq F^2 H^n \supseteq \cdots$ of the target groups, with$F^p H^n = 0$ for$p \gg 0$ and$F^p H^n = H^n$ for$p \leq 0$, such that the associated graded pieces satisfy$F^p H^n / F^{p+1} H^n \cong E_\infty^{p, n-p}$. The total degree is$n = p + q$, so the$n$th target group$H^n$ is assembled from the$E_\infty$ terms along the anti-diagonal$p + q = n$.

The critical caveat is that knowing $E_\infty^{p,q}$ determines only the *associated graded* of$H^n$, not$H^n$ itself. The group$H^n$ may be a non-split extension of$F^p H^n / F^{p+1} H^n$ by$F^{p+1} H^n / F^{p+2} H^n$, and recovering$H^n$ from its associated graded requires solving an extension problem. Over a field, all such extensions split and$H^n \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} E_\infty^{p,q}$; over$\mathbb{Z}$ or other rings, the extension problem may be genuinely obstructed. This gap between "knowing the associated graded" and "knowing the group" is called the *extension problem*, and it is a recurring subtlety in spectral sequence computations.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** A spectral sequence converging to $H^2 = \mathbb{Z}/4$. Suppose$E_\infty^{0,2} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ and$E_\infty^{1,1} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ (and all other$E_\infty^{p, 2-p} = 0$). The filtration on$H^2$ is$H^2 = F^0 H^2 \supseteq F^1 H^2 \supseteq F^2 H^2 = 0$ with$F^0/F^1 \cong E_\infty^{0,2} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ and$F^1/F^2 \cong E_\infty^{1,1} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. So$H^2$ is an extension$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to H^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$. The extension could be either$\mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$ (split) or$\mathbb{Z}/4$ (non-split). The spectral sequence alone does not distinguish between these two possibilities — one needs additional information (e.g., from a different computation) to resolve the extension problem.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Over a field $k$: if all groups$E_\infty^{p,q}$ are$k$-vector spaces, then every extension of$k$-vector spaces splits (since$k$-vector spaces are free modules over$k$), and$H^n \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} E_\infty^{p,q}$ as$k$-vector spaces. In particular,$\dim_k H^n = \sum_{p+q=n} \dim_k E_\infty^{p,q}$, and since$\dim E_\infty^{p,q} \leq \dim E_r^{p,q} \leq \cdots \leq \dim E_2^{p,q}$, one has the useful inequality$\dim H^n \leq \sum_{p+q=n} \dim E_2^{p,q}$, with equality if and only if the spectral sequence degenerates at$E_2$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The LHS spectral sequence for $1 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 1$ with$M = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (trivial action). The$E_2$ page is$H^p(\mathbb{Z}/2, H^q(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2))$. Since$H^q(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ for all$q \geq 0$, and$\mathbb{Z}/2$ acts trivially,$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ for all$p, q \geq 0$. The spectral sequence converges to$H^*(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/2)$, which is$\mathbb{Z}/2$ in every degree. The extension problem:$H^1(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, consistent with$E_\infty^{1,0} = E_\infty^{0,1} = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (if$d_2 = 0$) and the extension$0 \to E_\infty^{0,1} \to H^1 \to E_\infty^{1,0} \to 0$ being$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$, which must split since there is only one element of order 2 in$H^1 = \mathbb{Z}/2$... but wait,$H^1(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$, not$(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$. This forces$d_2 \neq 0$ in some degrees to achieve the correct answer.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Convergence conditions. Not all spectral sequences converge in the sense above. For a spectral sequence arising from a filtered complex, convergence is guaranteed when the filtration is *bounded*: $F^p C_n = C_n$ for$p \leq 0$ and$F^p C_n = 0$ for$p > n$ (a finite filtration in each degree). For infinite filtrations, one needs additional completeness conditions (Eilenberg's condition, or the requirement that the filtration is complete and Hausdorff). The boundedness condition is satisfied for the double complex spectral sequences (Section 41.2) when the double complex lives in the first quadrant.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The notation $E_2^{p,q} \Rightarrow H^{p+q}$ is standard but should be read carefully. It says: (1) there exists a filtration on$H^*$, and (2) the$E_\infty$ page provides the associated graded of$H^*$ with respect to this filtration. It does not say$H^n = \bigoplus_{p+q=n} E_\infty^{p,q}$ — that requires the extension problem to be trivially solved (e.g., over a field). The arrow$\Rightarrow$ is a reminder that this is an approximation, not an equality: the spectral sequence "approaches"$H^*$ asymptotically through its successive pages, and$H^*$ itself sits "above" all the pages in a precise algebraic sense.
\end{remark}
