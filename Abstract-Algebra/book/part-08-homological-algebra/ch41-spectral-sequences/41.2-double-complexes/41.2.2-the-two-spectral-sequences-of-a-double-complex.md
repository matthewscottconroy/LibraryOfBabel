# 41.2.2 The Two Spectral Sequences of a Double Complex

Every double complex $C^{p,q}$ gives rise to two spectral sequences, both converging to$H_*(\mathrm{Tot}(C))$, corresponding to the two possible orders in which one takes homology: vertical first then horizontal, or horizontal first then vertical. Each provides a different computational path to the same answer, and comparing the two is a fundamental technique.

The *vertical-first* (or ${}^I$) spectral sequence is obtained by filtering the total complex by columns:$F^p \mathrm{Tot}(C)^n = \bigoplus_{i \geq p, i+j=n} C^{i,j}$ (keeping only columns$i \geq p$). The$E_0$ page is${}^I E_0^{p,q} = C^{p,q}$, with$d_0 = d^v$ (the vertical differential). The$E_1$ page is${}^I E_1^{p,q} = H^q_v(C^{p,\bullet})$, the vertical cohomology of the$p$th column. The$d_1$ differential is then induced by the horizontal differential$d^h$, and${}^I E_2^{p,q} = H^p_h(H^q_v(C))$ — one takes vertical cohomology first, then horizontal.

The *horizontal-first* (or ${}^{II}$) spectral sequence reverses the order: filter by rows$F^q \mathrm{Tot}(C)^n = \bigoplus_{j \geq q, p+j=n} C^{p,j}$. The${}^{II} E_1^{p,q} = H^p_h(C^{\bullet,q})$ is the horizontal cohomology of the$q$th row, and${}^{II} E_2^{p,q} = H^q_v(H^p_h(C))$ — horizontal cohomology first, then vertical.

The power of having two spectral sequences converging to the same limit is that one can sometimes compute with one and extract information about the other. If one sequence degenerates at $E_2$ — all higher differentials are zero — then$H_n(\mathrm{Tot}(C)) \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} E_2^{p,q}$ from that sequence, and this gives explicit information about the other sequence's limit.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Proof that Ext is balanced (sketch). Let $P_\bullet \to M$ and$I^\bullet \to N$ be resolutions. The double complex$\mathrm{Hom}(P_p, I^q)$ has:
- Vertical cohomology of column $p$:$H^q(\mathrm{Hom}(P_p, I^\bullet)) = \mathrm{Ext}^q(P_p, N) = 0$ for$q > 0$ (since$P_p$ is projective) and$= \mathrm{Hom}(P_p, N)$ for$q = 0$.
So ${}^I E_1^{p,q} = \mathrm{Hom}(P_p, N)$ if$q = 0$ and$0$ if$q > 0$. The${}^I E_2$ page is$H^p(\mathrm{Hom}(P_\bullet, N)) = \mathrm{Ext}^p(M, N)$ (by definition of Ext from projective resolutions). Since${}^I E_2^{p,q} = 0$ for$q > 0$, the spectral sequence degenerates at$E_2$, and$H^n(\mathrm{Tot}) \cong \mathrm{Ext}^n(M, N)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Horizontal-first for the same double complex. The horizontal cohomology of row $q$:$H^p(\mathrm{Hom}(P_\bullet, I^q)) = \mathrm{Ext}^p(M, I^q) = 0$ for$p > 0$ (since$I^q$ is injective) and$= \mathrm{Hom}(M, I^q)$ for$p = 0$. So${}^{II} E_1^{p,q} = \mathrm{Hom}(M, I^q)$ if$p = 0$ and$0$ if$p > 0$. The${}^{II} E_2$ page concentrates on column$p = 0$, with${}^{II} E_2^{0,q} = H^q(\mathrm{Hom}(M, I^\bullet)) = \mathrm{Ext}^q(M, N)$ (from injective resolution). Again the sequence degenerates at$E_2$, giving$H^n(\mathrm{Tot}) \cong \mathrm{Ext}^n(M, N)$. Both spectral sequences give the same answer, confirming the balancing.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The two spectral sequences for the Künneth formula. Let $C_\bullet$ and$D_\bullet$ be free chain complexes. The double complex$C_p \otimes D_q$ has:
- Vertical-first: ${}^I E_2^{p,q} = H_p(C) \otimes H_q(D) \oplus \mathrm{Tor}_1(H_{p-1}(C), H_q(D))$ — the Künneth formula applied in the horizontal direction.
- Horizontal-first: ${}^{II} E_2^{p,q}$ similarly.
Both degenerate at $E_2$ (since$C$ and$D$ are free, hence flat), yielding the Künneth formula for$H_n(C \otimes D)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** A spectral sequence that does not degenerate at $E_2$. Consider the double complex with$C^{p,q} = k$ for$(p,q) \in \{(0,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,1)\}$ and all horizontal and vertical maps being the identity$k \to k$. The total complex$\mathrm{Tot}(C)$ has$\mathrm{Tot}^0 = k$,$\mathrm{Tot}^1 = k^2$,$\mathrm{Tot}^2 = k$ with total differential given by$d(x) = (d^h x, d^v x)$ etc. The vertical-first$E_2$ page:$H^0_v = k$ (column$p=0$:$0 \to k \xrightarrow{1} k \to 0$ has$H^0 = 0$?). Actually with both maps equal to the identity, the vertical cohomology$H^0(k \xrightarrow{1} k) = \ker = 0$ and$H^1 = k/k = 0$. So$E_1^{p,q} = 0$ for all$p, q$ in the columns with identity maps — the spectral sequence computes$H_*(\mathrm{Tot}(C)) = 0$ correctly (since the total complex is acyclic: the total differential is an isomorphism in each degree).
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The comparison of the two spectral sequences of a double complex is one of the most powerful techniques in homological algebra. It was used by Grothendieck to prove the Leray spectral sequence, by Cartan and Eilenberg to establish the basic properties of Ext and Tor, and by many others in algebraic topology and algebraic geometry. The key principle is always the same: if one spectral sequence degenerates early (at $E_1$ or$E_2$), the homology of the total complex is completely computable from that sequence, and this gives strong constraints on the other spectral sequence — which may then be used to compute more complex derived functor groups.
\end{remark}
