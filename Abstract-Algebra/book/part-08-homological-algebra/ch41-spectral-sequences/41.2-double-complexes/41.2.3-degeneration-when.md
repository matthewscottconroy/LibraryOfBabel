# 41.2.3 Degeneration: When $E_2 = E_\infty$

Degeneration is the best possible outcome of a spectral sequence computation: the sequence collapses at the $E_2$ page, all higher differentials are zero, and the$E_2$ page directly gives the associated graded of the target. When degeneration occurs, the extension problem (Section 41.1.4) is the only remaining obstacle, and in many situations it is easily resolved.

Formally, a spectral sequence *degenerates at $E_2$* if$d_r = 0$ for all$r \geq 2$, so that$E_2 = E_3 = \cdots = E_\infty$. In this case,$H^n$ has a filtration with associated graded$\bigoplus_{p+q=n} E_2^{p,q}$. If additionally all extension problems split — most notably when the base ring is a field or when the groups involved are free — then$H^n \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} E_2^{p,q}$ outright.

Degeneration can be established by several methods. The most common is a sparsity argument: if the $E_2$ page is concentrated in positions where the differential$d_r$ must go from a non-zero group to a zero group (or vice versa), then$d_r = 0$ for degree reasons, and the same argument propagates to all higher pages. This happens, for instance, when$E_2^{p,q} = 0$ for all odd$q$ — then the differentials going to rows with odd$q$ are automatically zero, and the argument can sometimes be iterated.

A second method is a *rank argument* (also called a degeneration by Euler characteristic count). If the spectral sequence converges to a known group $H^*$ over a field$k$, and one knows that$\sum_{p+q=n} \dim_k E_2^{p,q} = \dim_k H^n$ for all$n$, then since every differential kills some classes (reducing the dimension), the only consistent possibility is that all differentials are zero. This argument is available whenever one has independent information about the target.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Degeneration by sparsity: suppose $E_2^{p,q} = 0$ unless$q \in \{0, 1\}$ (the$E_2$ page has at most two non-zero rows). The differential$d_2: E_2^{p,q} \to E_2^{p+2, q-1}$ goes from row$q$ to row$q - 1$. If$d_2$ from row 1 to row 0 is zero (e.g., because it is a map between groups where one is zero), then$E_3 = E_2$. The differential$d_3$ goes from row$q$ to row$q - 2$, which exits the non-zero region, so$d_3 = 0$ also. Thus$E_\infty = E_2$ if and only if$d_2 = 0$ — degeneration at$E_2$ requires only checking$d_2$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Rank argument over $\mathbb{F}_p$: let$G = \mathbb{Z}/p$ act trivially on$M = \mathbb{F}_p$. The LHS spectral sequence for$1 \to G \to G \to 1 \to 1$ (trivial extension) has$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(1, H^q(G, \mathbb{F}_p))$. Since the quotient group is trivial,$E_2^{p,q} = H^q(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ for$p = 0$ and$0$ for$p > 0$. The spectral sequence degenerates trivially (only one column). But for$1 \to G \to G^2 \to G \to 1$ (direct product),$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(G, \mathbb{F}_p) \otimes H^q(G, \mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{F}_p^{p+1} \otimes \mathbb{F}_p^{q+1}$ — no, let us just say$E_2^{p,q} \cong \mathbb{F}_p$ for all$p, q \geq 0$. Since$H^n(G^2, \mathbb{F}_p) = \mathbb{F}_p^{n+1}$ and$\sum_{p+q=n} \dim E_2^{p,q} = n+1$ (there are$n+1$ positions with$p + q = n$ in the first quadrant), the rank count matches and the sequence degenerates at$E_2$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Degeneration in the Serre spectral sequence. For a product fibration $F \times B \to B$ (with$E = F \times B$ the trivial fibration), the Serre spectral sequence degenerates at$E_2$ for any coefficients in a field. The$E_2$ page is$H^*(B; k) \otimes H^*(F; k)$, and by a rank argument (since$H^*(F \times B; k) \cong H^*(B; k) \otimes H^*(F; k)$ by the Künneth theorem), all differentials vanish. This is consistent with the spectral sequence "not needing" to correct anything, since the fibration is trivial.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Non-degeneration: in the path-loop fibration $\Omega S^2 \to PS^2 \to S^2$ (where$PS^2$ is contractible, so$H^*(PS^2; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$ in degree 0), the Serre spectral sequence has$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(S^2; \mathbb{Z}) \otimes H^q(\Omega S^2; \mathbb{Z})$. Since$H^*(PS^2) = \mathbb{Z}$ (concentrated in degree 0), the convergence forces$E_\infty^{p,q} = \mathbb{Z}$ for$(p,q) = (0,0)$ and$0$ elsewhere. To achieve this from$E_2$ (which has$E_2^{0,q} = H^q(\Omega S^2)$ and$E_2^{2,q} = H^q(\Omega S^2)$ and 0 elsewhere), the$d_2$ differentials must be isomorphisms in all degrees:$d_2: E_2^{0,2k} \to E_2^{2, 2k-1}$. This forces$H^*(\Omega S^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}[u]$ (polynomial algebra on a degree-2 generator) — a classic computation by non-degeneration.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
Over a field of characteristic zero, many geometric spectral sequences degenerate at $E_2$ for deep reasons. The Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence (from the Hodge filtration on the de Rham complex) degenerates at$E_1$ for smooth projective varieties over$\mathbb{C}$, by Deligne's theorem (using the theory of mixed Hodge structures). The Leray spectral sequence degenerates at$E_2$ for proper smooth morphisms in characteristic zero (Deligne again). These are hard theorems, but their consequences — the decomposition of cohomology as a direct sum of$E_2$ terms — are extremely powerful and are widely used in algebraic geometry.
\end{remark}
