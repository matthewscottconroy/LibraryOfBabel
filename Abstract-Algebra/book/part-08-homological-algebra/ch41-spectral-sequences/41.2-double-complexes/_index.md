# 41.2 Double Complexes

A double complex is the most natural algebraic structure from which spectral sequences arise. Formally, a double complex $C^{\bullet, \bullet}$ consists of modules $C^{p,q}$ for $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$, equipped with horizontal differentials $\partial': C^{p,q} \to C^{p+1,q}$ and vertical differentials $\partial'': C^{p,q} \to C^{p,q+1}$, satisfying $(\partial')^2 = 0$, $(\partial'')^2 = 0$, and the anticommutativity condition $\partial' \partial'' + \partial'' \partial' = 0$ (or commutativity $\partial' \partial'' = \partial'' \partial'$, depending on sign convention). The total complex $\operatorname{Tot}(C)^n = \bigoplus_{p+q=n} C^{p,q}$ carries a single differential $D = \partial' + \partial''$ (with appropriate signs), and one verifies $D^2 = 0$ precisely from the anticommutativity condition. The cohomology of the total complex is the ultimate target.

The two spectral sequences of a double complex arise from the two natural filtrations. Filtering by column degree (fixing $p$ and taking the subcomplex $\bigoplus_{p' \leq p} C^{p', \bullet}$) produces a spectral sequence whose $E_1$ page is the vertical cohomology: ${}' E_1^{p,q} = H^q_{\partial''}(C^{p,\bullet})$. The $d_1$ differential on this page is induced by $\partial'$, so ${}' E_2^{p,q} = H^p_{\partial'} H^q_{\partial''}(C^{\bullet, \bullet})$ — cohomology of the horizontal cohomology. Filtering by row degree produces the other spectral sequence ${}'' E_2^{p,q} = H^q_{\partial''} H^p_{\partial'}(C^{\bullet, \bullet})$. Both spectral sequences converge to $H^*(\operatorname{Tot}(C))$ under appropriate boundedness conditions. The fact that two different spectral sequences converge to the same thing is the engine behind the balance theorems: by making $C^{p,q} = \operatorname{Hom}(P_p, I^q)$ for a projective resolution $P_\bullet \to M$ and an injective resolution $N \to I^\bullet$, both spectral sequences converge to $\operatorname{Ext}^*(M, N)$, and one page has been computed as the other for each spectral sequence, yielding independence of resolution.

Degeneration is the most favorable outcome: the spectral sequence is said to degenerate at page $E_r$ if all differentials $d_s = 0$ for $s \geq r$, so that $E_r = E_\infty$. For double complexes, degeneration at $E_2$ occurs when the double complex is concentrated in a single row or column — in which case one of the two spectral sequences collapses immediately — or more generally when enough vanishing conditions are satisfied. The Grothendieck spectral sequence, which computes the derived functors of a composite $F \circ G$ from those of $F$ and $G$ individually, uses precisely this framework: the $E_2$ page is $R^p F(R^q G(M))$, and the spectral sequence converges to $R^{p+q}(F \circ G)(M)$. This subsumes as special cases the Leray spectral sequence, the LHS spectral sequence, and the hypercohomology spectral sequence.
