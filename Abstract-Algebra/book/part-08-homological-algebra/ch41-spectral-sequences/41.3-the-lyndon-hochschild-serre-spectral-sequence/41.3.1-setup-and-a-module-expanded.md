# 41.3.1 The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre Spectral Sequence: Setup, the $E_2$ Page, and the Five-Term Sequence

The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre (LHS) spectral sequence is the central algebraic example of spectral sequences in group theory. Given a group extension $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ and a $G$-module $A$, it provides a systematic method for computing $H^*(G, A)$ from $H^*(Q, -)$ applied to $H^*(N, A)$. The construction exemplifies how filtrations on bar resolutions give rise to spectral sequences, and the low-degree terms yield a five-term exact sequence that is one of the most frequently cited results in the cohomology of groups.

## The Setup

Let $1 \to N \xrightarrow{i} G \xrightarrow{\pi} Q \to 1$ be a short exact sequence of groups (a *group extension*). Let $A$ be a left $G$-module. The group $N$ is a normal subgroup of $G$, and $Q = G/N$. The quotient group $Q$ acts on $H^q(N, A)$ for each $q \geq 0$, making $H^q(N, A)$ a $Q$-module (this action is defined by conjugation in $G$: for $\bar{g} \in Q$ and a cocycle $\phi: N^q \to A$, define $(\bar{g} \cdot \phi)(n_1, \ldots, n_q) = g \cdot \phi(g^{-1}n_1 g, \ldots, g^{-1} n_q g)$ for any lift $g$ of $\bar{g}$ to $G$; this is well-defined since $N \trianglelefteq G$).

\begin{theorem}[Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre]
Let $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ be a group extension and $A$ a $G$-module. There is a convergent spectral sequence of cohomological type:
$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Q,\, H^q(N,\, A)) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(G,\, A).$$
The differentials have bidegree $(r, 1-r)$: $d_r: E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}$. The filtration on $H^n(G, A)$ is:
$$H^n(G, A) = F^0 H^n \supseteq F^1 H^n \supseteq \cdots \supseteq F^n H^n \supseteq F^{n+1} H^n = 0,$$
with $E_\infty^{p,q} \cong F^p H^{p+q} / F^{p+1} H^{p+q}$.
\end{theorem}

## Reading the $E_2$ Page

The $E_2$ page $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Q, H^q(N, A))$ is a bigraded array of abelian groups. We visualize it with $p$ (the degree in $Q$) along the horizontal axis and $q$ (the degree in $N$) along the vertical axis.

The bottom row $E_2^{p,0} = H^p(Q, H^0(N, A)) = H^p(Q, A^N)$ consists of the cohomology of $Q$ with coefficients in the fixed-point module $A^N = \{a \in A : na = a\ \forall n \in N\}$. The left column $E_2^{0,q} = H^0(Q, H^q(N, A)) = H^q(N, A)^Q$ consists of the $Q$-invariants of the cohomology of $N$.

The differentials $d_2: E_2^{p,q} \to E_2^{p+2, q-1}$ potentially carry nontrivial information when $q \geq 1$.

## The Five-Term Exact Sequence

The most immediately useful output of the LHS spectral sequence is the **five-term exact sequence in low degrees**, which involves no differentials beyond $d_2$:

\begin{theorem}[Five-Term Exact Sequence]
With notation as above, there is an exact sequence:
$$0 \to H^1(Q, A^N) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{inf}} H^1(G, A) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}} H^1(N, A)^Q \xrightarrow{d_2} H^2(Q, A^N) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{inf}} H^2(G, A).$$
\end{theorem}

The maps in this sequence have explicit descriptions:
- **Inflation** $\mathrm{inf}: H^p(Q, A^N) \to H^p(G, A)$: given a cocycle $\phi: Q^p \to A^N$, inflate it to a cocycle $G^p \to A$ by composing with the quotient map $\pi^p: G^p \to Q^p$.
- **Restriction** $\mathrm{res}: H^n(G, A) \to H^n(N, A)$: restrict cocycles from $G$ to $N$.
- **The transgression** $d_2: H^1(N, A)^Q \to H^2(Q, A^N)$: this is the boundary map of the spectral sequence; it measures how a 1-cocycle of $N$ extends (or fails to extend) across the extension.

*Proof sketch.* The five-term exact sequence is extracted from the edge homomorphisms of the spectral sequence. The edge map $H^n(G, A) \to E_\infty^{n,0} \hookrightarrow E_2^{n,0} = H^n(Q, A^N)$ is inflation; the edge map $E_2^{0,n} = H^n(N,A)^Q \twoheadrightarrow E_\infty^{0,n} \to H^n(G, A)$ factors through restriction. The differential $d_2: E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2,0}$ provides the transgression. Exactness at each term is then a diagram chase. $\square$

## Worked Examples

**Example 1: Semidirect products.** Let $G = N \rtimes Q$ be a semidirect product (the extension splits). In this case, the LHS spectral sequence degenerates at $E_2$ in a strong sense: the inflation-restriction sequence splits, and $H^n(G, A) \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} E_\infty^{p,q}$ (though the extension problems are trivial). More precisely, the exact sequence:
$$0 \to H^1(Q, A^N) \to H^1(G, A) \to H^1(N, A)^Q \to H^2(Q, A^N) \to H^2(G, A)$$
becomes split-exact at $H^1$ since the splitting $s: Q \to G$ provides a section for restriction: the map $H^1(G, A) \to H^1(Q, A)$ induced by $s$ splits inflation, so $H^1(G, A) \cong H^1(Q, A^N) \oplus H^1(N, A)^Q$ when $d_2 = 0$.

**Example 2: $G = \mathbb{Z}$, $N = n\mathbb{Z}$, $Q = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, $A = \mathbb{Z}$.** The extension is $0 \to n\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to 0$. Since $\mathbb{Z}$ is free abelian, $H^k(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for $k \geq 2$ and $H^1(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. The LHS spectral sequence gives:
- $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z},\ H^q(n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}))$.
Since $n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H^q(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for $q \geq 2$, the only nonzero rows are $q = 0$ and $q = 1$:
- $E_2^{p,0} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z})$, which is $\mathbb{Z}$ for $p=0$, $0$ for $p$ odd, and $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ for $p$ even $\geq 2$.
- $E_2^{p,1} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z})$ as well, since $H^1(n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$-module.
The spectral sequence then computes $H^k(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for $k \geq 2$, consistent with $\mathbb{Z}$ being free.

**Example 3: Central extensions and $H^2$.** Suppose $N \subseteq Z(G)$ (central extension). Then $N$ acts trivially on $A$, so $H^q(N, A) = \mathrm{Hom}(N, \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})$ for $A = \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ with trivial action (using the cohomology of abelian groups). The five-term exact sequence becomes:
$$0 \to H^1(Q, A) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{inf}} H^1(G, A) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}} \mathrm{Hom}(N, A) \xrightarrow{d_2} H^2(Q, A) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{inf}} H^2(G, A).$$
The differential $d_2: \mathrm{Hom}(N, A) \to H^2(Q, A)$ is the *transgression*, and it detects whether a character of $N$ can be extended to a character of $G$. When $G = \hat{G}$ is a central extension of $Q$ by a cyclic group $N$, the transgression $d_2$ computes the class in $H^2(Q, A)$ corresponding to the extension — this is the identification of group extensions with $H^2$, the foundational result in the theory of central extensions.

## The Inflation-Restriction Exact Sequence

A special case of the five-term sequence, when $A$ has trivial $N$-action (so $A = A^N$ and $H^1(N, A) = \mathrm{Hom}(N, A)$), is the *inflation-restriction exact sequence*:
$$0 \to H^1(Q, A) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{inf}} H^1(G, A) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}} H^1(N, A)^Q.$$
In the presence of a splitting or under additional conditions, this extends to a long exact sequence called the Hochschild-Serre exact sequence, which is the foundation of Galois cohomology computations. For a Galois extension $L/K$ with group $G = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ and a normal intermediate extension $F/K$ with $H = \mathrm{Gal}(L/F)$ and $Q = \mathrm{Gal}(F/K)$, the sequence:
$$0 \to H^1(Q, (L^\times)^H) \to H^1(G, L^\times) \to H^1(H, L^\times)$$
computes Brauer group contributions and is central to class field theory.

## Connection to the Grothendieck Spectral Sequence

The LHS spectral sequence is a special case of the Grothendieck spectral sequence for the composition of derived functors. The two-functor composition here is:
$$A \mapsto H^0(N, A) = A^N \quad \text{(taking $N$-invariants)}, \quad \text{then} \quad B \mapsto H^0(Q, B) = B^Q.$$
The composition $H^0(Q, H^0(N, -)) = H^0(G, -)$ has derived functors $H^n(G, -)$, and the Grothendieck spectral sequence for the composition of the right-derived functors of $(-)^N$ and $(-)^Q$ is exactly the LHS spectral sequence. This connection places the LHS sequence in the general framework of Chapter 40 and shows that it is part of a much broader pattern: whenever two right-derived-functor computations compose, a spectral sequence interpolates.
