# 41.3.1 Setup: $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ and a$G$-Module$M$

The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre (LHS) spectral sequence is the primary computational tool for group cohomology of group extensions. Its setup is a short exact sequence of groups $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ — equivalently,$G$ is an extension of the quotient group$Q$ by the normal subgroup$N$ — together with a coefficient$G$-module$M$. The goal is to understand$H^*(G, M)$ in terms of the more accessible cohomologies$H^*(N, M)$ and$H^*(Q, -)$.

The motivation for such a spectral sequence is clear: knowing $G$ as an extension of$Q$ by$N$ gives a two-step picture of the group, and one expects the cohomology of$G$ to be assembled from the cohomologies of the two layers. But this assembly is not straightforward. The restriction map$\mathrm{res}: H^n(G, M) \to H^n(N, M)$ and the inflation map$\mathrm{inf}: H^n(Q, M^N) \to H^n(G, M)$ capture parts of the picture, but they do not by themselves determine$H^n(G, M)$ — there are "mixing terms" arising from the extension structure. The LHS spectral sequence organizes all of these mixing terms into a coherent framework.

One way to construct the LHS spectral sequence is to use a double complex. Choose a free $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-resolution$P_\bullet \to \mathbb{Z}$ and a free$\mathbb{Z}[Q]$-resolution$Q_\bullet \to \mathbb{Z}$. Since$N$ acts on$P_\bullet$ via the inclusion$N \hookrightarrow G$, and$Q = G/N$ acts on the$N$-coinvariants$(P_\bullet)_N$, one can form a double complex and filter by the$Q$-resolution. The$E_2$ page then computes$H^p(Q, H^q(N, M))$ as described in Section 41.3.2.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** $G = \mathbb{Z}/mn$,$N = \mathbb{Z}/n$ (the subgroup of order$n$),$Q = G/N \cong \mathbb{Z}/m$. The LHS spectral sequence relates$H^*(\mathbb{Z}/mn, M)$ to$H^*(\mathbb{Z}/m, (H^*(\mathbb{Z}/n, M))$. For$M = \mathbb{Z}$ with trivial action, this is the standard tool for computing the cohomology of cyclic groups.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** $G = S_3$ (symmetric group on 3 elements),$N = A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$ (alternating group),$Q = G/N \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. The extension$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/3 \to S_3 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 1$ is non-split (as a direct product) but splits as a semidirect product. The LHS spectral sequence has$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/2, H^q(\mathbb{Z}/3, M))$ and gives a systematic way to compute$H^*(S_3, M)$ from the cohomologies of$\mathbb{Z}/3$ and$\mathbb{Z}/2$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Galois-theoretic setup: $G = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^n})/\mathbb{Q})$ for a prime$p$,$N = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^n})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p))$ (the Galois group of the$p^n$th cyclotomic field over the$p$th cyclotomic field),$Q = G/N \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times$. The LHS spectral sequence computes the Galois cohomology of the tower, relevant to Iwasawa theory.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The direct product case: if $G = N \times Q$ (direct product), then the extension splits:$1 \to N \to N \times Q \to Q \to 1$. In this case, the LHS spectral sequence degenerates at$E_2$ (all differentials$d_r = 0$ for$r \geq 2$), and the Künneth formula gives
$$H^n(N \times Q, M) \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} H^p(Q, H^q(N, M))$$
(under suitable conditions on $M$, e.g., if$M$ has trivial$G$-action and is a$\mathbb{Q}$-vector space so all Tor corrections vanish). For coefficient field$k$ with trivial action, this gives$H^*(G, k) \cong H^*(N, k) \otimes_k H^*(Q, k)$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The LHS spectral sequence was discovered independently by Lyndon (1948), Hochschild–Serre (1953), and earlier (implicitly) by Koszul. The spectral sequence is a direct analogue of the Serre spectral sequence in topology, with the group extension $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ playing the role of a fibration$F \to E \to B$, the normal subgroup$N$ playing the role of the fiber, and the quotient$Q$ playing the role of the base. This analogy is made precise by the classifying space construction: the spectral sequence for the fibration$BN \to BG \to BQ$ (of classifying spaces) is the Serre spectral sequence, and it coincides with the LHS spectral sequence in cohomology.
\end{remark}
