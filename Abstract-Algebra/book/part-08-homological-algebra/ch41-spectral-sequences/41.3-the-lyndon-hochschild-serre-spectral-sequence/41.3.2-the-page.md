# 41.3.2 The $E_2$ Page:$H^p(Q, H^q(N,M))$

The $E_2$ page of the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence is
$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Q, H^q(N, M)),$$
where $H^q(N, M)$ is regarded as a$Q$-module via the conjugation action. This formula is the content of the spectral sequence: the second page is assembled by taking the cohomology of$N$ with coefficients in$M$ (varying the degree$q$), then taking the cohomology of$Q$ with coefficients in those groups (varying the degree$p$).

The $Q$-module structure on$H^q(N, M)$ requires explanation. Since$N$ is normal in$G$, the quotient$Q = G/N$ acts on$N$ by conjugation:$\bar{g} \cdot n = gng^{-1}$ for$g \in G$,$n \in N$,$\bar{g} \in Q$. This conjugation action on$N$ induces an action on the cochains$C^q(N, M) = \mathrm{Map}(N^q, M)$ by: for$c: N^q \to M$ and$\bar{g} \in Q$, define$(\bar{g} \cdot c)(n_1, \ldots, n_q) = g \cdot c(g^{-1}n_1 g, \ldots, g^{-1}n_q g)$ (using both the action of$g$ on$M$ and the conjugation of$g$ on$N$). This action preserves the cocycle and coboundary conditions and hence descends to an action on cohomology$H^q(N, M)$.

The differentials $d_r: E_r^{p,q} \to E_r^{p+r, q-r+1}$ for$r \geq 2$ are the *transgression* maps. Computing these differentials explicitly is generally difficult:$d_2$ can sometimes be identified with a known cohomological operation (such as the connecting homomorphism in a related long exact sequence or a cup product with the extension class), but$d_3$ and beyond are typically computed indirectly, by comparison with other known computations or by vanishing arguments.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** The $E_2$ page for$G = \mathbb{Z}/4$,$N = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (central subgroup),$Q = \mathbb{Z}/2$, and$M = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (trivial action). The$Q$-action on$N \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ is trivial (since$N$ is central). So$H^q(N, \mathbb{Z}/2) = H^q(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ for all$q \geq 0$ with trivial$Q$-action. The$E_2$ page is:
$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \quad \text{for all } p, q \geq 0.$$
The spectral sequence converges to $H^*(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/2)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** The $E_2$ page for$G = D_4$ (dihedral of order 8),$N = \mathbb{Z}/4$ (rotation subgroup),$Q = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (generated by a reflection). The conjugation action of the reflection$r \in Q$ on$N = \langle s \rangle$ sends$s \mapsto s^{-1}$. So$Q$ acts on$N$ by inversion, hence acts on$H^q(N, M)$ by the induced action. For$M = \mathbb{Z}$ with trivial$G$-action:$H^0(N, \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}$,$H^1(N, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ (since$N$ acts trivially on$\mathbb{Z}$ and$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$),$H^2(N, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4$. The$Q$-action on$H^2(N, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4$: since inversion on$N$ corresponds to the sign representation,$r$ acts as$-1$ on$H^2(N, \mathbb{Z})$. So$E_2^{p,2} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/4)$ with$\mathbb{Z}/2$ acting by$-1$ on$\mathbb{Z}/4$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The $E_2$ page for a direct product. If$G = N \times Q$ (direct product), then the conjugation action of$Q$ on$N$ is trivial (since elements of$N$ and$Q$ commute). Thus$H^q(N, M)$ is a trivial$Q$-module, and$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Q, H^q(N, M)) = H^p(Q, \mathbb{Z}) \otimes H^q(N, M)$ (when$H^q(N, M)$ is torsion-free, by the UCT). In general,$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Q, k) \otimes_k H^q(N, k)$ over a field$k$, giving the Künneth decomposition.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The transgression $d_2$ for the extension$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to \mathbb{Z}/p^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to 1$ with$M = \mathbb{Z}/p$ (trivial action). Here$N = \mathbb{Z}/p$,$Q = \mathbb{Z}/p$, and$H^q(\mathbb{Z}/p, \mathbb{Z}/p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$ for all$q \geq 0$ with trivial$Q$-action. The$E_2$ page has$E_2^{p,q} \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$ for all$p, q \geq 0$. The spectral sequence converges to$H^*(\mathbb{Z}/p^2, \mathbb{Z}/p)$, which is$\mathbb{Z}/p$ in every degree. For the spectral sequence to give the correct answer, many differentials$d_2$ must be non-zero: specifically$d_2: E_2^{0, 2k} \to E_2^{2, 2k-1}$ must be an isomorphism for$k \geq 1$, killing almost all of the$E_2$ page.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The $Q$-module structure on$H^q(N, M)$ is sometimes called the "Hochschild–Serre action" and is a key piece of data for the spectral sequence. In the case where$N$ is abelian, the action simplifies considerably:$Q$ acts on$N$ by group automorphisms (via conjugation), and this induces a natural action on$H^q(N, M)$ that can often be computed explicitly. For$N$ non-abelian, the conjugation action on$H^q(N, M)$ is more subtle but still defined by the formula above.
\end{remark}
