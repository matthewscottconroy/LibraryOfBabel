# 41.3.3 Convergence to $H^{p+q}(G,M)$

The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence converges to the cohomology of $G$:
$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Q, H^q(N,M)) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(G,M).$$
This convergence statement is the central theorem of the subject: it asserts that $H^n(G, M)$ carries a filtration
$$H^n(G,M) = F^0 H^n \supseteq F^1 H^n \supseteq \cdots \supseteq F^n H^n \supseteq F^{n+1} H^n = 0,$$
with associated graded pieces $F^p H^n / F^{p+1} H^n \cong E_\infty^{p, n-p}$. Thus the cohomology of$G$ in degree$n$ is built up from subquotients of the groups$H^p(Q, H^q(N, M))$ with$p + q = n$, filtered by the transgressions.

The significance of this convergence is both theoretical and computational. Theoretically, it shows that the cohomology of a group extension is completely determined — in principle — by the cohomologies of its subquotients and the extension data (encoded in the differentials). This is a profound organizational principle: the cohomology of a complicated group $G$ can be attacked by decomposing$G$ into simpler pieces and tracking how those pieces assemble. Computationally, when the spectral sequence degenerates at$E_2$ — which occurs, for example, when the extension splits or when all higher Ext groups between the relevant cohomology groups vanish — the convergence gives$H^n(G, M) \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} H^p(Q, H^q(N, M))$, a complete description in terms of the cohomologies of the factors.

The extension problem remains even after convergence: knowing $E_\infty^{p,q}$ determines the associated graded of$H^n(G, M)$, not$H^n(G, M)$ itself. In favorable cases (over a field, or when the filtration splits for other reasons), the convergence gives a direct decomposition.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Convergence for $G = \mathbb{Z}/4$. The LHS spectral sequence for$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 1$ with$M = \mathbb{F}_2$ gives$E_2^{p,q} \cong \mathbb{F}_2$ for all$p, q \geq 0$. The convergence says$H^n(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{F}_2) \cong \mathbb{F}_2$ for all$n \geq 0$. Since$\sum_{p+q=n} \dim E_2^{p,q} = n + 1 > 1$ for$n > 0$, but the target has dimension 1, the spectral sequence cannot degenerate at$E_2$ — many differentials must be non-zero to reduce the$E_n$ pages down to the correct answer.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Degeneration for a split extension. If $G = N \rtimes Q$ (semidirect product), the extension$1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ splits as a group extension (the map$Q \to G$ sending$q \mapsto (1, q)$ is a splitting). In this case, the spectral sequence degenerates at$E_2$ for suitable coefficients. For example, if$M$ is a$\mathbb{Q}$-vector space (so all torsion vanishes), then all differentials$d_r$ for$r \geq 2$ are zero, and$H^n(G, M) \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} H^p(Q, H^q(N, M))$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The filtration on $H^1(G, M)$. The filtration gives$H^1(G, M) = F^0 H^1 \supseteq F^1 H^1 \supseteq F^2 H^1 = 0$. Then$F^0/F^1 \cong E_\infty^{0,1} \subseteq E_2^{0,1} = H^1(N, M)^Q$ (the$Q$-invariants in$H^1(N, M)$, since$E_2^{0,1} = H^0(Q, H^1(N,M)) = H^1(N,M)^Q$) and$F^1 H^1 \cong E_\infty^{1,0} \subseteq E_2^{1,0} = H^1(Q, M^N)$. The 5-term exact sequence (Section 41.3.4) makes these inclusion/quotient maps explicit.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** Convergence for $H^2(G, M)$. The filtration gives$H^2 = F^0 \supseteq F^1 \supseteq F^2 \supseteq 0$ with:
- $F^0/F^1 \cong E_\infty^{0,2} \subseteq H^0(Q, H^2(N, M)) = H^2(N, M)^Q$,
- $F^1/F^2 \cong E_\infty^{1,1} \subseteq H^1(Q, H^1(N, M))$,
- $F^2 \cong E_\infty^{2,0} \subseteq H^2(Q, M^N)$.
This filtered structure on $H^2(G, M)$ encodes the three "sources" of second cohomology: cohomology of$N$, mixed cohomology, and cohomology of$Q$. When all differentials$d_2$ are zero, each source contributes directly as a direct summand.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The proof of convergence uses the identification of the filtration on $H^n(G, M)$ with the filtration by the "complexity" of cocycles. A$G$-cocycle$c: G^n \to M$ lies in$F^p H^n$ if it "looks like a$Q$-cocycle" to order$p$ — more precisely, if it vanishes whenever more than$n - p$ of its arguments lie in$N$. This definition of the filtration is compatible with the differential of the bar complex, and one can verify directly that the$E_2$ page computes$H^p(Q, H^q(N, M))$. The convergence then follows from the general theory of filtered complexes once one verifies the filtration is bounded.
\end{remark}
