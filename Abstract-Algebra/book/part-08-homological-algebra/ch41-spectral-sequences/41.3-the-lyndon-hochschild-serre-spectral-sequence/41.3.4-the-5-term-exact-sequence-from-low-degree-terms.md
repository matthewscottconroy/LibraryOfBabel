# 41.3.4 The 5-Term Exact Sequence from Low-Degree Terms

Even without computing the full spectral sequence, the low-degree terms of the LHS spectral sequence yield an exact sequence that is immediately useful in practice. This 5-term exact sequence (also called the *inflation-restriction exact sequence* in degree 1 and 2) can be read off from the $E_2$ page and$E_\infty$ page in small total degree, without needing to know any higher differentials.

The sequence is:
$$0 \to H^1(Q, M^N) \xrightarrow{\inf} H^1(G,M) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}} H^1(N,M)^Q \xrightarrow{d_2} H^2(Q, M^N) \xrightarrow{\inf} H^2(G,M).$$
Here $\inf$ denotes the inflation map (restriction of cocycles from$G/N = Q$ to$G$),$\mathrm{res}$ denotes the restriction map (from$G$-cocycles to$N$-cocycles), and$d_2$ is the transgression, which is the$d_2$ differential of the LHS spectral sequence at the position$E_2^{0,1}$.

To understand where this sequence comes from, one examines the $E_2$ page in small total degree. The groups of total degree 1 are$E_2^{1,0} = H^1(Q, M^N)$ and$E_2^{0,1} = H^0(Q, H^1(N,M)) = H^1(N,M)^Q$. The only possible differential in total degree 1 is$d_2: E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2,0}$, i.e.,$d_2: H^1(N,M)^Q \to H^2(Q, M^N)$. The convergence to$H^1(G,M)$ gives the filtration$H^1(G,M) = F^0 \supseteq F^1 \supseteq 0$ with$F^0/F^1 = E_\infty^{0,1}$ and$F^1 = E_\infty^{1,0}$. Reading out the relations between the maps inflation, restriction, and$d_2$ then yields the 5-term exact sequence.

The 5-term exact sequence is the standard first tool in any application of the LHS spectral sequence. It gives immediately useful information about $H^1$ and$H^2$ of$G$, and in many applications (especially in Galois theory and class field theory) these are the only degrees that matter.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Inflation-restriction for cyclic groups. Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/6$,$N = \mathbb{Z}/3$,$Q = \mathbb{Z}/2$, and$M = \mathbb{Z}$ with trivial action. Then$M^N = M^G = \mathbb{Z}$. The 5-term sequence is:
$$0 \to H^1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^1(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^1(\mathbb{Z}/3, \mathbb{Z})^{\mathbb{Z}/2} \to H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^2(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}).$$
Since $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for$n \geq 1$, all$H^1$ terms vanish. And$H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ and$H^2(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/6$. The inflation map$H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^2(\mathbb{Z}/6, \mathbb{Z})$ is the map$\mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/6$ induced by inflation — it is injective (since$3 | 6$ and the inflation is compatible with the degree-3 map).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** The Hochschild–Serre exact sequence for $G = S_3$,$N = A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$,$Q = \mathbb{Z}/2$,$M = \mathbb{Z}$. The 5-term sequence:
$$0 \to H^1(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^1(S_3, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^1(\mathbb{Z}/3, \mathbb{Z})^{\mathbb{Z}/2} \to H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^2(S_3, \mathbb{Z}).$$
All $H^1$ vanish (as$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$). The map$d_2: H^1(\mathbb{Z}/3, \mathbb{Z})^{\mathbb{Z}/2} \to H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z})$ has$H^1(\mathbb{Z}/3, \mathbb{Z}) = 0$, so the 5-term sequence gives$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to H^2(S_3, \mathbb{Z})$, i.e., the inflation$H^2(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^2(S_3, \mathbb{Z})$ is injective.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Application to detecting non-trivial extensions. Suppose the 5-term sequence gives a non-zero $d_2: H^1(N, M)^Q \to H^2(Q, M^N)$. Then the inflation map$\inf: H^2(Q, M^N) \to H^2(G, M)$ is not injective on the image of$d_2$. Concretely, elements of$H^2(Q, M^N)$ in the image of$d_2$ "disappear" in$H^2(G, M)$ — they are "inflated away" by the extension structure. This is the spectral sequence's way of saying that the extension$G$ of$Q$ by$N$ has killed certain cohomology classes.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The 5-term sequence and Hilbert 90. For $G = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ a finite Galois group acting on$M = L^\times$, Hilbert's Theorem 90 states$H^1(G, L^\times) = 0$. Applying the 5-term sequence to a subextension$K \subseteq F \subseteq L$ with$N = \mathrm{Gal}(L/F)$ and$Q = \mathrm{Gal}(F/K)$:
$$0 \to H^1(Q, (L^\times)^N) \to H^1(G, L^\times) \to H^1(N, L^\times)^Q \to \cdots.$$
By Hilbert 90, $H^1(G, L^\times) = H^1(N, L^\times) = 0$, and$(L^\times)^N = F^\times$. The sequence becomes$0 \to H^1(Q, F^\times) \to 0$, forcing$H^1(Q, F^\times) = H^1(\mathrm{Gal}(F/K), F^\times) = 0$ — recovering Hilbert 90 for the sub-extension$F/K$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The 5-term exact sequence is often stated more symmetrically as an "inflation-restriction sequence": for any normal subgroup $N \trianglelefteq G$ and$G$-module$M$,
$$0 \to H^1(G/N, M^N) \xrightarrow{\inf} H^1(G, M) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}} H^1(N, M)^{G/N} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{tra}} H^2(G/N, M^N) \xrightarrow{\inf} H^2(G, M),$$
where "tra" is the transgression. This sequence, together with the analogous long exact sequence in group homology, is the starting point for the theory of cohomological invariants of groups, Galois cohomology, and the Brauer group.
\end{remark}
