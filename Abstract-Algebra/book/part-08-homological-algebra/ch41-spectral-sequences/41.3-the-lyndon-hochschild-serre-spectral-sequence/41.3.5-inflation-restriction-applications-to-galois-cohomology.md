# 41.3.5 Inflation-Restriction; Applications to Galois Cohomology

The inflation and restriction maps are the edge homomorphisms of the LHS spectral sequence, and in the context of Galois cohomology they become fundamental tools connecting the arithmetic of different field extensions. Galois cohomology — the cohomology of absolute Galois groups with coefficients in Galois modules — is one of the central objects of modern number theory, and the LHS spectral sequence is the organizing framework for its structure.

The *inflation* map $\inf: H^n(Q, M^N) \to H^n(G, M)$ is induced by the quotient map$G \to Q = G/N$: a cocycle for the quotient group pulls back to a cocycle for the whole group via the surjection$G \twoheadrightarrow Q$. Concretely, if$c: Q^n \to M^N$ is a cocycle, then$\inf(c): G^n \to M$ is defined by$(\inf c)(g_1, \ldots, g_n) = c(\bar{g}_1, \ldots, \bar{g}_n)$ where$\bar{g}_i \in Q$ is the image of$g_i$. The *restriction* map$\mathrm{res}: H^n(G, M) \to H^n(N, M)$ is induced by the inclusion$N \hookrightarrow G$: a cocycle for$G$ restricts to a cocycle for the subgroup$N$ by restricting its domain. These maps appear on the bottom ($p$-direction) and left ($q$-direction) edges of the$E_\infty$ page, respectively.

In Galois cohomology, one takes $G = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)$ (the absolute Galois group of a field$K$),$N = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{K}/L)$ (the Galois group of an extension$L/K$), and$Q = G/N = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$. The module$M$ is typically a Galois module such as$\bar{K}^\times$,$\mu_n$ (the roots of unity), or an abelian variety. The 5-term exact sequence then reads:
$$0 \to H^1(\mathrm{Gal}(L/K), M^{\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{K}/L)}) \xrightarrow{\inf} H^1(G_K, M) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}} H^1(G_L, M)^{\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)} \xrightarrow{d_2} H^2(\mathrm{Gal}(L/K), M^{G_L}) \to \cdots$$
This sequence relates Galois cohomology over $K$ to Galois cohomology over the intermediate field$L$.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Hilbert's Theorem 90. For $G = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ a finite Galois extension with$M = L^\times$, the exact sequence$0 \to H^1(G, L^\times) \to H^1(\{1\}, L^\times) = 0$ (the restriction to the trivial subgroup is zero) is part of the Hochschild–Serre sequence, and combining with the 5-term sequence gives$H^1(G, L^\times) = 0$. More explicitly: a 1-cocycle$f: G \to L^\times$ satisfying$f(\sigma\tau) = f(\sigma) \cdot \sigma(f(\tau))$ for all$\sigma, \tau \in G$ is a coboundary$f(\sigma) = \sigma(a)/a$ for some$a \in L^\times$ — the additive "Normal Basis Theorem" version of Hilbert 90.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Kummer theory. Let $K$ contain a primitive$n$th root of unity$\zeta_n$, and consider$M = \mu_n$ (the$G_K = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)$-module of$n$th roots of unity, with$G_K$ acting via the cyclotomic character). By Hilbert 90,$H^1(G_K, \bar{K}^\times) = 0$. The long exact sequence for$0 \to \mu_n \to \bar{K}^\times \xrightarrow{x \mapsto x^n} \bar{K}^\times \to 0$ gives$H^1(G_K, \mu_n) \cong K^\times / (K^\times)^n$. The Kummer pairing$K^\times / (K^\times)^n \times \mathrm{Gal}(K(\sqrt[n]{K^\times})/K) \to \mu_n$ is then an isomorphism, showing that degree-$n$ cyclic extensions of$K$ are parametrized by$H^1(G_K, \mu_n) \cong K^\times/(K^\times)^n$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** The Brauer group. For a field $K$, the Brauer group$\mathrm{Br}(K)$ classifies central simple algebras over$K$ up to Morita equivalence. It can be computed as$H^2(G_K, \bar{K}^\times)$: by the long exact sequence for$0 \to \mu_n \to \bar{K}^\times \xrightarrow{n} \bar{K}^\times \to 0$ (using Hilbert 90 to show$H^1(G_K, \bar{K}^\times) = 0$), one gets$H^2(G_K, \mu_n) \cong \mathrm{Br}(K)[n]$ (the$n$-torsion of the Brauer group). For a local field$K$, class field theory gives$\mathrm{Br}(K) \cong H^2(G_K, \bar{K}^\times) \cong \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, with the inflation-restriction sequence relating the Brauer groups of$K$ and its extensions.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The Poitou–Tate exact sequence in global Galois cohomology. For a global field $K$ (number field or function field), the LHS spectral sequence for global and local cohomology leads to the 9-term Poitou–Tate exact sequence relating$H^i(G_K, M)$ for$i = 0, 1, 2$ to the Galois cohomology at each place$v$ of$K$. This is the arithmetic avatar of Poincaré duality, and it underlies the proof of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture in analytic rank 0, the study of Selmer groups, and Euler system arguments in Iwasawa theory.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
The interplay between inflation, restriction, and the transgression $d_2$ in Galois cohomology is the algebraic heart of much of modern number theory. The inflation-restriction exact sequence was first written down by Hochschild and Serre in 1953, and it has since become indispensable. In the context of the absolute Galois group$G_K$ of a number field$K$, the LHS spectral sequences for various normal subgroups (inertia groups, decomposition groups, wild ramification groups) organize the local-to-global principle: the cohomology of$G_K$ can be assembled, via these spectral sequences, from the cohomologies of the local Galois groups$G_{K_v}$ at each prime$v$. This is the functorial content of class field theory and its generalizations.
\end{remark}
