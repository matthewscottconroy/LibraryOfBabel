# 41.3 The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre Spectral Sequence

The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre (LHS) spectral sequence is the fundamental tool for computing the cohomology (and homology) of a group from data about a normal subgroup and the corresponding quotient. Given a short exact sequence of groups $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$ and a $G$-module $A$, the LHS spectral sequence has second page $E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Q; H^q(N; A))$ and converges to $H^{p+q}(G; A)$. The notation $E_2^{p,q} \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(G; A)$ encodes the convergence: the module $H^n(G; A)$ has a filtration whose associated graded pieces are the stable values $E_\infty^{p,q}$ with $p + q = n$. The $E_2$ page is itself a familiar object — it is the cohomology of the quotient group $Q$ with coefficients in the cohomology groups $H^q(N; A)$, on which $Q$ acts by conjugation in $G$.

The construction proceeds via the bar resolution. The group ring $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ is a module over $\mathbb{Z}[Q]$ (via the quotient map), and the bar resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ can be filtered by the "level" of the $Q$-component. The filtration produces a double complex, and the two spectral sequences of this double complex are the LHS spectral sequence and its cousin; only one is typically non-degenerate in the interesting range. The $E_1$ page is an acyclic complex in one direction (by the exactness of the bar resolution restricted to $N$), which after taking $N$-cohomology yields the $E_2$ page as stated. The differentials $d_r$ on subsequent pages are the "higher order" obstructions to the cohomology of $G$ splitting as a product of that of $N$ and $Q$.

The five-term exact sequence is the most immediately useful output of the LHS spectral sequence in low degrees. Reading off the edge homomorphisms and the transgression $d_2: E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2,0}$ from the $E_2$ page yields the exact sequence $H^1(Q, A^N) \xrightarrow{\text{inf}} H^1(G, A) \xrightarrow{\text{res}} H^1(N, A)^Q \xrightarrow{d_2} H^2(Q, A^N) \xrightarrow{\text{inf}} H^2(G, A)$. The maps "inf" (inflation) and "res" (restriction) are the natural maps induced by the quotient $G \to Q$ and the inclusion $N \hookrightarrow G$. The transgression $d_2$ is the first nontrivial differential of the spectral sequence, and its interpretation as measuring the obstruction to lifting $1$-cocycles from $N^Q$ to $G$ is a concrete illustration of how the spectral sequence encodes the interaction between the two layers of the extension. In Galois theory, this exact sequence specializes to the inflation-restriction exact sequence in Galois cohomology.
