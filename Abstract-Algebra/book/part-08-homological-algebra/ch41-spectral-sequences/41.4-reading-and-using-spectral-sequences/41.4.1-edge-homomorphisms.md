# 41.4.1 Edge Homomorphisms

In a first-quadrant spectral sequence converging to $H^*$, the groups along the bottom edge ($E_r^{n,0}$) and the left edge ($E_r^{0,n}$) are the simplest non-trivial terms, and they come with natural maps to and from$H^n$ called the *edge homomorphisms*. These maps are typically the first computable pieces of a spectral sequence and often have direct geometric or algebraic interpretations.

The bottom-edge homomorphism is a map $H^n \to E_\infty^{n,0}$. It arises because$E_\infty^{n,0}$ is a quotient of$E_2^{n,0}$, and the filtration on$H^n$ places$E_\infty^{n,0} \cong F^n H^n / F^{n+1} H^n = F^n H^n$ (the top piece of the filtration, since$F^{n+1} H^n = 0$). Thus there is a composite map$H^n \twoheadrightarrow F^n H^n \cong E_\infty^{n,0} \hookrightarrow E_2^{n,0}$, giving the bottom-edge map$H^n \to E_2^{n,0}$. It is well-defined because$E_\infty^{n,0}$ is a subquotient of$E_2^{n,0}$ (specifically, it is the intersection of all the kernels of differentials$d_r$ leaving from$(n, 0)$, which is a subgroup of$E_2^{n,0}$).

The left-edge homomorphism goes the other direction: $E_2^{0,n} \to H^n$. It arises because$E_2^{0,n}$ maps surjectively onto$E_\infty^{0,n}$ (since$E_\infty^{0,n}$ is a quotient of$E_2^{0,n}$), and$E_\infty^{0,n} \cong F^0 H^n / F^1 H^n$ is the "bottom" piece of the filtration. The map$E_\infty^{0,n} \to F^0 H^n = H^n$ (inclusion of a filtration piece) composed with the quotient$E_2^{0,n} \to E_\infty^{0,n}$ gives the left-edge map$E_2^{0,n} \to H^n$. This map need not be injective (since$E_\infty^{0,n}$ may be a proper quotient of$E_2^{0,n}$), and its image is the bottom piece$F^1 H^n / F^0 H^n$... no, its image is$F^0 H^n / F^1 H^n$ embedded in$H^n$.

In the LHS spectral sequence, the bottom edge is $E_2^{n,0} = H^n(Q, M^N)$ and the left edge is$E_2^{0,n} = H^n(N,M)^Q$. The bottom-edge map$H^n(G,M) \to H^n(Q, M^N)$ is the inflation map in reverse — more precisely, it is related to the co-restriction; and the left-edge map$H^n(N,M)^Q \to H^n(G,M)$ is related to the restriction map. Identifying and computing the edge homomorphisms is usually the first step in working with a new spectral sequence.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** Edge homomorphisms in the LHS spectral sequence for $1 \to N \to G \to Q \to 1$. The bottom edge gives a map$H^n(G, M) \to E_2^{n,0} = H^n(Q, M^N)$, which is the *inflation* map$\inf^*: H^n(G, M) \to H^n(Q, M^N)$... actually, the map goes the other direction from inflation. Inflation$\inf: H^n(Q, M^N) \to H^n(G, M)$ is the *left-edge-to-$H^n$* direction, not the bottom edge. The bottom-edge map$H^n(G, M) \to E_2^{n,0} = H^n(Q, M^N)$ is actually the restriction to the invariants, related to but not equal to the inflation. The two edge maps together with the differentials generate the 5-term exact sequence of Section 41.3.4.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Edge homomorphisms in the Serre spectral sequence for a fibration $F \to E \to B$. The bottom edge is$E_2^{n,0} = H^n(B; k)$ and the left edge is$E_2^{0,n} = H^n(F; k)$. The bottom-edge map$H^n(E; k) \to H^n(B; k)$ is the *pullback*$\pi^*$ (where$\pi: E \to B$ is the projection). The left-edge map$H^n(F; k) \to H^n(E; k)$ is the *restriction*$i^*$ (where$i: F \hookrightarrow E$ is the inclusion of the fiber). Thus the Serre spectral sequence organizes the maps$\pi^*$ and$i^*$ as edge homomorphisms.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Detecting the edge maps. In the Hodge spectral sequence for a smooth projective variety $X$ over$\mathbb{C}$, the$E_1$ page is$E_1^{p,q} = H^q(X, \Omega^p_X)$ and the spectral sequence degenerates at$E_1$ (by Deligne's theorem). The left edge$E_1^{0,q} = H^q(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ maps to$H^q(X; \mathbb{C})$ via the left-edge map, and the bottom edge$E_1^{p,0} = H^0(X, \Omega^p_X)$ maps to$H^p(X; \mathbb{C})$ via the bottom-edge map. The injectivity of the left-edge map (the Hodge symmetry$H^q(X, \mathcal{O}_X) \hookrightarrow H^q(X; \mathbb{C})$) is part of the Hodge decomposition$H^n(X; \mathbb{C}) \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} H^q(X, \Omega^p_X)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** When the edge maps are not isomorphisms. In a non-degenerate spectral sequence, the bottom-edge map $H^n \to E_2^{n,0}$ is only a quotient map (the differential$d_r$ may kill parts of$E_r^{n,0}$ before stabilization). For instance, if$d_2: E_2^{n-2, 1} \to E_2^{n,0}$ is non-zero, then$E_3^{n,0}$ is a proper quotient of$E_2^{n,0}$, and the bottom-edge map$H^n \to E_\infty^{n,0}$ factors through this quotient. In the Serre spectral sequence for$\Omega S^n \to PS^n \to S^n$, the differential$d_n: E_n^{0, n-1} \to E_n^{n, 0}$ is the key differential, and it forces the bottom-edge map$H^n(PS^n) \to H^n(S^n)$ to be zero (since$PS^n$ is contractible,$H^n(PS^n) = 0$), consistent with the spectral sequence killing$E_n^{n,0}$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
Edge homomorphisms are "free" information from a spectral sequence — they require no knowledge of any differential, only of the convergence and filtration structure. In many applications, the edge homomorphisms are the primary objects of interest (e.g., the restriction and inflation maps in Galois cohomology, or the pullback and restriction in the Serre spectral sequence), and the full spectral sequence is used only to constrain or compute them. This is why the 5-term exact sequence (which is assembled entirely from edge homomorphisms and the single $d_2$ differential) is so widely applicable: it requires only the$E_2$ page and the first differential to extract useful information.
\end{remark}
