# 41.4.2 The Extension Problem: From $E_\infty$ to$H^*$

A spectral sequence computation, even when carried out completely to the $E_\infty$ page, does not in general determine$H^n$ — it determines the associated graded of$H^n$ with respect to the filtration. Recovering$H^n$ from its associated graded is the *extension problem*, and it is a genuine algebraic obstruction that must be addressed separately. Understanding the extension problem, and how to circumvent or solve it in specific cases, is one of the essential skills in working with spectral sequences.

The issue is the following. The filtration on $H^n$ is a chain$H^n = F^0 H^n \supseteq F^1 H^n \supseteq \cdots \supseteq F^n H^n \supseteq 0$, with$E_\infty^{p, n-p} \cong F^p H^n / F^{p+1} H^n$ for each$p$. Knowing the graded pieces$E_\infty^{p, n-p}$ means knowing all the "layers" of$H^n$, but not how they fit together: there are successive group extensions
$$0 \to F^{p+1} H^n / F^{p+2} H^n \to F^p H^n / F^{p+2} H^n \to F^p H^n / F^{p+1} H^n \to 0,$$
and whether these split depends on the specific situation. The extension problem asks, for each step in the filtration, whether the corresponding extension is split or non-split.

Over a field $k$, all short exact sequences of$k$-vector spaces split, so$H^n \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=n} E_\infty^{p,q}$ — the extension problem has no content. Over$\mathbb{Z}$, extensions of abelian groups can be genuinely non-trivial: for instance,$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$ is a non-split extension, so knowing the associated graded$\mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$ does not determine whether the group is$\mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$ or$\mathbb{Z}/4$. Several strategies are available for resolving the extension problem in practice.

The most powerful strategy is to use additional structure on the spectral sequence. A *multiplicative structure* on the spectral sequence (when $H^*$ is a graded ring and the spectral sequence is one of graded algebras) means that the extension problem for the multiplicative structure is often forced: the ring structure of$H^*$ constrains the extensions, since products of generators must satisfy the ring axioms. In the Serre spectral sequence for a fibration, the cup product structure on$H^*(E; k)$ is compatible with the spectral sequence, and this often pins down the extensions completely.

\begin{example}
**Example 1.** The non-trivial extension in $H^*(K(\mathbb{Z}/2, 2); \mathbb{Z})$. Computing$H^*(K(\mathbb{Z}/2, 2); \mathbb{Z})$ (cohomology of the Eilenberg–Mac Lane space) via the Serre spectral sequence for the fibration$K(\mathbb{Z}/2, 1) \to PK(\mathbb{Z}/2, 2) \to K(\mathbb{Z}/2, 2)$ (with contractible total space), one finds$E_\infty^{0,2} = \mathbb{Z}/2$ and$E_\infty^{2,0} = \mathbb{Z}/2$ as the pieces of$H^2(K(\mathbb{Z}/2, 2); \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4$ — a non-split extension. The$E_\infty$ page alone suggests either$\mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$ or$\mathbb{Z}/4$, and only the multiplicative structure resolves the ambiguity.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 2.** Order arguments. Suppose $H^n(G, M)$ is known to be a finite group of order divisible by a prime$p$, and the$E_\infty$ page has two terms of total degree$n$:$E_\infty^{0,n} = \mathbb{Z}/p$ and$E_\infty^{n,0} = \mathbb{Z}/p$. The extension$0 \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to H^n(G,M) \to \mathbb{Z}/p \to 0$ is either$\mathbb{Z}/p \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p$ or$\mathbb{Z}/p^2$. If$H^n(G,M)$ is known to have exponent$p$ (e.g., because$|G|$ is prime and the action is trivial), then$H^n(G,M) = \mathbb{Z}/p \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p$. If additional information forces exponent$p^2$, then$H^n(G,M) = \mathbb{Z}/p^2$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 3.** Comparison with a direct computation. For the group $G = \mathbb{Z}/4$ and$M = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (trivial action), one can compute$H^n(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{Z}/2)$ directly from the periodic resolution of$\mathbb{Z}$ over$\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/4]$: the result is$\mathbb{Z}/2$ in every degree$n \geq 0$. The LHS spectral sequence for$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 1$ gives$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(\mathbb{Z}/2, H^q(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2)) = \mathbb{F}_2$ for all$p, q \geq 0$ (since$\mathbb{Z}/2$ acts trivially on$H^*(\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{F}_2) = \mathbb{F}_2[x]$). The extension problem is resolved by comparison:$H^n(\mathbb{Z}/4, \mathbb{F}_2) = \mathbb{F}_2$, so all filtration steps are split (each graded piece$\mathbb{F}_2$ extends trivially).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
**Example 4.** The universal coefficient theorem as an extension problem. The universal coefficient theorem $0 \to H_n(C) \otimes G \to H_n(C; G) \to \mathrm{Tor}_1(H_{n-1}(C), G) \to 0$ is itself an extension problem: it says$H_n(C; G)$ is an extension of$\mathrm{Tor}_1(H_{n-1}(C), G)$ by$H_n(C) \otimes G$. The sequence splits (unnaturally), so the extension problem is trivially resolved in this case — but the splitting is not functorial, meaning that different choices of splitting can give different (though isomorphic) splittings of$H_n(C; G)$.
\end{example}
\par
\begin{remark}
The extension problem is in principle solvable using the theory of extensions and Ext groups: the extension $0 \to A \to H \to B \to 0$ is classified by$\mathrm{Ext}^1(B, A)$, so knowing$\mathrm{Ext}^1(E_\infty^{p,q}, E_\infty^{p+1, q-1})$ tells one the possible extensions at each step in the filtration. But in practice, computing these Ext groups is no easier than computing the original cohomology groups. The most efficient approaches are therefore either (1) structural arguments that force the extensions to split (over a field, or when the groups involved have coprime orders), (2) multiplicative structure constraints, or (3) comparison with an independent computation. The extension problem is the final "hard" step in any spectral sequence computation, and its resolution often requires more ingenuity than all the earlier steps combined.
\end{remark}
