# 41.4 Reading and Using Spectral Sequences

Knowing the formal definition of a spectral sequence is necessary but not sufficient for using one effectively. The practical art lies in extracting information from a spectral sequence even when full knowledge of all differentials is unavailable — and in many computations, the most important task is to argue that differentials must vanish for degree or rank reasons, collapsing the spectral sequence before its differentials are individually identified. This section develops the tools for reading and manipulating spectral sequences: edge homomorphisms, the extension problem from $E_\infty$ to $H_*$, and collapse arguments.

The edge homomorphisms are natural maps connecting the spectral sequence to the target. In a first-quadrant spectral sequence converging to $H_*$, the bottom row $E_r^{p,0}$ consists of modules that can only receive differentials from modules that eventually leave the first quadrant; hence the differentials into $E_r^{p,0}$ eventually vanish, and there is a surjection $E_2^{p,0} \twoheadrightarrow E_\infty^{p,0}$. Similarly, the left column $E_r^{0,q}$ can only send differentials to modules that leave the first quadrant, and there is an injection $E_\infty^{0,q} \hookrightarrow E_2^{0,q}$. Since $E_\infty^{p,0}$ is a subquotient of $H_p$ (the bottom filtration piece) and $E_\infty^{0,q}$ maps to a quotient of $H_q$ (the top filtration piece), the edge homomorphisms provide natural maps $H_p \to E_2^{p,0}$ and $E_2^{0,q} \to H_q$. In the Serre spectral sequence, these are the maps $H_*(E) \to H_*(B)$ (push-forward along the fibration) and $H_*(F) \to H_*(E)$ (pull-back from the fiber).

Even when the spectral sequence does not fully collapse, the $E_\infty$ page determines $H_*$ only up to extensions: each $H_n$ has a filtration $0 = F_{-1} H_n \subset F_0 H_n \subset \cdots \subset F_n H_n = H_n$ with $E_\infty^{p,q} \cong F_p H_n / F_{p-1} H_n$ for $p + q = n$. Knowing the associated graded of $H_n$ does not uniquely determine $H_n$ as a module: the extension problem asks which module extension class connects successive filtration pieces. Over fields this problem is trivial (all extensions split), but over $\mathbb{Z}$ it is often the final and hardest step in a spectral sequence computation. Collapse arguments — showing that for sparsity reasons all differentials on page $E_r$ and beyond must be zero — bypass the extension problem entirely, and recognizing when a collapse argument applies (e.g., when $E_r^{p,q}$ is torsion-free in complementary positions) is one of the key skills in homological computation.
