# 42.1 Representations

The central idea of this section is deceptively simple: attach a vector space to a group. Formally, a representation of a group $G$ over a field $k$ is a group homomorphism $\rho: G \to GL(V)$, where $V$ is a $k$-vector space and $GL(V)$ is the group of invertible linear transformations on $V$. Every element $g \in G$ becomes an invertible matrix, every group relation becomes a matrix identity, and the abstract symmetry encoded in $G$ acquires a concrete linear-algebraic incarnation. The dimension of $V$ is called the degree of the representation, and when $V = k^n$ with a chosen basis, each $\rho(g)$ is literally a matrix in $GL_n(k)$.

The second definition — equivalent but differently flavored — packages the same data as a module. Recall the group algebra $k[G]$: the $k$-vector space with basis $\{e_g : g \in G\}$ and multiplication defined by $e_g \cdot e_h = e_{gh}$, extended bilinearly. A $k[G]$-module structure on $V$ is precisely a representation of $G$ on $V$: the action of $e_g$ on $V$ is the linear map $\rho(g)$. This reformulation is not merely a notational convenience. It allows the full machinery of module theory — submodules, quotient modules, direct sums, tensor products, Hom — to be brought to bear on representation theory. Throughout this chapter and the next, the two languages will alternate freely according to which makes a given argument most transparent.

The section develops the basic vocabulary that all further work depends upon. A $G$-equivariant map (or intertwiner) between representations $(\rho, V)$ and $(\sigma, W)$ is a linear map $\phi: V \to W$ satisfying $\phi \circ \rho(g) = \sigma(g) \circ \phi$ for all $g \in G$; these are precisely the $k[G]$-module homomorphisms, and they constitute the morphisms in the category of $G$-representations. Two representations are isomorphic if there is an invertible intertwiner between them — that is, if they describe the same abstract action in different coordinate systems. Subrepresentations and quotient representations complete the picture: a subrepresentation is a $G$-stable subspace, and the quotient construction works exactly as for modules. The direct sum and tensor product of representations, defined component-wise and via the tensor product of the underlying spaces respectively, provide the algebraic operations that make the set of representations into a rich structure.
