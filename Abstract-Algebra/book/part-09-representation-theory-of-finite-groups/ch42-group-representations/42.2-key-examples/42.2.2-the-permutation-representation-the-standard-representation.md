# 42.2.2 The Permutation Representation; the Standard Representation

One of the most natural ways for a group to arise is as a group of symmetries of a set — permuting the elements of that set. Representation theory begins by *linearizing* this situation: rather than permuting basis vectors, the group acts on the vector space spanned by those basis vectors. This construction produces the permutation representation, and its decomposition reveals the first genuine decomposition of a reducible representation.

**Construction.** Whenever a group $G$ acts on a finite set$X = \{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}$ (on the left, via a group homomorphism$G \to \mathrm{Sym}(X)$), define the *permutation representation* as follows. Take$V = k^X$, the$k$-vector space with basis$\{e_{x_1}, \ldots, e_{x_n}\}$ indexed by the elements of$X$, and define
$$\rho(g)(e_{x_i}) = e_{g \cdot x_i},$$
extended by linearity to all of $V$. Each$\rho(g)$ is a permutation matrix with respect to this basis: exactly one$1$ in each row and column, all other entries$0$. The map$g \mapsto \rho(g)$ is clearly a group homomorphism.

\begin{definition}
The \emph{permutation representation} of $G$ on$k^X$ is$\rho(g)(e_x) = e_{g \cdot x}$.
\end{definition}

**The trivial summand.** The permutation representation is never irreducible when $|X| \geq 2$ and the action is transitive. The reason is elementary: the vector$\mathbf{1} = e_{x_1} + \cdots + e_{x_n}$ is fixed by every$g \in G$, since$\rho(g)(\mathbf{1}) = \sum_i e_{g \cdot x_i} = \sum_{y \in X} e_y = \mathbf{1}$ (the action simply permutes the basis vectors). So$U = k \cdot \mathbf{1} \cong k$ is a$G$-stable 1-dimensional subspace carrying the trivial representation.

**The standard representation.** The orthogonal complement to the trivial summand (over $k = \mathbb{C}$, or more generally when$n$ is invertible in$k$) is the *standard representation*:
$$W = \ker\!\left(\textstyle\sum_{i} e_i^*\right) = \left\{ \sum_i a_i e_{x_i} : \sum_i a_i = 0 \right\},$$
the hyperplane of vectors whose coordinates sum to zero. This is $G$-stable: if$\sum_i a_i = 0$, then$\rho(g)(\sum_i a_i e_{x_i}) = \sum_i a_i e_{g \cdot x_i}$, and reindexing gives$\sum_{y \in X} a_{\sigma^{-1}(y)} e_y$; since the sum of the coefficients is unchanged, the vector stays in$W$. Maschke's theorem (§43.1) guarantees that$V = U \oplus W$ as representations.

The standard representation of $S_n$ on$\{1, \ldots, n\}$ has dimension$n-1$. It is irreducible for all$n \geq 2$ (when the characteristic of$k$ does not divide$n$), making it one of the key irreducibles in the representation theory of symmetric groups.

**Computing the character.** The character of the permutation representation has a beautiful combinatorial formula: $\chi_{\mathrm{perm}}(g) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(g)) = |\mathrm{Fix}(g)|$, the number of fixed points of$g$ in$X$. This is because$\rho(g)$ is a permutation matrix, and the diagonal entry in row$e_{x_i}$ is$1$ if$g(x_i) = x_i$ and$0$ otherwise. The character of the standard representation is then$\chi_W(g) = |\mathrm{Fix}(g)| - 1$.

**Example 1.** $G = S_3$ acting on$X = \{1, 2, 3\}$ by the natural action. The character of the permutation representation:
\begin{align*}
\chi_{\mathrm{perm}}(e) &= 3 \text{ (all three fixed)}, \\
\chi_{\mathrm{perm}}((12)) &= 1 \text{ (only 3 fixed)}, \\
\chi_{\mathrm{perm}}((123)) &= 0 \text{ (none fixed)}.
\end{align*}
The standard representation has character values $3-1=2$,$1-1=0$,$0-1=-1$. Using the inner product to check irreducibility:$\langle \chi_W, \chi_W \rangle = \frac{1}{6}(4 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 3 + 1 \cdot 2) = 1$, confirming$W$ is irreducible.

**Example 2.** $G = S_4$ acting on$X = \{1,2,3,4\}$. The character of the permutation representation by conjugacy class:

| Conjugacy class | $e$ |$(12)$ |$(12)(34)$ |$(123)$ |$(1234)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $|\mathrm{Fix}|$ |$4$ |$2$ |$0$ |$1$ |$0$ |

So $\chi_{\mathrm{perm}} = (4, 2, 0, 1, 0)$ on the five conjugacy classes. The standard representation has character$(3, 1, -1, 0, -1)$. One verifies$\langle \chi_W, \chi_W \rangle = \frac{1}{24}(9+12+4\cdot 3+8\cdot 0+6\cdot 1) = \frac{1}{24}(9+12+12+0+6) = \frac{39}{24}$... Let us recheck: the conjugacy class sizes in$S_4$ are$1, 6, 3, 8, 6$. So$\langle \chi_W, \chi_W \rangle = \frac{1}{24}(1 \cdot 9 + 6 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 1 + 8 \cdot 0 + 6 \cdot 1) = \frac{19}{24}$... this does not give 1, which indicates the standard representation of$S_4$ is irreducible (let us recompute more carefully). The standard representation character values are:$\chi_W(e)=3$,$\chi_W((12))=1$,$\chi_W((12)(34))=-1$,$\chi_W((123))=0$,$\chi_W((1234))=-1$. The norm:$\frac{1}{24}(1 \cdot 9 + 6 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 1 + 8 \cdot 0 + 6 \cdot 1) = \frac{19}{24}$? No:$1 \cdot 9 + 6 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 1 + 8 \cdot 0 + 6 \cdot 1 = 9+6+3+0+6 = 24$. So$\langle \chi_W, \chi_W \rangle = 24/24 = 1$: the standard representation of$S_4$ is indeed irreducible.

**Example 3.** $G = D_4$ acting on the four vertices$\{1,2,3,4\}$ of a square (labeled clockwise). The rotation$r$ by$90°$ acts as the 4-cycle$(1234)$, with 0 fixed points. The reflection$s$ fixing the midpoints of edges acts as$(13)$ on$\{1,3\}$ and fixes$\{2,4\}$... actually in the vertex-labeling, reflections fix either 0 or 2 vertices. The permutation representation$k^4$ decomposes as: the trivial representation (spanned by$\sum_i e_i$), the sign-of-rotation representation, and a 2-dimensional block. One can verify that the 3-dimensional standard representation$W$ of this action is *not* irreducible as a$D_4$-representation: it further splits into a 1-dimensional piece and a 2-dimensional irreducible.

**Example 4.** $G = A_4$ acting on$X = \{1,2,3,4\}$. The conjugacy classes of$A_4$ are:$\{e\}$,$\{(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}$,$\{(123),(134),(142),(243)\}$,$\{(132),(143),(124),(234)\}$. Fixed point counts:$e$ fixes 4; each$(ab)(cd)$ fixes 0; each 3-cycle fixes 1. So$\chi_{\mathrm{perm}} = (4,0,1,1)$ and$\chi_W = (3,-1,0,0)$. Checking:$\langle \chi_W, \chi_W \rangle = \frac{1}{12}(9 + 3 \cdot 1 + 4 \cdot 0 + 4 \cdot 0) = \frac{12}{12} = 1$. The standard representation of$A_4$ is irreducible.

The permutation representation is the gateway to more sophisticated constructions. It generalizes to the concept of an *induced representation* (Chapter 45): the permutation representation on $G/H$ is exactly$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H \mathbf{1}$, the representation induced from the trivial representation of a subgroup$H$. This connection transforms the study of permutation actions into the study of induced representations, a central theme of Chapter 45.
