# 44.1.3 Characters of Direct Sums, Tensor Products, and Duals

One of the most important features of the character is that it interacts cleanly with the natural operations on representations. When representations are combined, their characters combine in the simplest possible way. This algebraic compatibility is what makes characters so computationally powerful and theoretically natural.

\begin{proposition}[Character formulas]
Let $V$ and$W$ be representations of$G$ over$\mathbb{C}$. Then:
\begin{align*}
\chi_{V \oplus W}(g) &= \chi_V(g) + \chi_W(g) \quad \text{(direct sum)}, \\
\chi_{V \otimes W}(g) &= \chi_V(g) \cdot \chi_W(g) \quad \text{(tensor product)}, \\
\chi_{V^*}(g) &= \overline{\chi_V(g)} = \chi_V(g^{-1}) \quad \text{(dual/contragredient)}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Direct sum.} The matrix of $g$ on$V \oplus W$ in block form is$\begin{pmatrix}\rho_V(g) & 0 \\ 0 & \rho_W(g)\end{pmatrix}$, with trace$\mathrm{tr}(\rho_V(g)) + \mathrm{tr}(\rho_W(g)) = \chi_V(g) + \chi_W(g)$.

\textbf{Tensor product.} If $\rho_V(g)$ has eigenvalues$\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_m$ and$\rho_W(g)$ has eigenvalues$\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_n$, then$\rho_V(g) \otimes \rho_W(g)$ has eigenvalues$\{\lambda_i \mu_j\}_{i,j}$. Thus:
$$\chi_{V \otimes W}(g) = \sum_{i,j} \lambda_i \mu_j = \left(\sum_i \lambda_i\right)\left(\sum_j \mu_j\right) = \chi_V(g) \cdot \chi_W(g).$$

\textbf{Dual.} The dual representation $V^*$ has$\rho_{V^*}(g) = \rho_V(g^{-1})^T$ (the transpose-inverse). Since the eigenvalues of$A^T$ are the same as those of$A$, and the eigenvalues of$\rho_V(g)$ are roots of unity$\lambda_i$, the eigenvalues of$\rho_{V^*}(g)$ are$\lambda_i^{-1} = \overline{\lambda_i}$. Thus$\chi_{V^*}(g) = \sum \overline{\lambda_i} = \overline{\chi_V(g)} = \chi_V(g^{-1})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
For $G = S_3$: let$V_1$ = trivial,$V_2$ = sign,$V_3$ = standard. Then$V_1 \otimes V_2 \cong V_2$ (since$\chi_{V_1} \cdot \chi_{V_2} = 1 \cdot \chi_{V_2} = \chi_{V_2}$). Also,$V_2 \otimes V_3$:$\chi_{V_2 \otimes V_3}(e) = 1 \cdot 2 = 2$,$\chi_{V_2 \otimes V_3}((12)) = (-1)(0) = 0$,$\chi_{V_2 \otimes V_3}((123)) = (1)(-1) = -1$. These match$\chi_{V_3}$, so$V_2 \otimes V_3 \cong V_3$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $G = S_3$: compute$V_3 \otimes V_3$ where$V_3$ is the standard (2-dimensional) representation.$\chi_{V_3 \otimes V_3}(e) = 4$,$\chi_{V_3 \otimes V_3}((12)) = 0$,$\chi_{V_3 \otimes V_3}((123)) = 1$. Now decompose:$m_1 = \langle \chi_{V_3 \otimes V_3}, \chi_1 \rangle = \frac{1}{6}(1 \cdot 4 + 3 \cdot 1 \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 1) = \frac{6}{6} = 1$;$m_2 = \frac{1}{6}(1 \cdot 4 - 3 \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot 1) = 1$;$m_3 = \frac{1}{6}(2 \cdot 4 + 3 \cdot 0 - 2 \cdot 1) = 1$. So$V_3 \otimes V_3 \cong V_1 \oplus V_2 \oplus V_3$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $G = \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} = \langle \sigma \rangle$ with$\zeta = e^{2\pi i/3}$: the irreducibles are$V_k$ with$\chi_k(\sigma^j) = \zeta^{jk}$ for$k = 0, 1, 2$. Then$V_1 \otimes V_1$:$\chi_{V_1 \otimes V_1}(\sigma^j) = \zeta^j \cdot \zeta^j = \zeta^{2j} = \chi_2(\sigma^j)$. So$V_1 \otimes V_1 \cong V_2$. And$V_1^*$:$\chi_{V_1^*}(\sigma^j) = \overline{\zeta^j} = \zeta^{-j} = \zeta^{2j} = \chi_2(\sigma^j)$. So$V_1^* \cong V_2$.
\end{example}

These three formulas together mean that the character map $V \mapsto \chi_V$ is a ring homomorphism from the \emph{representation ring}$R(G)$ — where the ring structure is given by direct sum as addition and tensor product as multiplication — into the ring of class functions on$G$, where the ring structure is pointwise addition and multiplication. Explicitly:

$$\chi_{[V] + [W]} = \chi_V + \chi_W, \quad \chi_{[V] \cdot [W]} = \chi_V \cdot \chi_W.$$

This algebraic compatibility reflects the deep connection between the representation theory of $G$ and the function theory on its conjugacy classes. The character ring and its structure constants will be revisited in Section 44.4.4.

\begin{remark}
The symmetric and exterior powers of a representation also have character formulas. For the symmetric square $\mathrm{Sym}^2 V$:
$$\chi_{\mathrm{Sym}^2 V}(g) = \frac{1}{2}\left[(\chi_V(g))^2 + \chi_V(g^2)\right].$$
For the exterior square $\Lambda^2 V$:
$$\chi_{\Lambda^2 V}(g) = \frac{1}{2}\left[(\chi_V(g))^2 - \chi_V(g^2)\right].$$
These formulas arise from the decomposition $V \otimes V = \mathrm{Sym}^2 V \oplus \Lambda^2 V$ and the computation of eigenvalues on each summand.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}
For the standard representation $V_3$ of$S_3$ (2-dimensional): compute$\mathrm{Sym}^2 V_3$ and$\Lambda^2 V_3$.
\par
$\chi_{\mathrm{Sym}^2 V_3}(e) = \frac{1}{2}(4 + 2) = 3$;$\chi_{\mathrm{Sym}^2 V_3}((12)) = \frac{1}{2}(0 + \chi_3(e)) = \frac{1}{2}(0+2) = 1$;$\chi_{\mathrm{Sym}^2 V_3}((123)) = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \chi_3(e)) = \frac{1}{2}(1+2)$... wait:$(123)^2 = (132)$,$\chi_3((132)) = -1$. So$\chi_{\mathrm{Sym}^2 V_3}((123)) = \frac{1}{2}((-1)^2 + (-1)) = \frac{1}{2}(1-1) = 0$. Character$(3,1,0)$: decomposing over$S_3$,$m_1 = \frac{1}{6}(3+3+0)=1$,$m_2 = \frac{1}{6}(3-3+0)=0$,$m_3=\frac{1}{6}(6+0+0)=1$. So$\mathrm{Sym}^2 V_3 \cong V_1 \oplus V_3$ (trivial + standard).
\par
$\chi_{\Lambda^2 V_3}(e) = \frac{1}{2}(4-2) = 1$;$\chi_{\Lambda^2 V_3}((12)) = \frac{1}{2}(0-2) = -1$;$\chi_{\Lambda^2 V_3}((123)) = \frac{1}{2}(1-(-1)) = 1$. This is$\chi_2$ (sign representation). So$\Lambda^2 V_3 \cong V_2$, consistent with the fact that the determinant of any 2-dimensional representation of$S_3$ gives the sign character.
\end{example}

The tensor product formula also has implications for the structure of the representation ring $R(G)$. Since$\chi_V \cdot \chi_W = \chi_{V \otimes W}$, the pointwise product of two characters is always a character (with non-negative integer coefficients when expanded in irreducibles). This non-obvious positivity — pointwise products of characters decompose into actual representations — is guaranteed by the algebraic structure and would fail for arbitrary linear combinations of characters.
