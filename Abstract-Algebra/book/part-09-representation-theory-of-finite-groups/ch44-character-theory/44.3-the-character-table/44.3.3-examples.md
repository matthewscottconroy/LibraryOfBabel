# 44.3.3 Examples: $S_3$,$S_4$,$A_4$,$D_4$,$Q_8$

We construct the character tables of several key groups step by step, illustrating the general method.

**Example 1. $S_3$ (order 6).**

Conjugacy classes: $C_1 = \{e\}$ (size 1),$C_2 = \{(12),(13),(23)\}$ (size 3),$C_3 = \{(123),(132)\}$ (size 2). So$r = 3$ and dimensions satisfy$n_1^2 + n_2^2 + n_3^2 = 6$, forcing$(n_1,n_2,n_3) = (1,1,2)$.

The trivial character $\chi_1 \equiv 1$. The sign character$\chi_2$:$\chi_2(e) = 1$,$\chi_2(\text{transposition}) = -1$,$\chi_2(\text{3-cycle}) = 1$. The standard character$\chi_3$:$\chi_3(e) = 2$; column orthogonality with the first column gives$1+1+4=6 = |C_G(e)|$ \checkmark. For$\chi_3((12))$: from$\langle \chi_2, \chi_3 \rangle = 0$:$\frac{1}{6}(2 - 3\chi_3((12)) + 2\chi_3((123))) = 0$. From row norm:$1 + 3\chi_3((12))^2 + 2\chi_3((123))^2 = 6$. Trying$\chi_3((12)) = 0$: then$2\chi_3((123))^2 = 5$, not an integer. Trying$\chi_3((12)) = -1$: then$3 + 2\chi_3((123))^2 = 6$, so$\chi_3((123)) = \pm \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}$, not an algebraic integer of the right form. The correct approach: from the permutation representation,$\chi_{\mathrm{perm}} = \chi_1 + \chi_3$, so$\chi_3 = \chi_{\mathrm{perm}} - \chi_1$. Thus$\chi_3((12)) = 1-1=0$ and$\chi_3((123)) = 0-1=-1$.

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
S_3 & e & (12) & (123) \\
\hline
\chi_1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
\chi_2 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
\chi_3 & 2 & 0 & -1
\end{array}
\]

**Example 2. $D_4$ (order 8).**

$D_4 = \langle r, s \mid r^4 = s^2 = 1, srs^{-1} = r^{-1}\rangle$. Conjugacy classes:$\{1\}$,$\{r^2\}$,$\{r,r^3\}$,$\{s,r^2s\}$,$\{rs,r^3s\}$. Sizes:$1,1,2,2,2$. Five classes,$r=5$, dimension formula:$\sum n_i^2 = 8$ with$r=5$ forces$(1,1,1,1,2)$.

Four 1-dimensional characters come from $D_4^{\mathrm{ab}} = D_4/[D_4,D_4]$. Since$[D_4,D_4] = \{1,r^2\}$, the abelianization is$D_4/\{1,r^2\} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$. The four 1-dim characters take values$\pm 1$ on$r$ and$\pm 1$ on$s$. The 2-dimensional irreducible$\chi_5$ has$\chi_5(e)=2$. From column norm at$r^2$:$\sum_i |\chi_i(r^2)|^2 = |C_G(r^2)| = 8$, so$1+1+1+1+\chi_5(r^2)^2 = 8$, giving$\chi_5(r^2) = \pm 2$. Since$r^2$ is central,$\rho_5(r^2) = \lambda I$ and$\chi_5(r^2) = 2\lambda$. The element$r^2$ has order 2, so$\lambda = \pm 1$, giving$\chi_5(r^2) = \pm 2$. Using$\langle \chi_5, \chi_5 \rangle = 1$:$\frac{1}{8}(4 + \chi_5(r^2)^2 \cdot 1 + 0 + 0 + 0) = 1$ requires$\chi_5(r^2) = -2$.

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
D_4 & e & r^2 & r & s & rs \\
\hline
\chi_1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
\chi_2 & 1 & 1 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\
\chi_3 & 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 & -1 \\
\chi_4 & 1 & 1 & -1 & -1 & 1 \\
\chi_5 & 2 & -2 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}
\]

**Example 3. $Q_8$ (order 8).**

$Q_8 = \{\pm 1, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}$. Conjugacy classes:$\{1\}$,$\{-1\}$,$\{\pm i\}$,$\{\pm j\}$,$\{\pm k\}$. Sizes:$1,1,2,2,2$. Same count as$D_4$, same dimension vector$(1,1,1,1,2)$. The four 1-dim characters:$Q_8^{\mathrm{ab}} = Q_8/\{1,-1\} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$, same four characters as$D_4$. The 2-dim character:$\chi_5(-1) = -2$ (since$-1$ is central and acts as$-I$). But$\chi_5(\pm i) = \chi_5(\pm j) = \chi_5(\pm k) = 0$ by the row norm.

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
Q_8 & 1 & -1 & \pm i & \pm j & \pm k \\
\hline
\chi_1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
\chi_2 & 1 & 1 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\
\chi_3 & 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 & -1 \\
\chi_4 & 1 & 1 & -1 & -1 & 1 \\
\chi_5 & 2 & -2 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}
\]

The tables of $D_4$ and$Q_8$ look identical! Yet the groups are non-isomorphic. The difference lies in the group structure (e.g.,$D_4$ has elements of order 4 while$Q_8$ has elements of order 4 too, but their embedding differs), not in the character tables. This demonstrates that the character table does not always distinguish non-isomorphic groups, but it contains a great deal of information.

**Example 4. $S_4$ (order 24).** Five conjugacy classes with sizes$1, 6, 8, 6, 3$ (for$e$, transpositions, 3-cycles, 4-cycles, double transpositions). Dimensions$(1,1,2,3,3)$. The full table is:

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
S_4 & e & (12) & (123) & (1234) & (12)(34) \\
\hline
\chi_1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
\chi_2 & 1 & -1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
\chi_3 & 2 & 0 & -1 & 0 & 2 \\
\chi_4 & 3 & 1 & 0 & -1 & -1 \\
\chi_5 & 3 & -1 & 0 & 1 & -1
\end{array}
\]
