# 44.4.3 Detecting Normal Subgroups via Characters

A subgroup $N \leq G$ is normal if and only if it is a union of conjugacy classes, and the character table encodes conjugacy class membership through the structure of character values. This gives a precise and complete character-theoretic characterization of all normal subgroups of$G$.

\begin{definition}
For an irreducible character $\chi_i$, the \emph{kernel} of$\chi_i$ is
$$\ker \chi_i = \{g \in G : \chi_i(g) = \chi_i(e)\} = \{g \in G : \chi_i(g) = n_i\}.$$
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
$\ker \chi_i$ equals the kernel of the corresponding representation$\rho_i: G \to GL(V_i)$, and is therefore a normal subgroup of$G$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $g \in G$, the eigenvalues of$\rho_i(g)$ are roots of unity$\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_{n_i}$ with$|\lambda_k| = 1$. Then$\chi_i(g) = \sum_k \lambda_k$, and$|\chi_i(g)| \leq n_i = \chi_i(e)$ by the triangle inequality. Equality$\chi_i(g) = n_i$ (a real positive number) holds if and only if all$\lambda_k = 1$, i.e.,$\rho_i(g) = \mathrm{Id}_{V_i}$. So$\ker \chi_i = \ker \rho_i$.
\end{proof}

The deep result is the converse:

\begin{theorem}
Every normal subgroup of $G$ arises as an intersection of kernels of irreducible characters. Specifically, for any$N \trianglelefteq G$:
$$N = \bigcap_{\chi_i : N \subseteq \ker \chi_i} \ker \chi_i.$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $S = \{i : N \subseteq \ker \chi_i\}$. Clearly$N \subseteq \bigcap_{i \in S} \ker \chi_i$. For the reverse inclusion, consider the representation$W = \bigoplus_{i \notin S} V_i^{\oplus m_i}$ (some direct sum of irreducibles whose kernels do not contain$N$). Any$g \notin N$ satisfies$\rho_i(g) \neq \mathrm{Id}$ for some$i \notin S$ (by construction of$S$). So$g \notin \ker \chi_i$ for that$i$, hence$g \notin \bigcap_{i \in S} \ker \chi_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
$G$ is simple if and only if$\ker \chi_i = \{e\}$ for every non-trivial irreducible$\chi_i$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{example}
For $G = S_4$: the normal subgroups are$\{e\}$,$V_4 = \{e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}$,$A_4$, and$S_4$ itself. Reading from the character table:
\par
- $\ker \chi_1 = S_4$ (trivial character).
- $\ker \chi_2 = A_4$ (sign character:$\ker \mathrm{sgn} = A_4$).
- $\ker \chi_3 = V_4$: check$\chi_3(e) = 2$,$\chi_3((12)(34)) = 2$ (since the image of$V_4$ in$S_3 = S_4/V_4$ is the identity),$\chi_3((12)) = 0 \neq 2$. So$\ker \chi_3 \supseteq V_4$; by comparing sizes,$\ker \chi_3 = V_4$.
- $\ker \chi_4 = \{e\}$ and$\ker \chi_5 = \{e\}$ (the 3-dimensional representations are faithful).
\par
The three non-trivial normal subgroups $V_4$,$A_4$,$S_4$ are recovered as$\ker \chi_3$,$\ker \chi_2$,$\ker \chi_1$ respectively, and$\{e\} = \ker \chi_4 \cap \ker \chi_5$ (or any single faithful character).
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $G = A_4$: normal subgroups are$\{e\}$,$V_4$, and$A_4$. The four irreducibles have dimensions$(1,1,1,3)$. The three 1-dimensional characters have kernels containing$V_4$ (since the 1-dimensional characters factor through$A_4/V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, which has$V_4$ in the kernel). So$V_4 = \ker \chi_2 = \ker \chi_3 \cap \ker \chi_2$. The 3-dimensional character is faithful:$\ker \chi_4 = \{e\}$.
\end{example}

This approach makes normal subgroups readable from the character table without any further knowledge of the group. The procedure is complete: to find all normal subgroups of $G$, compute$\ker \chi_i$ for each irreducible and then form all possible intersections. Every normal subgroup appears exactly once in this procedure.

\begin{remark}
The connection between normal subgroups and characters has a refinement for abelian normal subgroups (Clifford theory). If $N \trianglelefteq G$ is abelian, then for any irreducible$V_i$ of$G$, the restriction$\mathrm{Res}^G_N V_i$ decomposes as a direct sum of characters of$N$ (all 1-dimensional since$N$ is abelian). These 1-dimensional constituents form a single orbit under the action of$G/N$ on$\hat{N}$ (the Pontryagin dual of$N$). This orbit structure — Clifford theory — governs how representations of$G$ restrict to normal subgroups and is the foundation for the Mackey machine described in Chapter 45.
\end{remark}
