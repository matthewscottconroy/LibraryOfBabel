# 45.1.2 Induced Representation $\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W = k[G] \otimes_{k[H]} W$

Restriction goes from $G$-representations down to$H$-representations by forgetting. The question of going in the opposite direction — building a$G$-representation from an$H$-representation — is answered by \emph{induction}. Given a representation$W$ of$H \leq G$, the induced representation$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$ constructs a$G$-representation that "extends"$W$ from$H$ to all of$G$ in the most natural way.

\begin{definition}
Let $H \leq G$ be a subgroup and$W$ a representation of$H$ over$k$. The \emph{induced representation} is
$$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W = k[G] \otimes_{k[H]} W.$$
Here $k[G]$ is viewed as a right$k[H]$-module via right multiplication, and the tensor product over$k[H]$ identifies$g \cdot h \otimes w$ with$g \otimes h \cdot w$ for$h \in H$.
\end{definition}

A more concrete description makes the construction visually accessible. Choose left coset representatives $g_1, g_2, \ldots, g_m$ of$H$ in$G$, where$m = [G:H]$. As a vector space:
$$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W \cong g_1 \otimes W \oplus g_2 \otimes W \oplus \cdots \oplus g_m \otimes W,$$
with each summand being a copy of $W$ indexed by a coset. The$G$-action is:
$$g \cdot (g_i \otimes w) = g_j \otimes (g_j^{-1} g g_i) \cdot w,$$
where $g_j$ is the unique coset representative of the coset$g g_i H$ (so$g_j^{-1} g g_i \in H$).

\begin{example}
Let $G = S_3$,$H = \langle (12) \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and$W = \mathrm{sign}$ (1-dimensional,$(12)$ acts as$-1$). The index$[G:H] = 3$, so$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$ has dimension 3. Coset representatives:$e, (123), (132)$. Compute the action of$(12)$:$(12) \cdot (e \otimes w) = e \otimes ((12)w) = e \otimes (-w)$.$(12) \cdot ((123) \otimes w) =$ need coset representative of$(12)(123) = (13)$'s coset in$\{e\}H, (123)H, (132)H$...$(13) \in (13)H = (132)(12)H = (132)H$, so$(12) \cdot ((123) \otimes w) = (132) \otimes ((132)^{-1}(12)(123) w)$. We have$(132)^{-1} = (123)$, so$(132)^{-1}(12)(123) = (123)(12)(123)$. Since$(123)(12)(123)^{-1} = (23)$ is a transposition,$(123)(12)(123) = (13)(123) =$ ... let's just note the resulting 3-dimensional representation. Computing its character:$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}(e) = 3$,$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}((12)) = -1$ (only coset$eH$ is stabilized, contributing$-1$),$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}((123)) = 0$ (no cosets stabilized). This is$\chi_3 - \chi_1 + \chi_2$... actually$\chi_1 + \chi_3$ minus something: decomposing$(3,-1,0)$:$m_1 = \frac{1}{6}(3-3+0)=0$,$m_2 = \frac{1}{6}(3+3+0) = 1$,$m_3 = \frac{1}{6}(6+0+0)=1$. So$\mathrm{Ind}^{S_3}_H \mathrm{sign} \cong \chi_2 \oplus \chi_3$.
\end{example}

The surprise here is that this construction is canonical despite depending on the choice of coset representatives: different choices of representatives give isomorphic $G$-representations. The induced representation is thus a well-defined functor$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H: \mathrm{Rep}(H) \to \mathrm{Rep}(G)$.

\begin{example}
Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} = \langle \sigma \rangle$,$H = \langle \sigma^2 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, and$W =$ trivial. Index$[G:H] = 2$. Coset representatives:$e, \sigma$. The induced representation$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$ has dimension 2. The$G$-action:$\sigma \cdot (e \otimes w) = \sigma \otimes w$,$\sigma \cdot (\sigma \otimes w) = \sigma^2 \otimes w = e \otimes (\sigma^2 \cdot w) = e \otimes w$ (since$\sigma^2 \in H$ acts trivially on$W$). So the matrix of$\sigma$ in the basis$\{e \otimes 1, \sigma \otimes 1\}$ is$\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$. Character:$\chi(e) = 2$,$\chi(\sigma) = 0$,$\chi(\sigma^2) = 0$... wait,$\chi(\sigma^2)$:$\sigma^2 \cdot (e \otimes w) = e \otimes w$ (since$\sigma^2 \in H$ and acts trivially), so all basis vectors are fixed, giving$\chi(\sigma^2) = 2$... Actually the matrix of$\sigma^2$ is$\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. So$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W \cong V_0 \oplus V_2$ where$V_k(\sigma^j) = i^{jk}$.
\end{example}
\par
Induction is the primary mechanism for building large representations from small ones, and it plays a central role in the classification of representations of symmetric groups, linear groups over finite fields, and many other families where representations of maximal subgroups are better understood than those of the full group.
