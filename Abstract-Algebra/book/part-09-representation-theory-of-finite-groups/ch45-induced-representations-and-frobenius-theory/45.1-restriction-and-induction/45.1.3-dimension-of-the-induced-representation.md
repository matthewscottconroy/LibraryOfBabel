# 45.1.3 Dimension of the Induced Representation: $[G:H] \cdot \dim W$

The dimension of the induced representation is easy to compute directly from the coset description, and the formula is as clean as one could hope.

\begin{proposition}
$\dim_k (\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W) = [G:H] \cdot \dim_k W$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From the coset decomposition $\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{[G:H]} g_i \otimes W$ as a$k$-vector space (the direct sum of$[G:H]$ copies of$W$, one per left coset), each summand has dimension$\dim_k W$. The total dimension is$[G:H] \cdot \dim_k W$.
\end{proof}

The contrast with restriction is instructive. Restriction $\mathrm{Res}^G_H V$ preserves the dimension of$V$ but may cause reducibility. Induction$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$ amplifies the dimension by the index$[G:H]$ while potentially producing irreducibility. This dimensional amplification reflects the fact that$G$ is larger than$H$ by a factor of$[G:H]$, and inducing forces the representation to "see" the full group.

\begin{example}
For $G = S_n$ and$H = S_{n-1} \times S_1$ (Young subgroup fixing last element):$[G:H] = n$. Inducing the trivial representation of$H$ gives$\mathrm{Ind}^{S_n}_{S_{n-1}} \mathbf{1}$, a representation of dimension$n$. This is the permutation representation of$S_n$ on$n$ letters, decomposing as trivial$\oplus$ standard.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $G = A_4$ and$H = \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \cong \langle (123) \rangle$:$[G:H] = 4$. Inducing a nontrivial 1-dimensional character$\chi$ of$H$ (with$\chi((123)) = \omega$,$\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$) gives a 4-dimensional representation. By Frobenius reciprocity (§45.2.1), its irreducible constituents are found by checking which irreducibles of$A_4$ have$H$-components matching$\chi$. The 3-dimensional irreducible of$A_4$ restricts to$H$ as$\omega \oplus \omega^2 \oplus 1$, and by the multiplicity formula one finds$\mathrm{Ind}^{A_4}_H \chi \cong V_4$ (the 3-dimensional irreducible plus one 1-dimensional piece), but we need to verify dimensions:$4 = 3 + 1$, consistent.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $G = S_4$ and$H = A_3 = \langle (123) \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$:$[G:H] = 8$. Inducing the nontrivial character$\chi_1$ of$H$ (with$\chi_1((123)) = \zeta_3$) gives an 8-dimensional representation of$S_4$. By Maschke's theorem this decomposes as a direct sum; by dimension counting the largest irreducible of$S_4$ has dimension 3, so the decomposition has at least three irreducible summands.
\end{example}

This connects to dimension-counting constraints elsewhere in the theory. If $V$ is an irreducible of$G$ with$\dim V = n$, then by Frobenius reciprocity, any irreducible$W$ of$H$ with nonzero multiplicity in$\mathrm{Res}^G_H V$ also has nonzero multiplicity in$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$, and$n \leq [G:H] \cdot \dim W$. This gives a lower bound on$\dim W$:
$$\dim W \geq \frac{n}{[G:H]} = \frac{\dim V}{[G:H]}.$$

\begin{remark}
The dimension formula $\dim(\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W) = [G:H] \cdot \dim W$ has an analogue for coinduction (the right adjoint to restriction):$\mathrm{coInd}^G_H W = \mathrm{Hom}_{k[H]}(k[G], W)$. The coinduced representation also has dimension$[G:H] \cdot \dim W$. For finite groups, induction and coinduction agree (a special feature of finite groups), so both functors produce the same representation and the same dimension.
\end{remark}

The dimension formula also interacts cleanly with the dimension formula for the group $\sum_i n_i^2 = |G|$. If$W$ is a 1-dimensional representation of$H$ (an irreducible of$H$ of the simplest kind), then$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$ has dimension$[G:H]$. The irreducible constituents of$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$ all have dimensions dividing$[G:H]$ in a loose sense (by the dimension constraints on$G$), though there is no general theorem forcing this. What is forced is the dimension identity:
$$\dim(\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W) = [G:H] = \sum_i m_i n_i,$$
where the sum runs over irreducible constituents $V_i$ of$G$ with multiplicities$m_i$. This is a linear Diophantine constraint on the possible decompositions.

\begin{example}
For $G = S_5$ and$H = S_4$ (fixing element 5):$[G:H] = 5$. Inducing the trivial character of$S_4$ gives a 5-dimensional representation of$S_5$. By Frobenius reciprocity, the multiplicity of each$S_5$-irreducible$\chi_\lambda$ in this induced character equals the multiplicity of the trivial character in$\mathrm{Res}^{S_5}_{S_4} \chi_\lambda$, which by Young's rule equals 1 if$\lambda$ is obtained from$(4)$ (the trivial partition of 4) by adding a box — but we start from the trivial of$S_4$, which corresponds to partition$(4)$ of$\{1,2,3,4\}$. Adding a box to$(4)$ gives$(5)$ (trivial of$S_5$) or$(4,1)$ (hook partition). So the 5-dimensional representation decomposes as the trivial (dim 1) plus the standard (dim 4). Dimension check:$1 + 4 = 5 = [S_5:S_4]$ \checkmark.
\end{example}

This example illustrates a general principle: inducing the trivial character from a subgroup always yields the permutation representation on the coset space $G/H$. The dimension$[G:H]$ is the number of cosets, and the action of$G$ on this coset space is the most natural permutation action associated to the subgroup$H$. The decomposition of this permutation character — a primary goal in many combinatorial applications — is thus equivalent, via Frobenius reciprocity, to understanding how$G$-irreducibles restrict to$H$.
