# 45.1.4 The Character Formula for Induced Representations

Having defined the induced representation, the next step is to compute its character. The character formula for induced representations gives an explicit recipe for $\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W}(g)$ in terms of the character$\chi_W$ of$H$ and the conjugacy structure of$G$.

\begin{theorem}[Character Formula for Induced Representations]
Let $H \leq G$ and$W$ a representation of$H$ with character$\chi_W$. Then for$g \in G$:
$$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W}(g) = \frac{1}{|H|} \sum_{\substack{x \in G \\ x^{-1}gx \in H}} \chi_W(x^{-1}gx).$$
Equivalently, using coset representatives $g_1, \ldots, g_m$ ($m = [G:H]$):
$$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W}(g) = \sum_{\substack{i = 1 \\ g_i^{-1} g g_i \in H}}^m \chi_W(g_i^{-1} g g_i).$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the coset basis $\{g_i \otimes e_j\}$ (where$e_j$ is a basis of$W$), the matrix of$g$ is block-structured: it maps the$g_i$-block to the$g_k$-block (where$g_k$ is the representative of$g g_i H$) via the matrix$\rho_W(g_k^{-1} g g_i)$. The trace is the sum of diagonal contributions, which occur only when$g g_i H = g_i H$ (so the block maps back to itself), i.e., when$g_i^{-1} g g_i \in H$. For such$i$, the contribution to the trace is$\mathrm{tr}(\rho_W(g_i^{-1} g g_i)) = \chi_W(g_i^{-1} g g_i)$.

The formula $\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{x \in G, x^{-1}gx \in H} \chi_W(x^{-1}gx)$ is equivalent: each coset$g_i H$ contributes$|H|$ elements$x = g_i h$ to the sum, and$x^{-1}gx = h^{-1}(g_i^{-1}gg_i)h$. When$g_i^{-1}gg_i \in H$, conjugation by$h \in H$ keeps it in$H$, and$\chi_W(h^{-1}(g_i^{-1}gg_i)h) = \chi_W(g_i^{-1}gg_i)$ (since$\chi_W$ is a class function on$H$). So each valid coset contributes$|H| \cdot \chi_W(g_i^{-1}gg_i)$ to the sum$\sum_{x: x^{-1}gx \in H}$, giving$\frac{1}{|H|} \cdot |H| \cdot \chi_W(g_i^{-1}gg_i) = \chi_W(g_i^{-1}gg_i)$ per coset. Summing over valid cosets recovers the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
For $G = S_3$,$H = \langle (12) \rangle$,$W = \mathrm{sign}$: compute$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}((12))$. The conjugates of$(12)$ in$G$:$g^{-1}(12)g$ for$g \in S_3$ gives all transpositions. Those in$H = \{e, (12)\}$: only$g^{-1}(12)g = (12)$ (when$g \in C_{S_3}((12)) = H$) lies in$H$. So:$\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x \in S_3, x^{-1}(12)x \in H} \chi_{\mathrm{sgn}}(x^{-1}(12)x) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot \chi_{\mathrm{sgn}}((12)) = (-1) = -1$.
\par
And $\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}((123))$: no conjugate of$(123)$ lies in$H = \{e,(12)\}$ (since$H$ has only transpositions and identity), so$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}((123)) = 0$. And$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}(e) = [G:H] \cdot \chi_W(e) = 3 \cdot 1 = 3$.
\par
So $\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}} = (3, -1, 0)$, which decomposes as$\chi_2 + \chi_3$ (sign + standard) as computed before.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $G = S_4$,$H = S_3$ (fixing element 4), and$W =$ standard 2-dim of$S_3$:$\chi_W$ has values$(2, 0, -1)$ on the three conjugacy classes of$S_3$. The index$[G:H] = 4$, so$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$ has dimension 8. The character$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}(g)$ for$g \in S_4$: if$g$ is a 4-cycle, no conjugate of$g$ lies in$S_3$ (which only contains permutations of$\{1,2,3\}$), so$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}(g) = 0$. For$g = e$:$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}(e) = 8$. Using Frobenius reciprocity (§45.2.1) to decompose:$\langle \chi_{\mathrm{Ind}}, \chi_i^{S_4} \rangle_{S_4} = \langle \chi_W, \chi_i^{S_4}|_{S_3} \rangle_{S_3}$.
\end{example}

The formula shows that the induced character depends not on $W$ globally, but only on the values$\chi_W$ takes on conjugates of$g$ inside$H$. This has a useful consequence: if$g$ is conjugate to no element of$H$, then$\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W}(g) = 0$. This vanishing is geometrically natural — the element$g$ fixes no coset of$H$ (since$g \notin$ any conjugate of$H$), so the trace of its action is zero. The character formula is the essential ingredient in the proof of Frobenius reciprocity.
