# 45.1 Restriction and Induction

Given a subgroup $H \leq G$ and a representation $(\rho, V)$ of $G$, one obtains a representation of $H$ simply by restricting the action: $(\text{Res}_H^G \rho, V)$ where $(\text{Res}_H^G \rho)(h) = \rho(h)$ for $h \in H$. This is the restriction functor, and it is as straightforward as it sounds. The interesting question is the reverse passage: given a representation $(\sigma, W)$ of $H$, how can we build a representation of the larger group $G$? Naive extension is impossible in general — we cannot simply declare $\rho(g) = \sigma(g)$ because $g$ may not lie in $H$. The correct construction is induction.

The induced representation $\text{Ind}_H^G \sigma$ is built by spreading $W$ over the cosets of $H$ in $G$. Choose coset representatives $g_1, \ldots, g_m$ so that $G = g_1 H \sqcup \cdots \sqcup g_m H$ with $m = [G:H]$; then $\text{Ind}_H^G W = \bigoplus_{i=1}^m g_i \otimes W$ as a vector space, with dimension $[G:H] \cdot \dim W$. The group $G$ acts on this space by left multiplication on the coset labels: for $g \in G$, the element $g \cdot g_i$ lies in some unique coset $g_j H$, so $g \cdot g_i = g_j h$ for some $h \in H$, and $G$ acts by sending $g_i \otimes w$ to $g_j \otimes \sigma(h)(w)$. This formula is independent of the choice of coset representatives, and it defines a genuine representation of $G$.

The character of the induced representation admits an explicit formula. For $g \in G$, $\chi^G(g) = \frac{1}{|H|} \sum_{x \in G,\, x^{-1}gx \in H} \sigma(x^{-1}gx)$. This counts, with appropriate weights, the contributions from elements of $G$-conjugates of $H$ that contain $g$. As a special case, if $\sigma = 1_H$ is the trivial representation of $H$, then $\text{Ind}_H^G 1_H$ is the permutation representation of $G$ on the coset space $G/H$, and the character value $\chi(g)$ counts the number of cosets fixed by left multiplication by $g$. This connects induction to the combinatorics of group actions explored in the early parts of this text.
