# 45.2.2 Categorical Interpretation: $\mathrm{Ind} \dashv \mathrm{Res}$

The equality $\langle \mathrm{Ind}^G_H W, V \rangle_G = \langle W, \mathrm{Res}^G_H V \rangle_H$ has a deeper structural meaning: it is exactly the statement that induction and restriction form an \emph{adjoint pair} of functors. This categorical perspective reveals why Frobenius reciprocity holds with such naturality and generality.

\begin{theorem}[Frobenius Reciprocity — Categorical Form]
There is a natural isomorphism of $\mathbb{C}$-vector spaces:
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{k[G]}(\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W,\, V) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{k[H]}(W,\, \mathrm{Res}^G_H V),$$
functorial in both $W \in \mathrm{Rep}(H)$ and$V \in \mathrm{Rep}(G)$. In categorical language,$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H \dashv \mathrm{Res}^G_H$ (induction is left adjoint to restriction).
\end{theorem}

The character-theoretic version of Frobenius reciprocity follows from this by taking dimensions of both sides: $\langle \mathrm{Ind} W, V \rangle_G = \dim \mathrm{Hom}_G(\mathrm{Ind} W, V) = \dim \mathrm{Hom}_H(W, \mathrm{Res} V) = \langle W, \mathrm{Res} V \rangle_H$, where we use the fact that$\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathrm{Hom}_G(U, V) = \langle \chi_U, \chi_V \rangle_G$ for semisimple representations.

The categorical adjunction is not a coincidence or a trick: it is the standard tensor-hom adjunction from module theory. Since $\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W = k[G] \otimes_{k[H]} W$, the tensor-hom adjunction gives:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Hom}_{k[G]}(k[G] \otimes_{k[H]} W,\, V) &\cong \mathrm{Hom}_{k[H]}(W,\, \mathrm{Hom}_{k[G]}(k[G], V)) \\
&\cong \mathrm{Hom}_{k[H]}(W,\, V) = \mathrm{Hom}_{k[H]}(W,\, \mathrm{Res}^G_H V).
\end{align*}
Here we use $\mathrm{Hom}_{k[G]}(k[G], V) \cong V$ (as$k[G]$-modules, evaluation at$1$ is an isomorphism), and the natural$k[H]$-module structure on$V$ from restricting scalars.

\begin{example}
The adjunction gives an explicit natural bijection: to a $G$-equivariant map$\phi: \mathrm{Ind}^G_H W \to V$, associate the$H$-equivariant map$\tilde{\phi}: W \to V$ defined by$\tilde{\phi}(w) = \phi(e \otimes w)$. Conversely, given$\psi: W \to \mathrm{Res}^G_H V$ ($H$-equivariant), define$\tilde{\psi}: \mathrm{Ind}^G_H W \to V$ by$\tilde{\psi}(g \otimes w) = g \cdot \psi(w)$ (well-defined by$H$-equivariance of$\psi$ and the tensor product relation). One checks these are inverse bijections, natural in$W$ and$V$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
For finite groups, induction and coinduction agree. The coinduction is $\mathrm{coInd}^G_H W = \mathrm{Hom}_{k[H]}(k[G], W)$ (as$k[H]$-module maps from$k[G]$ to$W$, with the left$G$-action$(g \cdot f)(x) = f(xg)$). This is the right adjoint of restriction:$\mathrm{Hom}_{k[G]}(V, \mathrm{coInd}^G_H W) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{k[H]}(\mathrm{Res}^G_H V, W)$. For finite groups,$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W \cong \mathrm{coInd}^G_H W$ (they are isomorphic as representations), so restriction has both a left and a right adjoint, and they coincide. This self-duality —$\mathrm{Ind}^G_H$ is simultaneously left and right adjoint to$\mathrm{Res}^G_H$ — is a special feature of the finite group setting with no analogue for infinite groups.
\end{remark}

The categorical perspective reveals why Frobenius reciprocity is so universal: any time one has a ring homomorphism $R \to S$, restriction of scalars (from$S$-modules to$R$-modules) has a left adjoint (extension of scalars$S \otimes_R -$). In representation theory, the subgroup inclusion$k[H] \hookrightarrow k[G]$ gives exactly this situation. The self-duality for finite groups follows from the fact that$k[G]$ is a free$k[H]$-module (of rank$[G:H]$), making$k[G] \otimes_{k[H]} W \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{k[H]}(k[G], W)$ as$k[G]$-modules.

\begin{example}
For $G = S_n$ and$H = S_\lambda = S_{\lambda_1} \times \cdots \times S_{\lambda_k}$ (Young subgroup for a partition$\lambda$ of$n$), the adjunction gives: the number of times an$S_n$-irreducible$V_\mu$ appears in$\mathrm{Ind}^{S_n}_{S_\lambda} \mathbf{1}$ (the permutation module) equals the number of times the trivial character of$S_\lambda$ appears in$\mathrm{Res}^{S_n}_{S_\lambda} V_\mu$. This is the \emph{Kostka number}$K_{\mu\lambda}$ of combinatorics, and Frobenius reciprocity connects it to both induction from Young subgroups (representation theory) and semistandard Young tableaux (combinatorics).
\end{example}
