# 45.3.1 Double Cosets and the Mackey Formula

Frobenius reciprocity answers one natural question about induction and restriction: how does the inner product $\langle \mathrm{Ind}^G_H W, V \rangle_G$ relate to restriction? A deeper question asks: if one first induces and then restricts to a different subgroup$K \leq G$, what does one get? The answer is the \emph{Mackey formula}, which expresses the composition$\mathrm{Res}^G_K \circ \mathrm{Ind}^G_H$ in terms of representations induced from subgroup intersections.

\begin{definition}
For subgroups $H, K \leq G$, the \emph{double cosets}$H \backslash G / K = \{HsK : s \in G\}$ partition$G$ into disjoint subsets. A set of \emph{double coset representatives} is a set$\{s_1, \ldots, s_t\}$ with$G = \bigsqcup_{j=1}^t H s_j K$.
\end{definition}

For $s \in G$ and a representation$W$ of$H$, the \emph{$s$-conjugate}${}^s W$ is the representation of$s^{-1}Hs$ defined by${}^s W(h) = W(shs^{-1})$ for$h \in s^{-1}Hs$.

\begin{theorem}[Mackey Formula]
Let $H, K \leq G$ and$W$ a representation of$H$. Then as$K$-representations:
$$\mathrm{Res}^G_K \mathrm{Ind}^G_H W \cong \bigoplus_{s \in H \backslash G / K} \mathrm{Ind}^K_{K \cap s^{-1}Hs}\, ({}^s W|_{K \cap s^{-1}Hs}),$$
where the sum is over double coset representatives $s \in H \backslash G / K$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The key step is to decompose the $G$-set$G/H$ into$K$-orbits. The$K$-orbits on the left coset space$G/H$ are exactly the double cosets: the orbit of the coset$sH$ under$K$ is$\{ksH : k \in K\} = K s H / H$, which corresponds to the double coset$KsH = Hs^{-1}K$ (reindexed). For each double coset$HsK$, the stabilizer in$K$ of the coset$sH$ is$\{k \in K : ksH = sH\} = \{k \in K : s^{-1}ks \in H\} = K \cap s^{-1}Hs$.

The restriction of $\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$ to$K$, decomposed along the$K$-orbits on$G/H$, gives one summand per double coset. For the double coset$HsK$, the corresponding summand is a representation induced from the stabilizer$K \cap s^{-1}Hs$ to$K$, starting from the twist${}^s W|_{K \cap s^{-1}Hs}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
For $G = S_3$,$H = K = \langle (12) \rangle$: the double cosets$H \backslash G / H$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $HeH = H = \{e, (12)\}$ (the coset$eH = H$ itself).
\item $H(123)H$:$(123)H = \{(123),(132)\}$ and$H(123) = \{(123),(13)\cdot(23)=(123)$... let us just enumerate:$H(123)H = \{h_1(123)h_2 : h_1, h_2 \in H\}$. Since$H = \{e,(12)\}$: elements are$(123)$,$(12)(123) = (23)$,$(123)(12) = (13)$,$(12)(123)(12) = (13)$... so$H(123)H = \{(123),(132),(13),(23)\}$.
\end{itemize}
So there are two double cosets with representatives $e$ and$(123)$, and the Mackey formula gives:
$$\mathrm{Res}^G_H \mathrm{Ind}^G_H W \cong \mathrm{Ind}^H_{H \cap H} W \oplus \mathrm{Ind}^H_{H \cap (123)^{-1}H(123)} {}^{(123)}W.$$
The first term is $W$ itself (restricted and re-induced, trivially). The second:$(123)^{-1}H(123) = (132)\{e,(12)\}(123) = \{e, (132)(12)(123)\} = \{e, (23)\}$. So$H \cap (123)^{-1}H(123) = \{e,(12)\} \cap \{e,(23)\} = \{e\}$. The induced representation$\mathrm{Ind}^H_{\{e\}} {}^{(123)}W|_{\{e\}}$ is just the induction of the trivial (or appropriate character) from the trivial subgroup to$H$, giving the regular representation of$H$ (both characters of$H$).
\end{example}

The proof of the Mackey formula works by writing out the coset description of $\mathrm{Ind}^G_H W$, decomposing the$G$-set$G/H$ into$K$-orbits (which are exactly the double cosets), and analyzing the$K$-action on each orbit. The Mackey formula is an important structural result that underlies many deeper theorems about representations of finite groups and, in the context of$p$-adic groups and$GL_n$, forms the basis for the theory of intertwining operators.
