# 45.3.3 Applications to Subgroup Structure

Mackey theory reaches beyond character computations into the structural theory of groups. The double coset machinery provides a bridge between global representation-theoretic properties of $G$ and local information about its subgroups — intersection patterns, normalizers, and fusion of$p$-elements.

**Primitive permutation representations.** A transitive $G$-set$X$ is \emph{primitive} if and only if the point stabilizer$G_x$ is a maximal subgroup of$G$. The permutation character$\chi_X = \mathrm{Ind}^G_{G_x} \mathbf{1}$ is the induced trivial character. Applying the Mackey formula for$K = H = G_x$:
$$\mathrm{Res}^G_{G_x} \mathrm{Ind}^G_{G_x} \mathbf{1} \cong \bigoplus_{s \in G_x \backslash G / G_x} \mathrm{Ind}^{G_x}_{G_x \cap s^{-1}G_xs} \mathbf{1}.$$
The double cosets $G_x \backslash G / G_x$ correspond to the orbits of$G_x$ on$X$ (since the cosets$G/G_x \cong X$ as$G$-sets, and the$G_x$-orbits on$X$ are the double cosets). The number of such double cosets is the \emph{rank} of the permutation representation. The trivial coset contributes$\mathbf{1}$ (the trivial$G_x$-representation), corresponding to the fixed point$x$ itself.

\begin{example}
For $G = S_4$ acting on pairs$\binom{4}{2} = \{12, 13, 14, 23, 24, 34\}$ (6 elements): the point stabilizer of "12" is$H = \{e, (12)\} \times S_{\{3,4\}} = \langle (12)(34) \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$? No, the stabilizer of the pair$\{1,2\}$ as a set is$\{g \in S_4 : g(\{1,2\}) = \{1,2\}\} = \langle (12), (34) \rangle \cong D_2$. The orbits of$H$ on the 6 pairs:$\{1,2\}$ (fixed),$\{3,4\}$ (fixed under$H$ since$(12)$ fixes$\{3,4\}$ and$(34)$ fixes$\{1,2\}$ but swaps 3,4),$\{1,3\},\{2,3\},\{1,4\},\{2,4\}$ (orbited). The rank is 3, suggesting the permutation character decomposes into 3 irreducible constituents.
\end{example}

**Sylow intersections and the Mackey formula.** If $P$ is a Sylow$p$-subgroup of$G$, the double coset decomposition$P \backslash G / P$ corresponds to the orbits of$P$ on$G/P$. The Mackey formula for$H = K = P$ expresses$\mathrm{Res}^G_P \mathrm{Ind}^G_P W$ in terms of representations induced from$P \cap s^{-1}Ps$ (for$s$ ranging over double coset representatives). The sizes and structure of these intersections encode the \emph{$p$-fusion pattern} of$G$ — how$p$-elements of$P$ become conjugate in$G$ but not in$P$.

\begin{remark}
This connects to the modern theory of \emph{fusion systems}: a fusion system over $P$ is an abstract category modeling the conjugacy relations among subgroups of$P$ that arise from the ambient group$G$. Mackey's formula, applied to Sylow subgroups, extracts this fusion information from the representation theory. The theory of blocks and the Brauer correspondent exploit this connection deeply.
\end{remark}

**The Mackey machine for semidirect products.** The most powerful application of Mackey theory classifies all irreducible representations of a semidirect product $G = N \rtimes H$. The key data are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Pontryagin dual $\hat{N}$ (set of irreducible characters of$N$, which are all 1-dimensional if$N$ is abelian);
\item The action of $H$ on$\hat{N}$ by conjugation:$(h \cdot \chi)(n) = \chi(h^{-1}nh)$;
\item For each orbit $\mathcal{O} \in H \backslash \hat{N}$, choose a representative$\chi_0 \in \mathcal{O}$ and let$H_{\chi_0} = \{h \in H : h \cdot \chi_0 = \chi_0\}$ be the stabilizer (or "little group").
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Mackey Machine]
The irreducible representations of $N \rtimes H$ are in bijection with pairs$(\mathcal{O}, \sigma)$ where$\mathcal{O} \in H \backslash \hat{N}$ is an$H$-orbit and$\sigma$ is an irreducible representation of$H_{\chi_0}$ (for any fixed$\chi_0 \in \mathcal{O}$). The corresponding irreducible of$G$ is$\mathrm{Ind}^G_{N \rtimes H_{\chi_0}} (\chi_0 \otimes \sigma)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{example}
For $G = D_n$ (dihedral group of order$2n$)$= C_n \rtimes C_2$:$\hat{C_n} = \{\chi_0, \chi_1, \ldots, \chi_{n-1}\}$ where$\chi_k(r^j) = \zeta^{jk}$. The$C_2$-action: the reflection$s$ acts by$\chi_k \mapsto \chi_{-k} = \chi_{n-k}$. Orbits:$\{\chi_0\}$ (fixed),$\{\chi_{n/2}\}$ if$n$ is even (also fixed), and$\{\chi_k, \chi_{n-k}\}$ for$1 \leq k < n/2$. For the 2-element orbits$\{\chi_k, \chi_{n-k}\}$, the stabilizer$H_{\chi_k} = \{e\}$ (trivial, since$C_2$ acts non-trivially), and the Mackey machine gives$\mathrm{Ind}^{D_n}_{C_n \rtimes \{e\}} \chi_k = \mathrm{Ind}^{D_n}_{C_n} \chi_k$, a 2-dimensional irreducible (by Mackey's criterion, since conjugates of$\chi_k$ and$\chi_k$ don't share$C_n$-components). This recovers the known 2-dimensional irreducibles of$D_n$.
\end{example}
