# 45.3 Mackey Theory

Frobenius reciprocity describes how induction and restriction interact when applied in sequence to the same subgroup. Mackey theory addresses a more refined question: what happens when one first induces from $H$ and then restricts to a possibly different subgroup $K$? The answer is governed by the double coset decomposition of $G$, and it provides the tools needed to analyze when induced representations are irreducible and how representations of $G$ behave when pulled back to various subgroups.

The Mackey formula decomposes the restriction $\text{Res}_K^G \text{Ind}_H^G \sigma$ explicitly. The double cosets $H \backslash G / K$ partition $G$ into finitely many pieces, and the restriction decomposes as $\text{Res}_K^G \text{Ind}_H^G \sigma \cong \bigoplus_{s \in H \backslash G / K} \text{Ind}_{K \cap sHs^{-1}}^K \sigma^s$, where $\sigma^s$ denotes the representation of $sHs^{-1} \cap K$ given by $h \mapsto \sigma(s^{-1}hs)$. Each double coset contributes an induced representation from the intersection $K \cap sHs^{-1}$, a subgroup that measures the overlap between $K$ and the $s$-conjugate of $H$. The formula reduces to Frobenius reciprocity when $K = H$ and the double coset decomposition is trivial. In general, it organizes the problem of analyzing $\text{Res}_K^G \text{Ind}_H^G \sigma$ into a sum of smaller induction problems, each indexed by a double coset.

Mackey's irreducibility criterion is a direct corollary. The induced representation $\text{Ind}_H^G \sigma$ is irreducible if and only if two conditions hold: first, $\sigma$ is irreducible; second, for every $g \in G \setminus H$, the representations $\sigma$ and $\sigma^g$ of $H \cap gHg^{-1}$ have no irreducible constituent in common (they are "disjoint"). The proof applies the Mackey formula with $K = H$ and uses the inner product criterion for irreducibility. This criterion is particularly useful for the symmetric groups and for groups defined by generators and relations, where the double coset structure can often be analyzed combinatorially. The broader framework developed by Mackey extends the theory to locally compact groups and underlies the representation theory of Lie groups and the Wigner classification of elementary particles in quantum mechanics.
