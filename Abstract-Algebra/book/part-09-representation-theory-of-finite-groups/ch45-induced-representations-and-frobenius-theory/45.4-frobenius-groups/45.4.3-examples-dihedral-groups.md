# 45.4.3 Examples: Dihedral Groups, $AGL(1, p)$

The two main families of Frobenius groups — dihedral groups with odd cycle length and affine groups over prime fields — illustrate the general theory concretely and completely.

**Example 1. Dihedral Groups $D_n$ for odd$n$.**

The dihedral group $D_n = \langle r, s \mid r^n = s^2 = e, srs^{-1} = r^{-1} \rangle$ acts on the vertices$\{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ of a regular$n$-gon by$r(k) = k+1 \pmod{n}$ and$s(k) = -k \pmod{n}$. Since$n$ is odd:
- Any non-identity rotation $r^j$ ($1 \leq j \leq n-1$) satisfies$r^j(k) = k$ iff$j \equiv 0 \pmod{n}$, i.e., never. So rotations are fixed-point-free.
- Any reflection $r^j s$ fixes the unique vertex$k$ satisfying$r^j s(k) = k$, i.e.,$-k + j \equiv k$, i.e.,$k \equiv j/2$. When$n$ is odd,$2$ is invertible mod$n$, so each reflection fixes exactly one vertex.

So $D_n$ (odd$n$) is a Frobenius group with complement$H = \langle s \rangle \cong C_2$ and kernel$K = \langle r \rangle \cong C_n$. The structure:$D_n = C_n \rtimes C_2$, with$C_2$ acting on$C_n$ by inversion$r \mapsto r^{-1}$ (the fixed-point-free automorphism of odd-order cyclic groups).

The representation theory of $D_n$ (from the Mackey machine):
- Inflations from $D_n/C_n \cong C_2$: two 1-dimensional representations (trivial and sign).
- Induced from nontrivial characters of $C_n$: for each pair$\{\chi_k, \chi_{n-k}\}$ ($1 \leq k < n/2$), the induced representation$\mathrm{Ind}^{D_n}_{C_n} \chi_k$ is a 2-dimensional irreducible with character$\chi_{\mathrm{ind}}(r^j) = \zeta^{jk} + \zeta^{-jk} = 2\cos(2\pi jk/n)$,$\chi_{\mathrm{ind}}(r^js) = 0$.

Total: $2 + (n-1)/2$ irreducibles of dimensions$1,1,2,2,\ldots,2$ ($(n-1)/2$ copies of 2). Dimension formula:$1 + 1 + 4 \cdot \frac{n-1}{2} = 2 + 2(n-1) = 2n = |D_n|$ \checkmark.

**Example 2. The Affine Group $AGL(1,p)$.**

For a prime $p$,$AGL(1,p) = \{x \mapsto ax+b : a \in \mathbb{F}_p^*, b \in \mathbb{F}_p\}$ is a Frobenius group of order$p(p-1)$ with kernel$K = \mathbb{F}_p$ (translations, order$p$) and complement$H = \mathbb{F}_p^* \cong C_{p-1}$ (dilations fixing 0).

The representation theory (by the Mackey machine for $K \rtimes H$):
- Inflations from $H = C_{p-1}$:$p-1$ one-dimensional irreducibles.
- Induced from nontrivial characters of $K \cong \mathbb{F}_p$: Since$H$ acts on$\hat{K} \cong \mathbb{F}_p$ by the same multiplication action ($m_a \cdot \chi_b = \chi_{ab}$), there is a single non-trivial orbit$\hat{K} \setminus \{\mathbf{1}\}$ (all$p-1$ nontrivial characters form one orbit under$H = C_{p-1} \cong \mathbb{F}_p^*$). The stabilizer of any$\chi_b$ ($b \neq 0$) in$H$ is$\{a : ab = b\} = \{1\}$. By Mackey's criterion (trivial intersections), each induction$\mathrm{Ind}^G_K \chi_b$ is irreducible, and all choices$b \neq 0$ give the same irreducible (since the orbit is one). Dimension:$[G:K] \cdot 1 = p-1$.

So $AGL(1,p)$ has$(p-1) + 1 = p$ irreducible representations:$(p-1)$ of dimension 1 and$1$ of dimension$p-1$. Dimension formula:$(p-1) \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot (p-1)^2 = (p-1)(1 + p-1) = p(p-1) = |G|$ \checkmark.

Frobenius groups appear naturally throughout algebra and combinatorics wherever one encounters "sharply 2-transitive" actions or groups acting on affine spaces. Their representation theory is particularly clean: the irreducible representations are either inflated from $G/K \cong H$ or induced from non-trivial irreducibles of$K$, and the latter all have dimension$|H|$ (from the single orbit under the Mackey machine). This makes Frobenius groups a natural testing ground for the methods developed throughout this chapter.
