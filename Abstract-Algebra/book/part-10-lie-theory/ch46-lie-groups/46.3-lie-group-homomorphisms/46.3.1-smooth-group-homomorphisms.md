# 46.3.1 Smooth Group Homomorphisms

A morphism in the category of Lie groups should respect both structures simultaneously. A *Lie group homomorphism* is a smooth map $\phi: G \to H$ between Lie groups that is also a group homomorphism:$\phi(gh) = \phi(g)\phi(h)$ for all$g, h \in G$, and$\phi(e_G) = e_H$. The smoothness condition is not automatic from the algebraic homomorphism condition alone — one must impose it explicitly. (There exist, assuming appropriate set-theoretic axioms, discontinuous group homomorphisms between Lie groups, but these are pathological and not morphisms in the category.)

The central fact about Lie group homomorphisms is that they are linearized by differentiation. If $\phi: G \to H$ is a Lie group homomorphism, then its differential at the identity,
$$d\phi_e: T_e G = \mathfrak{g} \to T_e H = \mathfrak{h},$$
is a linear map between the respective Lie algebras. What makes this more than just a linear map is that it preserves the Lie bracket: $d\phi_e([X, Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}) = [d\phi_e(X), d\phi_e(Y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}$. Thus$d\phi_e$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism. This is because the Lie bracket on$\mathfrak{g}$ is defined through the group structure of$G$ (via conjugation and commutators), and$\phi$ transports that structure to$H$.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{Lie algebra homomorphism} $\psi: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{h}$ is a linear map preserving the Lie bracket:$\psi([X,Y]) = [\psi(X), \psi(Y)]$ for all$X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$.
\end{definition}

The passage $G \mapsto \mathfrak{g}$ and$\phi \mapsto d\phi_e$ is therefore a functor from the category of Lie groups to the category of Lie algebras, written$\mathrm{Lie}: \mathbf{LieGrp} \to \mathbf{LieAlg}$. This functor "linearizes" Lie group theory: questions about smooth group homomorphisms can often be translated into questions about linear maps satisfying the bracket-preservation condition, which are far more tractable.

**Example 1.** The determinant map $\det: GL_n(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{R}^\times$ is a Lie group homomorphism. Its differential at the identity is$d(\det)_I(X) = \mathrm{tr}(X)$ (the trace), a Lie algebra homomorphism from$\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{R}) = M_n(\mathbb{R})$ to$\mathbb{R}$ (where$\mathbb{R}$ has the trivial bracket). This reflects the identity$\det(e^X) = e^{\mathrm{tr}(X)}$.

**Example 2.** The inclusion $\iota: SU(2) \hookrightarrow SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a smooth group homomorphism. Its differential at the identity is the inclusion$\mathfrak{su}(2) \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ of Lie algebras, which is a Lie algebra homomorphism. After complexification,$\mathfrak{su}(2) \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, but the real map$\mathfrak{su}(2) \to \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ itself is only an$\mathbb{R}$-linear injection.

**Example 3.** The adjoint representation $\mathrm{Ad}: G \to GL(\mathfrak{g})$,$g \mapsto (X \mapsto gXg^{-1})$, is a Lie group homomorphism. Its differential at the identity is$\mathrm{ad}: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})$,$X \mapsto [X, -]$. The bracket-preservation condition for$\mathrm{ad}$ is exactly the Jacobi identity, as explored in detail in Section 47.4.3.

**Example 4.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ with standard basis$\{e, f, h\}$, the map$\phi: \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}) \to \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ defined by$e \mapsto f$,$f \mapsto e$,$h \mapsto -h$ is a Lie algebra automorphism (an isomorphism from$\mathfrak{g}$ to itself). Verify:$\phi([h,e]) = \phi(2e) = 2f = [{-h}, f] = [\phi(h), \phi(e)]$. This is the automorphism corresponding to the longest element$w_0$ of the Weyl group, which acts on$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ by negating the root.

In the next subsection on functoriality (47.1.4), we will see that this functor is an equivalence when restricted to simply connected groups — meaning that, at the simply connected level, Lie groups and Lie algebras are essentially the same mathematical object. Lie algebra homomorphisms from the Lie algebra of a simply connected group always integrate uniquely to Lie group homomorphisms.
