# 46.3 Lie Group Homomorphisms

A Lie group homomorphism is a map $\phi: G \to H$ that is simultaneously a group homomorphism and a smooth map between manifolds. The two conditions are not independent: it turns out that any continuous group homomorphism between Lie groups is automatically smooth, a non-trivial regularity result that reflects the rigidity of the Lie group structure. The basic algebraic facts about group homomorphisms — kernels, images, quotients, first isomorphism theorem — all carry over to the smooth setting, but each requires a geometric argument: the kernel is a closed normal Lie subgroup, the image is a Lie subgroup (though not necessarily closed), and quotient Lie groups $G/N$ exist whenever $N$ is a closed normal subgroup.

The most fundamental construction is the quotient: if $N \trianglelefteq G$ is a closed normal subgroup, then the coset space $G/N$ is again a Lie group, and the projection $\pi: G \to G/N$ is a smooth surjective homomorphism. Discrete normal subgroups are particularly important: when $N = \Gamma$ is a discrete central subgroup, the quotient $G/\Gamma$ is locally diffeomorphic to $G$ (the map is a local diffeomorphism) and $\pi: G \to G/\Gamma$ is a covering map. This is the context of covering groups.

Every connected Lie group $G$ admits a universal covering group $\tilde{G}$: a simply connected Lie group equipped with a covering homomorphism $\pi: \tilde{G} \to G$ whose kernel is the fundamental group $\pi_1(G)$, which is necessarily a discrete central subgroup. The universal cover of $SO(3)$ is $SU(2)$, with $\pi_1(SO(3)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$; the universal cover of $S^1$ is $\mathbb{R}$, with $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. These covering relationships are not merely topological curiosities: they reflect the existence of projective representations and spinors in physics, and they are the global counterpart of the algebraic fact that isomorphic Lie algebras correspond to locally isomorphic Lie groups. Two connected Lie groups have the same Lie algebra if and only if they share the same universal cover, differing only in which discrete central subgroup is quotiented out.
