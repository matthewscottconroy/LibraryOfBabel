# 47.1 The Lie Algebra of a Lie Group

The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ of a Lie group $G$ is constructed by differentiating the group structure at the identity. As a vector space, $\mathfrak{g} = T_e G$ is simply the tangent space at the identity, which has dimension equal to $\dim G$. The non-trivial content is the bracket: the commutator of two elements in the group, computed to first order, gives a bilinear antisymmetric operation on $T_e G$ that satisfies the Jacobi identity. For matrix Lie groups $G \subset GL_n$, this has a clean description: $\mathfrak{g}$ consists of all matrices $X$ such that $e^{tX} \in G$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$, and the bracket is $[X, Y] = XY - YX$.

The most elegant approach defines the Lie algebra via left-invariant vector fields. A vector field $\xi$ on $G$ is left-invariant if $(L_g)_* \xi = \xi$ for every $g \in G$, i.e., if it commutes with all left-translations. The set of all left-invariant vector fields forms a Lie algebra under the Lie bracket of vector fields, and this Lie algebra is canonically isomorphic to $T_e G$ via the map $\xi \mapsto \xi(e)$. This approach makes the Lie bracket manifestly well-defined and shows that $\mathfrak{g}$ is not an ad hoc construction but a natural consequence of the group structure.

A fundamental consequence of this construction is functoriality: every Lie group homomorphism $\phi: G \to H$ induces a Lie algebra homomorphism $d\phi_e: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{h}$, and this assignment is compatible with composition. The induced map $d\phi_e$ is just the differential of $\phi$ at the identity. This gives a functor from Lie groups to Lie algebras, and the remarkable content of Lie's theorems is that, for simply connected groups, this functor is an equivalence of categories: isomorphic Lie algebras correspond to isomorphic simply connected Lie groups. The Lie algebra thus captures the local structure of the Lie group completely, and the passage from groups to algebras replaces a nonlinear problem (understanding smooth manifolds with group structure) with a linear one (understanding finite-dimensional algebras with a bracket).
