# 47.3 The Exponential Map

The exponential map $\exp: \mathfrak{g} \to G$ is the bridge between the Lie algebra and the Lie group. For matrix Lie groups it is given by the absolutely convergent power series $\exp(X) = I + X + \frac{X^2}{2!} + \frac{X^3}{3!} + \cdots$, and for general Lie groups it is defined by following the one-parameter subgroup through $X$ for time one: $\exp(X) = \gamma_X(1)$ where $\gamma_X: \mathbb{R} \to G$ is the unique smooth homomorphism with $\gamma_X'(0) = X$. The map $\exp$ is smooth, its differential at the origin is the identity, and by the inverse function theorem it is a local diffeomorphism near $0 \in \mathfrak{g}$ (or equivalently near $e \in G$). It thus provides local coordinates on the Lie group — the exponential coordinates — which are canonical in the sense that they depend only on the Lie algebra structure.

The exponential map is not generally surjective or injective. For compact connected Lie groups (such as $SU_n$ and $SO_n$) it is surjective: every element is a matrix exponential. For non-compact groups such as $SL_2(\mathbb{R})$, there are elements not in the image of $\exp$. Injectivity also fails globally: the exponential map from $\mathbb{R}$ to $S^1 = U(1)$ wraps the line around the circle with kernel $2\pi i \mathbb{Z}$, and more generally the kernel of $\exp$ near the identity is trivial but globally it is the cocharacter lattice.

The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff (BCH) formula addresses the fundamental question: given $X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$, what is $\exp(X)\exp(Y)$ expressed as $\exp(Z)$ for some $Z \in \mathfrak{g}$? The answer is $Z = X + Y + \frac{1}{2}[X,Y] + \frac{1}{12}([X,[X,Y]] - [Y,[X,Y]]) + \cdots$, an infinite series of nested Lie brackets. The BCH formula has a profound implication: the group multiplication law near the identity is entirely determined by the Lie algebra structure. It is this formula that makes precise the sense in which a simply connected Lie group is "reconstructible" from its Lie algebra. The dictionary between Lie subgroups of $G$ and Lie subalgebras of $\mathfrak{g}$, between normal subgroups and ideals, between covering maps and quotient algebras, is summarized here and follows from the properties of $\exp$ and the BCH formula.
