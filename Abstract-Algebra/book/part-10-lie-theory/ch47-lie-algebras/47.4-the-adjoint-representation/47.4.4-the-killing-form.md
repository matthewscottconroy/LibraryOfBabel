# 47.4.4 The Killing Form $B(X,Y) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}(X)\mathrm{ad}(Y))$

The adjoint representation does more than give $\mathfrak{g}$ a distinguished action on itself — it provides$\mathfrak{g}$ with a canonical bilinear form, the *Killing form*. Defined by$B(X, Y) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}(X) \circ \mathrm{ad}(Y))$, the Killing form is the trace of the composition of two adjoint endomorphisms, computed in any basis for$\mathfrak{g}$. The result is independent of the choice of basis since the trace is invariant under change of basis.

The Killing form possesses two key structural properties that make it the right "inner product" to use on $\mathfrak{g}$. First, it is$G$-invariant:$B(\mathrm{Ad}(g)X, \mathrm{Ad}(g)Y) = B(X, Y)$ for all$g \in G$ and$X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$. This follows because$\mathrm{Ad}(g)$ is an automorphism of$\mathfrak{g}$, hence conjugates the linear maps$\mathrm{ad}(X)$ and$\mathrm{ad}(Y)$, and trace is invariant under conjugation. At the infinitesimal level, this invariance reads$B([Z,X], Y) + B(X, [Z,Y]) = 0$ for all$Z \in \mathfrak{g}$ — the Killing form is *invariant* (or *associative*):$B([X, Y], Z) = B(X, [Y, Z])$.

The decisive theorem is Cartan's criterion: $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple if and only if the Killing form is non-degenerate (i.e.,$B(X, Y) = 0$ for all$Y$ implies$X = 0$). This criterion converts a structural question — does$\mathfrak{g}$ have nontrivial solvable ideals? — into a linear-algebraic question about a single bilinear form, which is far more tractable. The proof is developed in detail in Section 48.3.2.

When $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple, the Killing form plays the role of a Riemannian metric on$\mathfrak{g}$: for compact semisimple groups it is negative definite (one often uses$-B$ to get a positive definite form), and it is this metric that governs the geometry of the associated symmetric spaces and the normalization of root systems. The non-degeneracy of$B$ gives a canonical isomorphism$\mathfrak{g} \cong \mathfrak{g}^*$ (the Killing form identifies each$X$ with the functional$B(X, -)$), which is the basis for the identification$\mathfrak{h} \cong \mathfrak{h}^*$ used to define the inner product on root spaces.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ with basis$\{h, e, f\}$: compute$\mathrm{ad}(h) = \mathrm{diag}(0, 2, -2)$ (in basis$\{h, e, f\}$),$\mathrm{ad}(e)$ has matrix$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$... actually let us use a cleaner computation. In any basis,$B(h,h) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}(h)^2)$. We have$\mathrm{ad}(h)$ acting as multiplication by$0$ on$h$,$+2$ on$e$,$-2$ on$f$. So$\mathrm{ad}(h)^2$ has eigenvalues$0, 4, 4$, giving$B(h,h) = 0 + 4 + 4 = 8$. Similarly,$B(e, f) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}(e)\mathrm{ad}(f))$: the product$\mathrm{ad}(e)\mathrm{ad}(f)$ acts as$\mathrm{ad}(e)([f, -])$. Computing:$\mathrm{ad}(e)\mathrm{ad}(f)(h) = \mathrm{ad}(e)([f,h]) = \mathrm{ad}(e)(2f) = 2h$;$\mathrm{ad}(e)\mathrm{ad}(f)(e) = \mathrm{ad}(e)([f,e]) = \mathrm{ad}(e)(-h) = -[e,h] = 2e$;$\mathrm{ad}(e)\mathrm{ad}(f)(f) = \mathrm{ad}(e)([f,f]) = 0$. So the diagonal entries are$2, 2, 0$, giving$B(e,f) = 4$. And$B(e,e) = B(f,f) = 0$ (nilpotents have trace-zero adjoint action compositions). The Gram matrix is$\begin{pmatrix} 8 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 4 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, which has determinant$-128 \neq 0$, confirming$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is semisimple.

**Example 2.** For an abelian Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} = k^n$:$\mathrm{ad}(X) = 0$ for all$X$, so$B(X,Y) = \mathrm{tr}(0) = 0$ for all$X, Y$. The Killing form is identically zero, hence degenerate. This is consistent with$k^n$ being solvable (in fact abelian) and not semisimple.

**Example 3.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{C})$: one can show$B(X,Y) = 2n \cdot \mathrm{tr}(XY)$ (using the matrix trace). So the Killing form is proportional to the standard trace form$(X,Y) \mapsto \mathrm{tr}(XY)$. Both are non-degenerate on$\mathfrak{sl}_n$, confirming semisimplicity.

**Example 4.** For the compact real form $\mathfrak{su}(n)$: the Killing form is negative definite on$\mathfrak{su}(n)$ (since$X \in \mathfrak{su}(n)$ implies$iX$ is Hermitian, so$\mathrm{ad}(X)$ is skew-Hermitian on$\mathfrak{su}(n) \otimes \mathbb{C}$, and its eigenvalues are purely imaginary; their squares are negative real). This negative definiteness is the algebraic reflection of the compactness of$SU(n)$, and the form$-B$ is the natural$SU(n)$-invariant inner product on$\mathfrak{su}(n)$.

The Killing form is thus the bridge between the algebraic structure of $\mathfrak{g}$ and the geometric and analytic properties of the Lie group$G$. Its non-degeneracy (Cartan's criterion) is the key discriminant between semisimple and non-semisimple algebras, and its explicit values organize the root system into a Euclidean space with a canonical inner product.
