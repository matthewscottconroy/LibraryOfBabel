# 48.1.1 The Derived Series $\mathfrak{g}^{(0)} \supset \mathfrak{g}^{(1)} \supset \cdots$

A guiding question in the structure theory of Lie algebras is: how far is $\mathfrak{g}$ from being abelian? The derived series is the canonical tool for measuring this distance. It is defined by$\mathfrak{g}^{(0)} = \mathfrak{g}$,$\mathfrak{g}^{(1)} = [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$, and inductively$\mathfrak{g}^{(n+1)} = [\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}, \mathfrak{g}^{(n)}]$. At each stage one brackets the current subalgebra with itself, producing the "commutator of the commutator" and so on. The first derived subalgebra$\mathfrak{g}^{(1)} = [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$ is spanned by all brackets$[X, Y]$, capturing exactly the non-abelian part of$\mathfrak{g}$; subsequent terms iterate this process, asking how non-abelian the non-abelian part itself is.

Each term $\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}$ is an ideal in$\mathfrak{g}$. The argument is straightforward:$[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}^{(n)}] \subseteq \mathfrak{g}^{(n)}$ follows by induction, using that if$I$ is an ideal then$[I, I]$ is again an ideal (since ideals are preserved under bracketing with elements of$\mathfrak{g}$). The derived series thus produces a descending chain of ideals$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}^{(0)} \supseteq \mathfrak{g}^{(1)} \supseteq \mathfrak{g}^{(2)} \supseteq \cdots$, each normal in$\mathfrak{g}$. This parallels the derived series for groups, where one forms the commutator subgroup, then its commutator subgroup, and so forth; the Lie algebra construction is the linearization of the group-theoretic one.

The quotient $\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{(1)} = \mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$ is the *abelianization* of$\mathfrak{g}$: it is the largest abelian quotient of$\mathfrak{g}$, and every Lie algebra homomorphism from$\mathfrak{g}$ to an abelian Lie algebra factors through this quotient. Whether or not the derived series eventually reaches zero — and how quickly — is the defining question of solvability, taken up in the next subsection.

The derived series also interacts cleanly with Lie algebra homomorphisms. If $\phi: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{h}$ is a homomorphism, then$\phi(\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}) \subseteq \mathfrak{h}^{(n)}$ for all$n$: homomorphisms respect the derived series. In particular, isomorphic Lie algebras have isomorphic derived series. This means the derived series (together with the solvability class, when it terminates) is an isomorphism invariant.

**Example 1.** For an abelian Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} = k^n$:$\mathfrak{g}^{(1)} = [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] = 0$ immediately. The derived series terminates after one step. The abelianization is$\mathfrak{g}$ itself.

**Example 2.** For the Borel subalgebra $\mathfrak{b} = \mathrm{span}\{h, e\}$ of$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with$[h, e] = 2e$:$\mathfrak{b}^{(1)} = [\mathfrak{b}, \mathfrak{b}] = \mathrm{span}\{[h, e]\} = \mathrm{span}\{e\}$. Then$\mathfrak{b}^{(2)} = [\mathfrak{b}^{(1)}, \mathfrak{b}^{(1)}] = [\mathrm{span}\{e\}, \mathrm{span}\{e\}] = \{0\}$. The derived series is$\mathfrak{b} \supset \mathrm{span}\{e\} \supset 0$, terminating after two steps. So$\mathfrak{b}$ is solvable of derived length 2.

**Example 3.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}) = \mathrm{span}\{e, f, h\}$:$\mathfrak{sl}_2^{(1)} = [\mathfrak{sl}_2, \mathfrak{sl}_2] = \mathrm{span}\{[h,e], [h,f], [e,f]\} = \mathrm{span}\{2e, -2f, h\} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$. So$\mathfrak{sl}_2^{(1)} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$, and the derived series is constant:$\mathfrak{sl}_2^{(n)} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ for all$n$. The series never terminates, and$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is not solvable.

**Example 4.** For the upper triangular matrix algebra $\mathfrak{b}_n$ (the Borel subalgebra of$\mathfrak{sl}_n$, consisting of upper triangular traceless matrices): the derived subalgebra$[\mathfrak{b}_n, \mathfrak{b}_n]$ consists of strictly upper triangular matrices. One computes$\mathfrak{b}_n^{(1)} = \mathfrak{n}_n$ (strictly upper triangular),$\mathfrak{b}_n^{(2)} = [\mathfrak{n}_n, \mathfrak{n}_n]$ (matrices nonzero only above the second superdiagonal), and so on. The series terminates after$n-1$ steps, confirming$\mathfrak{b}_n$ is solvable of derived length$n-1$.

The derived series for a Lie algebra is entirely analogous to the derived series for a group, and the "solvability" condition — that the derived series reaches zero — is the same in both settings. The Lie algebra version is more tractable computationally, since everything reduces to linear algebra (spans of brackets), while the group version involves generally complicated commutator subgroups.
