# 48.2 Nilpotent Lie Algebras

Nilpotent Lie algebras form a special subclass of the solvable algebras, defined by the more rapidly terminating lower central series: $\mathfrak{g}^0 = \mathfrak{g}$, $\mathfrak{g}^{k+1} = [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}^k]$. A Lie algebra is nilpotent when $\mathfrak{g}^n = 0$ for some $n$. The distinction from solvability is that the lower central series uses $\mathfrak{g}$ itself at each step rather than the previous term; this is strictly stronger. Every nilpotent Lie algebra is solvable, but the two-dimensional non-abelian algebra $\mathfrak{r} = \text{span}\{e,f\}$ with $[e,f] = f$ is solvable but not nilpotent: its lower central series stabilizes at the line $\text{span}\{f\}$ rather than reaching zero.

Engel's theorem gives an intrinsic characterization of nilpotency that does not require computing series: a finite-dimensional Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent if and only if $\text{ad}_x$ is a nilpotent linear transformation for every $x \in \mathfrak{g}$. This is a remarkable criterion because it replaces a condition on the Lie algebra (iterated brackets going to zero) with a condition on its individual elements acting on itself. The proof uses Engel's lemma — if a Lie algebra of linear maps on a finite-dimensional space $V$ consists entirely of nilpotent maps, then the maps have a common eigenvector with eigenvalue zero — and proceeds by induction on $\dim \mathfrak{g}$.

The Heisenberg algebra $\mathfrak{h} = \text{span}\{p, q, z\}$ with $[p,q] = z$ and $[p,z] = [q,z] = 0$ is the canonical low-dimensional nilpotent algebra, arising in quantum mechanics from the canonical commutation relation. It is the Lie algebra of the Heisenberg Lie group of upper triangular $3\times 3$ unipotent matrices, and it is $2$-step nilpotent: $\mathfrak{h}^1 = \text{span}\{z\}$ and $\mathfrak{h}^2 = 0$. More generally, the algebra $\mathfrak{n}_n$ of strictly upper triangular $n \times n$ matrices is nilpotent of step $n-1$; every nilpotent Lie algebra is isomorphic (by Engel's theorem) to a subalgebra of some $\mathfrak{n}_n$. Nilpotent Lie algebras are in general far from classified: the classification problem grows wild rapidly with dimension, and already in dimension 7 there are continuous families of non-isomorphic nilpotent algebras parameterized by moduli.
