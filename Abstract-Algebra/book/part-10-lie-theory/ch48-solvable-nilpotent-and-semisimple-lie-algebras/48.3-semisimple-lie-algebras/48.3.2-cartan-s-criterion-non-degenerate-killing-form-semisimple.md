# 48.3.2 Cartan's Criterion: Non-Degenerate Killing Form $\Leftrightarrow$ Semisimple

The definition of semisimplicity in terms of the absence of solvable ideals is conceptually clear but difficult to check directly. Cartan's criterion replaces it with a concrete linear-algebraic condition. The *Killing form* of $\mathfrak{g}$ is the symmetric bilinear form$B(X, Y) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}(X) \circ \mathrm{ad}(Y))$, where the trace is taken of the composition of two adjoint operators. The Killing form is intrinsic to$\mathfrak{g}$ — it is defined purely in terms of the adjoint representation and requires no external data. Cartan's criterion then states: over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero (such as$\mathbb{C}$),$\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple if and only if$B$ is non-degenerate.

The proof of one direction uses Lie's theorem. If $\mathfrak{g}$ contains a nonzero solvable ideal$\mathfrak{a}$, then by Lie's theorem the elements of$\mathfrak{a}$ act by upper triangular matrices in any faithful representation; a calculation with traces of products of upper and strictly upper triangular matrices shows that$B(X, Y) = 0$ for all$X \in \mathfrak{a}$ and$Y \in \mathfrak{g}$, making$B$ degenerate. For the other direction, if$B$ is non-degenerate, one first establishes (also via a trace argument) that any abelian ideal lies in the radical of$B$, hence is zero; then a further structural argument using Engel's theorem shows the full radical must vanish.

The practical importance of Cartan's criterion is enormous. To test semisimplicity of a specific Lie algebra, one computes the Gram matrix of $B$ in a chosen basis and checks its determinant. For$\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, for instance, a direct computation yields$B(h, h) = 8$,$B(e, f) = 4$,$B(e, e) = B(f, f) = 0$, giving a Gram matrix with determinant$-128 \neq 0$, confirming semisimplicity. The non-degeneracy of$B$ also endows$\mathfrak{g}$ with a canonical isomorphism$\mathfrak{g} \cong \mathfrak{g}^*$ that is$\mathfrak{g}$-equivariant, a fact that plays a central role in the identification of the Cartan subalgebra with its dual and in the definition of the inner product on the space of roots.

There is also a Cartan criterion for solvability (not just semisimplicity): $\mathfrak{g}$ is solvable if and only if$B(X, Y) = 0$ for all$X \in [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$ and all$Y \in \mathfrak{g}$. This characterizes solvability in terms of the degeneration of$B$ restricted to the derived subalgebra, and it is sometimes phrased as "$\mathfrak{g}$ is solvable iff$B|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}] \times \mathfrak{g}} = 0$."

**Example 1.** Cartan's criterion for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$: the Gram matrix in the basis$\{e, h, f\}$ is$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 8 & 0 \\ 4 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (using$B(h,h) = 8$,$B(e,f) = B(f,e) = 4$, all others zero). The determinant is$-128 \neq 0$. Semisimple confirmed.

**Example 2.** For the abelian algebra $k^n$:$\mathrm{ad}(X) = 0$ for all$X$, so$B = 0$. The Killing form is identically zero, degenerate, confirming the algebra is not semisimple (it is abelian, hence solvable).

**Example 3.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$ (type$A_2$, dimension 8):$B(X,Y) = 6 \cdot \mathrm{tr}(XY)$ (one can verify this by computing on the Chevalley basis). Since$\mathrm{tr}(XY)$ is non-degenerate on$\mathfrak{sl}_3$ (the trace form is non-degenerate on traceless matrices),$B$ is non-degenerate, confirming$\mathfrak{sl}_3$ is semisimple.

**Example 4.** Cartan's solvability criterion for $\mathfrak{b} = \mathrm{span}\{h, e\}$ with$[h,e] = e$: check$B(X,Y) = 0$ for$X \in [\mathfrak{b}, \mathfrak{b}] = \mathrm{span}\{e\}$ and$Y \in \mathfrak{b}$. Compute$B(e, h) = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}(e)\mathrm{ad}(h))$. Now$\mathrm{ad}(e)(h) = [e,h] = -e$,$\mathrm{ad}(e)(e) = 0$;$\mathrm{ad}(h)(h) = 0$,$\mathrm{ad}(h)(e) = e$. Composing:$(\mathrm{ad}(e)\mathrm{ad}(h))(h) = \mathrm{ad}(e)(0) = 0$,$(\mathrm{ad}(e)\mathrm{ad}(h))(e) = \mathrm{ad}(e)(e) = 0$. So$B(e,h) = \mathrm{tr}(0) = 0$. Similarly$B(e,e) = 0$. The criterion confirms$\mathfrak{b}$ is solvable.

Cartan's criterion is the gateway to all of semisimple Lie algebra theory. Once the non-degeneracy of the Killing form is established, it provides the inner product on $\mathfrak{h}^*$ used to define roots, the duality between$\mathfrak{h}$ and$\mathfrak{h}^*$ used to normalize coroots, and the decomposition theory that leads to the classification.
