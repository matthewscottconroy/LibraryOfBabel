# 49.1.1 Cartan Subalgebras: Maximal Abelian, $\mathrm{ad}$-Diagonalizable

To study a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, one begins by finding the most tractable subalgebra from which the rest of$\mathfrak{g}$ can be analyzed. A *Cartan subalgebra*$\mathfrak{h} \subseteq \mathfrak{g}$ is a maximal abelian subalgebra with the additional property that every$\mathrm{ad}(H)$ for$H \in \mathfrak{h}$ is a diagonalizable (semisimple) linear map on$\mathfrak{g}$. The two conditions work together: abelianness ensures that the operators$\{\mathrm{ad}(H) : H \in \mathfrak{h}\}$ form a commuting family, and diagonalizability ensures they can be simultaneously diagonalized, decomposing$\mathfrak{g}$ into eigenspaces.

For the concrete algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{C})$, the Cartan subalgebra is the space of traceless diagonal matrices. These are manifestly commuting and diagonalizable in their adjoint action:$[H, E_{ij}] = (h_i - h_j) E_{ij}$ for any diagonal$H = \mathrm{diag}(h_1, \ldots, h_n)$, showing that each matrix unit$E_{ij}$ is an eigenvector for$\mathrm{ad}(H)$ with eigenvalue$h_i - h_j$. This computation illustrates the general principle: the Cartan subalgebra acts on the rest of$\mathfrak{g}$ by a simultaneously diagonalizable family, and the eigenvalues organize the structure of$\mathfrak{g}$.

The existence and uniqueness of Cartan subalgebras are non-trivial theorems. For any semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$ over$\mathbb{C}$, Cartan subalgebras exist, and any two are conjugate under the action of the adjoint group$\mathrm{Ad}(G)$ (the Lie group with Lie algebra$\mathfrak{g}$). This conjugacy means that the Cartan subalgebra is unique up to the symmetries of$\mathfrak{g}$ itself, and in particular its dimension is an invariant of$\mathfrak{g}$. This dimension is called the *rank* of$\mathfrak{g}$:$\mathfrak{sl}_n$ has rank$n-1$,$\mathfrak{so}(2n)$ has rank$n$,$\mathfrak{so}(2n+1)$ has rank$n$,$\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ has rank$n$, and the exceptional algebra$E_8$ has rank 8.

An element $H \in \mathfrak{g}$ is called *semisimple* if$\mathrm{ad}(H)$ is diagonalizable, and *nilpotent* if$\mathrm{ad}(H)$ is nilpotent. Over algebraically closed fields, every element has a Jordan decomposition$H = H_s + H_n$ with$H_s$ semisimple,$H_n$ nilpotent, and$[H_s, H_n] = 0$. A Cartan subalgebra is exactly a maximal abelian subalgebra of semisimple elements.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}) = \mathrm{span}\{e, f, h\}$: the Cartan subalgebra is$\mathfrak{h} = \mathrm{span}\{h\}$, the 1-dimensional space spanned by the diagonal element$h = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$. We verify:$[h, e] = 2e$,$[h, f] = -2f$,$[h, h] = 0$, so$\mathrm{ad}(h)$ has eigenvalues$2, 0, -2$ on$\mathfrak{sl}_2$. The rank is 1.

**Example 2.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$: the Cartan subalgebra is$\mathfrak{h} = \{H = \mathrm{diag}(h_1, h_2, h_3) : h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0\}$, a 2-dimensional abelian subalgebra. Standard basis:$H_1 = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0)$,$H_2 = \mathrm{diag}(0,1,-1)$. Each$E_{ij}$ (off-diagonal matrix unit) is an eigenvector of$\mathrm{ad}(H)$ for any$H \in \mathfrak{h}$. The rank is 2.

**Example 3.** For $\mathfrak{so}(2n)$: the Cartan subalgebra can be taken to be the block-diagonal matrices consisting of$n$ blocks of the form$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & t_i \\ -t_i & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. This is$n$-dimensional (one parameter$t_i$ per block), giving rank$n$. The simultaneous diagonalization over$\mathbb{C}$ uses the complexification: over$\mathbb{C}$, each block$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & t \\ -t & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues$\pm it$, and the complexified Cartan subalgebra acts on$\mathfrak{so}(2n, \mathbb{C})$ with eigenvalues$\pm(t_i \pm t_j)$ — the roots of type$D_n$.

**Example 4.** Non-uniqueness before conjugacy: in $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, both$\mathrm{span}\{h\}$ and$\mathrm{span}\{ie_1 - ie_2\}$ (for suitable elements) can serve as Cartan subalgebras. They are conjugate by an element of$\mathrm{Ad}(SL_2(\mathbb{C}))$ — specifically by a rotation in$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ that sends one diagonal to another. This is the content of the uniqueness theorem: Cartan subalgebras are conjugate, hence interchangeable for structural purposes.

The Cartan subalgebra is the starting point for the entire structure theory of semisimple Lie algebras: it provides the "frame" (the simultaneously diagonalizable family of operators) from which the root space decomposition is built, and its dimension (the rank) is the primary numerical invariant of the algebra.
