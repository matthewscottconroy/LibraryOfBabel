# 49.1 The Root Space Decomposition

The structural analysis of a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ begins by finding a maximal abelian subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ consisting entirely of elements that act semisimply (i.e., diagonalizably) under the adjoint representation. Such a subalgebra is called a Cartan subalgebra; its dimension $r = \dim \mathfrak{h}$ is the rank of $\mathfrak{g}$. Because the elements of $\mathfrak{h}$ commute and act semisimply, they are simultaneously diagonalizable on any finite-dimensional representation, including the adjoint action on $\mathfrak{g}$ itself. The resulting simultaneous eigenspace decomposition of $\mathfrak{g}$ with respect to $\mathfrak{h}$ is the root space decomposition: $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, where $\mathfrak{g}_\alpha = \{x \in \mathfrak{g} : [h,x] = \alpha(h)x \text{ for all } h \in \mathfrak{h}\}$ and $\alpha: \mathfrak{h} \to \mathbb{C}$ is a linear functional called a root.

For $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb{C})$, the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ is the diagonal matrices of trace zero, and the roots are the functionals $e_i - e_j$ (for $i \neq j$) that extract the $(i,i)$- minus $(j,j)$-diagonal entry. The corresponding root space $\mathfrak{g}_{e_i - e_j}$ is spanned by the elementary matrix $E_{ij}$. The set of roots $\Phi = \{e_i - e_j : i \neq j\} \subset \mathfrak{h}^*$ is the root system of type $A_{n-1}$.

A key structural fact is that each root space $\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$ is one-dimensional (for $\mathfrak{g}$ simple), and for each root $\alpha$ the three-dimensional space $\mathfrak{g}_\alpha \oplus \mathfrak{h}_\alpha \oplus \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}$ (where $\mathfrak{h}_\alpha$ is a canonical one-dimensional subspace of $\mathfrak{h}$) spans a subalgebra isomorphic to $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$. This $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple associated to each root is the workhorse of the theory: it allows one to apply the representation theory of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ — which is completely known from Chapter 47 — to control root strings, to show that the Cartan integers $\langle \beta, \alpha^\vee \rangle$ are integers, and ultimately to establish all the axioms of an abstract root system. The root space decomposition thus converts the study of an arbitrary semisimple Lie algebra into the study of a finite configuration of vectors in a real Euclidean space.
