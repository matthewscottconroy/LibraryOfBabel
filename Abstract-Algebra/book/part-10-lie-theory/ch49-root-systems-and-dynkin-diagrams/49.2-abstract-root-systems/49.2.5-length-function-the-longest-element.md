# 49.2.5 Length Function; the Longest Element $w_0$

Having established that the Weyl group $W$ is generated by simple reflections$s_1, \ldots, s_\ell$, one can measure the complexity of a group element by how efficiently it can be expressed as a product of simple reflections. The *length*$\ell(w)$ of$w \in W$ is the minimum number of simple reflections in any expression$w = s_{i_1} \cdots s_{i_k}$; an expression achieving this minimum is called *reduced*. The length function is intimately connected to the geometry of the root system: one can show that$\ell(w) = |\{\alpha \in \Phi^+ : w(\alpha) \in \Phi^-\}|$, the number of positive roots that$w$ sends to negative roots.

Since $W$ is finite and$|\Phi^+|$ is finite, there is a maximum possible length, and it is achieved by a unique element. The *longest element*$w_0 \in W$ is the unique element of maximum length, characterized by$w_0(\Phi^+) = \Phi^-$: it sends every positive root to a negative root, thereby reversing the entire choice of positivity. It follows that$w_0$ has order 2: applying it twice recovers all positive roots, so$w_0^2 = \mathrm{id}$. In some types$w_0 = -\mathrm{id}$ (the negative of the identity as a linear map on$V$), but this is not always the case.

The length function and the longest element are central to the combinatorics of representation theory. The *Bruhat order* on $W$, defined by$u \leq w$ if some reduced expression for$u$ appears as a subexpression of some reduced expression for$w$, gives$W$ the structure of a graded poset with unique minimum$\mathrm{id}$ and unique maximum$w_0$. This order governs the geometry of Schubert varieties and the structure of Verma modules.

The action of $w_0$ on the simple roots is also important:$w_0(\alpha_i) = -\alpha_{\sigma(i)}$ where$\sigma$ is a permutation of$\{1, \ldots, \ell\}$ called the *opposition involution* or *Dynkin diagram automorphism* induced by$w_0$. For types$A_n$ with$n \geq 2$,$\sigma$ is the reversal$i \mapsto n+1-i$; for$B_n, C_n, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2$,$\sigma = \mathrm{id}$; for$D_n$,$\sigma$ swaps the two spinor nodes; for$E_6$,$\sigma$ is the non-trivial automorphism.

**Example 1.** For $A_1$ ($W = \mathbb{Z}/2$):$w_0 = s_\alpha$ (the single simple reflection),$\ell(w_0) = 1 = |\Phi^+| = 1$. Here$w_0 = -\mathrm{id}$ on$V = \mathbb{R}^1$.

**Example 2.** For $A_2$ ($W = S_3$):$\ell(w_0) = |\Phi^+| = 3$. The longest element is$w_0 = s_1 s_2 s_1 = s_2 s_1 s_2$ (these two reduced expressions are equal by the braid relation). It sends$\alpha_1 \mapsto -\alpha_2$,$\alpha_2 \mapsto -\alpha_1$. The opposition involution is$1 \leftrightarrow 2$, consistent with the$A_2$ Dynkin diagram having a$\mathbb{Z}/2$ automorphism.

**Example 3.** For $B_2$ ($|W| = 8$,$|\Phi^+| = 4$):$\ell(w_0) = 4$. A reduced expression:$w_0 = s_1 s_2 s_1 s_2$. One can verify$w_0(\alpha_1) = -\alpha_1$ and$w_0(\alpha_2) = -\alpha_2$, so$w_0 = -\mathrm{id}$. In$B_2$, the opposition involution is trivial.

**Example 4.** For $A_3$ ($W = S_4$,$|\Phi^+| = 6$):$w_0 = s_1 s_2 s_3 s_1 s_2 s_1$ (the longest permutation$[4,3,2,1]$ in one-line notation).$\ell(w_0) = 6$. The opposition involution swaps nodes 1 and 3 (fixes node 2):$w_0(\alpha_1) = -\alpha_3$,$w_0(\alpha_2) = -\alpha_2$,$w_0(\alpha_3) = -\alpha_1$. This is the reversal automorphism of the$A_3$ Dynkin diagram.

The Bruhat order and the structure of $w_0$ appear in the computation of the Weyl character formula (the alternating sum over$W$ has$w_0$ as the unique element of maximal sign contribution), in the structure of Verma module homomorphisms, and in the theory of Schubert calculus on flag varieties.
