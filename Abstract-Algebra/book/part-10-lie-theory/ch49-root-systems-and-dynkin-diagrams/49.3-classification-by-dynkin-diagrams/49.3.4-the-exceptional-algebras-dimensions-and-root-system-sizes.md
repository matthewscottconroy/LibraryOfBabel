# 49.3.4 The Exceptional Algebras: Dimensions and Root System Sizes

The five exceptional Lie algebras $G_2$,$F_4$,$E_6$,$E_7$,$E_8$ are not curiosities or pathological edge cases — they are among the most important objects in mathematics, arising in geometry, physics, and number theory in ways that reflect their exceptional internal symmetry. Their basic numerical data is as follows:$G_2$ has dimension 14, rank 2, and$|\Phi^+| = 6$;$F_4$ has dimension 52, rank 4, and$|\Phi^+| = 24$;$E_6$ has dimension 78, rank 6, and$|\Phi^+| = 36$;$E_7$ has dimension 133, rank 7, and$|\Phi^+| = 63$;$E_8$ has dimension 248, rank 8, and$|\Phi^+| = 120$.

These dimensions are computed from the formula $\dim \mathfrak{g} = \ell + 2|\Phi^+|$ (rank plus twice the number of positive roots), reflecting the decomposition of$\mathfrak{g}$ into the Cartan subalgebra$\mathfrak{h}$ ($\ell$ dimensions) and the root spaces$\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$ for$\alpha \in \Phi^+$ and$\alpha \in \Phi^-$ (one dimension each). For$E_8$:$8 + 2 \cdot 120 = 248$. The dimension 248 of$E_8$ has become famous in theoretical physics because$E_8 \times E_8$ is the gauge group of one of the two anomaly-free heterotic string theories, and$E_8$ appears in the structure of the Monster group's moonshine module.

The algebra $E_8$ is in many respects the most remarkable: it is the largest and most symmetric of the exceptional algebras, its root system consists of 240 roots that form the vertices of a highly symmetric polytope in$\mathbb{R}^8$, and its Killing form restricted to the root lattice gives the densest packing of spheres in$\mathbb{R}^8$ (recently proved optimal by Viazovska in 2016). The exceptional algebras also appear in the classification of simple Jordan algebras and in the geometry of octonions and the projective plane$\mathbb{OP}^2$.

Each exceptional algebra has a Weyl group with computable order: $|W(G_2)| = 12$,$|W(F_4)| = 1152$,$|W(E_6)| = 51840$,$|W(E_7)| = 2903040$,$|W(E_8)| = 696729600$. These orders are products of numbers of the form$d_i$ (the degrees of the basic polynomial invariants of$W$), a deep result connecting root systems to invariant theory.

**Example 1.** $G_2$: dimension 14, rank 2. The Dynkin diagram has two nodes with a triple edge (arrow pointing from the long root to the short root). The 6 positive roots include 3 short and 3 long. The smallest irreducible representation (the standard representation, not the adjoint) has dimension 7. The next, the adjoint representation$L(\omega_2)$, has dimension 14. The$G_2$ algebra can be constructed as the derivation algebra of the octonions$\mathbb{O}$:$G_2 = \mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{O})$.

**Example 2.** $F_4$: dimension 52, rank 4. The Dynkin diagram is$\circ - \circ \Rightarrow \circ - \circ$ (a chain of 4 with a double edge in the middle). It has 24 positive roots (12 long, 12 short, since$F_4$ has two root lengths). The smallest representation has dimension 26 (the "exceptional" representation corresponding to$L(\omega_4)$).$F_4$ is the automorphism group of the exceptional Jordan algebra$J_3(\mathbb{O})$ (3-by-3 Hermitian matrices over the octonions).

**Example 3.** $E_6$: dimension 78, rank 6. The Dynkin diagram is a chain of 5 with one branch node (the 3rd node has degree 3). It has 36 positive roots. The smallest faithful representation has dimension 27, corresponding to the fundamental weight$\omega_1$.$E_6$ appears in the context of the exceptional Jordan algebra and in string theory/M-theory compactifications. It has a non-trivial Dynkin diagram automorphism (the symmetry that reverses the diagram), which corresponds to a non-inner outer automorphism of the Lie algebra.

**Example 4.** $E_7$: dimension 133, rank 7. The diagram is similar to$E_6$ with one extra node at the end of the long branch (6 nodes in the main chain, 1 branch node). The smallest representation has dimension 56.$E_7$ is self-dual (its Dynkin diagram has no outer automorphisms), and the 56-dimensional representation carries a symplectic structure, reflecting the fact that the compact real form of$E_7$ is related to the quaternionic projective plane$\mathbb{HP}^2$.

That these extraordinary objects are uniquely determined by the simple combinatorial data of their Dynkin diagrams — a handful of nodes and edges — is a testament to the power of the root system framework. The classification does not merely list the exceptional algebras but explains why no others can exist: any other connected positive-definite Dynkin diagram violates one of the integrality/positive-definiteness constraints.
