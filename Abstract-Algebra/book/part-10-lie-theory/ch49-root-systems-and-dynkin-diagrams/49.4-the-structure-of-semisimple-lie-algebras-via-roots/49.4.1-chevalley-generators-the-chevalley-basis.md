# 49.4.1 Chevalley Generators $e_\alpha$,$f_\alpha$,$h_\alpha$; the Chevalley Basis

The root space decomposition $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$ provides a natural vector-space basis for any semisimple Lie algebra, but one must choose basis vectors for each one-dimensional root space$\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$. The *Chevalley basis* is a canonical choice, due to Claude Chevalley, that normalizes the structure constants in a remarkable way. For each simple root$\alpha_i$, one chooses generators$e_i \in \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_i}$ and$f_i \in \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_i}$, and for each positive root$\alpha$ one defines$e_\alpha \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$ and$f_\alpha = e_{-\alpha} \in \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}$, together with$h_\alpha = [e_\alpha, f_\alpha] \in \mathfrak{h}$. The Chevalley normalization requires that the structure constants$N_{\alpha, \beta}$ defined by$[e_\alpha, e_\beta] = N_{\alpha,\beta} e_{\alpha+\beta}$ (when$\alpha + \beta$ is a root) are all integers.

The existence of such a basis is non-trivial. The proof uses the root string theory: the constants $N_{\alpha, \beta}$ are determined up to sign by the root string through$\beta$ for$\alpha$, and a careful global sign choice (requiring a compatibility condition around "triangles" in the root system) produces a consistent set of integer structure constants. Once this is done, the entire Lie algebra is defined over$\mathbb{Z}$: the bracket of any two basis elements is an integer linear combination of basis elements.

The consequences of the Chevalley basis being integral are far-reaching. Since $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_\alpha, f_\alpha, h_\alpha\}$ is a Lie algebra over$\mathbb{Z}$, one can tensor with any field$\Bbbk$ to obtain$\mathfrak{g}_\Bbbk = \mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \Bbbk$, a Lie algebra over$\Bbbk$. This works even for fields of positive characteristic$p$, enabling the study of modular Lie algebras and their representations, and — by passing to the corresponding algebraic groups — gives the *Chevalley groups* over finite fields, which constitute most of the infinite families of finite simple groups.

The Chevalley basis also makes explicit the triangular decomposition $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{n}^- \oplus \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^+$: the positive root vectors$\{e_\alpha : \alpha \in \Phi^+\}$ span$\mathfrak{n}^+$, the negative root vectors$\{f_\alpha : \alpha \in \Phi^+\}$ span$\mathfrak{n}^-$, and the coroots$\{h_\alpha : \alpha \text{ simple}\}$ form a basis of$\mathfrak{h}$.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$: the Chevalley basis is$e = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$,$f = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$,$h = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ with$[h,e] = 2e$,$[h,f] = -2f$,$[e,f] = h$. All structure constants are integers ($\pm 1$ or$\pm 2$). This is the standard basis.

**Example 2.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$: the Chevalley basis consists of$\{e_{12}, e_{23}, e_{13}, f_{12}, f_{23}, f_{13}, h_1, h_2\}$ where$e_{ij} = E_{ij}$ (matrix unit),$f_{ij} = E_{ji}$,$h_1 = E_{11} - E_{22}$,$h_2 = E_{22} - E_{33}$. The nontrivial bracket$[e_{12}, e_{23}] = e_{13}$ has integer coefficient 1. The coroots:$h_{\alpha_1} = h_1$,$h_{\alpha_2} = h_2$,$h_{\alpha_1 + \alpha_2} = h_1 + h_2$. All structure constants are$\pm 1$.

**Example 3.** For a general simple Lie algebra, the structure constant $N_{\alpha,\beta}$ for non-simple positive roots$\alpha, \beta$ with$\alpha + \beta \in \Phi$ satisfies$N_{\alpha,\beta} = \pm(p+1)$ where$p$ is the length of the$\alpha$-string through$\beta$ below$\beta$. For$\mathfrak{sl}_3$:$N_{\alpha_1, \alpha_2} = \pm 1$ since the$\alpha_1$-string through$\alpha_2$ is$\{\alpha_2, \alpha_1 + \alpha_2\}$ ($p = 0$, so$p + 1 = 1$). This is integer, confirming the Chevalley property.

**Example 4.** The Chevalley group $SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$: obtained by exponentiating the Chevalley basis of$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ over$\mathbb{F}_q$. Since the structure constants are integers, one can define$x_+(t) = \exp(te) = I + te \in SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$ and$x_-(t) = \exp(tf)$ for$t \in \mathbb{F}_q$. These generate$SL_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$, and for$q = p$ a prime,$PSL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is simple for$p \geq 5$. The Chevalley construction extends this to all types$A_n, B_n, \ldots, E_8$ over any field or even ring.

The Chevalley basis thus connects the elegant complex-analytic classification over $\mathbb{C}$ to the concrete arithmetic structure of Lie algebras over arbitrary fields, making the classification theorem applicable far beyond its original setting.
