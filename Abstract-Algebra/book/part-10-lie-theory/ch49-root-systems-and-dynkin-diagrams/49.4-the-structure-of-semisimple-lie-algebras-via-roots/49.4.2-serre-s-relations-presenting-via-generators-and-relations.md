# 49.4.2 Serre's Relations: Presenting $\mathfrak{g}$ via Generators and Relations

The root space decomposition and the Chevalley basis show that every semisimple Lie algebra is generated (as a Lie algebra) by the simple root generators $\{e_i, f_i, h_i : 1 \leq i \leq \ell\}$. The question is: what is the minimal set of relations among these generators that completely determines$\mathfrak{g}$? *Serre's theorem* provides a clean answer. The Lie algebra$\mathfrak{g}$ is isomorphic to the Lie algebra generated by$3\ell$ elements$\{e_i, f_i, h_i\}$ subject to the following relations: the$h_i$ commute with each other ($[h_i, h_j] = 0$); each$e_j$ is an eigenvector for$\mathrm{ad}(h_i)$ with eigenvalue$A_{ij}$ ($[h_i, e_j] = A_{ij} e_j$); each$f_j$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue$-A_{ij}$ ($[h_i, f_j] = -A_{ij} f_j$); opposite generators interact via the coroot ($[e_i, f_j] = \delta_{ij} h_i$); and the *Serre relations*$(\mathrm{ad}\, e_i)^{1-A_{ij}} e_j = 0$ and$(\mathrm{ad}\, f_i)^{1-A_{ij}} f_j = 0$ for$i \neq j$.

The first four families of relations are natural and easy to motivate. The relation $[e_i, f_j] = 0$ for$i \neq j$ says that generators from different simple roots do not interact across the positive/negative divide. The Serre relations are more subtle:$(\mathrm{ad}\, e_i)^{1-A_{ij}} e_j = 0$ says that the bracket$[e_i, [e_i, [\cdots [e_i, e_j]\cdots]]]$ with$1 - A_{ij}$ copies of$e_i$ is zero. Since$A_{ij} \in \{0, -1, -2, -3\}$ for$i \neq j$, this is a relation involving 1, 2, 3, or 4 brackets. The Serre relations encode the root string information: the$\alpha_i$-string through$\alpha_j$ has length$1 - A_{ij}$, and one cannot raise$\alpha_j$ by more than this many copies of$\alpha_i$ and remain in the root system.

The content of Serre's theorem is twofold. First, the given relations are satisfied in $\mathfrak{g}$: this follows from the root string analysis and the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-representation theory. Second — and this is the non-trivial part — these relations are sufficient: the Lie algebra defined by generators and these relations alone is finite-dimensional and isomorphic to$\mathfrak{g}$. The proof of sufficiency uses the fact that the "free" Lie algebra on these generators modulo only the first four families of relations is already quite constrained, and the Serre relations cut it down to the correct finite-dimensional algebra.

This presentation theorem shows that $\mathfrak{g}$ is completely and explicitly determined by the Cartan matrix, making the classification of simple Lie algebras via Dynkin diagrams fully rigorous and computational.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$:$\ell = 1$, one generator set$\{e, f, h\}$ with$[h, e] = A_{11} e = 2e$,$[h, f] = -2f$,$[e, f] = h$. No Serre relations (there is only one node, no$i \neq j$ pairs). This is the complete presentation of$\mathfrak{sl}_2$.

**Example 2.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$ ($A_2$, Cartan matrix$A_{12} = A_{21} = -1$): Serre relations are$(\mathrm{ad}\, e_1)^{1-(-1)} e_2 = (\mathrm{ad}\, e_1)^2 e_2 = [e_1, [e_1, e_2]] = 0$ and similarly$[e_2, [e_2, e_1]] = 0$ (and the$f$-versions). Since$1 - A_{12} = 2$, both Serre relations say that the double bracket vanishes. This means$[e_1, e_2]$ (the positive root$\alpha_1 + \alpha_2$) cannot be further raised by either$e_1$ or$e_2$.

**Example 3.** For $G_2$ (Cartan matrix$A_{12} = -1$,$A_{21} = -3$): the Serre relations for the simple roots are$(\mathrm{ad}\, e_1)^{1-(-1)} e_2 = (\mathrm{ad}\, e_1)^2 e_2 = 0$ and$(\mathrm{ad}\, e_2)^{1-(-3)} e_1 = (\mathrm{ad}\, e_2)^4 e_1 = 0$. The second relation involves a 4-fold bracket:$[e_2, [e_2, [e_2, [e_2, e_1]]]] = 0$. This long relation is what stops the root string$\alpha_1, \alpha_1 + \alpha_2, \alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2, \alpha_1 + 3\alpha_2$ from continuing further.

**Example 4.** The verification that Serre relations are satisfied in $\mathfrak{sl}_3$:$[e_1, [e_1, e_2]] = [E_{12}, [E_{12}, E_{23}]] = [E_{12}, E_{13}]$. Now$E_{12} \cdot E_{13} = 0$ (since position$(1,3)$ of$E_{12} \cdot E_{13}$ is the dot product of row 1 of$E_{12}$ with column 3 of$E_{13}$, which is zero since$E_{12}$ has its nonzero entry in position$(1,2)$ and$E_{13}$ has its nonzero entry in position$(1,3)$, so row-column incompatibility). Actually$[E_{12}, E_{13}] = E_{12}E_{13} - E_{13}E_{12} = 0 - E_{13}E_{12} = -E_{13}E_{12}$, and$E_{13}E_{12}$ has$(i,j)$-entry$\sum_k (E_{13})_{ik}(E_{12})_{kj} = \delta_{i1}\delta_{k3}\delta_{k1}\delta_{j2}$, which is zero since$k = 3 \neq 1$. So$[E_{12}, E_{13}] = 0$. The Serre relation holds.

Serre's theorem is the bridge between the abstract classification (Dynkin diagrams) and the concrete algebraic structure (generators and relations). It enables explicit computation with any semisimple Lie algebra by reducing everything to matrix operations on the $3\ell \times 3\ell$ system defined by the Cartan matrix.
