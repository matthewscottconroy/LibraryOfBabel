# 49.4 The Structure of Semisimple Lie Algebras via Roots

Having classified root systems combinatorially, one needs to verify the other direction of the correspondence: that every admissible Dynkin diagram actually arises from a semisimple Lie algebra, and that the Lie algebra is uniquely determined by its Dynkin diagram. Both statements are handled by an explicit presentation. The Chevalley generators are elements $e_i \in \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_i}$, $f_i \in \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_i}$, and $h_i = [e_i, f_i] \in \mathfrak{h}$ for each simple root $\alpha_i$; they satisfy the Chevalley-Serre relations: $[h_i, h_j] = 0$, $[h_i, e_j] = A_{ij} e_j$, $[h_i, f_j] = -A_{ij} f_j$, $[e_i, f_j] = \delta_{ij} h_i$, and the Serre relations $(\text{ad}_{e_i})^{1-A_{ij}}(e_j) = 0$ (and the analogous relations for the $f_i$). This is a presentation by generators and relations, and the remarkable content of Serre's theorem is that these relations are sufficient: the Lie algebra defined by this presentation is a simple Lie algebra with the given Dynkin diagram.

The Chevalley basis provides a $\mathbb{Z}$-form of the Lie algebra — a $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice inside $\mathfrak{g}$ spanned by certain root vectors with integral structure constants — which allows one to define analogues of the simple Lie algebras over any field, including finite fields. The Chevalley groups over finite fields, constructed from Chevalley bases, provide the bulk of the classification of finite simple groups; the connection between these two great classification theorems is mediated by the root system data.

The root space decomposition also reveals the dimension of a simple Lie algebra: $\dim \mathfrak{g} = r + |\Phi|$ where $r$ is the rank and $|\Phi|$ is the number of roots. For $\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}$ (type $A_n$), $r = n$ and $|\Phi| = n(n+1)$, giving $\dim \mathfrak{sl}_{n+1} = n + n(n+1) = n^2 + 2n = (n+1)^2 - 1$. For $E_8$, $r = 8$ and $|\Phi| = 240$, giving $\dim E_8 = 248$, which appears in the symmetry of the string theory superalgebra. The structure constants of the Chevalley basis — the numbers $N_{\alpha\beta}$ defined by $[e_\alpha, e_\beta] = N_{\alpha\beta} e_{\alpha+\beta}$ — are integers satisfying $N_{\alpha\beta} = -N_{\beta\alpha}$ and satisfying quadratic relations derived from the Jacobi identity; computing them explicitly for the classical series is a concrete exercise in the combinatorics of root systems that repays careful study.
