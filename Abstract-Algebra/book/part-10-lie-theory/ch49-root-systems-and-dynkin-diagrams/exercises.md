# Chapter 49 — Exercises

## Important Figures

- **Wilhelm Killing (1847–1923)** — discovered root systems while classifying Lie algebras (1888–1890); the Killing form; identified the exceptional Lie algebras $E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2$
- **Élie Cartan (1869–1951)** — corrected Killing's errors; rigorous proof of the classification; Cartan matrices; the theory in its definitive form (1894)
- **Eugene Dynkin (1924–2014)** — Dynkin diagrams (1947): clean graphical encoding of Cartan matrices; simplified the classification dramatically
- **Hermann Weyl (1885–1955)** — Weyl group; Weyl chamber; Weyl character formula

## References and Primary Sources

- **É. Cartan, "Sur la structure des groupes de transformations finis et continus" (1894)** — doctoral thesis; the classification
- **E. Dynkin, "The Structure of Semisimple Lie Algebras" (1947)** — *Uspehi Mat. Nauk* 2 — Dynkin diagrams introduced
- **J. Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory* (Springer, 1972)**, Chs. II–III — root systems; classification

## Examples, Applications, and Thought Experiments

- **The $A_2$ root system** — 6 roots in $\mathbb{R}^2$: $\pm\alpha_1, \pm\alpha_2, \pm(\alpha_1+\alpha_2)$; arranged as the vertices of a regular hexagon; Weyl group $\cong S_3$; Dynkin diagram $\circ\!-\!\circ$ (two nodes, one edge); corresponds to $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$
- **The ADE classification** — the simply-laced Dynkin diagrams are: type $A_n$ (chain), $D_n$ (fork), $E_6, E_7, E_8$; the same diagrams appear in the classification of finite subgroups of $SU(2)$ (McKay correspondence) and the classification of simple singularities; an unexpected and deep convergence in mathematics
- **The exceptional algebra $G_2$** — Dynkin diagram $\circ \Rightarrow \circ$ (double bond with arrow); the automorphism group of the octonions; 14-dimensional; its root system has roots of two different lengths; the exceptional Lie algebras are the "irregularities" that make the classification not just $A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n$
- **Thought experiment: "the crystal of a root system"** — imagine the roots as vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$; the Weyl group acts by reflections across root hyperplanes; the "Weyl chambers" are the connected components of the complement; the root system is a highly symmetric configuration that captures all the information needed to reconstruct the Lie algebra via the Serre relations

## Exercises

1. Verify that the six vectors $\Phi = \{\pm e_1, \pm e_2, \pm(e_1 - e_2)\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ (with $e_1, e_2$ the standard basis vectors, after appropriate rescaling to form a regular hexagon) constitute an abstract root system of type $A_2$. Check each axiom: spanning, the reflection axiom $s_\alpha(\Phi) = \Phi$, and the integrality of Cartan integers $\langle \beta, \alpha^\vee \rangle$.

2. Identify the root system of $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$ explicitly. Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be the diagonal matrices and identify the roots as functionals $e_i - e_j$ for $i \neq j$. Choose simple roots $\alpha_1 = e_1 - e_2$ and $\alpha_2 = e_2 - e_3$, write every positive root as a sum of simple roots, and draw the root system in the plane.

3. For each of the following root systems, identify the type ($A_n$, $B_n$, $C_n$, or $D_n$) and draw the Dynkin diagram: (a) rank 2 with roots $\{\pm \alpha, \pm \beta, \pm(\alpha + \beta)\}$ where $|\alpha| = |\beta|$ and $\angle(\alpha,\beta) = 120°$; (b) rank 2 with roots $\{\pm \alpha, \pm \beta, \pm(\alpha+\beta), \pm(2\alpha+\beta)\}$ where $|\beta| = \sqrt{2}|\alpha|$; (c) the root system of $\mathfrak{so}_5 \cong \mathfrak{sp}_4$.

4. Write down the Cartan matrix for the root system $B_2$: compute $A_{ij} = \langle \alpha_i, \alpha_j^\vee \rangle$ for the two simple roots, and verify that the Dynkin diagram has a double bond with arrow from the long root to the short root. Do the same for $G_2$ (triple bond).

5. Describe the Weyl group $W$ of type $A_n$ as a group of permutations of $\{1, 2, \ldots, n+1\}$, and show $W \cong S_{n+1}$. Then describe the Weyl group of type $B_2$ as a group of transformations of $\mathbb{R}^2$ generated by two reflections, identify it as a dihedral group $D_4$, and compute its order.

6. For the root system of type $A_2$, enumerate all positive roots, all Weyl chambers, and verify that the Weyl group $W \cong S_3$ acts simply transitively on the Weyl chambers. Show that the longest element $w_0 \in W$ sends every positive root to a negative root.

7. Use the Serre relations to verify that the Lie algebra presented by generators $e_1, f_1, h_1, e_2, f_2, h_2$ with relations $[h_i, e_j] = A_{ij} e_j$, $[h_i, f_j] = -A_{ij} f_j$, $[e_i, f_j] = \delta_{ij} h_i$, and Serre relations $(\text{ad}_{e_i})^{1-A_{ij}} e_j = 0$ (and similarly for $f$), with the Cartan matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$, is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$ by identifying the generators with the elementary matrices.

8. (Challenge) Classify all rank-2 root systems up to isomorphism by analyzing which Cartan matrices $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -a \\ -b & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ and $ab \leq 3$ yield positive-definite quadratic forms. Show that the only possibilities are $ab \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, corresponding to types $A_1 \times A_1$, $A_2$, $B_2$, and $G_2$ respectively, and verify the dimension formula $\dim \mathfrak{g} = \text{rank} + |\Phi|$ in each case.
