# 50.1.1 Representations of $\mathfrak{g}$;$\mathfrak{h}$-Semisimplicity; Weight Spaces$V_\lambda$

The central goal of this chapter is to classify all finite-dimensional representations of a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$. The key to this classification is understanding how the Cartan subalgebra$\mathfrak{h}$ acts. Because$\mathfrak{h}$ is abelian, its elements act simultaneously diagonalizably on any finite-dimensional$\mathfrak{g}$-module, and this forces the module to decompose into a direct sum of simultaneous eigenspaces. It is this decomposition — the weight space decomposition — that provides the combinatorial scaffolding for everything that follows.

A representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism$\rho: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{gl}(V)$, making$V$ into a$\mathfrak{g}$-module. Since$\mathfrak{h}$ consists of semisimple elements (by definition of a semisimple Lie algebra), each$H \in \mathfrak{h}$ acts on$V$ by a diagonalizable operator. The elements of$\mathfrak{h}$ also commute with one another, so they can be simultaneously diagonalized. This means$V$ decomposes as$V = \bigoplus_{\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*} V_\lambda$, where$V_\lambda = \{v \in V : H \cdot v = \lambda(H)v \text{ for all } H \in \mathfrak{h}\}$ is the simultaneous eigenspace for eigenvalue$\lambda$. A nonzero functional$\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ for which$V_\lambda \neq 0$ is called a *weight* of the representation, and$V_\lambda$ is the corresponding *weight space*.

The key insight is that the roots of $\mathfrak{g}$ — which were already weight spaces for the adjoint representation — interact with the weight spaces of any representation in a predictable way. Specifically, if$v \in V_\lambda$ and$e_\alpha \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$ (a root space), then$e_\alpha \cdot v \in V_{\lambda + \alpha}$: the root vectors shift weights by the corresponding root. The proof is a single line:$H \cdot (e_\alpha \cdot v) = [H, e_\alpha] \cdot v + e_\alpha \cdot (H \cdot v) = \alpha(H) e_\alpha v + \lambda(H) e_\alpha v = (\alpha + \lambda)(H) e_\alpha v$. This "root space action shifts weight" principle is the engine that drives the entire theory, allowing us to move between weight spaces and ultimately to pin down the structure of the whole module from a single extremal vector.

The weight space decomposition is not just a theoretical tool — it is a complete description of the $\mathfrak{h}$-module structure. The collection of weights (with multiplicities$\dim V_\lambda$) constitutes the *weight diagram* of the representation, a finite subset of$\mathfrak{h}^*$ that is invariant under the Weyl group action. For$\mathfrak{sl}_2$, the weight diagram is a set of integers symmetric about$0$; for higher-rank algebras it is a polytope-like pattern in the weight lattice.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ acting on$V = \mathbb{C}^2$ by the standard representation ($\rho(e) = \begin{pmatrix} 0&1\\0&0 \end{pmatrix}$, etc.): the Cartan element$h$ acts as$\begin{pmatrix} 1&0\\0&-1 \end{pmatrix}$. Weight spaces:$V_1 = \mathrm{span}\{e_1\}$ (eigenvalue$+1$) and$V_{-1} = \mathrm{span}\{e_2\}$ (eigenvalue$-1$). The weight diagram is$\{+1, -1\}$ with each weight space 1-dimensional.

**Example 2.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ acting on$V = \mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$ (3-dimensional): basis$\{x^2, xy, y^2\}$. Action:$h \cdot x^2 = 2x^2$,$h \cdot xy = 0$,$h \cdot y^2 = -2y^2$. Weight spaces:$V_2 = \mathrm{span}\{x^2\}$,$V_0 = \mathrm{span}\{xy\}$,$V_{-2} = \mathrm{span}\{y^2\}$. Weight diagram$\{2, 0, -2\}$, each 1-dimensional. This is the irreducible$V_2$ (spin-1 representation).

**Example 3.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$ acting on$\mathbb{C}^3$: the Cartan subalgebra$\mathfrak{h}$ consists of traceless diagonal matrices. The standard basis vector$e_i$ lies in the weight space$V_{\epsilon_i}$ where$\epsilon_i(H) = h_i$ for$H = \mathrm{diag}(h_1, h_2, h_3)$. The three weights are$\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3$ with$\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 + \epsilon_3 = 0$ (since we restrict to$\mathfrak{sl}_3$). The weight diagram is a triangle of three points in$\mathfrak{h}^*$.

**Example 4.** The weight diagram of the adjoint representation of $\mathfrak{g}$: the weights are exactly the roots$\Phi$ (each with multiplicity 1) plus$0$ (with multiplicity$\ell = \mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{g})$). For$\mathfrak{sl}_3$: the adjoint representation is 8-dimensional with weights being the 6 roots$\pm\alpha_1, \pm\alpha_2, \pm(\alpha_1+\alpha_2)$ (each multiplicity 1) and$0$ (multiplicity 2, corresponding to$\mathfrak{h}$). The weight diagram is the hexagonal arrangement of 6 roots plus the origin of multiplicity 2.

The weight space decomposition is the fundamental decomposition of any $\mathfrak{g}$-module, and the interaction between root spaces and weight spaces (root vectors shift weights) is the core mechanism of the entire representation theory.
