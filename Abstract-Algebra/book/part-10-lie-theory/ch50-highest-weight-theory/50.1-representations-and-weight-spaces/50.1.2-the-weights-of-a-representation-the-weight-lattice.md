# 50.1.2 The Weights of a Representation; the Weight Lattice $P$

Not all functionals $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ can appear as weights of finite-dimensional representations. The$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-theory (which we study in detail in the next subsection) forces the weights to be integral with respect to each coroot, and this integrality condition carves out a lattice inside$\mathfrak{h}^*$ that contains all the weights of any finite-dimensional module.

The *weight lattice* is the subgroup $P = \{\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^* : \langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ for all } \alpha \in \Phi\}$, where$\alpha^\vee = 2\alpha/\langle \alpha, \alpha \rangle$ is the coroot of$\alpha$. It suffices to require integrality only for the simple coroots$\alpha_i^\vee$, since these generate the coroot lattice. The roots themselves lie in$P$ — they appear as weights of the adjoint representation — and the root lattice$Q = \mathbb{Z}\Phi$ is a sublattice of$P$. The quotient$P/Q$ is always finite, and its order is the determinant of the Cartan matrix.

The *dominant weights* are those in $P^+ = \{\lambda \in P : \langle \lambda, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle \geq 0 \text{ for all simple } \alpha_i\}$, the intersection of$P$ with the closed dominant Weyl chamber. These will turn out to parametrize the irreducible representations. A distinguished basis for$P^+$ is provided by the *fundamental weights*$\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_\ell$, defined by the dual pairing$\langle \omega_i, \alpha_j^\vee \rangle = \delta_{ij}$. Every dominant integral weight is a non-negative integer combination of fundamental weights, so the dominant weights form a free commutative monoid with generators$\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_\ell$.

The weight lattice $P$ contains the root lattice$Q$ as a finite-index sublattice. The index$[P:Q] = \det A$ where$A$ is the Cartan matrix. For the classical types:$[P:Q]$ is$n+1$ for$A_n$,$2$ for$B_n$,$2$ for$C_n$,$4$ for$D_n$ (when$n$ is even) or$2$ (when$n$ is odd). The group$P/Q$ is called the *center* of the simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra$\mathfrak{g}$, and its order equals the number of connected Lie groups with the given Lie algebra.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$: the coroot is$h^\vee = h$,$\langle \lambda, h \rangle = \lambda(h)$. The weight lattice is$P = \{n\omega : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ where$\omega(h) = 1$. The root lattice is$Q = \{2n\omega : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ (since the root$\alpha = 2\omega$ generates$Q$). So$[P:Q] = 2$, consistent with the center of$SU(2)$ being$\mathbb{Z}/2$.

**Example 2.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_3$:$P = \{\lambda : \langle \lambda, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle \in \mathbb{Z}, i=1,2\}$. The fundamental weights are$\omega_1, \omega_2$ with$\langle \omega_i, \alpha_j^\vee \rangle = \delta_{ij}$. In coordinates:$\omega_1 = (2/3, -1/3, -1/3)$ and$\omega_2 = (1/3, 1/3, -2/3)$ in the basis$\epsilon_i$ of$\mathbb{R}^3$ restricted to the hyperplane$\sum h_i = 0$. The root lattice$Q = \mathbb{Z}\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2\}$ has index 3 in$P$, consistent with$Z(SU(3)) = \mathbb{Z}/3$.

**Example 3.** Dominant integral weights of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$:$P^+ = \{m\omega : m \geq 0, m \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. The non-negative integer multiples of the fundamental weight$\omega$. These correspond to the irreducible representations$V_m$ of dimension$m+1$.

**Example 4.** The weight lattice for $\mathfrak{sl}_3$: elements are$a\omega_1 + b\omega_2$ for$a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$. The dominant weights are those with$a, b \geq 0$. The representation$L(a\omega_1 + b\omega_2)$ has dimension$\frac{1}{2}(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)$ (Weyl dimension formula). The weights of$L(a,b)$ form a hexagonal pattern (or triangular for$a = 0$ or$b = 0$) in the weight lattice.

The weight lattice is the combinatorial skeleton on which the entire theory of representations is built. The fact that weights must lie in $P$ (and not just in$\mathfrak{h}^*$) is the integrality constraint that ultimately makes the representation theory of semisimple algebras so clean and computable.
