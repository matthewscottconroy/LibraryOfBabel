# 50.1.3 $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ and Its Irreducible Representations$V_n$

Before tackling the general theory, it is essential to master the case $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ completely. This algebra is small enough to classify by direct calculation, yet the classification is not trivial, and every step of the argument reappears in the general case in a more elaborate form. The slogan is: all of highest weight theory is$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-theory applied simultaneously to each$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple.

The algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C}) = \mathrm{span}\{e, f, h\}$ satisfies$[h,e] = 2e$,$[h,f] = -2f$,$[e,f] = h$. Here$h$ spans the Cartan subalgebra,$e$ is the positive root vector, and$f$ is the negative root vector. For a finite-dimensional representation, the eigenvalues of$h$ are integers (by the integrality argument applied to the single coroot$h$), and the operators$e$ and$f$ shift eigenvalues by$+2$ and$-2$ respectively. Since the eigenvalue set is finite, applying$e$ or$f$ enough times must annihilate any eigenvector, giving rise to the notion of a highest weight vector.

The finite-dimensional irreducible representations are $V_n$ for$n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, of dimension$n+1$, with basis$\{v_0, v_1, \ldots, v_n\}$ on which the action is$h \cdot v_k = (n - 2k) v_k$,$e \cdot v_k = (n - k + 1) v_{k-1}$, and$f \cdot v_k = (k+1) v_{k+1}$ (with$e \cdot v_0 = 0$ and$f \cdot v_n = 0$). The$h$-eigenvalues descend symmetrically from$n$ to$-n$ in steps of 2, and this symmetry is already a shadow of the Weyl group action. The highest weight is$n$ — the largest eigenvalue of$h$ — and this single integer completely determines the representation up to isomorphism.

The proof that $V_n$ is the unique irreducible of highest weight$n$ uses two facts. First, any highest weight vector$v$ (with$e \cdot v = 0$ and$h \cdot v = n \cdot v$) generates an invariant subspace:$\{f^k v : k = 0, 1, \ldots\}$ spans a subrepresentation. Second, in a finite-dimensional representation, applying$f$ repeatedly to$v$ must eventually give zero, say$f^{n+1} v = 0$. The relations$h f^k v = (n-2k) f^k v$ and$e f^k v = k(n-k+1) f^{k-1} v$ determine all the structure constants uniquely, giving exactly the formulas above. This is the prototype for the general theory: an irreducible representation is built by starting with a highest weight vector and applying lowering operators, with the highest weight determining everything.

**Example 1.** $V_0$ (trivial representation): 1-dimensional,$h \cdot v = 0$,$e \cdot v = f \cdot v = 0$. This is the trivial representation corresponding to a particle of spin 0.

**Example 2.** $V_1$ (standard representation): 2-dimensional,$v_0$ with$h \cdot v_0 = v_0$ and$v_1$ with$h \cdot v_1 = -v_1$. Action:$e \cdot v_1 = v_0$,$f \cdot v_0 = v_1$,$e \cdot v_0 = 0$,$f \cdot v_1 = 0$. This is the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ representation of physics.

**Example 3.** $V_2$ (adjoint representation): 3-dimensional, basis$\{v_0, v_1, v_2\}$ with$h$-eigenvalues$2, 0, -2$. Action:$f \cdot v_0 = v_1$,$f \cdot v_1 = 2v_2$,$e \cdot v_2 = v_1$,$e \cdot v_1 = 2v_0$. The factor of 2 appears because$e f^2 v_0 = 2 \cdot 1 \cdot f v_0 = 2v_1$. This is isomorphic to$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ itself under the adjoint representation (spin-1).

**Example 4.** $V_3$: 4-dimensional,$h$-eigenvalues$3, 1, -1, -3$. Action:$f \cdot v_0 = v_1$,$f \cdot v_1 = 2v_2$,$f \cdot v_2 = 3v_3$,$e \cdot v_3 = v_2$,$e \cdot v_2 = 2v_1$,$e \cdot v_1 = 3v_0$. This is the spin-$\frac{3}{2}$ representation. It can also be realized as$\mathrm{Sym}^3(\mathbb{C}^2)$, the space of cubic polynomials in two variables, with$SL_2$ acting by$\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \cdot p(x,y) = p(ax+cy, bx+dy)$.

The complete classification of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-representations is: every finite-dimensional completely reducible$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module is a direct sum of$V_n$'s (by Weyl's theorem), and the decomposition is determined by the eigenvalue multiplicities of$h$. This clean structure — parametrized by a single non-negative integer — is the model for the general highest weight classification.
