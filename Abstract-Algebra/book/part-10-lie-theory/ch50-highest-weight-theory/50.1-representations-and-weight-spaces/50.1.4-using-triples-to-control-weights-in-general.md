# 50.1.4 Using $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-Triples to Control Weights in General$\mathfrak{g}$

The power of the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-classification is not limited to the algebra$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ itself. For any semisimple Lie algebra$\mathfrak{g}$ and any root$\alpha \in \Phi$, the elements$e_\alpha, f_\alpha, h_\alpha$ span a subalgebra isomorphic to$\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$. By restricting a$\mathfrak{g}$-representation to this subalgebra, the full force of the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-theory becomes available along the$\alpha$-direction.

This restriction has immediate consequences. The $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-classification tells us that the eigenvalues of$h_\alpha$ on any finite-dimensional representation must be integers. Since$h_\alpha$ acts on the weight space$V_\lambda$ by the scalar$\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle$, this says$\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle \in \mathbb{Z}$ for every weight$\lambda$ and every root$\alpha$ — confirming that weights lie in the lattice$P$. Furthermore, the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-theory forces the set of weights along any$\alpha$-string to be symmetric about the reflection$s_\alpha(\mu) = \mu - \langle \mu, \alpha^\vee \rangle \alpha$, and each string has integer length with a specific pattern of multiplicities. This symmetry is the action of the Weyl group on the weight diagram.

More precisely: for any weight $\lambda$ of$V$ and any root$\alpha$, the set$\{\lambda + n\alpha : n \in \mathbb{Z}, V_{\lambda + n\alpha} \neq 0\}$ is an unbroken string (by the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-representation theory applied to the$h_\alpha$-eigenvalue$\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle$), and$\dim V_\lambda = \dim V_{s_\alpha(\lambda)}$. So the Weyl group acts on the weight diagram by permuting weight spaces and preserving multiplicities.

The key insight is that by applying the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple technique for every positive root simultaneously, one can control the entire weight space structure of a representation. This is the fundamental tool for proving that highest weight modules have the right structure — it is what forces the weight spaces of a Verma module (and hence of any highest weight module) to be arranged in the way the general theory predicts, and it is what ultimately establishes the finite-dimensionality of$L(\lambda)$ for dominant integral$\lambda$.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ and the standard representation$\mathbb{C}^3$: the three weights are$\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3$. Apply the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple for$\alpha_1 = \epsilon_1 - \epsilon_2$: the$h_{\alpha_1}$-eigenvalue on$V_{\epsilon_1}$ is$\langle \epsilon_1, \alpha_1^\vee \rangle = 1$, on$V_{\epsilon_2}$ is$-1$, on$V_{\epsilon_3}$ is$0$. The$\alpha_1$-string through$\epsilon_1$ is$\{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2\}$ (length 2), and through$\epsilon_3$ is$\{\epsilon_3\}$ (length 1). This is consistent with the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-representation$V_1 \oplus V_0$ when restricted to the$\alpha_1$-triple.

**Example 2.** Weyl group symmetry of weight diagrams: for $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ and the adjoint representation (dimension 8), the Weyl group$W = S_3$ acts on the 6 roots. The weight 0 (occurring with multiplicity 2) is fixed by$W$. The 6 roots are permuted as the vertices of a hexagon, and the 6-dimensional orbit$\{\pm\alpha_1, \pm\alpha_2, \pm(\alpha_1+\alpha_2)\}$ carries the Weyl symmetry. This symmetry is forced by the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple technique applied to each root.

**Example 3.** For the irreducible $L(2\omega_1)$ of$\mathfrak{sl}_3$ (the symmetric square of the standard representation): this 6-dimensional representation has weights$2\epsilon_1, 2\epsilon_2, 2\epsilon_3, \epsilon_1+\epsilon_2, \epsilon_1+\epsilon_3, \epsilon_2+\epsilon_3$. The$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple for$\alpha_1$ acts: the string from$2\epsilon_1$ goes through$\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2$ to$2\epsilon_2$ (since$\langle 2\epsilon_1, \alpha_1^\vee \rangle = 2$). The weight$\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2$ has$h_{\alpha_1}$-eigenvalue 0, lying in the middle of the string.

**Example 4.** The reflection principle for weight multiplicities: for any semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$ and irreducible$L(\lambda)$,$\dim L(\lambda)_{w\mu} = \dim L(\lambda)_\mu$ for all$w \in W$. This follows from the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple technique applied to each simple reflection: the string through any weight is symmetric, hence the multiplicity is preserved under each simple reflection, and since$W$ is generated by simple reflections, the Weyl group preserves all multiplicities.

The $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple technique is the unifying principle of the entire highest weight theory. Every major theorem — the integrality of weights, the Weyl group symmetry, the finite-dimensionality criterion, the Weyl character formula — is ultimately an application of the simple and completely classified$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-representation theory, applied repeatedly along each root direction.
