# 50.1 Representations and Weight Spaces

The representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras begins by applying the Cartan subalgebra's diagonalizability to arbitrary modules. If $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$, and $V$ is a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-module, then the elements of $\mathfrak{h}$ act semisimply on $V$ and — since they commute — they act simultaneously diagonalizably. The weight space decomposition $V = \bigoplus_{\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*} V_\lambda$ collects the simultaneous eigenvectors: $V_\lambda = \{v \in V : h \cdot v = \lambda(h) v \text{ for all } h \in \mathfrak{h}\}$. A linear functional $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ for which $V_\lambda \neq 0$ is called a weight of $V$, and $\dim V_\lambda$ is the multiplicity of that weight.

The root spaces $\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$ from Section 49.1 are weight spaces of the adjoint representation with weight $\alpha$, and this perspective unifies the root space decomposition with the general theory. A key structural fact is that root vectors shift weights: if $v \in V_\lambda$ and $e_\alpha \in \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, then $e_\alpha \cdot v \in V_{\lambda + \alpha}$. The positive root vectors raise weights; the negative root vectors lower them. This raising-and-lowering structure, visible already in the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ example where $e$ and $f$ are the raising and lowering operators on $V(n)$, is the mechanism behind the entire classification.

The case of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the master example. Its irreducible representations $V(n)$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ have weight spaces at $\{n, n-2, \ldots, -n+2, -n\}$, each of multiplicity one. The weight lattice $P \subset \mathfrak{h}^*$ is the set of $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ such that $\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle \in \mathbb{Z}$ for every coroot $\alpha^\vee$; for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ this is simply $\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}$ (in appropriate normalization), with the integer part giving the ordinary representation ring. The weight lattice is the natural domain for highest weight theory. For general semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$, the weight lattice $P$ is a free abelian group of rank $r$ with $\mathbb{Z}$-basis the fundamental weights $\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_r$ defined by $\langle \omega_i, \alpha_j^\vee \rangle = \delta_{ij}$; the root lattice $Q$ generated by the simple roots $\alpha_i$ is a sublattice of $P$, and the quotient $P/Q$ is a finite abelian group that coincides with the center of the simply connected cover of the corresponding Lie group.
