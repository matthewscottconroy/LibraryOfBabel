# 50.2 Highest Weights and Verma Modules

In any finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-module $V$, the weight-raising process must eventually stop: since $V$ is finite-dimensional, there can be no infinite strictly increasing chain of weights. A highest weight vector is a nonzero $v \in V_\lambda$ that is annihilated by all positive root vectors: $e_\alpha \cdot v = 0$ for every $\alpha \in \Phi^+$. Every nonzero finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-module has at least one highest weight vector. Moreover, in an irreducible module, the subspace generated by applying all lowering operators $f_\alpha$ to any highest weight vector is the entire module — the highest weight vector is a cyclic generator. This means the irreducible module is completely determined, as a $\mathfrak{g}$-module, by the highest weight $\lambda \in P$.

To construct irreducible modules systematically, one builds the Verma module $M(\lambda)$ as a universal object. This requires the universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$, which is the associative algebra generated by $\mathfrak{g}$ subject to the relation $XY - YX = [X,Y]$. The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt (PBW) theorem says that $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ has a $k$-basis consisting of ordered monomials in any ordered basis of $\mathfrak{g}$; in particular, using the root decomposition, one gets a basis of the form $f_{\beta_1}^{a_1} \cdots f_{\beta_s}^{a_s} \cdot h_1^{b_1} \cdots h_r^{b_r} \cdot e_{\gamma_1}^{c_1} \cdots e_{\gamma_t}^{c_t}$. The Verma module $M(\lambda)$ is then $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}) \otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{b})} k_\lambda$, where $\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^+$ is the Borel subalgebra and $k_\lambda$ is the one-dimensional $\mathfrak{b}$-module on which $\mathfrak{h}$ acts by $\lambda$ and $\mathfrak{n}^+$ acts by zero.

By the PBW theorem, $M(\lambda)$ has a $k$-basis indexed by monomials in the negative root vectors applied to the highest weight vector $v_\lambda$; it is infinite-dimensional in general. The key structural fact is that $M(\lambda)$ has a unique maximal proper submodule $N(\lambda)$, and the quotient $L(\lambda) = M(\lambda)/N(\lambda)$ is the unique irreducible $\mathfrak{g}$-module with highest weight $\lambda$. The module $L(\lambda)$ is finite-dimensional if and only if $\lambda$ is dominant integral — a condition that will be analyzed in Section 50.3. The Verma module thus serves as a "generating object" from which all irreducibles are obtained by quotienting out the submodule of "null vectors," a structure that has extensive generalizations in the modern theory of $\mathcal{D}$-modules and geometric representation theory.
