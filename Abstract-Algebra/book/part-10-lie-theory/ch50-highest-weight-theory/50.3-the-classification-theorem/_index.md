# 50.3 The Classification Theorem

The classification of finite-dimensional irreducible representations of a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over $\mathbb{C}$ is one of the most satisfying theorems in algebra: there is a canonical bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional irreducible $\mathfrak{g}$-modules and the set of dominant integral weights. A weight $\lambda \in P$ is dominant integral when $\langle \lambda, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ for every simple root $\alpha_i$; equivalently, when $\lambda$ is a non-negative integer combination of the fundamental weights: $\lambda = \sum_i n_i \omega_i$ with $n_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$. The fundamental weights $\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_r$ form a $\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$-basis for the dominant integral weights, and the corresponding irreducible modules $L(\omega_i)$ are called the fundamental representations.

The finite-dimensionality theorem — that $L(\lambda)$ is finite-dimensional if and only if $\lambda$ is dominant integral — is the heart of the classification. The "if" direction requires showing that the Verma module quotient $L(\lambda)$ is finite-dimensional when all $\langle \lambda, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle$ are non-negative integers; the key is to show that the lowering operators can only lower a weight finitely many times before hitting zero, using the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-subalgebra argument for each simple root. The "only if" direction, which shows that non-dominant-integral highest weights lead to infinite-dimensional irreducibles, follows by exhibiting explicit infinite chains of weight spaces.

The fundamental representations are the building blocks from which all irreducibles are assembled. For $\mathfrak{sl}_n$, the fundamental representation $L(\omega_k)$ is the $k$-th exterior power $\bigwedge^k \mathbb{C}^n$ of the standard representation, with $\dim L(\omega_k) = \binom{n}{k}$. Every irreducible $L(\lambda) = L(\sum n_i \omega_i)$ appears as a summand in the tensor product $L(\omega_1)^{\otimes n_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes L(\omega_r)^{\otimes n_r}$, making the fundamental representations true "building blocks" in the representation ring. The representation ring $R(\mathfrak{g})$ is a polynomial ring $\mathbb{Z}[\chi(\omega_1), \ldots, \chi(\omega_r)]$ in the characters of the fundamental representations — a fact that follows from the Weyl character formula and is the representation-theoretic content of the Harish-Chandra isomorphism discussed in Section 50.5.
