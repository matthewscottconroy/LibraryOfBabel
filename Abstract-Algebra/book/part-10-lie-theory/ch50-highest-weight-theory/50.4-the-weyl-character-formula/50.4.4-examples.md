# 50.4.4 Examples: $\mathfrak{sl}_2$,$\mathfrak{sl}_3$,$G_2$

Abstract formulas become meaningful through concrete computation. Working through the Weyl character formula and the dimension formula for specific Lie algebras — starting with the familiar $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, proceeding to the first genuinely new case$\mathfrak{sl}_3$, and then the exceptional algebra$G_2$ — illustrates both the power and the scope of the theory.

**$\mathfrak{sl}_2$.** There is a single positive root$\alpha$ and the Weyl group$W = \{1, s\}$ with$s(\lambda) = -\lambda$. The dominant integral weights are$m\omega$ for$m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, with$\omega$ the fundamental weight ($\omega = \alpha/2$). The Weyl vector is$\rho = \omega$. The Weyl character formula gives:
$$\mathrm{ch}(V_m) = \frac{e^{(m+1)\omega} - e^{-(m+1)\omega}}{e^\omega - e^{-\omega}} = e^{m\omega} + e^{(m-2)\omega} + \cdots + e^{-m\omega}.$$
This confirms that each weight in $\{m, m-2, \ldots, -m\}$ occurs with multiplicity$1$. The dimension formula gives$\dim V_m = m+1$, consistent with the direct construction. Tensor products decompose by Clebsch–Gordan:$V_m \otimes V_n \cong V_{m+n} \oplus V_{m+n-2} \oplus \cdots \oplus V_{|m-n|}$, a formula readable from the character product.

**$\mathfrak{sl}_3$.** The dominant integral weights are$\lambda = m\omega_1 + n\omega_2$ with$m, n \geq 0$. The Weyl group is$S_3$ (order 6), and the positive roots are$\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_1 + \alpha_2$. The Weyl vector is$\rho = \omega_1 + \omega_2$. The dimension formula gives$\dim L(m,n) = \frac{(m+1)(n+1)(m+n+2)}{2}$, which is always a positive integer (as the formula is$\binom{m+1}{1}\binom{n+1}{1}\binom{m+n+2}{2}/\binom{2}{2}$ up to normalization). Key examples:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L(1,0) = \mathbb{C}^3$ (standard representation, dim 3): weights$\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3$, each multiplicity 1.
\item $L(0,1) = (\mathbb{C}^3)^*$ (dual, dim 3): weights$-\epsilon_1, -\epsilon_2, -\epsilon_3$, each multiplicity 1.
\item $L(1,1) = \mathfrak{sl}_3$ (adjoint, dim 8): weights$\pm\alpha_1, \pm\alpha_2, \pm(\alpha_1+\alpha_2)$ (mult. 1 each) and$0$ (mult. 2).
\item $L(2,0) = \mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^3)$ (dim 6): weights$2\epsilon_1, 2\epsilon_2, 2\epsilon_3, \epsilon_1+\epsilon_2, \epsilon_1+\epsilon_3, \epsilon_2+\epsilon_3$, each mult. 1.
\item $L(3,0)$ (dim 10):$\mathrm{Sym}^3(\mathbb{C}^3)$ — weights are monomials$\epsilon_i + \epsilon_j + \epsilon_k$ for$i \leq j \leq k$.
\end{itemize}

The weight diagram of $L(m,n)$ is a hexagonal pattern in the 2-dimensional real span of$\omega_1, \omega_2$. The outermost weights (on the boundary of the convex hull$W \cdot \lambda$) have multiplicity 1; inner shells have increasing multiplicities. For$L(1,1)$: the 6 outer weights (the roots) have multiplicity 1, and the central weight$0$ has multiplicity 2.

The Weyl character formula for $L(m,n)$ in terms of$x_i = e^{\epsilon_i}$ (with$x_1 x_2 x_3 = 1$):
$$\mathrm{ch}(L(m,n)) = \frac{\det\begin{pmatrix} x_1^{m+n+2} & x_2^{m+n+2} & x_3^{m+n+2} \\ x_1^{n+1} & x_2^{n+1} & x_3^{n+1} \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}}{\det\begin{pmatrix} x_1^2 & x_2^2 & x_3^2 \\ x_1 & x_2 & x_3 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}}.$$
The denominator is the Vandermonde determinant $(x_1 - x_2)(x_1 - x_3)(x_2 - x_3)$, and the numerator is the Weyl alternating sum.

**$G_2$.** The root system has rank 2 and$|\Phi^+| = 6$ positive roots (3 short and 3 long). The Weyl group has order 12 (the dihedral group$D_6$). The dominant integral weights are$\lambda = m\omega_1 + n\omega_2$ where$\omega_1$ is associated to the short simple root and$\omega_2$ to the long. The dimension formula is:
$$\dim L(m,n) = \frac{(m+1)(n+1)(m+n+2)(m+2n+3)(m+3n+4)(2m+3n+5)}{120}.$$
Key representations:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L(\omega_1) = L(1,0)$: dim$7$ (the smallest non-trivial representation; not the adjoint).
\item $L(\omega_2) = L(0,1)$: dim$14$ (the adjoint representation$\mathfrak{g}_2$ itself).
\item $L(2,0)$: dim$27$.
\item $L(1,1)$: dim$64$.
\item $L(0,2)$: dim$77$.
\item $L(3,0)$: dim$77$.
\end{itemize}

The 7-dimensional representation $L(\omega_1)$ of$G_2$ arises from the action of$G_2 = \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{O})$ (the automorphism group of the octonions) on the imaginary octonions$\mathrm{Im}(\mathbb{O}) \cong \mathbb{R}^7$. The weight diagram of$L(\omega_1)$ consists of the 6 short roots of$G_2$ plus the zero weight with multiplicity 1, arranged as a regular hexagon with a central point.

The tensor product $L(\omega_1) \otimes L(\omega_1) \cong L(2\omega_1) \oplus L(\omega_2) \oplus L(\omega_1) \oplus L(0)$ ($7 \times 7 = 27 + 14 + 7 + 1 = 49$) illustrates the Clebsch–Gordan decomposition for$G_2$, which is richer than the classical type cases. These examples demonstrate that the Weyl character and dimension formulas work uniformly across all Lie types, reducing the combinatorics of representation theory to explicit polynomial formulas.
