# 50.4 The Weyl Character Formula

Knowing that finite-dimensional irreducibles are classified by dominant integral weights is only the beginning: one also wants to know the dimensions and weight multiplicities of each irreducible. The Weyl character formula, proved by Hermann Weyl in 1925–1926, answers both questions simultaneously. The character of a module $V$ is the formal sum $\text{ch}(V) = \sum_{\lambda} (\dim V_\lambda) e^\lambda$ in the group ring $\mathbb{Z}[P]$ of the weight lattice, where $e^\lambda$ are formal exponentials. Characters are additive on direct sums and multiplicative on tensor products, making the representation ring into a commutative ring. The Weyl character formula expresses the character of the irreducible $L(\lambda)$ as a ratio of two explicit antisymmetric sums over the Weyl group.

The formula is $\text{ch}(L(\lambda)) = \frac{\sum_{w \in W} (-1)^{\ell(w)} e^{w(\lambda + \rho)}}{\sum_{w \in W} (-1)^{\ell(w)} e^{w(\rho)}}$, where $\rho = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\alpha > 0} \alpha$ is the Weyl vector (the half-sum of positive roots, equal to the sum of the fundamental weights $\omega_1 + \cdots + \omega_r$). The denominator is the Weyl denominator $\sum_{w \in W} (-1)^{\ell(w)} e^{w(\rho)} = e^\rho \prod_{\alpha > 0}(1 - e^{-\alpha})$, an identity that is a theorem in its own right with deep connections to modular forms. The numerator is the similar antisymmetric sum with $\lambda + \rho$ in place of $\rho$.

The Weyl dimension formula follows by specializing the character formula to $e^\mu = 1$ for all $\mu$, which requires taking a limit; the result is $\dim L(\lambda) = \prod_{\alpha > 0} \frac{\langle \lambda + \rho, \alpha \rangle}{\langle \rho, \alpha \rangle}$. This product over positive roots is explicitly computable from the root system data: for $\mathfrak{sl}_3$, there are three positive roots $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$, $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2$, and the formula gives $\dim L(m\omega_1 + n\omega_2) = \frac{(m+1)(n+1)(m+n+2)}{2}$. Freudenthal's formula, developed in Section 50.4.5, gives the individual weight multiplicities $\dim L(\lambda)_\mu$ by a recursive computation using the inner product on the weight lattice, and it is the practical tool for computing weight diagrams of specific representations. Together, these formulas give a complete quantitative description of every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of every semisimple Lie algebra.
