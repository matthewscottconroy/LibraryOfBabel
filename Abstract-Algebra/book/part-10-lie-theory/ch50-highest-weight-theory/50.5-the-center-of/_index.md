# 50.5 The Center of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$

The universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ is a non-commutative associative algebra, but it has a rich center $Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))$ consisting of elements that commute with all of $\mathfrak{g}$. Central elements act as scalars on any irreducible module (by Schur's lemma), so they provide a way to distinguish non-isomorphic irreducibles by their "eigenvalues." The simplest central element is the Casimir element $C = \sum_i x_i x^i$, constructed from any self-dual basis $\{x_i\}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ with respect to the Killing form: $C$ is independent of the choice of basis, lies in $Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))$, and acts on $L(\lambda)$ by the scalar $\langle \lambda, \lambda + 2\rho \rangle$. For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with $C = \frac{1}{2}h^2 + ef + fe$, this scalar on $V(n)$ is $\frac{n(n+2)}{4}$ (in appropriate normalization), and distinct $n$ give distinct eigenvalues — confirming that the Casimir separates the irreducibles.

The full center $Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))$ is much larger than the span of the Casimir. The Harish-Chandra isomorphism identifies it explicitly: there is a canonical ring isomorphism $Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^*]^W$, where the right side is the ring of Weyl-group-invariant polynomial functions on $\mathfrak{h}^*$. The map is the Harish-Chandra homomorphism, defined by projecting elements of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ onto the $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h})$ factor via the PBW decomposition, followed by the "Weyl shift" $\lambda \mapsto \lambda - \rho$. The shift by $\rho$ is essential for the map to be a ring homomorphism; without it one gets a "twisted" algebra map.

The Harish-Chandra isomorphism has far-reaching consequences. Since $\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^*]^W$ is a polynomial ring in $r$ generators (by the Chevalley restriction theorem, these generators are the elementary symmetric functions of the eigenvalues, generalizing the Casimir), the center $Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))$ is a polynomial ring in $r = \text{rank}(\mathfrak{g})$ generators. The Casimir is one generator; the others correspond to higher-order invariants of the Weyl group action. For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, the center is generated by the single Casimir; for $\mathfrak{sl}_n$, the $r = n-1$ generators are the analogues of the elementary symmetric polynomials in the eigenvalues. The isomorphism also identifies the central characters: each irreducible $L(\lambda)$ has a central character $\chi_\lambda: Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})) \to \mathbb{C}$, and $\chi_\lambda = \chi_\mu$ if and only if $\lambda + \rho$ and $\mu + \rho$ are in the same $W$-orbit — confirming that the Weyl group symmetry is the precise obstruction to separating irreducibles by their central characters.
