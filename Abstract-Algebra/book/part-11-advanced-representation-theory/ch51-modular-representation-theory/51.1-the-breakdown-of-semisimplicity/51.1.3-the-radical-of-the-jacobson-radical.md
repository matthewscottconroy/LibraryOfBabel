# 51.1.3 The Radical of $k[G]$; the Jacobson Radical

In the semisimple case, the group algebra $k[G]$ is itself a direct product of matrix algebras, with no room for a non-trivial "nilpotent part." Once semisimplicity fails, the group algebra acquires a non-trivial radical — a nilpotent ideal that measures exactly how far the algebra is from being semisimple. Understanding this radical is the key to understanding the structure of the modular representation category.

The *Jacobson radical* $J(A)$ of an algebra$A$ admits several equivalent descriptions: it is the intersection of all maximal left ideals; it is the largest nilpotent two-sided ideal; and it is the set of elements that annihilate every simple$A$-module. For a finite-dimensional algebra, all three characterizations coincide, and$J(A)$ is nilpotent in the sense that$J(A)^n = 0$ for some$n$. The quotient$A/J(A)$ is always semisimple — by the Artin–Wedderburn theorem, it is a direct product of matrix algebras over division rings — and the simple$A$-modules are precisely the simple$A/J(A)$-modules.

For $k[G]$ in characteristic$p \mid |G|$, the radical$J(k[G])$ is non-zero. The most transparent case is$G$ a$p$-group: in this case$k[G]$ is a local ring and$J(k[G])$ equals the augmentation ideal$I_G = \ker(k[G] \to k)$, the ideal generated by all$\{g - 1 : g \in G\}$. The augmentation map$\varepsilon: k[G] \to k$ sends$\sum_g a_g g \mapsto \sum_g a_g$; its kernel is spanned by the elements$g - 1$ for$g \in G$. For a cyclic group$\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ with generator$g$, the augmentation ideal is generated by$x = g - 1$, and the nilpotency index is$p^n$ (since$x^{p^n} = (g-1)^{p^n} = g^{p^n} - 1 = 0$, but$x^{p^n - 1} = (g-1)^{p^n - 1} \neq 0$ in general). This nilpotency index reflects the depth of the non-semisimplicity.

For a general finite group $G$ in characteristic$p$, the radical$J(k[G])$ is spanned by elements that act non-trivially on the simples. The module filtration$V \supset J(k[G]) V \supset J(k[G])^2 V \supset \cdots \supset 0$ — the *radical series* of a module$V$ — replaces the direct sum decomposition of the semisimple case. The successive quotients$J^i(k[G]) V / J^{i+1}(k[G]) V$ — the *radical layers* — are semisimple modules whose isomorphism types encode the structure of$V$. The *Loewy length* of$V$ is the smallest$n$ such that$J^n(k[G]) V = 0$; it measures the "depth" of$V$ as a module.

The *radical series* and the dual *socle series* (the ascending series $0 = \mathrm{soc}^0(V) \subset \mathrm{soc}^1(V) \subset \cdots$ where$\mathrm{soc}^i(V)/\mathrm{soc}^{i-1}(V)$ is the socle of$V/\mathrm{soc}^{i-1}(V)$) together give a complete picture of the "layered" structure of a module. For a PIM$P(S)$, the two series meet at each layer: by the self-injectivity of$k[G]$, the$i$-th radical layer of$P(S)$ is dual (via the algebra's symmetric structure) to the$i$-th socle layer of$P(S)$ from the bottom. This duality between top and bottom is a powerful constraint and is crucial for computing the structure of PIMs in practice.

The nilpotency index of $J(k[G])$ — the smallest$n$ with$J(k[G])^n = 0$ — is a delicate group-theoretic invariant. For$G$ a$p$-group, the nilpotency index of$J(k[G])$ equals the nilpotency index of the augmentation ideal, which in turn is related to the dimension subgroups of$G$. For$G = \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$, the index is$p^n$. For$G = (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^r$ (an elementary abelian$p$-group of rank$r$), the nilpotency index is$r(p-1) + 1$. These indices appear naturally in questions about the depth of projective resolutions and the behavior of Ext groups.

\begin{example}
For $G = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ and$k = \mathbb{F}_p$, the group algebra$k[G] \cong k[x]/(x-1)^p$. Setting$u = x - 1$, we have$k[G] \cong k[u]/(u^p)$. The Jacobson radical is$J(k[G]) = (u) = k \cdot u \oplus k \cdot u^2 \oplus \cdots \oplus k \cdot u^{p-1}$, which is$(p-1)$-dimensional. The powers$J^i = (u^i)$ for$1 \leq i \leq p$, and$J^p = 0$. The unique simple module is$k[G]/(u) \cong k$. The regular module$k[G]$ has radical series$k[G] \supset (u) \supset (u^2) \supset \cdots \supset (u^{p-1}) \supset 0$, each quotient$(u^i)/(u^{i+1})$ being a one-dimensional (trivial) module. The Loewy length of$k[G]$ is$p$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $G = (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^2$ (the Klein four-group) and$k = \mathbb{F}_2$, label generators$g, h$ with$g^2 = h^2 = e$ and$gh = hg$. Let$x = g + 1$ and$y = h + 1$ in$k[G]$ (note: in characteristic 2,$-1 = 1$, so$g - 1 = g + 1$). Then$x^2 = y^2 = 0$,$xy = yx$, and$k[G] \cong k[x,y]/(x^2, y^2)$. The Jacobson radical is$J = (x, y)$, and$J^2 = (xy)$,$J^3 = 0$. The radical series of the 4-dimensional free module$k[G]$ is$k[G] \supset J \supset J^2 \supset 0$, with layers of dimensions$1, 2, 1$. The Loewy length is 3. Note that$\dim J = 3$ and$\dim J^2 = 1$, reflecting the structure$(k[G]/J) \oplus J/J^2 \oplus J^2 \cong k \oplus k^2 \oplus k$ as$k$-vector spaces.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $G = S_3$ (symmetric group on 3 letters) and$k = \mathbb{F}_3$, the group algebra$k[G]$ has dimension 6. There are two simple modules$S_1$ (trivial) and$S_2$ (sign), both one-dimensional. By direct computation,$J(k[G])$ has dimension 4 (so$k[G]/J(k[G]) \cong k \times k \cong k^2$ as$k$-algebras, confirming there are 2 simples). The nilpotency index of$J(k[G])$ is 3 (so$J^3 = 0$ but$J^2 \neq 0$). Each PIM has Loewy length 3, which can be seen directly from the radical series$P(S_i) \supset J \cdot P(S_i) \supset J^2 \cdot P(S_i) \supset 0$, with the radical layers being: head ($S_i$), middle (the other simple), socle ($S_i$). This three-layer "sandwich" structure of the PIMs is characteristic of blocks of defect 1 (blocks with cyclic defect group$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$), and its shape can be predicted from the theory of blocks with cyclic defect groups.
\end{example}
