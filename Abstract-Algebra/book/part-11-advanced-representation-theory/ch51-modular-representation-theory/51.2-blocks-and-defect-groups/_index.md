# 51.2 Blocks and Defect Groups

One of the central organizing principles of modular representation theory is the block decomposition. The center $Z(k[G])$ of the group algebra contains a distinguished set of primitive idempotents $e_1, \ldots, e_r$ — the block idempotents — with $e_i e_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$ and $e_1 + \cdots + e_r = 1$. These idempotents decompose the group algebra as a direct product $k[G] = B_1 \times \cdots \times B_r$ of indecomposable two-sided ideals $B_i = e_i \cdot k[G]$, called blocks. Every indecomposable $k[G]$-module $M$ satisfies $e_i M = M$ for exactly one $i$ and $e_j M = 0$ for $j \neq i$; thus every module belongs to exactly one block, and the module category $\mathrm{mod}(k[G])$ decomposes as a direct product of the block categories $\mathrm{mod}(B_i)$. Ordinary irreducible characters also belong to blocks, and the theory of blocks provides a deep link between the module-theoretic structure and the character-theoretic structure.

The key invariant of a block is its defect group. For a block $B$ with idempotent $e$, the defect group $D = D(B)$ is a $p$-subgroup of $G$, defined as a minimal subgroup such that $B$ is relatively $D$-projective: the identity element $e$ appears in the image of the relative trace map $\mathrm{Tr}_D^G : k[G] \to Z(k[G])$. The defect group is not unique, but all defect groups of a given block are conjugate in $G$. The defect $d = d(B)$ is the integer with $|D| = p^d$, and it measures the complexity of the block. A block of defect 0 has $|D| = 1$; such a block is a matrix algebra over $k$ and contains a single irreducible module, which is projective. At the other extreme, the principal block — the block containing the trivial module — typically has the largest defect group, namely a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$.

Brauer's First Main Theorem is the foundational result connecting the block theory of $G$ to the block theory of local subgroups. It states that there is a natural bijection between the blocks of $k[G]$ with defect group $D$ and the blocks of $k[N_G(D)]$ with defect group $D$, given by the Brauer correspondence. This theorem is remarkable because it reduces questions about a block of $G$ — a global object — to questions about a block of the normalizer $N_G(D)$ — a local object. It is the prototype of a family of local-global conjectures in modular representation theory, including Alperin's weight conjecture and the McKay conjecture, which assert that various numerical invariants of a block of $G$ (such as the number of irreducible modules) can be computed from the blocks of local subgroups. Most of these conjectures remain open and constitute one of the primary research frontiers in the field.

The section develops the block decomposition in detail, proves Brauer's First Main Theorem, and introduces the defect group as a $p$-local invariant. The structural result that emerges is a precise dictionary between the macroscopic structure of the group algebra — its block decomposition — and the $p$-local structure of the group $G$. Understanding a block of $k[G]$ is equivalent, in a precise sense, to understanding the $p$-local structure of $G$ at the defect group $D$.
