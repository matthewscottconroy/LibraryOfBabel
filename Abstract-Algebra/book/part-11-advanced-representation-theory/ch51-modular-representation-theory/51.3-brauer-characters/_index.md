# 51.3 Brauer Characters

The ordinary character theory of finite groups works over $\mathbb{C}$ by associating to each representation $\rho: G \to GL(V)$ the class function $\chi_V(g) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho(g))$, a sum of roots of unity. In characteristic $p$, traces are still defined, but the information they carry is corrupted by the characteristic — a trace that should be, say, $2$ might vanish because $2 = 0$ in characteristic $2$. Brauer's key insight was to recover a characteristic-zero invariant by restricting attention to the $p$-regular elements of $G$: those $g \in G$ whose order $\mathrm{ord}(g)$ is coprime to $p$. For a $p$-regular element, the eigenvalues of $\rho(g)$ in any algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$ are roots of unity of order coprime to $p$, and these can be canonically lifted to complex roots of unity via the Teichmüller lift. The Brauer character of a $k[G]$-module $M$ is then the class function $\varphi_M$ defined on $p$-regular elements by summing the Teichmüller lifts of the eigenvalues of $\rho(g)$ on $M$.

Brauer characters satisfy orthogonality relations entirely analogous to those of ordinary characters: the Brauer characters of the simple $k[G]$-modules form an orthonormal set with respect to the inner product $\langle \varphi, \psi \rangle = |G|^{-1} \sum_{g \in G'} \varphi(g)\overline{\psi(g)}$, where $G'$ denotes the set of $p$-regular elements. The number of irreducible Brauer characters equals the number of $p$-regular conjugacy classes of $G$, in precise analogy with the fact that the number of ordinary irreducible characters equals the number of conjugacy classes. However, there can be fewer $p$-regular conjugacy classes than total conjugacy classes, so there can be fewer irreducible Brauer characters than ordinary characters. This discrepancy is the source of the rich structure encoded in the decomposition matrix.

The decomposition matrix $D$ is the matrix with rows indexed by ordinary irreducible characters and columns indexed by irreducible Brauer characters, whose entry $d_{\chi\varphi}$ records how many times the Brauer character $\varphi$ appears in the restriction of $\chi$ to $p$-regular elements. The Cartan matrix $C$ has entries $c_{\varphi\psi} = \langle P(\varphi), \psi \rangle$, recording the multiplicity of the simple module with Brauer character $\psi$ in the projective indecomposable module $P(\varphi)$. The fundamental relation $C = D^T D$ is both elegant and constraining: it says that the Cartan matrix factors through the decomposition matrix, and in particular that $\det C$ is a power of $p$. The decomposition matrix for a given prime $p$ and group $G$ is an object of great computational and theoretical interest; its entries for the symmetric groups and groups of Lie type have been determined in many cases but remain open in general.

The section also addresses the lifting problem: when can a $k[G]$-module be lifted to a $\mathbb{Z}_p[G]$-module, and what is the relationship between a lifted module and its characteristic-zero specialization? This question is answered by the theory of $p$-adic representations and their reductions, which provides the precise framework in which Brauer characters live as the traces of $p$-adic lifts restricted to $p$-regular elements. The interplay between the three worlds — characteristic zero, $p$-adic, and characteristic $p$ — is formalized by the three-column diagram relating ordinary characters, $p$-adic characters, and Brauer characters, and it is this interplay that gives modular representation theory much of its depth and difficulty.
