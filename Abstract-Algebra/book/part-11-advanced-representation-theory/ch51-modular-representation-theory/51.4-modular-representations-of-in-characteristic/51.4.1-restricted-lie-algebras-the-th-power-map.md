# 51.4.1 Restricted Lie Algebras; the $p$-th Power Map

When passing from the representation theory of finite groups in characteristic $p$ to the representation theory of Lie algebras in characteristic$p$, new phenomena arise that have no analogue in characteristic 0. The most fundamental is the existence of an additional algebraic structure on Lie algebras in characteristic$p$ — the$p$-th power map — which drastically alters the representation theory. This structure is not optional: it is present canonically on the Lie algebra of any algebraic group over$\mathbb{F}_p$, and ignoring it means missing the most important examples.

In characteristic 0, one passes from a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ to its universal enveloping algebra$U(\mathfrak{g})$, which is infinite-dimensional and contains$\mathfrak{g}$ as a Lie subalgebra. The key computation: in any associative algebra of characteristic$p$, the Frobenius map$a \mapsto a^p$ is not a ring homomorphism in general (since$(a + b)^p$ involves cross terms), but for a Lie algebra element$x \in \mathfrak{g}$ acting in a representation, the operator$\mathrm{ad}(x)^p = \mathrm{ad}(x^{[p]})$ for some element$x^{[p]}$, where$\mathrm{ad}(x)(y) = [x, y]$.

A *restricted Lie algebra* (or *$p$-Lie algebra*) is a Lie algebra$\mathfrak{g}$ equipped with a$p$-th power map$x \mapsto x^{[p]}$ satisfying three axioms:
\begin{align*}
(\lambda x)^{[p]} &= \lambda^p x^{[p]} \quad \text{for } \lambda \in k \\
(ad\, x)^p &= ad(x^{[p]}) \\
(x + y)^{[p]} &= x^{[p]} + y^{[p]} + \sum_{i=1}^{p-1} s_i(x,y)
\end{align*}
where $s_i(x,y)$ are universal polynomials in$x$ and$y$ arising from the$p$-th power in the free associative algebra. The Lie algebra of an algebraic group over$\mathbb{F}_p$ is naturally restricted, with$x^{[p]}$ being the$p$-th power in the associative algebra of distributions (or, for matrix groups, the ordinary$p$-th power of the matrix$x$).

The representation theory of restricted Lie algebras is controlled by the *restricted enveloping algebra* $u(\mathfrak{g}) = U(\mathfrak{g})/(x^p - x^{[p]} : x \in \mathfrak{g})$. This quotient is finite-dimensional of dimension$p^{\dim \mathfrak{g}}$ — a dramatic contrast with the infinite-dimensional$U(\mathfrak{g})$ — and its modules are the *restricted representations* of$\mathfrak{g}$. The finiteness of$u(\mathfrak{g})$ means the category of restricted representations is much more tractable than the full representation category of$\mathfrak{g}$, and it is the correct setting for connecting Lie algebra representations to representations of algebraic groups.

Why does this finite-dimensionality matter so much? In characteristic 0, $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is a Noetherian domain of infinite global dimension, and studying its module category requires heavy machinery (Harish-Chandra isomorphism, Verma modules,$\mathcal{D}$-modules, etc.). The restricted enveloping algebra$u(\mathfrak{g})$ in characteristic$p$ is finite-dimensional, so the standard theory of finite-dimensional algebras applies: there are finitely many simples, the Cartan matrix and decomposition matrix are well-defined, and the block theory of the previous sections is directly applicable.

The connection to algebraic groups is tight. If $G$ is a connected reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed field$k$ of characteristic$p$, then$\mathfrak{g} = \mathrm{Lie}(G)$ is a restricted Lie algebra, and the restricted representations of$\mathfrak{g}$ (the modules of$u(\mathfrak{g})$) correspond precisely to representations of the *Frobenius kernel*$G_1 = \ker(F: G \to G^{(1)})$, the kernel of the Frobenius endomorphism. Here$G^{(1)}$ denotes the algebraic group obtained from$G$ by pulling back the structure sheaf along Frobenius. The representation theory of$G_1$ is thus exactly the modular Lie algebra theory, and the Steinberg tensor product theorem (see \S51.4.3) reduces the full rational representation theory of$G$ to a tower of these Frobenius kernels.

\begin{example}
For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{gl}_n(k)$ (all$n \times n$ matrices over$k = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$) with the Lie bracket$[x, y] = xy - yx$, the$p$-th power map is simply$x^{[p]} = x^p$ (the ordinary matrix$p$-th power). This satisfies the axioms since in matrix algebras,$(ad\, x)^p = ad(x^p)$ — a consequence of the fact that$(xy - yx)^p$ in the tensor algebra, when expanded, gives only$x^p y - y x^p$ by a telescoping identity in characteristic$p$. The restricted enveloping algebra$u(\mathfrak{gl}_n) = U(\mathfrak{gl}_n)/(x^p - x^p : x \in \mathfrak{gl}_n)$ has dimension$p^{n^2}$ over$k$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2(k)$ with basis$e = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$,$f = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$,$h = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$, the$p$-th power maps are$e^{[p]} = e^p = 0$ (since$e$ is nilpotent with$e^2 = 0$... for$p \geq 3$,$e^p = 0$),$f^{[p]} = f^p = 0$, and$h^{[p]} = h^p = h$ (since$h$ has eigenvalues$\pm 1$ and$(\pm 1)^p = \pm 1$ in characteristic$p > 2$). The restricted enveloping algebra is$u(\mathfrak{sl}_2) = k\langle e, f, h \rangle / ([h,e] = 2e, [h,f] = -2f, [e,f] = h, e^p = 0, f^p = 0, h^p = h)$, which has dimension$p^3$ over$k$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
For $\mathfrak{g} = k$ (a one-dimensional abelian Lie algebra over$k = \mathbb{F}_p$) with generator$x$, the$p$-th power map must satisfy$x^{[p]} = \lambda x$ for some$\lambda \in k$ (by the axiom$(\mathrm{ad}\, x)^p = \mathrm{ad}(x^{[p]})$, and since the Lie algebra is abelian,$\mathrm{ad}\, x = 0$ always, so$x^{[p]}$ is arbitrary). Different choices of$\lambda$ give non-isomorphic restricted Lie algebras. The restricted enveloping algebra for a choice$x^{[p]} = \lambda x$ is$k[x]/(x^p - \lambda x)$. For$\lambda = 0$ this is$k[x]/(x^p)$ (nilpotent case); for$\lambda \neq 0$,$x^p - \lambda x = x(x^{p-1} - \lambda)$ and the algebra splits as a product. This example illustrates how the restricted structure — the specific value of$x^{[p]}$ — has a significant effect on the representation theory.
\end{example}
