# 51.4.2 Restricted Representations; $p$-Characters

The theory of representations of a restricted Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field$k$ of characteristic$p$ does not end with the restricted representations — those for which$\rho(x)^p = \rho(x^{[p]})$. A richer picture emerges when one asks what happens when this equality fails. The deviation$\rho(x)^p - \rho(x^{[p]})$ turns out to be a highly structured operator, and organizing representations by how this deviation behaves leads to the theory of *$p$-characters*, one of the central tools in the modular representation theory of algebraic groups and restricted Lie algebras.

\begin{definition}
Let $(\mathfrak{g}, [p])$ be a restricted Lie algebra over$k$. A *$p$-character* of a$\mathfrak{g}$-module$V$ is a$k$-linear functional$\chi: \mathfrak{g} \to k$ such that$\rho(x)^p - \rho(x^{[p]}) = \chi(x)^p \cdot \mathrm{id}_V$ for all$x \in \mathfrak{g}$. The restricted representations are precisely those with$\chi = 0$.
\end{definition}

The key lemma underlying this definition is that for an irreducible module $V$, the operator$\rho(x)^p - \rho(x^{[p]})$ is central in the image of$\rho$, hence acts as a scalar by Schur's lemma. Since the map$x \mapsto \rho(x)^p - \rho(x^{[p]})$ is$p$-linear (it satisfies$f(\lambda x) = \lambda^p f(x)$), this scalar must be a$p$-th power, hence of the form$\chi(x)^p$. The linearity of$\chi$ in$x$ then follows from the additivity properties of the$p$-power map. The$p$-character$\chi$ therefore lies in the dual space$\mathfrak{g}^*$, and varying$\chi$ over all of$\mathfrak{g}^*$ parametrizes the full representation theory.

\begin{definition}
For $\chi \in \mathfrak{g}^*$, the *reduced enveloping algebra* is
$$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g}) = U(\mathfrak{g}) / \bigl( x^p - x^{[p]} - \chi(x)^p \cdot 1 : x \in \mathfrak{g} \bigr).$$
\end{definition}

The representations of $\mathfrak{g}$ with$p$-character$\chi$ are exactly the modules over$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$. Since$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$ is a quotient of$U(\mathfrak{g})$ by a central ideal, it is finite-dimensional of dimension$p^{\dim \mathfrak{g}}$, by the same PBW-type argument as for the restricted enveloping algebra$u(\mathfrak{g}) = U_0(\mathfrak{g})$.

**Example 1.** Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$, with basis$\{e, f, h\}$ and$p$-power maps$e^{[p]} = 0$,$f^{[p]} = 0$,$h^{[p]} = h$. For$\chi \in \mathfrak{g}^*$ with$\chi(e) = \chi(f) = 0$ and$\chi(h) = a$, the reduced enveloping algebra$U_\chi(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ has the additional relations$e^p = 0$,$f^p = 0$,$h^p - h = a^p$. The eigenvalues of$h$ in any$U_\chi$-module are the roots of$\lambda^p - \lambda = a^p$ in$k$. If$a = 0$, these are$\{0, 1, \ldots, p-1\} \subset \mathbb{F}_p$, and the module theory looks like the restricted case. For general$a$, the eigenvalues shift, and new simple modules appear.

**Example 2.** The most striking case is a *regular nilpotent* $p$-character: take$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ and$\chi$ with$\chi(e) = 1$,$\chi(f) = 0$,$\chi(h) = 0$. Then$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$ is an algebra in which$e^p - 1 = 0$, i.e.,$e$ is invertible. One can show directly that$U_\chi(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is isomorphic to the$p^3$-dimensional matrix algebra$M_{p}(k)$ — it is simple, with a unique simple module of dimension$p$. This is the baby Verma module for$\chi$. Note the contrast: for$\chi = 0$, there are$p$ distinct simple modules (one for each restricted weight$0, 1, \ldots, p-1$); for regular nilpotent$\chi$, there is exactly one.

**Example 3.** For general $\chi \in \mathfrak{g}^*$, the *baby Verma modules*$Z_\chi(\lambda)$ are defined by inducing from a character of the Borel subalgebra$\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^+$. Concretely,$Z_\chi(\lambda) = U_\chi(\mathfrak{g}) \otimes_{U_\chi(\mathfrak{b})} k_\lambda$, where$k_\lambda$ is the one-dimensional$\mathfrak{b}$-module on which$\mathfrak{h}$ acts by$\lambda$ and$\mathfrak{n}^+$ acts by zero. Every simple$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$-module is a quotient of some$Z_\chi(\lambda)$, and when$\chi|_{\mathfrak{n}^+}$ is regular nilpotent,$Z_\chi(\lambda)$ is itself simple of dimension$p^{|\Phi^+|}$.

\begin{theorem}[Kac–Weisfeiler conjecture, Premet 1995]
For $\mathfrak{g}$ semisimple over$\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ (with$p$ good for$\mathfrak{g}$), every irreducible$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$-module has dimension divisible by$p^{d(\chi)/2}$, where$d(\chi)$ is the dimension of the coadjoint orbit$G \cdot \chi \subset \mathfrak{g}^*$.
\end{theorem}

This theorem — proved by Premet after earlier work of Kac and Weisfeiler — is a deep structural result asserting that the $p$-character governs the minimum possible dimension of a module, much as symplectic geometry governs quantization in classical mechanics.

\begin{remark}
The variation of $U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$ as$\chi$ varies over$\mathfrak{g}^*$ organizes the full representation theory of$\mathfrak{g}$ into a family of finite-dimensional problems. The coadjoint orbit stratification of$\mathfrak{g}^*$ — the decomposition into$G$-orbits — reflects qualitatively different behavior: modules for$\chi$ on the zero orbit are restricted modules, modules for$\chi$ on a regular semisimple orbit are semisimple themselves (the algebra$U_\chi$ is semisimple in this case), and modules for$\chi$ on a nilpotent orbit exhibit the richest and most interesting behavior. This stratification mirrors the theory of primitive ideals in$U(\mathfrak{g})$ in characteristic zero.
\end{remark}

**Example 4.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{gl}_n$ over$\mathbb{F}_p$, let$\chi$ be the$p$-character corresponding to a regular semisimple element (a diagonal matrix with distinct eigenvalues modulo$p$). Then$U_\chi(\mathfrak{gl}_n)$ is Morita equivalent to a product of copies of$k$, and every module is completely reducible — one irreducible module for each eigenvalue assignment. This is the "semisimple" end of the spectrum. Moving to nilpotent$\chi$ introduces indecomposable non-simple modules, projective covers distinct from simple modules, and all the complexity familiar from the$\chi = 0$ case.

**Example 5.** The passage from $\chi = 0$ to general$\chi$ can be tracked via *translation in$p$-character*. If$V$ is a$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$-module and$W$ is a$U_0(\mathfrak{g})$-module (i.e., a restricted module), then$V \otimes W$ is a$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$-module: the$p$-character is additive under tensor product, so$p\text{-char}(V \otimes W) = p\text{-char}(V) + p\text{-char}(W) = \chi$ when$W$ is restricted. Translation functors$T_\chi^{\chi'}: U_\chi\text{-mod} \to U_{\chi'}\text{-mod}$ defined by$V \mapsto \mathrm{pr}_{\chi'}(V \otimes L(\mu))$ (for appropriate$\mu$) are thus available, connecting the module categories at different$p$-characters and providing a bridge between the theory at$\chi = 0$ and the theory at more general points.

The full modular representation theory of $\mathfrak{g}$ is thus the study of$U_\chi(\mathfrak{g})$ as$\chi$ ranges over$\mathfrak{g}^*$. The problem is finitistic at every point — each$U_\chi$ has dimension$p^{\dim \mathfrak{g}}$ — but the variation in$\chi$ requires understanding the global geometry of$\mathfrak{g}^*$ and its stratification by coadjoint orbits. This connects the purely algebraic representation theory to the symplectic geometry of the Lie algebra's dual, anticipating themes that recur throughout geometric representation theory.
