# 51.4 Modular Representations of $\mathfrak{g}$ in Characteristic $p$

When a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is defined over a field $k$ of characteristic $p > 0$, its representation theory acquires a new feature with no analogue in characteristic zero: the Frobenius map $x \mapsto x^p$ in the enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{g})$ does not leave the ideal generated by $[x,y] - xy + yx$ invariant, so $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is an infinite-dimensional algebra whose module theory is intractable without additional structure. The resolution is the notion of a restricted Lie algebra: a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ equipped with a $[p]$-power map $x \mapsto x^{[p]}$ satisfying axioms that make it compatible with the $p$-th power in any associative envelope. The restricted enveloping algebra $u(\mathfrak{g}) = U(\mathfrak{g})/(x^p - x^{[p]} : x \in \mathfrak{g})$ is finite-dimensional of dimension $p^{\dim \mathfrak{g}}$, and its representation theory — the theory of restricted representations — is the correct modular analogue of the Lie algebra representation theory in characteristic zero.

For the classical Lie algebras such as $\mathfrak{gl}_n, \mathfrak{sl}_n, \mathfrak{sp}_{2n}$, the restricted enveloping algebra has a rich but non-semisimple module theory. The simple modules of $u(\mathfrak{g})$ are parametrized by "restricted" dominant weights $\lambda$ satisfying $0 \leq \langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle \leq p-1$ for all simple coroots $\alpha^\vee$ — a condition that truncates the weight lattice to a finite "restricted" region. These simple modules $L(\lambda)$ are the characteristic-$p$ analogues of the finite-dimensional irreducible $\mathfrak{g}$-modules in characteristic zero, but their dimensions are generally not given by the Weyl character formula. Computing the dimensions and characters of $L(\lambda)$ for $p$ small relative to the Coxeter number is the subject of Lusztig's conjecture, one of the most important open problems in algebraic Lie theory, now known to be true for $p$ sufficiently large by work of Andersen–Jantzen–Soergel and subsequently by Fiebig.

The representation theory of algebraic groups over $\mathbb{F}_p$ introduces another layer via the Frobenius endomorphism: for a simply connected semisimple group $G$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, the Frobenius twist $M^{(1)}$ of a $G$-module $M$ is the module obtained by composing the action with the Frobenius $F: G \to G$. Steinberg's tensor product theorem is the cornerstone structural result: every irreducible rational $G$-module $L(\lambda)$ factors as a tensor product $L(\lambda_0) \otimes L(\lambda_1)^{(1)} \otimes \cdots \otimes L(\lambda_r)^{(r)}$, where $\lambda = \lambda_0 + p\lambda_1 + \cdots + p^r \lambda_r$ is the base-$p$ expansion of the dominant weight $\lambda$ into restricted dominant weights $\lambda_i$. This theorem, which has no characteristic-zero counterpart, reveals a profound self-similar structure in the representation theory of algebraic groups: any irreducible module decomposes as a tensor product of Frobenius twists of restricted modules, reducing all questions about irreducible representations to the restricted case. This fractal structure is one of the hallmarks of characteristic-$p$ representation theory and underlies many of the deepest results in the subject.
