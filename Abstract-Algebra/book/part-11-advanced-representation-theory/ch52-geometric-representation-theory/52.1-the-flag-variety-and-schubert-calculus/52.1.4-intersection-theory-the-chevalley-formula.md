# 52.1.4 Intersection Theory; the Chevalley Formula

Computing the ring structure of $H^*(G/B)$ in the Schubert basis requires understanding the cup products$[X_u] \cup [X_v]$, and while the full structure constants are complex combinatorial objects, a crucial special case admits a clean and explicit formula. The *Chevalley formula* computes the product of an arbitrary Schubert class$[X_w]$ with a Schubert divisor class$[X_{s_i}]$ corresponding to a simple reflection$s_i$. These divisor classes are the "atoms" of the ring in the sense that every Schubert class can be obtained from them by repeated multiplication.

\begin{theorem}[Chevalley's Formula, 1994 (see also Monk 1959 for $GL_n$)]
In $H^*(G/B; \mathbb{Z})$, the product of a divisor Schubert class with an arbitrary Schubert class is given by
$$[X_{s_i}] \cup [X_w] = \sum_{\substack{\beta \in \Phi^+ \\ \ell(ws_\beta) = \ell(w) + 1}} \langle \omega_i, \beta^\vee \rangle [X_{ws_\beta}],$$
where $\omega_i$ is the$i$-th fundamental weight,$s_\beta \in W$ is the reflection in the positive root$\beta$, and the sum ranges over positive roots$\beta$ such that$ws_\beta$ covers$w$ in the Bruhat order.
\end{theorem}

The condition $\ell(ws_\beta) = \ell(w) + 1$ ensures that we move up exactly one level in the Bruhat order, and the coefficient$\langle \omega_i, \beta^\vee \rangle$ is a positive integer determined by the root system data. This formula is completely explicit and recursive: applied repeatedly, it reduces any product of Schubert classes to a$\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$-linear combination of Schubert classes.

**Example 1.** For $G = GL_3$,$W = S_3$, simple reflections$s_1 = (12)$,$s_2 = (23)$, positive roots$\{e_1 - e_2, e_2 - e_3, e_1 - e_3\}$, fundamental weights$\omega_1 = e_1$,$\omega_2 = e_1 + e_2$. Compute$[X_{s_1}] \cup [X_{s_1 s_2}]$. The element$w = s_1 s_2$ has$\ell(w) = 2$. We need$\beta > 0$ with$\ell(s_1 s_2 \cdot s_\beta) = 3$:
- $\beta = e_1 - e_2$:$s_\beta = s_1$,$ws_\beta = s_1 s_2 s_1 = s_2 s_1 s_2 \cdot \ldots$ (need to check length): actually$s_1 s_2 s_1 = w_0 \cdot s_2^{-1}$... let us just note$\ell(s_1 s_2 \cdot s_1)$. Since$s_1 s_2 s_1 = s_2 s_1 s_2$ (braid relation), and this has length 3, so$\ell = 3$. Coefficient:$\langle \omega_1, (e_1 - e_2)^\vee \rangle = 1$.
- $\beta = e_1 - e_3$:$s_\beta = s_1 s_2 s_1$,$ws_\beta = s_1 s_2 \cdot s_1 s_2 s_1$... length calculation shows this is$s_2$ of length 1 — not length 3. Skip.
- $\beta = e_2 - e_3$:$s_\beta = s_2$,$ws_\beta = s_1 s_2 s_2 = s_1$, length 1 — not length 3. Skip.
So $[X_{s_1}] \cup [X_{s_1 s_2}] = 1 \cdot [X_{w_0}]$ — the class of a point times the fundamental class gives the point class, as expected from dimensional reasons (we land in the top cohomology$H^6$, degree$= |\Phi^+| = 3$).

**Example 2.** Compute $[X_{s_1}] \cup [X_{s_2}]$ in$H^*(GL_3/B; \mathbb{Z})$. Here$w = s_2$,$\ell(w) = 1$. We need$\beta > 0$ with$\ell(s_2 s_\beta) = 2$:
- $\beta = e_2 - e_3$:$s_\beta = s_2$,$s_2 s_2 = e$, length 0. Skip.
- $\beta = e_1 - e_2$:$s_\beta = s_1$,$s_2 s_1$, length 2. Coefficient:$\langle \omega_1, (e_1-e_2)^\vee \rangle = 1$.
- $\beta = e_1 - e_3$:$s_\beta = s_1 s_2 s_1$,$s_2 (s_1 s_2 s_1)$... length?$s_2 s_1 s_2 s_1$ has length 4$>$ dim. Skip.
So $[X_{s_1}] \cup [X_{s_2}] = [X_{s_2 s_1}]$. Similarly$[X_{s_2}] \cup [X_{s_1}] = [X_{s_1 s_2}]$ (by the symmetric calculation). This illustrates non-commutativity in the *un-ordered* Schubert basis: the two "degree-2" Schubert classes$[X_{s_1 s_2}]$ and$[X_{s_2 s_1}]$ are distinct, and the order of the simple reflections matters.

The Chevalley formula is not merely a computational tool — it reveals deeper structure. The coefficients $\langle \omega_i, \beta^\vee \rangle$ are precisely the entries of the Cartan matrix data, connecting the intersection theory directly to the root system.

\begin{corollary}
The Schubert basis structure constants $c^w_{u,v}$ are non-negative integers for all$u, v, w \in W$.
\end{corollary}

This follows from the Chevalley formula by induction: the divisor Chevalley coefficients $\langle \omega_i, \beta^\vee \rangle$ are positive integers, and an inductive argument on length shows that all products of Schubert classes have non-negative integer coefficients.

**Example 3.** For $G = Sp_4$ (type$C_2$), the flag variety has$|W| = 8$ elements (the dihedral group of order 8). The Chevalley formula for the divisor class$[X_{s_1}]$ gives$[X_{s_1}] \cup [X_{w}] = \sum_\beta \langle \omega_1, \beta^\vee \rangle [X_{ws_\beta}]$. For the type$C_2$ root system, some long roots contribute coefficient 2 (since$\langle \omega_1, \alpha_{long}^\vee \rangle = 2$), giving rise to Schubert structure constants$> 1$. This illustrates that the coefficients genuinely depend on the root system and are not always 1.

The Chevalley formula deepens further in the passage to *quantum cohomology* of $G/B$.

\begin{theorem}[Quantum Chevalley Formula, Fulton–Pandharipande]
The quantum cohomology ring $QH^*(G/B)$ is obtained from$H^*(G/B)$ by adding corrections from rational curves: the quantum product is$[X_{s_i}] *_q [X_w] = [X_{s_i}] \cup [X_w] + \sum_\beta q_\beta [X_{ws_\beta}]$ where the sum runs over positive coroots$\beta^\vee$ satisfying an appropriate divisibility condition, and$q_\beta$ are formal parameters (Novikov variables).
\end{theorem}

**Example 4.** For $G = SL_2$ and$G/B = \mathbb{P}^1$, the ordinary cohomology ring is$\mathbb{Z}[h]/(h^2)$ with$h = [X_{s}]$ (the point class). The quantum cohomology ring is$\mathbb{Z}[h, q]/(h^2 - q)$, or equivalently$\mathbb{Z}[[q]][h]/(h^2 - q)$: the product$[pt] *_q [pt] = q$ reflects the fact that there is exactly one degree-1 rational curve (line) in$\mathbb{P}^1$ through any two points — the whole$\mathbb{P}^1$ itself — giving the quantum correction$q$.

\begin{remark}
The quantum corrections in the quantum Chevalley formula are related to counts of curves by Gromov–Witten theory. For $G/B$, all Gromov–Witten invariants can be expressed in terms of the classical structure constants and the Chevalley formula data. This connection to curve counts links the flag variety's cohomology ring directly to the *Verlinde formula* of conformal field theory (the ranks of spaces of conformal blocks on curves) and to the representation theory of affine Lie algebras at positive level. The entire cohomological package of$G/B$ — classical and quantum — thus connects representation theory, algebraic geometry, and mathematical physics in a single object.
\end{remark}
