# 52.1 The Flag Variety and Schubert Calculus

The flag variety is both the starting point and the central object of geometric representation theory. For a reductive group $G$ over $\mathbb{C}$ with Borel subgroup $B$, the flag variety $X = G/B$ is a projective algebraic variety that parametrizes the complete flags in the defining representation: for $G = GL_n$, a point of $X$ is a chain $0 = V_0 \subset V_1 \subset \cdots \subset V_n = \mathbb{C}^n$ of subspaces with $\dim V_k = k$. The variety $X$ is simultaneously a smooth projective variety, a compact complex manifold, and a homogeneous $G$-space, and these three perspectives — algebraic, analytic, and group-theoretic — are all essential to its role in representation theory. The flag variety is not merely an ancillary construction; it is the geometric object whose sheaves are, after the Beilinson–Bernstein theorem, literally the representations of $\mathfrak{g}$.

The Bruhat decomposition $G = \bigsqcup_{w \in W} BwB$ (where $W$ is the Weyl group) descends to a cell decomposition of the flag variety: $G/B = \bigsqcup_{w \in W} C_w$, where the Schubert cell $C_w = BwB/B \cong \mathbb{A}^{\ell(w)}$ is an affine space of dimension equal to the length $\ell(w)$ of the Weyl group element $w$. This decomposition has striking properties: the cells are pairwise disjoint, they exhaust the flag variety, and the closure $X_w = \overline{C_w}$ of each cell — the Schubert variety — is the union of all Schubert cells $C_y$ with $y \leq w$ in the Bruhat order. The Bruhat order on $W$, defined by $y \leq w$ if and only if $X_y \subseteq X_w$, is thus simultaneously a combinatorial order on the Weyl group and a geometric containment relation among subvarieties of $X$. Schubert varieties are generally singular — their singular loci are themselves unions of smaller Schubert varieties — and their singularities are the source of the deep representation-theoretic information they encode.

Schubert calculus is the study of the cohomology ring $H^*(X, \mathbb{Z})$ via the Schubert classes. Each Schubert variety $X_w$ defines a fundamental class $[X_w] \in H^{2\ell(w)}(X, \mathbb{Z})$ (by Poincaré duality, a class in cohomological degree $2(\dim X - \ell(w))$), and these classes form a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis for the cohomology. The cup product of two Schubert classes $[X_u] \cdot [X_v] = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w [X_w]$ is expressed through the Littlewood–Richardson numbers $c_{u,v}^w$, which are non-negative integers counting the points in certain triple intersections of Schubert varieties in general position. The Chevalley formula gives a recursive description of these products when one of the classes is a divisor class (a Schubert class of codimension one), and the Pieri formula handles the case $G = GL_n$ with one class corresponding to a symmetric power. These formulas make the cohomology ring of the flag variety explicitly computable and connect it to the combinatorics of the symmetric group, Young tableaux, and the representation theory of $GL_n$.

The section establishes the Bruhat decomposition and the structure of Schubert cells, proves that Schubert classes form a basis for cohomology, and develops enough of Schubert calculus to compute intersection numbers in the Grassmannian and the full flag variety. The geometric picture that emerges — a projective variety stratified into affine cells with a combinatorial closure relation — will recur throughout the chapter, first as the source of $\mathcal{D}$-modules in the Beilinson–Bernstein theorem, and then as the geometric realization of representation-theoretic multiplicities through intersection cohomology.
