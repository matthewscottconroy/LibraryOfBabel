# 52.2.1 $\mathcal{D}$-Modules on a Variety

One of the central themes of geometric representation theory is that representations of Lie algebras and algebraic groups can be studied by placing them on varieties. The theory of $\mathcal{D}$-modules makes this precise: it provides a way to encode systems of differential equations, and hence representations, as sheaves on algebraic varieties. The key idea is that the algebra of differential operators on a smooth variety is rich enough to capture all the algebraic structure one could want.

\begin{definition}
On a smooth algebraic variety $X$ over$\mathbb{C}$, the *sheaf of differential operators*$\mathcal{D}_X$ is the subsheaf of$\mathcal{E}nd_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_X)$ generated (as a sheaf of$\mathcal{O}_X$-algebras on the left) by$\mathcal{O}_X$ (acting by multiplication) and the tangent sheaf$\Theta_X = \mathcal{D}er(\mathcal{O}_X)$ (acting by differentiation). A *$\mathcal{D}_X$-module* is a quasicoherent sheaf$\mathcal{M}$ of left$\mathcal{D}_X$-modules.
\end{definition}

In local coordinates $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ on$X$, the sheaf$\mathcal{D}_X$ is locally the Weyl algebra$\mathcal{O}_X \langle \partial_1, \ldots, \partial_n \rangle$ with relations$[\partial_i, x_j] = \delta_{ij}$ and$[\partial_i, \partial_j] = 0$. A$\mathcal{D}_X$-module$\mathcal{M}$ is then a sheaf that is both an$\mathcal{O}_X$-module and admits a connection in each coordinate direction, where the connection operators satisfy the same commutation relations.

**Example 1.** The structure sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X$ is a$\mathcal{D}_X$-module: vector fields act by differentiation, and functions act by multiplication. This is the simplest example, and in some sense the "trivial"$\mathcal{D}$-module, corresponding to the trivial flat connection.

**Example 2.** Let $Y \subset X$ be a smooth closed subvariety of codimension$d$. The *delta-function module*$\delta_Y = i_+(\mathcal{O}_Y)$ (the direct image of$\mathcal{O}_Y$ as a$\mathcal{D}$-module under the closed embedding$i: Y \hookrightarrow X$) is a$\mathcal{D}_X$-module supported on$Y$. In local coordinates where$Y = \{x_1 = \cdots = x_d = 0\}$, this is the module$\mathcal{O}_Y \otimes_{\mathbb{C}} \mathbb{C}[\partial_1, \ldots, \partial_d]$, where$\partial_i$ acts as "derivation perpendicular to$Y$." For$Y = \{pt\} \subset X$, this is the genuine delta-function distribution — the$\mathcal{D}$-module of all derivatives of the delta function at the point.

\begin{definition}
A $\mathcal{D}_X$-module$\mathcal{M}$ is *holonomic* if its characteristic variety$\mathrm{Ch}(\mathcal{M}) \subset T^*X$ is Lagrangian (has dimension$= \dim X$). Holonomic modules are the "finite type"$\mathcal{D}$-modules in the sense that they have finite length and their solutions form finite-dimensional local systems on the smooth locus.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bernstein's Inequality]
For any nonzero finitely generated $\mathcal{D}_X$-module$\mathcal{M}$,$\dim \mathrm{Ch}(\mathcal{M}) \geq \dim X$.
\end{theorem}

Holonomic $\mathcal{D}$-modules are those achieving the minimum dimension$\dim X$ for their characteristic variety.

The bridge from $\mathcal{D}$-modules to representation theory is the action of Lie algebras via vector fields. If an algebraic group$G$ with Lie algebra$\mathfrak{g}$ acts on$X$, then the action differentiates to a Lie algebra homomorphism$\mathfrak{g} \to \Theta(X)$ — each element of$\mathfrak{g}$ gives a vector field on$X$.

**Example 3.** Let $G = SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ act on$X = \mathbb{P}^1$ by Möbius transformations. The Lie algebra$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ has basis$\{e, f, h\}$, and these act as vector fields:
\begin{align*}
e &\mapsto -z^2 \partial_z, \\
f &\mapsto \partial_z, \\
h &\mapsto -2z \partial_z
\end{align*}
on the affine chart $\mathbb{A}^1 = \{z\} \subset \mathbb{P}^1$. Every$\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{P}^1}$-module$\mathcal{M}$ therefore acquires an$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-action on its global sections$\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{M})$: an element$x \in \mathfrak{sl}_2$ acts by the differential operator$\xi(x) \in \Theta(\mathbb{P}^1)$. This is the mechanism by which$\mathcal{D}$-modules on$G/B = \mathbb{P}^1$ produce$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-representations.

The connection between $\mathcal{D}$-modules and representations can be made more precise. Let$\mathcal{D}(G)$ denote the algebra of differential operators on$G$ (as an affine variety). The left$G$-invariant differential operators form the universal enveloping algebra$U(\mathfrak{g})$, and the right$G$-invariant differential operators also form$U(\mathfrak{g})$, acting on the right. These two copies commute, and this bimodule structure is the bridge between$\mathcal{D}$-modules on$G$ and representations of$\mathfrak{g}$.

\begin{proposition}
The center $Z(\mathfrak{g})$ of$U(\mathfrak{g})$ acts on$\Gamma(G/B, \mathcal{M})$ for any$G$-equivariant$\mathcal{D}$-module$\mathcal{M}$. The character by which$Z(\mathfrak{g})$ acts (the *central character*) is determined by the twist parameter$\lambda$ (the Chern class of the line bundle underlying the twisted sheaf of differential operators$\mathcal{D}_\lambda$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence provides a deep equivalence between holonomic $\mathcal{D}_X$-modules with regular singularities and perverse sheaves on$X$ (with$\mathbb{C}$ coefficients). Under this correspondence, the structure sheaf$\mathcal{O}_X$ with its natural$\mathcal{D}$-module structure corresponds to the constant sheaf$\mathbb{C}_X$; the delta-function module$\delta_Y$ at a subvariety corresponds to the intersection cohomology sheaf$\mathrm{IC}_Y$. This equivalence is the algebraic de Rham theorem for singular spaces, and it identifies the two main languages of geometric representation theory —$\mathcal{D}$-modules and perverse sheaves — as two presentations of the same data.
\end{remark}

**Example 4.** On the affine line $X = \mathbb{A}^1$, the$\mathcal{D}$-module$\mathcal{D}_X / \mathcal{D}_X \cdot z$ is the delta-function at the origin: it is isomorphic to$\mathbb{C}[\partial]$, a module generated by$\delta_0$ with$z \cdot \delta_0 = 0$ and$\partial \cdot \delta_0 = \partial\delta_0$. This is a simple holonomic$\mathcal{D}$-module with characteristic variety$= T^*_{\{0\}} \mathbb{A}^1$ (the conormal to the origin), the cotangent fiber. Under the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence, this corresponds to the perverse sheaf$i_*\mathbb{C}$ at the origin (the skyscraper sheaf), as expected.
