# 52.2.2 Localization of $\mathfrak{g}$-Modules to$\mathcal{D}_\lambda$-Modules on$G/B$

To pass between representation theory and geometry on the flag variety, one needs functors in both directions: from $\mathfrak{g}$-modules to$\mathcal{D}$-modules on$G/B$, and back. The construction requires a twist parameter$\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, because the flag variety admits a family of "shifted" sheaves of differential operators parameterized by weights.

\begin{definition}
For each weight $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, the *sheaf of twisted differential operators*$\mathcal{D}_\lambda$ on$G/B$ is the sheaf of differential operators acting on sections of the line bundle$\mathcal{L}_\lambda$: in local terms,$\mathcal{D}_\lambda(U) =$ the$\mathbb{C}$-algebra of differential operators on$\mathcal{L}_\lambda|_U$ for open$U \subset G/B$. At$\lambda = 0$,$\mathcal{D}_0 = \mathcal{D}_{G/B}$ is the ordinary sheaf of differential operators.
\end{definition}

The key properties of $\mathcal{D}_\lambda$ are: it is a coherent sheaf of algebras on$G/B$; it carries a canonical$G$-equivariant structure; and the Lie algebra$\mathfrak{g}$ maps to its global sections via the infinitesimal action. Explicitly, differentiating the$G$-action on$G/B$ gives a Lie algebra map$\mu: \mathfrak{g} \to \Theta(G/B)$, and composing with the inclusion$\Theta(G/B) \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}_{G/B} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}_\lambda$ gives a map$\mu_\lambda: U(\mathfrak{g}) \to \Gamma(G/B, \mathcal{D}_\lambda)$.

\begin{theorem}[Beilinson–Bernstein]
The map $\mu_\lambda: U(\mathfrak{g}) \to \Gamma(G/B, \mathcal{D}_\lambda)$ is surjective for all$\lambda$, and its kernel is the ideal$I_\lambda = \ker \chi_\lambda \cdot U(\mathfrak{g})$ generated by the kernel of the central character$\chi_\lambda: Z(\mathfrak{g}) \to \mathbb{C}$ corresponding to$\lambda$ via the Harish-Chandra isomorphism (sending$\lambda + \rho$ to its$W$-orbit).
\end{theorem}

In other words, $\Gamma(G/B, \mathcal{D}_\lambda) \cong U(\mathfrak{g}) / I_\lambda$. This is a remarkable statement: the global sections of the twisted differential operators on$G/B$ recover the primitive quotient of$U(\mathfrak{g})$ by the character$\chi_\lambda$.

\begin{definition}
For $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, let$M(\mathfrak{g}, \lambda)$ denote the category of finitely generated$\mathfrak{g}$-modules on which$Z(\mathfrak{g})$ acts by the character$\chi_\lambda$. The *localization functor*
$$\Delta_\lambda: M(\mathfrak{g}, \lambda) \to \mathcal{D}_\lambda\text{-mod}$$
is defined by $\Delta_\lambda(M) = \mathcal{D}_\lambda \otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g})} M$, using the map$\mu_\lambda: U(\mathfrak{g}) \to \Gamma(G/B, \mathcal{D}_\lambda)$ to view$\mathcal{D}_\lambda$ as a right$U(\mathfrak{g})$-module.
\end{definition}

In the reverse direction, the *global sections functor* $\Gamma: \mathcal{D}_\lambda\text{-mod} \to M(\mathfrak{g}, \lambda)$ sends$\mathcal{M} \mapsto \Gamma(G/B, \mathcal{M})$, which inherits a$\mathfrak{g}$-action from the$\mathcal{D}_\lambda$-module structure.

**Example 1.** For $G = SL_2(\mathbb{C})$,$G/B = \mathbb{P}^1$, and$\lambda = n\omega$ ($n \geq 0$). The localization of the Verma module$M(n\omega)$ is$\Delta_{n\omega}(M(n\omega)) = \mathcal{D}_{n\omega} \otimes_{U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)} M(n\omega)$. Since$M(n\omega)$ is the universal highest weight module, and$\mathcal{D}_{n\omega}$ acts on$\mathcal{O}(n)$, the localization is the line bundle$\mathcal{O}(n)$ viewed as a$\mathcal{D}$-module with its natural flat structure... actually, more precisely,$\Delta_{n\omega}(M(n\omega))$ is the *standard*$\mathcal{D}$-module$j_{!}(\mathcal{O}_{C_e})$ associated to the dense Schubert cell. For the simple module$L(n\omega)$ (finite-dimensional of dimension$n+1$), the localization$\Delta_{n\omega}(L(n\omega))$ is the line bundle$\mathcal{O}(n)$ with its natural$\mathcal{D}$-module structure (the flat connection with no poles).

**Example 2.** The global sections functor in the reverse direction: $\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(n)) = H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathcal{O}(n))$ is the space of degree-$n$ homogeneous polynomials in two variables, which is the$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module$L(n\omega)^*$ of dimension$n+1$, confirming that$\Gamma \circ \Delta_{n\omega}(L(n\omega)) \cong L(n\omega)^*$.

The two functors $(\Delta_\lambda, \Gamma)$ form an adjoint pair: there is a natural isomorphism$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_\lambda}(\Delta_\lambda(M), \mathcal{N}) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{M(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)}(M, \Gamma(\mathcal{N}))$ for$M \in M(\mathfrak{g}, \lambda)$ and$\mathcal{N} \in \mathcal{D}_\lambda\text{-mod}$.

\begin{proposition}
For any $M \in M(\mathfrak{g}, \lambda)$, there is a natural unit map$M \to \Gamma(\Delta_\lambda(M))$. For any$\mathcal{N} \in \mathcal{D}_\lambda\text{-mod}$, there is a natural counit map$\Delta_\lambda(\Gamma(\mathcal{N})) \to \mathcal{N}$.
\end{proposition}

The question of when these unit and counit maps are isomorphisms — when $\Delta_\lambda$ and$\Gamma$ are mutually inverse equivalences — is the content of the Beilinson–Bernstein theorem (Section 52.2.3). The answer depends critically on the dominance of$\lambda$.

\begin{remark}
The condition that $Z(\mathfrak{g})$ acts by$\chi_\lambda$ is necessary for the localization to be non-trivially$\mathcal{D}_\lambda$-linear. Different$\lambda$ in the same$W$-orbit under the dot action$w \cdot \lambda = w(\lambda + \rho) - \rho$ give the same central character$\chi_\lambda$, but different twisted differential operators$\mathcal{D}_\lambda$. This discrepancy between Weyl group orbits of$\lambda$ and Weyl group orbits of$\lambda + \rho$ is the source of the "dot action" in representation theory and is why the Weyl vector$\rho$ appears throughout the theory.
\end{remark}

**Example 3.** For $\lambda = -\rho$ (the negative Weyl vector), the central character is$\chi_{-\rho} = \chi_0$ (the character of the trivial$Z(\mathfrak{g})$-action). This corresponds to the case studied in the proof of the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjecture: the$\mathcal{D}_{-\rho}$-module localization sends Verma modules$M(w \cdot (-\rho))$ to standard$\mathcal{D}$-modules on Schubert cells, and the composition multiplicities$[M(w \cdot (-\rho)) : L(y \cdot (-\rho))]$ correspond to the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials$P_{y,w}(1)$ via the Beilinson–Bernstein equivalence and the decomposition theorem.
