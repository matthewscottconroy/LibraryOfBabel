# 52.3.1 Perverse Sheaves on Schubert Varieties

The Schubert varieties are generally singular — they fail to be smooth at the boundaries of Schubert cells where cells of lower dimension meet — and classical sheaf theory on singular spaces loses important properties like Poincaré duality. The theory of *perverse sheaves*, developed by Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne, and Gabber in the early 1980s, provides a robust replacement: a class of complexes of sheaves on stratified spaces that behaves like a category of "sheaves" in spite of being technically a subcategory of the derived category. On Schubert varieties, perverse sheaves are the geometric objects that classify simple $\mathfrak{g}$-modules via the Beilinson–Bernstein equivalence.

The key difficulty with singular varieties is that the ordinary derived category $D^b(X)$ does not have an intrinsic "abelian" heart adapted to the singularities. Perverse sheaves solve this by constructing a new$t$-structure on$D^b_c(X)$ (the derived category of constructible sheaves) whose heart is the abelian category of perverse sheaves. The conditions defining this$t$-structure — the *perversity conditions* — are designed so that the resulting theory has Verdier duality symmetry.

\begin{definition}
On a complex algebraic variety $X$ stratified by locally closed smooth strata$X = \bigsqcup_\alpha X_\alpha$ with$d_\alpha = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} X_\alpha$, a constructible complex$\mathcal{F}^\bullet \in D^b_c(X)$ is *perverse* if:
\begin{itemize}
\item (Support condition) $\mathcal{H}^i(j_\alpha^* \mathcal{F}^\bullet) = 0$ for all$\alpha$ and all$i > -d_\alpha$.
\item (Cosupport condition) $\mathcal{H}^i(j_\alpha^! \mathcal{F}^\bullet) = 0$ for all$\alpha$ and all$i < -d_\alpha$.
\end{itemize}
These are the *middle perversity conditions*.
\end{definition}

The two conditions together say that the complex $\mathcal{F}^\bullet$ has cohomology sheaves concentrated in a specific range of degrees on each stratum, depending on the stratum's dimension. The "middle" in middle perversity refers to the fact that the shift$-d_\alpha$ splits the difference between the support condition of a sheaf ($i > 0$) and that of its Verdier dual.

**Example 1.** The constant sheaf $\mathbb{C}_X[n]$ shifted by$n = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} X$ is perverse when$X$ is smooth. In this case, the perversity conditions reduce to the condition that the cohomology sheaves$\mathcal{H}^i(\mathcal{F}) = 0$ for$i > 0$ (since the support condition at each stratum$X_\alpha = X$ of dimension$n$ requires$i > -n$, i.e.,$i + n > 0$). Since$\mathcal{H}^i(\mathbb{C}_X[n]) = \mathbb{C}_X$ for$i = -n$ and 0 otherwise, both conditions are satisfied.

**Example 2.** On the flag variety $G/B$ stratified by Schubert cells$\{C_w\}_{w \in W}$, the simple perverse sheaves are the intersection cohomology sheaves$\mathrm{IC}_w = j_{w,!*}(\mathbb{C}_{C_w}[\ell(w)])$, one for each$w \in W$. The perversity conditions on$\mathrm{IC}_w$: at stratum$C_v$ (of dimension$\ell(v)$), the stalk cohomology$H^i(\mathrm{IC}_w|_{C_v}) = 0$ for$i > -\ell(v)$ and$i < -\ell(v)$ (when$v \leq w$, else the stalk is 0). For$v = w$ (the open stratum), the stalk is$\mathbb{C}[-\ell(w)]$, concentrated in degree$-\ell(w)$.

\begin{theorem}
The perverse sheaves on $G/B$ (stratified by Schubert cells) form an abelian category$\mathrm{Perv}(G/B)$. The simple objects of this category are exactly the intersection cohomology sheaves$\{\mathrm{IC}_w\}_{w \in W}$.
\end{theorem}

The proof that this is an abelian category uses a general result: any $t$-structure on a triangulated category has an abelian heart. The perverse$t$-structure (defined by the support and cosupport conditions) gives an abelian category whose simple objects are exactly the IC sheaves — a non-trivial theorem that depends on the stratification being *Whitney* and the$t$-structure being well-defined.

**Example 3.** For $G = GL_3(\mathbb{C})$ and$w = s_1 s_2 = (132) \in S_3$ (length 2), the Schubert variety$X_w$ is smooth (it is the blowup of$\mathbb{P}^2$ at a point, which is smooth). Therefore$\mathrm{IC}_w = \mathbb{C}_{X_w}[2]$, and the stalk of$\mathrm{IC}_w$ at any point of$X_w$ is$\mathbb{C}$ in degree$-2$ and 0 elsewhere. The KL polynomial$P_{v,w}(q) = 1$ for all$v \leq w$ (since$X_w$ is smooth), confirming the general principle that$P_{v,w}(q) = 1$ for all$v$ when$X_w$ is smooth.

The connection to representation theory via the Beilinson–Bernstein equivalence: under the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (which identifies perverse sheaves with holonomic $\mathcal{D}$-modules with regular singularities), the IC sheaves$\mathrm{IC}_w$ correspond exactly to the simple holonomic$\mathcal{D}$-modules on$G/B$, which correspond in turn to the simple$\mathfrak{g}$-modules$L(w \cdot \lambda)$.

\begin{remark}
The Decomposition Theorem of Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber (BBD) is the key tool for computing with perverse sheaves on Schubert varieties. It states: for a proper morphism $f: X \to Y$ of algebraic varieties and a semisimple perverse sheaf$\mathcal{F}$ on$X$, the pushforward$Rf_*(\mathcal{F})$ is a direct sum of shifted IC sheaves on$Y$. Applied to the map$\tilde{X}_w \to X_w$ from the Bott–Samelson resolution of a Schubert variety (a smooth variety mapping birationally to$X_w$), the decomposition theorem expresses the pushforward of$\mathbb{C}_{\tilde{X}_w}[\ell(w)]$ as$\bigoplus_{v \leq w} \mathrm{IC}_v^{\oplus m_{v,w}}$ where the multiplicities$m_{v,w}$ are the KL polynomials$P_{v,w}(1)$. This is the geometric proof of the KL conjecture.
\end{remark}

**Example 4.** For $G = SL_4$ and$w = (2143)$ (avoiding 3412 and 4231),$X_w$ is smooth, so all IC sheaves on$X_w$ are just shifted constant sheaves, and all KL polynomials$P_{v,w} = 1$ for$v \leq w$. For$w = (3412)$ (containing the pattern 3412),$X_w$ is singular, and$P_{v,w}(q) \neq 1$ for some$v < w$. The first occurrence of a non-trivial KL polynomial in type$A$ is exactly at this permutation:$P_{e,(3412)}(q) = 1 + q$.
