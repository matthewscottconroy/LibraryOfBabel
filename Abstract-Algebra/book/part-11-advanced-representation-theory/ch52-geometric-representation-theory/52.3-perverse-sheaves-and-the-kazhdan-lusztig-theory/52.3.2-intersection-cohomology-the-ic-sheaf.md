# 52.3.2 Intersection Cohomology; the IC Sheaf

Ordinary singular cohomology of a singular variety fails to satisfy Poincaré duality: on a smooth compact complex manifold of complex dimension $n$, one has$H^k(X; \mathbb{Q}) \cong H^{2n-k}(X; \mathbb{Q})$, but this isomorphism breaks down as soon as$X$ acquires singularities. This failure is not merely a nuisance — it signals that the ordinary cohomology is not the "right" invariant for singular varieties. *Intersection cohomology*, introduced by Goresky and MacPherson in 1980, remedies this defect: it is a cohomology theory for singular spaces that restores Poincaré duality, even when the space is not smooth.

The key idea is to replace the constant sheaf $\mathbb{Q}_X$ (whose cohomology is ordinary cohomology) with a more sophisticated complex of sheaves — the *intersection cohomology complex* or *IC sheaf*$\mathrm{IC}_X$ — that is uniquely characterized by extending the constant sheaf from the smooth locus of$X$ in a way that satisfies the middle perversity conditions.

\begin{definition}
Let $X$ be a complex algebraic variety,$U \subset X$ the smooth locus (a dense open subset), and$j: U \hookrightarrow X$ the open inclusion. The *intersection cohomology complex* of$X$ is the unique (up to isomorphism) perverse sheaf$\mathrm{IC}_X \in \mathrm{Perv}(X)$ satisfying:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{IC}_X|_U \cong \mathbb{Q}_U[\dim_{\mathbb{C}} X]$ (the shifted constant sheaf on the smooth locus).
\item $\mathrm{IC}_X$ has no subobject or quotient object (in the abelian category of perverse sheaves) supported on$X \setminus U$.
\end{itemize}
The *intersection cohomology groups* of $X$ are$IH^k(X; \mathbb{Q}) = H^{k-\dim X}(X; \mathrm{IC}_X)$.
\end{definition}

For smooth $X$, the IC sheaf is just the constant sheaf shifted:$\mathrm{IC}_X = \mathbb{Q}_X[\dim X]$, and intersection cohomology coincides with ordinary cohomology. For singular$X$, the IC sheaf is strictly more complicated.

\begin{theorem}[Goresky–MacPherson]
For any compact complex algebraic variety $X$ of dimension$n$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Poincaré duality: $IH^k(X; \mathbb{Q}) \cong IH^{2n-k}(X; \mathbb{Q})$.
\item Lefschetz hyperplane theorem.
\item Hard Lefschetz theorem: for an ample class $L$, multiplication by$L^{n-k}: IH^k \to IH^{2n-k}$ is an isomorphism.
\end{itemize}
These are the expected properties of a "good" cohomology theory, which ordinary cohomology fails to have on singular spaces.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** Let $X = \{(x,y,z) \in \mathbb{C}^3 : xy = z^2\}$ be the quadric cone (the simplest singular variety). The smooth locus is$U = X \setminus \{0\}$. Topologically,$X$ is contractible (the cone over$S^2$), so$H^k(X; \mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{Q}$ for$k = 0$ and 0 otherwise. But$X$ has real dimension 4 ($\dim_{\mathbb{C}} X = 2$), so Poincaré duality for ordinary cohomology fails:$H^0 \cong \mathbb{Q}$ but$H^4 = 0$ (since$X$ is contractible). For intersection cohomology:$IH^0(X) \cong IH^4(X) \cong \mathbb{Q}$ and$IH^2(X) = 0$ (the middle cohomology vanishes because the singular point contributes no middle-perversity correction). Poincaré duality$IH^k \cong IH^{4-k}$ holds.

For Schubert varieties, the IC sheaf $\mathrm{IC}_w = j_{w,!*}(\mathbb{Q}_{C_w}[\ell(w)])$ is the IC sheaf of the Schubert variety$X_w$, normalized so that$\mathrm{IC}_w|_{C_w} = \mathbb{Q}_{C_w}[\ell(w)]$.

\begin{theorem}
The stalk of $\mathrm{IC}_w$ at a point$p \in C_y \subset X_w$ (for$y < w$ in the Bruhat order) has cohomology:
$$\dim H^{-\ell(w)+2k}(\mathrm{IC}_w)_p = [q^k] P_{y,w}(q)$$
(the coefficient of $q^k$ in the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial$P_{y,w}(q)$). In particular,$H^i(\mathrm{IC}_w)_p = 0$ for all$i$ with$i + \ell(w)$ odd (the stalks are concentrated in even degree).
\end{theorem}

**Example 2.** For $G = SL_4$ and$w = (3412)$,$y = e$ (identity), the KL polynomial is$P_{e,(3412)}(q) = 1 + q$. This means: the stalk of$\mathrm{IC}_{(3412)}$ at a point$p \in C_e$ (the base point of$G/B$) has$\dim H^{-4}(\mathrm{IC}_{(3412)})_p = 1$ (from the constant term 1) and$\dim H^{-2}(\mathrm{IC}_{(3412)})_p = 1$ (from the coefficient of$q^1$). This non-trivial$H^{-2}$ is the geometric manifestation of the singularity of$X_{(3412)}$.

**Example 3.** For a smooth Schubert variety $X_w$ (one avoiding 3412 and 4231 in type$A$), all KL polynomials$P_{y,w}(q) = 1$ for$y \leq w$. This means the stalk of$\mathrm{IC}_w$ at every point is concentrated in a single degree, with no "off-diagonal" cohomology. The IC sheaf equals the constant sheaf$\mathbb{Q}_{X_w}[\ell(w)]$, and the intersection cohomology of$X_w$ equals the ordinary cohomology of$X_w$.

The surprise here is that the correction terms — the stalks of $\mathrm{IC}_w$ at lower strata — are not determined by topology alone, but by deep algebra. Specifically, the stalk of$\mathrm{IC}_w$ at a point$p \in C_y \subset X_w$ (for$y < w$ in the Bruhat order) is given by the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials: the dimension of the$k$-th cohomology of the stalk equals the coefficient of$q^k$ in the KL polynomial$P_{y,w}(q)$. This is the geometric interpretation of the KL polynomials — they measure how singular$X_w$ is along$X_y$.

\begin{corollary}
The coefficients of the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials $P_{y,w}(q)$ are non-negative integers.
\end{corollary}

This non-negativity, which is completely mysterious from the algebraic (Hecke algebra) definition of the KL polynomials, follows immediately from the geometric interpretation: the coefficients are dimensions of cohomology groups.

\begin{remark}
The theory of intersection cohomology has been vastly generalized beyond the Schubert variety setting. Intersection cohomology with coefficients in a *local system* (a locally constant sheaf) on the smooth locus defines the *IC sheaf with twist*, which appears in the geometric Satake equivalence and the representation theory of affine Lie algebras. The IC sheaves of affine Schubert varieties are particularly important: they compute Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for affine Weyl groups and (via the geometric Satake equivalence) the representation theory of the Langlands dual group.
\end{remark}
