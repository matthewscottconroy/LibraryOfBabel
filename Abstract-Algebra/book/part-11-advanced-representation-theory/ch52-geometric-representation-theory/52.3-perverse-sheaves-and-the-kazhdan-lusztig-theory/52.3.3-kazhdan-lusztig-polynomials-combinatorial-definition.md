# 52.3.3 Kazhdan–Lusztig Polynomials; Combinatorial Definition

The Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials $P_{y,w}(q)$ were introduced in 1979 by Kazhdan and Lusztig through a purely algebraic and combinatorial construction involving the Hecke algebra, before their geometric meaning in terms of intersection cohomology was understood. The discovery that these combinatorially-defined polynomials governed the topology of Schubert varieties and the representation theory of Lie algebras was one of the great surprises of late twentieth century mathematics.

The setting is the *Hecke algebra* $\mathcal{H}(W)$, a deformation of the group algebra$\mathbb{Z}[W]$ over the polynomial ring$\mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}, q^{-1/2}]$.

\begin{definition}
The *Hecke algebra* $\mathcal{H}(W, S)$ of a Coxeter group$(W, S)$ is the$\mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}, q^{-1/2}]$-algebra generated by$\{T_s\}_{s \in S}$, subject to:
\begin{itemize}
\item Braid relations: $T_s T_{s'} T_s \cdots = T_{s'} T_s T_{s'} \cdots$ (with$m_{ss'}$ factors on each side, where$m_{ss'}$ is the order of$ss'$ in$W$).
\item Quadratic relation: $(T_s - q)(T_s + 1) = 0$, i.e.,$T_s^2 = (q-1)T_s + q$.
\end{itemize}
As $q \to 1$,$(T_s - 1)(T_s + 1) = 0$, so$T_s^2 = 1$: the Hecke algebra degenerates to the group algebra$\mathbb{Z}[W]$.
\end{definition}

For $w \in W$ with reduced expression$w = s_{i_1} \cdots s_{i_k}$, one sets$T_w = T_{s_{i_1}} \cdots T_{s_{i_k}}$; this is well-defined by the braid relations. The set$\{T_w : w \in W\}$ is a$\mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}, q^{-1/2}]$-basis for$\mathcal{H}(W)$.

**Example 1.** For $W = S_2 = \{e, s\}$, the Hecke algebra is$\mathcal{H}(S_2) = \mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}, q^{-1/2}]\langle T_s \rangle / (T_s^2 = (q-1)T_s + q)$. A basis is$\{T_e = 1, T_s\}$. Multiplication:$T_s \cdot T_s = (q-1)T_s + q \cdot 1$.

The Hecke algebra admits a natural *bar-involution*: an anti-linear ring involution $\overline{(\cdot)}: \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{H}$ defined by$\overline{q^{1/2}} = q^{-1/2}$ and$\overline{T_s} = T_s^{-1} = q^{-1}T_s + (q^{-1} - 1)$. Explicitly,$\overline{T_w} = (T_{w^{-1}})^{-1}$.

\begin{theorem}[Kazhdan–Lusztig, 1979]
There exists a unique $\mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}, q^{-1/2}]$-basis$\{C_w\}_{w \in W}$ of$\mathcal{H}(W)$ — the *Kazhdan–Lusztig basis* — satisfying:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\overline{C_w} = C_w$ (bar-invariance).
\item $C_w \equiv T_w \pmod{q^{1/2} \mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}] \cdot \bigoplus_{v < w} \mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2}] T_v}$ (triangularity).
\end{itemize}
The *Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials* $P_{y,w}(q)$ are defined by
$$C_w = \sum_{y \leq w} (-1)^{\ell(w) - \ell(y)} q^{(\ell(w)-\ell(y))/2} P_{y,w}(q^{-1}) T_y.$$
\end{theorem}

**Example 2.** For $W = S_2$, there are two KL polynomials:$P_{e,e}(q) = P_{s,s}(q) = 1$ and$P_{e,s}(q)$. The KL basis element is$C_s = q^{-1/2}(T_s + 1)$ (check:$\overline{C_s} = q^{1/2}(T_s^{-1} + 1) = q^{1/2}(q^{-1}T_s + q^{-1} - 1 + 1) = q^{-1/2}T_s + q^{-1/2} = q^{-1/2}(T_s + 1) = C_s$, bar-invariant). So$P_{e,s}(q) = 1$ and all KL polynomials for$S_2$ are 1 (since the Schubert variety$X_s = \mathbb{P}^1$ is smooth).

**Example 3.** For $W = S_3$, all Schubert varieties for$GL_3/B$ are smooth (this can be checked by the pattern-avoidance criterion: all elements of$S_3$ avoid 3412 and 4231 since there are only 3 elements to permute). So all KL polynomials for$S_3$ are 1.

**Example 4.** The first non-trivial KL polynomial appears for $W = S_4$. The element$w = (3412) \in S_4$ (in one-line notation) satisfies$P_{e,(3412)}(q) = 1 + q$. This is computed by the recursive formula for KL polynomials: one uses the multiplication formula$T_{s_i} C_w = C_{s_i w}$ (if$\ell(s_i w) > \ell(w)$) or$T_{s_i} C_w = (q^{1/2} + q^{-1/2}) C_w$ (if$\ell(s_i w) < \ell(w)$) to build up the KL basis inductively, and the off-diagonal coefficients (the KL polynomials) track the "errors" introduced.

By construction, $P_{w,w}(q) = 1$ and$P_{y,w}(q)$ has degree at most$(\ell(w)-\ell(y)-1)/2$ for$y < w$. The deeper properties — that the coefficients are non-negative integers (proved geometrically, not combinatorially) and that they compute intersection cohomology stalks of Schubert varieties (the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjecture, proved by Beilinson–Bernstein and Brylinski–Kashiwara in 1981) — are not visible from the combinatorial definition alone.

\begin{remark}
There are also *inverse KL polynomials* $Q_{y,w}(q)$ defined by the matrix relation$\sum_{y \leq z \leq w} (-1)^{\ell(z)-\ell(y)} P_{y,z}(q) Q_{z,w}(q) = \delta_{y,w}$ (the inverse of the KL matrix). These arise in a parallel role in the study of the contragredient of the Verma filtration and in the computation of local cohomology. Together,$P_{y,w}$ and$Q_{y,w}$ determine the full structure of the standard modules in the principal block of Category$\mathcal{O}$.
\end{remark}
