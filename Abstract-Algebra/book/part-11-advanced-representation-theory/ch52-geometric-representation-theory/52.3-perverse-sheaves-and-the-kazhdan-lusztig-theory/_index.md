# 52.3 Perverse Sheaves and the Kazhdan–Lusztig Theory

Perverse sheaves are the correct notion of "sheaf" for singular spaces. On a smooth variety, a local system (a locally constant sheaf of vector spaces) behaves like a single coherent object and satisfies Poincaré duality. On a singular variety, the ordinary derived sheaf formalism breaks down: Poincaré duality fails, and the "right" objects are not the individual cohomology sheaves but certain complexes of sheaves occupying multiple cohomological degrees simultaneously. The theory of perverse sheaves, developed by Beilinson, Bernstein, and Deligne in the foundational 1982 paper *Faisceaux Pervers*, axiomatizes these objects via a pair of t-structure conditions on the derived category $D^b_c(X)$: a complex $\mathcal{F}^\bullet$ is perverse if $H^k(i_S^* \mathcal{F}^\bullet) = 0$ for $k > -\dim S$ and $H^k(i_S^! \mathcal{F}^\bullet) = 0$ for $k < -\dim S$, for every stratum $S$ in a stratification of $X$. The perverse sheaves form the heart of the perverse t-structure, an abelian category inside $D^b_c(X)$.

The most important perverse sheaf on a Schubert variety $X_w \subset G/B$ is the intersection cohomology sheaf $IC(X_w)$. This is the unique perverse sheaf on $X_w$ that restricts to the constant sheaf on the smooth locus $C_w$ and satisfies the normalization conditions of the BBD decomposition theorem. The decomposition theorem — one of the deepest results in algebraic geometry — states that for a proper morphism $f: X \to Y$, the derived pushforward $Rf_* IC(X)$ splits as a direct sum of intersection cohomology sheaves (shifted) on subvarieties of $Y$. Applied to Schubert varieties, this theorem implies that the stalks $H^k(IC(X_w))_x$ at a point $x \in C_y \subset X_w$ are computable from the Betti numbers of the fibers of a suitable resolution of singularities of $X_w$. The Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials $P_{y,w}(q)$ are precisely the Poincaré polynomials of these stalks: $P_{y,w}(q) = \sum_k \dim H^{2k}(IC(X_w))_x \cdot q^k$, where $x$ is any point of $C_y$.

The Kazhdan–Lusztig conjecture, formulated in 1979 entirely in terms of the combinatorics of the Hecke algebra, states that the multiplicity $[M(w \cdot \lambda) : L(y \cdot \lambda)]$ of the simple module $L(y \cdot \lambda)$ in the Verma module $M(w \cdot \lambda)$ equals $P_{y,w}(1)$. The conjecture was proved in 1981, independently by Brylinski–Kashiwara (using $\mathcal{D}$-modules on the flag variety) and by Beilinson–Bernstein (using their localization theorem together with the geometry of $IC$ sheaves). The proof establishes that under the Beilinson–Bernstein equivalence, the multiplicity $[M(w \cdot \lambda) : L(y \cdot \lambda)]$ equals the rank of the stalk of $IC(X_w)$ at a point of $C_y$, which by definition is $P_{y,w}(1)$. The theorem is a triumph of the geometric method: an algebraic multiplicity, defined without any reference to geometry, is computed by the topology of a singular algebraic variety.

The Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials themselves encode a wealth of information beyond the bare multiplicities. They are palindromic, with non-negative integer coefficients, and satisfy the purity theorem: their coefficients count the dimensions of pure-weight pieces of the stalk cohomology. For the flag variety, the purity follows from Deligne's proof of the Weil conjectures (the stalks are pure in the appropriate sense). The Hecke algebra of $W$ acts on the space spanned by the $IC$ sheaves through the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis, which has non-negative structure constants — a fact that is trivial geometrically (it counts intersection multiplicities) but is difficult to prove combinatorially and has deep consequences for the representation theory of the Hecke algebra and its specializations.
