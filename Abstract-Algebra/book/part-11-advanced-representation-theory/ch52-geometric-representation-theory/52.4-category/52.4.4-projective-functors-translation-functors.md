# 52.4.4 Projective Functors; Translation Functors

One of the most useful structural features of Category $\mathcal{O}$ is the existence of functors that move modules between different blocks while preserving the essential structure. The *translation functors*, introduced by Jantzen and Bernstein–Gelfand–Gelfand, are the key tool for this: they allow one to transport information from one block to another, reducing many proofs to the case of a single representative block.

\begin{definition}
For weights $\lambda, \mu \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ lying in the same Weyl group orbit (so that$\mu \in W \cdot \lambda$ in the dot-action), suppose$\mu - \lambda$ is in the weight lattice and equals the highest weight of a finite-dimensional$\mathfrak{g}$-representation$F$. The *translation functor*$T_\lambda^\mu: \mathcal{O}_\lambda \to \mathcal{O}_\mu$ is defined by
$$T_\lambda^\mu(M) = \mathrm{pr}_\mu(M \otimes F),$$
where $\mathrm{pr}_\mu$ denotes the projection onto the block$\mathcal{O}_\mu$ (a direct summand of$\mathcal{O}$).
\end{definition}

Tensoring with the finite-dimensional module $F$ is an exact functor (since$F$ is projective as a vector space), and projection to a block is exact (since blocks are direct summands). Hence translation functors are exact.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ and$\lambda = 0$,$\mu = 2\omega$ (the weight of the two-dimensional representation$F = L(\omega)$... wait, we want$\mu - \lambda = 2\omega - 0 = 2\omega$... let us use$F = L(2\omega)$ the 3-dimensional representation). The translation functor$T_0^{2\omega}$ sends$M(\lambda') \mapsto \mathrm{pr}_{2\omega}(M(\lambda') \otimes L(2\omega))$. For$\lambda' = 0$:$M(0) \otimes L(2\omega)$ has components in blocks for weights$0 + 0, 0 + 2, 0 - 2$ in the dot action: the relevant projection gives a module in the block$\mathcal{O}_{2\omega}$ with composition factors from Verma modules$M(2)$,$M(-4)$.

**Example 2.** For generic (regular) weights, translation functors are equivalences. Specifically, if $\lambda$ and$\mu$ are both regular dominant (not on any wall), then$T_\lambda^\mu: \mathcal{O}_\lambda \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}_\mu$ is an equivalence of categories with inverse$T_\mu^\lambda$. This means all regular blocks of$\mathcal{O}$ are isomorphic as abelian categories — the homological structure is identical, only the labels change.

The key structural theorem on translation functors in the regular case:

\begin{theorem}[Jantzen]
If both $\lambda$ and$\mu$ are regular (not on any wall), the translation functors$T_\lambda^\mu$ and$T_\mu^\lambda$ are mutually quasi-inverse equivalences. Under this equivalence:
\begin{itemize}
\item Verma modules: $T_\lambda^\mu(M(w \cdot \lambda)) \cong M(w \cdot \mu)$.
\item Simple modules: $T_\lambda^\mu(L(w \cdot \lambda)) \cong L(w \cdot \mu)$.
\item Projectives: $T_\lambda^\mu(P(w \cdot \lambda)) \cong P(w \cdot \mu)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_3$, any two regular blocks$\mathcal{O}_\lambda$ and$\mathcal{O}_\mu$ (with$\lambda$,$\mu$ regular) are equivalent via translation. In particular, the "principal block"$\mathcal{O}_0$ (containing the trivial representation$L(0)$) is equivalent to any other regular block. This means the Ext algebra of$\mathcal{O}_0$ (the algebra$\mathrm{Ext}^\bullet(\bigoplus_w L(w \cdot 0), \bigoplus_w L(w \cdot 0))$) computes the Ext algebra for every regular block. Since the KL polynomials for$S_3$ are all 1, the principal block of$\mathfrak{sl}_3$ is Koszul self-dual with a very specific structure.

The situation at singular weights (weights on walls of the Weyl chamber) is more subtle.

\begin{theorem}[Wall-Crossing, Jantzen]
If $\lambda$ is regular and$\mu$ is singular (lies on a wall of the Weyl chamber), the translation functor$T_\lambda^\mu: \mathcal{O}_\lambda \to \mathcal{O}_\mu$ is not an equivalence but a quotient functor: it sends$M(w \cdot \lambda) \mapsto M(w \cdot \mu)$ (where$w \cdot \mu$ may equal$w' \cdot \mu$ for different$w, w'$ in the stabilizer of$\mu$), and it kills the modules whose highest weight collapses to the same value under the singularity.
\end{theorem}

**Example 4.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ and$\mu = 0$ (on the wall between the two chambers), the block$\mathcal{O}_0$ consists of modules on which the Casimir acts by 0. In the dot-action,$\mu = 0$ satisfies$W \cdot 0 = \{0\}$ (a single element, since$s \cdot 0 = s(0 + \omega) - \omega = \omega - \omega = 0$). So the block$\mathcal{O}_0$ contains a single Verma module$M(0) = M(-\rho) = M(-1\cdot\omega)$... actually for$\mathfrak{sl}_2$,$0 \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ and$W \cdot 0 = \{-\rho\}$ only if$\rho = \omega$. The singular block for$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ has only one Verma module, so the translation from a regular to a singular weight collapses two Vermas to one.

The more general class of *projective functors* extends translation functors.

\begin{definition}
A *projective functor* $\theta: \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}$ is a functor that is isomorphic to a direct summand of a functor of the form$M \mapsto \mathrm{pr}_\chi(M \otimes V)$ for some finite-dimensional$G$-module$V$ and central character$\chi$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bernstein–Gelfand]
Every projective functor on $\mathcal{O}$ is isomorphic to a direct summand of a translation functor. The endomorphism ring of the direct sum of all projective functors is isomorphic to the Hecke algebra of$W$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Translation functors have a beautiful geometric interpretation under the Beilinson–Bernstein equivalence: they correspond to the *Hecke eigensheaf operators* on $\mathcal{D}_\lambda$-modules, which are defined by pulling back along the Hecke correspondences on$G/B \times G/B$. The wall-crossing behavior of translation functors corresponds geometrically to the degeneration of Hecke correspondences as the parameter$\lambda$ moves to a wall. This geometric perspective is the foundation for Soergel's theory of bimodules and for the categorical Hecke algebra action that underlies modern categorical representation theory.
\end{remark}
