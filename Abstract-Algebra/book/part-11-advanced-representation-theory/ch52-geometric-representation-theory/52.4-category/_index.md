# 52.4 Category $\mathcal{O}$

Category $\mathcal{O}$ is the natural abelian category in which the rich interaction between Verma modules, simple modules, and projective modules in the representation theory of a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ can be made fully precise. Introduced by Bernstein, Gelfand, and Gelfand in the mid-1970s, Category $\mathcal{O}$ is the full subcategory of $U(\mathfrak{g})$-modules consisting of those modules $M$ that are finitely generated over $U(\mathfrak{g})$, semisimple as $\mathfrak{h}$-modules (where $\mathfrak{h}$ is a fixed Cartan subalgebra), and locally finite for the action of the positive nilpotent part $\mathfrak{n}_+ \subset \mathfrak{b} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ (meaning every vector generates a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{n}_+$-stable subspace). This definition threads the needle between including the infinite-dimensional Verma modules (which are semisimple over $\mathfrak{h}$ but infinite-dimensional) while excluding pathological modules without enough structure to work with.

The fundamental objects in Category $\mathcal{O}$ are the Verma modules $M(\lambda) = U(\mathfrak{g}) \otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})} \mathbb{C}_\lambda$, one for each weight $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, where $\mathbb{C}_\lambda$ is the one-dimensional $\mathfrak{b}$-module on which $\mathfrak{h}$ acts by $\lambda$ and $\mathfrak{n}_+$ acts by zero. Verma modules play the role of standard objects: each Verma module $M(\lambda)$ has a unique simple quotient $L(\lambda)$, and every simple object in Category $\mathcal{O}$ is of the form $L(\lambda)$ for some $\lambda$. The set of weights $\mu$ for which $L(\mu)$ appears as a composition factor of $M(\lambda)$ is precisely the set of weights in the $W$-orbit of $\lambda$ under the dot action $w \cdot \lambda = w(\lambda + \rho) - \rho$ that are $\leq \lambda$ in the dominance order, and the multiplicities are the Kazhdan–Lusztig numbers $[M(\lambda) : L(\mu)] = P_{\mu,\lambda}(1)$.

Category $\mathcal{O}$ decomposes into blocks indexed by $W$-orbits under the dot action: two weights $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are in the same block if and only if $\mu = w \cdot \lambda$ for some $w \in W$. Within each block, the projective objects $P(\lambda)$ (the projective covers of $L(\lambda)$) are characterized by the BGG reciprocity: the Verma multiplicity $(P(\lambda) : M(\mu))$ — the number of times $M(\mu)$ appears in a Verma flag of $P(\lambda)$ — equals the composition multiplicity $[M(\mu) : L(\lambda)]$. This reciprocity, which is a formal consequence of the highest weight category axioms satisfied by Category $\mathcal{O}$, converts knowledge of the decomposition matrix (Kazhdan–Lusztig numbers) into knowledge of the projective modules, and vice versa. The projective functors — exact endofunctors of Category $\mathcal{O}$ given by tensoring with finite-dimensional modules and projecting onto a block — provide a powerful tool for moving between blocks and for constructing the projective objects explicitly.

The BGG resolution of the trivial module (and more generally of any finite-dimensional simple module $L(\lambda)$) is one of the most beautiful formulas in the theory: there is an exact complex $\cdots \to \bigoplus_{w: \ell(w) = k} M(w \cdot \lambda) \to \cdots \to M(\lambda) \to L(\lambda) \to 0$, where the maps are given by the Lie algebra homomorphisms between Verma modules. This resolution has length $N = \dim \mathfrak{n}_+$ (the number of positive roots), it resolves the simple module $L(\lambda)$ by Verma modules, and it can be used to compute the cohomology of $\mathfrak{g}$ with coefficients in $L(\lambda)$ — recovering the classical Bott–Borel–Weil theorem. Category $\mathcal{O}$ thus serves as the algebraic home for a suite of geometric results, providing the categorical language in which the geometry of the flag variety is reflected in the structure of Lie algebra representations.
