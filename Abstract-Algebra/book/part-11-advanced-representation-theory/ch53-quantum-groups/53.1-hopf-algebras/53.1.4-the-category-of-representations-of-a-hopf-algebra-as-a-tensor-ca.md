# 53.1.4 The Category of Representations of a Hopf Algebra as a Tensor Category

The category of finite-dimensional representations of a Hopf algebra $H$ has a richer structure than that of a mere abelian category. It is a *tensor category* (also called a *monoidal category*): it is equipped with a tensor product bifunctor$\otimes: \mathrm{Rep}(H) \times \mathrm{Rep}(H) \to \mathrm{Rep}(H)$, a unit object$\mathbf{1} = k$ (on which$H$ acts via the counit:$h \cdot 1 = \varepsilon(h)$), and natural associativity and unit isomorphisms satisfying the MacLane pentagon and triangle coherence axioms. The antipode makes this tensor category *rigid*: every object has a dual.

\begin{definition}
A *monoidal category* (tensor category) $(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, \mathbf{1}, \alpha, \lambda, \rho)$ consists of a category$\mathcal{C}$, a bifunctor$\otimes: \mathcal{C} \times \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}$, a unit object$\mathbf{1}$, and natural isomorphisms: associativity$\alpha_{X,Y,Z}: (X \otimes Y) \otimes Z \xrightarrow{\sim} X \otimes (Y \otimes Z)$, and unit isomorphisms$\lambda_X: \mathbf{1} \otimes X \xrightarrow{\sim} X$,$\rho_X: X \otimes \mathbf{1} \xrightarrow{\sim} X$, satisfying the pentagon and triangle axioms.
\end{definition}

For $\mathrm{Rep}(H)$, the tensor product$V \otimes W$ has$H$-action via$\Delta$:$h \cdot (v \otimes w) = \sum h_{(1)} v \otimes h_{(2)} w$. Coassociativity of$\Delta$ ensures that$(V \otimes W) \otimes X \cong V \otimes (W \otimes X)$ as$H$-modules (with the standard vector space associativity map being$H$-equivariant).

**Example 1.** For $H = k[G]$,$\mathrm{Rep}(k[G])$ is the ordinary category of$G$-representations with tensor product over$k$. The unit object is the trivial representation$k$ (with$g \cdot 1 = 1$ for all$g \in G$). Associativity and unit isomorphisms are the standard vector space ones. This is a symmetric monoidal category (cocommutativity of$k[G]$ gives the commutativity constraint$c_{V,W}: V \otimes W \to W \otimes V$,$v \otimes w \mapsto w \otimes v$).

**Example 2.** For $H = U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$,$\mathrm{Rep}(U(\mathfrak{sl}_2))$ (finite-dimensional) is the category of$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-modules. Tensor product:$(x \cdot v) \otimes w + v \otimes (x \cdot w)$ for$x \in \mathfrak{sl}_2$. The decomposition$V_m \otimes V_n \cong \bigoplus_{k=|m-n|}^{m+n} V_k$ (Clebsch–Gordan) holds. This is also symmetric:$V \otimes W \cong W \otimes V$ via$v \otimes w \mapsto w \otimes v$ (an$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module isomorphism by the cocommutativity of$U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$).

The commutativity properties of the tensor category reflect the cocommutativity properties of the Hopf algebra.

\begin{definition}
A monoidal category $\mathcal{C}$ is *symmetric* if there is a natural isomorphism$c_{V,W}: V \otimes W \xrightarrow{\sim} W \otimes V$ satisfying$c_{W,V} \circ c_{V,W} = \mathrm{id}_{V \otimes W}$ and the hexagon axioms. It is *braided* if there is such a natural isomorphism$c_{V,W}$ (called a *braiding*) satisfying only the hexagon axioms, but not necessarily$c_{W,V} \circ c_{V,W} = \mathrm{id}$.
\end{definition}

**Example 3.** For $H = U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at generic$q$, the braiding on$V_1 \otimes V_1$ (the tensor square of the 2-dimensional representation) is given by the$R$-matrix:
$$c_{V_1, V_1}(v_+ \otimes v_-) = v_- \otimes v_+ + (q - q^{-1}) v_+ \otimes v_-, \quad c_{V_1,V_1}(v_+ \otimes v_+) = q \cdot v_+ \otimes v_+.$$
The composition $c_{V_1,V_1} \circ c_{V_1,V_1}(v_+ \otimes v_-) = c_{V_1,V_1}(v_- \otimes v_+ + (q-q^{-1})v_+ \otimes v_-)$; working this out shows it is NOT equal to$v_+ \otimes v_-$ (for$q \neq \pm 1$), confirming the braiding is not symmetric.

For quantum groups, the almost-cocommutativity provided by the universal $R$-matrix upgrades the tensor category to a braided tensor category. The braiding$c_{V,W}$ is given explicitly by the action of the$R$-matrix:$c_{V,W}(v \otimes w) = \tau(R \cdot (v \otimes w))$, where$R \in U_q(\mathfrak{g}) \otimes U_q(\mathfrak{g})$.

\begin{theorem}
For any quasitriangular Hopf algebra $H$ with universal$R$-matrix$R$, the category$\mathrm{Rep}(H)$ is a braided monoidal category. The failure of$c_{W,V} \circ c_{V,W}$ to be the identity is controlled by the element$R^{21} R = (R_{21})^{-1} R$ in$H \otimes H$ (where$R_{21} = \tau(R)$).
\end{theorem}

**Example 4.** The *Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups* $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ at generic$q$ and the *Reshetikhin–Turaev tangles.* The braid group$B_n$ acts on$V^{\otimes n}$ for any$V \in \mathrm{Rep}(U_q(\mathfrak{g}))$ via the braiding: the generator$\sigma_i$ acts by$\mathrm{id}^{\otimes i-1} \otimes c_{V,V} \otimes \mathrm{id}^{\otimes n-i-1}$. For$V = V_1$ (the two-dimensional representation of$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$), the resulting representation of$B_n$ on$V_1^{\otimes n}$ is the *standard Burau representation*, and the associated knot invariant (via the Markov trace) is the Jones polynomial.

\begin{remark}
The passage from bialgebras to Hopf algebras (adding the antipode) is analogous to the passage from monoids to groups (adding inverses). Just as groups are the "right" objects for classical representation theory (because one needs inverses to form dual representations), Hopf algebras are the right objects for quantum representation theory. The hierarchy bialgebra $\subset$ Hopf algebra$\subset$ quasitriangular Hopf algebra$\subset$ ribbon Hopf algebra corresponds to the hierarchy monoidal category$\subset$ rigid monoidal category$\subset$ braided monoidal category$\subset$ ribbon category, with each level adding exactly the structure needed for the next layer of topological applications.
\end{remark}
