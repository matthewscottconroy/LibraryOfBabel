# 53.2.1 $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$: The$q$-Deformed$\mathfrak{sl}_2$

The simplest and most instructive quantum group is the $q$-deformation of$\mathfrak{sl}_2$. The Lie algebra$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is generated by three elements$E, F, H$ satisfying$[H, E] = 2E$,$[H, F] = -2F$, and$[E, F] = H$. The quantum group$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ deforms these relations by replacing the Cartan generator$H$ by a "quantum exponential"$K = q^H$ (for$q \in \mathbb{C}^*$ not a root of unity), which gives a formulation that degenerates gracefully to the classical algebra as$q \to 1$.

\begin{definition}
The quantum group $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is the$\mathbb{Q}(q)$-algebra generated by$E, F, K, K^{-1}$ with relations:
\begin{align*}
KK^{-1} &= K^{-1}K = 1, \\
KEK^{-1} &= q^2 E, \\
KFK^{-1} &= q^{-2} F, \\
EF - FE &= \frac{K - K^{-1}}{q - q^{-1}}.
\end{align*}
The Hopf algebra structure is given by:
\begin{align*}
\Delta(E) &= E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E, & S(E) &= -EK^{-1}, & \varepsilon(E) &= 0, \\
\Delta(F) &= F \otimes 1 + K^{-1} \otimes F, & S(F) &= -KF, & \varepsilon(F) &= 0, \\
\Delta(K) &= K \otimes K, & S(K) &= K^{-1}, & \varepsilon(K) &= 1.
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

The last relation recovers $[E, F] = H$ as$q \to 1$: since$K = q^H$, we have$\frac{K - K^{-1}}{q - q^{-1}} = \frac{q^H - q^{-H}}{q - q^{-1}} \to H$ as$q \to 1$.

**Example 1.** Verify the Hopf algebra axiom $(\Delta \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta) \circ \Delta$ on$E$:
\begin{align*}
(\Delta \otimes \mathrm{id})(\Delta(E)) &= (\Delta \otimes \mathrm{id})(E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E) \\
&= \Delta(E) \otimes K + \Delta(1) \otimes E \\
&= (E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E) \otimes K + 1 \otimes 1 \otimes E \\
&= E \otimes K \otimes K + 1 \otimes E \otimes K + 1 \otimes 1 \otimes E.
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
(\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta)(\Delta(E)) &= (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta)(E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E) \\
&= E \otimes \Delta(K) + 1 \otimes \Delta(E) \\
&= E \otimes K \otimes K + 1 \otimes (E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E) \\
&= E \otimes K \otimes K + 1 \otimes E \otimes K + 1 \otimes 1 \otimes E. \checkmark
\end{align*}

**Example 2.** The irreducible representations of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at generic$q$ (not a root of unity) are indexed by non-negative integers$n$. The$n$-th irreducible$V_n$ has basis$\{v_n, v_{n-2}, \ldots, v_{-n}\}$ (indexed by weight) with:
\begin{align*}
K \cdot v_m &= q^m v_m, \\
E \cdot v_m &= [(\tfrac{n-m}{2}+1)(\tfrac{n+m}{2})]_q \cdot v_{m+2}, \quad \text{using } [k]_q = \frac{q^k - q^{-k}}{q - q^{-1}}, \\
F \cdot v_m &= v_{m-2}.
\end{align*}
(where $v_m = 0$ for$|m| > n$). As$q \to 1$,$q^m \to 1$ (so$K \to 1$),$[k]_q \to k$, and the action matches the standard representation of$\mathfrak{sl}_2$.

**Example 3.** Compute the tensor product $V_1 \otimes V_1$ (two copies of the 2-dimensional representation). Bases:$V_1 = \mathrm{span}\{v_1, v_{-1}\}$. The tensor product$V_1 \otimes V_1$ has basis$\{v_1 \otimes v_1, v_1 \otimes v_{-1}, v_{-1} \otimes v_1, v_{-1} \otimes v_{-1}\}$. Using$\Delta(E) = E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E$:
$$E \cdot (v_{-1} \otimes v_1) = (E \cdot v_{-1}) \otimes (K \cdot v_1) + v_{-1} \otimes (E \cdot v_1) = v_1 \otimes q v_1 + 0 = q(v_1 \otimes v_1).$$
$$E \cdot (v_1 \otimes v_{-1}) = (E \cdot v_1) \otimes (K \cdot v_{-1}) + v_1 \otimes (E \cdot v_{-1}) = 0 + v_1 \otimes v_1 = v_1 \otimes v_1.$$
The highest weight vector in $V_1 \otimes V_1$ annihilated by$E$ is$(q^{-1} v_1 \otimes v_{-1} - v_{-1} \otimes v_1)$... checking: the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations split into$V_2$ and$V_0$ as in the classical case, with the same Clebsch–Gordan decomposition$V_1 \otimes V_1 \cong V_2 \oplus V_0$.

The $q$-integers$[n]_q = \frac{q^n - q^{-n}}{q - q^{-1}}$ play the role of ordinary integers throughout the theory. They satisfy$[0]_q = 0$,$[1]_q = 1$,$[n]_q \to n$ as$q \to 1$, and crucially,$[n]_q = 0$ when$q$ is a primitive$n$-th root of unity. This vanishing is what causes the representation theory to degenerate dramatically at roots of unity.

**Example 4.** The $q$-factorial$[n]_q! = [1]_q [2]_q \cdots [n]_q$ and the$q$-binomial coefficient$\binom{n}{k}_q = \frac{[n]_q!}{[k]_q! [n-k]_q!}$ are both polynomials in$q$ with non-negative integer coefficients (the Gaussian binomial coefficients). They count$\mathbb{F}_q$-rational points of flag varieties:$\binom{n}{k}_q = |\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)(\mathbb{F}_q)|$ is the number of$k$-planes in$\mathbb{F}_q^n$.

\begin{remark}
The non-cocommutative Hopf algebra structure of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ — specifically the asymmetry$\Delta(E) = E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E$ (with$K$ on the right) vs.$\Delta(F) = F \otimes 1 + K^{-1} \otimes F$ (with$K^{-1}$ on the left) — is what gives the braiding its non-trivial form. The$R$-matrix of$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is the "quantum exponential"$q^{H \otimes H / 2} \exp_q(...)$, and it gives the Jones polynomial when one takes the Markov trace of the braid group action on$V_1^{\otimes n}$. This connection — from the simple algebra of$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ to one of the deepest knot invariants — is one of the miracles of quantum group theory.
\end{remark}
