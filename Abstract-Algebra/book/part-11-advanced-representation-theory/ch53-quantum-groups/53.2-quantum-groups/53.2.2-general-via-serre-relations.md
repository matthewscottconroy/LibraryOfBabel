# 53.2.2 General $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ via$q$-Serre Relations

The construction of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ generalizes to any semisimple Lie algebra$\mathfrak{g}$ via a deformation of the Serre presentation. Recall that$\mathfrak{g}$ is generated by Chevalley generators$e_i, f_i, h_i$ for$i = 1, \ldots, r$ (where$r$ is the rank), subject to the Serre relations — quadratic and higher-degree relations encoding the Cartan matrix$(a_{ij})$. The quantum group$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ replaces each Cartan generator$h_i$ by a quantum exponential$K_i$ and deforms the Serre relations into$q$-analogues.

\begin{definition}
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan matrix$(a_{ij})$ and symmetrizing integers$d_i$ (so that$d_i a_{ij} = d_j a_{ji}$). Set$q_i = q^{d_i}$ and$[n]_i = \frac{q_i^n - q_i^{-n}}{q_i - q_i^{-1}}$. The *quantum group*$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ is the$\mathbb{Q}(q)$-algebra generated by$\{E_i, F_i, K_i^{\pm 1}\}_{i=1}^r$ with relations:
\begin{itemize}
\item $K_i K_j = K_j K_i$,$K_i K_i^{-1} = 1$.
\item $K_i E_j K_i^{-1} = q_i^{a_{ij}} E_j$,$\quad K_i F_j K_i^{-1} = q_i^{-a_{ij}} F_j$.
\item $E_i F_j - F_j E_i = \delta_{ij} \frac{K_i - K_i^{-1}}{q_i - q_i^{-1}}$.
\item $q$-Serre relations:$\sum_{k=0}^{1-a_{ij}} (-1)^k \binom{1-a_{ij}}{k}_{q_i} E_i^{1-a_{ij}-k} E_j E_i^k = 0$ for$i \neq j$ (and analogously with$F$'s).
\end{itemize}
The Hopf algebra structure generalizes that of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$:$\Delta(E_i) = E_i \otimes K_i + 1 \otimes E_i$,$\Delta(K_i) = K_i \otimes K_i$, etc.
\end{definition}

At $q = 1$, the$q$-Serre relations reduce to the classical Serre relations: the$q$-binomial$\binom{n}{k}_q \to \binom{n}{k}$, and the Serre relation becomes$\sum_{k=0}^{1-a_{ij}} (-1)^k \binom{1-a_{ij}}{k} e_i^{1-a_{ij}-k} e_j e_i^k = (\mathrm{ad}\, e_i)^{1-a_{ij}}(e_j) = 0$, the classical Serre relation.

**Example 1.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_3$ (type$A_2$), the Cartan matrix is$a_{ij} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and all$d_i = 1$, so$q_i = q$ for all$i$. The$q$-Serre relations are:
$$E_1^2 E_2 - (q + q^{-1}) E_1 E_2 E_1 + E_2 E_1^2 = 0, \quad E_2^2 E_1 - (q+q^{-1}) E_2 E_1 E_2 + E_1 E_2^2 = 0$$
(using $\binom{2}{1}_q = q + q^{-1} = [2]_q$). As$q \to 1$:$E_1^2 E_2 - 2E_1 E_2 E_1 + E_2 E_1^2 = [e_1, [e_1, e_2]] = 0$, the classical Serre relation.$\checkmark$

**Example 2.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sp}_4$ (type$C_2$), the Cartan matrix is$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ with$d_1 = 1, d_2 = 2$, so$q_1 = q$ and$q_2 = q^2$. The Serre relation for$i = 2, j = 1$ ($a_{21} = -2$) is:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{3} (-1)^k \binom{3}{k}_{q^2} E_2^{3-k} E_1 E_2^k = 0$$
using the $q^2$-binomials:$\binom{3}{1}_{q^2} = 1 + q^2 + q^4$, etc. This is a degree-4 relation in$E_2$ and$E_1$.

The PBW theorem holds for $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$: it has a triangular decomposition$U_q(\mathfrak{g}) = U_q^- \otimes U_q^0 \otimes U_q^+$, where$U_q^0 = \mathbb{Q}(q)[K_1^{\pm 1}, \ldots, K_r^{\pm 1}]$ is the quantum torus,$U_q^+$ is generated by the$E_i$'s, and$U_q^-$ is generated by the$F_i$'s.

\begin{theorem}[PBW Basis for $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$]
The algebra $U_q^+$ has a$\mathbb{Q}(q)$-basis given by monomials$E_{\beta_1}^{m_1} \cdots E_{\beta_N}^{m_N}$ (with$m_i \geq 0$), where$\beta_1 < \cdots < \beta_N$ are the positive roots in some fixed convex ordering, and$E_{\beta}$ for non-simple$\beta$ are the *quantum root vectors* constructed by Lusztig's braid group action. Similarly for$U_q^-$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** For $\mathfrak{sl}_3$, the positive roots are$\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_1 + \alpha_2$. The quantum root vector for$\alpha_1 + \alpha_2$ is$E_{\alpha_1 + \alpha_2} = E_1 E_2 - q^{-1} E_2 E_1$ (Lusztig's definition; different conventions exist). As$q \to 1$, this becomes$E_1 E_2 - E_2 E_1 = [E_1, E_2] = e_{\alpha_1 + \alpha_2}$, the classical root vector. The PBW basis of$U_q^+(\mathfrak{sl}_3)$ is$\{E_1^a E_{\alpha_1+\alpha_2}^b E_2^c : a, b, c \geq 0\}$.

\begin{proposition}
The specialization map $q \mapsto 1$ gives a surjective Hopf algebra map$U_q(\mathfrak{g})|_{q=1} \to U(\mathfrak{g})$ (after passing to the$\mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$-form of$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ and specializing to$q = 1$). The kernel consists of the$q$-deformation relations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The integer form $U_{\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]}(\mathfrak{g})$, using divided powers$E_i^{(n)} = E_i^n / [n]_i!$ (Lusztig's integral form), specializes well to any value of$q$. At$q = 1$, it gives Kostant's$\mathbb{Z}$-form of$U(\mathfrak{g})$. At$q = 0$, it gives the crystal basis structure. At$q = p$ (a prime), it gives the Frobenius kernel in characteristic$p$. And at$q =$ root of unity, the specialization has a rich non-semisimple structure (Section 53.2.4). This single family$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ thus interpolates between classical representation theory ($q=1$), combinatorial representation theory ($q=0$), modular representation theory ($q = p$), and conformal field theory ($q =$ root of unity) in a single algebraic object.
\end{remark}
