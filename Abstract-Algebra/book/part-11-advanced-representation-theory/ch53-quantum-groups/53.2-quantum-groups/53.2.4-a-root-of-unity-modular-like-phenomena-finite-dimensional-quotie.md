# 53.2.4 $q$ a Root of Unity: Modular-Like Phenomena; Finite-Dimensional Quotient

When $q = e^{2\pi i/\ell}$ is a primitive$\ell$-th root of unity, the representation theory of$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ undergoes a dramatic transformation. The$q$-integers$[n]_q = \frac{q^n - q^{-n}}{q - q^{-1}}$ vanish whenever$\ell \mid n$, and this vanishing causes Verma modules to become reducible in new and unexpected ways — exactly analogous to the phenomenon in characteristic$p$ representation theory where$p$-th divided powers of root vectors become central. The entire character of the theory changes, and one encounters a world of tilting modules, quantum Frobenius morphisms, and fusion categories that is richer and more intricate than the generic case.

**Example 1.** For $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at$q = e^{2\pi i/5}$ (a primitive 5th root of unity),$[5]_q = 0$. In the Verma module$M_q(\lambda)$ for weight$\lambda$, the coefficient of$E \cdot v$ in the relation$E F^n v = [n]_q [\lambda - n + 1]_q F^{n-1} v$ (for a highest weight vector$v$) vanishes when$n = 5$ (since$[5]_q = 0$). This means$F^5 v$ generates a submodule of$M_q(\lambda)$ for any$\lambda$, making the Verma module reducible in a way that has no classical analogue.

The analogy with modular representation theory is precise. In characteristic $p$, the group algebra$kG$ for a finite group$G$ over$\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is non-semisimple, and the correct building blocks are tilting modules and projective indecomposables. At$q = \ell$-th root of unity, the quantum group$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ similarly has a finite-dimensional quotient — the *small quantum group* — whose representation theory is non-semisimple.

\begin{definition}
The *small quantum group* (or *restricted quantum group*) $u_q(\mathfrak{g})$ at$q$ a primitive$\ell$-th root of unity is the finite-dimensional quotient of$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ by the ideal generated by$E_i^\ell$,$F_i^\ell$,$K_i^\ell - 1$ for all$i$. It has dimension$\ell^{\dim \mathfrak{g}}$.
\end{definition}

**Example 2.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ and$q = e^{2\pi i / 3}$ (a primitive cube root of unity,$\ell = 3$), the small quantum group$u_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is defined by$E^3 = F^3 = 0$ and$K^3 = 1$. It has dimension$3^3 = 27$. This algebra has 3 simple modules$L_q(0), L_q(1), L_q(2)$ (indexed by the restricted weights$\{0, 1, 2\} = \{0, 1, \ldots, \ell - 1\}$), just as$u(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ over$\overline{\mathbb{F}}_3$ has 3 restricted irreducibles$L(0), L(1), L(2)$.

\begin{definition}
A *tilting module* for $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ (at a root of unity) is a$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$-module that admits both a filtration by Weyl modules (quantum Verma modules evaluated at$q$) and a filtration by dual Weyl modules. Tilting modules are the correct analogue of projective-injective modules in the root-of-unity setting.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Quantum Frobenius, Lusztig]
There is a *quantum Frobenius homomorphism* $\mathrm{Fr}: U_q(\mathfrak{g}) \to U_1(\mathfrak{g}) \cong U(\mathfrak{g})$ (at$q = e^{2\pi i/\ell}$) sending$E_i^{(\ell)} \mapsto e_i$ (the$\ell$-th divided power to the classical generator),$F_i^{(\ell)} \mapsto f_i$, and$K_i \mapsto 1$. This homomorphism relates the quantum and classical worlds.
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** For $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at$q = i$ (so$\ell = 4$), the quantum Frobenius$\mathrm{Fr}: U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2) \to U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ sends$E^{(4)} = E^4/[4]_q! \mapsto e$,$F^{(4)} \mapsto f$,$K \mapsto 1$. Pulling back a classical representation$L(\lambda)$ along$\mathrm{Fr}$ gives a$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-representation$\mathrm{Fr}^*L(\lambda)$, the "Frobenius twist" of$L(\lambda)$. Every irreducible$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-module is a tensor product of a small quantum group irreducible (a "restricted" module, indexed by$0 \leq k < \ell$) and a Frobenius twist of a classical module — precisely the quantum analogue of Steinberg's tensor product theorem.

The most striking consequence at roots of unity is the existence of a *semisimple quotient category*.

\begin{theorem}[Verlinde Formula]
By passing to tilting modules and quotienting by negligible tilting modules (those of quantum dimension zero), one obtains a semisimple ribbon category whose simple objects are parameterized by the $\ell$-restricted dominant weights$\lambda$ (satisfying$\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle < \ell$ for all simple coroots), and whose tensor product rules are the *Verlinde fusion rules*:
$$[V_q(\lambda)] \otimes_{\mathrm{Ver}} [V_q(\mu)] = \sum_\nu N_{\lambda\mu}^\nu [V_q(\nu)],$$
where $N_{\lambda\mu}^\nu = \sum_w (-1)^{\ell(w)} c_{\lambda\mu}^{w \cdot \nu}$ (a$q$-analogue of the Littlewood–Richardson rule, corrected by the Weyl group).
\end{theorem}

**Example 4.** For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ and$\ell = 3$ ($q = e^{2\pi i/3}$), the$\ell$-restricted weights are$\{0, 1, 2\}$ (the dimensions of the simple modules being$1, 2, 3$). The Verlinde fusion rules are:
$$[V_q(1)] \otimes_{\mathrm{Ver}} [V_q(1)] = [V_q(0)] + [V_q(2)], \quad [V_q(1)] \otimes_{\mathrm{Ver}} [V_q(2)] = [V_q(1)], \quad [V_q(2)] \otimes_{\mathrm{Ver}} [V_q(2)] = [V_q(0)].$$
These are the fusion rules of the $A_1$ WZW model at level$\ell - h^\vee = 3 - 2 = 1$. The "truncation" compared to the classical Clebsch–Gordan — where$V_1 \otimes V_2 = V_1 \oplus V_3$ but$V_3$ is killed by the quotient — reflects the quantum group analogue of the Steinberg module being killed in the tilting quotient.

\begin{remark}
The connection between root-of-unity quantum groups, modular tensor categories, and conformal field theory (via the Verlinde formula and WZW models) is one of the deepest in mathematical physics. The "level $k$" of the WZW model corresponds to$\ell = k + h^\vee$ (where$h^\vee$ is the dual Coxeter number), and the Verlinde formula computes the dimensions of spaces of conformal blocks on Riemann surfaces. The entire construction yields$(2+1)$-dimensional topological quantum field theories via the Reshetikhin–Turaev construction, providing invariants of 3-manifolds (the Witten–Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants) from purely algebraic data.
\end{remark}
