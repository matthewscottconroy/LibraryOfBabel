# 53.2 Quantum Groups $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$

The quantum group $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ is a one-parameter family of Hopf algebras deforming the universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{g})$ of a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$. For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ with standard basis $\{e, f, h\}$, the quantum group $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is generated over $\mathbb{Q}(q)$ by elements $E, F, K, K^{-1}$ satisfying $KK^{-1} = K^{-1}K = 1$, $KE = q^2 EK$, $KF = q^{-2} FK$, and the $q$-Serre relation $[E, F] = \frac{K - K^{-1}}{q - q^{-1}}$. As $q \to 1$, with $K = q^H$, these relations specialize to $[H, E] = 2E$, $[H, F] = -2F$, $[E, F] = H$ — the standard relations of $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. The Hopf algebra structure is given by $\Delta(K) = K \otimes K$, $\Delta(E) = E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E$, $\Delta(F) = F \otimes 1 + K^{-1} \otimes F$, with counit $\varepsilon(K) = 1$, $\varepsilon(E) = \varepsilon(F) = 0$ and antipode $S(K) = K^{-1}$, $S(E) = -EK^{-1}$, $S(F) = -KF$. The non-cocommutativity of $\Delta$ — the fact that $\Delta(E) \neq \tau \circ \Delta(E)$ where $\tau$ swaps factors — is what distinguishes $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ from $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ and what produces a nontrivial $R$-matrix.

For a general semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ with Cartan matrix $(a_{ij})$ and simple roots $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r$, the quantum group $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ is generated by $E_i, F_i, K_i, K_i^{-1}$ for $i = 1, \ldots, r$ subject to the quantum analogues of the Chevalley–Serre relations: the commutation relations $K_i E_j = q^{a_{ij}} E_j K_i$ and $K_i F_j = q^{-a_{ij}} F_j K_i$, the $q$-Serre relations $[E_i, F_j] = \delta_{ij} \frac{K_i - K_i^{-1}}{q_i - q_i^{-1}}$ (where $q_i = q^{d_i}$ for symmetrizing integers $d_i$), and the quantum Serre relations $\sum_{k=0}^{1-a_{ij}} (-1)^k \binom{1-a_{ij}}{k}_{q_i} E_i^k E_j E_i^{1-a_{ij}-k} = 0$ (and the analogous ones for $F_i$), involving $q$-binomial coefficients $\binom{n}{k}_q = \frac{[n]!}{[k]![n-k]!}$. Lusztig proved that this algebra has a PBW basis analogous to the classical case, and Drinfeld proved that it carries a quasi-triangular structure.

At generic $q$ (not a root of unity), the representation theory of $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ exactly mirrors the classical theory. The simple modules $V(\lambda)$ are parametrized by dominant integral weights $\lambda$, their characters are given by the Weyl character formula, and the category $\mathrm{Rep}(U_q(\mathfrak{g}))$ is semisimple. The tensor product rules (Clebsch–Gordan coefficients) are the same as classically. At $q = \zeta$, a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, the story changes dramatically. The quantum integers $[n]_q$ satisfy $[\ell]_\zeta = 0$, causing the divided powers $E^{(\ell)} = E^\ell/[\ell]!$ and $F^{(\ell)} = F^\ell/[\ell]!$ to generate a new central element. The small quantum group $u_\zeta(\mathfrak{g})$ (the quotient of $U_\zeta(\mathfrak{g})$ by the ideal generated by $E_i^\ell, F_i^\ell, K_i^\ell - 1$) is a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra whose representation theory is non-semisimple and mirrors the modular representation theory of $G$ in characteristic $\ell$. Lusztig's conjecture — now a theorem for large primes, proved using the Kazhdan–Lusztig–Lusztig correspondence and geometric methods — identifies the simple modules of $u_\zeta(\mathfrak{g})$ with those of $G$ over $\mathbb{F}_\ell$ and computes their characters via Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for the affine Weyl group.
