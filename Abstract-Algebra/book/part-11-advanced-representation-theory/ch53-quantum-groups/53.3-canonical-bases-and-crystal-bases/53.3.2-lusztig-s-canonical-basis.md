# 53.3.2 Lusztig's Canonical Basis

Independently of Kashiwara's crystal basis (and arriving at essentially the same object by a different route), Lusztig constructed a distinguished basis for $U_q^-(\mathfrak{g})$ and for each irreducible representation$V(\lambda)$ called the *canonical basis* or *global crystal basis*. While Kashiwara's approach goes through the$q \to 0$ limit, Lusztig's is characterized by a self-duality property: the canonical basis is the unique basis fixed by a natural bar-involution and reducing modulo$q$ to the crystal basis. The two constructions agree, and the resulting basis has extraordinary positivity properties that neither construction makes transparent on its own.

\begin{definition}
The *bar-involution* on $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ is the$\mathbb{Q}$-algebra involution defined by$\bar{q} = q^{-1}$,$\bar{E}_i = E_i$,$\bar{F}_i = F_i$,$\bar{K}_i = K_i^{-1}$. For a$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$-module$V$, a *balanced triple*$(L, L', B)$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L$: a$\mathbb{Z}[q]$-lattice in$V$ stable under the crystal operators (a "crystal lattice").
\item $L'$: a$\mathbb{Z}[q^{-1}]$-lattice stable under the bar-involution.
\item $B$: a$\mathbb{Q}$-basis for$L \cap L' \cap \bar{L}$ (where$\bar{L}$ is the bar-involution of$L$).
\end{itemize}
The *canonical basis* is the unique balanced triple with $B$ in bijection with the crystal basis of$V$ modulo$q$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Kashiwara 1991; Lusztig 1990]
Every finite-dimensional irreducible $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$-module$V(\lambda)$ has a canonical basis$\mathbf{B}(\lambda)$. The canonical basis elements$b \in \mathbf{B}(\lambda)$ are uniquely characterized by:
\begin{itemize}
\item Bar-invariance: $\bar{b} = b$.
\item Integrality: $b \in L(\lambda) \cap L'(\lambda)$ (in both lattices).
\item Reduction: $b \equiv b_0 \pmod{qL(\lambda)}$ for a crystal basis element$b_0 \in B(\lambda)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** For $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ and$V(2)$ (the 3-dimensional representation), the crystal basis is$\{v_2, v_0, v_{-2}\}$ with$K$ acting by$q^2, 1, q^{-2}$ respectively. The canonical basis elements are exactly the crystal basis elements in this case (since for$\mathfrak{sl}_2$, the crystal and canonical bases coincide). The bar-invariance:$\bar{v}_m = v_m$ (since$K$ acts by$q^m$ and$\overline{q^m} = q^{-m}$, but$v_m$ is a weight vector, and the bar-involution is the identity on the vector space). Actually, bar-invariance is automatic for weight vectors of$V(\lambda)$ when$\lambda$ is dominant — the point is that the bar-involution of$U_q$ preserves the canonical basis of the module.

**Example 2.** The canonical basis of $U_q^-(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ (the lower triangular part, generated by$F$): the canonical basis consists of the elements$F^{(n)} = F^n/[n]_q!$ (Lusztig's divided powers), which are$\mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$-integral and bar-invariant. As$q \to 1$, these specialize to$f^n / n!$ (Kostant's divided powers), confirming the connection to Kostant's$\mathbb{Z}$-form of$U(\mathfrak{g})$.

The canonical basis has extraordinary positivity properties.

\begin{theorem}[Positivity of Canonical Basis Structure Constants]
In the canonical basis $\mathbf{B}(\lambda)$ of$V(\lambda)$ (and of$U_q^-(\mathfrak{g})$), the structure constants for multiplication of canonical basis elements and for tensor product decomposition are polynomials in$q$ with non-negative integer coefficients.
\end{theorem}

More precisely: if $b_1 \in \mathbf{B}(\lambda)$ and$b_2 \in \mathbf{B}(\mu)$, then the expansion of$b_1 \otimes b_2$ in the canonical basis of$V(\lambda) \otimes V(\mu) = \bigoplus_\nu V(\nu)^{\oplus N_{\lambda\mu}^\nu}$ has non-negative integer coefficients in$\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q, q^{-1}]$.

**Example 3.** For $\mathfrak{gl}_n$, the canonical basis of$V(\lambda)$ (for$\lambda$ a partition) is indexed by standard Young tableaux, and the structure constants for tensor products are exactly the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for the symmetric group (in a precise sense). Specifically, the canonical basis of$U_q^-(\mathfrak{gl}_n)$ indexed by semistandard Young tableaux has structure constants that are non-negative integer polynomials in$q$, giving a$q$-analogue of the Littlewood–Richardson rule.

The canonical basis specializes to natural bases at special values of $q$.

\begin{proposition}
The canonical basis $\mathbf{B}(\lambda)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Specializes at $q = 1$ to a natural basis for$L(\lambda)$ (related to the dual Kashiwara basis and to MV polytopes).
\item Specializes at $q = 0$ to the crystal basis$B(\lambda)$ (Kashiwara's combinatorial basis).
\item Specializes at $q = p$ (a prime, working over$\mathbb{F}_p$) to a basis for the$G$-representation$L(\lambda)$ with good integrality properties — related to the Jantzen filtration and decomposition numbers.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

**Example 4.** The *Ariki–Koike theorem* (1994): For the Hecke algebra $\mathcal{H}_n(q)$ of the symmetric group$S_n$ at a root of unity$q = e^{2\pi i/\ell}$, the decomposition numbers (the numbers$d_{\lambda\mu}$ counting simple composition factors in Specht modules) are the specializations at$q=1$ of the canonical basis elements of the Fock space representation of the affine quantum group$U_q(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_\ell)$. This theorem — connecting the representation theory of Hecke algebras, which is a modular analogue of the symmetric group, to the canonical basis of an affine quantum group — is a striking example of the reach of canonical basis theory.

\begin{remark}
The positivity of canonical basis structure constants, while visible from the definition via lattices, was originally proved geometrically by Nakajima using the theory of quiver varieties (Nakajima 1994). The quiver variety $\mathfrak{M}(\lambda)$ (a symplectic resolution of a quiver variety) has intersection cohomology that coincides, via the geometric Satake equivalence, with the canonical basis elements of$V(\lambda)$. This geometric proof of positivity places the canonical basis firmly in the same constellation as perverse sheaves and Kazhdan–Lusztig theory, and it is the deep reason why these structures all exhibit the same positivity phenomena.
\end{remark}
