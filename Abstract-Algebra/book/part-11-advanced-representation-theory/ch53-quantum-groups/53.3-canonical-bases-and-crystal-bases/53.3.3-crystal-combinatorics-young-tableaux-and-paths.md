# 53.3.3 Crystal Combinatorics: Young Tableaux and Paths

For the classical Lie algebras of type $A$ — corresponding to$\mathfrak{sl}_n$ and$GL_n$ — the crystal bases of irreducible representations admit a completely explicit combinatorial description in terms of *semistandard Young tableaux*. This makes the abstract theory of crystals computable and connects it to the rich combinatorial tradition of symmetric function theory, giving a unified framework for RSK, jeu de taquin, and the Littlewood–Richardson rule.

\begin{definition}
A *semistandard Young tableau* (SSYT) of shape $\lambda$ (a partition) with entries from$\{1, \ldots, n\}$ is a filling of the Young diagram of$\lambda$ with entries from$\{1, \ldots, n\}$ such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item Entries weakly increase along rows (left to right).
\item Entries strictly increase down columns.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Crystal Basis for $\mathfrak{sl}_n$]
The crystal $B(\lambda)$ of the irreducible$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_n)$-module$V(\lambda)$ (for$\lambda$ a partition of length$\leq n-1$) is in canonical bijection with the set$\mathrm{SSYT}(\lambda, n)$ of semistandard Young tableaux of shape$\lambda$ with entries from$\{1, \ldots, n\}$. The weight of a tableau$T$ is$\mathrm{wt}(T) = \sum_{k=1}^n c_k(T) \omega_k$, where$c_k(T)$ is the number of entries equal to$k$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** For $n = 2$ (so$\mathfrak{sl}_2$) and$\lambda = (m)$ (a single row of$m$ boxes), the SSYT of shape$(m)$ with entries from$\{1, 2\}$ are just weakly increasing words$1^a 2^b$ with$a + b = m$. There are$m+1$ such tableaux, matching$\dim V(m) = m+1$. The crystal basis element for the tableau$1^a 2^b$ is the weight vector$v_{m-2b}$ (of weight$m - 2b$ for$\mathfrak{sl}_2$).

**Example 2.** For $n = 3$ and$\lambda = (2,1)$ (a "hook" partition), the SSYT of shape$(2,1)$ with entries from$\{1, 2, 3\}$ are (reading rows top to bottom, columns left to right):
$$\begin{Young} 1 & 1 \cr 2 \cr \end{Young}, \quad \begin{Young} 1 & 1 \cr 3 \cr \end{Young}, \quad \begin{Young} 1 & 2 \cr 2 \cr \end{Young}, \quad \begin{Young} 1 & 2 \cr 3 \cr \end{Young}, \quad \begin{Young} 1 & 3 \cr 2 \cr \end{Young}, \quad \begin{Young} 2 & 2 \cr 3 \cr \end{Young}, \quad \begin{Young} 1 & 3 \cr 3 \cr \end{Young}, \quad \begin{Young} 2 & 3 \cr 3 \cr \end{Young}.$$
There are 8 such tableaux, matching $\dim V(\omega_1 + \omega_2) = 8$ (the adjoint representation of$\mathfrak{sl}_3$). Note: the tableau$\begin{Young} 1 & 3 \cr 2 \cr \end{Young}$ is semistandard: row 1 has$1 \leq 3$ (weakly increasing), column 1 has$1 < 2$ (strictly increasing), column 2 has only$3$. All conditions satisfied.

The crystal operators $\tilde{f}_i$ (lowering operators for the$i$-th simple root) are computed on tableaux via the *signature rule*.

\begin{definition}[Kashiwara's Signature Rule]
To compute $\tilde{f}_i(T)$ for a tableau$T$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Read the entries $i$ and$i+1$ in$T$ in a fixed canonical order (e.g., reading word: right to left across rows, top to bottom).
\item Write $+$ for each entry$i$ and$-$ for each entry$i+1$.
\item Cancel all adjacent $(-,+)$ pairs (i.e., a$-$ immediately followed by a$+$) from left to right.
\item After cancellation, $\tilde{f}_i$ changes the rightmost remaining$+$ (an$i$) to$i+1$.
\item If no $+$ remains,$\tilde{f}_i(T) = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

**Example 3.** Apply $\tilde{f}_1$ to the tableau$T = \begin{Young} 1 & 2 \cr 3 \cr \end{Young}$. Reading word (right to left, top to bottom):$2, 1, 3$. The entries$1$ and$2$: read as$-, +, \emptyset$ (where$+$ stands for entry$1$ and$-$ for entry$2$). Sequence:$-, +$. The pair$(-,+)$: a$-$ followed by$+$ cancels. After cancellation: empty. No remaining$+$, so$\tilde{f}_1(T) = 0$.

Now apply $\tilde{f}_1$ to$T' = \begin{Young} 1 & 1 \cr 2 \cr \end{Young}$. Reading word:$1, 1, 2$. Entries 1 and 2:$+, +, -$. Cancel the$(-,+)$ pair rightmost first: the$-$ at position 3 cancels with the$+$ at position 2. Remaining:$+$ at position 1.$\tilde{f}_1$ changes this$1$ to a$2$:$T' \mapsto \begin{Young} 1 & 2 \cr 2 \cr \end{Young}$ (the result has row entries$1, 2$ weakly increasing and column entries$1, 2$ strictly increasing: a valid SSYT).

The tensor product structure of crystals also has a clean combinatorial description: the crystal of $V(\lambda) \otimes V(\mu)$ is the set$B(\lambda) \times B(\mu)$ with the product crystal operators, where the operators on a pair$(b_1, b_2)$ act first on$b_2$ and spill over to$b_1$ when$b_2$ is annihilated. This rule, applied to pairs of tableaux, recovers the RSK correspondence.

**Example 4.** The highest-weight elements in $B(\lambda) \otimes B(\mu)$ (those annihilated by all$\tilde{e}_i$) are in bijection with Littlewood–Richardson tableaux, recovering the Littlewood–Richardson rule:$[V(\lambda) \otimes V(\mu) : V(\nu)] = |\{$ Littlewood–Richardson tableaux of shape$\nu / \lambda$ and weight$\mu\}|$. For$\mathfrak{sl}_3$,$\lambda = \mu = (1,0)$ ($= \omega_1$),$\nu$ can be$(2,0)$ or$(1,1)$: there is one LR tableau of each type, giving$V(\omega_1) \otimes V(\omega_1) = V(2\omega_1) \oplus V(\omega_2)$, which matches the classical decomposition$\mathbf{3} \otimes \mathbf{3} = \mathbf{6} \oplus \mathbf{\bar 3}$.

\begin{remark}
The *path model* of Littelmann provides an alternative (equivalent) description of crystals in terms of piecewise-linear paths in the weight space $\mathfrak{h}^*_{\mathbb{R}}$. In the path model, crystal operators$\tilde{f}_i$ and$\tilde{e}_i$ act by "reflecting" the path at certain times determined by the weight function$\langle \mathrm{path}(t), \alpha_i^\vee \rangle$. This model works for all types (not just type$A$) and gives a uniform description of crystals that is particularly useful for studying branching rules (restriction from$G$ to a subgroup), which correspond to projections of paths under the embedding of weight lattices. The Littelmann path model thus unifies the tableau combinatorics of type$A$ with analogous (but more complex) combinatorics for other root systems.
\end{remark}
