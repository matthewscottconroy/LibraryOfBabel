# 53.4.1 Knot Invariants: The Jones Polynomial via $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$

The *Jones polynomial* $V_L(q)$ of a knot or link$L$, discovered by Jones in 1984, was the first knot invariant beyond the Alexander polynomial to distinguish knots from their mirror images. Its original discovery went through von Neumann algebras and Markov traces on the Temperley–Lieb algebra, but its natural algebraic home — as emerged in the late 1980s through the work of Reshetikhin, Turaev, and others — is the representation theory of the quantum group$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$.

The construction uses the braided tensor category structure of $\mathrm{Rep}(U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2))$. Every oriented link$L$ can be expressed as the closure of a braid$\beta \in B_n$ for some$n$. Given a representation$V$ of$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ — typically the two-dimensional representation$V_1$ — the braid group$B_n$ acts on$V^{\otimes n}$ via the$R$-matrix: the generator$\sigma_i$ acts by$1^{\otimes i-1} \otimes R_{V,V} \otimes 1^{\otimes n-i-1}$, where$R_{V,V}: V \otimes V \to V \otimes V$ is the braiding isomorphism.

\begin{definition}
For a braid $\beta \in B_n$ and a$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-module$V$, the *Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant* of the link closure$\hat\beta$ is
$$RT_V(\hat\beta) = q^{\mathrm{writhe}(\beta) \cdot c_V} \cdot \mathrm{tr}_q(\rho_V(\beta)),$$
where $\rho_V: B_n \to \mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})$ is the braid group representation via the$R$-matrix,$\mathrm{tr}_q$ is the *quantum trace* (modified by the ribbon element), and$c_V$ is the eigenvalue of the quadratic Casimir on$V$.
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** For $V = V_1$ (the 2-dimensional representation) and the trefoil knot$T_{2,3}$ (the closure of the braid$\sigma_1^3 \in B_2$): The braid group$B_2 = \langle \sigma_1 \rangle$ acts on$V_1 \otimes V_1$ via$R_{V_1,V_1}$. The$R$-matrix on$V_1 \otimes V_1$ has eigenvalues$q$ (on the symmetric subspace$V_2$) and$-q^{-1}$ (on the antisymmetric subspace$V_0$). For$\beta = \sigma_1^3$, the representation gives$\rho_{V_1}(\sigma_1^3) = R^3$, with eigenvalues$q^3$ and$-q^{-3}$.

The quantum trace: $\mathrm{tr}_q(\rho_{V_1}(\sigma_1^3)) = q^{3/2} \cdot q^3 + q^{-3/2} \cdot (-q^{-3})$ (using the quantum dimensions). After accounting for the writhe factor ($w = 3$ for the right-handed trefoil) and normalizing, the Jones polynomial of the trefoil is:
$$V_{T_{2,3}}(q) = -q^{-4} + q^{-3} + q^{-1}$$
(in the convention where the unknot has polynomial 1). This is a non-trivial polynomial — notably, it changes when $q \mapsto q^{-1}$, i.e., when the trefoil is replaced by its mirror image:
$$V_{\bar T_{2,3}}(q) = -q^4 + q^3 + q.$$
The fact that $V_{T_{2,3}} \neq V_{\bar T_{2,3}}$ proves that the trefoil is not amphichiral (not equal to its mirror image) — a result the Alexander polynomial cannot detect.

**Example 2.** The *Kauffman bracket* $\langle L \rangle$ is a simpler polynomial invariant related to the Jones polynomial, defined by the skein relation:
$$\langle \raisebox{-3pt}{\rule{20pt}{.5pt}} \raisebox{0pt}{\rule{.5pt}{15pt}}\raisebox{-3pt}{\rule{20pt}{.5pt}}\rangle = A \langle \raisebox{0pt}{\rule{0pt}{0pt}}\raisebox{0pt}{\rule{20pt}{.5pt}} \raisebox{0pt}{\rule{20pt}{.5pt}}\rangle + A^{-1} \langle \,\bigcirc\, \rangle$$
(schematically). For $A = q^{-1/4}$, the Kauffman bracket with appropriate normalization recovers the Jones polynomial. The Kauffman bracket arises from the$R$-matrix of$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ on$V_1 \otimes V_1$ directly.

For $V = V_n$ (the$(n+1)$-dimensional representation), the construction gives the *colored Jones polynomials*$J_n(L, q)$, a family of polynomial invariants of$L$ indexed by$n$. Replacing$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with other Lie algebras gives further invariants.

\begin{theorem}[Reshetikhin–Turaev, 1990]
For any semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ and any finite-dimensional representation$V$ of$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ at generic$q$, the quantum trace construction produces a link invariant$RT_{V,\mathfrak{g}}(L)$ that:
\begin{itemize}
\item Satisfies a skein relation determined by the $R$-matrix eigenvalues.
\item Is invariant under isotopy (follows from the Yang–Baxter equation and the Reidemeister moves).
\item Specializes to the Jones polynomial for $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$,$V = V_1$.
\item Gives the HOMFLYPT polynomial for $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_n$,$V =$ standard.
\item Gives the Kauffman polynomial for $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{so}_n$ or$\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** The HOMFLYPT polynomial for the Hopf link $H$ (two interlocked circles): Using$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ gives the Jones polynomial; using$\mathfrak{sl}_3$ and the 3-dimensional representation gives the HOMFLYPT polynomial. The HOMFLYPT polynomial of the Hopf link is$\frac{-a^{-4} + a^{-2}(1 + z^2)}{z}$ (in standard variables$a, z$), which encodes information about the link's topology that is not accessible from the Jones polynomial alone.

The key insight is that the Yang–Baxter equation satisfied by the $R$-matrix guarantees topological invariance: the Reidemeister moves on diagrams correspond exactly to algebraic identities in the Hopf algebra.

\begin{remark}
The *Volume Conjecture* (Kashaev 1995, formalized by Murakami–Murakami) predicts that
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2\pi \log |J_n(K, e^{2\pi i/n})|}{n} = \mathrm{Vol}(S^3 \setminus K)$$
— the colored Jones polynomial at a root of unity computes the hyperbolic volume of the knot complement. This conjecture connects the algebraic (quantum group) invariants to the geometric (hyperbolic geometry) invariants, and remains one of the deepest open problems at the interface of quantum algebra and geometric topology.
\end{remark}
