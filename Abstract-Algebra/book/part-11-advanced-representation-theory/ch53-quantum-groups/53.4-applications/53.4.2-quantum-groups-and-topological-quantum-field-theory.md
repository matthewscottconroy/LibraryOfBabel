# 53.4.2 Quantum Groups and Topological Quantum Field Theory

The Reshetikhin–Turaev construction of knot and link invariants from quantum groups extends, when $q$ is a root of unity, to a far more powerful structure: a *topological quantum field theory* (TQFT), an invariant not just of links but of entire 3-manifolds. The key ingredient is that at a root of unity, the representation category of$U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ — after passing to the semisimple quotient by negligible objects — becomes a *modular tensor category*, which is precisely the structure needed to apply the Atiyah–Segal axioms for a TQFT.

\begin{definition}
A *modular tensor category* is a semisimple braided rigid tensor category $\mathcal{C}$ with:
\begin{itemize}
\item Finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects $\{V_i\}_{i \in I}$.
\item A non-degenerate *$S$-matrix*:$S_{ij} = \mathrm{tr}_q(c_{V_j, V_i} \circ c_{V_i, V_j}) \in \mathbb{C}$ (the matrix of quantum traces of double braidings), satisfying$\det(S) \neq 0$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The non-degeneracy of the $S$-matrix is the modular condition: it is what enables the surgery formula for 3-manifold invariants.

**Example 1.** For $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at$q = e^{2\pi i /\ell}$, the semisimple quotient (by negligible tilting modules) has simple objects$\{V_0, V_1, \ldots, V_{\ell-2}\}$ (the$(\ell-1)$-dimensional irreducibles with$n < \ell - 1$, i.e., the$\ell$-restricted weights). The$S$-matrix is:
$$S_{jk} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\ell}} \sin\left(\frac{\pi(j+1)(k+1)}{\ell}\right), \quad j, k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, \ell-2\}.$$
This is the $S$-matrix of the$A_1$ WZW model at level$\ell - 2$. The non-degeneracy follows from the fact that$\sin(\pi(j+1)(k+1)/\ell) \neq 0$ for$1 \leq j+1, k+1 \leq \ell-1$ — a consequence of the non-vanishing of the relevant cyclotomic values.

A modular tensor category $\mathcal{C}$ gives rise to a$(2+1)$-dimensional TQFT via the Crane–Yetter–Reshetikhin–Turaev (RT) construction.

\begin{theorem}[Reshetikhin–Turaev, 1991]
Given a modular tensor category $\mathcal{C}$, there is a$(2+1)$-TQFT$Z_{\mathcal{C}}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item To each closed oriented surface $\Sigma$,$Z_{\mathcal{C}}$ assigns a finite-dimensional vector space$Z_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)$ (the *Verlinde space* or *space of conformal blocks*).
\item To each closed oriented 3-manifold $M$,$Z_{\mathcal{C}}$ assigns a scalar$Z_{\mathcal{C}}(M) \in \mathbb{C}$ (the *partition function*).
\item $Z_{\mathcal{C}}$ satisfies the gluing axiom:$Z_{\mathcal{C}}(M_1 \cup_\Sigma M_2) = \langle Z_{\mathcal{C}}(M_1), Z_{\mathcal{C}}(M_2) \rangle_{Z_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

**Example 2.** The Verlinde formula computes $\dim Z_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma_g)$ (the dimension of the Verlinde space for a closed surface of genus$g$):
$$\dim Z_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma_g) = \sum_{i \in I} \left(\frac{S_{0i}}{S_{00}}\right)^{2-2g} = \sum_{i \in I} (\dim_q V_i)^{2-2g},$$
where $S_{0i}$ is the first column of the$S$-matrix and$\dim_q V_i = S_{0i}/S_{00}$ is the *quantum dimension* of$V_i$. For the$A_1$ model at level$k = \ell - 2$ and genus$g$:
$$\dim Z_{A_1,k}(\Sigma_g) = \left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)^{g-1} \sum_{j=0}^{k} \sin^{2-2g}\left(\frac{\pi(j+1)}{\ell}\right).$$
For $g = 1$ (the torus):$\dim Z_{A_1,k}(\Sigma_1) = \ell - 1$ (the number of simple objects).

**Example 3.** The partition function of $S^3$ (the 3-sphere):$Z_{\mathcal{C}}(S^3) = 1/S_{00}^2$ (in appropriate normalization). For$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at level$k = \ell - 2$:
$$Z(S^3) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\ell}} \left[\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{\ell}\right)\right]^{-1}.$$
The partition function $Z(M)$ of a 3-manifold$M$ is computed via a surgery formula: represent$M$ as Dehn surgery on a link$L \subset S^3$ and express$Z(M)$ as a sum of colored link invariants$RT_{V_i}(L)$ weighted by the$S$-matrix. This is the algebraic content of Witten's original path integral formula for Chern–Simons gauge theory.

\begin{proposition}
The partition function $Z(M)$ defined by the RT construction equals Witten's Chern–Simons invariant:
$$Z_{CS}(M) = \int [DA] \exp\left(\frac{ik}{4\pi} \int_M \mathrm{tr}(A \wedge dA + \tfrac{2}{3} A \wedge A \wedge A)\right)$$
(interpreted as a path integral over connections $A$ on a$G$-bundle over$M$), where$k = \ell - h^\vee$ is the Chern–Simons level.
\end{proposition}

**Example 4.** The Lens space $L(p, 1) = S^3 / (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})$ (the quotient of$S^3$ by a cyclic rotation). For$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at level$k$,$Z(L(p,1))$ is computable from the surgery formula and gives:
$$Z(L(p,1)) = \frac{1}{S_{00}} \sum_{j=0}^{k} S_{0j}^2 \cdot e^{2\pi i h_j p},$$
where $h_j = j(j+2)/(4\ell)$ is the conformal dimension (related to the quantum Casimir eigenvalue). This gives a family of 3-manifold invariants that distinguishes many lens spaces from each other.

\begin{remark}
The existence of a TQFT for each modular tensor category is one of the most beautiful applications of algebra to topology. The Atiyah–Segal axioms for a TQFT — functorial assignment of vector spaces to surfaces and scalars to 3-manifolds, with gluing — are abstract categorical axioms that translate directly into the language of monoidal functors from the bordism category. The modular tensor category provides all the structure needed to define such a functor: the $S$-matrix provides the Dehn surgery calculus, the braiding provides the Reidemeister moves, and the rigidity provides the cup-cap moves. The fact that quantum groups — defined by a few generators and relations — produce all this structure is one of the deepest results connecting algebra, topology, and physics.
\end{remark}
