## Examples, Applications, and Thought Experiments

- **$U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$** — generators $E, F, K$ with $KE = q^2 EK$, $KF = q^{-2} FK$, $[E,F] = \frac{K - K^{-1}}{q - q^{-1}}$; as $q \to 1$, these become $e, f, h$ of $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$; for generic $q$, the representation theory is identical to the classical case (same characters and dimensions); the deformation is "invisible" in the representation theory
- **Crystal bases** — as $q \to 0$, a basis of $V(\lambda)$ acquires a crystal structure: a colored directed graph with vertices = basis elements and edges labeled by the simple roots; no algebra remains, only combinatorics; the crystal of $V(\lambda)$ for $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ is a hexagonal weight diagram with colored arrows
- **Quantum groups at roots of unity** — if $q$ is a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, the representation theory acquires "modular" features: some Verma modules become reducible in unexpected ways; Lusztig showed this is related to the modular representation theory of $G$ in characteristic $\ell$; a surprising bridge between $q$-deformation and characteristic $p$
- **Quantum groups and the Yang–Baxter equation** — the $R$-matrix of a quasi-triangular Hopf algebra satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation $R_{12} R_{13} R_{23} = R_{23} R_{13} R_{12}$; solutions to Yang–Baxter give representations of the braid group; this connects representation theory to knot invariants (the Jones polynomial) and statistical mechanics

## Exercises

1. The quantum group $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is generated by $E, F, K, K^{-1}$ subject to $KK^{-1} = K^{-1}K = 1$, $KE = q^2 EK$, $KF = q^{-2} FK$, and $[E,F] = \frac{K - K^{-1}}{q - q^{-1}}$. Verify that as $q \to 1$ (more precisely, substituting $K = q^H$ and taking the limit), these relations reduce to the standard relations $[H,E] = 2E$, $[H,F] = -2F$, $[E,F] = H$ of $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. Then verify that the comultiplication $\Delta(E) = E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E$, $\Delta(F) = F \otimes 1 + K^{-1} \otimes F$, $\Delta(K) = K \otimes K$ defines a bialgebra structure: check that $\Delta$ is an algebra homomorphism on the generator $E$.

2. The standard 2-dimensional representation of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ on $V = \mathbb{C}(q)^2$ has basis $\{v_+, v_-\}$ with $K v_\pm = q^{\pm 1} v_\pm$, $E v_- = v_+$, $E v_+ = 0$, $F v_+ = v_-$, $F v_- = 0$. Compute the action of $E, F, K$ on $V \otimes V$ using the comultiplication $\Delta$. Identify the two irreducible submodules of $V \otimes V$ (of dimensions 3 and 1) and write down explicit basis vectors for each. Verify that for generic $q$, the dimensions and weights match the classical decomposition $\mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2} = \mathbf{3} \oplus \mathbf{1}$.

3. The $R$-matrix for $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ acting on $V \otimes V$ (with $V$ the standard 2-dimensional module) is given explicitly as $R = q \sum_i e_{ii} \otimes e_{ii} + \sum_{i \neq j} e_{ii} \otimes e_{jj} + (q - q^{-1}) \sum_{i > j} e_{ij} \otimes e_{ji}$ in the standard basis $\{e_{ij}\}$ of $\mathrm{End}(V)$. Write out $R$ as a $4 \times 4$ matrix in the ordered basis $\{v_+ \otimes v_+, v_+ \otimes v_-, v_- \otimes v_+, v_- \otimes v_-\}$, and verify that $R$ satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation $R_{12} R_{13} R_{23} = R_{23} R_{13} R_{12}$ as operators on $V \otimes V \otimes V$ (you may verify just one non-trivial entry).

4. The crystal basis of the standard representation $V = V(1)$ of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ (dimension 2) consists of two elements $\{b_+, b_-\}$ connected by the crystal operator $\tilde{f}_1: b_+ \mapsto b_-$ and $\tilde{e}_1: b_- \mapsto b_+$. Describe the crystal of the tensor product $V(1) \otimes V(1)$: the crystal is the Cartesian product $\{b_+, b_-\}^2$ with crystal operators defined by the tensor product rule. Draw the crystal graph with vertices $\{b_+ \otimes b_+, b_+ \otimes b_-, b_- \otimes b_+, b_- \otimes b_-\}$ and edges corresponding to $\tilde{f}_1, \tilde{e}_1$, and identify the connected components (which should correspond to $V(2)$ and $V(0)$).

5. Let $q = \zeta = e^{2\pi i / \ell}$ be a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity with $\ell \geq 2$. In $U_\zeta(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, the quantum integer $[n]_q = \frac{q^n - q^{-n}}{q - q^{-1}}$ satisfies $[\ell]_\zeta = 0$. Explain why this causes the representation theory of $U_\zeta(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ to differ from the generic case: specifically, why the Verma module $M(\ell - 1)$ (of highest weight $\ell - 1$) becomes reducible at $q = \zeta$, even though $M(n)$ is irreducible for $0 \leq n \leq \ell - 2$. Describe the structure of $M(\ell - 1)$ as a $U_\zeta(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-module.

6. A bialgebra $H$ is a Hopf algebra if it admits an antipode $S: H \to H$ satisfying $m \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta = \eta \circ \varepsilon = m \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes S) \circ \Delta$. For the group algebra $kG$, the antipode is $S(g) = g^{-1}$. For $U(\mathfrak{g})$, the antipode is $S(x) = -x$ for $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ (extended as an algebra anti-homomorphism). Verify the antipode axiom for $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ on the generator $e$ using $\Delta(e) = e \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes e$.

7. State the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjecture for quantum groups at roots of unity: for $G$ a simply connected semisimple algebraic group over $\mathbb{F}_p$ (with $p > h$, the Coxeter number) and $q = e^{2\pi i/p}$, the character of the simple module $L(\lambda)$ for restricted dominant $\lambda$ equals the character of the corresponding simple module in the category of tilting modules for $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$, and can be expressed in terms of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for the affine Weyl group. Verify this statement explicitly for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and $p = 5$: list the restricted dominant weights, state the Steinberg module, and identify which simple modules have the same character as the corresponding quantum group modules.

8. (Challenge) The canonical basis of $V(n)$ (the $(n+1)$-dimensional simple module for $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$) consists of elements $b_k = \frac{F^k}{[k]!} v_n$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, n$, where $[k]! = [1][2]\cdots[k]$ is the quantum factorial and $v_n$ is the highest weight vector. Show that these elements form a basis over $\mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$ (the divided power basis), and verify that they satisfy the bar-involution symmetry: the anti-linear map $\overline{q} = q^{-1}$ sends each $b_k$ to itself. Then explain why the positivity of the canonical basis structure constants (the fact that matrix elements of $E, F$ in the canonical basis have coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q, q^{-1}]$) is trivial for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ but non-trivial for general $\mathfrak{g}$, and state Lusztig's theorem on positivity via perverse sheaves.
