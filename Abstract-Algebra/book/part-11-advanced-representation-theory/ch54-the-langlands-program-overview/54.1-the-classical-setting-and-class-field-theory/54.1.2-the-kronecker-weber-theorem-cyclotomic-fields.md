# 54.1.2 The Kronecker–Weber Theorem; Cyclotomic Fields

The maximal abelian extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ is completely explicit: by the *Kronecker–Weber theorem*, every finite abelian extension of$\mathbb{Q}$ is a subfield of some cyclotomic field$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, where$\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i/n}$ is a primitive$n$-th root of unity. Hence$\mathbb{Q}^{ab} = \bigcup_{n \geq 1} \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, the union of all cyclotomic fields. Kronecker conjectured this result in the 19th century, and Weber gave the first proof (with later corrections); it can also be proved as a consequence of the general Artin reciprocity law, and the two lines of argument give complementary insights into why it is true.

\begin{theorem}[Kronecker–Weber]
Every finite abelian extension $L / \mathbb{Q}$ is contained in$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ for some$n \geq 1$. Equivalently,$\mathbb{Q}^{ab} = \mathbb{Q}^{cyc} = \bigcup_n \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** The quadratic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$ are$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ for squarefree$d$. By Kronecker–Weber, each is contained in some cyclotomic field. Explicitly:
- $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1}) = \mathbb{Q}(i) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_4)$ (since$i = \zeta_4$).
- $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$ (since$\zeta_5 + \zeta_5^{-1} = 2\cos(2\pi/5) = (\sqrt{5}-1)/2$, so$\sqrt{5} = 2\cos(2\pi/5) + 1 - 1 \in \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$).
- $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ (since$\zeta_3 = (-1 + \sqrt{-3})/2$, so$\sqrt{-3} = 2\zeta_3 + 1 \in \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$).
- More generally, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^*}) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ for$p^* = (-1)^{(p-1)/2} p$, by the Gauss sum calculation.

**Example 2.** The cubic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\cos(2\pi/9)) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_9)$ is a cyclic degree-3 extension of$\mathbb{Q}$. The minimal polynomial of$\cos(2\pi/9)$ over$\mathbb{Q}$ is$8x^3 - 6x + 1$ (a depressed cubic with roots$\cos(2\pi/9), \cos(4\pi/9), \cos(8\pi/9)$). This is an abelian extension (cyclic of degree 3), and it is contained in$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_9)$ by Kronecker–Weber.

The Galois group of $\mathbb{Q}^{ab}$ over$\mathbb{Q}$:

\begin{theorem}
$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{ab}/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \hat{\mathbb{Z}}^* = \varprojlim_n (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^* \cong \prod_p \mathbb{Z}_p^*$
where the product runs over all primes $p$ and$\mathbb{Z}_p^*$ is the group of$p$-adic units. The isomorphism sends$\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{ab}/\mathbb{Q})$ to the element$(a_p)_p$ where$\sigma(\zeta_{p^k}) = \zeta_{p^k}^{a_p}$ for all$k$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** The Artin map for $K = \mathbb{Q}$ is the isomorphism$\phi_{\mathbb{Q}}: \mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^*/\mathbb{Q}^* \cdot \mathbb{R}_{>0} \xrightarrow{\sim} \hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$. Concretely: for a prime$p$, the idèle with$p$ in the$p$-adic component and 1 elsewhere maps to the element$(1, \ldots, 1, p, 1, \ldots) \in \hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$ (with$p$ in the$p$-th slot). Under the isomorphism$\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^* \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{ab}/\mathbb{Q})$, this element corresponds to the Frobenius$\mathrm{Frob}_p$: the automorphism that sends$\zeta_n \mapsto \zeta_n^p$ for all$n$ with$\gcd(p, n) = 1$.

The adelic description makes class field theory functorial.

\begin{proposition}
For any finite extension $L/K$ of number fields, the norm map$N_{L/K}: \mathbb{A}_L^* \to \mathbb{A}_K^*$ is compatible with the Artin maps:$\phi_K \circ N_{L/K} = \phi_L|_{\mathrm{Gal}(K^{ab}/L^{ab})}$. This functoriality is the basis for the global-to-local comparison in the Langlands program.
\end{proposition}

**Example 4.** The global Langlands correspondence for $GL_1$ over$\mathbb{Q}$: the one-dimensional complex Galois representations of$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ — equivalently, the characters of$\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^* \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{ab}/\mathbb{Q})$ — are exactly the Dirichlet characters$\chi: (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^* \to \mathbb{C}^*$ (for varying$n$). The automorphic representations of$GL_1(\mathbb{A}) = \mathbb{A}^*$ of arithmetic type are the Hecke characters$\psi: \mathbb{A}^*/\mathbb{Q}^* \to \mathbb{C}^*$ that factor through the finite quotient$\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$ (i.e., are algebraic at infinity). The bijection$\chi \leftrightarrow \psi = \chi \circ \phi_{\mathbb{Q}}^{-1}$ is the$GL_1$ Langlands correspondence.

\begin{remark}
The Kronecker–Weber theorem generalizes to other number fields in the form of *Hilbert's 12th problem* (Kronecker's *Jugendtraum*): find explicit generators for all abelian extensions of a number field $K$. For$K = \mathbb{Q}$, the answer is cyclotomic fields (roots of unity). For imaginary quadratic fields$K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, the answer is provided by the theory of complex multiplication: the abelian extensions of$K$ are generated by special values of elliptic functions (the$j$-function and related functions) evaluated at CM points. For general number fields, Hilbert's 12th problem remains largely open and is one of the motivations for the Langlands program: the automorphic forms for$GL_1$ and higher-rank groups are expected to provide the correct generalization of cyclotomic fields to higher dimensions.
\end{remark}
