# 54.1.3 The Langlands Conjectures for $GL_n$

In a famous letter to André Weil in 1967, Robert Langlands proposed a sweeping generalization of class field theory. The key observation was that the Artin reciprocity law — the bijection between abelian Galois extensions of $K$ and Hecke characters of$\mathbb{A}_K^*$ — could be viewed as a correspondence between$GL_1$ Galois representations and automorphic representations of$GL_1(\mathbb{A}_K)$. The natural question then becomes: is there an analogous correspondence for higher-rank groups?

\begin{conjecture}[Langlands, 1967]
For each $n \geq 1$ and each number field$K$, there is a canonical bijection
$$\left\{ \begin{array}{c} \text{irreducible } n\text{-dimensional} \\ \text{representations of } W_K \end{array} \right\} \longleftrightarrow \left\{ \begin{array}{c} \text{cuspidal automorphic representations} \\ \text{of } GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K) \end{array} \right\}$$
(in appropriate senses, and with appropriate compatibility conditions), preserving $L$-functions:$L(s, \rho) = L(s, \pi)$.
\end{conjecture}

Here $W_K$ is the Weil group of$K$ (a specific topological group that is a dense subgroup of$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)$ and carries more structure, including the norm character$|\cdot|: W_K \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$). For$n = 1$, this is exactly the Artin reciprocity law (class field theory). For$n = 2$, it connects modular forms to 2-dimensional Galois representations.

**Example 1.** The $GL_1$ case (class field theory): A 1-dimensional representation$\chi: W_K \to \mathbb{C}^*$ factors through$W_K^{ab} \cong C_K$ (by class field theory), so it corresponds to a Hecke character$\psi = \chi \circ \phi_K^{-1}: C_K \to \mathbb{C}^*$. The$L$-functions agree:$L(s, \chi) = L(s, \psi)$, the Artin$L$-function equals the Hecke$L$-function. This is the$GL_1$ Langlands correspondence.

**Example 2.** The $GL_2$ case over$\mathbb{Q}$: A cuspidal automorphic representation$\pi$ of$GL_2(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})$ of weight$k$ and level$N$ corresponds, via the Langlands correspondence, to a 2-dimensional Weil group representation$\rho: W_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ (or, in the$\ell$-adic setting,$\rho_\ell: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to GL_2(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$). In one direction, starting from a cuspidal modular eigenform$f = \sum a_n q^n$ of weight$k$ and level$N$, the Galois representation$\rho_f$ is constructed from the$\ell$-adic Tate module of the Jacobian of the modular curve$X_0(N)$ — a highly non-trivial construction. The$L$-function match:$L(s, \rho_f) = L(s, f) = \sum a_n n^{-s}$ (the$L$-function of the modular form).

**Example 3.** The modularity of elliptic curves: For an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$, the$\ell$-adic Tate module$V_\ell(E) = T_\ell(E) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell} \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is a 2-dimensional$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$-representation. The Langlands correspondence for$GL_2$ predicts that this representation comes from a modular form. The *Modularity Theorem* (Taylor–Wiles 1995, Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor 2001) confirms this: every elliptic curve$E/\mathbb{Q}$ is modular, i.e., there exists a newform$f$ of weight 2 and level$N$ (the conductor of$E$) such that$\rho_f \cong V_\ell(E)$. The key application: Fermat's Last Theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Modularity Theorem, Taylor–Wiles 1995]
For every elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$, there exists a cuspidal newform$f \in S_2(\Gamma_0(N))$ such that$a_p(E) = a_p(f)$ for all primes$p \nmid N$, where$a_p(E) = p + 1 - |E(\mathbb{F}_p)|$ is the trace of Frobenius.
\end{theorem}

To see why this matters beyond the $GL_1$ case: the Langlands correspondence for$n = 2$ over$\mathbb{Q}$ connects modular forms to Galois representations, and this connection transformed number theory. The strategy for Fermat's Last Theorem: a non-trivial solution to$a^p + b^p = c^p$ would give a Frey elliptic curve$E: y^2 = x(x-a^p)(x+b^p)$; Ribet showed this curve is not modular (contradicting the Serre conjecture and epsilon-conjecture); but the modularity theorem says all elliptic curves over$\mathbb{Q}$ are modular. Contradiction.

The general Langlands conjectures for $GL_n$ over number fields remain largely open (proved only in special cases), but the case over *function fields*$\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ was proved by Drinfeld ($n = 2$, 1980) and Lafforgue ($n$ arbitrary, 2002).

\begin{remark}
The correspondence is not a bijection between arbitrary representations and arbitrary automorphic forms: one needs to specify which representations count. On the Galois side, the correct objects are *$\ell$-adic representations* that are "geometric" in the sense of Fontaine–Mazur (Section 54.3.3): almost everywhere unramified and de Rham at$\ell$. On the automorphic side, the correct objects are *algebraic* automorphic representations. The compatibility between these conditions — the "arithmetic" condition on Galois representations and the "algebraic" condition on automorphic forms — is one of the beautiful structural features of the Langlands correspondence.
\end{remark}
