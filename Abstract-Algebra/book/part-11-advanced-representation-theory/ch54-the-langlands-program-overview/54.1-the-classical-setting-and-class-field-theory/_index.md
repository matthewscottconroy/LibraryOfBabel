# 54.1 The Classical Setting: $GL_1$ and Class Field Theory

Class field theory is the complete solution to the $GL_1$ Langlands program, and understanding it in full detail is the indispensable prerequisite for the rest of the chapter. The central question of class field theory is: given a number field $F$, what are all abelian extensions $L/F$? The answer — provided by the Artin reciprocity law, the Kronecker–Weber theorem (for $F = \mathbb{Q}$), and the general theory of Shimura varieties in the abelian case — is that the abelian extensions of $F$ are classified, up to isomorphism, by the open subgroups of the idèle class group $C_F = \mathbb{A}_F^\times / F^\times$. More precisely, the Artin map $\mathrm{rec}_F: C_F \to \mathrm{Gal}(F^{ab}/F)$ is a continuous surjection whose kernel is the connected component of the identity in $C_F$, and it intertwines the local Artin maps at each place with the local reciprocity maps of local class field theory. The statement is beautifully clean: the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension is the profinite completion of the idèle class group.

The $GL_1$ Langlands correspondence says, in modern language, that continuous characters $\chi: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F) \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ (1-dimensional Galois representations) are in natural bijection with Hecke Grössencharacters, or equivalently with characters of the idèle class group $\mathbb{A}_F^\times / F^\times$. The bijection is given by composing the character with the Artin map. A key test of the correspondence is at the level of $L$-functions: the Artin $L$-function $L(\chi, s)$ of a Galois character equals the Hecke $L$-function $L(\pi_\chi, s)$ of the corresponding automorphic representation $\pi_\chi$ of $GL_1(\mathbb{A}_F)$. This equality of $L$-functions — which in the abelian case follows from class field theory — is the template for the $GL_n$ Langlands program: the fundamental conjecture is always that Galois $L$-functions equal automorphic $L$-functions.

The Kronecker–Weber theorem — that every abelian extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ is contained in a cyclotomic field — is the starting point for the explicit class field theory of $\mathbb{Q}$. Its proof, via the theory of cyclotomic polynomials and the Artin map, illustrates the general principle: abelian extensions are generated by special values of analytic functions (in this case, the roots of unity $e^{2\pi i/n}$, which are the values of the exponential at torsion points). For general number fields, the explicit construction of abelian extensions from special values — the theory of complex multiplication for imaginary quadratic fields, and Kronecker's Jugendtraum (dream of youth) for general fields — remains an active area. The theory of Shimura varieties and their special points provides the correct generalization, and the explicit reciprocity laws governing these special points are a refined form of the Langlands correspondence in the abelian case.

The section establishes the Artin map and proves the main theorem of class field theory for $\mathbb{Q}$, identifies the $GL_1$ Langlands correspondence as classical reciprocity, and develops the language of idèles and $L$-functions that will be used throughout the chapter. The goal is to make the classical theory serve as a template: the Langlands program for $GL_n$ is, in a precise sense, the unique natural generalization of class field theory from abelian to non-abelian Galois groups.
