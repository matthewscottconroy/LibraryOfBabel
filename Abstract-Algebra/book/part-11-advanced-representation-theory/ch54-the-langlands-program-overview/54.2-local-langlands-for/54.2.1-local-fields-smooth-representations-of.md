# 54.2.1 Local Fields $\mathbb{Q}_p$; Smooth Representations of$GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$

The Langlands program decomposes naturally into local and global components. The local story, which takes place at a single prime $p$, is both a testing ground for the global conjecture and a theorem in its own right. A *local field*$F$ is a field that is complete with respect to a discrete valuation and has finite residue field; the principal examples are$\mathbb{Q}_p$ (the$p$-adic numbers) and$\mathbb{F}_q((t))$ (formal Laurent series over a finite field). The local Langlands program asks for a correspondence between$n$-dimensional representations of the local Galois group$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$ and irreducible smooth representations of$GL_n(F)$.

\begin{definition}
A *local field* is a locally compact non-discrete topological field. The non-archimedean local fields are: $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (completion of$\mathbb{Q}$ at$p$), finite extensions of$\mathbb{Q}_p$, and$\mathbb{F}_q((t))$ (formal Laurent series). The archimedean local fields are$\mathbb{R}$ and$\mathbb{C}$.
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** The field $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of 2-adic numbers: elements are formal series$\sum_{n \geq n_0} a_n 2^n$ with$a_n \in \{0, 1\}$ and$n_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$. The 2-adic absolute value is$|x|_2 = 2^{-v_2(x)}$ where$v_2(x)$ is the 2-adic valuation (the exponent of the highest power of 2 dividing$x$). The ring of integers is$\mathbb{Z}_2 = \{x \in \mathbb{Q}_2 : |x|_2 \leq 1\}$, and the residue field is$\mathbb{F}_2 = \mathbb{Z}_2 / 2\mathbb{Z}_2$.

The group $GL_n(F)$, for a non-archimedean local field$F$, is a totally disconnected locally compact group — its topology is generated by cosets of compact open subgroups like$GL_n(\mathcal{O}_F)$ (where$\mathcal{O}_F$ is the ring of integers of$F$).

\begin{definition}
A complex representation $(\pi, V)$ of$GL_n(F)$ is *smooth* if every vector$v \in V$ is fixed by some compact open subgroup:$\pi(K) v = v$ for some compact open$K \subset GL_n(F)$. The category of smooth representations is the natural representation-theoretic context for$p$-adic groups.
\end{definition}

**Example 2.** The compact open subgroups of $GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ are (up to conjugacy) the congruence subgroups$K_m = \ker(GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p) \to GL_n(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z}))$ for$m \geq 0$. A smooth representation$V$ decomposes as$V = \bigcup_{m \geq 0} V^{K_m}$, where$V^{K_m}$ is the space of$K_m$-fixed vectors. For a *spherical* (or *unramified*) representation,$V^{K_0} = V^{GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)} \neq 0$ — there are vectors fixed by the maximal compact subgroup$GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$.

The simple smooth representations of $GL_n(F)$ are classified by the *Bernstein–Zelevinsky* theory.

\begin{theorem}[Bernstein–Zelevinsky Classification]
Every irreducible smooth representation of $GL_n(F)$ is a Langlands quotient of a representation parabolically induced from supercuspidal representations. Specifically, every irreducible is of the form$\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{GL_n}(\sigma_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes \sigma_k)$ where$P = GL_{n_1} \times \cdots \times GL_{n_k} \ltimes U$ is a parabolic subgroup and$\sigma_i$ are supercuspidal representations of$GL_{n_i}(F)$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** For $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, the irreducible smooth representations are:
- *Principal series*: induced from a character $\chi_1 \boxtimes \chi_2$ of the Borel (upper triangular matrices), for characters$\chi_i: \mathbb{Q}_p^* \to \mathbb{C}^*$. Irreducible when$\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} \neq |\cdot|_p^{\pm 1}$.
- *Special (Steinberg) representations*: $\chi \cdot \mathrm{St}$, where$\mathrm{St}$ is the Steinberg representation (the unique quotient of the principal series$\mathrm{Ind}_B^{GL_2}(|\cdot|^{1/2} \boxtimes |\cdot|^{-1/2})$).
- *Supercuspidal representations*: those not appearing as subquotients of any properly induced representation. For $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, supercuspidals arise from characters of the multiplicative group of the unramified quadratic extension$\mathbb{Q}_{p^2}^*$ (via the theory of compact induction from open compact-mod-center subgroups).

\begin{definition}
An irreducible smooth representation $\pi$ of$GL_n(F)$ is *supercuspidal* if the matrix coefficient$f_{v, \tilde{v}}: g \mapsto \langle \pi(g) v, \tilde{v} \rangle$ is compactly supported modulo the center$Z(GL_n(F)) = F^* \cdot I_n$, for every$v \in \pi$ and$\tilde{v} \in \tilde\pi$ (the contragredient).
\end{definition}

**Example 4.** The *depth-zero supercuspidals* of $GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$: induced from a cuspidal representation of the finite group$GL_n(\mathbb{F}_p) = GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)/GL_n(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p)$ (the residue group). Specifically, for an irreducible cuspidal representation$\rho$ of$GL_n(\mathbb{F}_p)$ (a representation that doesn't appear in any parabolically induced representation of the finite group), one inflates$\rho$ to$GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ and compactly induces to$GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$:$\pi = \mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_{GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p) \cdot F^*}^{GL_n(F)} \rho$. The result is an irreducible supercuspidal representation — one of the simplest examples of the correspondence between representation theory of finite groups and that of$p$-adic groups.

\begin{remark}
The key insight is that supercuspidal representations are the "atoms" of the local theory, in the same way that simple modules are the atoms of ordinary representation theory. The Bernstein–Zelevinsky classification reduces the study of all smooth representations to the study of supercuspidals, and the local Langlands correspondence must, at its core, describe the supercuspidals in terms of genuinely irreducible Galois representations. This is what makes the local Langlands conjecture deep: supercuspidals have no classical origin from lower-rank groups, so the bijection between them and irreducible Galois representations cannot be derived from simpler cases.
\end{remark}
