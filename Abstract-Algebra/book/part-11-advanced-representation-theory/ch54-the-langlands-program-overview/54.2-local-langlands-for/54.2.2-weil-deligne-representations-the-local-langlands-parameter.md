# 54.2.2 Weil–Deligne Representations; the Local Langlands Parameter

On the Galois side of the local Langlands correspondence, the correct objects are not simply $n$-dimensional representations of the local Galois group$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$, but a refined version that accounts for the monodromy arising from geometric situations. The refinement replaces the full Galois group by the *Weil group*$W_F$, and augments Weil group representations with a nilpotent operator.

\begin{definition}
The *Weil group* $W_F$ of a local field$F$ is the subgroup of$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$ consisting of elements whose image in$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k) \cong \hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ (the Galois group of the residue field extension$\overline{k}/k \cong \overline{\mathbb{F}}_q/\mathbb{F}_q$) is an integer power of the arithmetic Frobenius$\mathrm{Frob}_q: x \mapsto x^q$. The Weil group sits in the exact sequence$1 \to I_F \to W_F \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0$ (where$I_F \subset \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$ is the inertia group and$\mathbb{Z} \subset \hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the image of the arithmetic Frobenius).
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** For $F = \mathbb{Q}_p$, the Weil group$W_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ sits in:
$$1 \to I_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \to W_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
The inertia group $I_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p / \mathbb{Q}_p^{ur})$ (where$\mathbb{Q}_p^{ur}$ is the maximal unramified extension) has a specific structure: it contains a pro-$p$ subgroup (wild inertia$P_p$) and a pro-prime-to-$p$ quotient (tame inertia). An element of$W_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ that maps to$1 \in \mathbb{Z}$ is the geometric Frobenius element$\mathrm{Frob}_p^{-1}$ (inverse of the arithmetic Frobenius$x \mapsto x^p$).

A *Weil–Deligne representation* of rank $n$ is a pair$(r, N)$ where$r: W_F \to GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ is a continuous (semisimple) representation and$N \in M_n(\mathbb{C})$ is a nilpotent matrix satisfying$r(\sigma) N r(\sigma)^{-1} = |F(\sigma)| N$ for all$\sigma \in W_F$.

\begin{definition}
The *norm character* $|\cdot|_F: W_F \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ is defined by$|\sigma|_F = q^{-v(\sigma)}$ where$v(\sigma) \in \mathbb{Z}$ is the image of$\sigma$ in$\mathbb{Z} = W_F / I_F$. A *Weil–Deligne representation*$(r, N)$ is *Frobenius-semisimple* if$r(\mathrm{Frob}_q)$ is a semisimple (diagonalizable) matrix.
\end{definition}

**Example 2.** The unramified (or spherical) representations: $r: W_F \to GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ factors through$W_F \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}$, sending$\mathrm{Frob}_q$ to a semisimple matrix$A \in GL_n(\mathbb{C})$, and$N = 0$. These are the simplest Weil–Deligne representations: they are completely determined by the semisimple matrix$A$ (the "Satake parameter"). Under the local Langlands correspondence, they correspond to unramified (spherical) representations of$GL_n(F)$ — those with a$GL_n(\mathcal{O}_F)$-fixed vector.

**Example 3.** The Steinberg representation on the Galois side: the Weil–Deligne representation of rank 2 with $r(\sigma) = |\sigma|^{1/2} \cdot \mathrm{diag}(|\sigma|^{1/2}, |\sigma|^{-1/2})$ and$N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. This is the Weil–Deligne representation corresponding to the Steinberg representation of$GL_2(F)$ (the unique square-integrable non-supercuspidal irreducible). The nilpotent operator$N$ records the non-trivial monodromy: the Galois representation$r$ is "almost reducible" (a sum of two characters twisted by$|\cdot|^{\pm 1/2}$), and$N$ "mixes" the two characters.

By the Jacobson–Morozov theorem, a nilpotent $N$ is always part of an$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple$(N, H, N^-)$. This means Weil–Deligne representations are equivalently described as representations of the product group$W_F \times SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ — the *Langlands parameter space*. Specifically,$(r, N)$ corresponds to$\tilde r: W_F \times SL_2(\mathbb{C}) \to GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ defined by$\tilde r(w, \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}) = r(w) \cdot \exp(b \cdot N)$ (schematically; more precisely, the$SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ factor acts via$N$).

\begin{proposition}
Frobenius-semisimple Weil–Deligne representations of rank $n$ of$W_F$ are in natural bijection with continuous representations$\tilde r: W_F \times SL_2(\mathbb{C}) \to GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ satisfying:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tilde r$ is continuous (for the discrete topology on$GL_n(\mathbb{C})$).
\item $\tilde r|_{W_F}$ is Frobenius-semisimple.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

**Example 4.** The Weil–Deligne representation associated to an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}_p$:
- *Good reduction* ($p \nmid N_E$):$r = V_\ell(E)$ is unramified,$N = 0$. The Weil–Deligne representation is just$r$ with$N = 0$ (trivial monodromy).
- *Multiplicative (semistable) reduction*: $r = \chi_{\mathrm{cycl}} \oplus \mathbf{1}$ (direct sum of the cyclotomic character and the trivial character),$N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. The non-trivial$N$ captures the non-trivial monodromy of the Tate curve parameterization$E \cong \mathbb{G}_m / q^{\mathbb{Z}}$.
- *Additive (wild) reduction*: more complex, with ramified $r$.

\begin{remark}
The Weil group is preferred over the full Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$ because it carries the "weight" filtration via the norm character$|\cdot|_F$: the eigenvalues of$\mathrm{Frob}_q$ on a Weil–Deligne representation have specific absolute values (Weil numbers), and this weight structure is essential for defining$L$-functions and$\varepsilon$-factors. The$L$-function of a Weil–Deligne representation$(r, N)$ is$L(s, r, N) = \det(1 - q^{-s} r(\mathrm{Frob}_q) | (V^{I_F})^{N=0})^{-1}$, where$V^{I_F}$ is the inertia-fixed part and$(V^{I_F})^{N=0}$ is the kernel of$N$ on that space. For unramified representations ($I_F$ acts trivially,$N = 0$), this reduces to$\det(1 - q^{-s} A)^{-1}$ where$A = r(\mathrm{Frob}_q)$.
\end{remark}
