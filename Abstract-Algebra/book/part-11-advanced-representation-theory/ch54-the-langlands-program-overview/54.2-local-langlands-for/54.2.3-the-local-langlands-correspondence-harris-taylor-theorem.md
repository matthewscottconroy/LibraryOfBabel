# 54.2.3 The Local Langlands Correspondence (Harris–Taylor Theorem)

The *local Langlands correspondence* for $GL_n$ over a non-archimedean local field$F$ is a theorem, proved by Harris–Taylor (2001) via the geometry of Shimura varieties and independently by Henniart (2000) via a numerical characterization. It asserts the existence of a canonical bijection$\mathrm{rec}_F$ between the two parameter sets: irreducible smooth representations of$GL_n(F)$, and Frobenius-semisimple Weil–Deligne representations of rank$n$ of$W_F$. The word "canonical" carries weight here: the bijection is not just any bijection, but one uniquely characterized by a list of compatibilities with natural operations on both sides.

\begin{theorem}[Local Langlands Correspondence for $GL_n$, Harris–Taylor 2001 / Henniart 2000]
For each $n \geq 1$ and each non-archimedean local field$F$, there exists a unique bijection
$$\mathrm{rec}_F: \left\{ \begin{array}{c} \text{irreducible smooth} \\ \text{representations of } GL_n(F) \end{array} \right\} \xrightarrow{\sim} \left\{ \begin{array}{c} \text{Frobenius-semisimple WD} \\ \text{representations of } W_F \text{ of rank } n \end{array} \right\}$$
satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $n = 1$,$\mathrm{rec}_F =$ local Artin map (class field theory):$F^* \xrightarrow{\sim} W_F^{ab}$.
\item Compatibility with twisting: $\mathrm{rec}_F(\pi \otimes (\chi \circ \det)) = \mathrm{rec}_F(\pi) \otimes (\chi \circ r_{F})$, where$r_F: W_F \to F^*$ is the local Artin map.
\item $L$-factor compatibility:$L(s, \pi \times \pi') = L(s, \mathrm{rec}_F(\pi) \otimes \mathrm{rec}_F(\pi'))$ for all irreducible$\pi, \pi'$.
\item $\varepsilon$-factor compatibility:$\varepsilon(s, \pi \times \pi', \psi) = \varepsilon(s, \mathrm{rec}_F(\pi) \otimes \mathrm{rec}_F(\pi'), \psi)$ for all$\pi, \pi'$ and all additive characters$\psi: F \to \mathbb{C}^*$.
\item Supercuspidals correspond to irreducible WD representations: $\pi$ supercuspidal iff$\mathrm{rec}_F(\pi) = (r, 0)$ with$r$ irreducible.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

**Example 1.** The $GL_1$ case:$\mathrm{rec}_F$ is the local Artin map$F^* \to W_F^{ab}$, sending$p \in F^*$ (uniformizer) to the geometric Frobenius$\mathrm{Frob}_q^{-1} \in W_F^{ab}$, and$u \in \mathcal{O}_F^*$ to its image in the inertia group. The$L$-factor:$L(s, \chi) = (1 - \chi(\varpi_F) q^{-s})^{-1}$ for an unramified character$\chi$ (where$\varpi_F$ is the uniformizer). This matches the Artin$L$-function of the Weil group character$\mathrm{rec}_F(\chi)$.

**Example 2.** For $GL_2$ and the unramified principal series$\pi = \mathrm{Ind}_B^{GL_2}(\chi_1 \boxtimes \chi_2)$ with unramified characters$\chi_i$ determined by their values$\chi_i(\varpi_F) = \alpha_i \in \mathbb{C}^*$: the correspondence sends$\pi$ to the Weil–Deligne representation$r(\mathrm{Frob}_q^{-1}) = \mathrm{diag}(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ and$N = 0$. The$L$-factor:$L(s, \pi) = (1 - \alpha_1 q^{-s})^{-1} (1 - \alpha_2 q^{-s})^{-1}$, matching the$L$-factor of$\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$.

**Example 3.** For the Steinberg representation $\mathrm{St}_{GL_2}$ of$GL_2(F)$:$\mathrm{rec}_F(\mathrm{St}_{GL_2}) = (r, N)$ where$r(\sigma) = |\sigma|_F^{1/2} \cdot \mathrm{diag}(|\sigma|_F^{1/2}, |\sigma|_F^{-1/2})$ and$N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (the unipotent monodromy). The$L$-factor:$L(s, \mathrm{St}_{GL_2}) = (1 - q^{-s})^{-1}$ (the$L$-factor of the unipotent Weil–Deligne representation), which is the$L$-factor of an elliptic curve with multiplicative reduction at$p$.

The proof via Harris–Taylor uses global methods: they embed the local situation into a global one (choosing a global CM field and a specific unitary Shimura variety), count points over finite fields using the Lefschetz trace formula, and identify the local correspondence through the resulting Galois-automorphic comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Henniart's Numerical Criterion]
There is at most one bijection $\mathrm{rec}_F$ satisfying conditions (1)–(5) above. Henniart proved this uniqueness, and used it to give an alternative proof of the correspondence by checking the numerical conditions without using Shimura varieties.
\end{theorem}

**Example 4.** The local-global compatibility: in the global Langlands correspondence, if $\pi$ is a cuspidal automorphic representation of$GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$ and$\rho: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K) \to GL_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ is the corresponding Galois representation, then the local compatibility requires:
$$\mathrm{rec}_{K_v}(\pi_v) \cong \rho|_{W_{K_v}} \quad \text{for all places } v,$$
where $\pi_v$ is the local component of$\pi$ at$v$ and$\rho|_{W_{K_v}}$ is the restriction of$\rho$ to the local Weil group. This compatibility is what makes the local Langlands correspondence a "building block" for the global correspondence: the global correspondence is essentially the compatibility of all local correspondences.

\begin{remark}
The proof of the local Langlands correspondence for $GL_n$ via Shimura varieties exemplifies a recurring strategy in the Langlands program: use global methods (geometry of Shimura varieties, Galois cohomology, the Weil conjectures) to establish local results. The local correspondence cannot currently be proved by purely local means (at least not by methods as clean as the global approach), reflecting the deep entanglement between local and global phenomena in number theory. This is in sharp contrast to the$GL_1$ case, where the local-global relationship is transparent from the explicit Artin map.
\end{remark}
