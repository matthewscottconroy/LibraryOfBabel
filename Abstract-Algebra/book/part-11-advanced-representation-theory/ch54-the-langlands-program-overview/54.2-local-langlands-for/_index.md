# 54.2 Local Langlands for $GL_n$

The local Langlands correspondence for $GL_n(F)$, where $F$ is a non-archimedean local field (a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ or $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$), is one of the great theorems of the late twentieth century. Proved by Harris–Taylor and Henniart in 2001 (with a different proof later by Scholze), it asserts a canonical bijection between two sets: the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible smooth representations of $GL_n(F)$ over $\mathbb{C}$, and the set of isomorphism classes of $n$-dimensional Frobenius-semisimple Weil–Deligne representations of the Weil group $W_F$. Both sides are infinite sets — there are infinitely many irreducible representations of $GL_n(F)$ and infinitely many Weil–Deligne representations of $W_F$ — but the bijection is canonical, is compatible with twisting by characters, with duality, and with $L$-functions and $\varepsilon$-factors, and it specializes to the local Artin map when $n = 1$.

The objects on the two sides of the correspondence require some unpacking. An irreducible smooth representation $(\pi, V)$ of $GL_n(F)$ is a representation of the locally compact totally disconnected group $GL_n(F)$ on a complex vector space $V$ such that every vector has open stabilizer (smoothness) and $V$ contains no proper nontrivial $GL_n(F)$-stable subspace (irreducibility). The classification of such representations is given by the Bernstein–Zelevinsky theory: every irreducible smooth representation is a quotient of a parabolic induction from a supercuspidal representation, and the supercuspidal representations are the "atoms" — those not arising by parabolic induction from any proper Levi subgroup. On the Galois side, the Weil group $W_F$ is a dense subgroup of $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$ generated by a lift of the Frobenius $\mathrm{Frob}_F$; a Weil–Deligne representation is a pair $(\rho, N)$ where $\rho: W_F \to GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ is a representation (with open kernel) and $N \in \mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{C})$ is a nilpotent operator satisfying $\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_F) N \rho(\mathrm{Frob}_F)^{-1} = |F|^{-1} N$. The nilpotent operator $N$ encodes the monodromy of the representation and has no analogue on the automorphic side in the unramified case.

The local Langlands correspondence intertwines the natural invariants on both sides. Under the bijection $\pi \leftrightarrow (\rho_\pi, N_\pi)$: the central character $\omega_\pi: F^\times \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ equals $\det \rho_\pi$ via the local Artin map; the contragredient $\tilde{\pi}$ corresponds to the contragredient $(\tilde{\rho}_\pi, -\tilde{N}_\pi)$; the local $L$-factor $L(\pi, s)$ equals $L(\rho_\pi, s)$ and the local $\varepsilon$-factor $\varepsilon(\pi, s, \psi)$ equals $\varepsilon(\rho_\pi, s, \psi)$, where $\psi: F \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ is an additive character. These compatibilities — proved by Henniart as the "numerical local Langlands correspondence" before the full bijection was established — show that the correspondence is not merely a bijection of sets but a deep structural fact about how the two worlds of harmonic analysis and Galois theory are related.

The proof of the local Langlands correspondence for $GL_n$ requires the full machinery of $\ell$-adic cohomology of Shimura varieties (in the Harris–Taylor approach) or the theory of perfectoid spaces and diamonds (in Scholze's approach). The key idea in both proofs is to realize the correspondence geometrically: both sides appear simultaneously in the cohomology of certain moduli spaces (the Lubin–Tate tower or the Drinfeld upper half-plane), and the Galois action on the cohomology is matched with the $GL_n$-action to produce the bijection. This geometric realization of the local correspondence is not just a proof strategy; it is a deeper structural fact about the nature of the correspondence itself, and it points toward the global geometric Langlands program.
