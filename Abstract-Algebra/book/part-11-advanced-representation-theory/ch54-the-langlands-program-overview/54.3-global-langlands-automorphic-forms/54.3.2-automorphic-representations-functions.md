# 54.3.2 Automorphic Representations; $L$-Functions

The analytic heart of the global Langlands correspondence is the theory of *$L$-functions*. To each automorphic representation$\pi$ of$GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$, and to each$n$-dimensional Galois representation$\sigma: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K) \to GL_n(\mathbb{C})$, one can attach an$L$-function — a Dirichlet series with an Euler product expansion — that encodes arithmetic information. The Langlands correspondence predicts that these two types of$L$-functions match, and this equality is both a test of the conjecture and, in many cases, the only known method for establishing the correspondence.

\begin{definition}
The *automorphic $L$-function*$L(s, \pi) = \prod_v L(s, \pi_v)$ is defined as an Euler product over all places$v$ of$K$:
\begin{itemize}
\item At a non-archimedean place where $\pi_v$ is spherical with Satake parameters$\alpha_{v,1}, \ldots, \alpha_{v,n}$:
$L(s, \pi_v) = \prod_{i=1}^n (1 - \alpha_{v,i} q_v^{-s})^{-1}$.
\item At a ramified non-archimedean place: $L(s, \pi_v) = L(s, \mathrm{rec}_{K_v}(\pi_v))$ (via the local Langlands correspondence).
\item At an archimedean place: $L(s, \pi_v) = \prod_j \Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s - \mu_j)$ or$\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s - \mu_j)$ for Langlands parameters$\mu_j$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** For $GL_1(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})$ and a Dirichlet character$\chi: (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^* \to \mathbb{C}^*$ (viewed as an automorphic representation of$GL_1(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})$): the$L$-function is$L(s, \chi) = \prod_{p \nmid N} (1 - \chi(p) p^{-s})^{-1} \cdot (\text{ramified factors at } p \mid N) = \sum_{n \geq 1} \chi(n) n^{-s}$, the Dirichlet$L$-function. The functional equation is$L(s, \chi) = \varepsilon(\chi) \cdot (N/2\pi)^{1/2-s} \cdot \Gamma(s/2 + \delta)/\Gamma((1-s)/2 + \delta) \cdot L(1-s, \bar\chi)$, where$\delta = (1 - \chi(-1))/2$.

**Example 2.** For a cuspidal modular eigenform $f = \sum a_n q^n$ of weight$k$ and level$N$: the$L$-function is$L(s, \pi_f) = \sum a_n n^{-s}$, the classical$L$-function of the modular form. The Euler product at$p \nmid N$ is$(1 - \alpha_p p^{-s})^{-1}(1 - \beta_p p^{-s})^{-1}$ where$\alpha_p + \beta_p = a_p$ and$\alpha_p \beta_p = p^{k-1}$ (the Hecke eigenvalues and the weight). The archimedean factor is$L(s, \pi_\infty) = \Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s + (k-1)/2) = 2(2\pi)^{-(s+(k-1)/2)} \Gamma(s + (k-1)/2)$. The completed$L$-function$\Lambda(s, f) = N^{s/2} L_\infty(s, f) L(s, f)$ satisfies the functional equation$\Lambda(s, f) = \varepsilon(f) \Lambda(1, f)$ (where$\varepsilon(f) = \pm 1$ is the root number).

The global Langlands conjecture for $GL_n$ over$K$ asserts: for every irreducible$n$-dimensional Galois representation (or$\ell$-adic representation)$\sigma: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K) \to GL_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$, there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation$\pi$ of$GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$ such that$L(s, \sigma) = L(s, \pi)$.

**Example 3.** The Artin conjecture: for an irreducible $n$-dimensional complex Galois representation$\rho: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ ($n > 1$), the Artin$L$-function$L(s, \rho) = \prod_p \det(1 - p^{-s} \rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p) | \rho^{I_p})^{-1}$ should be entire (have no poles). This is implied by the Langlands correspondence: if$\rho$ corresponds to a cuspidal automorphic representation$\pi$, then$L(s, \rho) = L(s, \pi)$ is entire (cuspidal$L$-functions are entire). The$n = 2$ case of the Artin conjecture, for representations$\rho$ with solvable image, was proved by Langlands and Tunnell (1983) and was used by Wiles in his proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Rankin–Selberg Method]
For two cuspidal automorphic representations $\pi$ of$GL_m(\mathbb{A}_K)$ and$\pi'$ of$GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$, the *Rankin–Selberg$L$-function*$L(s, \pi \times \pi') = \prod_v L(s, \pi_v \times \pi'_v)$ has a meromorphic continuation to all of$\mathbb{C}$, with at most a simple pole at$s = 1$ (which occurs iff$m = n$ and$\pi' \cong \tilde\pi$). The analytic properties of$L(s, \pi \times \pi')$ are a key tool for establishing the functoriality of automorphic representations.
\end{theorem}

**Example 4.** For $m = n = 2$ and$f, g$ cuspidal eigenforms, the Rankin–Selberg$L$-function$L(s, f \times g) = \zeta(2s) \sum_n a_n(f) \overline{a_n(g)} n^{1-2s}$ (roughly) is related to the classical Rankin–Selberg convolution. The pole at$s = 1$ iff$f = g$ (self-convolution) — a non-trivial fact used in applications like bounding the size of Fourier coefficients of modular forms.

\begin{remark}
The equality $L(s, \sigma) = L(s, \pi)$ of Galois and automorphic$L$-functions is not merely a numerical coincidence — it encodes a deep structural identity. On the Galois side,$L(s, \sigma)$ is defined arithmetically via the Frobenius elements at each prime (counting points on varieties over finite fields). On the automorphic side,$L(s, \pi)$ is defined analytically via Hecke operators and harmonic analysis on$GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$. The fact that these two constructions, arising from completely different branches of mathematics, produce the same function is what the Langlands correspondence asserts — and the equality of$L$-functions is both the deepest motivation for the conjecture and the most powerful consequence of its validity.
\end{remark}
