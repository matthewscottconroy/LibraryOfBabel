# 54.3.3 The Fontaine–Mazur Conjecture; Galois Representations

Not every $\ell$-adic Galois representation should correspond to an automorphic form — the Langlands correspondence is expected to hold only for representations that "come from geometry" in a precise sense. The *Fontaine–Mazur conjecture* (1995) gives a precise characterization of which$\ell$-adic representations are geometric and, hence, which should be automorphic.

\begin{definition}
A continuous $\ell$-adic representation$\rho: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K) \to GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ is *geometric* if:
\begin{itemize}
\item It is almost everywhere unramified: $\rho$ is unramified at all but finitely many primes$v$.
\item It is *de Rham* (or Hodge–Tate) at primes $v \mid \ell$: the restriction$\rho|_{G_{K_v}}$ is a de Rham representation in the sense of$p$-adic Hodge theory (the period module$D_{dR}(\rho) = (\rho \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} B_{dR})^{G_{K_v}}$ is free of rank$n$ over$K_v$).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** The $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character$\chi_\ell: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^*$, defined by$\sigma(\zeta_{\ell^n}) = \zeta_{\ell^n}^{\chi_\ell(\sigma)}$, is geometric. It is unramified at all$p \neq \ell$ (since$\sigma$ acts on$\zeta_{\ell^n}$ only through the$\ell$-part), and it is de Rham at$\ell$ with Hodge–Tate weight 1 (the de Rham cohomology class of$dz/z$ on$\mathbb{G}_m$). The corresponding automorphic representation is the "algebraic part of the norm character" of$GL_1$.

**Example 2.** The $\ell$-adic Tate module of an elliptic curve$E/\mathbb{Q}$:$V_\ell(E) = T_\ell(E) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell} \mathbb{Q}_\ell$, a 2-dimensional$\ell$-adic representation. This is geometric: unramified at all$p$ of good reduction for$E$ (hence all but finitely many), and de Rham at$\ell$ with Hodge–Tate weights$\{0, 1\}$ (corresponding to the holomorphic and antiholomorphic differentials on$E$). The Fontaine–Mazur conjecture predicts that$V_\ell(E)$ comes from a cuspidal automorphic form for$GL_2$ — confirmed by the Modularity Theorem.

\begin{conjecture}[Fontaine–Mazur, 1995]
Every irreducible geometric $\ell$-adic representation$\rho: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K) \to GL_n(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ arises from the étale cohomology of a smooth proper variety over$K$:
$$\rho \cong \mathrm{sub-quotient\ of}\ H^k_{\text{ét}}(X_{\overline{K}}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)(m)$$
for some smooth proper $X/K$,$k \geq 0$, and Tate twist$m$.
\end{conjecture}

Combined with the expected Langlands correspondence, this predicts: every irreducible geometric Galois representation is automorphic.

The de Rham condition at $v \mid \ell$ controls the Hodge–Tate weights of$\rho$.

\begin{definition}
For a de Rham representation $\rho|_{G_{K_v}}$, the *Hodge–Tate weights* are the integers$h_1 \leq h_2 \leq \cdots \leq h_n$ such that$D_{dR}(\rho) \cong \bigoplus_i K_v \cdot e_i$ with$e_i$ in filtration degree$h_i$ (the graded pieces of the Hodge filtration). For the Tate module of an abelian variety of dimension$g$, the Hodge–Tate weights are$\{0^g, 1^g\}$ (each with multiplicity$g$).
\end{definition}

**Example 3.** For the symmetric power $\mathrm{Sym}^k(V_\ell(E))$ ($E$ an elliptic curve), the$\ell$-adic representation has Hodge–Tate weights$\{0, 1, 2, \ldots, k\}$ (each with multiplicity 1). By Fontaine–Mazur and the Langlands correspondence, this should correspond to a cuspidal automorphic representation of$GL_{k+1}$. For$k = 1$, this is the modularity of$E$. For$k = 2$, the symmetric square$\mathrm{Sym}^2(V_\ell(E))$ should correspond to an automorphic form on$GL_3$ — and this "symmetric square lift" was constructed by Shimura and Gelbart–Jacquet.

**Example 4.** The *de Rham condition* is essential for the conjecture. Without it, there are many "pathological" $\ell$-adic representations: for any$\lambda \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ with$|\lambda| = 1$, the character$\sigma \mapsto \lambda^{v(\chi_\ell(\sigma))}$ (where$v$ is the$\ell$-adic valuation) is an$\ell$-adic character that is almost everywhere unramified but is NOT de Rham at$\ell$ (for general$\lambda$). Such characters are not automorphic and do not come from geometry.

\begin{theorem}[Partial Fontaine–Mazur, various]
For $n = 1$: the Fontaine–Mazur conjecture is implied by class field theory (de Rham 1-dimensional representations over$\mathbb{Q}$ are Hecke characters). For$n = 2$ over$\mathbb{Q}$: the Fontaine–Mazur conjecture (that odd, irreducible, geometric 2-dimensional representations of$\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ are automorphic) follows from the proof of the Serre conjecture by Khare–Wintenberger (2009). For$GL_n$ over totally real fields: significant progress via the$p$-adic Langlands program and modularity lifting theorems (Kisin, Emerton, Taylor, and others).
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The $p$-adic Hodge theory underlying the de Rham condition connects Galois representations to differential equations via Fontaine's period rings$B_{cris}$,$B_{st}$,$B_{dR}$. A representation is:
\begin{itemize}
\item *Hodge–Tate* if $\rho \otimes B_{HT} \cong B_{HT}^n$ (very weak condition).
\item *de Rham* if $\rho \otimes B_{dR} \cong B_{dR}^n$ (strong condition, implies Hodge–Tate).
\item *crystalline* if $\rho \otimes B_{cris} \cong B_{cris}^n$ (strongest: corresponds to good reduction at$p$).
\item *semistable* if $\rho \otimes B_{st} \cong B_{st}^n$ (corresponds to semistable reduction).
\end{itemize}
These conditions correspond precisely to the type of reduction of the variety $X$ at$p$: the Tate module of$E$ is crystalline at$p$ iff$E$ has good reduction at$p$, semistable iff semistable reduction. This connection between$p$-adic algebra and the geometry of reduction is one of the deepest in modern number theory.
\end{remark}
