# 54.3.4 The Langlands Functoriality Conjecture

The individual Langlands correspondences — for $GL_n$, for other reductive groups, over various fields — do not exist in isolation. They are unified by a single overarching meta-conjecture called *Langlands functoriality*, which predicts that the automorphic representations of different groups are systematically related whenever their$L$-groups are related. Functoriality is arguably the most powerful single conjecture in modern number theory: a large fraction of the deepest results and conjectures in the field — base change, symmetric power lifts, the Ramanujan conjecture, the Riemann hypothesis for$L$-functions — are either consequences of or closely related to special cases of functoriality.

\begin{conjecture}[Langlands Functoriality]
Let $G$ and$H$ be connected reductive algebraic groups over a global field$K$, with$L$-groups${}^L G = \hat G \rtimes W_K$ and${}^L H = \hat H \rtimes W_K$. Given a homomorphism$\phi: {}^L H \to {}^L G$ of$L$-groups (compatible with the projections to$W_K$), there exists a *functorial transfer* of automorphic representations: for every automorphic representation$\pi_H$ of$H(\mathbb{A}_K)$, there is an automorphic representation$\pi_G = \phi_*(\pi_H)$ of$G(\mathbb{A}_K)$ such that$\mathrm{rec}_{K_v}(\pi_{G,v}) = \phi \circ \mathrm{rec}_{K_v}(\pi_{H,v})$ at every place$v$.
\end{conjecture}

**Example 1.** *Automorphic induction* (for $G = GL_n$,$H = GL_1$ over an extension): Let$L/K$ be a degree-$n$ extension of number fields,$G = GL_n$ over$K$,$H = GL_1$ over$L$. The$L$-group map$\phi: \hat{GL}_1^L \rtimes W_K \to \hat{GL}_n^K \rtimes W_K$ comes from the induced representation$\mathrm{Ind}_{W_L}^{W_K}: \mathrm{Rep}(W_L) \to \mathrm{Rep}(W_K)$. Functoriality predicts: given a Hecke character$\chi: L^* \backslash \mathbb{A}_L^* \to \mathbb{C}^*$, there is an automorphic representation$\mathrm{AI}_{L/K}(\chi)$ of$GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$. This *automorphic induction* is known for cyclic$L/K$ (Langlands, Arthur–Clozel) and for general$L/K$ when$n = 2$ (Jacquet–Langlands for base change of$GL_2$).

**Example 2.** *Symmetric power lifts* (for $GL_2 \to GL_{n+1}$): The$n$-th symmetric power representation$\mathrm{Sym}^n: GL_2(\mathbb{C}) \to GL_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})$ gives an$L$-group map${}^L GL_2 = GL_2(\mathbb{C}) \times W_K \to {}^L GL_{n+1} = GL_{n+1}(\mathbb{C}) \times W_K$. Functoriality predicts: for every cuspidal automorphic representation$\pi$ of$GL_2(\mathbb{A}_K)$, there is an automorphic representation$\mathrm{Sym}^n(\pi)$ of$GL_{n+1}(\mathbb{A}_K)$ with$L$-functions satisfying$L(s, \mathrm{Sym}^n(\pi)) = L(s, \pi, \mathrm{Sym}^n)$. This has been proved for:
- $n = 2$: Shimura (1975), Gelbart–Jacquet (1978).
- $n = 3$: Kim–Shahidi (2002).
- $n = 4$: Kim (2003).
- $n \geq 5$: open in general (partial results via potential automorphy, Newton–Thorne 2021 for$GL_2$ over$\mathbb{Q}$).

**Example 3.** *Base change* (for $G = H$, but over a field extension): For a cyclic Galois extension$L/K$ of degree$n$ and a reductive group$G$ over$K$, base change is the functorial transfer from automorphic representations of$G(\mathbb{A}_K)$ to automorphic representations of$G(\mathbb{A}_L)$ along the natural map${}^L G_L \to {}^L G_K$ (inclusion of$L$-groups via$W_L \subset W_K$). For$G = GL_n$ and$L/K$ cyclic, base change is known (Langlands for$GL_2$ over solvable extensions, Arthur–Clozel for$GL_n$ over cyclic extensions). The Langlands–Tunnell theorem (1983) — base change for$GL_2$ over solvable extensions — was the key input for Wiles's proof of the Shimura–Taniyama conjecture for semistable elliptic curves.

The breadth of what functoriality encapsulates is staggering.

\begin{proposition}
The Ramanujan conjecture (that Satake parameters of cuspidal $GL_n$ automorphic forms are pure of weight 0) follows from functoriality: if all symmetric power lifts$\mathrm{Sym}^k(\pi)$ are automorphic and cuspidal, the Ramanujan conjecture for$\pi$ follows from the analytic properties of the symmetric power$L$-functions.
\end{proposition}

**Example 4.** The *Jacquet–Langlands correspondence*: for a quaternion algebra $D/K$, the group$D^*$ is an inner form of$GL_2$, with the same$L$-group:${}^L(D^*) = {}^L(GL_2) = GL_2(\mathbb{C}) \times W_K$. Functoriality for the identity map${}^L(D^*) = {}^L(GL_2)$ predicts a lifting from automorphic representations of$(D \otimes_K \mathbb{A}_K)^*$ to those of$GL_2(\mathbb{A}_K)$. This is the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence (proved for$GL_2$ by Jacquet–Langlands 1970, and for$GL_n$ by Rogawski–Vigneras–Badulescu): every automorphic representation of$(D \otimes_K \mathbb{A}_K)^*$ lifts to a discrete-series automorphic representation of$GL_2(\mathbb{A}_K)$ ramified exactly at the places where$D$ is ramified.

\begin{remark}
The vast majority of cases of functoriality remain conjectural. The proved cases — cyclic base change (Langlands, Arthur–Clozel), Jacquet–Langlands, and low symmetric powers for $GL_2$ (Kim, Shahidi) — have each required major new techniques. The primary tools are the *trace formula* (Arthur's stabilization of the Selberg trace formula), which relates automorphic representations to Galois representations via their$L$-functions, and the *converse theorem* (Cogdell–Piatetski-Shapiro), which produces automorphic representations from$L$-functions with the right analytic properties. The combination of these tools — together with$p$-adic methods, potential automorphy, and the geometric Langlands program — is gradually expanding the known cases of functoriality, though a proof in full generality remains a distant goal.
\end{remark}
