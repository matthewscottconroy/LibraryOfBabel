# 54.3 Global Langlands: Automorphic Forms

The global Langlands program is the most ambitious part of the web of conjectures and, for number fields, the least understood. The central conjecture asserts a correspondence between $n$-dimensional representations of the absolute Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$ of a number field $F$ and cuspidal automorphic representations of $GL_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$. Both sides are large and complex: the Galois representations are $\ell$-adic (continuous homomorphisms $\rho: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F) \to GL_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$) satisfying certain regularity conditions (algebraicity, purity, or Hodge–Tate conditions), while the automorphic representations are irreducible constituents of $L^2(GL_n(F) \backslash GL_n(\mathbb{A}_F))$ that are eigenvectors for all Hecke operators. The correspondence should match them up in a way compatible with all local Langlands correspondences simultaneously: for every prime $p$, the local component $\pi_p$ of the automorphic representation (an irreducible smooth representation of $GL_n(F_p)$) should correspond to the local Galois representation $\rho|_{W_{F_p}}$ via the local Langlands bijection.

Automorphic forms are the analytic objects on the automorphic side. A classical modular form of weight $k$ and level $N$ is a holomorphic function $f: \mathfrak{H} \to \mathbb{C}$ on the upper half-plane satisfying the transformation law $f(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}) = (cz+d)^k f(z)$ for $\binom{a\,b}{c\,d} \in \Gamma_0(N)$. From the adèlic perspective, such an $f$ gives rise to an automorphic form $\phi_f: GL_2(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash GL_2(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) \to \mathbb{C}$ that transforms by $K_0(N)$ on the right and by the character $y \mapsto y^k$ on the archimedean component. The automorphic representation $\pi_f$ generated by $\phi_f$ is an irreducible constituent of $L^2(GL_2(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash GL_2(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}))$, and its Hecke eigenvalues (the eigenvalues $a_p(f)$ of the Hecke operators $T_p$) determine the local components $\pi_p$ of $\pi_f$. The global Langlands correspondence, in this case, assigns to $f$ a compatible family of $\ell$-adic Galois representations $\rho_{f,\ell}: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to GL_2(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ with $\mathrm{tr}(\rho_{f,\ell}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)) = a_p(f)$ for $p \nmid N\ell$.

The Fontaine–Mazur conjecture specifies exactly which Galois representations should arise from automorphic forms. It asserts that an irreducible continuous representation $\rho: \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to GL_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ comes from the cohomology of an algebraic variety over $\mathbb{Q}$ (and hence from an automorphic form, by the Langlands correspondence) if and only if it is geometric: unramified at all but finitely many primes, and de Rham (equivalently, Hodge–Tate) at $\ell$. This conjecture, if proved, would give a complete characterization of the geometric Galois representations — the "interesting" ones from the automorphic perspective — in purely local terms at the prime $\ell$. It represents one of the deepest open problems in number theory, known in only special cases.

The Langlands functoriality conjecture is a vast generalization of the correspondence itself. For two reductive groups $G$ and $H$ over a number field $F$, and a homomorphism $\rho: \hat{H} \to \hat{G}$ between their Langlands dual groups, functoriality predicts a transfer of automorphic representations: every automorphic representation $\pi$ of $H(\mathbb{A}_F)$ should give rise to an automorphic representation $\Pi = \rho(\pi)$ of $G(\mathbb{A}_F)$, with the property that the local components are related by the local Langlands correspondence under $\rho$. Functoriality unifies an enormous range of classical results — the theory of base change, symmetric power lifts, and endoscopy — and its proof in special cases has been one of the primary engines of progress in the Langlands program. The proof of functoriality for $GL_2$ to $GL_3$ (by Kim and Shahidi) and $GL_2$ to $GL_4$ uses the theory of automorphic $L$-functions and the converse theorem, and it represents the current frontier of what is known.
