# 54.4.1 Curves over $\mathbb{F}_q$; Function Field Analogy

One of the most productive analogies in all of mathematics is the analogy between number fields and function fields. A *number field* is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$; a *function field* of a curve is the field$K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$ of rational functions on a smooth projective curve$C$ over a finite field$\mathbb{F}_q$. These two types of global fields share a remarkable amount of structure: both have rings of integers (algebraic integers vs. regular functions), both have finite residue fields at every place, and both have an adèle ring and an idèle class group. Class field theory and the Langlands program have perfect analogues in the function field setting.

The key advantage of function fields is that the abstract analytic objects of number theory become geometric.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\textbf{Number fields} & \textbf{Function fields} \\
\hline
Number field $K/\mathbb{Q}$ & Function field$K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$ \\
Ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$ & Regular functions on$C$ \\
Place $v$ of$K$ & Closed point$x \in C$ \\
Residue field $\mathbb{F}_{q_v}$ & Residue field$\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_x}}$ \\
$GL_n(K) \backslash GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$ &$\mathrm{Bun}_n(C)(\mathbb{F}_q)$ \\
Galois representations & $\ell$-adic local systems on$C$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

**Example 1.** The simplest case: $C = \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_q}$, so$K = \mathbb{F}_q(t)$. The closed points of$C$ are the primes of$K$: the prime$(t - a)$ for$a \in \mathbb{F}_q$ (residue field$\mathbb{F}_q$) and the prime "at infinity"$1/t$ (also residue field$\mathbb{F}_q$). The ring of integers is$\mathbb{F}_q[t]$ (away from$\infty$). The analogue of$GL_n(\mathbb{Z}) \subset GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ is$GL_n(\mathbb{F}_q[t]) \subset GL_n(\mathbb{F}_q(t))$. The moduli space$\mathrm{Bun}_n(\mathbb{P}^1)$ of rank-$n$ vector bundles on$\mathbb{P}^1$ is classified by splitting type$\mathcal{O}(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathcal{O}(d_n)$ for integers$d_1 \geq d_2 \geq \cdots \geq d_n$; the rational points are$\mathrm{Bun}_n(\mathbb{P}^1)(\mathbb{F}_q) = \{$ isomorphism classes of rank-$n$ bundles$\}$.

The crucial geometrization: the double quotient $GL_n(K) \backslash GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K) / GL_n(\prod_x \mathcal{O}_x)$ equals the set of$\mathbb{F}_q$-rational points of the *moduli stack*$\mathrm{Bun}_n(C)$ of rank-$n$ vector bundles on$C$.

\begin{theorem}[Weil, Lafforgue]
The unramified automorphic representations of $GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$ (those spherical at every place) are in bijection with irreducible$n$-dimensional$\ell$-adic local systems on$C$ (continuous representations$\pi_1(C, \bar x) \to GL_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ unramified at every closed point).
\end{theorem}

**Example 2.** A local system $\mathcal{L}$ on$C = \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_q}$ that is unramified everywhere (étale local system on$\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \emptyset = \mathbb{P}^1$) must be trivial (since$\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_q}) = \hat{\mathbb{Z}} \to GL_n$ factors through the maximal pro-prime-to-char quotient). For a genus-1 curve (elliptic curve$E/\mathbb{F}_q$), there are non-trivial unramified local systems: the$\ell$-adic Tate module$T_\ell(E)$ (for$\ell \neq \text{char}$) is a 2-dimensional$\ell$-adic local system on$\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F}_q)$, and pulling it back to$E$ via the structure map gives an unramified local system.

Lafforgue's theorem (2002) proved the full Langlands correspondence over function fields.

\begin{theorem}[Lafforgue, 2002]
For any function field $K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$ over a finite field, the global Langlands correspondence holds for$GL_n$: there is a canonical bijection
$$\left\{ \begin{array}{c} \text{cuspidal automorphic representations} \\ \text{of } GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K) \end{array} \right\} \longleftrightarrow \left\{ \begin{array}{c} \text{irreducible } n\text{-dim } \ell\text{-adic} \\ \text{local systems on } C \end{array} \right\}$$
compatible with $L$-functions. For$n = 2$, this was proved by Drinfeld (1980).
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** The function field analogue of the Ramanujan conjecture: for a cuspidal automorphic representation $\pi$ of$GL_n(\mathbb{A}_K)$, the eigenvalues of$\mathrm{Frob}_x$ on the corresponding local system should be algebraic integers of absolute value$q_x^{(n-1)/2}$ (purity). This was proved by Lafforgue as a consequence of his correspondence: the local systems on curves over finite fields satisfy Purity by Deligne's theorem (Weil II), and the correspondence transfers this to the automorphic side.

The passage from function fields to geometry — replacing $\mathbb{F}_q$-rational points by the full algebraic stack over$\mathbb{C}$ — is what opens the door to geometric Langlands.

\begin{remark}
The function field analogy has been extraordinarily productive: Weil proved the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields by analogy with the classical Riemann hypothesis; Drinfeld defined shtukas (a function-field analogue of abelian varieties with complex multiplication) to prove the $GL_2$ Langlands correspondence; and Lafforgue used stacks of shtukas to prove the$GL_n$ case. The geometric Langlands program, which replaces$\mathbb{F}_q$-points by sheaves and works over$\mathbb{C}$, is the "universal" version of these results — it strips away the arithmetic and reveals the underlying categorical structure, which is expected to shed light on the number-field case via the analogy.
\end{remark}
