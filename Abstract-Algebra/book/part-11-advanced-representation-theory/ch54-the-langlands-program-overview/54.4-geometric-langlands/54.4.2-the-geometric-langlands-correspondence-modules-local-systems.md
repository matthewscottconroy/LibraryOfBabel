# 54.4.2 The Geometric Langlands Correspondence: $\mathcal{D}$-Modules$\leftrightarrow$ Local Systems

The *geometric Langlands correspondence* is a categorical lift of the function-field Langlands correspondence: instead of a bijection between sets (local systems and automorphic functions on $\mathrm{Bun}_n(C)(\mathbb{F}_q)$), it predicts an equivalence of categories. Working over$\mathbb{C}$ (rather than a finite field), one replaces functions on$\mathrm{Bun}_n(C)(\mathbb{F}_q)$ by$\mathcal{D}$-modules on$\mathrm{Bun}_n(C)$ (using the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence between$\mathcal{D}$-modules and perverse sheaves), and replaces$\ell$-adic local systems by de Rham local systems (flat vector bundles, or equivalently$\mathcal{D}$-modules on$C$).

\begin{definition}
A *$\hat G$-local system* on a smooth curve$C$ over$\mathbb{C}$ is a$\hat G$-torsor on$C$ with flat connection (equivalently, a principal$\hat G$-bundle with flat connection, or a representation$\pi_1(C, x) \to \hat G(\mathbb{C})$ for a basepoint$x$). For$\hat G = GL_n$, a$\hat G$-local system is an$n$-dimensional local system (flat vector bundle of rank$n$).
\end{definition}

The moduli stack of $\hat G$-local systems on$C$ is the *de Rham stack*$\mathrm{Loc}_{\hat G}(C) = \mathrm{Map}_{dR}(C, B\hat G)$. For$\hat G = GL_n$, it is the stack of flat rank-$n$ vector bundles on$C$.

\begin{definition}
A $\mathcal{D}$-module$\mathcal{F}$ on$\mathrm{Bun}_n(C)$ is a *Hecke eigensheaf* for a local system$\mathcal{E} \in \mathrm{Loc}_{GL_n}(C)$ if for every closed point$x \in C$, there is a functorial isomorphism
$$H_x \cdot \mathcal{F} \cong \mathcal{E}_x \boxtimes \mathcal{F},$$
where $H_x$ is the Hecke correspondence (modifying the vector bundle at$x$) and$\mathcal{E}_x$ is the fiber of$\mathcal{E}$ at$x$ (a vector space, or more precisely, an$n$-dimensional space).
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** For $n = 1$ (abelian geometric Langlands): The moduli stack$\mathrm{Bun}_1(C) = \mathrm{Pic}(C)$ is the Picard stack of line bundles on$C$. A$GL_1$-local system$\mathcal{E}$ on$C$ (a flat line bundle) gives a rank-1 Hecke eigensheaf on$\mathrm{Pic}(C)$: the$\mathcal{D}$-module of flat sections of the flat line bundle on$\mathrm{Pic}(C)$ induced from$\mathcal{E}$ by the Abel–Jacobi map. This is the geometric analogue of Hecke Grössencharacters — the$GL_1$ case of geometric Langlands, which is a theorem.

The geometric Langlands conjecture predicts:

\begin{conjecture}[Geometric Langlands, Beilinson–Drinfeld]
For every irreducible $GL_n$-local system$\mathcal{E} \in \mathrm{Loc}_{GL_n}(C)$, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) nonzero irreducible Hecke eigensheaf$\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{E}}$ on$\mathrm{Bun}_n(C)$. This defines a functor (or correspondence)
$$\mathcal{E} \mapsto \mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{E}}: \mathrm{Loc}_{GL_n}(C) \dashrightarrow \mathcal{D}\text{-mod}(\mathrm{Bun}_n(C)).$$
\end{conjecture}

**Example 2.** For $n = 2$ and$C = \mathbb{P}^1$ with marked points (the "ramified" case), the geometric Langlands correspondence produces Hecke eigensheaves on$\mathrm{Bun}_2(\mathbb{P}^1)$ starting from flat$GL_2$-connections on$\mathbb{P}^1$ with regular singularities at the marked points. This is related to the theory of *opers* (special$\hat G$-connections) and to the Gaudin model in mathematical physics. The Beilinson–Drinfeld *geometric Satake* construction produces Hecke eigensheaves for opers explicitly.

**Example 3.** The *Beilinson–Drinfeld D-modules* for geometric Langlands: the Hitchin fibration on $\mathrm{Bun}_n$ provides a commutative family of Hecke operators that diagonalize the eigensheaves. Beilinson and Drinfeld showed that the$\mathcal{D}$-modules on$\mathrm{Bun}_G$ corresponding to *opers* (specific$\hat G$-connections with a special flag structure) are Hecke eigensheaves. This construction uses the Lie algebra$\hat{\mathfrak{g}}$ (the Kac–Moody Lie algebra of$\mathfrak{g}$) at the critical level, connecting geometric Langlands to representation theory of affine Kac–Moody algebras.

The connection to the Beilinson–Bernstein theory of $\mathcal{D}$-modules (Chapter 52) is deep and not accidental.

\begin{theorem}[Beilinson–Drinfeld]
The localization theorem for affine Kac–Moody algebras at the critical level provides $\mathcal{D}$-modules on$\mathrm{Bun}_G$ that are Hecke eigensheaves: specifically, the critical-level localization of a$\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{crit}$-module (at the critical level$\kappa = -h^\vee$) produces a$\mathcal{D}$-module on$\mathrm{Bun}_G$ that is automatically a Hecke eigensheaf.
\end{theorem}

**Example 4.** The Frenkel–Ben-Zvi–Nadler version of geometric Langlands for $GL_n$ over$\mathbb{C}$: the geometric Langlands correspondence is expected to be an equivalence of derived categories
$$D^b(\mathcal{D}\text{-mod}(\mathrm{Bun}_G)) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{Loc}_{\hat G}(C))),$$
where the left side is derived $\mathcal{D}$-modules on$\mathrm{Bun}_G$ and the right side is quasi-coherent sheaves on the moduli of local systems. This categorical equivalence is the "full" geometric Langlands conjecture, much stronger than the pointwise statement about individual eigensheaves. For$GL_1$, this reduces to Fourier–Mukai transform on$\mathrm{Pic}(C)$ (a classical result). For higher$n$, it is an active area of research.

\begin{remark}
The geometric Langlands program has been proven for $GL_2$ (Drinfeld), for$GL_n$ over function fields (Lafforgue), and in various forms for$GL_n$ over$\mathbb{C}$ by Ben-Zvi–Nadler, Arinkin–Gaitsgory, and others. The full categorical formulation — as an equivalence of derived categories — was recently proved by Gaitsgory–collaborators as part of a multi-part work (2024), building on decades of foundational development. The geometric Langlands program is important not only as a mathematical end in itself, but also because it illuminates the structure of the arithmetic Langlands correspondence: many properties of automorphic representations that are difficult to see directly become transparent in the geometric setting.
\end{remark}
