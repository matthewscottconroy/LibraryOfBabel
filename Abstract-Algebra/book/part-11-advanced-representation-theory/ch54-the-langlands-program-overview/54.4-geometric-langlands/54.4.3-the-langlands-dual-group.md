# 54.4.3 The Langlands Dual Group ${}^L G$

One of the most surprising features of the Langlands program is that the correspondence for a reductive group $G$ is not between$G$-automorphic forms and representations of$G$'s Galois group, but between$G$-automorphic forms and representations of a *different* reductive group$\hat{G}$ — the *Langlands dual group*. The dual group$\hat{G}$ is defined by exchanging roots and coroots: if$G$ has root datum$(\mathbb{X}, \Phi, \mathbb{X}^\vee, \Phi^\vee)$, then$\hat{G}$ has root datum$(\mathbb{X}^\vee, \Phi^\vee, \mathbb{X}, \Phi)$. This interchange of roots and coroots is an involution on the set of root data, and the resulting duality is the foundation of the entire program.

\begin{definition}
For a connected reductive algebraic group $G$ with root datum$(X^*(T), \Phi, X_*(T), \Phi^\vee)$ (character lattice, roots, cocharacter lattice, coroots), the *Langlands dual group* (or *$L$-group*) is the connected reductive algebraic group$\hat G$ over$\mathbb{C}$ with root datum$(X_*(T), \Phi^\vee, X^*(T), \Phi)$. The *$L$-group* is${}^L G = \hat G(\mathbb{C}) \rtimes W_K$ where$W_K$ acts on$\hat G$ via pinned automorphisms induced by the Galois action on the root datum.
\end{definition}

**Example 1.** The self-dual groups:
- $GL_n$: The root datum of$GL_n$ has$X^*(T) = \mathbb{Z}^n$ (characters$e_i: \mathrm{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_n) \mapsto t_i$) and$X_*(T) = \mathbb{Z}^n$ (cocharacters), with roots$e_i - e_j$ and coroots$e_i^\vee - e_j^\vee$. Exchanging gives the same datum:$\hat{GL}_n = GL_n$. Thus$GL_n$ is Langlands self-dual.
- $G_2$: Self-dual (the root system of$G_2$ is self-dual: the long and short roots exchange, giving$G_2$ again).

**Example 2.** The non-self-dual classical groups:
- $SL_n$:$X^*(T) = \{(a_i) \in \mathbb{Z}^n : \sum a_i = 0\}$ (root lattice), coroots are the standard$e_i^\vee - e_j^\vee$. The dual:$\hat{SL}_n$ has character lattice$= X_*(T_{SL_n}) = \mathbb{Z}^n / \mathbb{Z}(1,\ldots,1)$, which is the cocharacter lattice of$PGL_n$. So$\widehat{SL}_n = PGL_n$.
- $SO_{2n+1}$ (type$B_n$): The root system of$B_n$ has dual the root system of$C_n$. So$\widehat{SO_{2n+1}} = Sp_{2n}$, and vice versa:$\widehat{Sp_{2n}} = SO_{2n+1}$.
- $SO_{2n}$ (type$D_n$): Self-dual:$\widehat{SO_{2n}} = SO_{2n}$ (the$D_n$ root system is self-dual).

\begin{theorem}[Satake Isomorphism]
For a split reductive group $G$ over a local field$F$, the Hecke algebra$\mathcal{H}(G(F), G(\mathcal{O}_F))$ of$G(\mathcal{O}_F)$-biinvariant compactly supported functions on$G(F)$ is canonically isomorphic to$\mathbb{C}[X_*(T)]^W$, the Weyl-invariant polynomial functions on the cocharacter lattice of$G$ — which is the representation ring of the dual group$\hat G$:
$$\mathcal{H}(G(F), G(\mathcal{O}_F)) \cong \mathrm{Rep}(\hat G).$$
\end{theorem}

**Example 3.** For $G = GL_2$ and$F = \mathbb{Q}_p$: the Hecke algebra$\mathcal{H}(GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p), GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p))$ is generated by$T_p = \mathrm{char}(GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p) \mathrm{diag}(p,1) GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p))$ (the "Hecke operator at$p$") and$S_p = \mathrm{char}(GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p) \mathrm{diag}(p,p) GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p))$ (the central operator). The Satake isomorphism sends$T_p \mapsto \alpha + \beta$ and$S_p \mapsto \alpha\beta$, where$\alpha, \beta$ are the tautological characters of the maximal torus of$\hat{GL}_2 = GL_2$. A spherical representation of$GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ with Satake parameters$(\alpha, \beta)$ has Hecke eigenvalues$\lambda(T_p) = \alpha + \beta$ and$\lambda(S_p) = \alpha\beta$.

The *geometric Satake equivalence* is the categorical and geometric enhancement of the Satake isomorphism.

\begin{theorem}[Mirkovic–Vilonen, building on Lusztig–Ginzburg]
For a connected reductive group $G$ over$\mathbb{C}$ with dual group$\hat G$, there is an equivalence of symmetric tensor categories:
$$P_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_G) \simeq \mathrm{Rep}(\hat G),$$
where $\mathrm{Gr}_G = G(\mathcal{K})/G(\mathcal{O})$ (with$\mathcal{K} = \mathbb{C}((t))$,$\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{C}[[t]]$) is the affine Grassmannian, and$P_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_G)$ is the abelian category of$G(\mathcal{O})$-equivariant perverse sheaves on$\mathrm{Gr}_G$.
\end{theorem}

**Example 4.** For $G = GL_2$: the affine Grassmannian$\mathrm{Gr}_{GL_2} = GL_2(\mathbb{C}((t))) / GL_2(\mathbb{C}[[t]])$ has connected components$\mathrm{Gr}_{GL_2}^d$ indexed by$d \in \mathbb{Z}$ (the determinant degree). The$GL_2(\mathbb{C}[[t]])$-orbits on each component are indexed by partitions$\lambda = (\lambda_1 \geq \lambda_2)$ of the same value$d = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2$; each orbit is a finite-dimensional variety$\overline{S^\lambda}$ (Schubert variety in the affine Grassmannian). The IC sheaves$\mathrm{IC}_\lambda$ on these orbits correspond, under geometric Satake, to the irreducible$\hat{GL}_2 = GL_2$-representation$V(\lambda)$ of highest weight$\lambda = (\lambda_1, \lambda_2)$.

\begin{remark}
The geometric Satake equivalence is a fundamental theorem that constructs the Langlands dual group $\hat G$ purely geometrically, from the topology of the affine Grassmannian of$G$. This is remarkable: the dual group, which in the classical Langlands program appears "by hand" via root datum duality, is here recovered from the perverse sheaves on a natural infinite-dimensional variety. The equivalence also explains why the local Langlands correspondence pairs representations of$G(F)$ with representations of$\hat G$: the Hecke eigensheaves on the automorphic side are parametrized by$\hat G$-local systems, and the geometric Satake equivalence provides the functor from$\hat G$-representations to Hecke operators on the automorphic side.
\end{remark}
