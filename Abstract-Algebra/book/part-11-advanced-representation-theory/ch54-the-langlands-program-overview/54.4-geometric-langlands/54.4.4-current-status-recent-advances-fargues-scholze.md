# 54.4.4 Current Status; Recent Advances (Fargues–Scholze)

The Langlands program, as of 2025–2026, has reached a remarkable juncture: several major branches have been proved, while the deepest cases remain open and are driving the development of entirely new mathematical theories. The landscape of known and unknown cases defines the frontiers of research in number theory and representation theory.

The *local Langlands correspondence for $GL_n$* over non-archimedean local fields is proven in full generality: Harris–Taylor (2001) proved it for$p$-adic local fields via global methods using Shimura varieties, and Henniart (2000) gave a second proof via a numerical characterization. Extensions to other classical groups —$GSp_4$, quasi-split classical groups — have been achieved by Arthur and others using the stabilization of the trace formula. The *global Langlands for$GL_2$ over$\mathbb{Q}$* is the content of the modularity theorem (Wiles–Taylor–Wiles, 1995), which proved that every elliptic curve over$\mathbb{Q}$ is modular, with Fermat's Last Theorem as the celebrated corollary. The *global Langlands for$GL_n$ over function fields* was proved by Lafforgue (2002), for which he was awarded the Fields Medal; his proof uses the geometric techniques of Drinfeld's shtukas and constructs the Galois representation attached to each automorphic representation. The *geometric Langlands over$\mathbb{C}$*, in its fully categorical form, has seen major recent progress: the work of Arinkin–Gaitsgory establishes the derived geometric Langlands conjecture for$GL_n$ in a precise formulation, and the program is moving toward a proof for general reductive$G$.

The most striking recent development is the *Fargues–Scholze program*, initiated in a 2021 preprint. Fargues and Scholze construct a "global" geometric object from purely local $p$-adic data: the *Fargues–Fontaine curve*$X_{FF}$, a curve in the world of Scholze's perfectoid geometry and diamonds whose closed points are in bijection with the Weil group representations of$\mathbb{Q}_p$. The local Langlands correspondence for$p$-adic fields, which is normally formulated as a bijection of sets, becomes in their framework the *geometric Langlands correspondence* on$X_{FF}$ — a Hecke eigensheaf problem. This unification of local arithmetic Langlands with geometric Langlands is the deepest synthesis yet achieved in the program, and it draws on Scholze's theory of perfectoid spaces, diamonds, and condensed mathematics in an essential way. The global number-field Langlands for$GL_n$ with$n \geq 3$ remains the central open problem, currently accessible only through potential automorphy theorems (Taylor and collaborators), which establish the correspondence for "most" Galois representations even if not all.
