# 54.4 Geometric Langlands

The geometric Langlands program is the version of the Langlands correspondence that replaces a number field with the function field of an algebraic curve, and then further replaces the analytic and arithmetic aspects with purely geometric ones. The function field of a smooth projective curve $C$ over a field $k$ plays the role of a number field: its completions at closed points (local fields) are formal Laurent series fields $k(v) = \mathbb{F}_{q^{d_v}}((t))$, its ring of adèles is $\mathbb{A}_C = \prod'_{v} k(v)$, and its "Galois group" (the étale fundamental group $\pi_1^{et}(C)$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, or the topological fundamental group $\pi_1(C)$ over $\mathbb{C}$) plays the role of the absolute Galois group. When $k = \mathbb{C}$, the function field analogy becomes an analogy between algebraic geometry (curves over $\mathbb{C}$) and number theory (number fields), and the Langlands correspondence becomes a statement about complex algebraic geometry — a statement that can be formulated precisely and, in many cases, proved.

In the geometric Langlands correspondence over $\mathbb{C}$, the objects on the Galois side are $\hat{G}$-local systems on $C$: locally constant sheaves of $\hat{G}$-torsors (where $\hat{G}$ is the Langlands dual group), or equivalently, flat $\hat{G}$-bundles on $C$. These are the geometric analogues of $n$-dimensional Galois representations. On the automorphic side, the objects are $\mathcal{D}$-modules on the moduli stack $\mathrm{Bun}_G(C)$ of algebraic $G$-bundles on $C$ — the geometric analogues of automorphic forms. The Beilinson–Drinfeld conjecture (now largely proved) asserts that there is an equivalence of derived categories $D(\mathcal{D}\text{-mod}(\mathrm{Bun}_G)) \simeq D(\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{LocSys}_{\hat{G}}))$, where $\mathrm{LocSys}_{\hat{G}}$ is the moduli stack of $\hat{G}$-local systems on $C$. This is an equivalence of triangulated categories — algebra on one side, geometry on the other — and it carries the structure of a Fourier–Mukai transform: it is given by a kernel object on the product $\mathrm{Bun}_G \times \mathrm{LocSys}_{\hat{G}}$.

The Fargues–Scholze program, announced in 2021, provides a new approach to the local Langlands program that is simultaneously more geometric and more general than earlier approaches. Fargues and Scholze construct a "curve" $X_{FF}$ in $p$-adic geometry — the Fargues–Fontaine curve — whose properties miraculously mimic those of a curve over $\mathbb{C}$: it is a smooth proper "curve" over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (in the sense of $p$-adic geometry), and $\mathrm{Bun}_G(X_{FF})$ is a reasonable geometric object. Their main conjecture, now proved in significant cases, is that the local Langlands correspondence for $G$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is realized geometrically as an equivalence of categories on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(X_{FF})$, in exact analogy with the geometric Langlands program over $\mathbb{C}$. This program, if completed, would give the most conceptual proof of the local Langlands correspondence and would establish a direct geometric link between the local and global Langlands programs.

The Langlands dual group $\hat{G}$ — the group whose representations parametrize the Galois/geometric side — is constructed by exchanging the root datum of $G$ for its dual: the roots of $G$ become the coroots of $\hat{G}$, and vice versa. For $G = GL_n$, the dual group is again $\hat{G} = GL_n(\mathbb{C})$; for $G = SL_n$, the dual group is $\hat{G} = PGL_n(\mathbb{C})$; for $G = SO_{2n+1}$, the dual group is $\hat{G} = Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$; for $G = Sp_{2n}$, the dual group is $\hat{G} = SO_{2n+1}(\mathbb{C})$. The Satake isomorphism, which identifies the spherical Hecke algebra $\mathcal{H}(G(F), G(\mathcal{O}_F))$ with the representation ring of $\hat{G}$, provides the clearest algebraic manifestation of this duality. The geometric Satake equivalence — an equivalence between the category of $G(\mathcal{O})$-equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian $Gr_G = G(\mathcal{K})/G(\mathcal{O})$ and the category of representations of $\hat{G}$ — is the geometric enhancement of the Satake isomorphism and is one of the foundational inputs to the geometric Langlands program.
