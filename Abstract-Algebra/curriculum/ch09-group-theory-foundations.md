## 9.1 The Group Axioms

### 9.1.1 Definition

A **group** is a set $G$ with a binary operation $\cdot: G \times G \to G$ (often written multiplicatively, sometimes additively as $+$) satisfying:

- **(G1) Closure:** $a \cdot b \in G$ for all $a, b \in G$
- **(G2) Associativity:** $(a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$
- **(G3) Identity:** $\exists e \in G,\, \forall a \in G,\, e \cdot a = a \cdot e = a$
- **(G4) Inverses:** $\forall a \in G,\, \exists a^{-1} \in G,\, a \cdot a^{-1} = a^{-1} \cdot a = e$

A group is **abelian** (or **commutative**) if additionally $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$ for all $a, b$.

### 9.1.2 Elementary Consequences

Provable from the axioms alone (not assumed):
- The identity $e$ is unique
- Each element has a unique inverse
- $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}$ (reversal of order)
- $(a^{-1})^{-1} = a$
- Left/right cancellation: $ab = ac \Rightarrow b = c$ and $ba = ca \Rightarrow b = c$

### 9.1.3 Order

The **order** of $G$ is $|G|$ (its cardinality). $G$ is **finite** or **infinite** accordingly.

The **order of an element** $a \in G$ is the smallest positive integer $n$ with $a^n = e$, written $\mathrm{ord}(a)$ or $|a|$. If no such $n$ exists, $a$ has **infinite order**.

## 9.2 Examples of Groups

### 9.2.1 Number Groups

| Group | Operation | Abelian? | Order |
|-------|-----------|----------|-------|
| $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ | Addition | Yes | $\infty$ |
| $(\mathbb{Q}, +)$ | Addition | Yes | $\infty$ |
| $(\mathbb{Q}^*, \cdot)$ | Multiplication | Yes | $\infty$ |
| $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, +)$ | Addition mod $n$ | Yes | $n$ |
| $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ | Mult mod $n$ | Yes | $\phi(n)$ |

### 9.2.2 Symmetric Groups

The **symmetric group** $S_n$ is the group of all **permutations** (bijections) of $\{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$, under composition. $|S_n| = n!$.

**Cycle notation:** $(1\; 3\; 5)(2\; 4)$ means $1 \mapsto 3 \mapsto 5 \mapsto 1$ and $2 \mapsto 4 \mapsto 2$.

A **transposition** is a 2-cycle $(i\; j)$. Every permutation is a product of transpositions.

**Sign homomorphism:** $\mathrm{sgn}: S_n \to \{+1, -1\}$; even permutations have $\mathrm{sgn} = +1$, odd have $-1$. The **alternating group** $A_n = \ker(\mathrm{sgn})$ has order $n!/2$ for $n \geq 2$.

### 9.2.3 Dihedral Groups

The **dihedral group** $D_n$ (or $D_{2n}$ in some notations) is the symmetry group of a regular $n$-gon. It has order $2n$, generated by a rotation $r$ of order $n$ and a reflection $s$ of order $2$:
$$D_n = \langle r, s \mid r^n = s^2 = e, srs^{-1} = r^{-1} \rangle$$

### 9.2.4 Matrix Groups

- $GL_n(F) = \{A \in M_{n \times n}(F) \mid \det A \neq 0\}$ — **general linear group**
- $SL_n(F) = \{A \in GL_n(F) \mid \det A = 1\}$ — **special linear group**
- $O(n) = \{A \in M_{n \times n}(\mathbb{R}) \mid A^T A = I\}$ — **orthogonal group**
- $SO(n) = O(n) \cap SL_n(\mathbb{R})$ — **special orthogonal group**
- $U(n)$ — **unitary group**

These are the symmetry groups of geometry and physics.

## 9.3 Subgroups

### 9.3.1 Definition and Subgroup Test

$H \subseteq G$ is a **subgroup** (written $H \leq G$) if $H$ is itself a group under the restriction of $G$'s operation.

**Two-step subgroup test:** $H \neq \emptyset$ and for all $a, b \in H$, $ab^{-1} \in H$.

**Finite subgroup test:** $H \neq \emptyset$ and $H$ is closed under the operation.

### 9.3.2 Examples

- $n\mathbb{Z} = \{nk \mid k \in \mathbb{Z}\} \leq \mathbb{Z}$
- $\{e\}$ and $G$ are always subgroups (trivial and improper)
- $A_n \leq S_n$
- $SL_n(F) \leq GL_n(F)$
- The center $Z(G) = \{g \in G \mid gx = xg \text{ for all } x\} \leq G$

### 9.3.3 Generating Sets and Cyclic Subgroups

For $a \in G$, the **cyclic subgroup** $\langle a \rangle = \{a^n \mid n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ is the smallest subgroup containing $a$.

A group is **cyclic** if $G = \langle g \rangle$ for some $g$ (a **generator**).

**Classification of cyclic groups:**
- If $|g| = n < \infty$: $\langle g \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$
- If $|g| = \infty$: $\langle g \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}$

## 9.4 Cosets and Lagrange's Theorem

### 9.4.1 Cosets

For $H \leq G$ and $a \in G$, the **left coset**:
$$aH = \{ah \mid h \in H\}$$

The **right coset**: $Ha = \{ha \mid h \in H\}$.

**Properties:**
- $a \in aH$ for all $a$
- $aH = bH \Leftrightarrow a^{-1}b \in H \Leftrightarrow a$ and $b$ are in the same left coset
- Two left cosets are equal or disjoint — they partition $G$

The collection of left cosets $G/H = \{aH \mid a \in G\}$ is the **left coset space**. Its cardinality is the **index** $[G:H]$.

### 9.4.2 Lagrange's Theorem

**Theorem (Lagrange):** If $G$ is finite and $H \leq G$, then:
$$|G| = |H| \cdot [G:H]$$

In particular, $|H|$ divides $|G|$.

**Corollaries:**
- The order of any element divides $|G|$: $a^{|G|} = e$ for all $a \in G$ (Fermat's little theorem is the case $G = (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^*$)
- If $|G| = p$ (prime), then $G$ is cyclic
- If $|H| = |G|$, then $H = G$

**Important:** The converse is false — a group of order $n$ need not have a subgroup of order $d$ for every $d \mid n$. (But see Cauchy's theorem and Sylow's theorems in Chapter 10.)

## 9.5 Normal Subgroups and Quotient Groups

### 9.5.1 Normal Subgroups

$N \leq G$ is **normal** (written $N \trianglelefteq G$) if $gNg^{-1} = N$ for all $g \in G$.

**Equivalent conditions:**
- $gN = Ng$ for all $g$ (left and right cosets coincide)
- $gng^{-1} \in N$ for all $g \in G$, $n \in N$
- $N$ is the kernel of some homomorphism $G \to H$

**Examples:**
- Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal
- $A_n \trianglelefteq S_n$
- $SL_n(F) \trianglelefteq GL_n(F)$
- $Z(G) \trianglelefteq G$
- $\{e\}$ and $G$ are always normal

### 9.5.2 The Quotient Group

If $N \trianglelefteq G$, the quotient set $G/N = \{aN \mid a \in G\}$ becomes a group under:
$$(aN)(bN) = (ab)N$$

This is well-defined because $N$ is normal. The **quotient group** $G/N$ has order $[G:N]$.

**The canonical projection** $\pi: G \to G/N$ given by $\pi(g) = gN$ is a surjective group homomorphism with $\ker \pi = N$.

## 9.6 Cyclic Groups and Their Structure

### 9.6.1 $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$

The additive group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} = \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ (residues mod $n$) is cyclic of order $n$, generated by $1$.

**Subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$:** For each divisor $d \mid n$, there is a unique subgroup of order $d$, namely $\langle n/d \rangle$.

**Euler's $\phi$-function:** $\phi(n) = |(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*|$ = number of integers in $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ coprime to $n$.

### 9.6.2 Generators and Automorphisms

An element $k \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ generates the group iff $\gcd(k, n) = 1$.

The **automorphism group** $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^*$ of order $\phi(n)$.

**Classification of cyclic groups:** Up to isomorphism, there is exactly one cyclic group of each order (finite or infinite).

## Key Theorems

| Theorem | Statement |
|---------|-----------|
| Lagrange | $|H|$ divides $|G|$ for $H \leq G$ finite |
| Cauchy | If $p \mid |G|$ and $p$ prime, $G$ has an element of order $p$ |
| Cyclic group structure | $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ has a unique subgroup of order $d$ for each $d \mid n$ |
| Normal $\Leftrightarrow$ Kernel | $N \trianglelefteq G$ iff $N = \ker \phi$ for some homomorphism $\phi$ |

## Milestone Exercises

1. Prove that the identity element of a group is unique.

2. In $S_4$, write $(1\;2\;3\;4)$ as a product of transpositions. Is it even or odd?

3. List all subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ and $D_4$. Draw the subgroup lattice.

4. Prove: the center $Z(G)$ is always a normal subgroup of $G$.

5. Prove Lagrange's theorem from scratch.

6. Prove: If $|G| = 4$, then $G$ is either cyclic or isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (Klein four-group).

7. Prove: $A_n$ is the unique subgroup of index 2 in $S_n$.

8. Show $Q_8 = \{\pm 1, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}$ (quaternion group) is a non-abelian group of order 8 with only one element of order 2. How does this compare to $D_4$?
