## 22.1 The Universal Enveloping Algebra

### 22.1.1 Definition

The **universal enveloping algebra** $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the associative $k$-algebra:
$$\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}) = T(\mathfrak{g}) / I$$

where $T(\mathfrak{g}) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathfrak{g}^{\otimes n}$ is the tensor algebra and $I$ is the two-sided ideal generated by:
$$X \otimes Y - Y \otimes X - [X, Y], \quad X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$$

**Universal property:** $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the "most general" associative algebra containing $\mathfrak{g}$ as Lie algebra generators.

$$\left\{\text{Lie algebra maps } \mathfrak{g} \to A\right\} \longleftrightarrow \left\{\text{associative algebra maps } \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}) \to A\right\}$$

### 22.1.2 Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt Theorem

**Theorem (PBW):** Choose an ordered basis $x_1, x_2, \ldots$ of $\mathfrak{g}$. The **PBW monomials**:
$$x_1^{k_1} x_2^{k_2} \cdots x_n^{k_n}, \quad k_i \geq 0$$

form a $k$-basis of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$.

**Consequence:** The natural map $\mathfrak{g} \to \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ is injective (so $\mathfrak{g}$ embeds as a Lie subalgebra).

$\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ is a filtered algebra with associated graded $\mathrm{gr}\,\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})$ (the polynomial algebra on $\mathfrak{g}$).

### 22.1.3 Representations as Modules

A representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ is the same as a left $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$-module. All of module theory (Chapter 12) applies.

## 22.2 The Triangular Decomposition

For a semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$ with root decomposition $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{n}^- \oplus \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^+$:

$$\mathfrak{n}^+ = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha, \quad \mathfrak{n}^- = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}$$

**Borel subalgebra:** $\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^+$ (upper triangular part).

**PBW for $\mathfrak{g}$:** $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}) \cong \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{n}^-) \otimes \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}) \otimes \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{n}^+)$ as vector spaces.

## 22.3 Verma Modules

### 22.3.1 Definition

For $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, the **Verma module** $M(\lambda)$ is the $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$-module:
$$M(\lambda) = \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}) \otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{b})} k_\lambda$$

where $k_\lambda$ is the 1-dimensional $\mathfrak{b}$-module on which $H \in \mathfrak{h}$ acts by $\lambda(H)$ and $\mathfrak{n}^+$ acts by 0.

**Explicit construction:** $M(\lambda) = \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{n}^-)$ (as a vector space), with highest weight vector $v_\lambda = 1$ such that:
- $H \cdot v_\lambda = \lambda(H) v_\lambda$ for all $H \in \mathfrak{h}$
- $e_\alpha \cdot v_\lambda = 0$ for all positive roots $\alpha$ (highest weight condition)

### 22.3.2 Properties

- $M(\lambda)$ has a unique maximal proper submodule $N(\lambda)$
- The quotient $L(\lambda) = M(\lambda)/N(\lambda)$ is the unique irreducible with highest weight $\lambda$
- By PBW: $M(\lambda) = \mathrm{span}\{f_{\alpha_1}^{k_1} \cdots f_{\alpha_r}^{k_r} v_\lambda \mid k_i \geq 0\}$ (all basis monomials applied to $v_\lambda$)
- Weight spaces: $M(\lambda)_{\lambda - \sum_i k_i \alpha_i}$ for non-negative integer combinations of roots

### 22.3.3 When Is $M(\lambda)$ Irreducible?

$M(\lambda)$ is irreducible $\Leftrightarrow$ $\lambda + \rho$ is not in the form $s_\alpha(\mu + \rho)$ for any $\mu$ dominant integral and $\alpha \in \Phi^+$.

In particular, if $\lambda$ is dominant integral, $M(\lambda)$ is NOT irreducible (it has a finite-dimensional quotient).

## 22.4 Classification of Finite-Dimensional Representations

### 22.4.1 Dominant Integral Weights

A weight $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ is **dominant integral** if:
$$\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle = \frac{2(\lambda, \alpha)}{(\alpha, \alpha)} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} \quad \text{for all simple roots } \alpha$$

The set of dominant integral weights is the **dominant Weyl chamber intersected with the weight lattice**:
$$P^+ = \{\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*_\mathbb{Z} \mid \langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle \geq 0 \text{ for all simple } \alpha\}$$

$P^+$ is a free commutative monoid on the **fundamental weights** $\{\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_r\}$ defined by $\langle \omega_i, \alpha_j^\vee \rangle = \delta_{ij}$.

### 22.4.2 Classification Theorem

**Theorem:** There is a bijection:
$$P^+ \longleftrightarrow \{\text{finite-dimensional irreducible } \mathfrak{g}\text{-modules, up to iso}\}$$
$$\lambda \longmapsto L(\lambda)$$

where $L(\lambda) = M(\lambda)/N(\lambda)$ is the irreducible with highest weight $\lambda$.

**Proof sketch:**
- ($\Rightarrow$) Every finite-dimensional irreducible has a highest weight vector; the weight must be dominant integral (otherwise the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ subalgebras force infinite-dimensional spaces).
- ($\Leftarrow$) For $\lambda \in P^+$, the Verma module $M(\lambda)$ has a finite-dimensional irreducible quotient $L(\lambda)$.

## 22.5 The Weyl Character Formula

### 22.5.1 Characters of Representations

The **formal character** of a representation $V = \bigoplus_\mu V_\mu$:
$$\mathrm{ch}(V) = \sum_\mu (\dim V_\mu) e^\mu \in \mathbb{Z}[P]$$

(a formal sum in the group ring of the weight lattice $P$).

For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$: $\mathrm{ch}(V_\lambda) = e^\lambda + e^{\lambda-2} + \cdots + e^{-\lambda}$ (the character counts weight multiplicities).

### 22.5.2 Weyl Character Formula

Let $\rho = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \alpha$ (the **Weyl vector**, = sum of fundamental weights).

**Theorem (Weyl):**
$$\mathrm{ch}(L(\lambda)) = \frac{\sum_{w \in W} \mathrm{sgn}(w) e^{w(\lambda + \rho)}}{\sum_{w \in W} \mathrm{sgn}(w) e^{w(\rho)}}$$

The denominator is the **Weyl denominator**: $\prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} (e^{\alpha/2} - e^{-\alpha/2})$.

### 22.5.3 Weyl Dimension Formula

Setting $e^\mu = 1$ formally (i.e., taking $e^\mu \to e^{(t\rho, \mu)}$ and $t \to 0$):

$$\dim L(\lambda) = \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} \frac{(\lambda + \rho, \alpha)}{(\rho, \alpha)}$$

**Example ($\mathfrak{sl}_2$):** $\rho = \alpha/2$, $\Phi^+ = \{\alpha\}$, $\lambda = n\omega$ (fundamental weight). Then $\dim L(n\omega) = \frac{(n+1)(\alpha/2, \alpha)}{(\alpha/2, \alpha)} = n+1$. ✓

## 22.6 The Center of $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ and Harish-Chandra

### 22.6.1 The Casimir Element

For any orthonormal basis $\{x_i\}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ (with respect to the Killing form):
$$C = \sum_i x_i^2 \in \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$$

$C$ is in the center $Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))$. On $L(\lambda)$, $C$ acts by the scalar $(\lambda, \lambda + 2\rho)$.

### 22.6.2 Harish-Chandra Isomorphism

**Theorem (Harish-Chandra):** The center $Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))$ is a polynomial algebra:
$$Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})) \cong \mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^*]^W$$

where $W$ acts on $\mathfrak{h}^*$ and the isomorphism involves a $\rho$-shift.

The $r$ generators correspond to the $r$ algebraically independent Casimir elements (one per simple root).

## Key Theorems

| Theorem | Statement |
|---------|-----------|
| PBW theorem | PBW monomials form a basis for $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$ |
| Classification | FD irreducibles $\leftrightarrow$ dominant integral weights |
| Weyl character formula | $\mathrm{ch}(L(\lambda)) = \sum_w \mathrm{sgn}(w) e^{w(\lambda+\rho)} / \prod_\alpha (e^{\alpha/2} - e^{-\alpha/2})$ |
| Weyl dimension formula | $\dim L(\lambda) = \prod_{\alpha > 0} (\lambda+\rho, \alpha)/(\rho, \alpha)$ |
| Harish-Chandra | $Z(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})) \cong \mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^*]^W$ |

## Milestone Exercises

1. For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$: construct $M(2)$ (Verma module with highest weight 2) explicitly. Find $N(2)$ and show $L(2) = M(2)/N(2)$ has dimension 3.

2. Compute $\dim L(\lambda)$ for $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ and $\lambda = \omega_1 + \omega_2$ (adjoint representation, weight $(1,1)$). Should give 8.

3. For $G_2$: the fundamental representations have dimensions 7 and 14. Verify using the dimension formula.

4. Prove the Weyl denominator identity: $\sum_{w \in W} \mathrm{sgn}(w) e^{w\rho} = e^\rho \prod_{\alpha \in \Phi^+} (1 - e^{-\alpha})$.

5. Verify the Weyl character formula for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with $\lambda = 2\omega$: show $\mathrm{ch}(L(2\omega)) = e^2 + 1 + e^{-2}$ (i.e., weight spaces of dimensions 1,1,1).

6. What is the Casimir eigenvalue on the standard representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$?

7. For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_3$, determine all irreducible representations appearing in $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathfrak{g})$.
