# Basal Cognition: Intelligence Before the Brain

**A Comprehensive Textbook on the Cognitive Capacities of Non-Neural Life**

---

## About This Book

This textbook traces a single thread from the very beginning of life to the frontiers of contemporary research: the thread of intelligence. Not intelligence as we once defined it — as the exclusive property of brains, neurons, and nervous systems — but intelligence as it actually appears in the living world: diffuse, ancient, distributed, and startlingly sophisticated.

*Basal Cognition* is written for graduate students, advanced undergraduates, and researchers in biology, cognitive science, philosophy of mind, and artificial intelligence who are ready to take seriously the possibility that cognition is not a brain-centric phenomenon, but a biological one. It is written for anyone who has ever looked at a slime mold solving a maze, or a plant tracking sunlight, or a bacterium navigating a gradient, and wondered: what is happening here, exactly?

The book is designed to be both rigorous and readable. It does not shy away from difficult philosophy or cutting-edge empirical research, but it takes seriously its obligation to the reader: to explain, to connect, to motivate, and to delight.

## Pedagogical Features

Each chapter contains:

- **An orienting introduction** that frames the chapter's central question in concrete terms
- **Section introductions** that situate each section within the broader argument
- **Subsections** with primary content, including citations and key concepts
- **Exercises and thought experiments** at the end of each chapter, including original laboratory investigations
- **Further Reading and References** — a curated bibliography with annotations
- **Key Researchers** — profiles of the people doing this work
- **Key Concepts** — a glossary of terms introduced in the chapter

## A Note on Tone

This book is written as a conversation between an author and a curious, intelligent reader. It does not condescend, but it also does not assume prior expertise. It is willing to be wrong, willing to be uncertain, and committed to saying so clearly when that is the case. Where the science is contested, this book says so. Where the philosophy is unresolved — which is often — it names the unresolved questions and explains why they are hard.

The goal is not to convince you that basal cognition is real. The goal is to give you the tools to decide for yourself, rigorously and honestly.

## A Note on Citations

Non-common-knowledge claims are cited throughout the text. A full bibliography appears at the end of each chapter, and a consolidated reference list appears in Appendix D. Where empirical claims are contested or based on preliminary findings, this is noted explicitly in the text.
