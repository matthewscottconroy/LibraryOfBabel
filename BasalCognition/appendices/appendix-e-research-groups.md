# Appendix E: Research Groups and Centers

The field of basal cognition does not yet have a dedicated professional society or annual conference of its own, but a growing number of laboratories and centers are explicitly working on questions of cognition, intelligence, and information processing in non-neural organisms. What follows is a guide to the most active groups, organized roughly by research area. Website information is not included (web addresses change frequently); readers are advised to search by institution and PI name to locate current contact and publication information through institutional webpages.

A note on scope: not every group listed here would use the term "basal cognition" to describe its own work. Some identify with plant neurobiology, unconventional computing, theoretical biology, or philosophy of mind. They are included because their research directly bears on the questions this book addresses.

---

## Bioelectricity and Developmental Cognition

**Allen Discovery Center at Tufts University**
*Institution:* Tufts University, Medford, Massachusetts, USA
*Principal Investigator:* Michael Levin
*Focus:* Bioelectric signaling in morphogenesis, regeneration, and cancer; cognitive light cone theory; xenobots and reconfigurable organisms; the relationship between bioelectric pattern memory and cognition at multiple scales of biological organization. The Levin lab is the most productive single research group in basal cognition, with active projects spanning developmental biology, computational modeling, synthetic biology, and philosophy of mind.
*Institutional webpage*

---

## Minimal Cognition Theory

**Lyon Laboratory**
*Institution:* University of Adelaide, Australia
*Principal Investigator:* Pamela Lyon
*Focus:* Theoretical and philosophical foundations of the biogenic approach to cognition; the cognitive cell concept; standards of evidence for cognitive attribution in simple organisms; the relationship between minimal cognition, autonomy, and evolution. Lyon's group is the intellectual home of the biogenic approach and continues to define the field's theoretical standards.
*Institutional webpage*

---

## Bacterial Cognition and Quorum Sensing

**Bassler Laboratory**
*Institution:* Princeton University, Princeton, New Jersey, USA
*Principal Investigator:* Bonnie Bassler
*Focus:* Molecular mechanisms of quorum sensing; autoinducer chemistry; bacterial social behavior; the evolution of bacterial communication systems; anti-quorum sensing strategies for combating bacterial infections. The Bassler lab has produced the most detailed molecular picture of how bacteria assess population density and coordinate collective behavior. Bassler is also a Howard Hughes Medical Institute Investigator.
*Institutional webpage*

**Süel Laboratory**
*Institution:* University of California, San Diego, California, USA
*Principal Investigator:* Gürol Süel
*Focus:* Electrical signaling in bacterial biofilms; ion channel function in bacteria; single-cell decision-making in *Bacillus subtilis*; metabolic oscillations in microbial communities. The Süel lab produced the landmark 2015 *Nature* paper demonstrating potassium-wave signaling in bacterial biofilms.
*Institutional webpage*

## Protist Intelligence and Unconventional Computing

**Nakagaki Laboratory**
*Institution:* Hokkaido University, Sapporo, Japan
*Principal Investigator:* Toshiyuki Nakagaki
*Focus:* Physarum polycephalum cognition and network computation; mechanisms of maze-solving; oscillatory dynamics of cytoplasmic streaming; comparison of biological and engineered network architectures. Nakagaki's group produced the founding demonstrations of protist intelligence and continues to lead the mechanistic investigation of Physarum behavior.
*Institutional webpage*

**Unconventional Computing Laboratory**
*Institution:* University of the West of England, Bristol, UK
*Principal Investigator:* Andrew Adamatzky
*Focus:* Computational capacities of biological substrates, including Physarum polycephalum, fungal mycelium, and other unconventional materials; biological implementation of logical and arithmetic operations; hybrid biological-electronic computing systems. Adamatzky has published more widely on biological computing than any other single researcher and directs one of the few laboratories explicitly devoted to demonstrating computational capacities in non-neural organisms.
*Institutional webpage*

## Plant Cognition and Behavior

**International Laboratory of Plant Neurobiology (LINV)**
*Institution:* University of Florence, Italy
*Principal Investigator:* Stefano Mancuso
*Focus:* Plant electrical signaling; root navigation and obstacle avoidance; social behavior between plants; plant memory and learning; the evolution of plant intelligence. LINV is the most active center for plant neurobiology research in Europe and has produced a substantial body of empirical work on plant information processing.
*Institutional webpage*

**Baluška Laboratory**
*Institution:* University of Bonn, Germany
*Principal Investigator:* František Baluška
*Focus:* Plant cell biology with emphasis on synaptic-like vesicle trafficking in root apex cells; endocytosis and exocytosis in plant signaling; the root apex as a cognitive center; evolutionary connections between plant signaling and animal neural signaling. Baluška's molecular work provides much of the cellular basis for claims about plant neurobiology.
*Institutional webpage*

**Gagliano Laboratory**
*Institution:* University of Southern Denmark (formerly University of Western Australia)
*Principal Investigator:* Monica Gagliano
*Focus:* Plant learning and associative conditioning; plant bioacoustics; plant behavioral ecology; philosophical and ethical implications of plant cognition. Gagliano's laboratory produced the most-discussed plant learning experiments of the past decade and continues to push methodological and conceptual boundaries in the field.
*Institutional webpage*

**Karban Laboratory**
*Institution:* University of California, Davis, California, USA
*Principal Investigator:* Richard Karban
*Focus:* Plant communication through volatile chemicals; induced resistance to herbivory; plant-plant and plant-insect signaling; ecological consequences of plant information exchange. The Karban lab applies rigorous ecological methodology to questions of plant information processing and provides important empirical counterweight to more speculative plant cognition claims.
*Institutional webpage*

---

## Fungal Intelligence and Network Biology

**Kiers Laboratory**
*Institution:* Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam (VU Amsterdam), Netherlands
*Principal Investigator:* Toby Kiers
*Focus:* Mycorrhizal network economics; resource allocation and trade in fungal-plant symbioses; evolution of cooperation in symbiotic relationships; network-level decision-making in fungal systems. The Kiers lab applies evolutionary economics frameworks to fungal networks and has produced the most rigorous evidence for market-like behavior in mycorrhizal systems.
*Institutional webpage*

**Fricker Laboratory**
*Institution:* University of Oxford, UK
*Principal Investigator:* Mark Fricker
*Focus:* Mathematical and network-theoretic analysis of fungal mycelial architecture; transport network optimization in biological systems; comparison of biological and engineered network topologies; quantitative approaches to fungal ecology. Fricker's group provides the analytical tools for understanding how fungal network structure relates to function.
*Institutional webpage*

---

## Collective Intelligence

**Gordon Laboratory**
*Institution:* Stanford University, Stanford, California, USA
*Principal Investigator:* Deborah Gordon
*Focus:* Harvester ant colony behavior; interaction networks in ant colonies; colony-level regulation of foraging and reproduction; the evolution of collective behavior; ant colonies as a model for distributed computation. Gordon's decades of fieldwork in the Arizona desert have produced some of the most detailed longitudinal data on animal collective intelligence.
*Institutional webpage*

**Seeley Laboratory**
*Institution:* Cornell University, Ithaca, New York, USA
*Principal Investigator:* Thomas Seeley
*Focus:* Honeybee swarm intelligence; nest-site selection as distributed decision-making; bee waggle dance communication; the evolution of collective cognition in social insects. Seeley's work on honeybee democracy is the most complete account of distributed deliberation in any non-human animal.
*Institutional webpage*

**Garnier Laboratory (Swarm Lab)**
*Institution:* New Jersey Institute of Technology, Newark, New Jersey, USA
*Principal Investigator:* Simon Garnier
*Focus:* Swarm intelligence in ants, locusts, and other social organisms; algorithmic principles of collective behavior; bio-inspired robotics; crowd dynamics in human and animal groups. The Swarm Lab bridges biology and engineering, using swarm principles to design self-organizing robotic systems and to understand human collective behavior.
*Institutional webpage*

---

## Philosophy of Basal and Animal Minds

**Godfrey-Smith Group**
*Institution:* University of Sydney, Australia
*Principal Investigator:* Peter Godfrey-Smith
*Focus:* Philosophy of biology; evolution of mind and cognition; philosophy of animal experience; cephalopod cognition and behavior; the history of materialist philosophy of mind. Godfrey-Smith's work defines the philosophical methodology that basal cognition aspires to: rigorous functional analysis combined with principled agnosticism about phenomenal experience.
*Institutional webpage*

---

## Theoretical Neuroscience and Active Inference

**Computational Neurology and Psychiatry Group (Friston Lab)**
*Institution:* University College London (Wellcome Centre for Human Neuroimaging), UK
*Principal Investigator:* Karl Friston
*Focus:* Free Energy Principle and active inference; predictive coding; the application of variational inference to biological systems at all scales; computational psychiatry. Friston's theoretical work provides the most mathematically rigorous candidate framework for a unified theory of basal cognition, though its empirical application to simple organisms remains an active area of investigation.
*Institutional webpage*

## Key Research Centers and Journals

**Santa Fe Institute**
*Location:* Santa Fe, New Mexico, USA
*Focus:* Complexity science, emergence, and self-organization in biological and social systems. The Santa Fe Institute is not specifically a basal cognition center, but it has been the intellectual home of much of the theoretical work on complex adaptive systems, emergence, and collective intelligence that underlies basal cognition research. Its working papers and public lecture series are valuable resources.
*Institutional webpage*

### Key Journals Publishing Basal Cognition Research

The following journals are the primary venues for research in this field. Readers tracking the current literature should follow them closely.

**Frontiers in Psychology (section: Evolutionary Psychology)**
A leading open-access venue for theoretical and empirical work on basal cognition. Michael Levin's cognitive light cone paper, Baluška and Levin's collaborative theoretical work, and many other foundational papers in the field have appeared here.

**Frontiers in Microbiology**
The primary venue for research on bacterial cognition, quorum sensing, and microbial decision-making. Pamela Lyon's "The Cognitive Cell" paper and many companion pieces have appeared here.

**Adaptive Behavior**
A peer-reviewed journal focused on behavior in adaptive systems — biological and artificial. Barandiaran and Moreno's foundational MCO framework paper, and much of the subsequent formal theoretical work in minimal cognition, has appeared in this journal.

**Proceedings of the Royal Society B**
A major venue for high-profile empirical work in basal cognition, including Dussutour's Physarum habituation paper and much of the swarm intelligence literature.

**Plant Signaling and Behavior**
The primary specialist journal for plant communication, learning, and behavior research. A useful complement to the broader ecology and physiology literature for Unit V topics.
