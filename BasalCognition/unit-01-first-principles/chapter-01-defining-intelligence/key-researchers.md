# Key Researchers — Chapter 1: Defining Intelligence

---

## Pamela Lyon
**Affiliation**: University of Adelaide, Australia (School of Medicine)
**Primary Contribution**: Developer of the "biogenic approach" to cognition — a systematic framework for grounding the concept of cognition in biological science rather than cognitive science. Lyon's approach begins with the logic of living systems and derives the cognitive capacities they must have, rather than extending cognitive science concepts downward.
**Key Works**:
- Lyon, P. (2006). The biogenic approach to cognition. *Cognitive Processing*, 7(1), 11–29.
- Lyon, P. (2015). The cognitive cell: Bacterial behavior reconsidered. *Frontiers in Microbiology*, 6, 264.
**Characterization**: Lyon's work is distinguished by its philosophical rigor and biological precision. She is one of the few researchers in this field who holds both a deep command of the relevant philosophy of mind and a serious engagement with cell biology and microbiology.

---

## Xabier Barandiaran
**Affiliation**: University of the Basque Country, Spain (Department of Philosophy)
**Primary Contribution**: With Alvaro Moreno, developed the concept of the "minimally cognitive organization" (MCO) — a formal framework specifying the necessary and sufficient conditions for a biological system to exhibit minimal cognition. The MCO framework is the most technically rigorous account of minimal cognition currently available.
**Key Works**:
- Barandiaran, X.E., & Moreno, A. (2008). On what makes certain dynamical systems cognitive: A minimally cognitive organization (MCO) program. *Adaptive Behavior*, 16(5), 293–309.
- Barandiaran, X.E. (2008). *Mental Life: A Naturalistic Ontology*. PhD Thesis, University of the Basque Country.
**Characterization**: Philosophically trained with strong expertise in dynamical systems theory and enactivism. Approaches the basal cognition question through the lens of biological autonomy and agency.

---

## Alvaro Moreno
**Affiliation**: University of the Basque Country, Spain (Department of Logic and Philosophy of Science)
**Primary Contribution**: Philosopher of biology who has developed systematic accounts of agency, autonomy, and cognition in biological systems. With Barandiaran, formulated the MCO framework.
**Key Works**:
- Barandiaran, X.E., & Moreno, A. (2008). On what makes certain dynamical systems cognitive. *Adaptive Behavior*, 16(5), 293–309.
- Moreno, A., & Mossio, M. (2015). *Biological Autonomy: A Philosophical and Theoretical Enquiry*. Springer.
**Characterization**: A leading philosopher of life who has spent decades building a rigorous account of what distinguishes biological systems from non-biological ones, with implications for both the philosophy of mind and the biology of cognition.

---

## Norbert Wiener (1894–1964)
**Affiliation**: Massachusetts Institute of Technology
**Primary Contribution**: Founder of cybernetics — the science of control and communication in animals and machines. Wiener showed that goal-directed behavior can be fully characterized in terms of negative feedback, providing the mechanistic account of purpose that undergirds all of systems biology and cognitive science.
**Key Works**:
- Wiener, N. (1948). *Cybernetics: Or, Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine*. MIT Press.
- Wiener, N. (1950). *The Human Use of Human Beings: Cybernetics and Society*. Houghton Mifflin.
**Characterization**: A polymath mathematician and engineer whose cybernetic framework has proved more foundational than even its creator anticipated. Without Wiener's work, the mechanistic account of goal-directedness that makes basal cognition scientifically tractable would not exist.

---

## Humberto Maturana (1928–2021) and Francisco Varela (1946–2001)
**Affiliation**: University of Chile (Maturana); University of Paris, then CNRS (Varela)
**Primary Contribution**: Co-developers of the theory of autopoiesis — the account of living systems as self-producing, organizationally closed networks of processes. Their work established the deepest available account of the relationship between life and cognition, arguing that cognition is constitutive of living, not an add-on to it.
**Key Works**:
- Maturana, H.R., & Varela, F.J. (1980). *Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living*. D. Reidel.
- Varela, F.J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience*. MIT Press.
- Maturana, H.R., & Varela, F.J. (1987). *The Tree of Knowledge: The Biological Roots of Human Understanding*. Shambhala.
**Characterization**: Together, Maturana and Varela wrote the most influential theoretical account of cognition as a biological phenomenon. Varela, with Thompson and Rosch, extended their ideas into the enactivist movement that continues to shape cognitive science. Their work is foundational for this entire field.

## Peter Godfrey-Smith
**Affiliation**: University of Sydney, Australia (Philosophy)
**Primary Contribution**: Philosopher of biology who has worked extensively on the evolution of mind and the minimal conditions for cognition. His book *Other Minds* is a masterclass in thinking about animal cognition without anthropocentric assumptions. His earlier work *Complexity and the Function of Mind in Nature* introduced the concept of "minimal cognition" in a philosophically rigorous way.
**Key Works**:
- Godfrey-Smith, P. (1996). *Complexity and the Function of Mind in Nature*. Cambridge University Press.
- Godfrey-Smith, P. (2016). *Other Minds: The Octopus, the Sea, and the Deep Origins of Consciousness*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
**Characterization**: The rare philosopher who is also a certified scuba diver and octopus researcher. Brings philosophical precision to empirical questions about the evolution and nature of cognition, and has done as much as anyone to make the concept of minimal cognition tractable.
