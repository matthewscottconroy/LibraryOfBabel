# Key Concepts: Chapter 5

---

**Action Potential (non-neural)**
A rapid, stereotyped depolarization and repolarization of the membrane potential in a non-neural cell, propagating across the cell or along connected tissue. Examples include action potentials in plant phloem (mediating wound responses), in *Chara* algae (regulating cytoplasmic streaming), and in some developing embryonic cells. While the molecular mechanisms differ from neural action potentials, the functional role — rapid, propagating electrical signaling — is analogous. *Why it matters*: The existence of action potential-like events in diverse non-neural organisms suggests that fast electrical signaling is a general biological capability, not a neural invention. It places the neural action potential in evolutionary context as an elaboration of ancient cellular machinery.

**Bioelectric Pattern**
A spatially organized distribution of membrane potentials across a tissue or organism, maintained by differential expression of ion channels and pumps and mediated by gap junction coupling. Bioelectric patterns are dynamic — they change during development and in response to injury — and appear to carry positional information that guides cell fate decisions and morphogenesis. *Why it matters*: Bioelectric patterns represent a layer of developmental information that is above the molecular genetics level and cannot be reduced to it in any simple way. They constitute a novel information medium through which cells communicate about their anatomical context.

**Cognitive Light Cone**
A concept developed by Michael Levin describing the spatiotemporal scale over which an agent's actions are coherent and purposive. A system with a "small" cognitive light cone (like a bacterium) coordinates behavior over micrometers and milliseconds; one with a "large" cognitive light cone (like a human brain) coordinates over kilometers and years. Bioelectric signaling, Levin argues, is the mechanism that expands the cognitive light cone of multicellular organisms beyond what individual cells could achieve. *Why it matters*: The cognitive light cone provides a scale-independent, mechanistically grounded measure of cognitive sophistication that can be applied across the full range of biological systems, from cells to organisms to societies.

**Gap Junction**
An intercellular channel formed by aligned hemichannels (connexins in vertebrates) that allows direct cytoplasmic continuity between adjacent cells. Ions, second messengers, and small metabolites can pass through gap junctions, electrically coupling cells into a tissue-wide network. Gap junction permeability is regulated and can be modified by developmental signals, creating dynamic changes in the topology of bioelectric communication. *Why it matters*: Gap junctions transform a collection of individual cells with independent membrane potentials into a tissue-wide bioelectric network, enabling tissue-scale spatial patterning and long-range coordination of cellular behavior. They are essential to the information-processing capacity of bioelectric systems.

**Galvanotaxis**
Directed cell migration in response to an applied electric field. Cells including epithelial cells, macrophages, fibroblasts, and neurons exhibit galvanotaxis, with migration direction determined by cell type and field polarity. The mechanism involves asymmetric redistribution of membrane proteins (including receptor tyrosine kinases) driven by the electric field, which creates a spatial bias in downstream signaling analogous to the spatial bias generated by a chemical gradient in chemotaxis. *Why it matters*: Galvanotaxis shows that the electric fields generated by wounded tissue are functional signals — not just physical epiphenomena — that guide the migration of cells involved in wound healing and immune response. It provides a mechanistic link between bioelectric tissue physics and cellular behavior.

**Ion Pump**
A membrane protein that actively transports ions against their electrochemical gradients, using the energy of ATP hydrolysis or other energy sources. Key examples include the Na+/K+-ATPase (exports Na+, imports K+), the H+-ATPase (exports protons), and the Ca2+-ATPase (exports calcium). Ion pumps maintain the ion concentration gradients that are the "batteries" driving all bioelectric signaling. *Why it matters*: Ion pumps are the energy-converting foundation of bioelectricity. By maintaining transmembrane ion gradients, they store electrochemical potential energy that can be rapidly released through ion channels to generate signals. The investment of metabolic energy in ion pump activity is the cell's "payment" for the information-processing capacity that bioelectricity provides.

**Morphogenetic Field**
A region of embryonic tissue with the intrinsic capacity to produce a specific anatomical structure (e.g., an eye, a limb, a vertebra). The concept, developed by experimental embryologists in the early 20th century, captures the observation that embryonic tissue has spatial organization and regulatory capacity beyond what individual cells can account for. Bioelectric patterns appear to be one of the physical substrates of morphogenetic fields. *Why it matters*: The morphogenetic field concept captures a genuinely supra-cellular level of biological organization — a level at which the properties of the tissue cannot be reduced to the properties of its individual cells without losing essential information. Bioelectricity provides a mechanistic grounding for this long-standing developmental biology concept.

**Resting Membrane Potential**
The stable electrical potential difference maintained across a cell membrane in the absence of active signaling, typically between -20 mV and -90 mV (inside negative relative to outside). It arises from the combined effects of ion concentration gradients (established by pumps) and selective membrane permeability (determined by open channels). Different cell types maintain different resting potentials, and changes in resting potential signal changes in cell state. *Why it matters*: The resting membrane potential is the baseline from which all bioelectric signaling departs. Its value encodes information about the cell's metabolic state, activity level, and developmental context. It is the fundamental unit of bioelectric "language."

**Transepithelial Potential**
The voltage difference across an intact epithelial sheet, maintained by active ion transport (predominantly Na+ and Cl- in most epithelia) from the apical to basal surface (or vice versa). Normally 10-100 mV. Disruption of the epithelial barrier (by wounding) creates a local "short circuit" that drives electric current through the wound and generates an endogenous electric field in the surrounding tissue. *Why it matters*: The transepithelial potential is the source of the wound electric field that guides galvanotaxis and coordinates wound healing. It provides a direct link between the barrier function of epithelia and their bioelectric communication role — the tissue uses its normal ion-transport function as a sensing mechanism for tissue integrity.

**Xenobot**
A novel synthetic living system constructed from dissociated *Xenopus laevis* cells, shaped by computational optimization (often using evolutionary algorithms) to perform specific functions such as directional locomotion, object transport, or maze navigation. Xenobots are not genetically modified — they use the native cellular machinery of frog cells — but are organized in novel morphological configurations not found in natural organisms. They exhibit emergent behaviors not present in the frog cells' native context. *Why it matters*: Xenobots demonstrate that cognitive and behavioral capacities are not solely encoded in genomes but are partly determined by morphological and bioelectric context. They represent a proof of concept that living cells can be reorganized into novel functional agents, with implications for synthetic biology, regenerative medicine, and our understanding of the relationship between form and behavior.
