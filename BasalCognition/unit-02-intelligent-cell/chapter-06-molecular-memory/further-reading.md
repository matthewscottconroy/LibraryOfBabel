# Further Reading: Chapter 6

---

**Dexter, J. P., Prabakaran, S., & Gunawardena, J. (2019). A complex hierarchy of avoidance behaviors in a single-cell eukaryote. *Current Biology*, *29*(24), 4323–4329.e2.**
The modern confirmation of *Stentor* habituation. Uses microfluidics and quantitative analysis to establish that a unicellular organism habituates to repeated mechanical stimulation with the canonical features of genuine habituation: stimulus specificity, reversibility, and dishabituation. Accessible and clearly written; the methods section provides a model for rigorous behavioral analysis in unicellular systems.

---

**Mitchell, A., Romano, G. H., Groisman, B., Yona, A., Dekel, E., Kupiec, M., Dahan, O., & Pilpel, Y. (2009). Adaptive prediction of environmental changes by microorganisms. *Nature*, *460*(7252), 220–224.**
The landmark paper demonstrating that *E. coli* regulatory networks anticipate the lactose-maltose correlation in the gut environment. Shows that lactose presence triggers pre-induction of maltose metabolism genes — a predictive, not merely reactive, response. A conceptually important paper that establishes the framework of evolutionarily encoded behavioral prediction.

---

**Heard, E., & Martienssen, R. A. (2014). Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance: myths and mechanisms. *Cell*, *157*(1), 95–109.**
A rigorous and balanced review of transgenerational epigenetic inheritance, written by two leading epigenetics researchers. Critically assesses the evidence for TEI in different organisms, distinguishes what is established from what remains speculative, and discusses the mechanistic challenges that any credible model of TEI in mammals must address. An antidote to overclaiming in this area, and a model of scientific critical thinking.

---

**Netea, M. G., Quintin, J., & van der Meer, J. W. M. (2011). Trained immunity: a memory for innate host defense. *Cell Host & Microbe*, *9*(5), 355–361.**
The original paper proposing and characterizing trained immunity — non-specific immunological memory in innate immune cells mediated by epigenetic changes. A conceptually important paper that expanded the definition of immunological memory beyond adaptive immune cells. Relatively short and accessible; worth reading alongside a current review of the field to see how the concept has developed.

---

**Strahl, B. D., & Allis, C. D. (2000). The language of covalent histone modifications. *Nature*, *403*(6765), 41–45.**
The original paper proposing the "histone code" hypothesis — that specific combinations of histone modifications constitute a regulatory language read by effector proteins that determine chromatin state and gene expression. A foundational paper in epigenetics that established the conceptual framework for understanding how histone modifications store and transmit regulatory information. Readable and historically important.

---

**Rechavi, O., Minevich, G., & Hobert, O. (2011). Transgenerational inheritance of an acquired small RNA-based antiviral response in *C. elegans*. *Cell*, *147*(6), 1248–1256.**
A clean experimental demonstration of transgenerational epigenetic inheritance in *C. elegans*: small RNAs generated in response to viral infection are inherited by offspring and confer enhanced antiviral resistance. Mechanistically grounded and rigorously controlled; a good model for how TEI research should be done. Establishes the small RNA pathway as a carrier of hereditary epigenetic information.

---

**Kandel, E. R. (2001). The molecular biology of memory storage: a dialogue between genes and synapses. *Science*, *294*(5544), 1030–1038.**
Kandel's Nobel Prize lecture, providing a comprehensive overview of the molecular mechanisms of synaptic memory in *Aplysia* and beyond. Essential background for understanding what the "standard" (neural) view of cellular learning looks like, and for appreciating how the non-neural learning phenomena described in this chapter compare to and differ from it. Candel's framework — memory as changes in synaptic strength mediated by gene expression and structural synaptic changes — provides the foil against which cellular learning without synapses can be understood.

---

**Jablonka, E., & Lamb, M. J. (2005). *Evolution in Four Dimensions: Genetic, Epigenetic, Behavioral, and Symbolic Variation in the History of Life*. MIT Press.**
A book-length argument that evolution operates through four distinct inheritance systems — genetic, epigenetic, behavioral, and symbolic — not just the genetic system recognized by the Modern Synthesis. The epigenetic inheritance chapter is directly relevant to this chapter's themes and provides a philosophical framework for understanding why epigenetic memory matters for evolution, not just for cell biology. Provocative and well-argued, though its broader claims remain contested.

---

**Alon, U. (2007). *An Introduction to Systems Biology: Design Principles of Biological Circuits*. Chapman & Hall/CRC.**
The section on network motifs and their computational properties is particularly relevant here, especially the analysis of how feed-forward loops implement temporal filtering and conditional logic — the molecular architecture through which predictive regulatory behavior like the Mitchell et al. result is implemented. Chapter 4 of Alon's book, on network motifs, is essential background for understanding the molecular implementation of anticipatory behavior in gene regulatory networks.

---

**Jennings, H. S. (1906). *Behavior of the Lower Organisms*. Columbia University Press.**
The original and still-remarkable account of behavioral observation in unicellular organisms, including the *Stentor* experiments that are the historical starting point for this chapter. Jennings was a master observer and a thoughtful philosopher, and his willingness to use cognitive vocabulary for unicellular behavior — and his care in defending that usage — is intellectually courageous and prescient. Chapters on *Stentor*, *Paramecium*, and amoeba are directly relevant. Available in various reprints and digitized editions.
