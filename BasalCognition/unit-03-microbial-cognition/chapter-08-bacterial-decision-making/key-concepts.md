# Key Concepts: Chapter 8

---

**Chemotaxis**
Directed movement of a bacterium (or other cell) in response to a chemical gradient, implemented in bacteria through a biased random walk: runs are extended when moving up-gradient toward attractants (or down-gradient away from repellents), and tumbles are more frequent otherwise. Requires temporal comparison of current and past receptor occupancy, implemented by the methylation-based adaptation system. *Why it matters*: Chemotaxis is the paradigmatic example of bacterial navigation and is among the best-understood examples of molecular-level information processing. It demonstrates that sophisticated goal-directed behavior is possible with a minimal molecular toolkit.

**Cyclic di-GMP (c-di-GMP)**
A second messenger molecule (cyclic dinucleotide) that controls the switch between motile and biofilm-forming lifestyles in many bacteria. High c-di-GMP promotes sessile behavior (biofilm matrix synthesis, adhesion, reduced motility); low c-di-GMP promotes motile planktonic behavior. Levels are set by the balance of diguanylate cyclase (synthesis) and phosphodiesterase (degradation) enzymes, which are regulated by environmental inputs. *Why it matters*: c-di-GMP is a lifestyle decision molecule — it integrates environmental information into a global binary decision about whether to stay in place (and form a biofilm) or continue swimming. This decision has major consequences for the bacterium's survival and fitness.

**Methylation-Based Adaptation**
The mechanism by which *E. coli* chemoreceptors implement short-term memory and perfect adaptation. CheR constitutively methylates receptor glutamate residues (increasing receptor activity), while CheB (activated by phospho-CheA) demethylates them. Attractant binding suppresses CheA, suppresses CheB activation, and allows CheR-mediated methylation to accumulate, restoring receptor activity toward baseline. The current methylation state of receptors encodes a chemical "memory" of recent attractant conditions. *Why it matters*: Methylation-based adaptation is a molecular implementation of integral feedback control — it ensures perfect, robust adaptation of tumbling frequency to sustained changes in attractant concentration, while remaining sensitive to transient changes. It is the key molecular mechanism enabling temporal comparison in chemotaxis.

**Network Motif**
A recurring pattern of regulatory interactions in gene or signaling networks that appears more often than expected by chance and confers specific computational properties. Key motifs include the feedforward loop, negative autoregulation, and the bi-fan (multiple transcription factors co-regulating multiple targets). First systematically characterized in *E. coli* transcriptional networks by Alon and colleagues. *Why it matters*: Network motifs reveal that bacterial regulatory networks are not random — they have been structured by evolution to implement specific signal-processing computations. Understanding the motif structure of a network allows prediction of its dynamic behavior from its topology.

**Persister Cells**
A small sub-population of bacteria within any culture that spontaneously enters a dormant, metabolically quiescent state, rendering them tolerant to antibiotics and other growth-dependent stresses. Persister formation is driven by stochastic fluctuations in toxin-antitoxin module protein levels, not by genetic mutation. After antibiotic treatment ends, persisters resume growth and re-establish the population. *Why it matters*: Persister cells are a bet-hedging mechanism that maintains a survival option in any bacterial population at low cost under normal conditions but high benefit under antibiotic challenge. They are clinically important as the likely source of antibiotic treatment failures in many chronic bacterial infections.

**Run-Tumble Algorithm**
The bacterial chemotaxis behavioral strategy: alternating between straight runs (flagella rotating counterclockwise) and tumbles (brief chaotic rotation, random reorientation). Tumble probability is decreased when the cell is moving up an attractant gradient (by suppressing CheY-P) and increased when moving down-gradient. The result is a biased random walk that navigates up-gradient. The algorithm is stochastic but effective, and has formal similarities to simulated annealing in optimization theory. *Why it matters*: The run-tumble algorithm is the behavioral implementation of bacterial decision-making — the output through which molecular information processing is expressed as physical movement in the world. Its stochastic nature is a feature, not a bug: it enables exploration of the environment rather than deterministic convergence to a local maximum.

**SOS Response**
A global bacterial stress response to DNA damage, orchestrated by the RecA protein and the LexA repressor. DNA damage generates single-stranded DNA that activates RecA, which then stimulates LexA autocleavage, derepressing >40 SOS genes including DNA repair enzymes, cell division inhibitors, and error-prone DNA polymerases. The response is graduated — early genes are activated at lower damage levels, late genes at higher levels. *Why it matters*: The SOS response is a paradigm of bacterial decision-making under duress. It demonstrates how bacteria integrate quantitative information (degree of DNA damage) with a graduated, prioritized response that is calibrated to the severity of the threat.

**Sporulation**
A developmental program in certain bacteria (*Bacillus subtilis* and relatives) that transforms a vegetative cell into a dormant, stress-resistant endospore, capable of surviving for years under conditions that would kill vegetative cells. Initiated when multiple stress signals (starvation, heat, etc.) are integrated by the Spo0A phosphorelay and Spo0A-P exceeds a bistable threshold. Sporulation is irreversible once committed and requires coordinated expression of >100 genes over 8 hours. *Why it matters*: Sporulation is the most complex developmental decision bacteria make and the most extreme commitment. The bistable switch that commits the cell to sporulation illustrates principles of irreversible decision-making that apply across biology.

**Stringent Response**
A global transcriptional reprogramming in bacteria triggered by amino acid starvation, mediated by the alarmone (p)ppGpp. When uncharged tRNA accumulates in the ribosomal A site, the RelA enzyme synthesizes (p)ppGpp, which globally inhibits rRNA synthesis, ribosome biogenesis, and growth while activating amino acid biosynthesis and stress tolerance genes. The response amounts to a cellular decision to abandon growth and invest in survival. *Why it matters*: The stringent response is a clear example of cellular decision-making in response to resource scarcity. Its global, coordinated reprogramming of cellular priorities — shifting from growth to survival — demonstrates that single bacterial cells can execute complex, multi-gene behavioral programs in response to specific environmental signals.

**Two-Component Signal Transduction System (TCS)**
The dominant bacterial signal transduction architecture, consisting of a sensor histidine kinase (which detects an environmental signal and autophosphorylates) and a response regulator (which receives the phosphoryl group and activates or represses target genes). Approximately 30 TCS pairs in *E. coli*, hundreds in complex bacteria. Modular architecture allows diversification through evolution. *Why it matters*: TCS systems are the primary interface between bacterial cells and their chemical, physical, and biological environment. Their modular architecture has allowed evolution to generate a vast diversity of environmental sensors connected to diverse regulatory outputs, creating the complexity of bacterial signal transduction networks.
