# Chapter 12: Further Reading

## Annotated Bibliography

**Bonner, J. T. (2009). *The Social Amoebae: The Biology of Cellular Slime Molds*. Princeton University Press.**
The definitive synthesis of Dictyostelium biology by the researcher who spent his career studying it. Bonner writes with the authority of decades of direct observation and with a refreshing intellectual honesty about what the evidence does and does not show. This is the essential starting point for any serious engagement with Dictyostelium as a model system for basal cognition.

**Devreotes, P. N., & Horwitz, A. R. (2015). Signaling networks controlling cell migration. *Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology*, 7(8), a005959.**
A thorough and accessible review of the intracellular signaling networks that control directed cell migration, with particular emphasis on the LEGI model and its empirical support. The authors are careful to distinguish what has been established from what remains controversial. Essential background for understanding gradient sensing at a mechanistic level.

**Pollard, T. D., & Borisy, G. G. (2003). Cellular motility driven by assembly and disassembly of actin filaments. *Cell*, 112(4), 453–465.**
The canonical review of actin-based motility, written by two of the field's leading figures. Covers the biochemistry of actin polymerization, the role of Arp2/3 and other regulators, and the integration of these mechanisms into directed cell movement. Dense but rewarding for those willing to engage with the molecular detail.

**Strassmann, J. E., Zhu, Y., & Queller, D. C. (2000). Altruism and social cheating in the social amoeba Dictyostelium discoideum. *Nature*, 408(6815), 965–967.**
The paper that established Dictyostelium as a model for the evolution of cooperation and conflict. Clear experimental design, important results, and a discussion that frames the findings in the context of evolutionary theory with admirable precision.

**Strassmann, J. E., & Queller, D. C. (2011). Evolution of cooperation and control of cheating in a social microbe. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 108(Suppl 2), 10855–10862.**
An expansive review of the Dictyostelium social system, incorporating a decade of work following the 2000 cheating paper. Discusses kin discrimination, the molecular basis of recognition, and the evolutionary dynamics of cooperation and defection in a framework accessible to readers without a specialist background in evolutionary biology.

**Goldbeter, A. (1996). *Biochemical Oscillations and Cellular Rhythms*. Cambridge University Press.**
A mathematically rigorous treatment of the oscillatory dynamics underlying Dictyostelium cAMP signaling, embedded in a broader treatment of biochemical oscillations in biology. Goldbeter's models of the cAMP relay system are classics of mathematical biology. Readers without strong mathematical backgrounds should focus on Chapters 4–6 and use the mathematical sections for reference.

**Parent, C. A., & Devreotes, P. N. (1999). A cell's sense of direction. *Science*, 284(5415), 765–770.**
A clear and influential account of how cells detect gradients and transduce them into asymmetric intracellular responses. The paper introduces the conceptual framework of local excitation/global inhibition in accessible form and remains one of the best entry points into the gradient sensing literature.

**Weiner, O. D., Marganski, W. A., Wu, L. F., Altschuler, S. J., & Kirschner, M. W. (2007). An actin-based wave generator organizes cell motility. *PLOS Biology*, 5(9), e221.**
Demonstrates that spontaneous actin waves — generated without an external gradient — underlie the stochastic pseudopod dynamics that are biased, rather than created, by chemoattractant gradients. This paper significantly changed how researchers think about the relationship between internal dynamics and external signals in directed cell movement.

**Hirose, S., Benabentos, R., Ho, H. I., Kuspa, A., & Shaulsky, G. (2011). Self-recognition in social amoebae is mediated by allelic pairs of tiger genes. *Science*, 333(6041), 467–470.**
Identifies the molecular basis of kin discrimination in Dictyostelium — the TgrB1/TgrC1 cell-surface receptor pairs that mediate self/non-self recognition. A landmark paper in the molecular biology of social behavior in protists.

**Bhatt, M. V., & Bhatt, M. (various years). Molecular mechanisms of virulence in Entamoeba histolytica.**
For those wishing to go deeper on parasitic cognition, the primary literature on Entamoeba virulence regulation is extensive and technically demanding. Start with recent reviews in journals such as *PLOS Pathogens* and *Infection and Immunity* that cover virulence gene regulation and host-parasite signaling.

**Arrieumerlou, C., & Meyer, T. (2005). A local coupling model and compass parameter for eukaryotic chemotaxis. *Developmental Cell*, 8(2), 215–227.**
Introduces a quantitative framework for analyzing how cells translate receptor occupancy into directional movement, including the stochastic pseudopod model. Particularly valuable for the discussion of how cells "choose" directions without an explicit representation of the gradient vector.
