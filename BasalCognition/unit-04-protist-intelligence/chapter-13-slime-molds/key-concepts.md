# Chapter 13: Key Concepts

## Glossary

**Plasmodium**
The multinucleate, single-membrane life stage of a plasmodial slime mold such as Physarum polycephalum. A plasmodium contains thousands to millions of nuclei sharing a common cytoplasm, enclosed in a single continuous membrane. It is neither a single cell (in the conventional sense) nor a multicellular organism; it occupies a conceptual category between these. The plasmodium is the active, feeding, problem-solving stage that is the subject of most cognitive experiments.

*Why it matters*: The plasmodium challenges our ordinary categories for biological individuality. Understanding what kind of thing a plasmodium is helps clarify why attributions of cognition and behavior to it are conceptually unusual — and why they are interesting.

---

**Cytoplasmic Streaming**
The rhythmic, bidirectional flow of cytoplasm through the tubes of the Physarum plasmodium, driven by oscillatory contractions of actomyosin in the tube walls. Streaming reverses direction approximately every 60–90 seconds. It is the primary mechanism for distributing nutrients and signals throughout the organism, and its dynamic patterns encode information about the spatial distribution of food sources and other environmental conditions.

*Why it matters*: Cytoplasmic streaming is the physical substrate of Physarum's information processing. Understanding how changes in streaming dynamics encode and transmit information about the environment is equivalent to understanding how the organism senses and responds to its world.

---

**Flow Reinforcement**
The positive feedback mechanism by which tubes carrying higher flow rates in the Physarum network grow thicker (more conductive), while tubes carrying lower flow rates thin and eventually disappear. The feedback arises because tube conductivity scales strongly with tube diameter (via Hagen-Poiseuille dynamics), so thicker tubes are more conductive, carry more flow, and are therefore more strongly reinforced. This mechanism drives the network to optimize efficiency without any centralized computation.

*Why it matters*: Flow reinforcement is the mechanistic explanation for Physarum's maze-solving and network optimization abilities. It converts a biological nutrient-transport mechanism into a functional optimization algorithm — a beautiful example of how evolution repurposes physical dynamics for adaptive function.

---

**Excitable Medium**
Repeated from Chapter 13 here in its Physarum context: in the Physarum plasmodium, the actomyosin system of the tube walls constitutes an excitable medium — a system with a stable resting state, threshold-dependent large responses to stimulation, and a refractory period. This produces propagating waves of contraction/relaxation that carry information about stimuli (food, light, temperature) through the network.

*Why it matters*: Framing Physarum's information propagation in terms of excitable medium dynamics connects it to a body of mathematical theory that predicts how such systems behave. It also reveals deep structural similarities between Physarum's information processing and that of other excitable media, including the nervous system.

---

**Entrainment**
The locking of an oscillating system to an external periodic stimulus, such that the system's oscillation matches the frequency and phase of the stimulus. In the context of Physarum memory, the cold-shock anticipation experiment is interpreted by some researchers as resulting from entrainment of the organism's internal cytoplasmic streaming oscillation to the periodic cold shocks, rather than from discrete memory storage.

*Why it matters*: The entrainment interpretation of Physarum "memory" illustrates the importance of mechanistic explanation for cognitive claims. Two systems might look, from the outside, like they both "remember" a periodic event, but one might do so through discrete information storage (memory in the neural sense) and another through dynamical coupling (entrainment). Whether these are the same phenomenon at the functional level, or genuinely different, is a deep conceptual question.

---

**Unconventional Computing**
A field of research exploring computational substrates other than conventional silicon-based digital computers. Includes chemical computers (reaction-diffusion systems), quantum computers, DNA computers, mechanical computers, and biological computers (including Physarum). Unconventional computing research asks both practical questions (are these substrates useful for specific applications?) and theoretical questions (what is computation, at its most general?).

*Why it matters*: Physarum computing sits within the broader unconventional computing program. Understanding this context helps calibrate claims about slime mold intelligence — Adamatzky's program is explicitly framed as computing research, with engineering implications, rather than as cognitive science per se.

---

**Network Optimization**
The problem of designing a network (a set of nodes connected by edges) that optimizes some combination of objectives — typically efficiency (short paths between nodes), robustness (resilience to node or edge failure), and cost (minimal total edge length). Physarum's tube networks approximate solutions to network optimization problems through flow reinforcement dynamics, as demonstrated in the Tokyo rail network experiments.

*Why it matters*: Network optimization is a well-defined computational problem with engineering applications. The fact that Physarum approximates solutions to it provides a concrete benchmark against which to evaluate the organism's problem-solving capabilities — and a concrete illustration of what it means to "compute" without algorithms.

---

**Adaptive Memory (Physarum context)**
A term used to describe the apparent ability of Physarum to modify its future behavior based on past experience — including the temporal anticipation of periodic stimuli demonstrated by Saigusa et al. (2008). The term "adaptive memory" is preferable to simply "memory" because it flags that the mechanism may differ fundamentally from neural memory, while preserving the observation that past experience adaptively influences future behavior.

*Why it matters*: The concept of adaptive memory captures a real functional property of Physarum while remaining agnostic about mechanism. This is the epistemically appropriate posture for a finding whose mechanistic interpretation remains contested.
