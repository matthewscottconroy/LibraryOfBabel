# Chapter 14: Key Researchers

## Herbert Spencer Jennings (Historical)
**Institution**: Johns Hopkins University (from 1906)  
**Key Contribution**: Jennings is the founding figure of comparative behavioral science for single-celled organisms. Working in the early twentieth century, he conducted meticulous observational studies of Paramecium, Stentor, Vorticella, and many other protists, establishing that their behavior could not be adequately described by simple tropism models. His 1906 book *Behavior of the Lower Organisms* remains a landmark in the history of behavioral biology, arguing — against the mechanist consensus of his day — that trial-and-error exploratory behavior was present in organisms without nervous systems. He was also a significant figure in early Mendelian genetics and later in human genetics (where his positions on eugenics evolved significantly over time). As a behavioral scientist, his contributions have proved remarkably durable.

**Key Works**:
- Jennings, H. S. (1906). *Behavior of the Lower Organisms*. Columbia University Press.
- Jennings, H. S. (1899). Studies on reactions to stimuli in unicellular organisms. II. The mechanism of the motor reactions of Paramecium. *American Journal of Physiology*, 2(5), 311–341.

## Tracy Morton Sonneborn (Historical)
**Institution**: Indiana University  
**Key Contribution**: Sonneborn was one of the greatest geneticists of the twentieth century, and his career was built primarily on Paramecium. He established the genetics of mating types, discovered the cytoplasmic hereditary determinant "kappa" (the endosymbiotic bacterium that causes the "killer" trait), and conducted the cortical inheritance experiments with Beisson that are discussed in Section 3. His work demonstrated that inheritance is not exclusively genomic — that the structure of the cell itself can be inherited across generations — a finding that remains philosophically provocative today. He was also a pioneer of what would eventually become the field of epigenetics, though he worked before the term came into common use.

**Key Papers**:
- Beisson, J., & Sonneborn, T. M. (1965). Cytoplasmic inheritance of the organization of the cell cortex in Paramecium aurelia. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 53(2), 275–282.
- Sonneborn, T. M. (1938). Mating types in Paramecium aurelia. *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society*, 79(3), 411–434.

## Roger Eckert (Historical)
**Institution**: University of California, Los Angeles  
**Key Contribution**: Eckert established the ionic basis of Paramecium behavior, demonstrating that calcium influx through the anterior membrane drives ciliary reversal and therefore the avoiding reaction. His 1972 Science paper is the mechanistic foundation for understanding how electrical signaling in a single cell produces coordinated behavioral responses. By showing that Paramecium's behavior could be understood in terms of biophysical mechanisms familiar from neurophysiology — action potentials, ion channels, calcium as a second messenger — Eckert made it possible to analyze ciliate behavior with the full toolkit of cellular neuroscience.

**Key Papers**:
- Eckert, R. (1972). Bioelectric control of ciliary activity. *Science*, 176(4034), 473–481.

## David Wood / Joseph Gunawardena Group
**Institution**: Harvard University / University of Pittsburgh  
**Key Contribution**: The modern reinvestigation of Stentor behavioral complexity by this group — published in *Current Biology* in 2019 — represents the most rigorous contemporary experimental engagement with Jennings' century-old behavioral descriptions. By bringing quantitative, video-based analysis to the question of what Stentor actually does when irritated, the group confirmed the core phenomenology while revealing important variability and complexity that Jennings' account had not fully captured. The group's broader research program is concerned with how cells make decisions using molecular signaling networks — a framework that brings Stentor into contact with contemporary systems biology and cell decision-making theory.

**Key Papers**:
- Dexter, J. P., Prabakaran, S., & Gunawardena, J. (2019). A complex hierarchy of avoidance behaviors in a single-cell eukaryote. *Current Biology*, 29(24), 4323–4329.

## Mochizuki Kazufumi
**Institution**: Institute of Molecular Biotechnology (IMBA), Vienna  
**Key Contribution**: Mochizuki has been the leading researcher on the small RNA machinery that guides DNA elimination during ciliate macronuclear development. His work on Tetrahymena has established the molecular details of how scan RNAs (scnRNAs) mediate the comparison between the parental macronuclear genome (which specifies what to retain) and the developing macronucleus (in which the comparison is implemented), providing the mechanistic basis for the epigenetic inheritance of macronuclear genome organization. This work sits at the intersection of small RNA biology, genome evolution, and epigenetic inheritance.

**Key Papers**:
- Mochizuki, K., & Gorovsky, M. A. (2004). Small RNAs in genome rearrangement in Tetrahymena. *Current Opinion in Genetics & Development*, 14(2), 181–187.
- Mochizuki, K., Fine, N. A., Fujisawa, T., & Gorovsky, M. A. (2002). Analysis of a piwi-related gene implicates small RNAs in genome rearrangement in Tetrahymena. *Cell*, 110(6), 689–699.
