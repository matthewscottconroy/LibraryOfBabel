# Chapter 15: Key Researchers

## Nicole King
**Institution**: University of California, Berkeley, Howard Hughes Medical Institute  
**Key Contribution**: King is the leading researcher on choanoflagellate biology and its implications for animal origins. Her group sequenced the genome of Monosiga brevicollis (King et al., 2008), establishing the extent to which "animal-specific" gene families were present in the unicellular ancestor of animals. She has also led the development of Salpingoeca rosetta as a model choanoflagellate, including the discovery that rosette colony formation is induced by bacterial signals. Her work has fundamentally changed how biologists think about the origins of animal multicellularity and the molecular prerequisites for cell-cell signaling.

**Key Papers**:
- King, N., Westbrook, M. J., Young, S. L., Kuo, A., Abedin, M., Chapman, J., ... & Rokhsar, D. (2008). The genome of the choanoflagellate Monosiga brevicollis and the origin of metazoans. *Nature*, 451(7180), 783–788.
- Alegado, R. A., Brown, L. W., Cao, S., Dermenjian, R. K., Zuzow, R., Fairclough, S. R., ... & King, N. (2012). A bacterial sulfonolipid triggers multicellular development in the closest living relatives of animals. *eLife*, 1, e00013.

## David Kirk
**Institution**: Washington University in St. Louis (emeritus)  
**Key Contribution**: Kirk spent his career studying the genetics and development of Volvox and the volvocine algae. His work identified the regA gene as a master regulator of soma cell fate in Volvox and developed the conceptual framework for understanding the evolution of germ-soma differentiation as a series of identifiable genetic changes. His book *Volvox: Molecular-Genetic Origins of Multicellularity and Cellular Differentiation* (Cambridge University Press, 1998) remains the definitive treatment of the organism.

**Key Papers**:
- Kirk, D. L. (2005). A twelve-step program for evolving multicellularity and a division of labor. *BioEssays*, 27(3), 299–310.
- Kirk, M. M., Stark, K., Miller, S. M., Müller, W., Taillon, B. E., Bhatt, H., ... & Kirk, D. L. (1999). regA, a Volvox gene that plays a central role in germ-soma differentiation, encodes a novel regulatory protein. *Development*, 126(4), 639–647.

## John Maynard Smith (Historical) and Eörs Szathmáry
**Institution**: University of Sussex (Maynard Smith, emeritus at time of publication); Collegium Budapest / Eötvös University (Szathmáry)  
**Key Contribution**: Maynard Smith was one of the twentieth century's great evolutionary biologists, with foundational contributions to game theory in evolution, the evolution of sex, and the theory of life history. His collaboration with Szathmáry produced *The Major Transitions in Evolution* (1995), which provided the organizing framework for thinking about how evolutionary innovations in information storage and transmission produce new levels of biological organization. The book directly informs the discussion of how the multicellularity transition restructures cognition.

**Key Works**:
- Maynard Smith, J., & Szathmáry, E. (1995). *The Major Transitions in Evolution*. Oxford University Press.
- Maynard Smith, J., & Szathmáry, E. (1999). *The Origins of Life: From the Birth of Life to the Origin of Language*. Oxford University Press.

## Seth Grant
**Institution**: University of Edinburgh, Centre for Clinical Brain Sciences  
**Key Contribution**: Grant's research program on the "synaptome" — the complete molecular composition of the synapse — has led him and his collaborators to investigate the deep evolutionary origins of synaptic proteins, including their presence in choanoflagellates and other unicellular relatives of animals. His work provides the connection between comparative genomics and the molecular basis of synaptic plasticity, arguing that the machinery for synaptic learning has its roots in pre-neural cellular signaling systems.

**Key Papers**:
- Ryan, T. J., & Grant, S. G. N. (2009). The origin and evolution of synapses. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 10(10), 701–712.
- Bhatt, D. L., & Grant, S. G. N. (various). Synapse molecular complexity and the evolution of intelligence.
