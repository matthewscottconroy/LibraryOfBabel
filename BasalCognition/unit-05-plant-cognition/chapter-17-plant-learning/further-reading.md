# Chapter 17: Further Reading

## Annotated Bibliography

**Gagliano, M., Renton, M., Depczynski, M., & Mancuso, S. (2014). Experience teaches plants to learn faster and forget slower in environments where it matters. *Oecologia*, 175(1), 63–72.**
The original Mimosa habituation paper — read it carefully and critically. Note the experimental design, the controls included and not included, the sample sizes, and the statistical analyses. Then compare to the Jiang et al. replication paper. The difference between these two papers illustrates how empirically contested science actually works.

**Gagliano, M., Vyazovskiy, V. V., Borbély, A. A., Grimonprez, M., & Depczynski, M. (2016). Learning by association in plants. *Scientific Reports*, 6, 38427.**
The associative learning paper. Read this in conjunction with the critiques in subsequent correspondence. Pay particular attention to the controls for alternative explanations and to the criteria used to claim that the observed behavior constitutes associative learning.

**Jiang, F., Jeschke, W. D., Hartung, W., & Turner, N. C. (2018). Does Mimosa pudica habituate to repeated touch and drop stimuli? *Biologia Plantarum*, 62(2), 377–384.**
The replication study that raises significant questions about the persistence of the Mimosa habituation effect. Essential for understanding why the original findings remain contested.

**Sung, S., & Amasino, R. M. (2004). Vernalization in Arabidopsis thaliana is mediated by the PHD finger protein VIN3. *Nature*, 427(6970), 159–164.**
A landmark paper in the molecular characterization of vernalization, identifying VIN3 as the cold-induced chromatin modifier that initiates FLC silencing. For students who want to understand the molecular basis of plant memory, this paper and the subsequent literature from the Dean laboratory are essential.

**De Lucía, F., Crevillen, P., Jones, A. M., Greb, T., & Dean, C. (2008). A PHD-polycomb repressive complex 2 triggers the epigenetic silencing of FLC during vernalization. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 105(44), 16831–16836.**
Elucidates the mechanism by which PRC2 is recruited to the FLC locus during vernalization, providing the molecular basis for the stable, heritable silencing of FLC through subsequent cell divisions. A key mechanistic paper in the vernalization story.

**Crisp, P. A., Ganguly, D., Eichten, S. R., Borevitz, J. O., & Pogson, B. J. (2016). Reconsidering plant memory: Intersections between stress recovery, RNA turnover, and epigenetics. *Science Advances*, 2(2), e1501340.**
A thoughtful review that attempts to rigorously define what "plant memory" means and to distinguish genuine memory phenomena from resetting/recovery processes. Particularly valuable for its discussion of the criteria that should be used to evaluate plant memory claims.

**Conrath, U., Beckers, G. J., Langenbach, C. J., & Jaskiewicz, M. R. (2015). Priming for enhanced defense. *Annual Review of Phytopathology*, 53, 97–119.**
The most comprehensive review of priming in plant immunity, covering the phenomenology, ecology, molecular mechanisms, and evolutionary significance of priming. Essential for understanding SAR and the chromatin-based mechanisms of immune memory.

**Thompson, R. F., & Spencer, W. A. (1966). Habituation: A model phenomenon for the study of neuronal substrates of behavior. *Psychological Review*, 73(1), 16–43.**
The classic paper defining habituation and specifying the operational criteria that distinguish genuine habituation from sensory adaptation and fatigue. Reading the original criteria paper is essential for evaluating whether Mimosa's behavior satisfies them.

**Rasmann, S., De Vos, M., Casteel, C. L., Tian, D., Halitschke, R., Sun, J. Y., ... & Agrawal, A. A. (2012). Herbivory in the previous generation primes plants for enhanced insect resistance. *Plant Physiology*, 158(2), 854–863.**
A study demonstrating that plants whose parents experienced herbivore attack show enhanced resistance to herbivory themselves — one of the cleaner demonstrations of transgenerational priming in plants. Useful as a counterpoint to more contested transgenerational memory claims.
