# Chapter 17: Key Researchers

## Monica Gagliano
**Institution**: University of Sydney / Southern Cross University (has held positions at multiple institutions)  
**Key Contribution**: Gagliano has been the most prolific and provocative researcher in the field of plant behavioral ecology and plant learning, publishing a series of papers claiming to demonstrate habituation, associative learning, and other cognitively sophisticated behaviors in plants. Her work has attracted enormous media attention and has been genuinely influential in making plant cognition a topic of serious scientific discussion. Whether her specific experimental results will ultimately be validated by replication and mechanistic investigation remains to be seen. Her broader intellectual project — making the case that plants deserve to be taken seriously as cognitive subjects — has had real effects on how the field is framed.

**Key Papers**:
- Gagliano, M., Renton, M., Depczynski, M., & Mancuso, S. (2014). Experience teaches plants to learn faster and forget slower in environments where it matters. *Oecologia*, 175(1), 63–72.
- Gagliano, M., Vyazovskiy, V. V., Borbély, A. A., Grimonprez, M., & Depczynski, M. (2016). Learning by association in plants. *Scientific Reports*, 6, 38427.

## Richard Amasino
**Institution**: University of Wisconsin-Madison, Department of Biochemistry  
**Key Contribution**: Amasino has led the molecular characterization of vernalization in Arabidopsis thaliana, identifying the key genes (VIN3, FLC) and mechanisms that underlie the chromatin-based memory of winter. His laboratory has established the FLC/VIN3 system as a model for epigenetic memory in plants, and the mechanistic picture that has emerged from his group's work is one of the clearest accounts of biological memory at the molecular level in any organism.

**Key Papers**:
- Sung, S., & Amasino, R. M. (2004). Vernalization in Arabidopsis thaliana is mediated by the PHD finger protein VIN3. *Nature*, 427(6970), 159–164.
- Amasino, R. M., & Michaels, S. D. (2010). The timing of flowering. *Plant Physiology*, 154(2), 516–520.

## Caroline Dean
**Institution**: John Innes Centre, Norwich (UK)  
**Key Contribution**: Dean's laboratory has made major contributions to the molecular characterization of FLC regulation and vernalization. Her group's work on the noncoding RNAs involved in FLC regulation and on the PRC2 recruitment mechanism provided the mechanistic basis for understanding how cold exposure silences FLC through a Polycomb-based epigenetic switch. Her research on FLC has become a model system for studying how quantitative epigenetic memory is established and maintained.

**Key Papers**:
- De Lucía, F., Crevillen, P., Jones, A. M., Greb, T., & Dean, C. (2008). A PHD-polycomb repressive complex 2 triggers the epigenetic silencing of FLC during vernalization. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 105(44), 16831–16836.
- Heo, J. B., & Sung, S. (2011). Vernalization-mediated epigenetic silencing by a long intronic noncoding RNA. *Science*, 331(6013), 76–79.

## Uwe Conrath
**Institution**: RWTH Aachen University, Plant Physiology  
**Key Contribution**: Conrath's group has been central to characterizing the molecular mechanisms of priming in plant immunity — the "readiness state" that allows previously stressed plants to respond faster and more strongly to subsequent challenges. His work has identified the chromatin modifications associated with the primed state and has contributed to understanding how priming is established, maintained, and eventually reversed.

**Key Papers**:
- Conrath, U., Beckers, G. J., Langenbach, C. J., & Jaskiewicz, M. R. (2015). Priming for enhanced defense. *Annual Review of Phytopathology*, 53, 97–119.
- Jaskiewicz, M., Conrath, U., & Peterhänsel, C. (2011). Chromatin modification acts as a memory for systemic acquired resistance in the plant stress response. *EMBO Reports*, 12(1), 50–55.
