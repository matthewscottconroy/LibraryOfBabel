# Chapter 19: Key Researchers

## Suzanne Simard
**Institution**: University of British Columbia, Department of Forest and Conservation Sciences  
**Key Contribution**: Simard's 1997 Nature paper demonstrating carbon transfer between ectomycorrhizal trees through fungal networks was the foundational empirical result that launched the "wood wide web" concept. Her subsequent research has explored the extent to which mycorrhizal networks facilitate resource sharing among forest trees, including the hypothesis that "mother trees" preferentially support kin seedlings through these networks. Her popular writing (*Finding the Mother Tree*, 2021) has brought this research to wide public attention. Scientists should engage with both the empirically solid aspects of her work and the more speculative claims that have entered the popular narrative.

**Key Papers**:
- Simard, S. W., Perry, D. A., Jones, M. D., Myrold, D. D., Durall, D. M., & Molina, R. (1997). Net transfer of carbon between ectomycorrhizal tree species in the field. *Nature*, 388(6642), 579–582.

## Ted Turlings
**Institution**: University of Neuchâtel, Institute of Biology  
**Key Contribution**: Turlings has been a leading researcher on tritrophic chemical signaling — the herbivore-induced plant volatiles that attract predatory and parasitoid insects. His work has characterized the specificity of herbivore-induced VOC blends in maize and other crops and demonstrated their effectiveness in attracting natural enemies of the attacking herbivores. This research has both fundamental significance for understanding plant communication and applied significance for biological pest control.

**Key Papers**:
- Turlings, T. C. J., Tumlinson, J. H., & Lewis, W. J. (1990). Exploitation of herbivore-induced plant odors by host-seeking parasitic wasps. *Science*, 250(4985), 1251–1253.
- Turlings, T. C. J., & Erb, M. (2018). Tritrophic interactions mediated by herbivore-induced plant volatiles: Mechanisms, ecological relevance, and application potential. *Annual Review of Entomology*, 63, 433–452.

## Jurriaan Ton
**Institution**: University of Sheffield, Department of Animal and Plant Sciences  
**Key Contribution**: Ton has made significant contributions to understanding the molecular basis of plant defense priming, including SAR and the epigenetic mechanisms that maintain the primed state. His work on defense signal transmission through mycorrhizal networks and on the ecological significance of priming in field conditions has helped establish priming as a major mechanism of plant immunity.

**Key Papers**:
- Ton, J., D'Alessandro, M., Jourdie, V., Jakab, G., Karlen, D., Held, M., ... & Turlings, T. C. J. (2007). Priming by airborne signals boosts direct and indirect resistance in maize. *Plant Journal*, 49(1), 16–26.

## Inderjit Singh
**Institution**: University of Delhi, Department of Environmental Studies  
**Key Contribution**: Inderjit (who publishes under his first name alone) has been a leading authority on the science of allelopathy, working to establish rigorous methodological standards for demonstrating allelopathic effects in complex ecological settings and to distinguish allelopathic inhibition from resource competition and indirect effects. His critical approach to the field has improved its scientific rigor.

**Key Papers**:
- Inderjit, & Duke, S. O. (2003). Ecophysiological aspects of allelopathy. *Planta*, 217(4), 529–539.
- Inderjit, & Callaway, R. M. (2003). Experimental designs for the study of allelopathy. *Plant and Soil*, 256(1), 1–11.

## Merlin Sheldrake
**Institution**: Independent researcher (associated with University of Cambridge)  
**Key Contribution**: Sheldrake is primarily known as the author of *Entangled Life* (2020), the most widely read popular science account of fungal biology including mycorrhizal networks. While his scientific credentials are real — he completed a PhD on mycorrhizal networks — his profile in this list reflects his role as a communicator of the science to general audiences. His work illustrates both the opportunities and the risks of science communication: the book has done enormous good in bringing attention to fungal biology, but its enthusiastic framing has contributed to some of the overclaiming about mycorrhizal "intelligence" in popular culture.

**Key Works**:
- Sheldrake, M. (2020). *Entangled Life: How Fungi Make Our Worlds, Change Our Minds & Shape Our Futures*. Random House.
