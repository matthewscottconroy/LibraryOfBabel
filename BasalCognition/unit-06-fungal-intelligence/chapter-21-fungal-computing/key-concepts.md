# Chapter 21 Key Concepts: Fungal Computing and Unconventional Intelligence

---

## Unconventional Computing
A broad category encompassing any approach to computation that does not rely on the silicon transistor-based digital architecture that dominates modern computing. Unconventional computing substrates include chemical reaction-diffusion systems, DNA molecules, quantum-mechanical systems, mechanical metamaterials, and biological organisms. The appeal of unconventional computing is not that these substrates are generally superior to silicon but that they may perform certain classes of computation naturally and cheaply that are difficult or expensive in a conventional framework. Fungal computing falls in this category, along with Physarum computing and other forms of biological computation.

---

## Spike Train
A time series of discrete electrical events — spikes or action potentials — recorded from a neuron or (in this context) from a fungal hypha. The information content of a spike train resides primarily in the temporal pattern of spikes — the inter-spike intervals, the clustering into bursts, the response to stimuli — rather than in the properties of individual spikes, which are typically stereotyped. Analysis of spike train statistics is a major subdiscipline of computational neuroscience, and the methods developed in that context have been applied by Adamatzky and colleagues to fungal electrical recordings.

---

## Zipf's Law
An empirical regularity in which the frequency of an element (a word in a text, a city by population, a spike pattern in a spike train) is inversely proportional to its rank: the most common element occurs twice as often as the second most common, three times as often as the third, and so on. Zipf distributions arise from a wide variety of generative processes, not all of which involve language or communication. The presence of a Zipf distribution in a signal does not, by itself, establish that the signal constitutes communication; additional evidence about semantic content and receiver response is required.

---

## Logical Gate
A device that implements a logical operation — AND, OR, NOT, XOR, etc. — by mapping input values to output values according to a defined truth table. All digital computation can be decomposed into combinations of logical gates. The implementation of logical gates in biological substrates — using the growth of Physarum through spatial configurations, or the propagation of electrical signals through mycelium — is a key demonstration in unconventional computing that shows physical systems can implement formal computational operations.

---

## Physical Computation
The exploitation of the natural dynamics of a physical system to solve computational problems. In physical computation, the physics of the substrate performs the computation: a mycelium finding the shortest path between two food sources "computes" the shortest path because the physics of cytoplasmic flow naturally amplifies the shortest-path route. Physical computation does not require explicit representation of the problem or any deliberate manipulation of symbols; the solution emerges from the physical dynamics of the system when those dynamics are appropriately configured.

---

## Wetware
Computing systems that use biological materials — cells, proteins, nucleic acids, or whole organisms — as their physical substrate. "Wetware" is a portmanteau of "hardware" and the wetness associated with biological systems. The term encompasses a range of technologies, from well-established biosensors and DNA computing to more speculative proposals such as neural organoid computing and fungal computing. The distinguishing feature of wetware is that its physical substrate is alive or derived from living material, and therefore possesses properties — self-assembly, self-repair, adaptation, chemical specificity — that are difficult or impossible to replicate in conventional hardware.

---

## Embodied Cognition
The theoretical position that cognitive processes are not confined to the brain or central nervous system but are constituted by the ongoing coupling of an organism with its environment through its body. Embodied cognition emphasizes that the body's physical properties, and its sensorimotor interactions with the environment, are not merely peripheral to cognition but are part of its constitutive basis. For fungal systems, embodied cognition provides a framework for understanding how mycelial intelligence might work without any central processing: the mycelium's "cognition" is its physical coupling with its chemical environment, not something that happens in any particular location within the network.

---

## Enactivism
A specific version of embodied cognition, associated primarily with Francisco Varela, Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch, that holds that cognition is not the internal processing of externally acquired information but the enactment of meaning through the organism's ongoing sensorimotor engagement with its world. On the enactivist account, there is no sharp boundary between organism and environment — the two are mutually constituted through their coupling. Mycelium is a compelling case study for enactivism: its identity as an individual is partly constituted by its network of environmental connections, and its "computation" is inseparable from its physical growth through space.

---

## Semantic Content
The property of a signal whereby it refers to, or encodes information about, something outside the signal system itself. Semantic content is essential to language and communication but is not a property of all structured signals. Structured electrical activity in mycelium shows non-random temporal organization, but whether that organization encodes information about environmental states — whether specific spike patterns correspond to specific environmental conditions in a reliable, functional way — is the key unresolved question in the "fungal language" debate. Demonstrating semantic content requires showing both that specific patterns covary reliably with specific conditions and that the receiving system uses that covariation to guide its behavior.
