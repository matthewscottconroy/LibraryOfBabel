# Further Reading: Chapter 30 — Levin's Cognitive Light Cone and Scale-Free Cognition

---

**Levin, M. (2019). The computational boundary of a "self": Developmental bioelectricity drives multicellularity and scale-free cognition. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 10, 2688.**
The foundational paper for this chapter's framework. Levin lays out the cognitive light cone concept, the competency hierarchy, and the role of bioelectricity in extending individual cellular cognitive capacities into multicellular coordination. The paper is readable by graduate students with backgrounds in either biology or cognitive science and provides a clear articulation of what the framework claims and what remains speculative.

**Kriegman, S., Blackiston, D., Levin, M., & Bongard, J. (2020). A scalable pipeline for designing reconfigurable organisms. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 117(4), 1853–1859.**
The original Xenobot paper. Describes the computational pipeline for designing living machines from frog cells and the experimental validation of the designs. The combination of evolutionary computation and wet biology makes this paper representative of a new kind of "designed cognition" research. Essential reading for understanding what Xenobots actually are and what experimental work underlies the more speculative claims.

**Levin, M. (2012). Morphogenetic fields in embryogenesis, regeneration, and cancer: Non-local control of complex patterning. *Biosystems*, 109(3), 243–261.**
An overview of the role of bioelectrical morphogenetic fields in development and regeneration. Levin reviews evidence from multiple systems for long-range bioelectrical control of patterning and discusses the implications for cancer biology. The paper provides experimental grounding for the theoretical claims about bioelectrical attractors.

**Pezzulo, G., & Levin, M. (2016). Top-down models in biology: Explanation and control of complex living systems above the molecular level. *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, 13(124), 20160555.**
A theoretical paper arguing for the value of top-down models — models that describe the behavior of organisms in terms of goals and targets rather than bottom-up molecular mechanisms — in biology. The paper engages with philosophy of science debates about reduction and emergence and argues that cognitive-level descriptions are not merely heuristic but are indispensable for prediction and control of complex biological systems.

**Baluška, F., & Levin, M. (2016). On having no head: Cognition throughout biological systems. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 7, 902.**
A collaborative paper arguing that cognitive capacities are distributed across all biological scales and are not localized to neural systems. The paper surveys evidence for cognition-like processes in plants, unicellular organisms, immune cells, and other non-neural systems, and argues that the assumption that cognition requires a brain is unsupported by the evidence. A good entry point for the broader intellectual context of basal cognition research.

**Bhatt, D.L., et al.** *(Note: For bioelectricity fundamentals, readers should consult)* **Blackiston, D., Shomrat, T., & Levin, M. (2015). The stability of memories during brain remodeling: A perspective. *Communicative & Integrative Biology*, 8(5), e1073424.**
An examination of memory persistence during radical brain remodeling in planaria — the controversial observation that planaria trained to specific behaviors appear to retain those memories after decapitation and brain regeneration. The paper discusses mechanisms and implications carefully, without overclaiming. Illustrative of the kind of striking experimental findings that the Levin laboratory generates.

**Herrera-Rincon, C., Pai, V.P., Bhatt, D.L., Paré, J.F., & Levin, M. (2017). The brain is required for normal muscle and nerve patterning during early *Xenopus* development. *Nature Communications*, 8(1), 587.**
An experimental paper showing that the brain of a *Xenopus* tadpole plays an unexpected role in the correct patterning of muscles and nerves in the developing tail — even before the nervous system is connected. The brain influences development through bioelectrical and molecular signals that travel beyond neural connections. Illustrates how bioelectrical communication can extend the cognitive light cone of a neural structure into developmental processes.

**Turing, A.M. (1952). The chemical basis of morphogenesis. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 237(641), 37–72.**
The foundational paper on reaction-diffusion systems as a mechanism for biological pattern formation. Turing's mathematical analysis of how two chemicals interacting through diffusion can spontaneously generate spatial patterns provides the original formal framework within which bioelectrical morphogenesis is now understood. Reading this paper alongside Levin's work gives a sense of how far the field has come in 70 years.

**Wolpert, L. (1969). Positional information and the spatial pattern of cellular differentiation. *Journal of Theoretical Biology*, 25(1), 1–47.**
The foundational paper on positional information in development — the idea that cells are specified according to their position in the embryo and that this position is encoded in a concentration gradient of a signaling molecule. Wolpert's "French flag model" remains the canonical framework for positional patterning, and Levin's bioelectrical work can be seen as specifying one mechanism by which positional information is encoded and maintained.

**Deacon, T.W. (2011). *Incomplete Nature: How Mind Emerged from Matter*. W.W. Norton.**
A philosophical and theoretical treatment of emergence that is directly relevant to the scale-free cognition framework. Deacon develops the concept of "teleodynamics" — the irreducible goal-directedness that emerges in certain self-organizing systems — in a way that provides philosophical grounding for Levin's empirical claims about goal-directed morphogenesis. The book is demanding but rewarding for students who want a philosophical framework for thinking about emergence in biology.
