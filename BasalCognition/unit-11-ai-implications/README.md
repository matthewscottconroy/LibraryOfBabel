# Unit XI: Basal Cognition and Artificial Intelligence

---

> *"The field of AI has spent seventy years trying to build intelligence from the top down: logic, rules, symbols, neural networks that mimic the brain. Basal cognition suggests there is another path — the path that life has taken for four billion years. What would it mean to build intelligence from the bottom up, the way evolution did?"*

---

## Unit Introduction

Artificial intelligence and basal cognition are, on their surfaces, very different fields. AI builds computational systems; basal cognition studies biological ones. AI is concerned with performance on tasks; basal cognition is concerned with the organizational principles of living systems. AI has practical commercial applications; basal cognition is, largely, basic science.

But the fields are connected by a question that neither can answer alone: what is intelligence, and how does it work?

AI has been asking this question from the engineering direction: build things that exhibit intelligent behavior, and learn from what works. Basal cognition asks it from the biological direction: study organisms that exhibit intelligent behavior, and learn from what evolution found. The two approaches, pursued simultaneously, illuminate each other.

This unit examines three kinds of connection between basal cognition and AI:

1. **Lessons from biology for AI**: What can the study of slime mold optimization algorithms, bacterial decision-making, and plant signaling networks teach us about designing more robust, adaptive, and energy-efficient artificial systems?

2. **Unconventional and biological computing**: Can living systems be used as computing substrates? Can their cognitive principles be directly implemented in hardware or wetware?

3. **The embodiment challenge**: Why do current AI systems, despite their impressive performance on many tasks, lack the kind of flexible, embodied, real-world intelligence that bacteria and plants exhibit? What does basal cognition suggest about what is missing?

## The Deepest Connection

The deepest connection between basal cognition and AI is philosophical rather than technical. Both fields are grappling with the question of what intelligence requires — what organizational features a system must have to count as genuinely intelligent rather than merely sophisticated. Basal cognition research suggests that the requirements are less demanding than we thought: no neurons, no brain, no centralized architecture. What is required is the right kind of functional organization — the sensing-integrating-acting triad, operating in service of the system's continued organization.

This has implications for AI that go beyond specific architectural suggestions. It suggests that the goal of building intelligence should not be to replicate the human brain, or even to replicate the human mind, but to understand the organizational principles that make living systems cognitively effective — and to implement those principles in whatever substrate is available.

The simplest bacterium and the most powerful AI system in the world share a fundamental property: they are systems that process information in service of some goal. Understanding what makes one more effective, more adaptive, more genuinely "intelligent" than the other — in specific contexts, for specific purposes — is one of the most important open questions connecting basal cognition to artificial intelligence.
