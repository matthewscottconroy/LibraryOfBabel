# Chapter 34: Further Reading

## Foundational Works

**Brooks, R. A. (1991). Intelligence without representation. *Artificial Intelligence*, 47(1–3), 139–159.**
The founding document of behavior-based robotics. Brooks's argument against internal representation remains one of the clearest and most provocative contributions to cognitive science. Read this paper carefully, then read it again after reading the biological material in this book — it gains depth from both directions.

**Pfeifer, R., & Bongard, J. (2007). *How the Body Shapes the Way We Think: A New View of Intelligence*. MIT Press.**
An accessible but rigorous account of embodied cognition in both biology and robotics. The authors develop the concept of morphological computation — the idea that the body performs cognitive work — and illustrate it with examples from animal locomotion, soft robotics, and developmental biology. Essential reading for anyone working at the intersection of AI and biological intelligence.

**Dorigo, M., & Stützle, T. (2004). *Ant Colony Optimization*. MIT Press.**
The definitive technical treatment of ACO by the field's founders. Covers the mathematical foundations, the main algorithm variants, and applications across a range of combinatorial optimization problems. More technical than the other entries here, but invaluable if you plan to work with these algorithms.

## Swarm Intelligence and Collective Computation

**Bonabeau, E., Dorigo, M., & Theraulaz, G. (1999). *Swarm Intelligence: From Natural to Artificial Systems*. Oxford University Press.**
The first comprehensive treatment of swarm intelligence as a scientific field. Covers ant colony optimization, particle swarm optimization, and the biological systems that inspired them, with careful attention to the relationship between the models and the biology. An excellent bridge between biology and engineering.

**Camazine, S., Deneubourg, J.-L., Franks, N. R., Sneyd, J., Theraulaz, G., & Bonabeau, E. (2001). *Self-Organization in Biological Systems*. Princeton University Press.**
A rigorous treatment of self-organization across biological scales, from cellular pattern formation to insect colonies. The authors show how similar mathematical principles — positive feedback, negative feedback, and randomness — underlie self-organization from bacterial biofilms to bird flocks. This book provides the biological foundation for understanding why swarm AI works.

**Kennedy, J., Eberhart, R. C., & Shi, Y. (2001). *Swarm Intelligence*. Morgan Kaufmann.**
The authoritative treatment of particle swarm optimization and its variants. Combines algorithmic detail with discussion of the biological inspiration and the broader theoretical context of swarm intelligence.

## Neuromorphic Computing

**Furber, S. (2016). Large-scale neuromorphic computing systems. *Journal of Neural Engineering*, 13(5), 051001.**
A clear overview of the neuromorphic computing landscape by one of the field's pioneers (creator of the SpiNNaker architecture at Manchester). Discusses the motivation, the current state of the art, and the remaining challenges with admirable clarity. An excellent entry point for students without an engineering background.

**Maass, W. (1997). Networks of spiking neurons: The third generation of neural network models. *Neural Networks*, 10(9), 1659–1671.**
Maass's foundational paper establishing the theoretical computational power of spiking neural networks. Shows that spiking networks are, in principle, more computationally expressive than rate-coded networks, providing the theoretical justification for taking the SNN approach seriously.

**Indiveri, G., & Liu, S.-C. (2015). Memory and information processing in neuromorphic systems. *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 103(8), 1379–1397.**
A thorough review of how memory is implemented in neuromorphic hardware, with careful attention to the differences between biological and silicon implementations. Essential for understanding both what has been achieved and what remains to be done.

## Embodied Cognition

**Varela, F. J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience*. MIT Press.**
The philosophical foundation of enactivism. Despite its age, this remains the clearest statement of the thesis that cognition is constituted by organism-environment interaction rather than internal representation. Challenging but essential.

**Pfeifer, R., Lungarella, M., & Iida, F. (2007). Self-organization, embodiment, and biologically inspired robotics. *Science*, 318(5853), 1088–1093.**
A compact but rich review of morphological computation and embodied robotics, with striking examples. Accessible to readers without a robotics background and useful for conveying the core ideas quickly.

**Rus, D., & Tolley, M. T. (2015). Design, fabrication and control of soft robots. *Nature*, 521(7553), 467–475.**
A review of soft robotics by two leaders of the field. Covers the biological inspiration, the materials science, and the control challenges, and gives a sense of the state of the art. The discussion of how soft bodies perform morphological computation is particularly relevant to the themes of this chapter.
