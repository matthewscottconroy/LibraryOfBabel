# Chapter 34: Key Concepts

**Subsumption Architecture** — A layered control architecture for robots in which each layer generates complete behaviors by reading sensors and producing motor outputs directly. Higher layers subsume (inhibit or modify) lower layers rather than commanding them. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The architecture was explicitly inspired by the behavioral hierarchy of insect nervous systems, and it demonstrated that robust intelligent behavior could emerge without rich internal world models — anticipating theoretical insights from enactivism and observations about non-neural cognition.

**Stigmergy** — Indirect coordination through environmental modification, in which an agent's action modifies the environment in a way that influences the subsequent actions of other agents. First described in termite nest construction, now understood as a general principle of collective intelligence. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Stigmergy is the mechanism by which many non-neural collectives — ant colonies, mycelial networks, bacterial biofilms — implement distributed problem-solving without central control, and is the inspiration for ant colony optimization algorithms.

**Morphological Computation** — The exploitation of a body's physical properties (shape, compliance, mass distribution, dynamics) to perform computational work, reducing the burden on explicit neural or algorithmic processing. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Morphological computation is ubiquitous in biological systems: plant roots exploit the mechanics of growing tissue to navigate soil; slime molds use cytoplasmic flow dynamics to implement path optimization. Understanding this principle is necessary for understanding how non-neural cognition achieves what it does.

**Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)** — A family of probabilistic optimization algorithms inspired by the foraging behavior of ant colonies, in which artificial "ants" deposit virtual pheromone on solution components and follow pheromone trails probabilistically, with evaporation preventing premature convergence. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* ACO is a proof of concept that the distributed, stigmergic computation of biological collectives can be formalized and applied to difficult engineering problems, establishing a productive dialogue between biology and computer science.

**Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs)** — Artificial neural networks in which neurons communicate via discrete action potentials (spikes), as in biological nervous systems, rather than continuous real-valued signals. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* SNNs are the software foundation for neuromorphic computing, and their energy efficiency advantage over rate-coded networks mirrors the efficiency of biological computation. The challenge of training SNNs reflects genuine biological mysteries about how the nervous system achieves efficient learning.

**Neuromorphic Computing** — The design of hardware that implements neural computation using principles derived from biology — co-located memory and processing, spiking dynamics, local learning rules — rather than the von Neumann architecture of standard digital computers. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Neuromorphic computing takes seriously the biological claim that efficient intelligence requires architectural compatibility with the physics of computation — a claim supported by the energy efficiency of biological cognitive systems at every scale.

**The Von Neumann Bottleneck** — The throughput limitation imposed by the separation of memory and processing in standard computer architectures, requiring data to traverse a bus for every operation. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The bottleneck does not exist in biological computation, where memory (synaptic weights, molecular state) and processing are co-located. Understanding this architectural difference illuminates why biological systems can perform adaptive computation far more efficiently than silicon.

**Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP)** — A Hebbian learning rule in biological synapses in which synaptic strength is modified based on the precise relative timing of pre- and postsynaptic spikes: if the presynaptic neuron fires before the postsynaptic neuron, the synapse is strengthened; if after, it is weakened. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* STDP is a local learning rule — it requires only information available at the synapse — and is therefore implementable in distributed systems without a global supervisor. It represents one possible bridge between the local learning of cellular systems and the global adaptive behavior of networks.

**Embodiment Gap** — The discrepancy between the performance of AI systems on benchmark tasks and their performance on tasks requiring real-time physical interaction with an unstructured environment. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The embodiment gap reflects the absence of the sensorimotor grounding that biological cognitive systems — at every level of organization — possess. Understanding how non-neural biological systems achieve robust sensorimotor coupling may provide insight into how to close this gap in engineered systems.
