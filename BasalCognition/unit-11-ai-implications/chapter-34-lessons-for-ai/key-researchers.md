# Chapter 34: Key Researchers

## Rodney Brooks
**Institution:** MIT (emeritus); iRobot Corporation (co-founder)
**Primary contributions:** Subsumption architecture, behavior-based robotics, the anti-representationalist thesis in AI. Brooks's 1991 paper "Intelligence Without Representation" challenged the dominant planning-based AI paradigm by arguing that intelligent behavior could be generated by direct sensorimotor coupling without internal world models.

**Key works:**
- Brooks, R. A. (1991). Intelligence without representation. *Artificial Intelligence*, 47(1–3), 139–159.
- Brooks, R. A. (1986). A robust layered control system for a mobile robot. *IEEE Journal on Robotics and Automation*, 2(1), 14–23.

**Characterization:** An engineer-philosopher who built his arguments in hardware, forcing a reckoning with what embodied intelligence actually requires.

---

## Marco Dorigo
**Institution:** Université Libre de Bruxelles, IRIDIA Laboratory
**Primary contributions:** Ant Colony Optimization and the broader field of swarm intelligence. Dorigo formalized the stigmergic computation of real ant colonies into a family of optimization algorithms that have been applied to hundreds of engineering problems.

**Key works:**
- Dorigo, M., Maniezzo, V., & Colorni, A. (1996). Ant system: Optimization by a colony of cooperating agents. *IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics, Part B*, 26(1), 29–41.
- Dorigo, M., & Gambardella, L. M. (1997). Ant colony system: A cooperative learning approach to the traveling salesman problem. *IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation*, 1(1), 53–66.
- Dorigo, M., & Stützle, T. (2004). *Ant Colony Optimization*. MIT Press.

**Characterization:** The translator of biological collective intelligence into mathematical optimization, and the founder of swarm intelligence as a scientific discipline.

---

## Rolf Pfeifer
**Institution:** University of Zurich (emeritus), Shanghai Jiao Tong University
**Primary contributions:** Embodied cognition and morphological computation in robotics. Pfeifer developed the concept that the body itself performs cognitive work — that shape, compliance, and dynamics are part of the computational system — and built robots that demonstrated this principle.

**Key works:**
- Pfeifer, R., & Scheier, C. (1999). *Understanding Intelligence*. MIT Press.
- Pfeifer, R., & Bongard, J. (2007). *How the Body Shapes the Way We Think*. MIT Press.
- Pfeifer, R., Lungarella, M., & Iida, F. (2007). Self-organization, embodiment, and biologically inspired robotics. *Science*, 318(5853), 1088–1093.

**Characterization:** The principal architect of morphological computation theory, who showed that the distinction between brain and body is less clean than classical AI assumed.

---

## Giacomo Indiveri
**Institution:** ETH Zurich, Institute of Neuroinformatics
**Primary contributions:** Neuromorphic circuit design, analog VLSI implementations of neural computation, and the theoretical basis of neuromorphic engineering. Indiveri's laboratory builds silicon circuits that implement neural computation using analog electronics — exploiting the physical properties of transistors to implement biologically realistic neural dynamics.

**Key works:**
- Indiveri, G., Linares-Barranco, B., Hamilton, T. J., et al. (2011). Neuromorphic silicon neuron circuits. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 5, 73.
- Indiveri, G., & Liu, S.-C. (2015). Memory and information processing in neuromorphic systems. *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 103(8), 1379–1397.

**Characterization:** The leading theorist and builder of analog neuromorphic circuits, whose work bridges computational neuroscience and very-large-scale integration engineering.

---

## Carver Mead
**Institution:** Caltech (emeritus)
**Primary contributions:** Invented the field of neuromorphic engineering with his seminal work on analog VLSI circuits that implement neural computation. Mead recognized in the 1980s that silicon transistors operating in the subthreshold regime (where current is exponential in voltage) naturally implement the same I-V relationships as biological ion channels.

**Key works:**
- Mead, C. (1989). *Analog VLSI and Neural Systems*. Addison-Wesley.
- Mead, C. (1990). Neuromorphic electronic systems. *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 78(10), 1629–1636.

**Characterization:** The godfather of neuromorphic computing, who noticed that silicon and biology are, at the physical level, more similar than they appear — and built the first chips to exploit that similarity.

---

## Josh Bongard
**Institution:** University of Vermont, Morphology, Evolution and Cognition Laboratory
**Primary contributions:** Evolutionary robotics, morphogenetic robotics, and the study of how body plan and cognitive control co-evolve. Bongard's work on self-modeling robots — systems that can build internal models of their own bodies and use these to generate adaptive locomotion after physical damage — is particularly relevant to basal cognition.

**Key works:**
- Bongard, J., Zykov, V., & Lipson, H. (2006). Resilient machines through continuous self-modeling. *Science*, 314(5802), 1118–1121.
- Pfeifer, R., & Bongard, J. (2007). *How the Body Shapes the Way We Think*. MIT Press.

**Characterization:** An evolutionary roboticist who designs machines that discover what they are — and in doing so, illuminates how biological organisms achieve the same feat.
