# Chapter 35: Key Concepts

**DNA Computing** — A form of computation that uses the hybridization properties of single-stranded DNA to perform parallel search through combinatorial solution spaces. Candidate solutions are represented as DNA sequences; biochemical filtering selects correct solutions. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* DNA computing demonstrates that the information-processing capacity of nucleic acids — developed by evolution for storage and expression of genetic information — is also a general-purpose computational resource, supporting the view that computation is a physical process implementable in any appropriately organized substrate.

**Strand Displacement Cascade** — A molecular computing architecture in which single-stranded DNA "input" molecules react with double-stranded DNA "gate" complexes through toehold-mediated strand displacement, releasing "output" strands that serve as inputs to downstream gates. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Strand displacement cascades implement Boolean logic and even neural network-like computations using only DNA hybridization thermodynamics — demonstrating molecular computation without proteins or metabolic energy input beyond the initial reactant concentrations.

**Repressilator** — A synthetic gene circuit in which three genes each repress the next in a cycle (A represses B, B represses C, C represses A), generating sustained oscillations in protein levels in *E. coli*. Designed by Elowitz and Leibler (2000). *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The repressilator was the first demonstration that gene regulatory networks could be rationally designed to implement specific dynamic behaviors, founding synthetic biology and establishing genetic circuits as a cognitive engineering platform.

**Genetic Toggle Switch** — A synthetic bistable circuit in which two mutually repressing genes can exist in either of two stable states (one gene on, the other off) and can be switched between states by an appropriate input signal. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The toggle switch implements one-bit binary memory in gene regulatory dynamics, showing that cellular information storage can be engineered from first principles — and providing a model for how natural bistable gene circuits implement long-term cellular memory.

**Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) Reaction** — A chemical oscillating reaction in which the oxidation of an organic substrate by bromate, catalyzed by a metal ion, generates periodic changes in color and, in thin layers, spiral waves of oxidation that propagate through the medium as an excitable wave. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The BZ reaction demonstrates computation in non-biological chemical systems — spiral waves implement logical operations through collision dynamics, and BZ media can solve maze problems and implement image processing. This establishes chemistry itself as a computational substrate independent of biology.

**Xenobot** — A novel living machine assembled from pluripotent frog (*Xenopus laevis*) cells according to a body plan designed by an evolutionary algorithm; xenobots are neither conventional organisms nor machines, but self-propelled biological entities capable, under some conditions, of kinematic self-replication. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Xenobots demonstrate that designed configurations of biological cells exhibit emergent behaviors — including self-replication — not present in the individual cells and not explicitly programmed, showing that biological cognition is a property of cell organization rather than individual cells.

**Morphogenetic Computation** — The information processing performed by developmental biological systems as they generate spatial pattern and body plan from an initially undifferentiated or simple state, typically through reaction-diffusion dynamics (Turing patterns) or bioelectric signaling. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Morphogenetic computation is among the most ancient and widespread forms of biological information processing — the embryo computing its own form. Understanding it as computation rather than mere chemistry is essential for a complete account of biological intelligence.

**Turing Pattern** — A spatial pattern of chemical concentration (spots, stripes, irregular domains) generated spontaneously from a near-uniform initial state by a two-component reaction-diffusion system in which an activator diffuses more slowly than its inhibitor. Named for Alan Turing's 1952 theoretical analysis. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Turing patterns are a form of morphogenetic computation — chemistry generating spatial information — and have been demonstrated in the patterning of fish skin, digit formation, and other developmental contexts. They illustrate how complex, biologically meaningful structure can emerge from local chemical rules without any global blueprint.

**Organoid Computing** — The use of three-dimensional brain organoids — miniature tissue structures grown from induced pluripotent stem cells that recapitulate aspects of brain region development — as biological computing substrates, interfaced with electronic read/write systems. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Organoid computing represents the most direct attempt to use biological neurons as a computing platform, raising both technical questions (how effectively can organoids be trained?) and ethical questions (what is the moral status of a functional neural tissue running a computation?).
