# Chapter 36: Key Researchers

## Philip Goff
**Institution:** Durham University, Department of Philosophy
**Primary contributions:** Leading contemporary defender of panpsychism within analytic philosophy. Goff has developed constitutive Russellian panpsychism — the view that phenomenal properties are the intrinsic nature of the physical properties described by science — and has engaged seriously with the combination problem as the central challenge to panpsychist views.

**Key works:**
- Goff, P. (2017). *Consciousness and Fundamental Reality*. Oxford University Press.
- Goff, P. (2019). *Galileo's Error: Foundations for a New Science of Mind*. Pantheon Books.

**Characterization:** The philosopher who made panpsychism scientifically respectable again — not by avoiding its difficulties, but by taking them seriously.

---

## David Chalmers
**Institution:** New York University, Center for Mind, Brain and Consciousness
**Primary contributions:** Named and analyzed the "hard problem of consciousness" — the question of why physical processes are accompanied by subjective experience — establishing it as the central challenge in philosophy of mind. Chalmers's work has driven both the revival of panpsychism and the development of IIT and other consciousness theories.

**Key works:**
- Chalmers, D. J. (1995). Facing up to the problem of consciousness. *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 2(3), 200–219.
- Chalmers, D. J. (1996). *The Conscious Mind: In Search of a Fundamental Theory*. Oxford University Press.
- Chalmers, D. J. (2010). *The Character of Consciousness*. Oxford University Press.

**Characterization:** The philosopher who forced consciousness back onto the scientific agenda and has spent his career refusing to let the hard problem be explained away.

---

## Galen Strawson
**Institution:** University of Texas at Austin, Department of Philosophy
**Primary contributions:** Developed the argument that committed physicalism entails panpsychism, through the claim that consciousness is undeniably real and therefore must be a physical property — and that the most parsimonious physical view that accommodates this is panpsychism.

**Key works:**
- Strawson, G. (2006). Realistic monism: Why physicalism entails panpsychism. *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 13(10–11), 3–31.
- Strawson, G. (2010). *Mental Reality* (2nd ed.). MIT Press.

**Characterization:** The philosopher who took physicalism seriously enough to see where it leads — and followed it there, even when the destination surprised him.

---

## Peter Godfrey-Smith
**Institution:** University of Sydney, School of History and Philosophy of Science
**Primary contributions:** Philosophy of biology and philosophy of mind, with a focus on the evolution of cognition and experience. Godfrey-Smith uses the independent evolution of complex cognitive life in cephalopods as a window onto the question of how minds are distributed in the natural world.

**Key works:**
- Godfrey-Smith, P. (2016). *Other Minds: The Octopus, the Sea, and the Deep Origins of Consciousness*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
- Godfrey-Smith, P. (2017). *Philosophy of Biology*. Princeton University Press.

**Characterization:** A philosopher who gets in the water — literally, as a scuba diver studying octopuses — to ground abstract questions about mind in biological reality.

---

## Giulio Tononi
**Institution:** University of Wisconsin-Madison, Wisconsin Institute for Sleep and Consciousness
**Primary contributions:** Developed Integrated Information Theory (IIT), a mathematical framework for quantifying consciousness in terms of the integrated causal power of a system's physical structure. IIT implies that consciousness is a fundamental property that varies in degree — present in any system with the right causal structure — which gives it implications for the spectrum of mind.

**Key works:**
- Tononi, G. (2004). An information integration theory of consciousness. *BMC Neuroscience*, 5, 42.
- Oizumi, M., Albantakis, L., & Tononi, G. (2014). From the phenomenology to the mechanisms of consciousness: Integrated information theory 3.0. *PLOS Computational Biology*, 10(5), e1003588.

**Characterization:** The neuroscientist who built a mathematics of consciousness — whether it is the right mathematics is genuinely uncertain, but the effort is serious and has driven productive debate.

---

## Thomas Nagel
**Institution:** New York University, School of Law and Department of Philosophy (emeritus)
**Primary contributions:** Demonstrated the irreducibility of subjective experience to objective physical description ("What is it like to be a bat?", 1974), making the hard problem unavoidable. Also developed a position in *Mind and Cosmos* (2012) that is not quite panpsychism but argues that the physical world must have properties that make the emergence of mind, reason, and value comprehensible — a teleological naturalism that mainstream science and philosophy found deeply uncomfortable.

**Key works:**
- Nagel, T. (1974). What is it like to be a bat? *Philosophical Review*, 83(4), 435–450.
- Nagel, T. (1986). *The View from Nowhere*. Oxford University Press.
- Nagel, T. (2012). *Mind and Cosmos*. Oxford University Press.

**Characterization:** The philosopher who has been most consistently unwilling to accept easy answers to hard questions about mind — and has paid a considerable professional price for the refusal.
