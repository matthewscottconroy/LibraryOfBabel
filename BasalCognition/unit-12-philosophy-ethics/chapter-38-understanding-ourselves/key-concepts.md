# Chapter 38: Key Concepts

**Holobiont** — A host organism together with all its associated microbial symbionts (bacteria, archaea, viruses, fungi, protists), considered as a unit of biological organization. The term was coined by Lynn Margulis and is now widely used in evolutionary biology and ecology. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The holobiont concept dissolves the boundary between "self" and "other" at the biological level, making the cognitive contributions of microbial partners to host cognition — behavior, mood, decision-making — conceptually tractable and scientifically investigable.

**Hologenome** — The combined genetic information of a holobiont — the sum of the host genome and all the microbial genomes associated with it. The hologenome theory of evolution proposes that natural selection acts on holobionts as units. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* If selection acts on the hologenome, then adaptations that appear to be "host" adaptations may be holobiont adaptations — including adaptations related to cognitive and behavioral function. The microbiome's influence on cognition may be an evolved, adaptive feature of the holobiont rather than an incidental side effect.

**Gut-Brain Axis** — The bidirectional communication system between the gut (including the enteric nervous system and gut microbiome) and the central nervous system, mediated by the vagus nerve, spinal cord pathways, and blood-borne signals including hormones, immune factors, and microbial metabolites. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The gut-brain axis is the mechanism through which microbial cognition — the collective information processing of the gut microbiome — influences the information processing of the central nervous system, making "human" cognition a function of the holobiont rather than of the brain alone.

**Psychobiotics** — Live microorganisms that, when ingested in adequate amounts, confer a mental health benefit on the host (Dinan, Stanton, & Cryan, 2013). An emerging category of therapeutic agent that manipulates the gut microbiome to improve psychological outcomes. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Psychobiotics are the therapeutic application of the insight that gut bacteria influence cognition. Their development and evaluation is an ongoing scientific project that will determine how far the gut-brain axis can be exploited therapeutically.

**Endosymbiosis** — A form of symbiosis in which one organism (the endosymbiont) lives within the cells of another (the host), typically having been internalized by phagocytosis and retained rather than digested. The most evolutionarily significant instance is the origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts from bacterial endosymbionts approximately 1.5–2 billion years ago. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Endosymbiosis makes the eukaryotic cell — including every human neuron — a biological composite. The cognitive function of neurons depends on mitochondrial ATP production, making every act of human cognition dependent on the contribution of ancestral bacterial partners.

**Bioelectric Medicine** — A therapeutic approach that targets the bioelectric state of tissues — patterns of membrane voltage, ion flow, and electric field — to treat disease or promote regeneration, rather than targeting specific molecular components. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Bioelectric medicine treats the body as a distributed cognitive system in which bioelectric patterns encode information about tissue state and target morphology. Cancer, from this perspective, is a failure of bioelectric governance; regeneration is the body's bioelectric system being helped to achieve its target state.

**Target Morphology** — In Levin's bioelectric framework, the representation — encoded in the bioelectric pattern of a tissue — of the morphological state that the tissue is "trying" to achieve through development or regeneration. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The target morphology concept is the most direct application of cognitive vocabulary to developmental biology — it implies that tissues are goal-directed systems pursuing a represented target state, not merely following local rules without global direction.

**Cumulative Cultural Evolution** — The process, apparently unique to humans, by which cultural knowledge accumulates across generations, with each generation building on and adding to the knowledge inherited from the previous one. Distinguished from simple cultural transmission (present in many species) by the "ratchet effect" — new solutions are not lost but are retained as the baseline for further improvement. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Cumulative cultural evolution may be one of the few properties that is genuinely degree-different rather than kind-different between humans and other species. Understanding why it developed in humans, and what cognitive prerequisites it requires, is essential for characterizing human cognitive distinctiveness in light of the continuity of basal cognition.
