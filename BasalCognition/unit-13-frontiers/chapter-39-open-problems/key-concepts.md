# Chapter 39: Key Concepts

**Biogenic Approach (to cognition)** — A methodology for studying cognitive function in non-neural organisms that begins from what simple organisms actually do, rather than comparing them to human cognitive capacities. Associated with Pamela Lyon (2006). *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The biogenic approach addresses the anthropocentric baseline problem by refusing to treat human cognition as the reference point, instead asking what information processing is minimally necessary for adaptive behavior in a given ecological context.

**Anthropocentric Baseline** — The implicit use of human cognitive capacities as the standard against which non-human systems are measured, leading to systematic underestimation of non-human cognitive complexity (systems that compute differently than humans are judged as non-cognitive) or overestimation (systems that display behaviors superficially similar to human cognitive behaviors are attributed capacities they do not have). *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The anthropocentric baseline is a systematic source of error in basal cognition research. Avoiding it requires explicitly specifying criteria for cognition that are not defined by reference to the human case.

**Transfer Entropy** — A measure of directed statistical dependence between two variables over time — how much knowing the past of X reduces uncertainty about the future of Y, beyond what Y's own past would already provide. A non-parametric measure of information transfer in the direction from X to Y. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Transfer entropy can detect directed information flow in biological systems without requiring assumptions about the mechanism, making it a powerful tool for characterizing basal cognition in systems where the mechanisms are unknown.

**Unlimited Associative Learning (UAL)** — Ginsburg and Jablonka's proposed criterion for the evolution of consciousness: the capacity to associate any combination of stimuli with any combination of reinforcers, with the associations being immediately generalizable to new situations. Distinguished from limited associative learning (which cannot generalize). *Why it matters for basal cognition:* UAL is proposed as the evolutionary threshold for consciousness — systems capable of UAL are conscious; those capable only of limited associative learning are not. If correct, this criterion places the threshold for consciousness well above the basal cognition systems discussed in this book, though the argument for why UAL is the right criterion remains controversial.

**Neural Correlates of Consciousness (NCCs)** — The minimal neural mechanisms jointly sufficient for a specific conscious experience. The search for NCCs has been a major program in neuroscience, motivated by the assumption that consciousness is implemented by specific neural processes. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* The NCC approach assumes that consciousness requires neural implementation. Basal cognition research challenges this assumption by demonstrating cognitive function without neurons, raising the question of whether there are non-neural correlates of consciousness in simpler organisms.

**Counterfactual Sensitivity** — A property of cognitive systems: the degree to which behavior responds to hypothetical or potential environmental conditions, not just actual ones. A system that adjusts its behavior in anticipation of conditions that have not yet occurred, or that maintains alternative behavioral possibilities until environmental information resolves the ambiguity, displays counterfactual sensitivity. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Counterfactual sensitivity is a proposed metric for cognitive function that captures something beyond reactive stimulus-response coupling — the system is "modeling" its environment, not just responding to it.

**Goal-Directedness (teleological behavior)** — Behavior organized with respect to a future outcome — behavior that converges toward a specific state across different environmental perturbations. Distinguished from apparent goal-directedness (behavior that converges on a predictable outcome by mechanical necessity) by the robustness of convergence to perturbation. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Goal-directedness is one of the most intuitive criteria for cognition, and it is present, to varying degrees, in the behaviors of many non-neural organisms. Establishing genuine teleology rather than apparent teleology requires careful experimental design, making it one of the central methodological challenges of the field.

**Nociception** — The detection of noxious stimuli — stimuli that damage or threaten to damage tissue — and the production of avoidance behavior in response. Distinguished from pain, which additionally involves subjective experience of the noxious state. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Nociceptive behavior is present in organisms across the tree of life, including those with no neural system. The presence of nociceptive behavior in non-neural organisms raises the question of whether pain — the experiential aspect of nociception — extends beyond neural systems, or whether non-neural nociception is "dark" — nociception without experience.

**Adaptive Compression** — A proposed metric for cognitive function: the degree to which a system's behavioral repertoire adapts its complexity to match the complexity of the environment, expanding in variable environments and contracting in stable ones. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Adaptive compression would distinguish cognitive systems (which modulate the complexity of their information processing in response to environmental complexity) from simpler reactive systems (which maintain a fixed level of response complexity regardless of environmental variability).
