# Chapter 39: Key Researchers

## Pamela Lyon
**Institution:** University of Adelaide, School of Biological Sciences
**Primary contributions:** Developed the biogenic approach to cognition — a methodology for studying cognitive function in non-neural organisms that starts from what simple organisms actually do rather than comparing them to human cognitive capacities. Lyon's work has been influential in establishing theoretically rigorous frameworks for basal cognition research.

**Key works:**
- Lyon, P. (2006). The biogenic approach to cognition. *Cognitive Processing*, 7(1), 11–29.
- Lyon, P. (2015). The cognitive cell: Bacterial behavior reconsidered. *Frontiers in Microbiology*, 6, 264.

**Characterization:** The philosopher-biologist who insisted on intellectual rigor in how we think about cognition in simple organisms — and provided the methodological tools to achieve it.

---

## Eva Jablonka
**Institution:** Tel Aviv University, Cohn Institute for the History and Philosophy of Science and Ideas
**Primary contributions:** Evolution of epigenetic inheritance, the evolution of consciousness and learning (with Simona Ginsburg), and the extension of evolutionary theory beyond the gene-centric model. Jablonka and Ginsburg's *The Evolution of the Sensitive Soul* (2019) is the most rigorous attempt to identify the evolutionary transition to consciousness by specifying a learnable criterion.

**Key works:**
- Jablonka, E., & Lamb, M. J. (2005). *Evolution in Four Dimensions: Genetic, Epigenetic, Behavioral, and Symbolic Variation in the History of Life*. MIT Press.
- Ginsburg, S., & Jablonka, E. (2019). *The Evolution of the Sensitive Soul: Learning and the Origins of Consciousness*. MIT Press.

**Characterization:** An evolutionary theorist who has consistently pushed the boundaries of how we think about inheritance, learning, and the evolutionary origins of mind.

---

## Toshiyuki Nakagaki
**Institution:** Hokkaido University, Research Institute for Electronic Science
**Primary contributions:** Experimental and theoretical study of *Physarum polycephalum* as an intelligent system, including the famous maze-solving demonstration, the anticipation of periodic stimuli, and the mathematical modeling of network optimization. Nakagaki's work has been instrumental in establishing slime molds as model systems for basal cognition research.

**Key works:**
- Nakagaki, T., Yamada, H., & Tóth, Á. (2000). Maze-solving by an amoeboid organism. *Nature*, 407(6803), 470.
- Saigusa, T., Tero, A., Nakagaki, T., & Kuramoto, Y. (2008). Amoebae anticipate periodic events. *Physical Review Letters*, 100(1), 018101.
- Tero, A., Takagi, S., Saigusa, T., Ito, K., Bebber, D. P., Fricker, M. D., ... & Nakagaki, T. (2010). Rules for biologically inspired adaptive network design. *Science*, 327(5964), 439–442.

**Characterization:** The physicist who gave slime molds a career in computer science — demonstrating, with elegant experiments, that intelligence does not require neurons.

---

## Simona Ginsburg
**Institution:** The Open University of Israel, Natural Sciences Department
**Primary contributions:** Co-developed, with Eva Jablonka, the theory that the evolution of unlimited associative learning marks the transition to phenomenal consciousness, and wrote *The Evolution of the Sensitive Soul* (2019) presenting this theory with extensive biological and philosophical justification.

**Key works:**
- Ginsburg, S., & Jablonka, E. (2019). *The Evolution of the Sensitive Soul: Learning and the Origins of Consciousness*. MIT Press.
- Ginsburg, S., & Jablonka, E. (2010). The evolution of associative learning: A factor in the Cambrian explosion. *Journal of Theoretical Biology*, 266(1), 11–20.

**Characterization:** The biologist who took the consciousness question seriously enough to ask it evolutionarily — and proposed a specific, testable answer.

---

## Anil Seth
**Institution:** University of Sussex, Sackler Centre for Consciousness Science
**Primary contributions:** Neuroscientific theories of consciousness, perceptual inference, and the self as a controlled hallucination. Seth's work is particularly relevant to the open problems of basal cognition because it provides empirically grounded frameworks for thinking about what consciousness is and how to study it — frameworks that might, with modification, be applicable to non-neural systems.

**Key works:**
- Seth, A. K. (2021). *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*. Dutton.
- Seth, A. K., & Bayne, T. (2022). Theories of consciousness. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 23(7), 439–452.

**Characterization:** The neuroscientist who has done the most to make consciousness science empirically tractable — and who is honest about what the science currently cannot answer.

---

## Christof Koch
**Institution:** Allen Institute for Brain Science
**Primary contributions:** Neuroscience of consciousness, neural correlates of consciousness, and advocacy for Integrated Information Theory as a framework for understanding and measuring consciousness. Koch's work is relevant to the measurement problem in basal cognition because IIT, if correct, provides a quantitative measure of consciousness applicable to any system.

**Key works:**
- Koch, C. (2019). *The Feeling of Life Itself: Why Consciousness Is Widespread but Can't Be Computed*. MIT Press.
- Koch, C., Massimini, M., Boly, M., & Tononi, G. (2016). Neural correlates of consciousness: Progress and problems. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 17(5), 307–321.

**Characterization:** The neuroscientist who has spent thirty years searching for the neural correlates of consciousness and has arrived at a position more expansive — and more philosophically challenging — than he expected at the start.
