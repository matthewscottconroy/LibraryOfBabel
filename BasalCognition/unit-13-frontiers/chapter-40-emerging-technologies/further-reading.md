# Chapter 40: Further Reading

## Imaging and Sensing

**Miyawaki, A., Llopis, J., Heim, R., McCaffery, J. M., Adams, J. A., Ikura, M., & Tsien, R. Y. (1997). Fluorescent indicators for Ca²⁺ based on green fluorescent proteins and calmodulin. *Nature*, 388(6645), 882–887.**
The paper that introduced FRET-based calcium indicators — the conceptual predecessor of the modern GCaMP family. Required reading for understanding the development of genetically encoded indicators.

**Hell, S. W. (2009). Microscopy and its focal switch. *Nature Methods*, 6(1), 24–32.**
A clear and historically situated account of the super-resolution microscopy revolution by one of its pioneers. Explains the physical principles underlying STED and related techniques in a way that is accessible to biologists without optics backgrounds.

**Fromm, J., & Lautner, S. (2007). Electrical signals and their physiological significance in plants. *Plant, Cell & Environment*, 30(3), 249–257.**
A review of plant electrical signals — action potentials and variation potentials — and their physiological consequences. The primary review paper for understanding what has been established about plant bioelectricity and what remains open.

## Optogenetics

**Nagel, G., Szellas, T., Huhn, W., Kateriya, S., Adeishvili, N., Berthold, P., ... & Bamberg, E. (2003). Channelrhodopsin-2, a directly light-gated cation-selective membrane channel. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 100(24), 13940–13945.**
The channelrhodopsin-2 paper — the discovery that launched the optogenetics field. Read it for the biological discovery (a light-gated ion channel in algae) and appreciate both its immediate significance and its origin in the study of algal phototaxis.

**Deisseroth, K. (2015). Optogenetics: 10 years of microbial opsins in neuroscience. *Nature Neuroscience*, 18(9), 1213–1225.**
A comprehensive retrospective on the first decade of optogenetics in neuroscience, by the field's most prominent practitioner. Covers the technical development, key biological discoveries, and future directions. Useful as an orientation to the field before exploring its non-neural applications.

**Papanatsiou, M., Petersen, J., Henderson, L., Wang, Y., Christie, J. M., & Blatt, M. R. (2019). Optogenetic manipulation of stomatal kinetics improves carbon assimilation, water use, and growth. *Science*, 363(6427), 1456–1459.**
The landmark paper demonstrating optogenetic control of plant physiology. Shows that plant guard cells can be controlled with light to improve water use efficiency — a demonstration with direct agricultural relevance and with implications for understanding plant bioelectric control.

## Computational Modeling

**Tero, A., Takagi, S., Saigusa, T., Ito, K., Bebber, D. P., Fricker, M. D., ... & Nakagaki, T. (2010). Rules for biologically inspired adaptive network design. *Science*, 327(5964), 439–442.**
The essential paper on computational modeling of *Physarum* network optimization. See earlier note.

**Turing, A. M. (1952). The chemical basis of morphogenesis. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London, Series B*, 237(641), 37–72.**
The founding paper of reaction-diffusion theory in biology. See earlier note.

**Wilensky, U. (1999). NetLogo. Center for Connected Learning and Computer-Based Modeling, Northwestern University.**
NetLogo is the most accessible agent-based modeling platform for biological applications. The full environment (including many example models relevant to biology) is available free at ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo. The documentation and tutorial resources are excellent.

## Synthetic Biology

**Endy, D. (2005). Foundations for engineering biology. *Nature*, 438(7067), 449–453.**
See earlier note. The conceptual foundation of the BioBricks approach to synthetic biology.

**Khalil, A. S., & Collins, J. J. (2010). Synthetic biology: Applications come of age. *Nature Reviews Genetics*, 11(5), 367–379.**
A comprehensive review of synthetic biology applications, from medical diagnostics to biofuels, with a discussion of the principles of genetic circuit design. Useful as an orientation to the field's scope beyond the examples discussed in this chapter.

**Cameron, D. E., Bashor, C. J., & Collins, J. J. (2014). A brief history of synthetic biology. *Nature Reviews Microbiology*, 12(5), 381–390.**
A historical account of synthetic biology's development from early genetic engineering to the modern field of engineered gene circuits. Useful for understanding how the field's tools and goals have evolved.

## Directed Evolution

**Arnold, F. H. (2019). Innovation by evolution: Bringing new chemistry to life (Nobel Lecture). *Angewandte Chemie International Edition*, 58(41), 14420–14426.**
Frances Arnold's Nobel Lecture on directed evolution — the technique for evolving proteins with desired properties in the laboratory. A model of clear scientific communication, and essential background for thinking about how directed evolution might be applied to evolving cognitive capacities.
