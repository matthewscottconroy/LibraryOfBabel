# Chapter 40: Key Concepts

**Genetically Encoded Voltage Indicators (GEVIs)** — Fluorescent protein constructs whose fluorescence intensity or spectral properties change with membrane voltage, allowing membrane potential to be imaged optically in living cells without physical contact. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* GEVIs enable real-time imaging of bioelectric state in non-neural organisms — the key measurement needed to test hypotheses about bioelectric cognition in developing tissues, plant cells, and other non-neural systems.

**Optogenetics** — A technology using genetically encoded light-sensitive proteins (channelrhodopsins, halorhodopsins, and their relatives) to control the electrical state of specific cells with pulses of light, with millisecond temporal precision. Originally developed for neuroscience; increasingly applied to non-neural systems. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Optogenetics converts correlational observations (this bioelectric state accompanies this behavior) into causal tests (this bioelectric state is necessary and sufficient for this behavior) — the same logical transformation that made optogenetics transformative in neuroscience.

**Channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2)** — A light-gated cation channel from the green alga *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii* that opens when illuminated with blue light, depolarizing the cell membrane. The primary tool of modern optogenetics, discovered by Nagel et al. (2003). *Why it matters for basal cognition:* ChR2 originated as a photosensory protein in a unicellular alga performing phototaxis — a basal cognitive behavior. Its appropriation by neuroscience exemplifies how studying non-neural cognition can produce tools with applications far beyond the original context.

**Agent-Based Model (ABM)** — A computational model in which individual agents (cells, bacteria, organisms) follow a set of rules governing their behavior, and the collective behavior of the system is observed as an emergent property of agent interactions. Used to study how collective cognitive behaviors emerge from individual cellular behaviors. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* ABMs are the primary tool for testing whether the collective behaviors of bacterial communities, slime mold networks, and other non-neural systems can be explained by known individual-level rules, or whether additional higher-level principles are required.

**Transfer Entropy (in modeling context)** — See Chapter 39 key concepts; also relevant here as a measure used in computational analysis of biological network dynamics to identify directed information flow without mechanistic assumptions.

**Super-Resolution Microscopy** — A family of fluorescence microscopy techniques (including STED, STORM, and PALM) that circumvent the diffraction limit of light to achieve lateral resolutions of 10–50 nanometers in living or fixed cells. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Super-resolution microscopy enables visualization of the molecular machinery — receptor clusters, signaling complexes, ion channels — that implements basal cognitive functions at the nanoscale, connecting molecular mechanism to cellular behavior.

**Reaction-Diffusion Model** — A system of partial differential equations describing the concentration of two or more chemical species diffusing through space and reacting with each other, used to model the emergence of spatial patterns (Turing patterns) in biological development and in chemical systems. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Reaction-diffusion models capture one of the most general and widespread mechanisms of biological information processing — the self-organizing generation of spatial pattern from local chemical interactions — and provide a mathematical language for morphogenetic intelligence.

**Directed Evolution** — A technique for engineering proteins or circuits with desired properties by subjecting a population of genetic variants to iterative rounds of mutation and selection, allowing evolutionary processes to find functional solutions not accessible to rational design. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Directed evolution can be applied to evolve cognitive capacities in simplified biological systems, providing a reconstruction approach to understanding what genetic and molecular architectures are sufficient for specific cognitive behaviors.

**Single-Cell RNA Sequencing (scRNA-seq)** — A technique for measuring the complete transcript profile of individual cells simultaneously across thousands of cells, producing a high-dimensional readout of the gene expression state of each cell in a tissue. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* scRNA-seq reveals the heterogeneity of cell states within biological communities — the range of different signaling and gene expression states that coexist — which is directly relevant to understanding distributed decision-making in non-neural systems.

**Closed-Loop Bioelectric Control** — An experimental system that measures the bioelectric state of a tissue optically (using GEVIs or voltage-sensitive dyes), compares it to a desired target state, and applies corrective optical stimulation (using optogenetic actuators) to reduce the deviation. The technological realization of the target morphology concept. *Why it matters for basal cognition:* Closed-loop bioelectric control enables direct experimental tests of the hypothesis that specific bioelectric patterns cause specific morphogenetic or behavioral outcomes, and demonstrates the feasibility of bioelectric medicine approaches in principle.
