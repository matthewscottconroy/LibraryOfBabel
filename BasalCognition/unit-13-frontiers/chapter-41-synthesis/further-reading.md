# Chapter 41: Further Reading

## Unified Frameworks for Biological Cognition

**Levin, M. (2019). The computational boundary of a "self": Developmental bioelectricity drives multicellularity and scale-free cognition. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 10, 2688.**
The clearest statement of Levin's scale-free cognition framework. Accessible and well-argued; essential for understanding the most developed current proposal for a unified theory of biological intelligence.

**Lyon, P. (2006). The biogenic approach to cognition. *Cognitive Processing*, 7(1), 11–29.**
The foundational paper for the biogenic approach. Provides the methodological framework for studying cognitive function across the full range of biological systems without presupposing human cognitive architecture as the reference point.

**Maturana, H. R., & Varela, F. J. (1987). *The Tree of Knowledge: The Biological Roots of Human Understanding*. Shambhala.**
A classic synthesis of autopoiesis theory and its implications for understanding cognition as a biological phenomenon. Accessible and beautifully written; argues that cognition is constitutive of life itself and traces the implications for understanding language, consciousness, and human self-understanding. One of the most important books in the intellectual tradition that this course builds on.

## Evolutionary Continuity

**Godfrey-Smith, P. (2016). *Other Minds: The Octopus, the Sea, and the Deep Origins of Consciousness*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.**
Essential. The evolutionary analysis of the origins of experience, using cephalopod cognition as the primary lens.

**Ginsburg, S., & Jablonka, E. (2019). *The Evolution of the Sensitive Soul: Learning and the Origins of Consciousness*. MIT Press.**
The most rigorous evolutionary account of the transition to consciousness. Essential for thinking about the continuity and discontinuity of cognitive evolution.

**Calcott, B., & Sterelny, K. (Eds.). (2011). *The Major Transitions in Evolution Revisited*. MIT Press.**
A comprehensive treatment of the major evolutionary transitions — from replicators to cells, from prokaryotes to eukaryotes, from unicellular to multicellular life — relevant to understanding the evolutionary context of the transition from cellular to neural cognition.

## The Philosophy of Mind and Biological Continuity

**Varela, F. J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience*. MIT Press.**
Essential. The founding text of enactivism, which grounds cognitive science in the biological reality of organism-environment coupling. Required for understanding the philosophical framework underlying the continuity thesis.

**Thompson, E. (2007). *Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind*. Harvard University Press.**
A sophisticated development of the enactivist program, drawing on both phenomenological philosophy and cognitive science. Thompson extends the autopoiesis-enactivism framework to address questions about consciousness, life, and their relationship in ways that are directly relevant to the synthesis attempted in this chapter.

**Nagel, T. (2012). *Mind and Cosmos: Why the Materialist Neo-Darwinian Conception of Nature Is Almost Certainly False*. Oxford University Press.**
A controversial but important book that argues that the standard materialist account of the evolution of mind is inadequate — not because it is insufficiently supernatural, but because it cannot account for the emergence of consciousness, cognition, and value from matter. Engages with the continuity thesis from the perspective of a philosopher deeply skeptical of easy answers.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

**Chalmers, D. J. (2010). *The Character of Consciousness*. Oxford University Press.**
The most comprehensive treatment of the consciousness question from one of the field's most important contributors. Covers the hard problem, IIT, panpsychism, and the relationship between functional and phenomenal properties. Essential for understanding the state of the consciousness question.

**Goff, P. (2019). *Galileo's Error: Foundations for a New Science of Mind*. Pantheon Books.**
Essential. See earlier note. The most accessible recent treatment of why the consciousness question requires a more radical rethinking of the scientific worldview than mainstream cognitive science has been willing to undertake.

**Seth, A. K. (2021). *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*. Dutton.**
A neuroscientist's account of what consciousness is and how the brain generates it. Seth's "controlled hallucination" framework — the idea that perception is a form of prediction — connects to the anticipation and predictive processing themes in this book, and his discussion of what the science can and cannot tell us about the limits of consciousness is refreshingly honest.
