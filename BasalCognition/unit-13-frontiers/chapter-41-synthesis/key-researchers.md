# Chapter 41: Key Researchers

## Michael Levin
**Institution:** Tufts University, Allen Discovery Center; Harvard University Wyss Institute
**Primary contributions:** Developmental bioelectricity, morphogenesis, regeneration, and the scale-free cognition framework. Levin's work has produced both empirical discoveries (bioelectric patterns as causal determinants of morphogenesis; the target morphology concept; bioelectric reprogramming of cancer cells) and a theoretical synthesis (scale-free cognition; the cognitive light cone) that constitutes the most developed current proposal for a unified theory of biological intelligence.

**Key works:**
- Levin, M. (2019). The computational boundary of a "self": Developmental bioelectricity drives multicellularity and scale-free cognition. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 10, 2688.
- Levin, M. (2021). Bioelectric signaling: Reprogrammable circuits underlying embryogenesis, regeneration, and cancer. *Cell*, 184(8), 1971–1989.
- Levin, M., & Dennett, D. C. (2020). Cognition all the way down. *Aeon*. (Essay, freely available online.)

**Characterization:** The scientist who has most systematically developed the case that cognition is a fundamental property of biological organization at every scale — and built the experimental program to test it.

## Humberto Maturana (historical, 1928–2021)
**Institution:** Universidad de Chile, Faculty of Sciences (most of his career)
**Primary contributions:** Co-developed autopoiesis theory with Francisco Varela, establishing the organization of living systems as the proper focus of biology. Maturana's claim that cognition is constitutive of life — that any autopoietic system is by definition engaged in a form of knowing — is the foundational insight of enactivism and the philosophical ancestor of scale-free cognition.

**Key works:**
- Maturana, H. R., & Varela, F. J. (1980). *Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living*. D. Reidel.
- Maturana, H. R., & Varela, F. J. (1987). *The Tree of Knowledge: The Biological Roots of Human Understanding*. Shambhala.

**Characterization:** The neurobiologist who concluded from studying how frogs see that the nervous system does not represent the world but enacts it — and followed that conclusion wherever it led.

---

## Francisco Varela (historical, 1946–2001)
**Institution:** CNRS Paris, École Polytechnique (final appointment)
**Primary contributions:** Co-developed autopoiesis with Maturana; led the development of enactivism in cognitive science (with Thompson and Rosch); pioneered neurophenomenology as a research program integrating first-person and third-person methods. Varela's synthesis of biology, cognitive science, and phenomenological philosophy is one of the most original intellectual contributions to the field.

**Key works:**
- Varela, F. J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience*. MIT Press.
- Varela, F. J., Maturana, H. R., & Uribe, R. (1974). Autopoiesis: The organization of living systems, its characterization and a model. *BioSystems*, 5(4), 187–196.

**Characterization:** The biologist-philosopher whose tragically short life produced ideas that the field is still working through — and whose integration of scientific rigor with philosophical depth remains a model for how to think about cognition.

## Evan Thompson
**Institution:** University of British Columbia, Department of Philosophy
**Primary contributions:** Developed enactivism from its Varelian foundations into a comprehensive philosophy of mind and life, addressing consciousness, subjectivity, and the relationship between the biological and the experiential. Thompson's *Mind in Life* (2007) is the most systematic treatment of the continuity between biological organization and phenomenal consciousness.

**Key works:**
- Varela, F. J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience*. MIT Press.
- Thompson, E. (2007). *Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind*. Harvard University Press.
- Thompson, E. (2015). *Waking, Dreaming, Being: Self and Consciousness in Neuroscience, Meditation, and Philosophy*. Columbia University Press.

**Characterization:** The philosopher who has done the most rigorous work of developing the enactivist program into a comprehensive account of consciousness and its relationship to life.

---

## Karl Friston
**Institution:** University College London, Wellcome Centre for Human Neuroimaging
**Primary contributions:** The free energy principle — a mathematical framework proposing that living systems minimize prediction error (free energy) in their exchanges with the environment, and that this minimization is equivalent to maintaining their identity against entropy. Friston's framework applies to any self-organizing system and provides a mathematical bridge between autopoiesis, active inference, and Bayesian brain theories.

**Key works:**
- Friston, K. (2010). The free-energy principle: A unified brain theory? *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 11(2), 127–138.
- Friston, K. J., Wiese, W., & Hobson, J. A. (2021). Sentience and the free energy principle. *Physics of Life Reviews*, 36, 208–214.

**Characterization:** The mathematical physicist of cognition — whose free energy principle attempts to derive the properties of cognitive systems from first principles of non-equilibrium thermodynamics, and whose framework applies, in principle, to any self-organizing living system.

## Lynn Margulis (historical, 1938–2011)
**Institution:** University of Massachusetts Amherst
**Primary contributions:** See Chapter 38. In the context of this synthesis chapter, Margulis is relevant as the scientist who most forcefully demonstrated the evolutionary continuity of life — the argument that eukaryotic complexity evolved from prokaryotic complexity through symbiosis, and that the boundaries between organisms are less fixed than classical biology assumed. Her vision of life as fundamentally collaborative, rather than competitive, informs the holobiont perspective and the scale-free cognition framework.

**Key works:**
- Margulis, L. (1981). *Symbiosis in Cell Evolution*. W. H. Freeman.
- Margulis, L., & Sagan, D. (1995). *What Is Life?* Simon & Schuster.

**Characterization:** The evolutionary visionary who saw that life, at every scale, is collaboration — and who paid the intellectual price of being right before the evidence was in.
