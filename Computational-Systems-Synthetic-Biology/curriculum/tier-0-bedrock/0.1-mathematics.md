# 0.1 Mathematics

The language every other tier is written in. Weakness here propagates upward into every model, simulation, and analysis you build.

---

## Calculus & Analysis

### Single-Variable Calculus
- Limits and continuity
- Derivatives: rules, chain rule, implicit differentiation
- Integrals: definite, indefinite, substitution, by parts
- Taylor series and approximations
- Series convergence

### Multivariable Calculus
- Partial derivatives, gradient vectors
- Jacobian matrix — critical for linearizing ODE systems around fixed points
- Hessian matrix — second-order behavior, optimization
- Multiple integrals, change of variables
- Vector fields, divergence, curl

### Ordinary Differential Equations
- First-order: separable, linear (integrating factor), exact
- Second-order: homogeneous, particular solutions, characteristic equations
- Systems of ODEs — this is the primary language of systems biology
- Phase portraits: fixed points, nullclines, trajectories
- Stability analysis: linearization, eigenvalue sign determines stability
- Limit cycles and oscillatory behavior
- Bifurcation theory: how system behavior changes as parameters vary
  - Saddle-node bifurcation (bistability appears/disappears)
  - Pitchfork bifurcation (symmetry breaking)
  - Hopf bifurcation (oscillations emerge from steady state)

### Partial Differential Equations
- Diffusion equation — Fick's second law, morphogen gradients
- Wave equation
- Reaction-diffusion systems (Turing pattern formation lives here)
- Method of characteristics
- Finite difference discretization

## Linear Algebra

### Fundamentals
- Vectors and vector spaces
- Matrix operations: multiplication, transpose, inverse
- Determinants
- Linear transformations and their geometric interpretation

### Core Decompositions
- Eigenvalues and eigenvectors — appear in:
  - Stability analysis of ODE systems (sign of eigenvalues)
  - Principal component analysis
  - Markov chains (stationary distribution)
  - Network analysis (centrality)
- Singular value decomposition (SVD) — foundation of PCA and dimensionality reduction
- LU decomposition — solving linear systems efficiently
- QR decomposition

### Advanced Concepts
- Null space and column space — the null space of the stoichiometric matrix **S** gives the feasible flux cone in metabolic modeling
- Rank, nullity, and their relationship
- Positive definite matrices
- Sparse matrices and efficient storage (genomic data is always sparse)

## Probability & Statistics

### Probability Theory
- Sample spaces, events, axioms of probability
- Conditional probability, Bayes' theorem — foundational for all Bayesian inference
- Independence
- Random variables: discrete and continuous
- Expectation, variance, covariance

### Probability Distributions
- Discrete: Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson (gene expression counts), geometric, negative binomial
- Continuous: uniform, Gaussian, exponential (event waiting times), beta, gamma
- Multivariate: multivariate normal, Dirichlet
- The Poisson process — models arrival of rare events (mutations, binding events)

### Statistical Inference
- Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) — fitting parameters to data
- Method of moments
- Confidence intervals
- Hypothesis testing: null/alternative hypotheses, p-values, Type I/II error
- Multiple testing correction:
  - Bonferroni (family-wise error rate) — conservative
  - Benjamini-Hochberg (false discovery rate) — standard in genomics

### Regression
- Linear regression: OLS, interpretation of coefficients
- Logistic regression: binary outcomes, log-odds
- Generalized linear models (GLMs): link functions, families
- Regularization: ridge (L2), LASSO (L1), elastic net

### Bayesian Statistics
- Prior, likelihood, posterior — Bayes' theorem applied to inference
- Conjugate priors
- Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC): Metropolis-Hastings, Gibbs sampling
- Bayesian model comparison: Bayes factors, BIC
- Credible intervals vs. confidence intervals

### Markov Chains
- Transition matrices
- Stationary distributions
- Ergodicity and mixing
- Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) — the core probabilistic model in sequence analysis (CpG islands, gene finding, sequence alignment)

## Discrete Mathematics & Graph Theory

### Graph Fundamentals
- Directed vs. undirected graphs
- Weighted vs. unweighted
- Adjacency matrices and adjacency lists
- Paths, cycles, connectivity, components

### Graph Properties
- Degree distribution — power-law distributions in biological networks
- Clustering coefficient — local network density
- Shortest paths: Dijkstra's algorithm, breadth-first search
- Spanning trees: minimum spanning trees (Kruskal, Prim)
- Centrality: degree, betweenness, closeness, eigenvector centrality
- Graph isomorphism — network alignment problem

### Network Motifs
- Subgraph patterns that appear more than expected by chance
- Autoregulation (negative → noise filtering; positive → bistability)
- Feedforward loops: 8 types — each with distinct dynamics
- Feedback loops: positive (bistability, memory) vs. negative (homeostasis, oscillations)
- These map directly to circuits found in gene regulatory networks

### Boolean Algebra
- Truth tables, logical operators
- Boolean networks as coarse-grained models of gene regulation
- Attractors in Boolean networks as cellular states

## Information Theory

- Shannon entropy: H(X) = -Σ p(x) log p(x)
- Mutual information: how much knowing X reduces uncertainty about Y
- Kullback-Leibler divergence: distance between probability distributions
- Applications in biology:
  - Sequence logos and position-weight matrices (bits of information per position)
  - Inferring gene regulatory networks from expression data
  - Evaluating model fit
