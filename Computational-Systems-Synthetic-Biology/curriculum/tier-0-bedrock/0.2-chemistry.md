# 0.2 Chemistry

Biology is chemistry with a memory. You cannot model what you cannot understand at the molecular level.

---

## General Chemistry

### Thermodynamics
- First law: energy conservation; enthalpy (H)
- Second law: entropy (S) always increases in the universe
- Gibbs free energy: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS — the master equation for spontaneity
  - ΔG < 0: spontaneous (exergonic)
  - ΔG > 0: non-spontaneous (endergonic)
  - ΔG = 0: equilibrium
- Standard Gibbs free energy: ΔG° (pH 7 convention in biology: ΔG°')
- Relationship to equilibrium: ΔG° = -RT ln(Keq)
- Coupled reactions: unfavorable reactions driven by favorable ones (ATP hydrolysis)

### Chemical Equilibrium
- Equilibrium constant Keq: products/reactants at equilibrium
- Reaction quotient Q: current state vs. equilibrium
- Le Chatelier's principle: system response to perturbation
- Effect of temperature on equilibrium (van't Hoff equation)

### Acid-Base Chemistry
- Brønsted-Lowry definition: proton donors/acceptors
- pH = -log[H+]
- pKa: the pH at which 50% of molecules are protonated
- Henderson-Hasselbalch equation: pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA])
- Buffers: how living cells maintain pH 7.4 in cytoplasm
- Amino acid charge states as a function of pH — critical for protein behavior

### Kinetics
- Reaction rates and rate laws
- Zero, first, and second order reactions
- Rate constants and the Arrhenius equation: k = A·e^(-Ea/RT)
- Activation energy and transition states
- Catalysts: lower activation energy, not thermodynamics
- Pre-steady state vs. steady-state kinetics

## Organic Chemistry

### Functional Groups
Recognize and understand the chemistry of:
- Carboxylic acids (–COOH): acidic, forms peptide bonds
- Amines (–NH2): basic, nucleophilic
- Hydroxyl (–OH): hydrogen bonding, phosphorylation target
- Thiol (–SH): forms disulfide bonds, nucleophilic (cysteine)
- Aldehyde (–CHO) and ketone (C=O): electrophilic, reactive
- Phosphate esters: high-energy bonds, signaling, nucleic acid backbone

### Reaction Mechanisms
- Nucleophilic substitution (SN1, SN2)
- Elimination (E1, E2)
- Nucleophilic addition to carbonyls
- Esterification and hydrolysis
- Oxidation and reduction in organic context

### Stereochemistry
- Chirality and enantiomers — biology is chiefly L-amino acids and D-sugars
- Diastereomers, meso compounds
- R/S nomenclature
- Cis/trans isomerism in double bonds (crucial for fatty acid properties)
- Protein active sites are chiral environments: stereochemistry determines binding

## Biochemistry

This is the most critical section — the direct substrate for systems and synthetic biology.

### Amino Acids & Proteins

**Amino Acid Properties** (must know all 20)
- Nonpolar aliphatic: Gly, Ala, Val, Leu, Ile, Pro, Met
- Aromatic: Phe, Trp, Tyr
- Polar uncharged: Ser, Thr, Cys, Asn, Gln
- Positively charged: Lys, Arg, His
- Negatively charged: Asp, Glu
- Know pKa values, hydrophobicity indices, and role in protein structure

**Protein Structure Hierarchy**
- Primary: amino acid sequence — encodes everything
- Secondary: α-helix (right-handed, 3.6 residues/turn, H-bonds within strand), β-sheet (H-bonds between strands)
- Tertiary: 3D fold — hydrophobic core, disulfides, ion pairs
- Quaternary: multi-subunit assemblies
- Intrinsically disordered regions: function without fixed structure

**Protein Stability**
- Hydrophobic effect as primary driving force for folding
- Hydrogen bonds, van der Waals, electrostatics as modulating forces
- Disulfide bridges: oxidizing environment (ER, extracellular)
- Chaperones: Hsp70, GroEL/GroES — preventing aggregation during folding
- Proteostasis: the balance of synthesis, folding, and degradation

### Enzyme Kinetics

**Michaelis-Menten Model**
- Derivation from mechanism: E + S ⇌ ES → E + P
- Quasi-steady-state assumption (Briggs-Haldane)
- Km: the substrate concentration at half-maximal velocity — proxy for affinity
- Vmax: maximum velocity at saturating substrate
- kcat (turnover number): reactions per enzyme per second
- kcat/Km: catalytic efficiency, the "specificity constant"
- Lineweaver-Burk plots: 1/v vs. 1/[S] — historically used, now fit nonlinearly

**Inhibition**
- Competitive: inhibitor binds active site; increases apparent Km, Vmax unchanged
- Uncompetitive: inhibitor binds ES complex; decreases both apparent Km and Vmax
- Noncompetitive/mixed: binds enzyme or ES; decreases Vmax, Km depends on type
- Irreversible inhibition: covalent modification (aspirin, penicillin)

**Cooperativity & Allosterism**
- Hill equation: v = Vmax[S]^n / (K0.5^n + [S]^n)
- Hill coefficient n > 1: positive cooperativity, ultrasensitive response
- Allosteric regulation: conformational change upon effector binding at a distant site
- MWC (Monod-Wyman-Changeux) model: T-state vs. R-state equilibrium
- Significance: switch-like responses in signaling, gene regulation

### Nucleic Acid Chemistry

**DNA Structure**
- Purine (adenine, guanine) and pyrimidine (cytosine, thymine, uracil) bases
- Watson-Crick base pairing: A–T (2 H-bonds), G–C (3 H-bonds)
- B-form DNA: right-handed helix, ~10.5 bp/turn, major and minor grooves
- A-form: RNA/DNA hybrids, RNA duplexes
- Z-form: left-handed, found at high salt; biological role debated
- Supercoiling: linking number, writhe, twist; topoisomerases manage this

**RNA Structure**
- Single-stranded but extensively self-complementary
- Secondary structures: stem-loops, hairpins, bulges, internal loops, pseudoknots
- RNA tertiary structure: ribozymes, riboswitches, ribosomal RNA
- Chemical differences from DNA: 2'-OH group makes RNA more reactive and susceptible to hydrolysis

**Thermodynamics of Base Pairing**
- Nearest-neighbor model for calculating melting temperatures
- ΔG of duplex formation — relevant for primer design, guide RNA design, aptamer stability

### Lipids & Membranes

**Lipid Types**
- Fatty acids: saturated vs. unsaturated, chain length effects on melting temperature
- Glycerophospholipids: the major membrane component
- Sphingolipids: sphingomyelin, glycosphingolipids
- Sterols: cholesterol in eukaryotes, hopanoids in bacteria

**Membrane Biophysics**
- Fluid mosaic model
- Lipid bilayer self-assembly: hydrophobic effect
- Membrane fluidity: temperature, unsaturation, cholesterol effects
- Lipid rafts: ordered microdomains enriched in sphingolipids and cholesterol
- Membrane potential: electrochemical gradients, Nernst equation
- Transport: simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, active transport (primary and secondary)

### Carbohydrates

- Monosaccharides: glucose, fructose, galactose, ribose
- Glycosidic bonds: α and β linkages — determines digestibility and structure
- Disaccharides: lactose, sucrose, maltose
- Polysaccharides: starch (energy storage), cellulose (structural, β-1,4), glycogen
- Glycosylation of proteins: N-linked, O-linked — affects folding, stability, signaling

### Coenzymes & Metabolic Currency

- ATP/ADP/AMP: the energy currency; ATP hydrolysis ΔG°' = -30.5 kJ/mol
- NAD+/NADH: electron carrier in catabolism (glycolysis, TCA, β-oxidation)
- NADP+/NADPH: electron donor in anabolism (fatty acid synthesis, pentose phosphate)
- FAD/FADH2: electron carrier, tightly bound to enzymes
- CoA/acyl-CoA: carrier for acyl groups (fatty acid metabolism, TCA)
- Tetrahydrofolate (THF): one-carbon carrier
- S-adenosylmethionine (SAM): methyl donor

## Physical Chemistry (Selected Topics)

### Spectroscopy Basics
- UV-Vis absorbance: Beer-Lambert law (A = εlc), chromophores, protein quantification at A280
- Fluorescence: excitation/emission spectra, quantum yield, FRET (Förster resonance energy transfer) — measures nanometer-scale distances
- NMR basics: chemical shifts, J-coupling — relevant for metabolomics and protein structure

### Diffusion
- Fick's first law: flux proportional to concentration gradient
- Fick's second law: time evolution of concentration gradient
- Einstein relation: diffusion coefficient, temperature, viscosity
- Relevance: morphogen gradients, protein diffusion in cells, signal propagation

### Statistical Thermodynamics (Essentials)
- Boltzmann distribution: probability ∝ e^(-E/kBT)
- Partition function: sum over all states
- Connection to thermodynamics: F = -kBT ln(Z)
- Relevance: protein conformation probabilities, binding equilibria from first principles
