## Cell Biology

### Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Architecture
- Prokaryotes: no nucleus, circular chromosome, 70S ribosomes, no membrane-bound organelles
- Eukaryotes: nucleus, linear chromosomes, 80S ribosomes, extensive internal membranes
- Archaea vs. Bacteria: distinct lipid chemistry, transcription machinery more eukaryote-like
- Cell size and its constraints: diffusion limits, surface-area-to-volume ratio

### Membrane Biology
- Plasma membrane as a selective barrier
- Channel proteins: passive, ion-selective, gated (voltage, ligand, mechanosensitive)
- Transporters: uniport, symport, antiport
- Primary active transporters: ATPases (Na+/K+ ATPase, V-ATPase, ABC transporters)
- Receptor tyrosine kinases, GPCRs, nuclear receptors — three major signaling receptor classes
- Endocytosis: clathrin-mediated, caveolae, macropinocytosis

### Organelles
- **Nucleus**: double membrane, nuclear pore complexes, nucleolus (rRNA synthesis)
- **Mitochondria**: double membrane, own genome and ribosomes (endosymbiotic origin), cristae for ATP synthesis
- **Endoplasmic reticulum**: rough (ribosomes, protein synthesis) vs. smooth (lipid synthesis, Ca2+ storage)
- **Golgi apparatus**: protein processing, sorting, glycosylation, secretion
- **Ribosomes**: 70S (bacteria) vs. 80S (eukaryotes); large and small subunits
- **Lysosomes/vacuoles**: degradation, pH ~5, hydrolases
- **Peroxisomes**: fatty acid β-oxidation, catalase for H2O2 detoxification

### Cytoskeleton
- **Actin filaments**: 6 nm, dynamic polymerization/depolymerization, cell shape and movement
- **Microtubules**: 25 nm, tubulin dimers, mitotic spindle, motor protein tracks
- **Intermediate filaments**: 10 nm, structural support, tissue-specific
- Motor proteins: myosin (actin-based), kinesin and dynein (microtubule-based)
- Treadmilling and dynamic instability

### Cell Cycle
- G1 (growth) → S (DNA synthesis) → G2 (growth/preparation) → M (mitosis)
- Checkpoints: G1/S (restriction point), G2/M, spindle assembly checkpoint
- Cyclin-CDK complexes drive each transition
- Tumor suppressor proteins (p53, Rb) and oncogenes — the network logic of cell cycle control
- Apoptosis: intrinsic pathway (mitochondria, cytochrome c, caspases) and extrinsic (death receptors)

## Molecular Biology

### DNA Replication
- Semiconservative replication
- Origin of replication (ori): prokaryotes have one, eukaryotes have many
- Replication machinery:
  - Helicase: unwinds double helix
  - Primase: synthesizes RNA primers
  - DNA polymerase III (bacteria) / Pol δ, ε (eukaryotes): 5'→3' synthesis, 3'→5' proofreading exonuclease
  - DNA polymerase I: removes RNA primers (bacteria)
  - Ligase: seals nicks
  - Topoisomerase I/II: relieves supercoiling ahead of replication fork
- Leading strand (continuous) vs. lagging strand (Okazaki fragments)
- Telomeres and telomerase: end-replication problem

### Transcription
- RNA polymerase (RNAP) recognizes promoter sequences
- **Bacteria**: single RNAP, σ factor for promoter recognition, -10 and -35 elements
- **Eukaryotes**: three RNAPs (I: rRNA, II: mRNA, III: tRNA/5S), general transcription factors
- Promoter structure: TATA box, Inr, DPE in eukaryotes
- Enhancers and silencers: cis-regulatory elements, can act over long distances via looping
- Transcription factors: sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins, activation and repression domains
- Termination: intrinsic (hairpin + poly-U) vs. Rho-dependent in bacteria

### RNA Processing (Eukaryotes)
- 5' capping: m7G cap; protects mRNA, aids translation initiation
- 3' polyadenylation: poly-A tail; protects mRNA, nuclear export
- Splicing: spliceosome removes introns, joins exons
  - 5' splice site (GU), branch point (A), 3' splice site (AG)
  - Two transesterification reactions
- Alternative splicing: tissue-specific isoforms, one gene → many proteins
- RNA editing: A-to-I editing by ADARs, C-to-U editing (ApoB)

### Translation
- mRNA reading frame, codons (3 nucleotides → 1 amino acid)
- Genetic code: degeneracy (synonymous codons), universality (with exceptions)
- Start codon (AUG) and stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA)
- tRNA structure: anticodon loop, CCA-3' aminoacyl acceptor
- Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases: the second genetic code
- Ribosome structure: A site (aminoacyl), P site (peptidyl), E site (exit)
- Translation initiation: 43S preinitiation complex, cap-dependent scanning (eukaryotes); Shine-Dalgarno/start codon (bacteria)
- Elongation cycle: decoding, peptidyl transfer, translocation
- Termination: release factors recognize stop codons

### Post-Translational Modification
- Phosphorylation (kinases/phosphatases): signaling, activity regulation
- Ubiquitination: proteasomal degradation or signaling
- Acetylation: histones and metabolic enzymes
- Methylation: histones, DNA
- Glycosylation: N-linked (Asn), O-linked (Ser/Thr)
- Lipidation: membrane anchoring
- Proteolytic processing: signal peptide cleavage, proprotein activation

### Mutation & DNA Repair
- **Mutation types**: point mutations (transitions and transversions), insertions, deletions, frameshifts
- **Chromosomal rearrangements**: inversions, translocations, duplications, deletions
- **Sources**: replication errors, UV (CPD, 6-4 PP), ionizing radiation (DSBs), chemical mutagens
- **Repair mechanisms**:
  - Mismatch repair (MMR): corrects replication errors
  - Base excision repair (BER): oxidized, deaminated, alkylated bases
  - Nucleotide excision repair (NER): bulky adducts (UV lesions)
  - Homologous recombination (HR): DSB repair using sister chromatid, high fidelity
  - Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ): DSB repair, error-prone
  - Translesion synthesis (TLS): bypass of lesions, mutagenic

## Genetics & Genomics

### Classical Genetics
- Mendelian inheritance: dominance, recessiveness
- Dihybrid crosses, independent assortment
- Epistasis: one gene masks another
- Penetrance and expressivity
- Linkage and recombination: Morgan's mapping

### Chromosome Structure
- Histone proteins: H2A, H2B, H3, H4 (octamer) + H1 (linker)
- Nucleosome: 147 bp DNA wrapped around octamer
- Chromatin fiber, loops, topologically associating domains (TADs)
- Heterochromatin (condensed, transcriptionally silent) vs. euchromatin (open, active)

### Epigenetics
- **DNA methylation**: CpG dinucleotides, 5-methylcytosine; gene silencing, imprinting, transposon suppression
- **Histone modifications**:
  - H3K4me3: active promoters
  - H3K27ac: active enhancers
  - H3K27me3: Polycomb-repressed
  - H3K9me3: heterochromatin, silencing
- Chromatin remodelers: SWI/SNF, NuRD, ISWI — reposition nucleosomes
- ATAC-seq: measures chromatin accessibility
- Imprinting: allele-specific expression determined by parental origin

### Genome Organization
- Gene density: ~20,000 protein-coding genes in humans, occupying ~2% of genome
- Introns, exons, and alternative splicing
- Repetitive elements: SINEs (Alu), LINEs, tandem repeats
- Transposable elements: Class I (retrotransposons, copy-and-paste), Class II (DNA transposons, cut-and-paste)
- Non-coding RNA genes: rRNA, tRNA, snRNA, snoRNA, lncRNA, miRNA

### Microbial Genomics
- Bacterial genome size: typically 1–10 Mb, tightly packed genes
- Horizontal gene transfer (HGT): transformation, transduction, conjugation
- Plasmids: extrachromosomal DNA, replication origins, copy number, incompatibility groups
- Operons: co-transcribed gene clusters; lac operon as canonical example
- Sigma factors: alternative σ factors reprogram transcription in stress/sporulation

## Microbiology

### Bacterial Growth
- Growth curve: lag phase, exponential (log) phase, stationary phase, death phase
- Exponential growth equation: N(t) = N0 · 2^(t/τ), where τ = doubling time
- Monod equation: μ = μmax · [S] / (Ks + [S]) — the Michaelis-Menten of bacterial growth
- Nutrient limitation as the primary regulator of growth rate
- Batch vs. continuous culture (chemostat): steady-state analysis of chemostat

### Quorum Sensing
- N-acyl homoserine lactones (AHL) in gram-negative bacteria
- Auto-inducing peptides (AIP) in gram-positive bacteria
- LuxI/LuxR system in *V. fischeri*: the canonical model
- Positive feedback loop generates sharp switch-like activation
- Applications: controlling synthetic gene circuits with population-level signals

### Genetic Manipulation Tools
- Plasmids: selection markers (antibiotic resistance), origin of replication, multiple cloning site
- Transformation: heat shock, electroporation
- Transduction: phage-mediated DNA transfer
- Conjugation: F plasmid, pili
- λ Red recombinase system for precise chromosomal edits

### CRISPR-Cas in Native Biology
- Adaptive immune system of bacteria and archaea
- CRISPR array: spacers (memory of past infections), repeats
- Cas proteins: processing and effector functions
- Three stages: acquisition, processing, interference
- Type I, II, III systems — Type II (Cas9) is the basis of most genome editing tools
- PAM sequences: limit target range; vary by Cas ortholog

### Bacteriophage Biology
- Lytic cycle: infection → replication → cell lysis → new phage
- Lysogenic cycle: integration into host chromosome → prophage → induction
- λ phage as the canonical example of a genetic switch (CI/Cro)
- M13 phage: ssDNA, non-lytic, used in phage display
- T4 phage: structural complexity, timing of gene expression

## Evolutionary Biology

### The Four Evolutionary Forces
1. **Natural selection**: differential reproduction based on phenotype
2. **Genetic drift**: random fluctuation in allele frequencies; more powerful in small populations
3. **Mutation**: source of all new variation
4. **Gene flow**: migration between populations

### Population Genetics
- Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium: p² + 2pq + q² = 1 (baseline for detecting selection/drift)
- Effective population size (Ne): smaller than census size
- Coalescent theory: tracing lineages backward in time to common ancestor
- Selective sweep: beneficial allele drags linked variants to high frequency
- Balancing selection: maintains multiple alleles (MHC diversity)

### Molecular Evolution
- Neutral theory (Kimura): most molecular variation is selectively neutral
- dN/dS ratio (ω): ratio of nonsynonymous to synonymous substitution rates
  - ω < 1: purifying selection
  - ω = 1: neutral evolution
  - ω > 1: positive selection
- Substitution models: JC69, Kimura 2-parameter, HKY, GTR
- Molecular clock: constant substitution rate assumption and its limitations

### Fitness Landscapes
- W.S. Wright's concept: fitness as a function of genotype space
- Local optima and adaptive peaks
- Epistasis shapes landscape ruggedness
- Sequence space dimensionality: 20^n possible proteins of length n
- Relevance to directed evolution: navigating the landscape efficiently

### Horizontal Gene Transfer
- Prevalence in bacteria: ~20% of *E. coli* genome acquired horizontally
- Genomic islands: regions of atypical GC content, flanked by IS elements
- Fitness consequences: often confers new metabolic capabilities or resistance
- Complicates phylogenetic inference from single genes
