# 1.2 Genomics

Understanding the structure, variation, and annotation of genomes — the blueprint level.

## Variant Calling

### Types of Genomic Variation
- **SNP (Single Nucleotide Polymorphism)**: single base change; most common
- **Indel**: insertion or deletion of 1–50 bp; causes frameshifts in coding regions
- **Structural variant (SV)**: >50 bp; deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations
- **Copy number variant (CNV)**: large duplications or deletions, often >1 kb
- **Tandem repeat expansion**: STRs (short tandem repeats), relevant to many neurological diseases

### The GATK Best Practices Pipeline
Standard germline variant calling workflow:

1. **Raw reads** → FASTQ
2. **Alignment**: BWA-MEM2 → SAM/BAM
3. **Mark duplicates**: Picard MarkDuplicates or GATK MarkDuplicatesSpark
4. **Base quality score recalibration (BQSR)**: correct systematic sequencing errors using known variant sites
5. **Variant calling**: GATK HaplotypeCaller
   - Local de-novo assembly at candidate regions
   - Genotyping using PairHMM (probabilistic likelihood of reads given genotype)
   - Outputs GVCF (genomic VCF) with reference blocks for joint genotyping
6. **Joint genotyping**: GenomicsDBImport + GenotypeGVCFs for cohorts
7. **Variant quality score recalibration (VQSR)** or hard filtering
8. **Annotation**: ANNOVAR, VEP, SnpEff

### Population Genetics Metrics
- **Allele frequency**: fraction of chromosomes carrying an allele in a population
- **Heterozygosity**: fraction of sites where an individual has two different alleles
- **Ti/Tv ratio**: transitions (Ts) to transversions (Tv); expected ~2 for whole genome, ~3 for exome
- **Linkage disequilibrium (LD)**: non-random association of alleles at different loci
  - D' and r²: measures of LD; r² threshold of 0.8 for "in LD"
  - LD blocks: regions where haplotypes are conserved
  - LD decay: distance over which LD falls off (shorter in humans with larger effective population size)

### GWAS (Genome-Wide Association Studies)
- Test association between ~millions of SNPs and a phenotype
- Linear regression (quantitative traits) or logistic regression (case-control)
- Manhattan plot: -log₁₀(p-value) vs. genomic position
- Significance threshold: p < 5×10⁻⁸ (Bonferroni for ~1M independent tests)
- LD clumping/pruning to identify independent signals
- Fine-mapping: identifying causal variants within associated loci
- Heritability: variance explained by common variants (SNP heritability, h²_SNP)

### Somatic Variant Calling
- Tumor-normal paired analysis: find variants present in tumor but not matched normal
- Key callers: Mutect2 (GATK), Strelka2, VarScan2
- Challenges: tumor purity, copy number changes, clonal heterogeneity
- Mutational signatures: patterns of somatic mutations characteristic of different mutational processes (COSMIC signatures database)

## Genome Annotation

### Ab Initio Gene Prediction
- Uses statistical models of gene structure (codon usage, splice sites, start/stop codons)
- Hidden Markov Models or generalized HMMs
- **AUGUSTUS**: gene predictor with many organism-specific trained parameters
- **GeneMark**: widely used for prokaryotes and eukaryotes
- Limitations: cannot predict genes with novel features, struggles with short exons

### Evidence-Based Annotation
- Uses RNA-seq reads or protein homology as evidence
- **Trinity**: de novo transcriptome assembly from RNA-seq (no reference needed)
- **STAR + StringTie**: reference-guided transcriptome assembly
- **BRAKER**: combines AUGUSTUS with RNA-seq evidence
- **MAKER**: multi-evidence annotation pipeline; integrates ab initio + protein homology + RNA-seq

### Functional Annotation
- **BLAST against databases**: assign function by homology to characterized proteins
- **InterProScan**: scan against protein domain databases (Pfam, PRINTS, PANTHER, TIGRFAM)
- **GO term assignment**: Gene Ontology terms mapped from InterPro hits or BLAST
- **KEGG pathway mapping**: assign genes to metabolic pathways and functional modules
- **COG (Cluster of Orthologous Groups)**: functional categories

### Annotation File Formats
- GFF3 and GTF: hierarchical feature model (gene → mRNA → exon/CDS/UTR)
- GenBank flat file: rich annotation embedded in sequence record
- Ensembl GTF: standard for well-annotated eukaryotic genomes

## Comparative Genomics

### Synteny Analysis
- Conserved order of genes between species = synteny
- Syntenic blocks indicate shared evolutionary history
- **MUMmer/MUMmer4**: whole-genome alignment; NUCmer for nucleotide, PROmer for protein
- **Mauve**: multiple genome aligner; visualization of synteny
- **SyMAP**, **MCscan**: synteny block identification and visualization
- Chromosomal rearrangements: inversions, translocations disrupt synteny; parsimony of rearrangements

### Ortholog/Paralog Identification
- **Ortholog**: homolog arising from speciation (usually same function)
- **Paralog**: homolog arising from gene duplication (often diverged function)
- **Xenolog**: homolog arising from horizontal gene transfer
- **OrthoFinder**: all-vs-all BLAST + clustering; produces orthogroups
- **OMA**, **PANTHER**: alternative ortholog databases
- **BUSCO**: benchmarks completeness using conserved single-copy orthologs

### Whole Genome Duplication
- Polyploidy events: evidence from gene count inflation and synteny
- Prevalent in plants, some fish, yeast
- Duplicated genes as source of evolutionary innovation
- Detecting WGD: synonymous distance (Ks) peaks in paralog distributions

### Pan-Genomes
- Core genome: genes present in all strains of a species
- Accessory genome (shell + cloud): genes present in some but not all strains
- Pan-genome: union of all genes across all strains
- Open pan-genome: adding more strains continues to find new genes
- Closed pan-genome: adding more strains adds few new genes
- Tools: Roary, Panaroo (bacteria), PGGB (graph-based, more general)

## Metagenomics

### Shotgun Metagenomics
- Sequencing all DNA in an environmental sample
- Taxonomic profiling: classify reads to known genomes
  - Kraken2: exact k-mer matching to taxonomic database
  - MetaPhlAn3: marker gene-based profiling
- Functional profiling: what metabolic functions are present?
  - HUMAnN3: pathway abundance estimation
  - KEGG and COG annotations
- Metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs): binning and assembling genomes from metagenomes
  - Assemblers: MEGAHIT, metaSPAdes
  - Binning: MetaBAT2, CONCOCT, MaxBin — uses tetranucleotide frequencies and coverage

### 16S/18S/ITS Amplicon Sequencing
- Targeted sequencing of phylogenetic marker genes
- 16S rRNA gene: V3-V4 region most common
- DADA2 or QIIME2 + denoising: ASV (amplicon sequence variant) vs. OTU approaches
- Diversity metrics:
  - α-diversity: richness (within sample): Shannon index, Faith's PD
  - β-diversity: community composition (between samples): Bray-Curtis, UniFrac
- Ordination: PCoA, NMDS for visualizing community structure
- Differential abundance: DESeq2 applied to microbiome, ANCOM-BC

## Epigenomics

### Chromatin Accessibility
- **ATAC-seq**: Tn5 transposase cuts accessible chromatin; inserts sequencing adapters
- Peaks indicate open chromatin: likely enhancers, promoters, CTCF sites
- Peak calling: MACS2 (`macs2 callpeak`)
- Nucleosome positioning from read distribution

### Histone Modification Profiling
- **ChIP-seq**: chromatin immunoprecipitation + sequencing
- H3K4me3 peaks: active promoters
- H3K27ac peaks: active enhancers
- Broad marks (H3K27me3): requires different peak calling (broad mode in MACS2)
- CUT&RUN, CUT&TAG: alternatives with lower cell input requirements and less background

### DNA Methylation
- **WGBS (Whole Genome Bisulfite Sequencing)**: gold standard, genome-wide
  - Bisulfite converts unmethylated C to U; methylated C remains
  - Bismark aligner + methylation extractor
- **RRBS (Reduced Representation Bisulfite Sequencing)**: MspI digest enriches CpG-rich regions
- **EPIC array**: 850K CpG probe array; cost-effective for human studies
- Differential methylation: DSS, methylKit, limma on M-values

### Hi-C and Chromatin Architecture
- Capture chromatin contacts genome-wide via proximity ligation
- A/B compartments: active (A) vs. inactive (B) chromatin
- Topologically Associating Domains (TADs): self-interacting chromatin domains
- Loops: often anchored by CTCF and cohesin
- Tools: HiCExplorer, Juicer, HOMER

## Practical Genomics Workflow Summary

```
Raw reads (FASTQ)
      │
      ▼
QC: FastQC → MultiQC
      │
      ▼
Trimming: fastp
      │
      ▼
Alignment: BWA-MEM2 (DNA) / STAR (RNA) / Bismark (methylation)
      │
      ▼
BAM processing: samtools sort/index, Picard MarkDuplicates
      │
      ├── Variant calling: GATK HaplotypeCaller → VCF
      ├── Peak calling: MACS2 → BED
      ├── Coverage: deepTools bamCoverage → bigWig
      └── Assembly: SPAdes / Hifiasm → FASTA
```
