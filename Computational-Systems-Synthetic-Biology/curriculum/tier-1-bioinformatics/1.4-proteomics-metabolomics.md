## Mass Spectrometry Fundamentals

All proteomics and metabolomics data ultimately comes from a mass spectrometer. You must understand how they work to understand the data they produce.

### How a Mass Spectrometer Works
1. **Ionization**: convert analytes to gas-phase ions
2. **Mass analysis**: separate ions by mass-to-charge ratio (m/z)
3. **Detection**: measure ion abundance at each m/z

### Ionization Methods

**ESI (Electrospray Ionization)**
- Analyte in solution sprayed through high-voltage capillary
- Produces multiply charged ions: [M + nH]^n+
- Multiply charged: large proteins at accessible m/z range
- Soft ionization: intact molecules, minimal fragmentation
- Works with liquid chromatography (LC-MS) — most common for proteomics

**MALDI (Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption Ionization)**
- Analyte co-crystallized with matrix compound
- Laser pulse causes desorption and ionization
- Produces singly charged ions: [M + H]+
- Typically coupled with TOF analyzer
- Applications: MALDI imaging, protein identification from gels, microbial identification

### Mass Analyzers

**Orbitrap**
- Ions trapped in electrostatic field; rotate around central electrode
- Detection via image current — frequency of rotation ∝ m/z
- Resolving power: up to 500,000
- Mass accuracy: <1 ppm
- Standard in modern proteomics instruments (Thermo Q Exactive, Eclipse, Astral)

**TOF (Time of Flight)**
- Ions accelerated through fixed voltage; lighter ions travel faster
- Resolution: 20,000–40,000
- Can measure very large intact proteins
- Combined with MALDI or as second stage in Q-TOF

**Triple Quadrupole (QQQ)**
- Three quadrupole stages for targeted quantification
- Q1: precursor ion selection → Q2: fragmentation (collision cell) → Q3: product ion selection
- MRM (Multiple Reaction Monitoring): most sensitive targeted quantification
- SRM: similar, single reaction monitoring

### Tandem MS (MS/MS)
- Precursor ion selected, fragmented (collision-induced dissociation, CID; HCD)
- Fragment ions measured in second MS scan
- For peptides: b-ions (N-terminal fragments) and y-ions (C-terminal fragments)
- Fragment ion series gives amino acid sequence (de novo sequencing or database matching)

## Proteomics

### Shotgun (Bottom-Up) Proteomics

**Workflow**
1. Protein extraction (lysis, centrifugation)
2. Reduction (DTT/TCEP) and alkylation (iodoacetamide) of cysteines
3. Enzymatic digestion: trypsin (cleaves after K, R) → peptides of 6–25 residues
4. LC separation: reversed-phase C18 column, 90–240 min gradient
5. Data-dependent acquisition (DDA): top N most intense precursors fragmented
6. Database searching to identify peptides

**Database Searching**
- Theoretical fragmentation of all peptides in proteome database
- Match experimental spectra to theoretical spectra
- **Mascot**, **Sequest/Comet**, **MaxQuant/Andromeda**
- Target-decoy approach: estimate false discovery rate (FDR) using reversed sequences
- Peptide spectral match (PSM) FDR < 1%, protein FDR < 1%

**MaxQuant**
- Full DDA pipeline: peak detection, isotope clustering, peptide identification, protein assembly
- Label-free quantification (LFQ): intensity-based; MaxLFQ algorithm
- Output: proteinGroups.txt — the primary result file
- Perseus: downstream statistical analysis of MaxQuant output

**Protein Inference Problem**
- Multiple peptides can map to multiple proteins (shared peptides)
- Parsimony principle: report minimum proteins explaining observed peptides
- Protein groups: proteins that cannot be distinguished given observed peptides

### Quantitative Proteomics

**Label-Free Quantification (LFQ)**
- MS1 intensity comparison across runs after alignment
- No chemical labeling cost
- High variation between runs requires good normalization
- Precursor intensity or spectral count (less accurate)

**SILAC (Stable Isotope Labeling by Amino acids in Cell culture)**
- Heavy isotope amino acids (¹³C, ¹⁵N Lys, Arg) incorporated by cell growth
- Light and heavy samples mixed; peptide pairs differ by known mass
- Ratio of light:heavy = protein abundance ratio
- Advantages: mixing before sample processing eliminates handling variation
- Limitation: metabolic labeling requires live cells

**TMT (Tandem Mass Tags) / iTRAQ**
- Chemical labeling of amine groups after digestion
- TMT 6-plex, 10-plex, 16-plex, 18-plex (TMTpro)
- All labeled samples mixed; isobaric labels appear as one peak in MS1
- Quantification in MS2 (or MS3 for accuracy) via reporter ions
- Advantages: multiplexing 6–18 samples; no labeling bias from different metabolisms
- Disadvantage: ratio compression from co-isolated interfering precursors (SPS-MS3 mitigates this)

### Data-Independent Acquisition (DIA)

**DIA vs. DDA**
- DDA: stochastic selection of precursors; misses low-abundance peptides across runs
- DIA: systematic isolation and fragmentation of all precursors in m/z windows
- Each fragment ion spectrum is a composite of all precursors in the isolation window

**DIA Analysis**
- Spectral library generation from DDA experiments or in silico (Prosit)
- **DIA-NN**: neural network-based scoring; handles library-free analysis; fastest
- **Spectronaut**: commercial; highly optimized for DIA
- **OpenSWATH** + PyProphet: open-source workflow
- DIA enables deep, reproducible, quantitative proteomes

### Structural & Interaction Proteomics

**Crosslinking Mass Spectrometry (XL-MS)**
- Chemical crosslinker connects nearby amino acid residues (<30 Å)
- Identifies residues in contact → distance constraints for structural modeling
- Tools: xQuest, pLink, xiSearch

**AP-MS (Affinity Purification-MS)**
- Bait protein pulled down with its interaction partners
- Identify prey proteins by mass spectrometry
- Distinguish specific vs. contaminants: SAINT, CRAPome-filtering
- Build protein-protein interaction networks

**BioID / TurboID**
- Promiscuous biotinylase fused to bait protein
- Labels proteins within ~10 nm radius
- Streptavidin capture + MS
- Advantage over AP-MS: captures transient interactions, membrane proteins

**Hydrogen-Deuterium Exchange (HDX-MS)**
- Measures backbone amide exchange rate: solvent-exposed/flexible regions exchange faster
- Maps protein dynamics, binding interfaces, allosteric effects
- Tools: HDExaminer, DynamX

### Post-Translational Modification Proteomics

**Phosphoproteomics**
- Enrichment: TiO₂, IMAC (immobilized metal affinity chromatography)
- Phosphorylation: serine (86%), threonine (12%), tyrosine (2%)
- Localization: multiple phosphorylatable residues in one peptide — pRS, phosphoRS
- Kinase substrate inference: NetworKIN, PhosphoSitePlus

**Ubiquitinomics**
- di-Gly remnant on Lys after trypsin digest of ubiquitinated proteins
- Enrichment: di-Gly antibodies (Cell Signaling Technology)
- Maps ubiquitination sites; can profile proteasome substrates

## Metabolomics

Metabolites are the direct readout of cellular biochemistry — the end products of gene expression and enzyme activity.

### Targeted vs. Untargeted

**Targeted Metabolomics**
- Predefined list of metabolites measured quantitatively
- MRM on triple quadrupole: highest sensitivity and accuracy
- Absolute quantification with isotope-labeled standards
- Trade-off: only measures what you designed the method to detect

**Untargeted (Global) Metabolomics**
- Measure as many features as possible
- Features defined by m/z and retention time
- Downstream annotation of features to metabolites
- Discovers unexpected metabolite changes
- Trade-off: most features cannot be confidently annotated

### NMR vs. MS

**NMR Metabolomics**
- No separation needed; all metabolites measured in one scan
- Quantitative: signal intensity directly proportional to concentration
- Limited sensitivity: ~µM detection limit
- Reproducible: same spectrum every time
- Limited coverage: ~100 common metabolites

**LC-MS Metabolomics**
- High sensitivity: pM-nM detection limit
- High coverage: hundreds to thousands of metabolites
- Sample extraction solvent determines coverage (polar vs. nonpolar)
- Requires chromatographic method development

### LC-MS Metabolomics Workflow

1. Sample extraction: methanol/acetonitrile quench of metabolism; polar extraction
2. Chromatography: HILIC (hydrophilic interaction, polar metabolites) or RPLC (nonpolar)
3. MS acquisition: usually high-res Orbitrap; positive and negative ion mode
4. Data processing:
   - Peak picking: mzMine, XCMS, MS-DIAL
   - Alignment across samples
   - Gap filling
5. Normalization: total signal, internal standards, median normalization
6. Statistical analysis: univariate (t-test, ANOVA), multivariate (PCA, PLS-DA)
7. Annotation: accurate mass matching, MS/MS matching to databases

### Metabolite Annotation

**Databases**
- HMDB (Human Metabolome Database): comprehensive human metabolite database
- KEGG Compound: metabolites with pathway context
- MetaboLights: public metabolomics data repository
- mzCloud: MS/MS spectral database

**Annotation Confidence Levels (Metabolomics Standards Initiative)**
- Level 1: confirmed by comparison with authentic standard (retention time + MS/MS)
- Level 2: putative identification (library MS/MS match, no standard)
- Level 3: putative compound class (spectral similarity)
- Level 4: unknown but consistently detected feature

**In Silico Tools**
- SIRIUS + CSI:FingerID: molecular formula prediction + structure annotation from MS/MS
- CANOPUS: compound class prediction without reference spectrum

### Metabolic Flux Analysis

**13C Metabolic Flux Analysis (¹³C MFA)**
- Feed cells with ¹³C-labeled substrate (e.g., [1-¹³C] glucose)
- Measure isotopomer distribution in metabolites by MS or NMR
- Fit to stoichiometric model to infer intracellular fluxes
- Resolves parallel pathways that cannot be resolved by concentration alone

**EMU (Elementary Metabolite Unit) Framework**
- Efficient computational method for simulating isotope labeling patterns
- Decomposes network into minimal subunits for isotope propagation calculation
- Implemented in INCA, OpenMebius, WUFlux

**Isotopologue vs. Isotopomer**
- Isotopologue: molecule with same molecular formula but different number of heavy atoms
- Isotopomer: same molecular formula AND same positions of heavy atoms
- MS measures isotopologues (mass); NMR measures positional isotopomers

## Multi-Omics Integration

### Integration Strategies

**Concatenation**: stack all omics matrices → PCA/MOFA
**Network-based**: build each omics network separately, find conserved modules
**Statistical**: find correlations between omics layers
**Knowledge-based**: map to known pathways (KEGG, Reactome)

### Key Tools
- **MOFA+ (Multi-Omics Factor Analysis)**: latent factor model; finds shared variation across omics layers
- **DIABLO (mixOmics)**: supervised integration for classification
- **COSMOS**: network-based causal integration (transcriptomics + metabolomics)
- **OmicsIntegrator**: prize-collecting Steiner forest for network integration
