# 1.5 Structural Bioinformatics

Three-dimensional structure determines function. This tier bridges sequence information to atomic-resolution understanding of how proteins work.

---

## The Protein Data Bank (PDB)

### Structure Determination Methods
**X-ray Crystallography**
- Protein crystallized → X-ray diffraction pattern → electron density map → atomic model
- Resolution: 1–3 Å typical; <1.5 Å = high resolution
- Resolution affects: quality of side chain placement, water molecules, ligand confidence
- R-factor: measure of how well the model explains the diffraction data (Rwork, Rfree)
- B-factor (temperature factor): atomic displacement parameter; high = flexible/disordered
- Phase problem: solved by molecular replacement, MIR, SAD/MAD

**Cryo-Electron Microscopy (Cryo-EM)**
- Protein frozen in vitreous ice → electron beam → 2D projection images
- Single-particle analysis: combine millions of images → 3D reconstruction
- Revolution since ~2013: near-atomic resolution without crystals
- Advantages: large complexes, membrane proteins, multiple conformational states
- Resolution range: 2–10 Å; <3 Å competitive with X-ray for many purposes
- Tools: RELION, cryoSPARC

**NMR Spectroscopy**
- Protein in solution; measures through-bond and through-space H-H distances
- Limited to ~50 kDa proteins
- Provides dynamics information (order parameters)
- Multiple conformer ensembles capture flexibility

### PDB File Format
- HEADER, TITLE, REMARK sections: metadata
- SEQRES: amino acid sequence
- ATOM records: x, y, z coordinates + atom type + residue + chain + B-factor + occupancy
- HETATM: ligands, ions, water molecules
- CONECT: explicit bonds for ligands
- mmCIF: modern replacement; handles large structures, multi-model entries

### Assessing Structure Quality
- Resolution (X-ray, cryo-EM): lower Å = better
- Rwork/Rfree (X-ray): Rfree should be <30%, Rwork ~5% less than Rfree
- MolProbity score: all-atom contact analysis; Ramachandran outliers
- Ramachandran plot: φ-ψ dihedral angles should fall in allowed regions
- Clashscore: unfavorable atomic contacts

## Structure Comparison and Classification

### Pairwise Structure Comparison
**RMSD (Root Mean Square Deviation)**
- Average distance between aligned Cα atoms after superposition
- Good for: overall structural similarity of similar proteins
- Problem: sensitive to flexible regions, long proteins artificially inflate RMSD

**TM-score (Template Modeling Score)**
- Length-normalized structural similarity score
- TM-score > 0.5: same fold (same SCOP superfamily, typically)
- TM-score > 0.17: structural similarity above random
- Preferred over RMSD for comparing proteins of different lengths
- Tool: TM-align

### Structural Classification Databases
**SCOP (Structural Classification of Proteins)**
- Hierarchy: Class → Fold → Superfamily → Family
- Manually curated; evolutionary relationships inferred from structure

**CATH (Class, Architecture, Topology, Homologous superfamily)**
- Automated classification with manual curation
- Larger coverage than SCOP
- Useful for: identifying structural relatives beyond sequence homology

**Pfam → Domains**
- Sequence-based but validated by structure
- Most proteins are mosaic of domains
- Understanding domain architecture is key to function prediction

## Homology Modeling

### When to Use
- Target sequence has >30% identity to a template with known structure
- Below 30% identity: twilight zone — modeling becomes unreliable
- Below 20%: night zone — structure prediction methods preferred

### The Modeling Process
1. Template identification: BLAST/HHpred against PDB
2. Target-template alignment: critical step; errors here propagate to model
3. Model building: copy conserved regions; model loops ab initio; place side chains
4. Refinement: energy minimization
5. Quality assessment: MolProbity, ProSA, DOPE score

### Tools
- **MODELLER**: gold standard; fragment-based + satisfaction of spatial restraints; Python API
- **SWISS-MODEL**: web server; automated pipeline; good for exploration
- **I-TASSER**: ab initio modeling + structural fragment assembly; handles templates with low identity

## Structure Prediction

### AlphaFold2

**The Architecture**
- Input: multiple sequence alignment (MSA) + template structures
- Evoformer: attention-based processing of MSA and pairwise distance representations
  - Row-wise and column-wise attention over MSA captures coevolution
  - Triangle update operations propagate distance information
- Structure module: Invariant Point Attention (IPA); places residues in 3D space
- End-to-end differentiable: trained on PDB structures

**Confidence Metrics**
- **pLDDT** (predicted Local Distance Difference Test): per-residue confidence 0–100
  - >90: very high confidence (usually correct)
  - 70–90: confident
  - 50–70: low confidence; use with caution
  - <50: disordered or unreliable region
- **PAE** (Predicted Aligned Error): predicted error in residue pair positions
  - Low PAE between domains: confident relative domain orientation
  - High inter-domain PAE: flexible linker between confidently folded domains

**Limitations**
- Trained on single-state structures; may not capture multiple conformations
- Cannot predict intrinsically disordered regions
- Ligand-bound states not directly predicted (without modification)
- No explicit prediction of dynamics or allostery

**AlphaFold3**
- Extends to nucleic acids, small molecules, ions, modified residues
- Uses diffusion-based structure generation
- Available as server (AlphaFold Server); code released for academic use

**ESMFold (Meta)**
- Language model only — no MSA required
- Faster than AlphaFold2; useful for high-throughput predictions
- Lower accuracy, especially for proteins with little homology

**RoseTTAFold**
- Three-track architecture (1D, 2D, 3D information processed jointly)
- Foundation for RoseTTAFold2, RFdiffusion (protein design)

### Coevolutionary Methods
- Residues in contact coevolve: if position A mutates, position B must compensate
- Direct Coupling Analysis (DCA): disentangle direct from indirect correlations
- EVCouplings, RaptorX-Contact
- Used as distance restraints in structure prediction
- Now largely superseded by AlphaFold2's attention mechanism

## Molecular Docking

### What Docking Does
- Predict preferred binding pose of a small molecule (ligand) in a protein binding site
- Score binding affinity: distinguishing binders from non-binders

### Docking Protocols

**Rigid Docking**
- Both protein and ligand treated as rigid bodies
- Fast; misses conformational changes upon binding
- AutoDock Vina: free; widely used; empirical + knowledge-based scoring

**Flexible Ligand / Rigid Protein**
- Ligand conformation sampled; protein rigid
- Standard in most docking tools
- Systematic or stochastic sampling of ligand rotatable bonds

**Flexible Protein Docking**
- Model protein side chain flexibility (induced fit)
- Computationally expensive
- **Glide (Schrödinger)**: commercial; XP mode with induced fit; high accuracy
- **AutoDock4**: flexible protein side chains; slower but free

### Scoring Functions
- **Force field-based**: MM energy (electrostatics + van der Waals + desolvation)
- **Empirical**: trained on experimental binding affinities
- **Knowledge-based**: derived from statistical analysis of crystal structures
- All are approximate: virtual screening performance (enrichment factor, ROC AUC) more informative than predicted ΔG accuracy

### Virtual Screening
- Screen large compound libraries (millions) against target structure
- Hierarchical: fast filters → rigorous docking → hit confirmation
- ZINC, ChEMBL as compound databases
- Docking enrichment factor: how much better docking is than random at finding actives

## Protein Design

### Computational Protein Design

**Rosetta**
- Sequence design + structure prediction + docking
- Monte Carlo search with Rosetta energy function
- Fixed backbone design: find sequence that folds to target structure
- Flexible backbone: alternate backbone sampling (FastRelax, backrub)
- Applications: enzyme design, protein-protein interface design, de novo protein design

**ProteinMPNN**
- Graph neural network trained on PDB structures
- Input: protein backbone (Cα, Cβ, N, C coordinates)
- Output: amino acid probability distribution per position
- Very fast (~seconds); used for sequence design in RFdiffusion pipelines
- Available: Dauparas et al. 2022; widely used benchmark

**RFdiffusion**
- Generative diffusion model for protein backbone design
- Applications: binder design, enzyme active site scaffolding, symmetric oligomers
- Unconditional and conditional generation (given target binding site)
- Combined with ProteinMPNN for full pipeline: RFdiffusion → ProteinMPNN → AlphaFold validation

**ESM-2 and Protein Language Models**
- Transformer trained on 250M protein sequences
- Representations capture structural, functional, and evolutionary information
- ESM-IF1: inverse folding (structure → sequence)
- Fine-tune ESM representations for downstream tasks (thermostability, function prediction)

### Directed Evolution + Computational Screening
- Generate variant library computationally (saturating mutagenesis, combinatorial)
- Score with energy functions or ML models
- Prioritize variants for experimental testing
- Dramatically reduces experimental burden
