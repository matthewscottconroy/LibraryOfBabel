# Section 6: Tree Support and Evaluation

You have inferred a phylogenetic tree. Before you interpret it, publish it, or use it as input for any downstream analysis, you need to evaluate how much confidence to place in each of its clades. This is not a formality — phylogenetic clades vary enormously in their evidential support, from nodes supported by broad genomic consensus to nodes that reflect only a handful of informative sites or a systematic artifact of the model.

The dominant approach to evaluating support is bootstrap resampling, introduced by Felsenstein in 1985. The bootstrap asks: how reproducible is this clade across random subsamples of the data? By resampling alignment columns with replacement, it creates a distribution of trees from slightly different views of the data, and bootstrap support measures how consistently a clade appears across this distribution. Bootstrap support ≥ 70% is the conventional threshold for "reasonable support," and ≥ 95% for "strong support" — thresholds that have been empirically calibrated but are approximate.

What bootstrap support is not: the probability that the clade is correct. A clade with 100% bootstrap support can still be wrong if there is a systematic bias in the data — such as long-branch attraction — that affects all bootstrap replicates equally. Understanding the distinction between data consistency (what bootstrap measures) and statistical probability of correctness (what it cannot directly measure) is essential for interpreting phylogenetic evidence.

This section also introduces two more recent and complementary approaches. Approximate likelihood ratio tests (aLRT, SH-aLRT) provide fast alternatives to bootstrap that test whether the ML tree is significantly better than its nearest competitor topology at each branch. Concordance factors — both site concordance factors (sCF) and gene concordance factors (gCF) — measure what fraction of the individual data points (sites, genes) actually support each clade, rather than measuring consistency across resamples of the full dataset. The finding that many clades with high bootstrap support have low concordance factors — supported by only 30–40% of sites or genes — has been one of the most important revelations of modern phylogenomics, revealing that high bootstrap can be generated by a minority of concordant data rather than broad genomic consensus.

Together, bootstrap, aLRT, and concordance factors provide complementary views of phylogenetic support that collectively give a much more complete picture than any single metric alone.
