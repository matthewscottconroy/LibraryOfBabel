# 3.1 Genetic Parts and Devices

The component layer of synthetic biology — the physical elements from which circuits are assembled.

---

## The Parts Hierarchy

**Parts** → **Devices** → **Systems**

- Part: a sequence with a single defined function (promoter, RBS, terminator)
- Device: combination of parts that performs a function (inducible expression cassette)
- System: combination of devices implementing a complex behavior (biosensor, oscillator)

This hierarchy only works when parts behave predictably in combination. Much of synthetic biology research is about making this true.

## Promoters

### Constitutive Promoters (Bacteria)

Function: direct RNA polymerase to initiate transcription at a defined rate.

**Structure (σ70 promoters in *E. coli*)**
- -35 element: TTGACA (consensus) — σ70 recognition
- -10 element: TATAAT (Pribnow box) — strand separation, RNAP isomerization
- Spacer: 17 ± 1 bp between -35 and -10 — critical for activity
- Extended -10: TGNTATAAT — stronger promoters
- UP element: A/T rich, upstream — binds α-subunit CTD; activates strong promoters

**Promoter Strength**
- Expressed in PoPS (Polymerases Per Second) or relative to reference
- Measured by: fluorescent reporter assays, RNA-seq, in vitro transcription
- BioBrick promoters: J23 series (J23100–J23119); covers ~factor of 10 in strength
- Promoter libraries: FACS-sort reporters → sequence → map genotype to strength

**Sigma Factor Engineering**
- Different σ factors: σ70 (housekeeping), σ32 (heat shock), σ54 (nitrogen), σ28 (flagella)
- Orthogonal RNAP: T7 RNAP driven by synthetic σ factor; completely orthogonal to host
- T7 RNAP: extremely fast (5× E. coli RNAP); high levels of expression

### Inducible Promoters

**lac/lacUV5 System**
- Mechanism: LacI tetramer binds operator (O1, O2, O3) → blocks transcription
- Inducer: IPTG (non-metabolized), allolactose (natural)
- IPTG binding: conformational change → LacI-DNA affinity drops 1000×
- Hill coefficient: ~2 (due to tetramer binding two operators in DNA loop)
- Transfer function: characterize [IPTG] → fluorescence with Hill fit

**tet (tetracycline repressor) System**
- TetR represses PLtet-O1
- Inducer: anhydrotetracycline (aTc) — non-antibiotic analog
- High dynamic range: aTc titration covers ~100-fold expression range
- Used in: toggle switch (Gardner et al. 2000)
- Reverse Tet system (rTetR): rtTA activates when doxycycline present

**ara (arabinose) System**
- AraC: dual-function activator/repressor depending on arabinose
- Without arabinose: AraC loops DNA (repression)
- With arabinose: AraC binds I1 and I2 → activates pBAD
- Bimodal response in populations (not always graded in individual cells)

**Characterization Best Practices**
- Measure dose-response at multiple growth conditions (media, temperature)
- Use isogenic strains — chromosomal location affects expression
- Characterize dynamic response: induction and de-induction kinetics
- Report: uninduced leakiness, maximum expression, EC50, Hill coefficient

### Promoter Engineering

**Random Mutagenesis Libraries**
- Mutate spacer, -35, -10 regions randomly
- Screen by fluorescence sorting → sequence → map mutations to strength
- Often: -35 and -10 deviated from consensus → graded library

**Computational Design**
- Thermodynamic model of promoter-RNAP interaction
- Promoter Calculator (Salis lab): predict transcription rate from sequence
- Inverse design: specify target strength → generate sequence

### Eukaryotic Promoters

**Minimal Promoters**
- TATA box (if present), Initiator (Inr), downstream promoter element (DPE)
- Minimal promoters from CYC1, HSP, CMV: low baseline for inducible systems

**Strong Constitutive**
- EF1α, CMV, PGK, UBC: strong ubiquitous expression
- CAG hybrid promoter: CMV enhancer + chicken β-actin promoter + splice donor

**Inducible (Mammalian)**
- Tet-On/Tet-Off: rtTA or tTA systems; doxycycline regulated
- Gal4-UAS: yeast hybrid activator; orthogonal to mammalian genome
- Light-inducible: CRY2-CIB, LightOn (Vivid-based), PhyB-PIF (far-red light)
- Chemical: rapamycin-induced dimerization (FKBP-FRB), gibberellin (GAI-GID1)

## Ribosome Binding Sites (RBS) and Translation Control

### RBS Function (Bacteria)

The RBS is the sequence that recruits the ribosome to the mRNA start codon.

**Key sequence elements:**
- Shine-Dalgarno (SD) sequence: ~AAGG, ~8-10 nt upstream of start codon
- Complementary to 16S rRNA 3' end (anti-SD sequence)
- Spacer between SD and AUG: optimal ~7-8 nt
- Start codon: AUG (most efficient), GUG, UUG
- Downstream sequence: secondary structure around start codon reduces TIR

**RBS Strength ↔ Translation Initiation Rate (TIR)**
- TIR quantified as ribosome flux (ribosomes initiating per mRNA per unit time)
- Ranges over ~100,000-fold from near-zero to maximum

### Salis RBS Calculator

Thermodynamic model predicting TIR from sequence:

```
ΔG_total = ΔG_mRNA:rRNA - ΔG_start - ΔG_spacing - ΔG_standby + ΔG_SD·AUG
```

- ΔG_mRNA:rRNA: energy of ribosome binding to SD region
- ΔG_start: energy of ribosome reaching the start codon
- Secondary structure penalties for occlusion of SD or start codon

**Using the RBS Calculator (Python API):**
```python
from RBS_Calculator import RBS_Calculator

calc = RBS_Calculator(pre_seq, post_seq, start_range=(0, 30), org='Escherichia coli str. K-12 substr. MG1655')
results = calc.run()
print(results['TIR'])  # translation initiation rate
```

**Inverse design:** specify target TIR → generate RBS sequences

### Codon Optimization

Synonymous codons are not equivalent:
- Rare codons: corresponding tRNA less abundant → translational pausing
- Ribosome queuing: stalls upstream ribosomes → reduces overall rate
- mRNA secondary structure: codon choice affects folding of nascent mRNA

**Codon Optimization Tools:**
- CAI (Codon Adaptation Index): ratio of actual to maximum usage frequency
- COOL (Codon Optimization Online): balance CAI with avoid repeat sequences
- JCat: organism-specific codon optimization
- IDT Codon Optimization Tool

**Cautions:**
- Maximum CAI ≠ maximum expression: secondary structure effects matter
- Avoid repeat sequences: cause recombination instability
- Species-specific: human codons are suboptimal in *E. coli*

## Terminators

### Intrinsic Terminators (Bacteria)

Sequence-dependent; no protein required:
1. RNA forms stable stem-loop (G/C rich, high ΔG folding)
2. Followed by poly-U run (~8 U)
3. RNA-DNA hybrid weakens → RNAP releases
4. Efficiency: fraction of RNAPs that terminate (vs. read through)

**Well-characterized terminators:**
- BBa_B0010, BBa_B0012: strong BioBrick terminators
- rrnB T1 and T2: ribosomal RNA operons; very strong
- T7 Te: used in T7 expression systems

**Terminator Libraries:** Cambray et al. 2013 — large library characterizing terminator strength vs. structure

### Rho-Dependent Termination

- Rho helicase tracks along mRNA
- At Rho utilization sites (rut): binds mRNA
- Rho translocates and catches paused RNAP → releases RNA
- Exploited for CRISPRi — dCas9 blocks RNAP → Rho termination

### Terminators as Insulators

Terminators between transcriptional units prevent:
- Readthrough transcription from upstream promoters
- Context-dependent expression variation

Double terminators (two in series) increase insulation.

## Regulatory RNA

### Small RNAs (sRNAs)

**Hfq-Dependent sRNAs (Bacteria)**
- 50–250 nt; act post-transcriptionally
- Base-pair with mRNA target: typically near ribosome binding site
- Hfq chaperone: stabilizes sRNA-mRNA interaction
- Mechanism: block translation initiation OR target mRNA for degradation (often both)
- Design: synthetic sRNA by changing base-pairing region + keeping Hfq scaffold

**Advantages of sRNA regulation:**
- Fast: changes in 5-10 minutes (vs. transcription factor: 30-60 min)
- No new protein needed: sRNA itself is the regulator
- Multiplexable: one sRNA can target multiple mRNAs

### Riboswitches

A riboswitch is an mRNA element (usually 5' UTR) that changes conformation upon ligand binding → controls expression of downstream gene.

**Structure:**
- Aptamer domain: folds to bind specific ligand with high specificity
- Expression platform: changes structure based on aptamer state → ON or OFF

**Common classes:**
| Ligand | Riboswitch class | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| SAM | SAM-I (S-box) | Translation OFF (hairpin sequesters SD) |
| Adenine | add A-riboswitch | Translation ON (stabilizes open structure) |
| TPP (B1) | thi box | Translation OFF; most widespread |
| Lysine | L-box | Translation OFF; metabolite feedback |
| Fluoride | flFMN? No — fluoride aptamer | Ribozyme cleavage |
| Cyclic di-GMP | GEMM | Translation/transcription control |

**Engineering riboswitches:**
- In vitro selection (SELEX) of aptamers for synthetic ligands
- Theophylline riboswitch: widely used in synthetic biology for small molecule control
- Incorporate into 5' UTR of gene of interest

### CRISPR-Based Post-Transcriptional Regulation

**CRISPRi (dCas9 + sgRNA)**
- dCas9: catalytically dead Cas9; binds DNA but does not cut
- sgRNA directs dCas9 to target gene promoter or coding region
- Mechanism: steric block of RNAP; Rho-dependent termination if targeting coding strand
- Repression: 10–1000-fold depending on target position and dCas9 variant
- Orthogonal: multiple dCas9 orthologs (SpdCas9, SadCas9, AsdCas9) can target different genes independently

**CRISPRa (dCas9 + activation domain)**
- dCas9-VP64, dCas9-VPR, dCas9-SAM: activation domains
- Target upstream of TSS → recruit transcription machinery
- Less predictable than CRISPRi; depends heavily on target site

### Toehold Switches

RNA-based translational switches (Green et al. 2014):
- Hairpin structure: sequesters RBS and start codon in stem loop
- Trigger RNA: designed to base-pair with linear toehold region
- Binding unfolds hairpin → RBS exposed → translation initiated
- Advantage: programmable logic, cell-free deployment
- Used in: point-of-care diagnostics (Zika, SARS-CoV-2)

## Insulators and Genetic Context

### The Context-Dependence Problem

Expression level of a part depends not only on the part itself but on surrounding sequences. Sources:
- Promoter read-through from upstream
- mRNA secondary structure extending into RBS
- Transcriptional terminator efficiency varies with flanking sequences
- Codon usage of upstream ORF affects downstream translation (ribosome queuing)

### Insulator Elements

**Transcriptional Insulators**
- Strong terminators between transcription units
- Sequence-independent spacers (random sequence or library-selected)

**Translational Insulators**
- Sequence-independent 5' UTRs
- Bicistronic designs: include short ORF before gene of interest to normalize ribosomal context

**Ribozyme-Based Insulators (RiboJ)**
- Self-cleaving ribozyme (from hammerhead or HDV family) in 5' UTR
- Cleaves itself → creates defined mRNA 5' end independent of upstream transcription start
- Normalizes expression across different promoters (removes 5' sequence context effects)

### Measurement and Reporting Standards

**PoPS (Polymerases Per Second)**: standard unit for promoter strength
**RIPS (Ribosomes Initiated Per Second)**: standard unit for translational strength

iGEM Registry and BioBrick standards: attempt to standardize part characterization
Key limitation: context-dependence means absolute PoPS/RIPS values are context-specific

**Best practice:** always characterize in the same genetic context as planned use; document context completely.
