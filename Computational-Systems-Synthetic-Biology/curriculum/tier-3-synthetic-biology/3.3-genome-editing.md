# 3.3 Genome Editing

Rewriting the genome with precision — from targeted cuts to base-level changes.

---

## CRISPR-Cas9: Mechanism

### Components
- **Cas9**: endonuclease from *Streptococcus pyogenes* (SpCas9)
- **sgRNA (single guide RNA)**: fusion of crRNA + tracrRNA; ~100 nt
  - Spacer: 20 nt complementary to target DNA
  - Scaffold: binds Cas9; forms characteristic structure
- **PAM (Protospacer Adjacent Motif)**: 5'-NGG-3' for SpCas9; on non-template strand

### Mechanism
1. sgRNA loaded into Cas9 → surveillance complex
2. Cas9 scans DNA for PAM sequences
3. PAM found → strand invasion; RNA-DNA hybridization at protospacer
4. R-loop formation: RNA displaces non-complementary strand
5. HNH domain cleaves complementary strand; RuvC domain cleaves non-complementary strand
6. DSB (double-strand break) 3 bp upstream of PAM

### Repair Pathways

**NHEJ (Non-Homologous End Joining)**
- Default repair in most cells
- Fast but error-prone: insertions and deletions (indels) at cut site
- Frameshift mutations → loss of function (gene knockout)
- Predominates in: mitotic and post-mitotic cells, at any phase of cell cycle

**HDR (Homology-Directed Repair)**
- Requires homology template (plasmid, ssODN, or sister chromatid)
- Can introduce precise mutations, insertions, or tags
- Predominates in: S/G2 phase of cell cycle; more efficient in dividing cells
- Efficiency: typically 1–30% of NHEJ frequency; depends heavily on cell type

**MMEJ (Microhomology-Mediated End Joining)**
- Short homology regions (2–25 bp) flanking the cut
- Predictable deletions: can be engineered for specific outcomes
- InDelphi tool: predicts repair outcome distribution from cut site sequence

## Guide RNA Design

### Design Principles
- Select 20-nt spacer with minimal off-target homology in the genome
- Avoid: 5'-TTTT (Pol III termination), homopolymer runs
- GC content: 40–70% for efficient binding
- Position relative to PAM: spacer is directly 5' of NGG

### Off-Target Prediction
- Off-targets: sites with sequence similarity to spacer + PAM
- Cas9 tolerates mismatches, especially in the 5' (seed region is 3' proximal ~12 bp)
- Up to ~5 mismatches can be tolerated for some spacers

**Tools:**
- **Cas-OFFinder**: exhaustive search for off-targets with defined mismatch count
- **CRISPOR**: comprehensive guide selection; multiple off-target algorithms + rule sets
- **CHOPCHOP**: web interface; integrates multiple prediction algorithms
- **CRISPR-ML**: machine learning models for on-target efficiency prediction

**Empirical off-target validation:**
- GUIDE-seq: DSB-trapping approach; unbiased genome-wide
- CIRCLE-seq: in vitro selection of Cas9-accessible off-targets
- Digenome-seq: whole-genome sequencing after in vitro Cas9 cleavage

### Minimizing Off-Target Activity
- Truncated guides (17-18 nt): reduces off-target while maintaining on-target
- Paired nickases (Cas9 D10A): two nicks = DSB only when both bind nearby; 50-1500× reduced off-target
- High-fidelity Cas9 variants: eSpCas9, SpCas9-HF1, HypaCas9 — altered PAM-interacting residues
- Reduce Cas9 exposure time: electroporation of ribonucleoprotein (RNP) instead of plasmid

## Base Editors

### Concept
Make single nucleotide changes without double-strand breaks.

**Cytosine Base Editor (CBE): C → T (or G → A on complementary strand)**
- Components: nCas9 (nickase, cuts only one strand) + cytidine deaminase (e.g., APOBEC1)
- Mechanism: nCas9 creates R-loop; cytidine deaminase converts C → U in ssDNA window
- U is read as T → C:G base pair → T:A after replication
- Editing window: positions 4-8 of protospacer (counting from PAM-distal end)

**Adenine Base Editor (ABE): A → G (or T → C)**
- Components: nCas9 + engineered adenosine deaminase (ABE8e)
- A → I (inosine); inosine read as G → A:T → G:C after replication
- Most ABE variants: editing window positions 4-7

**Base Editor Applications:**
- Correct pathogenic SNPs directly (no HDR template needed)
- Introduce stop codons (C-to-T in codon)
- Engineering phenotypes: modify protein function at specific residues

**Limitations:**
- Bystander editing: other C's or A's in the window also edited
- Base editor-specific off-targets: genome-wide cytosine/adenosine editing
- Window constraints: cannot edit all positions

## Prime Editing

### Concept (Anzalone et al. 2019)
All types of precise edits (substitutions, small insertions, small deletions) without DSBs and without HDR templates.

### Components
- **PE2**: nCas9 fused to engineered reverse transcriptase (M-MLV RT)
- **pegRNA (prime editing guide RNA)**: extended sgRNA with:
  - Spacer: targets genomic site
  - PBS (primer binding site): primes reverse transcription from nicked 3' flap
  - RT template: sequence encoding the desired edit + flanking sequence

### Mechanism
1. PE2-pegRNA complex binds target site
2. nCas9 nicks the PAM-containing strand
3. 3' flap generated; hybridizes to PBS in pegRNA
4. RT uses pegRNA RT template to synthesize edited 3' flap
5. 5' flap (unedited) removed; new 3' flap ligated
6. Second strand synthesis or nick repair → heteroduplex
7. Mismatch repair resolves to edited or unedited sequence

### PE3
- Additional sgRNA nicks the non-edited strand → forces mismatch repair to use edited strand as template
- Increases editing efficiency ~3×, but also increases indels slightly

### PE5
- Inhibit mismatch repair (MLH1 dominant-negative) + PE3 → higher efficiency, especially for insertions

### Limitations
- Less efficient than base editors for simple transitions: ~10–50% vs. 20–80%
- Larger pegRNA construct; more delivery challenge
- RT template length constrains insertion size (~50 bp max)

## Recombineering

### Lambda Red System

Enables precise chromosomal modifications using short homology arms in *E. coli* and related bacteria.

**Components:**
- Gam: inhibits RecBCD nuclease (protects linear DNA from degradation)
- Beta: ssDNA annealing protein (inserts ssDNA oligos)
- Exo: 5'→3' exonuclease (generates ssDNA from dsDNA)

**Applications:**
- Point mutations via ssDNA oligonucleotides (60-90 nt homology arms)
- Gene knockouts: replace with selection cassette + flanking homology
- Seamless recombineering: use counter-selection to remove selection cassette

**Multiplex Automated Genome Engineering (MAGE)**
- Multiple oligos simultaneously → high-throughput genome editing
- Used to: recode E. coli genome (Church lab); optimize metabolic pathways
- Iterative cycles: each cycle introduces mutations at targeted sites

### CRISPR-Assisted Recombineering
- CRISPR DSB at target site + recombineering oligo with repair template
- HDR efficiency dramatically increased by DSB at target
- Counter-select cells with unrepaired cuts (Cas9 lethality selects for repair)

## Genome-Scale Engineering

### Metabolic Engineering Applications
- Sequential knockouts using CRISPR: eliminate competing pathways
- Safe harbor sites: identified genomic loci that tolerate insertions without fitness cost
  - *S. cerevisiae*: CAN1, URA3 locus; GIII locus
  - Human: AAVS1 (PPP1R12C), Rosa26 equivalent, H11 locus

### CRISPRi/a Genome-Wide Screens
- sgRNA library: one sgRNA per gene → arrayed or pooled
- Pooled screen: transduce cells; apply selection; sequence sgRNA abundance
- Fitness screen: essential genes depleted over time
- Gene activation screen (CRISPRa): discover genes conferring resistance or new phenotypes
- **Mageck**: analysis pipeline for CRISPR screen data

### Genome Synthesis
- Total DNA synthesis: Venter group synthesized first synthetic bacterial chromosome (2010)
- JCVI-syn3.0: 473-gene minimal genome; demonstrates which genes are truly essential
- Genome recoding: replace all 321 TAG stop codons in *E. coli* (Krishnaswamy et al.)
  - Frees a codon for non-natural amino acid incorporation
- Synthetic yeast genome (Sc2.0 project): replace entire S. cerevisiae genome with designed sequence

## Delivery Methods

| System | Method | Efficiency | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bacteria | Electroporation, heat shock | High | Limited cargo size |
| Yeast | Electroporation, LiAc | Moderate | |
| Cell lines | Lipofection, electroporation | Variable | Toxicity |
| Primary cells | Nucleofection (RNP) | Variable | Cell-type specific |
| In vivo | AAV, LNP (lipid nanoparticle) | Tissue-dependent | Packaging size, immune |
| Plants | Agrobacterium, biolistics | Moderate | Regeneration required |

### RNP (Ribonucleoprotein) Delivery
- Pre-assemble Cas9 protein + sgRNA in vitro → deliver as complex
- Advantages: transient Cas9 exposure (lower off-targets), no DNA integration risk
- Method: electroporation most effective for primary cells
