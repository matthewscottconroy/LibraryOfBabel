# 3.5 Directed Evolution

Harnessing evolutionary forces with engineering intent — the most powerful tool for optimizing molecular function.

---

## Principles

### Why Directed Evolution?

Rational protein engineering requires complete mechanistic understanding: active site geometry, catalytic mechanism, conformational dynamics. We rarely have all of this.

Directed evolution circumvents this:
- Generate diversity (mutations or recombination)
- Apply selection or screening for desired property
- Amplify winners
- Iterate

It is optimization over sequence space without requiring mechanistic understanding.

### Fitness Landscapes

W.S. Wright's concept: protein fitness as a function of amino acid sequence.

- **Sequence space**: 20^n possible proteins of length n (~10^130 for a 100 aa protein)
- **Fitness landscape**: topography of this space for any measurable property
- **Local optima**: sequences surrounded by lower-fitness neighbors; evolution gets trapped here
- **Global optimum**: best possible sequence; may be unreachable from any starting point
- **Ruggedness**: depends on epistasis between mutations

**Epistasis shapes evolution:**
- Magnitude epistasis: combination of mutations has more or less effect than expected additively
- Sign epistasis: combination beneficial, but individually one is neutral or deleterious
- Reciprocal sign epistasis: creates fitness valleys between peaks — evolution gets stuck

### What This Means for Engineering

- **Starting point matters**: begin from the best available wild-type or known variant
- **Recombination**: jumps between peaks that share beneficial mutations; can cross valleys
- **Step size**: point mutations explore neighborhood; recombination explores across space
- **Order of mutations matters**: some orderings traverse fitness valleys; others don't

## Generating Diversity

### Error-Prone PCR (epPCR)

Reduce fidelity of PCR:
- Use Mn2+ (Manganese) instead of Mg2+: destabilizes Watson-Crick base pairing
- Imbalanced dNTP concentrations: biases toward specific mutation types
- Use low-fidelity polymerases: Mutazyme II, GeneMorph II

**Mutation rate control:**
- Target: 1–3 amino acid changes per gene (too many = most mutations deleterious)
- Calculate from: bp length × mutation rate = average substitutions/molecule

**Bias:**
- Transitions (A↔G, C↔T) more frequent than transversions
- Some residues systematically under-sampled
- Solution: use multiple epPCR protocols or combine with saturation mutagenesis

### Saturation Mutagenesis

Randomize specific positions completely:
- Use degenerate codons: NNK (32 codons, all 20 amino acids, 1 stop codon) or NNS
- At position X: replace with NNK → generate all possible amino acids
- Combinatorial: randomize 2-3 positions simultaneously → 20² to 20³ variants
- Focus on: active site residues, predicted key positions from structure

**Smart libraries:**
- CADENAS (Combinatorial Active-Site Saturation Mutagenesis): focus on active site shell
- CASTing (Combinatorial Active-Site Saturation Test): iterative, position-by-position
- OmniChange: simultaneous saturation of 5 positions with overlapping primers

### Chemical Mutagenesis

- **EMS (ethyl methanesulfonate)**: alkylates guanine → G:C → A:T transitions
- **NTG (N-methyl-N'-nitro-N-nitrosoguanidine)**: similar to EMS, also causes frameshifts
- **Mutator strains**: bacteria with defective mismatch repair (mutD, mutL, mutS)
- Used for: whole-genome mutagenesis; ALE (adaptive laboratory evolution)

### Recombination Methods

**DNA Shuffling (Stemmer 1994)**
1. Fragment genes from multiple homologs with DNase I
2. Reassemble fragments by PCR without primers (priming on homologous regions)
3. Full-length recombinant genes emerge
4. Requires ~50% homology between parents

**StEP (Staggered Extension Process)**
- Very short extensions with many PCR cycles
- Short extension → polymerase dissociates → reassociates to new template
- Effectively recombines sequences during PCR

**SCHEMA (Structure-guided Recombination)**
- Uses protein structure to minimize disruptive breaks
- Choose recombination points in loops/linkers between structural elements
- Maintains structural integrity while sampling new combinations
- Romero et al. 2013: SCHEMA-designed cytochrome P450 library

**Heteroduplex Recombination / Oligonucleotide-Directed Evolution**
- Introduce diversity with synthetic oligonucleotides at defined positions
- Highly targeted; can introduce non-natural amino acids

## Selection vs. Screening

### The Distinction

**Selection**: only variants with desired property survive or grow
- Extremely high throughput: test 10^8–10^12 variants at once
- Requires tight linkage between desired property and survival/growth
- Examples: antibiotic resistance, complementation of auxotrophy, survival under lethal condition

**Screening**: measure property of individual variants; choose best
- Requires assay per variant: throughput limited to 10^3–10^6 typically
- More information per variant (not just pass/fail)
- Allows optimization of any measurable property

**Rule**: use selection when possible; design selection to link your desired property to survival.

### Designing Selections

**Antibiotic resistance complementation**
- Replace target protein with variant library; cells lacking activity die on antibiotic
- Works for: enzymes with resistance role, target protein needed for antibiotic efflux

**Auxotrophy complementation**
- Delete essential biosynthetic gene; express library as complementation
- Works for: enzymes in essential metabolic pathways

**Phage display selection**
- Display protein variant on phage surface
- Bind to target → enriched by affinity pull-down
- Non-binders → washed away
- Works for: antibody engineering, binding protein evolution, peptide ligands

**SELEX (Systematic Evolution of Ligands by Exponential enrichment)**
- For nucleic acid aptamers (not proteins)
- Incubate library with target → recover bound sequences → amplify → repeat
- After 8-15 rounds → enriched aptamers with high affinity

### Flow Cytometry Sorting (FACS)

Transform screening into ultra-high-throughput (10^6–10^8 cells/hour):
- Link enzyme activity to fluorescence in cell
- Sort cells by fluorescence intensity
- Recovery of fluorescent cells → re-grow → sequence

**Coupling strategies:**
- Product activates fluorescent biosensor (metabolite sensor + reporter)
- Enzyme activity generates fluorescent product directly
- Growth rate correlates with activity: sort for fastest-growing cells

**FACS sorting considerations:**
- Cell sorting: ~20,000 events/second standard; ~100,000 with specialized systems
- Each cell = one variant if library is diverse enough
- Gate on top 0.1–1% of fluorescent cells
- Multiple rounds of sorting enriches further

### Droplet Microfluidics

Ultra-high throughput (10^6–10^8 variants) with quantitative screening:
- Encapsulate individual cells in water-in-oil droplets (~1–10 pL)
- Each droplet = one cell = one variant
- Incubate: product accumulates in droplet (not diluted by diffusion)
- Sort droplets by fluorescence (optical detection + electrocoalescence or acoustics)

**Capabilities:**
- Any colorimetric or fluorescence assay
- Can lyse cells and measure secreted products or permeabilized-cell activity
- Combine with MALDI-MS or other readouts

**Key limitation**: only works if library is cellularly expressed.

## Machine Learning-Guided Directed Evolution (MLDE)

### The Oracle Problem

Can we predict fitness of unobserved sequences from a small number of measurements?

**The MLDE cycle:**
1. Generate initial variant library; measure fitness for ~100–1000 variants
2. Train surrogate model: sequence → fitness
3. Use model to propose next variants (acquisition function)
4. Measure proposed variants; add to training data
5. Retrain model; repeat

This prioritizes experimental effort on promising regions of sequence space.

### Sequence Representations

- **One-hot encoding**: 20 × L binary matrix; simple but ignores relationships
- **ESM-2 embeddings**: 1280-dim vector per residue; captures evolutionary context
- **ProtTrans embeddings**: similar; alternative transformer-based
- **Structure-based**: graph neural network features from 3D coordinates

### Surrogate Models

- **Gaussian process**: uncertainty estimates; natural for Bayesian optimization
- **Random forest**: robust to small datasets; interpretable feature importance
- **Deep neural networks**: best performance with large training sets (>1000 variants)
- **Linear models**: useful for analyzing epistasis; simple but fast

### Acquisition Functions (Bayesian Optimization)

Balance exploration (unknown regions) vs. exploitation (near known good sequences):
- **Greedy**: always propose highest-predicted fitness → exploitation
- **Expected Improvement (EI)**: expected improvement over current best; balances both
- **Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)**: mean + k × uncertainty; tune k
- **Thompson Sampling**: sample from posterior → propose highest sampled value

### Published MLDE Examples

- **De novo protein design with ProteinMPNN → AlphaFold validation → synthesis → wet lab** (David Baker group workflow)
- **Antibody optimization**: train on binding data → propose higher-affinity variants
- **Stability prediction**: train on Tm data → design more thermostable enzymes
- **EVOLVEpro** (Rice lab): generalized MLDE framework

## Characterizing Directed Evolution Results

### What to Measure

For the winning variants, fully characterize:
- **Specific activity** (kcat): purified enzyme, multiple substrate concentrations
- **Km**: substrate concentration for half-max rate
- **kcat/Km**: specificity constant
- **Thermostability (Tm)**: circular dichroism thermal melt or differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF)
- **Substrate scope**: activity on related substrates (selectivity)
- **Stereoselectivity**: enantiomeric excess (ee%) for chiral reactions
- **Expression level**: solubility in recombinant host

### Understanding Mutations

- Structure of mutant (X-ray, cryo-EM, or AlphaFold2 for homology modeling)
- Identify mechanism: active site geometry change? New hydrogen bonds? Reduced flexibility?
- Combine mutations: test for additivity or synergy; build toward global optimum
- Publish with: full sequence, detailed characterization, mechanism hypothesis

## Natural vs. Directed Evolution

| Feature | Natural Evolution | Directed Evolution |
|---|---|---|
| Selection pressure | Survival/reproduction | Engineered for desired property |
| Timescale | Millions of years | Days to months |
| Mutation rate | ~10⁻⁹/bp/generation | 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻¹/bp (directed) |
| Population size | Millions to billions | 10⁶ to 10¹² (typical library) |
| Selective advantage | Subtle | Often binary (survive/die) |
| Accessible space | Limited by population size | Limited by library size |
| Memory | Mutation accumulation | Single round = no memory |
