# 3.6 Cell-Free Systems

Biology without cells — rapid prototyping platforms and programmable biosensors.

---

## What Are Cell-Free Systems?

Cell-free systems use the molecular machinery extracted from cells (or reconstituted from purified components) to perform biochemical reactions outside living cells.

**Advantages over cell-based systems:**
- Fast cycle time: design → test in 4–8 hours (vs. days for cell transformation)
- Open system: add substrates, adjust pH, temperature, redox
- No growth requirements: no competition between production and growth
- Direct measurement: add sensors directly to reaction
- Toxic products tolerated: no cell death
- Non-natural conditions: non-natural substrates, solvents, extremes

**Disadvantages:**
- Not self-replicating: must reconstitute each reaction
- Limited energy supply: finite ATP regeneration
- Higher cost at scale than fermentation
- Shorter timescale: typically hours, not days

---

## PURE System

The most defined cell-free translation system.

**Components (fully reconstituted from purified proteins):**
- Ribosomes (70S)
- All 20 aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases
- All translation factors (IFs, EFs, RF, RRF)
- All tRNAs
- RNA polymerase (T7)
- Energy regeneration: creatine kinase + creatine phosphate (or other)
- ATP, GTP, amino acids, NTPs

**Why PURE matters:**
- Completely defined: no unknown components
- Can modify anything: add new components, remove existing ones
- Supports: non-natural amino acid incorporation (amber suppression)
- Commercial versions: PURExpress (NEB), myTXTL (Arbor Biosciences)

**Limitations:**
- More expensive than extract-based systems
- Lower yield than optimized E. coli extracts

## E. coli Extract-Based Systems

Crude cell lysate contains all transcription/translation machinery.

**Preparation:**
1. Grow E. coli to mid-log or stationary phase
2. Wash and lyse cells (bead-beating, French press, or sonication)
3. Run-off reaction: deplete endogenous mRNA
4. Centrifuge to remove cell debris
5. Dialyze to remove small molecules
6. Supplement with: buffer, energy regeneration, amino acids, NTPs

**Yields:**
- Typical: 0.5–3 mg/mL protein from batch reaction
- Optimized systems: up to 6+ mg/mL
- Reaction volume: 10 µL (microwell plate) to 100 L (industrial)

**Energy regeneration systems:**
- Creatine phosphate/creatine kinase (CP/CK): most common; ~4 hours
- Maltose/maltodextrin: longer duration; more complex
- Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP): clean but expensive
- Oxidative phosphorylation (PURE+membranes): renewable; technically demanding

## Applications

### Rapid Genetic Circuit Prototyping

Test circuit designs in hours, not days:
- Synthesize DNA (PCR product or plasmid)
- Add to cell-free reaction
- Measure output (fluorescence, luminescence) in plate reader
- Iterate design 5–10 cycles per day

**Comparison to in vivo:**
- Cell-free results generally correlate with in vivo behavior at circuit topology level
- Quantitative parameters often differ (different concentrations, turnover)
- Use cell-free to filter out non-working designs before committing to cell-based work

**Example workflow (Noireaux lab, Sun et al.):**
- 384-well plate: test 384 promoter variants simultaneously
- Each well: one PCR-amplified construct + extract + buffer
- Read fluorescence every 5 min for 8 hours
- Full promoter library characterized in one experiment

### Biosensors and Diagnostics

**Toehold switch-based diagnostics:**
- Lyophilized extract + toehold switch on paper
- Add patient sample → trigger RNA activates switch if target present → colorimetric output
- Zika virus detection (Pardee et al. 2016): shelf-stable, no refrigeration required
- SARS-CoV-2 diagnostics during pandemic

**Advantages for point-of-care:**
- Lyophilize extract + DNA → shelf-stable at room temperature
- Rehydrate with patient sample
- Visual readout (LacZ → yellow, GFP → green)
- No electricity or cold chain required

### Non-Natural Amino Acid Incorporation

In PURE system, introduce orthogonal aaRS/tRNA pair that reads amber codon (UAG):
- Suppress UAG with non-natural amino acid
- Incorporate: azide groups (for click chemistry), fluorescent amino acids, crosslinkers
- Genetic code expansion for: protein labeling, biophysical probes, unnatural polymer synthesis

### Cell-Free Metabolic Engineering

Run multi-enzyme pathways in vitro:
- Add purified (or crude) enzymes sequentially
- No cell growth requirements: optimize conditions for each enzyme independently
- Toxic intermediates: no cell death
- Test pathway designs rapidly before engineering into cells

**Example**: pyruvate → acetaldehyde → ethanol in cell-free reaction (validate pathway before in vivo)

### Synthetic Cells (Minimal Life)

Building functional protocells:
- Lipid vesicles enclosing cell-free machinery
- PURE system inside GUV (giant unilamellar vesicle)
- Can: transcribe, translate, and potentially divide
- Challenges: energy regeneration, membrane protein incorporation, self-replication

**Active research areas:**
- Self-replicating RNA (ribozymes)
- Minimal genetic systems in lipid vesicles
- Open protocell systems with semipermeable membranes

## Quantitative Characterization with Cell-Free

Cell-free enables systematic parameter measurement:

**Promoter characterization:**
- Linear dose-response between promoter copy number and expression (at low concentrations)
- Allows direct measurement of PoPS (polymerases per second) by varying DNA concentration

**RBS characterization:**
- Measure protein output per mRNA (translation initiation rate)
- Compare RBS variants under identical conditions

**Protein-protein interactions:**
- Cell-free co-immunoprecipitation
- FRET measurements on cell-free expressed proteins

## Limitations to Know

### What Cell-Free Cannot Do
- Model growth dynamics (no cell division)
- Test long-term genetic stability
- Model metabolic burden on growing cells
- Predict promoter strength when chromosome copy number matters

### Quantitative Differences from In Vivo
- Molecular crowding: cellular crowding affects diffusion and folding; extract differs
- Chaperone availability: proper folding of complex proteins may require specific chaperones not abundant in extract
- Post-translational modifications: many mammalian PTMs absent in E. coli extract
- pH and redox: differ between cellular compartments and test tube

### Use Cell-Free For:
- Rapid design iteration (topology testing)
- High-throughput part characterization
- Diagnostics and portable biosensors
- Toxic or non-biological reactions

### Use Cells For:
- Final functional validation
- Long-term production
- Growth-coupled optimization
- Multi-day fermentation processes
