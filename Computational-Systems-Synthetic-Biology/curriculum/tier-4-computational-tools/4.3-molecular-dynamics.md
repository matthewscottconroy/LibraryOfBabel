# 4.3 Molecular Dynamics Simulation

Simulating atomic motion — from protein folding to drug binding to membrane dynamics.

---

## Theory

### What MD Simulates

Molecular dynamics numerically integrates Newton's equations of motion for a system of atoms:
```
F_i = m_i · a_i = -∂V/∂r_i
```

At each time step (~2 fs), compute forces from potential energy function; update velocities and positions.

Result: a trajectory — atomic positions as a function of time, from which thermodynamic, structural, and kinetic properties are computed.

### Force Fields

A force field is a mathematical function describing the potential energy of the system as a function of atomic coordinates.

**Bonded terms:**
```
V_bonds    = Σ k_b(r - r₀)²              (harmonic bond stretching)
V_angles   = Σ k_θ(θ - θ₀)²             (angle bending)
V_dihedrals = Σ V_n[1 + cos(nφ - δ)]    (torsion rotation)
V_impropers = Σ k_ω(ω - ω₀)²           (out-of-plane motions)
```

**Non-bonded terms:**
```
V_LJ = Σ ε[(σ/r)¹² - 2(σ/r)⁶]         (van der Waals: Lennard-Jones)
V_elec = Σ q_iq_j / (4πε₀r_ij)         (Coulombic electrostatics)
```

**Major force field families:**

| FF | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| AMBER (ff19SB, ff14SB) | Proteins, nucleic acids | Most validated for proteins |
| CHARMM (CGenFF, CHARMM36) | Proteins, lipids, carbohydrates | Excellent lipid parameters |
| OPLS-AA/M | Proteins, small molecules | Good for organic solvents |
| GROMOS | Proteins in explicit solvent | Common in Europe |
| CGenFF | CHARMM small molecules | Automatic parameterization |
| GAFF2 (AMBER) | Small molecule ligands | General Amber FF |

**Force field parameterization:**
- Parameters derived from: QM calculations, experimental data (X-ray, NMR, thermodynamic)
- Limitation: fixed charges (no polarizability); transferability across systems
- Polarizable FFs: Drude, AMOEBA — more accurate, much more expensive

### Integrators

**Verlet algorithm:**
```
r(t+Δt) = 2r(t) - r(t-Δt) + F(t)/m · Δt²
```
- Time-reversible; energy-conserving (in NVE ensemble)
- Numerically stable

**Leapfrog:**
- Velocities at half-step; positions at full step
- Equivalent to Verlet; used in GROMACS

**Velocity Verlet:**
```
r(t+Δt) = r(t) + v(t)Δt + F(t)/(2m)Δt²
v(t+Δt) = v(t) + [F(t) + F(t+Δt)]/(2m)Δt
```
- Stores positions and velocities at same time
- Used in NAMD, OpenMM

### Time Step

- Standard proteins: Δt = 2 fs (bonds involving H constrained with LINCS/SHAKE)
- With H-mass repartitioning: Δt = 4 fs
- Without constraints: Δt = 1 fs
- Too large → energy drift; explosion of system

### Periodic Boundary Conditions (PBC)

Simulate bulk behavior with finite box:
- Box replicated in all directions
- Atoms leaving one face re-enter from opposite face
- Minimum image convention: interact with nearest periodic image

**Box shapes:**
- Cubic: simplest; most wasteful for globular proteins (corners are vacuum)
- Rhombic dodecahedron: smallest box containing sphere; ~29% less water than cube
- Hexagonal prism: for membrane simulations

### Long-Range Electrostatics

Coulomb interactions decay as 1/r — cannot simply truncate:
- **PME (Particle Mesh Ewald)**: split into short-range (real space) + long-range (reciprocal space)
- Ewald sum: exact; O(N^1.5) → PME: O(N log N)
- Ewald error controlled by: real space cutoff (~10-12 Å), grid spacing (~1.2 Å), PME order (4-6)
- Standard: always use PME for charged or polar systems

## MD in Practice

### System Preparation

**1. Start from structure (PDB or AlphaFold)**
- Download from PDB or AlphaFold database
- Check: missing residues, missing atoms, multiple conformations, non-standard residues
- Protonate: H++ or PropKa for pKa-based protonation; manually verify active sites

**2. Add hydrogen atoms**
- X-ray structures lack H (below X-ray resolution); cryo-EM and NMR may have H
- pdb2gmx, tleap, or VMD/psfgen for CHARMM topology

**3. Define force field topology**
- Assign atom types, partial charges, bonded parameters
- GROMACS: `pdb2gmx -ff charmm36m-iw -water tip3p`
- AMBER: `tleap` with AMBER force field and frcmod files

**4. Solvate**
- Fill box with water molecules
- Minimum 10-15 Å between protein and box edge
- `gmx solvate -cp protein.gro -cs spc216.gro -o solvated.gro`

**5. Add ions**
- Neutralize charge: add Na+ or Cl- to achieve charge balance
- Add salt: typically 150 mM NaCl to mimic physiological conditions
- `gmx genion -s ions.tpr -neutral -conc 0.15`

### Minimization and Equilibration

**Energy minimization**
- Remove bad contacts from structure preparation
- Steepest descent until forces < threshold
- 1000–5000 steps typically sufficient

**NVT equilibration** (constant N, V, T)
- Fix protein backbone (restraints); let solvent equilibrate
- 100 ps; target temperature with thermostat (V-rescale or Nosé-Hoover)
- Verify: temperature stable at target; no large energy drifts

**NPT equilibration** (constant N, P, T)
- Release backbone restraints gradually
- Allow box size to equilibrate to target pressure (1 bar)
- 1-5 ns; verify: pressure, density, box dimensions stable

**Production MD**
- No restraints; collect data
- Duration: depends on process:
  - Local conformational sampling: 100 ns–1 µs
  - Large domain motions: µs–ms (requires enhanced sampling)
  - Protein folding: µs–ms for small proteins
  - Membrane equilibration: 100 ns–µs

### Thermostats and Barostats

**Thermostats** (maintain target temperature):
- Berendsen: velocity rescaling; fast; not strictly canonical (use only for equilibration)
- V-rescale (GROMACS): stochastic velocity rescaling; canonical ensemble
- Nosé-Hoover: extended system; canonical; preferred for production

**Barostats** (maintain target pressure):
- Berendsen: isotropic; fast equilibration; not strictly NPT
- Parrinello-Rahman: extended system; correct fluctuations; use for production
- Semi-isotropic: for membranes (z different from xy)

## Trajectory Analysis

### Basic Structural Analysis

```python
import MDAnalysis as mda
import numpy as np

u = mda.Universe('topology.tpr', 'trajectory.xtc')
protein = u.select_atoms('protein')

# RMSD: structural deviation from reference
from MDAnalysis.analysis import rms
R = rms.RMSD(protein, select='backbone')
R.run()
print(f"Average RMSD: {R.rmsd[:,2].mean():.2f} Å")

# RMSF: per-residue flexibility
rmsf = rms.RMSF(protein).run()
# High RMSF = flexible (loop regions); low RMSF = rigid (secondary structure elements)

# Radius of gyration: compactness
from MDAnalysis.analysis.hydrogenbonds.hbond_analysis import HydrogenBondAnalysis
Rg = protein.radius_of_gyration()
```

### Secondary Structure
- DSSP algorithm: assign H/E/C (helix/sheet/coil) per residue per frame
- Track secondary structure changes over trajectory

### Contact Maps and Interaction Analysis

```python
# Pairwise Cα distances over trajectory
n_frames = len(u.trajectory)
n_res = len(protein.residues)
contacts = np.zeros((n_res, n_res))

for ts in u.trajectory:
    ca_pos = protein.select_atoms('name CA').positions
    for i in range(n_res):
        for j in range(i+4, n_res):
            d = np.linalg.norm(ca_pos[i] - ca_pos[j])
            if d < 8.0:  # 8 Å contact threshold
                contacts[i,j] += 1

contacts /= n_frames  # fraction of frames in contact
```

### Free Energy Calculations

**MM-PBSA / MM-GBSA**
- Post-processing method: fast binding free energy estimate from MD trajectory
- ΔG_bind = G_complex - G_protein - G_ligand
- Each term: MM energy + solvation (PB or GB) + entropy estimate (normal mode or entropic)
- AMBER: MMPBSA.py; GROMACS: gmx_MMPBSA
- Accuracy: relative binding affinities for similar ligands; kcal/mol; errors ~2-5 kcal/mol

**FEP (Free Energy Perturbation)**
- Mutate one ligand to another via non-physical intermediate states (λ pathway)
- Thermodynamic cycle: compute relative binding free energy
- Requires: overlap between λ states; slow transformation; many simulations
- More accurate than MM-PBSA (~1 kcal/mol errors); more expensive
- Tools: RBFE protocol in Schrödinger FEP+; OpenFE (open-source)

**Umbrella Sampling + WHAM**
- Apply harmonic restraints at different positions along reaction coordinate
- Sample each window; reconstruct PMF (potential of mean force) by WHAM or MBAR
- Applications: ion channel permeation, membrane insertion, enzyme product release
- WHAM (Grossfield): combines overlapping window distributions

## Enhanced Sampling

Standard MD gets trapped in local minima for timescales > 1 µs:

**Replica Exchange MD (REMD)**
- Run multiple copies (replicas) at different temperatures
- Periodically swap configurations between replicas based on Metropolis criterion
- High T replicas overcome barriers; exchange allows low T to sample diverse states
- GROMACS: `gmx mdrun -multidir`; scales with number of replicas × simulation time

**Metadynamics**
- Add repulsive Gaussians along collective variable (CV) during simulation
- Filled free energy wells are discouraged → system forced to explore new minima
- Converges to full free energy surface along CV
- Well-tempered metadynamics: Gaussians decrease over time → better convergence
- PLUMED: plugin for GROMACS, NAMD, AMBER; implements enhanced sampling

**Steered MD (SMD)**
- Apply constant force or constant velocity to pull molecule along a direction
- Jarzynski equality: free energy from work distribution over many pulls
- Applications: membrane insertion energy, protein unfolding, force-extension profiles
- NAMD, OpenMM: implement SMD

## Coarse-Grained MD

Replace groups of atoms with single "super-atoms" (beads):

**MARTINI Force Field**
- ~4 heavy atoms → 1 bead
- 3–4 bead types: polar, nonpolar, charged, apolar
- ~100–1000× faster than atomistic MD
- Well-parameterized for: lipid membranes, proteins, DNA
- Applications: membrane protein insertion, vesicle formation, large protein complexes

**Go-like Models**
- Protein topology: contacts in native state are stabilizing; non-native contacts neutral
- Used for: studying folding pathways, transition states, mechanical properties
- Can model folding of >100 residue proteins on µs timescale
