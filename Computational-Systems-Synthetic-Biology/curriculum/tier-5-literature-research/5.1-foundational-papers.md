# 5.1 Foundational Papers

The canon. Read these in the order listed — each builds on what came before.

---

## How to Read a Paper

**Three-pass method:**

**First pass (5–10 minutes)**: read title, abstract, introduction, section headings, conclusion/discussion figures.
- Goal: understand what question was asked and what was found
- Decide: is this worth a full read?

**Second pass (30–60 minutes)**: read fully except methods. Focus on figures — each figure should tell one part of the story.
- Understand: experimental design, controls, logic of the argument
- Stop at any figure you cannot interpret; understand it before moving on

**Third pass (1–3 hours)**: read methods in detail; verify every claim
- For computational papers: try to reproduce one figure
- Identify: unstated assumptions, potential confounds, alternative interpretations

## Systems Biology Canon

### Dynamics and Network Motifs

**1. Tyson, Chen & Novak (2003) "Sniffers, buzzers, toggles and blinkers: dynamics of regulatory and signaling pathways in the cell" — *Current Opinion in Cell Biology***
- The most pedagogically clear paper on network motif dynamics
- Read this before touching any biological ODE model

**2. Alon (2007) "Network motifs: theory and experimental approaches" — *Nature Reviews Genetics***
- Comprehensive treatment of network motifs from the Alon lab
- Pairs with: Alon's book *An Introduction to Systems Biology* (2006, 2nd ed. 2019)

**3. Milo et al. (2002) "Network Motifs: Simple Building Blocks of Complex Networks" — *Science***
- Original motif analysis paper; defines the statistical framework
- Applied to: E. coli GRN, yeast, neural networks, World Wide Web

**4. Elowitz & Leibler (2000) "A synthetic oscillatory network of transcriptional regulators" — *Nature***
- The repressilator: first synthetic oscillator in a living cell
- Demonstrates: bottom-up approach to understanding oscillation; also first synthetic biology landmark

**5. Gardner, Cantor & Collins (2000) "Construction of a genetic toggle switch in Escherichia coli" — *Nature***
- First bistable genetic switch; demonstrates cell memory and state switching
- Same issue of *Nature* as Elowitz & Leibler — a landmark double paper

**6. Elowitz et al. (2002) "Stochastic Gene Expression in a Single Cell" — *Science***
- Quantifies intrinsic vs. extrinsic noise in gene expression using dual-reporter
- Changes understanding: gene expression is probabilistic, not deterministic

**7. Goldbeter & Koshland (1981) "An amplified sensitivity arising from covalent modification in biological systems" — *PNAS***
- Derives zero-order ultrasensitivity mathematically
- The most cited paper in the bistability/ultrasensitivity literature

**8. Ferrell & Machleder (1998) "The Biochemical Basis of an All-or-None Cell Fate Switch in Xenopus Oocytes" — *Science***
- Experimental demonstration of bistability from positive feedback in MPF activation
- Shows: bistability requires both cooperativity and positive feedback

**9. Bhalla & Iyengar (1999) "Emergent Properties of Networks of Biological Signaling Pathways" — *Science***
- Computational model of signaling shows emergent bistability, oscillations, memory
- Introduces: cascades can create bistability through kinetic amplification

### Metabolic Modeling

**10. Varma & Palsson (1994) "Stoichiometric flux balance models quantitatively predict growth and metabolic by-product secretion in wild-type Escherichia coli W3110" — *Applied and Environmental Microbiology***
- First demonstration of FBA predicting growth accurately
- Establishes the constraint-based modeling paradigm

**11. Ibarra, Edwards & Palsson (2002) "Escherichia coli K-12 undergoes adaptive evolution to achieve in silico predicted optimal growth" — *Nature***
- ALE experiment validates FBA predictions
- Demonstrates: cells evolve toward metabolic optimality

### Whole-Cell Modeling

**12. Karr et al. (2012) "A Whole-Cell Computational Model Predicts Phenotype from Genotype" — *Cell***
- First whole-cell model of a living organism (*Mycoplasma genitalium*)
- Read the supplementary methods; each submodel design is instructive
- A landmark: proves that bottom-up mechanistic models can make cell-level predictions

## Synthetic Biology Canon

### Foundations

**13. Endy (2005) "Foundations for engineering biology" — *Nature***
- The philosophical manifesto of synthetic biology
- Defines: standardization, abstraction, decoupling as engineering principles
- Still relevant; still contested

**14. Keasling (2010) "Manufacturing Molecules Through Metabolic Engineering" — *Science***
- Artemisinin story and the vision for synthetic biology in medicine
- Demonstrates: industrial-scale impact of metabolic engineering

**15. Salis, Mirsky & Voigt (2009) "Automated design of synthetic ribosome binding sites to control protein expression" — *Nature Biotechnology***
- RBS Calculator: quantitative model predicts translation efficiency from sequence
- The paper that made part characterization quantitative

**16. Gibson et al. (2010) "Creation of a Bacterial Cell Controlled by a Chemically Synthesized Genome" — *Science***
- First synthetic bacterial genome
- Gibson Assembly method introduced here (and as a separate methods paper)
- Venter lab: demonstrates full-genome synthesis is feasible

### Circuit Design

**17. Brophy & Voigt (2014) "Principles of genetic circuit design" — *Nature Methods***
- Best review of practical genetic circuit design principles
- Covers: part characterization, composition, retroactivity, burden

**18. Nielsen et al. (2016) "Genetic circuit design automation" — *Science***
- CELLO paper: automated genetic circuit design from truth table to DNA sequence
- Demonstrates: abstraction hierarchy enables automated design

**19. Elowitz & Leibler (2000) [listed above] — foundational**

**20. Danino et al. (2010) "A synchronized quorum of genetic clocks" — *Nature***
- Synchronized oscillators via quorum sensing
- Shows: coupling genetic oscillators through intercellular communication

### Genome Editing and CRISPR

**21. Doudna & Charpentier (2012) "A Programmable Dual-RNA–Guided DNA Endonuclease in Adaptive Bacterial Immunity" — *Science***
- Original Cas9 biochemistry paper: the work that won the 2020 Nobel Prize
- Read this alongside the 2013 mammalian cell editing papers (Cong et al., Mali et al.)

**22. Komor et al. (2016) "Programmable editing of a target base in genomic DNA without double-stranded DNA cleavage" — *Nature***
- First base editor (cytosine → thymine)
- Opens: precise genome editing without DSB

**23. Anzalone et al. (2019) "Search-and-replace genome editing without double-strand breaks or donor DNA" — *Nature***
- Prime editing: the most versatile precision genome editing tool

## Bioinformatics Canon

### Sequence Analysis

**24. Smith & Waterman (1981) "Identification of Common Molecular Subsequences" — *Journal of Molecular Biology***
- Local sequence alignment (Smith-Waterman algorithm)
- Dynamic programming formulation; foundation of all alignment tools

**25. Needleman & Wunsch (1970) "A general method applicable to the search for similarities in the amino acid sequence of two proteins" — *Journal of Molecular Biology***
- Global sequence alignment: the foundational DP approach

**26. Altschul et al. (1990) "Basic local alignment search tool" — *Journal of Molecular Biology***
- BLAST: heuristic sequence search enabling practical database searches
- Most-cited paper in computer science (approaching 100,000 citations)

### Genomics

**27. Venter et al. (2001) "The Sequence of the Human Genome" — *Science***
- Whole-genome shotgun sequencing methodology; the first human genome (draft)
- Read alongside: Lander et al. 2001 (public consortium genome) in *Nature*

**28. Mardis (2008) "Next-Generation DNA Sequencing Methods" — *Annual Review of Genomics and Human Genetics***
- Best review of 2nd-generation sequencing technologies
- Historical context for: what changed after 2007

### Structure and Function

**29. Jumper et al. (2021) "Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold" — *Nature***
- AlphaFold2: arguably the most important paper in biology since 2000
- Read the methods supplement for the architecture; revolutionary

**30. Barabási & Albert (1999) "Emergence of Scaling in Random Networks" — *Science***
- Scale-free network model (Barabási-Albert preferential attachment)
- Motivates: why hub proteins are evolutionarily ancient and essential

## Statistics and Methods

**31. Benjamini & Hochberg (1995) "Controlling the False Discovery Rate: A Practical and Powerful Approach to Multiple Testing" — *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society***
- The most important statistical paper for genomics
- FDR correction: used in every differential expression analysis

**32. Love, Huber & Anders (2014) "Moderated estimation of fold change and dispersion for RNA-seq data with DESeq2" — *Genome Biology***
- DESeq2 paper: how to correctly do differential expression analysis
- Read the vignette on Bioconductor alongside the paper

**33. van der Maaten & Hinton (2008) "Visualizing Data using t-SNE" — *JMLR***
- t-SNE paper: understand what t-SNE does and doesn't preserve
- Read alongside: McInnes et al. 2018 (UMAP)
