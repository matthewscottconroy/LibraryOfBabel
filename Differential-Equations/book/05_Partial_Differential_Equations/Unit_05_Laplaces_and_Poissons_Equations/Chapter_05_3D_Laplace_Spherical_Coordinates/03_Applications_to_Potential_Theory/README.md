# Applications to Potential Theory

The theory of spherical harmonics and Legendre polynomials reaches its culmination in classical potential theory — the study of gravitational and electrostatic potentials generated by extended bodies. The key result is that the potential outside any bounded charge or mass distribution admits an expansion in inverse powers of distance $r$, with angular dependence captured by spherical harmonics. This **multipole expansion** organizes the potential by terms that decay at rates $r^{-1}$ (monopole), $r^{-2}$ (dipole), $r^{-3}$ (quadrupole), and so on. Inside a source-free region, the same harmonic structure enables the solution of the Dirichlet problem on a ball and its exterior via spherical harmonic series. This section develops these applications systematically, connecting the abstract spectral theory of the Laplace-Beltrami operator to concrete physical problems.

## The Dirichlet Problem on a Ball: Revisited

Let $B_R = \{|\mathbf{x}| < R\}$ and suppose $g \in L^2(S^2)$ is prescribed boundary data. Expand $g$ in spherical harmonics:

$$g(\theta,\phi) = \sum_{\ell=0}^\infty \sum_{m=-\ell}^\ell g_{\ell m} Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi), \qquad g_{\ell m} = \int_{S^2} g(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \overline{Y_\ell^m(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}\, dS.$$

Seeking $u$ harmonic in $B_R$ with $u = g$ on $\partial B_R$, write $u = \sum_{\ell,m} A_{\ell m}(r) Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi)$. The radial equation (Euler equation) has solutions $r^\ell$ and $r^{-\ell-1}$; regularity at the origin forces $A_{\ell m}(r) = c_{\ell m} r^\ell$. Matching boundary data at $r = R$ gives $c_{\ell m} R^\ell = g_{\ell m}$, so:

$$u(r,\theta,\phi) = \sum_{\ell=0}^\infty \sum_{m=-\ell}^\ell g_{\ell m} \left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^\ell Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi), \qquad r < R.$$

**Exterior Dirichlet problem.** For $|\mathbf{x}| > R$ with $u \to 0$ as $|\mathbf{x}| \to \infty$, regularity at infinity forces the $r^{-\ell-1}$ solution:

$$u(r,\theta,\phi) = \sum_{\ell=0}^\infty \sum_{m=-\ell}^\ell g_{\ell m} \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\ell+1} Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi), \qquad r > R.$$

Both series converge in $L^2$ for $g \in L^2(S^2)$, and in $C^\infty$ for any $r$ bounded away from $R$ (interior convergence follows from $|g_{\ell m}| \leq \|g\|_{L^2}$ and the geometric decay $(r/R)^\ell$ for fixed $r < R$).

**Poisson formula recovered.** The interior series can be summed in closed form using the addition theorem $\sum_{m=-\ell}^\ell Y_\ell^m(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\overline{Y_\ell^m(\hat{\mathbf{y}})} = \frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_\ell(\cos\gamma)$ (where $\gamma$ is the angle between $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$):

$$u(\mathbf{x}) = \int_{S^2} g(\hat{\mathbf{y}}) \sum_{\ell=0}^\infty \frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^\ell P_\ell(\cos\gamma)\, dS(\hat{\mathbf{y}}).$$

The sum $\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty (2\ell+1)s^\ell P_\ell(t) = (1-s^2)(1-2st+s^2)^{-3/2}$ (differentiation of the generating function) gives the Poisson kernel $K(r,\gamma) = (R^2-r^2)/(4\pi R(R^2-2Rr\cos\gamma+r^2)^{3/2})$ in 3D, recovering the Poisson formula $u(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{R^2-r^2}{4\pi R}\int_{S^2}\frac{g(\hat{\mathbf{y}})}{|\mathbf{x}-R\hat{\mathbf{y}}|^3}\, dS(\hat{\mathbf{y}})$ obtained via Green's functions.

## Newtonian Gravity and the Gravitational Potential

The gravitational potential $\Phi$ of a mass distribution $\rho(\mathbf{y})$ satisfies $\Delta\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$ (Poisson's equation). For a body occupying a bounded region $\Omega$ with $|\mathbf{x}| > R$ for all $\mathbf{x} \notin \Omega$:

$$\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = -G\int_\Omega \frac{\rho(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|}\, d\mathbf{y}.$$

Using the Legendre expansion of the Coulomb kernel with $r_< = |\mathbf{y}| < R < |\mathbf{x}| = r_>$:

$$\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|} = \sum_{\ell=0}^\infty \frac{|\mathbf{y}|^\ell}{|\mathbf{x}|^{\ell+1}} P_\ell(\cos\gamma),$$

and applying the addition theorem to replace $P_\ell(\cos\gamma)$:

$$\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = -G\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty \sum_{m=-\ell}^\ell \frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1} \frac{I_{\ell m}}{|\mathbf{x}|^{\ell+1}} Y_\ell^m(\hat{\mathbf{x}}),$$

where the **mass multipole moments** are:

$$I_{\ell m} = \int_\Omega |\mathbf{y}|^\ell \rho(\mathbf{y}) \overline{Y_\ell^m(\hat{\mathbf{y}})}\, d\mathbf{y}.$$

**Monopole ($\ell = 0$).** $Y_0^0 = 1/\sqrt{4\pi}$, so $I_{00} = M/\sqrt{4\pi}$ where $M = \int\rho\, d\mathbf{y}$ is the total mass. The $\ell=0$ term gives $\Phi \approx -GM/|\mathbf{x}|$ — the potential of a point mass. This is the leading behavior at large distances: all mass distributions with the same total mass look like a point mass at infinity.

**Dipole ($\ell = 1$).** The three components $I_{1m}$ are proportional to the components of the center of mass $\mathbf{x}_{\text{cm}} = M^{-1}\int\mathbf{y}\rho\, d\mathbf{y}$. If coordinates are chosen with origin at the center of mass, the dipole term vanishes.

**Quadrupole ($\ell = 2$).** The five moments $I_{2m}$ encode the inertia tensor. For an axially symmetric body with symmetry axis $z$, only $I_{20}$ is nonzero, and it captures oblateness: the Earth's gravitational potential has a dominant quadrupole correction due to the equatorial bulge, giving the $J_2$ coefficient in geodesy.

**Earth's geoid.** The exterior gravitational potential of the Earth is expressed as:

$$\Phi = -\frac{GM}{r}\left[1 - \sum_{\ell=2}^\infty \sum_{m=-\ell}^\ell J_{\ell m}\left(\frac{R_E}{r}\right)^\ell Y_\ell^m(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\right],$$

where modern models (EGM2020) include terms up to $\ell = 2190$. The spherical harmonic decomposition of Earth's gravity field has applications in satellite orbit prediction, oceanography, and solid Earth geophysics.

## Electrostatics and the Multipole Expansion

The electrostatic potential $u$ of a charge distribution $\rho$ satisfies $\Delta u = -\rho/\varepsilon_0$. For charges confined to a ball of radius $R$, the potential outside ($|\mathbf{x}| > R$) is:

$$u(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \sum_{\ell=0}^\infty \sum_{m=-\ell}^\ell \frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1} \frac{q_{\ell m}}{|\mathbf{x}|^{\ell+1}} Y_\ell^m(\hat{\mathbf{x}}),$$

with **electric multipole moments** $q_{\ell m} = \int |\mathbf{y}|^\ell \rho(\mathbf{y})\overline{Y_\ell^m(\hat{\mathbf{y}})}\, d\mathbf{y}$.

**Worked example: two point charges.** Place charge $+q$ at $(0,0,d/2)$ and $-q$ at $(0,0,-d/2)$ with $d \ll |\mathbf{x}|$. This is an electric dipole. By symmetry and $m=0$, the relevant moments are $q_{\ell 0} = q\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^\ell + (-q)\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^\ell(-1)^\ell = q(d/2)^\ell(1-(-1)^\ell)$. This is nonzero only for odd $\ell$: $q_{10} = qd/2 \cdot Y_1^0$-coefficient is $qd\sqrt{3}/(\sqrt{4\pi}\cdot 2)$... More directly, $q_{10} = q\cdot(d/2) + (-q)\cdot(-d/2) = qd$ (since $P_1(\cos\theta) = \cos\theta$ evaluated along the $z$-axis). The potential is:

$$u \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_{10}}{|\mathbf{x}|^2}Y_1^0(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \cdot \frac{4\pi}{3} = \frac{qd\cos\theta}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2},$$

the classical dipole potential $u = \mathbf{p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{x}}/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2)$ with $\mathbf{p} = qd\hat{\mathbf{z}}$.

## Interior Problem: Potential Inside a Charged Shell

Consider a spherical shell of radius $R$ carrying surface charge density $\sigma(\theta,\phi)$. The potential inside the shell satisfies $\Delta u = 0$ with boundary data $u|_{r=R} = V(\theta,\phi)$ determined by the shell charge via appropriate electrostatic jump conditions. Writing $V = \sum_{\ell m}V_{\ell m}Y_\ell^m$, the interior potential is:

$$u(r,\theta,\phi) = \sum_{\ell=0}^\infty\sum_{m=-\ell}^\ell V_{\ell m}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^\ell Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi).$$

For a shell at uniform potential $V_0$ (a grounded conducting sphere connected to a battery), only the $\ell=0$ term survives: $u = V_0$ throughout the interior. This confirms the electrostatic shielding theorem: the potential inside a conducting shell depends only on the shell's total charge, regardless of external fields.

## Connection Between Multipole Expansion and Green's Function

The Green's function for the ball (derived via the Kelvin transform) can also be derived using the multipole expansion. The identity $1/|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}| = \sum_\ell (r_<^\ell/r_>^{\ell+1}) P_\ell(\cos\gamma)$ is precisely the expansion of the free-space Green's function $\Phi(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}) = 1/(4\pi|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|)$ in spherical harmonics. The correction term (the regular harmonic function subtracted to enforce zero boundary conditions on $\partial B_R$) has the form $\sum_\ell a_\ell r^\ell P_\ell(\cos\gamma)$, and matching on $|\mathbf{x}|=R$ using the addition theorem yields the Kelvin-transform expression for the Green's function. The two constructions — method of images and multipole expansion — thus give the same result from different starting points.

## Energy and the Multipole Expansion

The electrostatic energy stored in a charge distribution is $W = \frac{1}{2}\int\rho(\mathbf{x})u(\mathbf{x})\, d\mathbf{x}$. For a distribution expanded in multipole moments, the interaction energy between two well-separated distributions depends only on their multipole moments: at large separation $d$, the leading interaction is the monopole-monopole term (Coulomb energy $\sim q_1 q_2/d$), followed by monopole-dipole ($\sim 1/d^2$), dipole-dipole ($\sim 1/d^3$), etc. This multipole expansion of interaction energies is the foundation of molecular force fields in computational chemistry.

The orthogonality of spherical harmonics means that multipole moments of different $(\ell,m)$ contribute independently to the energy: there are no cross terms between $\ell \neq \ell'$ in the self-energy of a charge distribution, reflecting the fact that spherical harmonics of different degrees decouple under the Laplacian.
