# Chapter 1 — Notes

---

For the core metric space and analysis material, Rudin's *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* (Chapters 2, 3, 4, 7) is still the standard, and still good. It's terse and demanding, but it's terse in the way that forces you to fill in gaps rather than glossing over them. If you find Rudin too compressed, Pugh's *Real Mathematical Analysis* covers the same ground with more commentary and better exercises. Don't try to read both simultaneously — pick one and commit.

The functional analysis in Section 1.6 — normed spaces, Banach spaces, Hilbert spaces — is covered cleanly in Rudin's *Functional Analysis*. For a more pedagogical treatment, Conway's *A Course in Functional Analysis* is warmer and more detailed. If you want to see functional analysis and operator theory developed simultaneously (which is relevant for the Koopman operator material later), Reed and Simon's *Methods of Mathematical Physics, Vol. 1* is the right place to look.

For the Baire Category Theorem and its applications, Oxtoby's *Measure and Category* is a beautiful short book — perhaps 100 pages — that develops the precise analogy between measure-zero sets and meager sets. The core thesis: meager and null are both notions of "small," they often agree but sometimes disagree, and the interplay is where the interesting mathematics lives. It's the right supplementary read after you've worked through the basics here.

The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem (Section 1.3) is more important than its placement in the chapter might suggest. It reappears in the proof of existence of invariant measures (Chapter 6), in the construction of stable manifolds (Chapter 9), and in the theory of attractors for infinite-dimensional systems (Chapter 15). If you feel uncertain about it, work Exercise 1.8 carefully — it's worth your time.

One thing the chapter doesn't say explicitly: the metric space framework is essentially the entire setting for topological dynamics (Chapters 6-9), while the Hilbert and Banach space machinery is the setting for ergodic theory and spectral analysis (Chapters 7, 11-13). The two streams run parallel for a while before converging. Keep that division in mind as you proceed.
