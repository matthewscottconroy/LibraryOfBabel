# Chapter 3 — Topology and Smooth Manifolds

> *A dynamical system is a map on a space. The topology of the space determines which behaviors are possible. The differential structure determines how fast they happen.*

---

Topology is the study of spaces up to continuous deformation — which properties survive stretching and bending, even if not breaking. For dynamical systems, topology is both the setting and a source of constraints: a map on a torus behaves differently from a map on a sphere, not because of any particular formula, but because the spaces have different topological types.

This chapter builds on the metric space framework of Chapter 1. We'll see that metric spaces are a special case of topological spaces, but the topological perspective reveals more about the global structure of spaces. We then develop smooth manifolds — the arenas for continuous-time dynamics — and the key fixed-point theorems that constrain what maps can do.

**Prerequisites:** Chapter 1 (metric spaces, continuity).

**What this chapter builds:**

The abstract topological framework for phase spaces; homotopy and the fundamental group for classifying spaces up to continuous deformation; smooth manifolds as the arenas for flows; differential forms and Stokes' theorem as the language of integration on manifolds; and the fixed-point theorems (Brouwer, Poincaré-Hopf, Lefschetz) that constrain dynamical behavior.
