# Chapter 5 — Advanced Linear Algebra and Spectral Theory

> *Eigenvalues determine stability. The spectrum of a linear operator is its dynamical fingerprint. Learning to read that fingerprint is the prerequisite for everything that follows.*

---

Linear algebra is where dynamical systems theory and functional analysis converge most visibly. The spectrum of a linear operator — the set of its eigenvalues and, in infinite dimensions, the subtler parts of its spectrum — governs exponential growth rates, mixing, recurrence, and entropy. Before we can understand any of these, we need to understand the spectrum.

This chapter deepens the linear algebra you already know. We assume undergraduate linear algebra (matrices, eigenvalues, diagonalization) and the Banach and Hilbert space theory from Chapter 1.

**What this chapter builds:**

Jordan canonical form and matrix functions are needed for solving linear ODE systems (Chapter 4) and for understanding polynomial growth in the stability analysis. The spectral theorem for normal operators is the foundation for Koopman operator analysis — the central tool for connecting ergodic theory to spectral theory. The SVD and low-rank approximations are needed for data-driven dynamics (DMD). Bounded operators on Hilbert spaces — their spectrum, adjoints, and compact approximations — are needed for transfer operators and quantum information theory. And the Perron-Frobenius theorem governs the behavior of positive matrices, with direct applications to symbolic dynamics and entropy.
