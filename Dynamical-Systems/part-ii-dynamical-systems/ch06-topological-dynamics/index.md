# Chapter 6 — Topological Dynamics

> *Topological dynamics is the study of continuous maps on topological spaces. With no metric and no measure, it isolates the purely qualitative: which orbits are dense, which sets are attracting, which systems are equivalent.*

---

## What This Chapter Is About

Here is a question you can ask about any dynamical system, before you even write down a differential equation: where does the orbit of a point end up, eventually? Not "what is the exact trajectory," but qualitatively — does it wander? Does it keep returning? Does it fill up the space?

Topological dynamics is the branch of the subject devoted to exactly these questions. It strips away the metric structure and the measure theory and asks what remains. What you find is surprisingly rich: a whole world of concepts — recurrence, transitivity, minimality, chaos — that can be formulated and studied using only the topology of the phase space and the continuity of the map.

This chapter lays the qualitative groundwork for everything that follows. The key objects are orbits and their limit sets. The key questions are about density, recurrence, and equivalence. The key theorems — Poincaré recurrence, Baire category and transitivity, the Krylov-Bogoliubov existence theorem — are the ones every dynamicist uses every day, sometimes without noticing.

One payoff arrives at the end: the Krylov-Bogoliubov theorem guarantees that every continuous map on a compact space has at least one invariant measure. This is the bridge into the measure-theoretic world of Chapter 7. You have to build the bridge before you can cross it.

**Prerequisites:** Chapters 1 (metric spaces, Baire theorem) and 3 (topology, compact spaces).

---

## What This Chapter Builds

- The framework of **orbits, limit sets, and recurrence** — the vocabulary for describing long-term behavior.
- **Topological transitivity** and **minimality**: notions that capture how thoroughly a map mixes the space.
- **Equicontinuity and distality**: the "tame" end of the dynamical spectrum, as far from chaos as you can get.
- **Topological conjugacy**: the correct notion of isomorphism for dynamical systems.
- **Sensitive dependence and chaos**: Devaney's definition and the Li-Yorke theorem.
- **Invariant measures**: the Krylov-Bogoliubov theorem (existence) and the Weyl-Oxtoby theorem (unique ergodicity).
- **The Ellis semigroup**: an algebraic structure that encodes all asymptotic dynamics.
