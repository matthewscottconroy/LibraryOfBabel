# Chapter Notes — Chapter 9

A guide to the literature of hyperbolic dynamics — a rich but sometimes overwhelming subject.

---

**The foundational paper.** Smale's *Differentiable Dynamical Systems* (*Bulletin of the AMS*, 1967) is required reading. It introduces Axiom A, structural stability, the spectral decomposition theorem, the horseshoe, and homoclinic bifurcations — essentially, it creates the subject. It is beautifully written and surprisingly readable for a research paper from 60 years ago. Read it early.

**The comprehensive reference.** Katok and Hasselblatt's *Introduction to the Modern Theory of Dynamical Systems* (Cambridge, 1995) is the definitive modern reference for hyperbolic dynamics and smooth ergodic theory. It is comprehensive and rigorous. Chapters 6, 7, and 18-20 cover the material in this chapter. The book is not easy, but it's the standard.

**Bowen's work.** Rufus Bowen's *Equilibrium States and the Ergodic Theory of Anosov Diffeomorphisms* (Springer Lecture Notes, 1975) is short — about 100 pages — and essential. It develops the thermodynamic formalism, Markov partitions, and the theory of SRB measures in a remarkably clean way. Bowen died in 1978 at age 31, but his contributions to the subject were extraordinary.

**SRB measures.** The original papers are Sinai (1972, in Russian), Ruelle (1976, *American Journal of Mathematics*), and Bowen-Ruelle (1975, *Inventiones*). For a modern treatment with connections to nonequilibrium statistical mechanics, see the survey by Young (*JAMS*, 2002) — "What Are SRB Measures and Which Systems Have Them?" — which is excellent.

**The shadowing lemma.** Bowen's *Symbolic Dynamics for Hyperbolic Flows* (1975) and Pilyugin's *Shadowing in Dynamical Systems* (Springer, 1999) both treat the shadowing lemma in detail. The numerical application — validating computer simulations via shadowing — is developed in Hammel-Grebogi-Yorke (*Science*, 1987), a paper that made the lemma famous outside of pure mathematics.

**Guckenheimer-Holmes.** *Nonlinear Oscillations, Dynamical Systems, and Bifurcations of Vector Fields* (Springer, 1983, revised 1990) is a classic that complements the more abstract approach of Katok-Hasselblatt. Chapters 3 and 5 cover the horseshoe, homoclinic orbits, and the Smale-Birkhoff theorem with excellent pictures and concrete examples. The book is more accessible than Katok-Hasselblatt and a good place to build intuition.

**Partial hyperbolicity.** This is an active research area. The survey by Pesin (*Ergodic Theory and Dynamical Systems*, 2004) and the book by Brin and Stuck *Introduction to Dynamical Systems* (Cambridge, 2002) are good starting points. For the resolution of the Pugh-Shub conjecture in the center-bunched case, see Burns-Wilkinson (*Annals of Mathematics*, 2010).

**Open problems.** The question of whether every Anosov diffeomorphism is topologically conjugate to an infranilmanifold automorphism is one of the major open problems in dynamics. A positive answer would complete the classification program begun by Smale. Recent work of Rodriguez Hertz-Rodriguez Hertz-Ures has made significant progress on the partial hyperbolicity side.
