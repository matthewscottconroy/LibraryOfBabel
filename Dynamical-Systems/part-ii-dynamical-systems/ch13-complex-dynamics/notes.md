# Chapter Notes — Chapter 13

## The Essential Reference

Read Milnor's *Dynamics in One Complex Variable* (Princeton University Press, 3rd ed.). It is beautifully written, at exactly the right level, and available free on Milnor's website. If you read only one thing from this chapter's bibliography, make it this. Milnor explains the subject the way it should be explained: with the geometry first and the formalism in service of the geometry.

Carleson and Gamelin's *Complex Dynamics* (Springer) is the other standard text and is more concise and efficient. Beardon's *Iteration of Rational Functions* (Springer) covers classical results thoroughly.

## On the Mandelbrot Set

The original rigorous development of the theory is in Douady and Hubbard's *Orsay Notes* (written around 1982-85) — scanned copies are available online. Reading them is a historical pleasure; the style is very French, very geometric, and very direct. Their papers on polynomial-like maps and the Straightening Theorem are the foundational documents.

For the MLC conjecture: Lyubich's survey articles (available on arXiv) are the best guide to what is known and what the main approaches are. This is one of the genuinely great open problems, and the partial results (Yoccoz, Lyubich, Graczyk-Swiątek, Kahn-Lyubich) are beautiful theorems in their own right.

Shishikura's theorem that $\dim_H(\partial\mathcal{M}) = 2$ (1998, *Annals of Mathematics*) is stunning. The proof uses quasiconformal surgery to construct Julia sets with dimension close to 2 and then show they approximate the Mandelbrot boundary. It requires the full quasiconformal toolkit.

## Sullivan's Proof

Sullivan's No Wandering Domains theorem (1985) is in *Annals of Mathematics*. The paper is 10 pages and is not easy to read, but Section 2 (the main argument) is worth studying carefully. The key insight — using quasiconformal deformations and Teichmüller theory to derive a contradiction from a wandering domain — was entirely novel and opened a new era in complex dynamics.

The contrast with transcendental functions is important: Baker proved in 1976 that transcendental entire functions *can* have wandering domains. The key difference is that a degree-$d$ rational map has only $2d-2$ critical points, so its Teichmüller space is finite-dimensional. A transcendental entire function has infinitely many critical values in general, removing the finite-dimensionality constraint.

## Quasiconformal Maps

The standard reference for quasiconformal maps is Ahlfors' *Lectures on Quasiconformal Mappings* — short (about 160 pages) and definitive. For the Measurable Riemann Mapping Theorem in the context of complex dynamics, the treatment in Branner-Fagella's *Quasiconformal Surgery in Holomorphic Dynamics* (Cambridge) is comprehensive and modern.

## Research Entry Points

The main active areas accessible from this chapter: (1) the MLC problem and local connectivity of Julia sets; (2) the statistical properties of non-hyperbolic parameters (Collet-Eckmann maps, the Jakobson measure); (3) transcendental dynamics (entire and meromorphic functions) where wandering domains exist and the theory is much less complete; (4) higher-dimensional complex dynamics (maps on $\mathbb{C}^2$ and more), where even the basic Fatou-Julia decomposition is more complicated. For (3), the survey by Bergweiler (*Iteration of Meromorphic Functions*, 1993) is the starting point.
