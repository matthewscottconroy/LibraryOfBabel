# Notes — Chapter 17

Start with Rényi's original paper: *On Measures of Entropy and Information* (Proceedings of the 4th Berkeley Symposium, 1961). It is concise, beautifully written, and introduces both the family $H_\alpha$ and the axiomatic characterization. Rényi was a remarkable mathematician — also famous for his quip that "a mathematician is a machine for turning coffee into theorems" — and the paper reflects his clarity.

For min-entropy and smooth min-entropy, the foundational reference is Robert Renner's PhD thesis, *Security of Quantum Key Distribution* (ETH Zürich, 2005). This thesis essentially created one-shot information theory as a systematic subject. Renner showed that smooth min-entropy (and its dual, smooth max-entropy) gives the correct operational characterizations for randomness extraction and data compression in the single-shot regime. If you ever need to prove a security bound for a cryptographic protocol, this is the framework.

For von Neumann entropy and strong subadditivity: the original Lieb-Ruskai proof appears in *Proof of the Strong Subadditivity of Quantum-Mechanical Entropy* (Journal of Mathematical Physics, 1973). It uses Lieb's concavity theorem, which is proved in a companion paper. The proof is technically demanding. Dénes Petz later gave a cleaner proof via the Peierls-Bogoliubov inequality and the Lieb concavity theorem; this appears in his book *Quantum Information Theory and Quantum Statistics* (Springer, 2008). Ruskai's 2002 survey *Inequalities for Quantum Entropy: A Review with Conditions for Equality* (Journal of Mathematical Physics) is also highly recommended.

The maximum entropy principle is from Jaynes' two 1957 papers in Physical Review: *Information Theory and Statistical Mechanics* (Parts I and II). Jaynes' interpretation of statistical mechanics as inference is compelling but not universally accepted. The Jaynes-Cummings debate with more traditional statistical physicists is worth reading for context.

For Tsallis entropy: Tsallis' original paper is *Possible Generalization of Boltzmann-Gibbs Statistics* (Journal of Statistical Physics, 1988). The book *Nonextensive Statistical Mechanics* by Tsallis (Springer, 2009) collects the theoretical developments. Apply this framework cautiously: there is ongoing debate about its foundations and the physical meaning of the $q$ parameter.

The connection between Rényi entropy and fractal dimensions (Exercise 17.5) is developed in Falconer's *Fractal Geometry* and Pesin's *Dimension Theory in Dynamical Systems*. The Rényi dimension $D_q$ of a measure is defined as $D_q = \frac{1}{q-1}\lim_{r \to 0}\frac{\log \sum_i \mu(B_r(x_i))^q}{\log r}$, and for the Bernoulli measures on the doubling map it computes to a family of dimensions that characterize the multifractal structure of the measure. This is the bridge between information theory and the fractal geometry in Part II of this book.
