# Notes — Chapter 21

The textbook literature for quantum information theory is excellent. Start with Nielsen and Chuang's *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information* (Cambridge, 2000) — the standard reference, encyclopedic and well-written. Chapters 9-12 cover quantum information theory specifically (source coding, channel capacity, entanglement, error correction). The notation used in this chapter follows Nielsen-Chuang.

For a more information-theoretically focused treatment: Wilde's *Quantum Information Theory* (Cambridge, second edition 2017) is comprehensive and rigorous, with careful proofs of all the major results including the HSW theorem, the LSD theorem, and strong subadditivity. Wilde also has a free version of the first edition on arXiv. For the most mathematically rigorous treatment of quantum information, Watrous' *The Theory of Quantum Information* is available free online and is impeccably precise.

Strong subadditivity (Section 21.6) is the cornerstone of the whole theory. The original proof by Lieb and Ruskai (*Proof of the Strong Subadditivity of Quantum-Mechanical Entropy*, Journal of Mathematical Physics, 1973) is a landmark. Petz's 1988 proof using the Peierls-Bogoliubov inequality is more accessible; it appears in his book. Ruskai's 2002 survey (*Inequalities for Quantum Entropy: A Review with Conditions for Equality*) discusses the equality conditions that characterize quantum Markov chains.

The Hastings counterexample to additivity of minimum output entropy (*Superadditivity of Communication Capacity Using Entangled Inputs*, Nature Physics, 2009) settled a 20-year-old conjecture negatively. The proof is probabilistic — it shows that a random quantum channel has the desired property — and is a tour de force of random matrix theory applied to quantum information.

For quantum error correction: Gottesman's PhD thesis *Stabilizer Codes and Quantum Error Correction* (Caltech, 1997) is the foundational reference for stabilizer codes and remains readable. The surface code, the leading practical candidate, is covered comprehensively in Fowler, Martinis, et al.'s *Surface Codes: Towards Practical Large-Scale Quantum Computation* (Physical Review A, 2012). The recent experimental realization of surface codes below threshold at Google and IBM has made the fault-tolerance threshold theorem more than an abstraction.

For entanglement theory: the Bennett et al. papers from the late 1990s (on entanglement distillation, dilution, and the uniqueness of entanglement entropy for pure states) are classics. The irreversibility of mixed-state entanglement is covered in Vidal and Cirac's work. A good survey is Plenio and Virmani's *An Introduction to Entanglement Measures* (Quantum Information and Computation, 2007).

For those coming from dynamical systems: the connection between quantum channels and dynamical systems runs through the Lindblad equation (quantum master equation), which is the quantum analogue of the Fokker-Planck equation. The long-time behavior of the Lindblad equation — convergence to a stationary state, quantum detailed balance — is an active area connecting quantum information to functional analysis and quantum probability.
