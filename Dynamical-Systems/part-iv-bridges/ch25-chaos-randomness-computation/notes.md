# Chapter Notes — Chapter 25

## On chaos as information production

The connection between Lyapunov exponents and information production is developed through Pesin's formula (Chapter 22) and the ergodic AEP (Chapter 23). A readable treatment with explicit examples is in:

- **Eckmann and Ruelle**, "Ergodic theory of chaos and strange attractors," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 57 (1985) — a classic survey that established the modern view

For the physical interpretation of Lyapunov exponents as information production rates, Ruelle's *Chance and Chaos* (Princeton, 1991) is accessible and precise.

## On algorithmic randomness of chaotic orbits

Fouché's results on ML-randomness and the doubling map are in:

- **Fouché**, "Dynamics of a generic Brownian motion: recursive aspects," *Theoretical Computer Science* 394 (2008)

The effective Birkhoff theorem (Theorem 25.3.2) is developed in:

- **Bienvenu, Gács, Hoyrup, Rojas, Shen**, "Algorithmic tests and randomness with respect to a class of measures," *Proceedings of the Steklov Institute of Mathematics* 274 (2011)
- **Franklin and Towsner**, "Randomness and non-ergodic systems," *Moscow Mathematical Journal* 14 (2014)

For a systematic treatment of computable ergodic theory, see Hoyrup and Rojas' work on the effective ergodic theorem.

## On undecidability in dynamical systems

Berger's undecidability of the Wang tiling problem is in:

- **Berger**, "The undecidability of the domino problem," *Memoirs of the AMS* 66 (1966)

Robinson's simpler aperiodic tile set (1971) and Penrose's famous aperiodic tilings (1974) followed. The connection to 2D SFTs is developed in:

- **Kari**, "Theory of cellular automata: A survey," *Theoretical Computer Science* 334 (2005)

For the undecidability of entropy for cellular automata and the $\Pi_1^0$-completeness of emptiness for 2D SFTs:

- **Hochman and Meyerovitch**, "A characterization of the entropies of multidimensional shifts of finite type," *Annals of Mathematics* 171 (2010) — proves that the entropies of 2D SFTs are exactly the $\Pi_1^0$ real numbers (right-recursively enumerable numbers)

This result characterizes exactly which real numbers are achievable as entropies of 2D SFTs — a beautiful confluence of logic, dynamical systems, and computability.

## On computable analysis and Julia sets

The computability of the Mandelbrot set and non-computability of Julia sets on the boundary are in:

- **Braverman and Yampolsky**, "Non-computable Julia sets," *Journal of the AMS* 19 (2006)
- **Braverman and Yampolsky**, *Computability of Julia sets* (Springer, 2009)

Braverman's PhD thesis is available and highly readable. Weihrauch's *Computable Analysis* (Springer, 2000) provides the general framework (Type-2 computability theory).

## On computational complexity of dynamical properties

The $\#P$-hardness of entropy for 2D piecewise affine maps and the polynomial-time computability of 1D entropy are surveyed in:

- **Koiran and Moore**, "Closed-form analytic maps in one and two dimensions can simulate universal Turing machines," *Theoretical Computer Science* 210 (1999)

For the relationship between dynamical complexity and circuit complexity, see the work of Blondel and Tsitsiklis on the decidability of dynamical systems with algebraic structure.
