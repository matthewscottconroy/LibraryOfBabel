# Chapter Notes — Chapter 28

## On Learning Category Theory

If you're new to categories, the right entry point is Lawvere and Schanuel's *Conceptual Mathematics* (Cambridge, 2009) — it was written for exactly this kind of audience, people with mathematical maturity who want to understand categories without wading through abstract algebraic topology. It covers products, limits, and functors in a way that makes the ideas vivid.

For the more sophisticated material in this chapter — topoi, enriched categories — Goldblatt's *Topoi: The Categorical Analysis of Logic* (Dover) is surprisingly readable, and Mac Lane and Moerdijk's *Sheaves in Geometry and Logic* is the definitive reference.

## On the Koopman Functor and Ergodic Theory

The Koopman-von Neumann spectral theory is developed in Halmos's *Lectures on Ergodic Theory* — still one of the best books in the field. The categorical formulation (Koopman as a contravariant functor to Hilbert spaces) is implicit in most of the classical literature; making it explicit is the kind of reformulation that category theory enables.

The Halmos-von Neumann theorem (complete classification of ergodic rotations by spectrum) is in Halmos-von Neumann's *Operator methods in classical mechanics II* (Annals of Math., 1942). It remains one of the most beautiful classification theorems in ergodic theory — and one of the few.

## On Groupoids and C*-Algebras

Renault's *A Groupoid Approach to C*-Algebras* (Springer, 1980) is the foundational text for the groupoid perspective. It's technical but essential if you want to understand why the orbit groupoid determines the crossed product.

The Giordano-Putnam-Skau theorem is in two papers in *J. Reine Angew. Math.*: the 1995 paper *Topological orbit equivalence and C*-crossed products* and the 1999 extension. Together, they give a complete classification of minimal $\mathbb{Z}$-systems up to orbit equivalence. The K-theory invariants used in the proof are the ordered K-groups (K-groups with an additional order structure), and the order is essential — not just $K_0$ but the positive cone.

## On Categorical Entropy

Leinster's characterization of entropy is in *A Characterization of Entropy in Terms of Information Loss* (Entropy, 2011). It's short and beautifully written. Reading it alongside the original Faddeev (1956) axiomatization will give you a clear sense of what the categorical perspective adds.

## Looking Ahead

The connection between C*-algebras and dynamical systems runs deeper than this chapter suggests. Elliott's classification program for C*-algebras — classifying separable nuclear C*-algebras by K-theoretic data — is in many ways a continuation of the Giordano-Putnam-Skau approach. The recent proof of the Toms-Winter conjecture (by Castillejos, Evington, Tikuisis, White, and Winter, 2021) shows that a large class of C*-algebras are classified by their K-theory and tracial data. Dynamics is one of the primary sources of examples — and one of the primary motivations.

For operator algebras applied to quantum field theory (a different direction), Davidson's *C*-Algebras by Example* (Chapter VIII) covers crossed products with many examples, and Williams's *A Tour Through Mathematical Physics* connects these to physics.
