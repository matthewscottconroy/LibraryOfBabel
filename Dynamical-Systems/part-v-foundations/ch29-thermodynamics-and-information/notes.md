# Chapter Notes — Chapter 29

## On the Foundations

Jaynes's *Information Theory and Statistical Mechanics* (1957, two papers in Physical Review) established the information-theoretic foundations of statistical mechanics, arguing that statistical mechanics is not a theory about matter but a theory about inference — the maximum entropy principle is Bayesian probability applied to physical systems. These papers are still worth reading. The second one, in particular, derives all of equilibrium statistical mechanics from the principle of maximum entropy.

Landauer's original paper, *Irreversibility and Heat Generation in the Computing Process* (IBM Journal of Research and Development, 1961), is remarkably prescient. He was trying to understand the physical cost of computation in a time before this seemed like an urgent practical question. The argument is short and clear; the paper is more accessible than you'd expect from its age.

The experimental verification of Landauer's principle is in Bérut, Arakelyan, Petrosyan, Ciliberto, Dillenschneider, and Lutz (Nature, 2012): *Experimental verification of Landauer's principle linking information and thermodynamics*. They used a colloidal particle in a double-well optical trap as a single bit of memory and measured the heat dissipated during erasure.

## On Fluctuation Theorems

The Jarzynski equality is in Jarzynski's 1997 Physical Review Letters paper *Nonequilibrium equality for free energy differences*. The Crooks fluctuation theorem is in Crooks (1999), *Entropy production fluctuation theorem and the nonequilibrium work relation for free energy differences* (Physical Review E).

A clear pedagogical treatment of both is in Seifert's review *Stochastic Thermodynamics, Fluctuation Theorems, and Molecular Machines* (Reports on Progress in Physics, 2012). The information-theoretic interpretation — that KL divergence between forward and reverse path measures equals average entropy production — is due to Kawai, Parrondo, and Van den Broeck (2007).

## On Thermodynamic Formalism

Ruelle's *Thermodynamic Formalism* (Cambridge, 2004, 2nd ed.) is the standard reference. The connection to statistical mechanics is explicit in Chapter 2; the rest develops the theory for general Axiom A systems. Bowen's *Equilibrium States and the Ergodic Theory of Anosov Diffeomorphisms* (Springer, 1975) is the other foundational text — more focused on the symbolic dynamics side.

The connection between phase transitions in dynamics and non-differentiability of pressure is developed in detail for interval maps in Przytycki and Urbański's *Conformal Fractals* (Cambridge, 2010).

## Looking Ahead

The free energy gradient flow perspective — that many PDEs of mathematical physics are gradient flows of free energy in Wasserstein space — is the subject of Chapter 30. The identification of the Fokker-Planck equation as the Wasserstein gradient flow of free energy (Jordan-Kinderlehrer-Otto, 1998) is a direct application of the framework developed here, and it shows that optimal transport provides the natural geometry for thermodynamic evolution.
