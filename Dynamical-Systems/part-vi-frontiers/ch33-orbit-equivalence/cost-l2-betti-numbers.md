# 33.5 Cost and $\ell^2$-Betti Numbers

How efficiently can you generate an orbit equivalence relation? If the orbits are the connected components of some graph on the space $X$, how many edges per vertex do you need, on average? This is the *cost* of an action, introduced by Levitt in 1995.

**Definition 33.5.1 (Levitt, 1995).** The *cost* of a free ergodic MPT action $\Gamma \curvearrowright X$ is:
$$\text{cost}(\Gamma \curvearrowright X) = \inf_\Phi \int |(\Phi(x))|\ d\mu(x),$$
where the infimum is over all generating graphings $\Phi$ (Borel graphs on $X$ whose connected components are the orbits).

A graphing is a Borel graph on $X$ whose connected components are exactly the $\Gamma$-orbits. The cost measures the minimum average degree needed to generate all the orbits. If you can generate the orbits with a graph where each point has on average 2 neighbors, the cost is at most 2.

Gaboriau's 2000 theorem connects cost to the $\ell^2$-Betti numbers we saw in section 33.3.

**Theorem 33.5.2 (Gaboriau, 2000).** For a free ergodic action:
$$\text{cost}(\Gamma \curvearrowright X) = 1 + \beta_1^{(2)}(\Gamma) - \beta_0^{(2)}(\Gamma).$$

For $\Gamma = F_r$ (free group on $r$ generators): $\text{cost}(F_r \curvearrowright X) = r$.

This is beautiful: the cost of a free group action equals the number of generators. The Cayley graph of $F_r$ (with $r$ generators) gives a graphing of cost $r$, and you cannot do better. The cost encodes how many generators the group needs.

For amenable groups, the cost is always 1 — consistent with Dye's theorem, since all their actions are OE-equivalent and the hyperfinite relation can be generated by a single partial bijection.

Now here is a famous open problem.

**Open Problem 33.5.3 (Fixed Price Problem).** Does every free ergodic action of $\Gamma$ have the same cost (independent of the action)? This is true for amenable groups and free groups, but open in general.

The fixed price conjecture says the cost is a property of the group, not the action. Gaboriau proved this for free groups (cost $= r$) and amenable groups (cost $= 1$). For groups with property (T), the cost is also known to be fixed (at 1, by Gaboriau-Popa). But for many natural groups — including $F_2 \times \mathbb{Z}$, for example — the question is open.

This is live research. The fixed price problem is one of the central open problems in measured group theory. Its resolution would tell us whether cost is a group-theoretic invariant or whether it depends on the action in some subtle way.
