# Chapter Notes — Chapter 33

This is an area where the primary papers are genuinely readable and worth your time. Don't just read surveys — go to the originals.

**Foundational papers.** Dye's two papers are: *On groups of measure preserving transformations I* (Amer. J. Math., 1959) and *II* (Amer. J. Math., 1963). The 1959 paper proves Dye's theorem for $\mathbb{Z}$-actions; the 1963 paper extends it. Ornstein-Weiss is: *Ergodic theory of amenable group actions I* (Bull. AMS, 1980), extended in their 1987 paper in the Journal d'Analyse.

**The rigidity papers.** Furman's rigidity theorem: *Gromov's measure equivalence and rigidity of higher rank lattices* (Annals of Math., 1999). This is where the modern rigidity program really starts — read it. Gaboriau's $\ell^2$-Betti numbers: *Invariants $\ell^2$ de relations d'équivalence et de groupes* (Publ. Math. IHES, 2002). This paper introduces cost and proves the fixed price result for free groups; it is beautifully written.

**Popa's superrigidity.** The key paper is: *Strong rigidity of II$_1$ factors arising from malleable actions of $w$-rigid groups, I, II* (Invent. Math., 2006). Popa has written several excellent survey papers; search for his ICM 2006 address. Ioana's theorem is in: *Cocycle superrigidity for profinite actions of property (T) groups* (Duke Math. J., 2011).

**Surveys.** Furman's *A survey of measured group theory* appears in the volume *Geometry, Rigidity, and Group Actions* (University of Chicago Press, 2011) — this is the best single overview of the whole field. Gaboriau's Séminaire Bourbaki talk on cost (2000) is also essential.

**The von Neumann algebra connection.** Connes's *Classification of Injective Factors* (Annals of Math., 1976) classifies hyperfinite II$_1$ factors and is the precursor to the orbit equivalence story from the operator-algebraic side. The orbit equivalence relation $\mathcal{R}$ determines the group measure space von Neumann algebra $L^\infty(X) \rtimes \Gamma = L(\mathcal{R})$, and the passage from OE to von Neumann algebra theory is a rich and active area.

**Where to go next.** The fixed price problem is still open. The most recent progress uses techniques from $\ell^2$-cohomology and measured group theory. For current research, look at papers by Gaboriau, Lyons, and Tsankov (on "spectral gap" for equivalence relations), and at Hayes's recent work connecting sofic groups to operator algebras.
