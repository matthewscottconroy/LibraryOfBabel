# Chapter 35 — The Isomorphism Problem in Ergodic Theory

> *When are two measure-preserving systems the same? Ornstein's theorem answers for Bernoulli shifts: entropy is the complete invariant. For general systems, the Foreman-Rudolph-Weiss theorem shows no Borel complete invariant can exist. The isomorphism problem is inherently undecidable — not in the Gödelian sense, but in a descriptive set-theoretic sense.*

**Prerequisites:** Chapter 7 (KS entropy, Ornstein's theorem), Chapter 32 (descriptive set theory, Borel equivalence relations), Chapter 33 (orbit equivalence).

---

## What This Chapter Is About

The isomorphism problem is this: given two measure-preserving systems, are they the same? It's one of the most natural questions in ergodic theory, and the answer has turned out to be one of the most surprising.

Ornstein solved it for Bernoulli shifts in 1970. Entropy is the complete invariant: two Bernoulli shifts are isomorphic if and only if they have the same entropy. This is a clean, beautiful answer, and it felt like the beginning of a classification theory for all ergodic systems.

The Foreman-Rudolph-Weiss theorem of 2011 ended that hope. Their result says: the isomorphism relation on ergodic systems is not classifiable by countable structures. There is no Borel complete invariant — no sequence of reals, no countable group, no countable anything that perfectly separates isomorphism classes. The isomorphism problem is, in a precise descriptive set-theoretic sense, intractable.

This chapter tells both sides of the story: the beautiful classification results that do exist, and the precise sense in which general classification is impossible.
