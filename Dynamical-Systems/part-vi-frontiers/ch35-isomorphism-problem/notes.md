# Chapter Notes — Chapter 35

**Ornstein's theorem.** The original papers: *Bernoulli shifts with the same entropy are isomorphic* (Advances in Math., 1970); *Factors of Bernoulli shifts* (Israel J. Math., 1970). His book *Ergodic Theory, Randomness, and Dynamical Systems* (Yale University Press, 1974) is the standard reference for the classical theory. Ornstein's approach via "finitary" codings remains essential.

**Foreman-Rudolph-Weiss.** The main paper: *The conjugacy problem in ergodic theory* (Annals of Math., 2011). The proof uses Borel reducibility in an essential way — you need to understand the Baire category theorem and Borel complexity to follow it. An introduction to descriptive set theory is in Kechris's *Classical Descriptive Set Theory* (Springer, 1995). Foreman's survey *Classifying and Unclassifying* appears in *Descriptive Set Theory and Dynamical Systems* (Cambridge, 2000).

**Rank-one systems.** King's mixing density result: *The commutant is the weak closure of the powers for rank-one transformations* (Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems, 1988). Foreman-Weiss on rank-one classification: *A anti-classification theorem for ergodic maps* (2019, arXiv). The construction of examples uses the "cutting and stacking" technique described in Friedman's *Introduction to Ergodic Theory* (Van Nostrand, 1970).

**Halmos-von Neumann.** *Operator methods in classical mechanics, II* (Annals of Math., 1942). The extension to general compact group rotations is in Halmos's *Lectures on Ergodic Theory* (Chelsea, 1956).

**Williams' conjecture and its disproof.** Williams's original conjecture: *Classification of shifts of finite type* (Annals of Math., 1973). Kim-Roush's disproof: *Decidability of shift equivalence* (Dynamical Systems, LMS Lecture Note Series, 1992). The current state of the 1D conjugacy problem is surveyed in Boyle's *Open problems in symbolic dynamics* (2008, available on arXiv).

**Giordano-Putnam-Skau.** *Topological orbit equivalence and $C^*$-crossed products* (J. Reine Angew. Math., 1995). The K-theory machinery they use connects to Elliott's classification program for $C^*$-algebras.
