# Chapter Notes — Chapter 36

**The foundational references.** Zimmer's papers: *Strong rigidity for ergodic actions of semisimple Lie groups* (Annals of Math., 1980) introduced cocycle superrigidity. His book *Ergodic Theory and Semisimple Groups* (Birkhäuser, 1984) remains the standard reference and is still in print. Margulis's superrigidity is in *Discrete Subgroups of Semisimple Lie Groups* (Springer, 1991). A more accessible introduction is Dave Witte Morris's *Introduction to Arithmetic Groups*, available free on arXiv (math/0106063).

**Brown-Fisher-Hurtado.** The main paper: *Zimmer's conjecture: Subexponential growth, measure rigidity, and strong property (T)* (Annals of Math., 2022). The published version appeared after a long revision process from the 2017 preprint. David Fisher's survey *Recent progress in the Zimmer program* (2020, arXiv:2005.04540) is the best accessible overview of what was proved and what remains open. Read this survey before tackling the main paper.

**Technical tools.** KAM theory in the context of group actions: Damjanovic-Katok's work on local rigidity of algebraic actions provides background. The non-stationary normal forms technique is developed in Brown-Fisher-Hurtado's earlier paper *C^1 actions of semisimple Lie groups* (2020). The harmonic analysis on $G/\Gamma$ uses the "property (T) with exponential rate" (strong property (T)) introduced by Lafforgue.

**Stuck-Zimmer theorem.** *Topological properties of lattice actions on manifolds* (Annals of Math., 1994). The proof uses the cocycle superrigidity theorem in a clever way.

**What's still open.** The full Zimmer conjecture for non-cocompact lattices in groups other than $SL(n, \mathbb{R})$ is not fully resolved. Lattices in $Sp(2n, \mathbb{R})$, $SO(n, 1)$ (rank 1 — where the conjecture fails in general), and exotic groups are active areas. The case of $C^1$ (rather than $C^\infty$) actions is also open and may require entirely different techniques.
