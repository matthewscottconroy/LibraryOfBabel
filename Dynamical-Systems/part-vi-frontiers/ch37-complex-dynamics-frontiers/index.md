# Chapter 37 — Frontiers in Complex Dynamics

> *The Mandelbrot set is locally connected (MLC conjecture: open). Renormalization explains the universality of Feigenbaum constants. The holomorphic motion theorem (Lambda lemma) is the key tool. Thurston rigidity classifies branched covers up to homotopy — connecting complex dynamics to topology.*

**Prerequisites:** Chapter 13 (complex dynamics, Julia/Fatou sets, Mandelbrot set), Chapter 10 (renormalization, universality), Chapter 14 (KAM, quasi-periodic orbits).

## What This Chapter Is About

The Mandelbrot set is one of the most studied objects in mathematics. Forty years of work by some of the finest mathematicians — Douady, Hubbard, Thurston, Yoccoz, Lyubich, Shishikura — has produced a deep theory of complex dynamics. And yet the central question about the Mandelbrot set remains open: is it locally connected?

MLC — the conjecture that the Mandelbrot set is locally connected — is more than a topological curiosity. If true, it would give a complete topological model of the Mandelbrot set via external angles, it would imply density of hyperbolic dynamics in the parameter space, and it would complete the combinatorial classification of complex quadratic polynomials.

This chapter surveys where the field stands. MLC is known in many cases — finitely renormalizable parameters, bounded combinatorial type — but the general case remains open. Beyond MLC, we discuss polynomial-like maps and renormalization (explaining the Feigenbaum universality from Chapter 10 via a fixed point of the renormalization operator), the Lambda lemma and holomorphic motions, Thurston's topological characterization of rational maps, and the surprising phenomenon of parabolic implosion.
