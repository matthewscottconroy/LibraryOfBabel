# Chapter Notes — Chapter 39

**Renner's smooth entropy.** The foundational work is Renner's PhD thesis: *Security of Quantum Key Distribution* (ETH Zürich, 2005, available on arXiv:quant-ph/0512258). This is the paper that introduced smooth min- and max-entropy and proved the Quantum Leftover Hash Lemma. It's a remarkably complete document — read it. Renner also has an excellent follow-up: *Symmetry of large physical systems implies independence of subsystems* (Nature Physics, 2007).

**The modern framework.** Tomamichel's book *Quantum Information Processing with Finite Resources* (Springer, 2016) is the comprehensive reference for the smooth entropy framework. His earlier PhD thesis (ETH, 2012, arXiv:1203.2142) is also excellent and free online. Both cover the quantum case in depth.

**Second-order channel coding.** Polyanskiy-Poor-Verdú's *Channel coding rate in the finite blocklength regime* (IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, 2010) is the classical paper that started the modern second-order analysis. The "PPV bounds" are now standard benchmarks for code design. Hayashi's *Information Spectrum Approach to Second-Order Coding Rate in Channel Coding* (IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, 2009) is a complementary approach using the information spectrum method.

**Quantum thermodynamics.** Dahlsten-Renner-Rieper-Vedral's *Inadequacy of von Neumann entropy for characterizing extractable work* (New J. Physics, 2011) introduced the one-shot second law. Åberg's *Truly work-like work extraction via a single-shot analysis* (Nature Commun., 2013) extended this. The resource theory of thermodynamics is surveyed in Brandão-Horodecki-Oppenheim-Renes-Spekkens (2013).

**Leftover Hash Lemma.** The classical version: Impagliazzo-Levin-Luby (STOC, 1989). The version with quantum adversaries: Renner-König (2005). For a clean exposition, see König-Renner-Schaffner (2009).

**Where to go next.** The resource theory of asymmetry, coherence, and thermodynamics is an active area connecting smooth entropy, quantum channels, and thermodynamics. See the review by Chitambar-Gour (2019) in Rev. Modern Physics.
