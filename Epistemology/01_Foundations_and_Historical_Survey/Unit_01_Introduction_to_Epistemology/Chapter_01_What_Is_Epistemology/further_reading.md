# Further Reading and Primary References

## Primary Sources

- Plato. *Meno*. (~380 BCE). The dialogue that introduces the paradox of inquiry and the distinction between knowledge and true belief.
- Plato. *Theaetetus*. (~369 BCE). The foundational text for the analysis of knowledge; the first sustained examination of what knowledge is.
- Aristotle. *Posterior Analytics*. (~350 BCE). Aristotle's theory of scientific knowledge, demonstration, and the relationship between knowledge and explanation.
- Descartes, René. *Meditations on First Philosophy*. (1641). The founding text of modern epistemology; introduces the method of doubt and the search for certainty.
- Locke, John. *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*. (1689). Books I–IV cover the origins, extent, and nature of human knowledge from an empiricist perspective.
- Kant, Immanuel. *Critique of Pure Reason*. (1781/1787). Synthesizes rationalism and empiricism; introduces the synthetic a priori and the limits of human knowledge.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Chisholm, Roderick. *Theory of Knowledge*. 3rd ed. Prentice Hall, 1989. A concise systematic introduction to the main problems of epistemology.
- BonJour, Laurence and Ernest Sosa. *Epistemic Justification: Internalism vs. Externalism, Foundations vs. Virtues*. Blackwell, 2003. A structured debate between two leading epistemologists.
- Greco, John and Ernest Sosa, eds. *The Blackwell Guide to Epistemology*. Blackwell, 1999. Comprehensive survey of the major issues.
- Lehrer, Keith. *Theory of Knowledge*. 2nd ed. Westview, 2000. A systematic account from a coherentist perspective.
- Moser, Paul, Dwayne Mulder, and J.D. Trout. *The Theory of Knowledge: A Thematic Introduction*. Oxford University Press, 1998. Accessible thematic coverage.
- Steup, Matthias. *An Introduction to Contemporary Epistemology*. Prentice Hall, 1996. Standard introductory text covering the main debates.
- Sosa, Ernest, Jaegwon Kim, Jeremy Fantl, and Matthew McGrath, eds. *Epistemology: An Anthology*. 2nd ed. Blackwell, 2008. The leading anthology for the field.
- Williamson, Timothy. *Knowledge and Its Limits*. Oxford University Press, 2000. Influential argument for knowledge-first epistemology.

## Journals

- *Episteme: A Journal of Social Epistemology*. Cambridge University Press.
- *Synthese*. Springer. Wide coverage of epistemology and philosophy of science.
- *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research*. Wiley-Blackwell.
- *Philosophical Studies*. Springer. Analytic epistemology focus.
