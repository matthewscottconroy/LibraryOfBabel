# Further Reading and Primary References

## Primary Sources

- Ryle, Gilbert. *The Concept of Mind*. Hutchinson, 1949. Introduces the distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that; chapter 2 is essential.
- Russell, Bertrand. *The Problems of Philosophy*. Oxford University Press, 1912. Chapter 5 develops the distinction between knowledge by acquaintance and knowledge by description.
- Kripke, Saul. *Naming and Necessity*. Harvard University Press, 1980. Disentangles the necessary/contingent distinction from the a priori/a posteriori distinction.
- Kant, Immanuel. *Critique of Pure Reason*. Introduction. The classic statement of the analytic/synthetic and a priori/a posteriori distinctions.
- Quine, W.V.O. "Two Dogmas of Empiricism." *Philosophical Review* 60 (1951): 20–43. Classic attack on the analytic/synthetic distinction.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Casullo, Albert. *A Priori Justification*. Oxford University Press, 2003. Systematic treatment of the a priori distinction and its epistemological significance.
- BonJour, Laurence. *In Defense of Pure Reason*. Cambridge University Press, 1998. Defense of a priori knowledge against empiricist challenges.
- Devitt, Michael. "Naturalism and the A Priori." *Philosophical Studies* 92 (1998): 45–65. Naturalist critique of a priori knowledge.
- Sosa, Ernest. "The Analysis of 'Knowledge That P'." *Analysis* 25 (1964): 1–8. Discussion of the propositional/non-propositional knowledge distinction.
- Stanley, Jason and Timothy Williamson. "Knowing How." *Journal of Philosophy* 98 (2001): 411–444. Influential argument that knowing-how is a species of knowing-that.
- Bengson, John and Marc Moffett, eds. *Knowing How: Essays on Knowledge, Mind, and Action*. Oxford University Press, 2011. Comprehensive coverage of the knowing-how debate.

## On Propositional Attitudes

- Fodor, Jerry. *Propositional Attitudes*. MIT Press, 1981.
- Stalnaker, Robert. *Our Knowledge of the Internal World*. Oxford University Press, 2008.
