# Key Figures

## Aristotle (384–322 BCE)
Aristotle is the founder of the demonstrative model of scientific knowledge and the intellectual virtues tradition. His *Posterior Analytics* argues that genuine knowledge (*episteme*) is knowledge through causes, achieved by demonstrating facts from first principles known by *nous* (intellectual intuition). Unlike Plato, Aristotle grounds knowledge in observation of the sensory world — universals are grasped through, not independently of, experience. His account of the five intellectual virtues (*episteme*, *nous*, *sophia*, *phronesis*, *techne*) in *Nicomachean Ethics* Book VI remains a touchstone for virtue epistemology.

## Alexander of Aphrodisias (c. 200 CE)
Known as "The Commentator," Alexander of Aphrodisias wrote the definitive ancient commentaries on Aristotle. His interpretation of Aristotle's active intellect and his defense of Aristotelian empiricism against Platonic rationalism were foundational for subsequent scholastic and Islamic philosophy.

## Theophrastus (c. 371–287 BCE)
Aristotle's student and successor as head of the Lyceum. Theophrastus extended Aristotle's epistemological work, with particular attention to the theory of sense perception and its relationship to knowledge. His *On Sense Perceptions* provides a critical survey of Pre-Socratic and Platonic views.

## Jonathan Barnes (1942–)
The leading contemporary Aristotle scholar in the analytic tradition. Barnes's translation and commentary on the *Posterior Analytics* has set the terms for contemporary philosophical engagement with Aristotle's epistemology. His reading emphasizes the foundationalist structure of Aristotle's theory and its connections to contemporary debates about justification.

## Terence Irwin (1947–)
Irwin's *Aristotle's First Principles* is the most comprehensive contemporary treatment of Aristotle's epistemology. Irwin argues that Aristotle's foundationalism is more subtle than it appears — that the "first principles" of demonstration are themselves arrived at through dialectical inquiry and not simply intuited without argument.

## Richard Sorabji (1934–)
Sorabji's work on Aristotle's philosophy of mind and perception (*Aristotle on Memory*, *Matter, Space and Motion*) has been central to understanding the epistemological implications of Aristotle's accounts of sense perception and intellectual grasp. His ancient commentators series made the scholastic reception of Aristotle accessible to contemporary philosophers.
