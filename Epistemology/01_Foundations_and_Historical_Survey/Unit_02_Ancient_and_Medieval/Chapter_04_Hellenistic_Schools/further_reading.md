## Primary Sources

- Sextus Empiricus, *Outlines of Pyrrhonism*, trans. R. G. Bury, Harvard University Press (Loeb Classical Library), 1933. The foundational text of Pyrrhonist skepticism, presenting the ten modes of Aenesidemus and the five modes of Agrippa in systematic form.

- Sextus Empiricus, *Against the Mathematicians (Adversus Mathematicos)*, trans. R. G. Bury, Harvard University Press (Loeb Classical Library), 1935–1949. A comprehensive attack on every theoretical discipline; Books VII–VIII constitute *Against the Logicians* and are indispensable for understanding Pyrrhonist critique of the criterion of truth.

- Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of the Eminent Philosophers*, trans. Pamela Mensch, Oxford University Press, 2018. Book VII covers Stoic doctrine (including Zeno, Cleanthes, and Chrysippus on the *phantasia kataleptike*); Book X presents Epicurus's own letters, including the crucial "Letter to Herodotus" on canonic epistemology.

- Epicurus, *The Epicurus Reader: Selected Writings and Testimonia*, trans. Brad Inwood and L. P. Gerson, Hackett, 1994. Contains the principal Epicurean documents on the canon—sensation, prolepsis, and feelings—alongside helpful commentary on their epistemological significance.

- Cicero, *Academica*, trans. James S. Reid, Macmillan, 1885. The essential Latin source for the Academic skeptical tradition, contrasting Arcesilaus and Carneades with Stoic dogmatism; still valuable despite the incomplete manuscript tradition.

- Cicero, *De Natura Deorum* and *De Divinatione*, trans. P. G. Walsh, Oxford University Press, 1997–2008. Contains important Stoic and Academic positions on theological knowledge and divination, illuminating how epistemology interacted with theology in the Hellenistic period.

- Long, A. A. and Sedley, D. N. (eds.), *The Hellenistic Philosophers*, 2 vols., Cambridge University Press, 1987. The standard scholarly sourcebook; Vol. 1 provides translations and commentary, Vol. 2 the Greek and Latin originals, covering all major schools systematically.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Annas, Julia and Barnes, Jonathan, *The Modes of Scepticism: Ancient Texts and Modern Interpretations*, Cambridge University Press, 1985. A careful philological and philosophical analysis of Sextus's ten modes and five modes, essential for understanding the structure of Pyrrhonist argumentation.

- Frede, Michael, *Essays in Ancient Philosophy*, University of Minnesota Press, 1987. Influential essays on Stoic epistemology, the *kataleptic impression*, and the Academic skeptical response; a landmark in the scholarship.

- Striker, Gisela, *Essays on Hellenistic Epistemology and Ethics*, Cambridge University Press, 1996. The finest collection of essays on Stoic and skeptical epistemology by one of the leading scholars in the field.

- Burnyeat, Myles (ed.), *The Skeptical Tradition*, University of California Press, 1983. A seminal collection examining both ancient skepticism and its reception, with important essays by Burnyeat, Barnes, and Frede.

- Hankinson, R. J., *The Sceptics*, Routledge, 1995. A lucid and thorough treatment of all forms of ancient skepticism—Academic, Pyrrhonist, and medical—suitable for advanced undergraduates and researchers alike.

- Allen, James, *Inference from Signs: Ancient Debates about the Nature of Evidence*, Oxford University Press, 2001. Examines Epicurean and Stoic theories of inference and sign-based reasoning, revealing how both schools developed sophisticated epistemologies of indirect knowledge.

- Vogt, Katja Maria, *Belief and Truth: A Skeptic Reading of Plato*, Oxford University Press, 2012. Though focused on Plato, includes important reassessments of the relationship between Academic skepticism and the Platonic tradition.

- Bailey, Cyril, *The Greek Atomists and Epicurus*, Clarendon Press, 1928. A classic study that remains valuable for its careful treatment of Epicurean physics and its relationship to Epicurean epistemology.

- Sedley, David, *Lucretius and the Transformation of Greek Wisdom*, Cambridge University Press, 1998. Examines how Epicurean epistemology was transmitted and adapted in Lucretius's *De Rerum Natura*, with implications for understanding prolepsis and sensory evidence.

- Brunschwig, Jacques, *Papers in Hellenistic Philosophy*, Cambridge University Press, 1994. Important essays on Stoic and Epicurean epistemology, including the influential "The Stoic Theory of the Supreme Genus" and analyses of the *phantasia kataleptike*.
