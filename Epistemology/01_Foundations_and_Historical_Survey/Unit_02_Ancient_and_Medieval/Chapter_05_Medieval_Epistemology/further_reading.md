## Primary Sources

- Augustine of Hippo, *Against the Academicians (Contra Academicos)*, trans. Peter King, Hackett, 1995. Augustine's early dialogue refuting Academic skepticism and defending the possibility of certain knowledge through divine illumination; a key text for understanding the Platonic dimension of medieval epistemology.

- Augustine of Hippo, *On the Teacher (De Magistro)*, trans. Peter King, in *Against the Academicians and The Teacher*, Hackett, 1995. Argues that all genuine learning requires the inner illumination of Christ as the interior teacher, not merely the transmission of signs by human instructors.

- Augustine of Hippo, *On the Trinity (De Trinitate)*, trans. Edmund Hill, New City Press, 1991. Books VIII–XV develop the psychological analogy for the Trinity and contain Augustine's mature epistemology of *mens*, memory, and self-knowledge as image of God.

- Thomas Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae*, trans. Fathers of the English Dominican Province, Benziger Brothers, 1920 (repr. Christian Classics, 1981). Questions 84–89 of the First Part address the soul's cognitive powers, abstraction, and the extent to which we can know God; the foundational Thomistic epistemological text.

- Thomas Aquinas, *Quaestiones Disputatae de Veritate*, trans. Robert Mulligan et al., Henry Regnery, 1952–1954. A systematic treatment of truth, knowledge, the divine ideas, and the agent intellect, more analytically detailed than the corresponding passages in the *Summa*.

- William of Ockham, *Philosophical Writings: A Selection*, trans. Philotheus Boehner, Hackett, 1990. Contains key passages on intuitive and abstractive cognition, the fictum theory, and the breakdown of the Aristotelian universal—essential for understanding Ockham's nominalist epistemology.

- Avicenna (Ibn Sīnā), *The Book of Healing (Kitāb al-Shifāʾ): Logic and Psychology*, partial trans. in various collections. The *De Anima* section develops the theory of the floating man and the abstraction of intelligibles via the Active Intellect; Fazlur Rahman's translation *Avicenna's Psychology* (Oxford, 1952) remains useful.

- Averroes (Ibn Rushd), *Commentarium Magnum in Aristotelis De Anima*, trans. Richard C. Taylor, Marquette University Press, 2009. Averroes's controversial doctrine of the unity of the material intellect and its implications for individual knowledge are central to Latin debates about intellection and personal immortality.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Copleston, Frederick, *A History of Medieval Philosophy*, Harper & Row, 1972. A reliable survey of the major medieval thinkers and their epistemological positions, suitable as an orientation before engaging primary texts.

- Gilson, Étienne, *The Christian Philosophy of Saint Augustine*, Random House, 1960. The classic philosophical study of Augustine's thought, with extended treatment of the illumination theory and its Platonic roots.

- Stump, Eleonore, *Aquinas*, Routledge, 2003. A rigorous analytical reconstruction of Aquinas's philosophy, including his theory of abstraction, the agent intellect, and analogical predication of divine attributes.

- Adams, Marilyn McCord, *William Ockham*, 2 vols., University of Notre Dame Press, 1987. The definitive scholarly study; Vol. 1 is devoted to Ockham's epistemology, covering intuitive cognition, the status of universals, and the ontology of knowledge.

- Pasnau, Robert, *Theories of Cognition in the Later Middle Ages*, Cambridge University Press, 1997. A careful analytical examination of the late scholastic debates about how the intellect grasps its objects, covering Aquinas, Godfrey of Fontaines, and Ockham.

- McGinn, Bernard, *The Foundations of Mysticism*, Crossroad, 1991. Indispensable for understanding the mystical-experiential dimension of medieval epistemology alongside the scholastic theoretical tradition.

- Davidson, Herbert A., *Alfarabi, Avicenna, and Averroes on Intellect*, Oxford University Press, 1992. The standard scholarly account of the development of Islamic Aristotelian theories of intellect and their transmission to Latin scholasticism.

- Leaman, Oliver, *Averroes and His Philosophy*, Clarendon Press, 1988. An accessible philosophical introduction to Averroes's main positions, including the contentious doctrine of the unity of the intellect and its epistemological consequences.

- Marrone, Steven P., *The Light of Thy Countenance: Science and Knowledge of God in the Thirteenth Century*, 2 vols., Brill, 2001. Examines the persistence and transformation of Augustinian illuminationism in thirteenth-century scholasticism, particularly in Bonaventure and Roger Bacon.

- Spade, Paul Vincent (ed.), *The Cambridge Companion to Ockham*, Cambridge University Press, 1999. A collection of essays covering Ockham's logic, metaphysics, and epistemology, including his responses to skeptical worries about intuitive cognition.
