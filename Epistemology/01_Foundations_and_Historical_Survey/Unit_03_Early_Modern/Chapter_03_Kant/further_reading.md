## Primary Sources

- Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, trans. Paul Guyer and Allen W. Wood, Cambridge University Press, 1998. The definitive English translation of Kant's most important work; the Guyer-Wood edition includes the A (1781) and B (1787) variants and is the standard scholarly reference.

- Immanuel Kant, *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics*, trans. Gary Hatfield, Cambridge University Press, 2004. Kant's more accessible summary of the *Critique*, written after Kant perceived that the *Critique* was being misread as a form of Berkeleyan idealism; contains the clearest statement of the Copernican revolution analogy.

- Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Practical Reason*, trans. Mary Gregor, Cambridge University Press, 1997. The second *Critique*; its preface and first part illuminate Kant's view of reason's practical use and the postulates of pure practical reason, showing how the limitations established in the first *Critique* serve a positive function.

- Immanuel Kant, *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*, trans. Mary Gregor, Cambridge University Press, 1997. Though primarily ethical, the Groundwork's analysis of the concept of freedom as a condition of moral agency connects to the theoretical epistemology of the first *Critique*.

- Immanuel Kant, *Metaphysical Foundations of Natural Science*, trans. Michael Friedman, Cambridge University Press, 2004. The application of the critical epistemology to the foundations of Newtonian physics; essential for understanding what Kant meant by "pure natural science."

## Key Secondary Sources

- Strawson, P. F., *The Bounds of Sense: An Essay on Kant's Critique of Pure Reason*, Methuen, 1966. The most influential analytical study of the first *Critique*; Strawson accepts the "analytic" core of Kant's project (the conditions of experience) while rejecting the idealist "transcendental" framework; essential for understanding how analytic philosophers have engaged with Kant.

- Allison, Henry E., *Kant's Transcendental Idealism: An Interpretation and Defense*, revised ed., Yale University Press, 2004. The most important defence of Kant's transcendental idealism against Strawson's criticism; argues that the distinction between appearances and things-in-themselves is epistemological, not ontological.

- Longuenesse, Béatrice, *Kant and the Capacity to Judge*, Princeton University Press, 1998. An examination of the categories and their derivation from the logical forms of judgment; the most philosophically rigorous treatment of the metaphysical deduction and its relationship to the transcendental deduction.

- Guyer, Paul, *Kant and the Claims of Knowledge*, Cambridge University Press, 1987. A detailed and critical examination of Kant's theory of experience, the transcendental aesthetic, and the analytic; argues that Kant's project is both more ambitious and more problematic than Allison's defence suggests.

- Gardner, Sebastian, *Kant and the Critique of Pure Reason*, Routledge, 1999. An excellent introductory study that moves carefully through the major sections of the *Critique*, providing detailed exposition and critical assessment.

- Ameriks, Karl (ed.), *The Cambridge Companion to German Idealism*, Cambridge University Press, 2000. Situates Kant in relation to Fichte, Schelling, and Hegel; important for understanding how Kant's critical epistemology generated the idealist tradition.

- Friedman, Michael, *Kant and the Exact Sciences*, Harvard University Press, 1992. Examines Kant's epistemology of mathematics and physics, arguing that his notion of pure intuition is best understood in relation to the Euclidean geometry and Newtonian mechanics of his time.

- Caygill, Howard, *A Kant Dictionary*, Blackwell, 1995. An indispensable reference work explaining Kant's technical vocabulary in context; invaluable for navigating the *Critique's* specialized terminology.

- Wood, Allen W., *Kant*, Blackwell, 2005. A comprehensive and reliable introduction to all aspects of Kant's philosophy, with a clear account of the epistemological doctrines and their systematic connections.

- Bird, Graham, *The Revolutionary Kant: A Commentary on the Critique of Pure Reason*, Open Court, 2006. A massive and meticulously detailed commentary arguing against both the phenomenalist and the two-worlds interpretations of transcendental idealism; indispensable for serious scholars.

- Buroker, Jill Vance, *Kant's Critique of Pure Reason: An Introduction*, Cambridge University Press, 2006. A pedagogically excellent introduction that treats the main sections of the *Critique* in order without sacrificing philosophical rigour; ideal for advanced undergraduates.
