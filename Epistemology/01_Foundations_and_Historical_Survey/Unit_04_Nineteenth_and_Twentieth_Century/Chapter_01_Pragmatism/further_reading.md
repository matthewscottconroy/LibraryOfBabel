## Primary Sources

- Charles Sanders Peirce, *Collected Papers*, eds. Charles Hartshorne and Paul Weiss, 8 vols., Harvard University Press, 1931–1958. The standard edition; for epistemology, Volumes V and VI (Pragmatism and Pragmaticism; Scientific Metaphysics) are most important, including "The Fixation of Belief" and "How to Make Our Ideas Clear."

- Charles Sanders Peirce, *The Essential Peirce*, 2 vols., eds. Nathan Houser and Christian Kloesel, Indiana University Press, 1992–1998. A well-selected thematic anthology making the key epistemological texts accessible without requiring navigation of the *Collected Papers*.

- William James, *Pragmatism: A New Name for Some Old Ways of Thinking*, Harvard University Press, 1975 (orig. 1907). James's popular lectures presenting his version of pragmatism; Lectures II and VI are especially important for epistemology, containing the cash-value account of truth and the will-to-believe doctrine in brief.

- William James, *The Will to Believe and Other Essays in Popular Philosophy*, Harvard University Press, 1979 (orig. 1897). Contains the title essay arguing that in certain conditions of genuine option, evidential insufficiency, and forced choice, we have the right to believe beyond the evidence; James's most discussed epistemological contribution.

- John Dewey, *The Quest for Certainty*, Minton, Balch, 1929. Dewey's diagnosis of the Western philosophical tradition's misguided search for certainty in a world of irreducible contingency; argues for replacing the spectator theory of knowledge with an instrumentalist account of inquiry.

- John Dewey, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry*, Henry Holt, 1938. The most detailed account of Dewey's epistemology; develops the pattern of inquiry (indeterminate situation → problem → hypothesis → testing → settled situation) as the model for all knowledge acquisition.

- Richard Rorty, *Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature*, Princeton University Press, 1979. The most influential neo-pragmatist work; argues that the epistemological tradition is built on the untenable "mirror" metaphor and should be replaced by a conversation-based, anti-representationalist practice.

- Robert Brandom, *Making It Explicit: Reasoning, Representing, and Discursive Commitment*, Harvard University Press, 1994. The most technically sophisticated neo-pragmatist epistemology; develops an inferentialist semantics in which meaning is constituted by the inferential roles concepts play in reasoning practices.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Misak, Cheryl, *The American Pragmatists*, Oxford University Press, 2013. A reliable historical and philosophical survey of classical pragmatism, covering Peirce, James, and Dewey with particular attention to the divergences in their epistemological positions.

- Hookway, Christopher, *Peirce*, Routledge, 1985. The most philosophically rigorous introduction to Peirce's epistemology, covering fallibilism, the community of inquiry, the theory of signs, and the ideal limit account of truth.

- Putnam, Hilary, *Pragmatism: An Open Question*, Blackwell, 1995. A short but important work in which Putnam defends a neo-pragmatist epistemology while criticizing both Rorty's anti-realism and classical correspondence theories; highly accessible.

- Bernstein, Richard J., *John Dewey*, Washington Square Press, 1966. A reliable introductory study of Dewey's pragmatist epistemology and its relationship to his broader philosophical anthropology and social philosophy.

- Haack, Susan, *Evidence and Inquiry: Towards Reconstruction in Epistemology*, Blackwell, 1993. Defends "foundherentism" as a synthesis of foundationalism and coherentism, developing a position that is Peircean in spirit while engaging contemporary analytic debates.

- Brandom, Robert, *Articulating Reasons: An Introduction to Inferentialism*, Harvard University Press, 2000. A more accessible presentation of the inferentialist project than *Making It Explicit*; argues that knowing is a normative status constituted by inferential commitments and entitlements.

- Rorty, Richard, *Consequences of Pragmatism*, University of Minnesota Press, 1982. A collection of essays developing the neo-pragmatist position and engaging with analytic epistemology, continental philosophy, and the history of philosophy.

- Misak, Cheryl, *Truth, Politics, Morality: Pragmatism and Deliberation*, Routledge, 2000. Defends a Peircean account of truth in ethics and politics, showing how pragmatist epistemology extends beyond natural science to normative inquiry.

- Thayer, H. S., *Meaning and Action: A Critical History of Pragmatism*, Bobbs-Merrill, 1968. A comprehensive historical survey of classical pragmatism that situates the epistemological disputes within broader developments in American philosophy.
