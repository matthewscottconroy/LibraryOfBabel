## Primary Sources

- Plato, *Meno*, trans. G. M. A. Grube, in *Plato: Complete Works*, ed. John M. Cooper, Hackett, 1997. Contains the tethering metaphor (97a–98b) distinguishing knowledge from true belief: true opinion becomes knowledge when "tethered" by an account, and the metaphor of the statues of Daedalus captures the difference between beliefs that can be "lost" and those that are secured.

- Plato, *Theaetetus*, trans. M. J. Levett, rev. Myles Burnyeat, in *Plato: Complete Works*, Hackett, 1997. The dialogue most directly concerned with the analysis of knowledge; examines and rejects the definitions of knowledge as perception, as true belief, and as true belief with an account (*logos*).

- Chisholm, Roderick M., *Theory of Knowledge*, 3rd ed., Prentice-Hall, 1989. The classic analytic statement of foundationalist epistemology, with a systematic account of the three conditions for knowledge and the variety of epistemic concepts that fill out the analysis.

- Ayer, A. J., *The Problem of Knowledge*, Macmillan, 1956. An influential mid-century treatment of knowledge and certainty that articulates the JTB structure and its challenges before Gettier; Chapter 1 provides an elegant statement of the three conditions.

- Russell, Bertrand, *The Problems of Philosophy*, Oxford University Press, 1912. Chapter 13, "Knowledge, Error, and Probable Opinion," provides an early analytic treatment of the distinction between different grades of epistemic attitude and the conditions for genuine knowledge.

- Lehrer, Keith, *Theory of Knowledge*, Westview Press, 1990 (2nd ed., 2000). A systematic treatment of knowledge that situates the JTB analysis in relation to coherentism, reliabilism, and the Gettier problem; Chapter 1 is particularly valuable for the belief condition.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Shope, Robert K., *The Analysis of Knowing: A Decade of Research*, Princeton University Press, 1983. The most thorough survey of post-Gettier analyses of knowledge, covering all major proposals from 1963 to 1983; invaluable for mapping the research landscape that followed from the JTB framework.

- Steup, Matthias and Turri, John and Sosa, Ernest (eds.), *Contemporary Debates in Epistemology*, 3rd ed., Blackwell, 2014. Contains paired essays defending and attacking major positions in the theory of knowledge, including the JTB analysis and its rivals.

- Williams, Michael, *Problems of Knowledge: A Critical Introduction to Epistemology*, Oxford University Press, 2001. An accessible critical introduction that traces the development of the JTB framework and the pressures on it, suitable for advanced undergraduates.

- Pollock, John L. and Cruz, Joseph, *Contemporary Theories of Knowledge*, 2nd ed., Rowman & Littlefield, 1999. A systematic survey of the major positions in the theory of knowledge, with careful attention to the justification condition and the varieties of internalism and externalism.

- Bonjour, Laurence, *The Structure of Empirical Knowledge*, Harvard University Press, 1985. A rigorous defence of coherentism as a theory of justification, engaging the JTB framework and articulating the belief condition and truth condition in relation to the coherentist account of warrant.

- Klein, Peter, "Human Knowledge and the Infinite Regress of Reasons," in *Philosophical Perspectives*, 13 (1999), pp. 297–325. An important paper on the structure of justification and the regress problem that bears directly on the justification condition in the JTB analysis.

- Greco, John and Sosa, Ernest (eds.), *The Blackwell Guide to Epistemology*, Blackwell, 1999. A comprehensive reference collection with essays on all major topics in the theory of knowledge, including the JTB analysis, truth, belief, and justification.

- Nozick, Robert, *Philosophical Explanations*, Harvard University Press, 1981. Chapter 3, "Knowledge and Skepticism," contains one of the most influential post-Gettier analyses of knowledge and bears directly on the justification condition by replacing it with a sensitivity condition.

- Chalmers, David J., "The Nature of Epistemic Space," in *Epistemic Modality*, eds. Andy Egan and Brian Weatherson, Oxford University Press, 2011. Situates the JTB analysis in a two-dimensional semantics framework; relevant to the truth condition and the varieties of belief content.

- Zagzebski, Linda, *Virtues of the Mind: An Inquiry into the Nature of Virtue and the Ethical Foundations of Knowledge*, Cambridge University Press, 1996. Argues that Gettier problems arise from a deeper structural feature of the JTB analysis and motivates the shift to virtue epistemology as an alternative.
