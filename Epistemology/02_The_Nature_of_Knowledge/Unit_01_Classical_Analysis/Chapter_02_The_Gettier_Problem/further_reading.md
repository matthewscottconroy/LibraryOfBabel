## Primary Sources

- Gettier, Edmund L., "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?", *Analysis*, 23:6 (1963), pp. 121–123. The three-page paper that launched the post-Gettier research program; essential reading for any student of the theory of knowledge.

- Goldman, Alvin I., "A Causal Theory of Knowing," *Journal of Philosophy*, 64:12 (1967), pp. 357–372. Goldman's response to Gettier: proposes that knowledge requires an appropriate causal connection between the fact known and the belief, which the original Gettier cases and the fake barns case both violate.

- Goldman, Alvin I., "Discrimination and Perceptual Knowledge," *Journal of Philosophy*, 73:20 (1976), pp. 771–791. Introduces the fake barns case and the relevant alternatives framework; argues that knowledge requires the ability to discriminate the target fact from relevant alternative possibilities.

- Harman, Gilbert, *Thought*, Princeton University Press, 1973. Contains Harman's influential "no false lemmas" response to Gettier: knowledge is blocked when the justified inference passes through a false intermediate belief.

- Chisholm, Roderick M., *Theory of Knowledge*, 3rd ed., Prentice-Hall, 1989. Contains Chisholm's catalogue of Gettier-type cases and his attempts to specify a fourth condition that would block them; useful for seeing the range of the problem.

- Russell, Bertrand, *Human Knowledge: Its Scope and Limits*, Simon and Schuster, 1948. Chapter II contains a precursor to Gettier cases: Russell notes that a stopped clock happens to show the correct time twice a day, and that a person who looks at it at the right moment has a true justified belief without knowledge.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Shope, Robert K., *The Analysis of Knowing: A Decade of Research*, Princeton University Press, 1983. The definitive survey of the first two decades of post-Gettier theorizing; essential for understanding the proliferation of proposed fourth conditions.

- Zagzebski, Linda, "The Inescapability of Gettier Problems," *Philosophical Quarterly*, 44:174 (1994), pp. 65–73. Argues that Gettier problems will arise for any analysis of knowledge that treats knowledge as true belief plus some further condition; motivates the shift to virtue epistemology.

- Turri, John, "In Gettier's Wake," in *Epistemology: The Key Thinkers*, ed. Stephen Hetherington, Continuum, 2012. A clear survey of the Gettier literature suitable for undergraduates, covering the main cases and the major responses.

- Weatherson, Brian, "What Good Are Counterexamples?", *Philosophical Studies*, 115:1 (2003), pp. 1–31. Questions the methodology of thought-experiment-based counterexamples to philosophical analyses, with direct implications for how we should assess Gettier cases.

- Hetherington, Stephen, *Knowledge Puzzles: An Introduction to Epistemology*, Westview Press, 1996. Provides accessible analyses of the major Gettier cases and situates them within the broader structure of the theory of knowledge.

- Ichikawa, Jonathan Jenkins and Steup, Matthias, "The Analysis of Knowledge," in *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2018 (regularly updated). A comprehensive survey of the JTB analysis and the Gettier problem, keeping pace with current literature.

- Kaplan, Mark, "It's Not What You Know That Counts," *Journal of Philosophy*, 82:7 (1985), pp. 350–363. Challenges the centrality of the Gettier problem by arguing that practical and theoretical contexts require different standards; provocative for reassessing what the Gettier literature shows.

- Klein, Peter D., "A Proposed Definition of Propositional Knowledge," *Journal of Philosophy*, 68:16 (1971), pp. 471–482. Klein's defeasibility analysis; proposes that knowledge requires not just justified true belief but justification that is not defeated by any true proposition.

- Neta, Ram and Rohrbaugh, Guy, "Luminosity and the Safety of Knowledge," *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly*, 85:4 (2004), pp. 396–406. Examines the relationship between safety conditions and Gettier cases; useful for connecting the Gettier problem to the safety-based analysis in Sosa and Williamson.

- Sosa, Ernest, *A Virtue Epistemology: Apt Belief and Reflective Knowledge*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Reframes the Gettier problem within a virtue-theoretic framework; knowledge requires belief that is accurate because of the knower's intellectual virtue, not merely by luck.
