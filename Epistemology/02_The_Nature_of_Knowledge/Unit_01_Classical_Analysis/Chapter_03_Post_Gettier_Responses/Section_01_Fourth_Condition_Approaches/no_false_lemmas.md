# The No-False-Lemmas Approach to Gettier

The most immediate and natural response to Gettier's cases is the **no-false-lemmas** approach: add a fourth condition requiring that the subject's justification not pass through any false intermediate belief. This approach captures the immediate diagnosis of what's wrong with Gettier's original cases and has been defended by several philosophers, most notably Gilbert Harman.

## The Approach Stated

The no-false-lemmas (NFL) condition: S knows that P only if S's justification for P does not essentially rest on any false lemma.

More precisely: S's justification for P is a chain of reasoning. If any step in that chain is a false belief, and the justification essentially depends on that step, then S doesn't know P.

In Gettier's Case I: Smith's justification for "the man who will get the job has ten coins in his pocket" essentially depends on the false lemma that Jones will get the job. Since Jones doesn't get the job, the lemma is false. NFL blocks knowledge attribution.

In Gettier's Case II: Smith's justification essentially depends on the false lemma that Jones owns a Ford. NFL blocks knowledge attribution again.

## Harman's Formulation

Gilbert Harman developed one of the most sophisticated versions of the NFL approach. His condition: S knows that P if and only if there is no false lemma in any reasoning chain by which S's belief in P is supported. This is strengthened to handle cases where the false lemma can be excised.

Harman also distinguishes between knowledge and justified belief: you can have justified belief without knowledge, and the presence of false lemmas in the reasoning chain is one way justification fails to yield knowledge.

## Successes

The NFL approach succeeds with Gettier's original cases and with many variants. It has a natural motivation: reasoning from false premises is epistemically defective, and if the truth of the conclusion depends on the reasoning, then the false premise taints the knowledge. It preserves the internalist flavor of the JTB analysis while adding a constraint that rules out the troublesome cases.

## The Barn Façade Problem

The NFL approach fails for barn façade cases. Recall: you look at a real barn in Fake Barn County and form the belief that there is a barn. Your reasoning involves no false lemmas — your perceptual belief is directly formed from seeing the real barn.

Yet you don't seem to know there's a barn. The NFL condition is not violated, but knowledge still seems absent. This shows that false lemmas are sufficient for lack of knowledge in Gettier's original cases but are not necessary for Gettier-style failures in general.

## The Henry Case

A variant: suppose Henry is looking at a real barn and forms the belief "there is a barn." No false lemmas. But suppose Henry doesn't know that he's in Fake Barn County. If he did know, he would withhold judgment. He lacks a piece of relevant information that would defeat his knowledge.

This motivates a different approach: **defeasibility**. The issue is not false lemmas in the reasoning but the existence of true information that, if possessed, would defeat the justification. We turn to this next.

## The General Problem for Fourth Conditions

The NFL approach illustrates a general problem: any specific fourth condition will be adequate for some cases and inadequate for others. New Gettier cases are designed to satisfy whatever conditions are added. The NFL condition handles Gettier's original cases but misses environment-based cases. The proliferation of cases suggests that finding the right fourth condition requires a more fundamental understanding of what knowledge is — not just a list of conditions.
