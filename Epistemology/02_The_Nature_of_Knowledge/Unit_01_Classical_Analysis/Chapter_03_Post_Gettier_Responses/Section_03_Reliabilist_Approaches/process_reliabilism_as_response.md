# Process Reliabilism as a Response to Gettier

Process reliabilism — primarily associated with Alvin Goldman's later work, especially *Epistemology and Cognition* (1986) — provides an externalist response to the Gettier problem by replacing the justification condition with a reliability condition. Rather than asking whether the believer has good reasons, reliabilism asks whether the belief was produced by a reliable process.

## The Basic Reliabilist Analysis

S knows that P if and only if:
1. P is true
2. S believes that P
3. S's belief that P was produced by a reliable cognitive process (one that tends to produce true beliefs)
4. (Variants add further conditions, e.g., the absence of a reliable process that would have led to a different belief)

A reliable cognitive process is one that has a high rate of producing true beliefs across the range of inputs it receives. Perception under normal conditions is reliable; wishful thinking is not; valid deductive reasoning is reliable; astrology is not.

## How Reliabilism Handles Gettier Cases

**Original Gettier cases**: In Case I, Smith's belief formation passes through the false belief that Jones will get the job. The process involves making an inference from a false belief, which is not generally reliable. The process condition is not satisfied.

**Barn façade**: Henry forms his belief through perception, which is generally reliable. But perception in Fake Barn County is not reliable — it usually produces false beliefs (because of the façades). Reliabilism, with the right level of process individuation, can generate the verdict that Henry doesn't know.

The key question is how to *individuate* the process. Is the relevant process "vision" (reliable) or "vision in Fake Barn County" (unreliable)? This is the "generality problem" for reliabilism.

## The Generality Problem

Every token of a cognitive process can be described at many levels of generality:
- "Visual perception" (very general, very reliable)
- "Visual perception of medium-sized objects at close range" (somewhat more specific)
- "Visual perception of medium-sized objects at close range in Fake Barn County on Tuesday afternoons" (very specific, unreliable)

Reliabilism needs to specify which level of description is relevant. This is the **generality problem**: there is no principled way (from within the reliabilist framework alone) to determine which level of process individuation is correct.

Different levels give different verdicts, and reliabilism needs a principled choice between them to yield determinate answers.

## Virtues of Reliabilism

Despite the generality problem, reliabilism has significant virtues as a response to Gettier:

**It is externalist**: Whether a process is reliable is a matter of fact about the world, not something the believer needs to know or have access to. This means reliabilism can attribute knowledge to creatures (infants, animals, pre-reflective humans) whose beliefs are formed reliably but who can't reflect on the reliability of their processes.

**It connects knowledge to truth-tracking**: By requiring that the process reliably produce true beliefs, reliabilism ensures that knowledge isn't accidentally true — the reliability condition builds in a systematic (if fallible) connection to truth.

**It handles barn façade**: With appropriate process individuation, reliabilism can give the right verdict in barn façade cases, which the causal theory could not.

**It grounds epistemic norms**: If good epistemic processes are reliable ones, then epistemological recommendations (be skeptical of testimony from biased sources, seek corroborating evidence, use calibrated instruments) have a natural rationale: they increase reliability.

## Process Reliabilism and Justification

Goldman distinguished between applying reliabilism to knowledge and applying it to justification. Process reliabilism is primarily a theory of *epistemic justification*: a belief is justified if and only if it is produced by a reliable process.

This gives a fully externalist account of justification: whether your belief is justified depends on facts about the reliability of your belief-forming processes, not on what you can access from the inside. The Evil Demon problem is handled asymmetrically: you might be justified (if your processes are reliable) even though you can't tell from the inside whether they are.

The justification account feeds into the knowledge account: knowledge is justified (reliably produced) true belief, with appropriate conditions to rule out Gettier cases.
