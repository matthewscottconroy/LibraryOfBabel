# The Virtue-Theoretic Response to Gettier

Virtue epistemology — particularly in the work of Ernest Sosa — provides a different kind of response to the Gettier problem. Rather than adding conditions to JTB, virtue epistemology reconceptualizes what knowledge requires at a more fundamental level: knowledge is belief that is *true because of* the believer's cognitive competence.

## The Credit Intuition

The starting intuition: in knowledge, the believer deserves *credit* for the truth of their belief. When you form a true belief through careful reasoning, attentive perception, or reliable memory, the truth is to your credit — it's because of your cognitive achievement, not despite it or independently of it.

In Gettier cases, the believer does not deserve credit for the truth of their belief:
- Smith believes "the man who gets the job has ten coins" because of his justified reasoning about Jones — but the belief is true because of himself, not Jones. The truth is not to his credit.
- Henry believes "there's a barn" — and he's right — but in Fake Barn County, the truth is environmentally lucky, not to his credit.

The credit intuition: knowledge requires that the accuracy of the belief be *attributable to* the believer's cognitive competences, not to luck.

## Sosa's Analysis: Apt Belief

Ernest Sosa articulates the virtue-theoretic analysis through his concept of *apt belief*. A belief is:
- **Accurate**: True
- **Adroit**: Manifesting the believer's cognitive competence
- **Apt**: Accurate *because* adroit (accurate through adroitness)

The key condition is aptness: the accuracy must result from the exercise of cognitive competence. In Gettier cases, the belief may be accurate and even adroit (formed through an exercise of competence), but the accuracy is not *due to* the adroitness — there is a gap between the competence and the truth.

## The Archery Analogy

Sosa uses an archery analogy to illuminate the structure. An archer's shot is:
- Accurate if it hits the target.
- Adroit if it manifests the archer's skill.
- Apt if it hits the target *through* the skill.

A skilled archer who shoots accurately but in a fierce wind — where the wind just happens to correct what would otherwise have been an error — has an accurate and adroit shot, but not an apt one. The accuracy is not due to the skill.

In Gettier cases, the believer is like the archer whose shot reaches the target by environmental luck, not through the exercise of skill alone.

## Animal and Reflective Knowledge

Sosa distinguishes between two levels of knowledge:

**Animal knowledge**: First-order reliable belief formed by the exercise of cognitive faculties. Children and animals have animal knowledge — their perceptions and memories reliably track truth, and their beliefs are apt in the relevant sense.

**Reflective knowledge**: Knowledge accompanied by *meta-coherence* — the believer knows not just the first-order facts but also that their belief-forming processes are reliable, that their evidence is adequate, that they are in a good epistemic position. This is knowledge from a reflective standpoint.

Reflective knowledge is not merely more animal knowledge — it involves a different kind of cognitive achievement: seeing one's own beliefs as fitting into a broader, coherent picture of one's epistemic situation.

## Virtue Epistemology and the AAA Structure

The apt belief framework (Accurate, Adroit, Apt) has been extended by John Greco, Duncan Pritchard, and others to handle different kinds of Gettier cases:

- **Apt belief** handles basic cases where the belief is true through the exercise of competence.
- **Apt reflection** handles cases of reflective knowledge.
- **Safe belief** (not easily false) handles environmental luck cases like barn façade.

The integration of these conditions into a unified virtue-theoretic account remains an active research project.

## The Significance for the Analysis of Knowledge

Virtue epistemology represents a shift in the unit of epistemological analysis. Rather than analyzing the conditions on the *belief*, it analyzes the conditions on the *believer* — their competences, their cognitive character, their epistemic position. This shift from property of belief to property of believer is one of the most significant developments in post-Gettier epistemology.
