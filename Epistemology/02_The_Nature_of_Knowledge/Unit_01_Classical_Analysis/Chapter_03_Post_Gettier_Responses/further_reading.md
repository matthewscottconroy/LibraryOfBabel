## Primary Sources

- Nozick, Robert, *Philosophical Explanations*, Harvard University Press, 1981. Chapter 3 develops the sensitivity condition for knowledge (if p were false, S would not believe p) as part of a broader subjunctivist epistemology; the source for tracking theories of knowledge.

- Goldman, Alvin I., "What Is Justified Belief?", in *Justification and Knowledge*, ed. George Pappas, D. Reidel, 1979, pp. 1–23. The canonical statement of reliabilism: justified belief is belief produced by a reliable belief-forming process; foundational for the externalist tradition in post-Gettier epistemology.

- Harman, Gilbert, *Thought*, Princeton University Press, 1973. Develops the "no false lemmas" and defeasibility approaches to the Gettier problem; an important early contribution that influenced subsequent defeasibility analyses.

- Williamson, Timothy, *Knowledge and Its Limits*, Oxford University Press, 2000. The foundational text of the knowledge-first movement; argues against analyzing knowledge and for treating it as a primitive mental state; includes the safety condition and the argument against luminosity.

- Sosa, Ernest, *A Virtue Epistemology: Apt Belief and Reflective Knowledge*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Develops the virtue-theoretic safety-based account: knowledge is apt belief, belief that is accurate because of the knower's competence; Vol. 1 of Sosa's two-volume virtue epistemology.

- Greco, John, *Achieving Knowledge: A Virtue-Theoretic Account of Epistemic Normativity*, Cambridge University Press, 2010. Defends an agent-reliabilist virtue epistemology; knowledge requires that true belief be the product of an intellectual virtue, providing a response to Gettier cases.

- Lehrer, Keith and Paxson, Thomas, "Knowledge: Undefeated Justified True Belief," *Journal of Philosophy*, 66:8 (1969), pp. 225–237. The classic defeasibility analysis: knowledge is justified true belief that is not defeated by any true proposition.

- Klein, Peter D., "A Proposed Definition of Propositional Knowledge," *Journal of Philosophy*, 68:16 (1971), pp. 471–482. An alternative defeasibility analysis; knowledge requires that there be no true defeater that would undermine the believer's justification.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Steup, Matthias, "The Analysis of Knowledge," in *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, ed. Edward N. Zalta, 2018. An authoritative survey of post-Gettier analyses, covering sensitivity, safety, reliabilism, virtue epistemology, and knowledge-first approaches.

- Alston, William P., *Epistemic Justification: Essays in the Theory of Knowledge*, Cornell University Press, 1989. A collection of influential essays on justification, reliability, and the internal/external distinction; important for situating Goldman's reliabilism in the broader epistemological landscape.

- Greco, John, "Agent Reliabilism," *Philosophical Perspectives*, 13 (1999), pp. 273–296. Defends a virtue-theoretic reliabilism in which the relevant reliable processes are the stable intellectual traits of the agent rather than merely local belief-forming mechanisms.

- Sosa, Ernest, *Knowledge in Perspective: Selected Essays in Epistemology*, Cambridge University Press, 1991. Collects Sosa's foundational papers on virtue epistemology and the safety condition; traces the development from the "intellectual virtues" approach to the apt/safe distinction.

- Zagzebski, Linda, *Virtues of the Mind: An Inquiry into the Nature of Virtue and the Ethical Foundations of Knowledge*, Cambridge University Press, 1996. Motivates virtue epistemology as the only systematic escape from the Gettier problem; connects intellectual virtues to the Aristotelian tradition.

- BonJour, Laurence and Sosa, Ernest, *Epistemic Justification: Internalism vs. Externalism, Foundations vs. Virtues*, Blackwell, 2003. A sustained dialogue between internalism (BonJour) and virtue epistemology (Sosa); useful for understanding the internalism/externalism dimension of post-Gettier epistemology.

- Pritchard, Duncan, *Epistemic Luck*, Oxford University Press, 2005. A systematic treatment of the role of luck in knowledge; distinguishes varieties of epistemic luck (veritic, reflective, environmental, intervening) and evaluates safety and sensitivity conditions against each type.

- Conee, Earl and Feldman, Richard, *Evidentialism: Essays in Epistemology*, Oxford University Press, 2004. Defends an evidentialist response to Gettier and against reliabilism; argues that epistemic justification is determined solely by the evidence available to the believer.

- Turri, John, "Is Knowledge Justified True Belief?", *Synthese*, 184:3 (2011), pp. 247–259. Presents experimental philosophy data on how ordinary speakers respond to Gettier cases, bearing on whether post-Gettier analyses track an intuitive concept of knowledge.
