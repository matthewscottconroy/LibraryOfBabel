## Primary Sources

- Russell, Bertrand, *The Problems of Philosophy*, Oxford University Press, 1912. Chapter 13 distinguishes knowledge of truths from knowledge of things, situating propositional knowledge within a broader epistemological taxonomy; Russell's elegant presentation remains the best introduction.

- Frege, Gottlob, "On Sense and Reference (Über Sinn und Bedeutung)," trans. Max Black, in *Translations from the Philosophical Writings of Gottlob Frege*, eds. Geach and Black, Blackwell, 1952. The foundational paper for the theory of propositions as objects of knowledge; the distinction between sense and reference bears directly on the de dicto/de re distinction in the theory of knowledge.

- Quine, W. V. O., "Two Dogmas of Empiricism," *Philosophical Review*, 60:1 (1951), pp. 20–43. Challenges the analytic/synthetic distinction and the notion of meaning that grounds the theory of propositions; relevant to the question of what the objects of propositional knowledge are.

- Lewis, David, "Attitudes De Dicto and De Se," *Philosophical Review*, 88:4 (1979), pp. 513–543. Argues that belief and knowledge states are not best analyzed as relations to propositions but as self-locating attitudes; introduces the de se/de dicto distinction for propositional attitudes.

- King, Jeffrey C., *The Nature and Structure of Content*, Oxford University Press, 2007. A recent systematic treatment of propositional content that engages the Fregean and Russellian traditions; argues that propositions are structured entities composed of objects, properties, and relations.

- Soames, Scott, *Propositions*, Princeton University Press, 2010. Defends a cognitive-act theory of propositions in which propositions are types of cognitive acts rather than abstract objects; challenges both the Fregean and possible-worlds approaches.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Salmon, Nathan, *Frege's Puzzle*, MIT Press, 1986. Examines the puzzle of informative identity statements and its implications for the theory of propositional content and propositional attitudes; a major contribution to the philosophy of language that bears directly on the de dicto/de re distinction.

- Chalmers, David J., *The Conscious Mind: In Search of a Fundamental Theory*, Oxford University Press, 1996. Chapter 2's two-dimensional semantics distinguishes primary and secondary intensions for propositions, with implications for the epistemology of a priori and a posteriori knowledge.

- Stalnaker, Robert, *Our Knowledge of the Internal World*, Oxford University Press, 2008. Develops the possible-worlds semantics for propositional content and applies it to questions about self-knowledge and the objects of belief and knowledge.

- Schiffer, Stephen, *The Things We Mean*, Oxford University Press, 2003. A systematic treatment of propositions, properties, and other "pleonastic" abstract entities; examines what we commit ourselves to when we say that knowledge has propositional objects.

- Kripke, Saul, *Naming and Necessity*, Harvard University Press, 1980. The arguments for a posteriori necessity and its implications for propositional knowledge are directly relevant to the de re/de dicto distinction and the epistemology of necessary truths.

- Plantinga, Alvin, *The Nature of Necessity*, Clarendon Press, 1974. Develops a possible-worlds account of propositions and their truth conditions; foundational for understanding the modal dimension of propositional knowledge.

- Sosa, Ernest, *Judgment and Agency*, Oxford University Press, 2015. Examines the relationship between propositional attitudes and epistemic agency; argues that judgment (as a full propositional commitment) plays a distinctive role in knowledge that mere belief does not.

- Bach, Kent, "The Myth of Conventional Implicature," *Linguistics and Philosophy*, 18:3 (1995), pp. 327–366. Argues that propositions expressed and propositions communicated often diverge, with implications for what the objects of testimonial knowledge are.

- Zimmerman, Michael J., *The Nature of Intrinsic Value*, Rowman & Littlefield, 2001. While focused on value theory, includes a relevant analysis of propositional attitudes and their objects that illuminates the structure of knowledge relations.

- Speaks, Jeff, "Theories of Meaning," in *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, ed. Edward N. Zalta, 2021. An authoritative survey of propositional theories of meaning and their implications for the analysis of propositional knowledge; an excellent secondary reference.
