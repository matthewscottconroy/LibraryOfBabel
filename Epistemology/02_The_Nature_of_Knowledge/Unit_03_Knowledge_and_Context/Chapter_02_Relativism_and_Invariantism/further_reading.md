## Primary Sources

- Hawthorne, John, *Knowledge and Lotteries*, Oxford University Press, 2004. The canonical defence of subject-sensitive invariantism (SSI); argues that the truth conditions of "S knows p" include S's practical interests, locating context-sensitivity in the subject rather than the attributor.

- Stanley, Jason, *Knowledge and Practical Interests*, Oxford University Press, 2005. A systematic defence of SSI (which Stanley calls "interest-relative invariantism"); argues that what counts as an alternative to what one knows is affected by one's practical situation.

- MacFarlane, John, "The Assessment Sensitivity of Knowledge Attributions," in *Oxford Studies in Epistemology*, Vol. 1, eds. Tamar Gendler and John Hawthorne, Oxford University Press, 2005. The foundational paper for assessor relativism; argues that knowledge attributions are true or false relative to a context of assessment, not just a context of utterance.

- MacFarlane, John, *Assessment Sensitivity: Relative Truth and Its Applications*, Oxford University Press, 2014. The full book-length development of relativism for epistemic modals, knowledge attributions, and taste predicates; the most rigorous treatment of the relativist position.

- Fantl, Jeremy and McGrath, Matthew, *Knowledge in an Uncertain World*, Oxford University Press, 2009. Develops "pragmatic encroachment" as a form of SSI; argues that whether S knows p depends on what S's evidence warrants her to do, not merely on what S believes and how reliably.

- Weatherson, Brian, "Can We Do Without Pragmatic Encroachment?", *Philosophical Perspectives*, 19:1 (2005), pp. 417–443. A critical examination of pragmatic encroachment; argues that the data motivating SSI can be explained without treating practical interests as partly constitutive of knowledge.

- Cappelen, Herman and Hawthorne, John, *Relativism and Monadic Truth*, Oxford University Press, 2009. A critical assessment of MacFarlane's relativism and related positions; defends a form of invariantism against both contextualism and relativism.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Ichikawa, Jonathan Jenkins (ed.), *The Routledge Handbook of Epistemic Contextualism*, Routledge, 2017. A comprehensive reference that covers contextualism, SSI, and relativism in a single volume; includes entries on all major positions and their interrelations.

- Blome-Tillmann, Michael, *Knowledge and Presuppositions*, Oxford University Press, 2014. Develops an alternative contextualist account based on presupposition theory rather than the Lewis/DeRose relevant alternatives framework; responds to objections about the semantics of knowledge attributions.

- Sosa, Ernest, "The Relevant Alternative Explanation of Knowledge," in *The Blackwell Guide to Epistemology*, eds. John Greco and Ernest Sosa, Blackwell, 1999. Situates the relevant alternatives and contextualist tradition within the broader landscape of epistemological approaches.

- Neta, Ram, "Contextualism and the Problem of the External World," *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research*, 66:1 (2003), pp. 1–31. Examines whether contextualism genuinely solves the problem of the external world or merely relocates it; relevant for assessing the anti-skeptical ambitions of contextualism and SSI.

- Brown, Jessica, "Subject-Sensitive Invariantism and the Knowledge Norm for Practical Reasoning," *Noûs*, 42:2 (2008), pp. 167–189. A careful critical examination of the interaction between SSI and the knowledge norm for action; raises several objections to Hawthorne's and Stanley's positions.

- Rickless, Samuel C., "The Overprotective Knowledge Norm," *Philosophical Studies*, 125:2 (2005), pp. 175–194. Argues that the knowledge norm for assertion and practical reasoning—if S knows p, S may act on p—is too demanding and generates counterintuitive results when combined with SSI.

- Pritchard, Duncan, "Relevant Alternatives, Contextualism and Closure," *Erkenntnis*, 69:3 (2008), pp. 291–316. Examines the closure properties of the relevant alternatives framework under both contextualist and SSI interpretations; argues that neither position can fully accommodate the closure intuition.

- Kompa, Nikola, "The Context Sensitivity of Knowledge Ascriptions," *Grazer Philosophische Studien*, 64:1 (2002), pp. 1–18. An early survey of the contextualism debate from a linguistic perspective; examines the evidence for treating "knows" as a context-sensitive expression.

- Cohen, Stewart, "Skepticism and Everyday Knowledge Attributions," in *Skepticism: A Contemporary Reader*, eds. Keith DeRose and Ted Warfield, Oxford University Press, 1999. Applies the contextualist framework to the relationship between everyday knowledge claims and philosophical skepticism; a useful overview of the anti-skeptical strategy.
