## Primary Sources

- Endel Tulving, *Elements of Episodic Memory*, Oxford University Press, 1983. The foundational text for the episodic/semantic distinction, arguing that episodic memory for specific past events is a distinct cognitive system from semantic memory for general knowledge.
- Endel Tulving, "Episodic Memory: From Mind to Brain," *Annual Review of Psychology* 53, 2002. Tulving's mature account of episodic memory as "mental time travel" and its neurological basis, with significant philosophical implications.
- C. B. Martin and Max Deutscher, "Remembering," *Philosophical Review* 75, 1966. Introduces and defends the causal theory of memory, arguing that genuine remembering requires an appropriate causal chain connecting the original experience to the subsequent memory state.
- Frederic Bartlett, *Remembering: A Study in Experimental and Social Psychology*, Cambridge University Press, 1932. Classic psychological work demonstrating through schema experiments that memory is reconstructive rather than reproductive, influencing both cognitive science and epistemology.
- Elizabeth Loftus and John Palmer, "Reconstruction of Automobile Destruction: An Example of the Interaction Between Language and Memory," *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior* 13, 1974. Seminal experimental demonstration that post-event information systematically distorts memory reports.
- Elizabeth Loftus, *Eyewitness Testimony*, Harvard University Press, 1979. Comprehensive treatment of the psychological research on memory distortion, with philosophical and legal implications.
- Bertrand Russell, *The Analysis of Mind*, Allen & Unwin, 1921. Contains the classic philosophical analysis of memory as involving a belief, a feeling of familiarity, and an image — and raises the skeptical possibility (later developed as the 5-minute hypothesis) that memory may not track the past at all.
- Henri Bergson, *Matter and Memory*, trans. N. M. Paul and W. S. Palmer, Zone Books, 1991. Influential early phenomenological account distinguishing habit memory from pure memory of the past.
- John Sutton, *Philosophy and Memory Traces: Descartes to Connectionism*, Cambridge University Press, 1998. Historical and philosophical study of the memory trace concept, from early modern philosophy through contemporary cognitive science.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Sven Bernecker, *The Metaphysics of Memory*, Springer, 2008. Systematic philosophical analysis of memory, covering the causal theory, the nature of memory traces, and the relationship between memory and personal identity.
- Sven Bernecker and Kourken Michaelian (eds.), *The Routledge Handbook of Philosophy of Memory*, Routledge, 2017. Comprehensive reference covering all major topics in the philosophy of memory, from metaphysics to epistemology to cognitive science.
- Kourken Michaelian, *Mental Time Travel: Episodic Memory and Our Knowledge of the Personal Past*, MIT Press, 2016. Defends a simulation theory of episodic memory, arguing that episodic memory is a form of constructive mental time travel.
- Daniel Schacter, *Searching for Memory: The Brain, the Mind, and the Past*, Basic Books, 1996. Accessible survey of cognitive neuroscience research on memory, including the "seven sins of memory."
- Murray Shanahan, "Memory, Perception, and the Causal Theory of Reference," *Philosophy* 61, 1986. Examines causal theories of memory in relation to causal theories of reference.
- Don Locke, *Memory*, Macmillan, 1971. Still one of the most careful philosophical analyses of the concept of memory, distinguishing remembering from other forms of memory and examining the role of images.
- Norman Malcolm, *Memory and Mind*, Cornell University Press, 1977. Wittgensteinian approach to memory, challenging the view that memory essentially involves inner mental representations.
- Sydney Shoemaker, "Personal Identity: A Materialist's Account," in Shoemaker and Swinburne, *Personal Identity*, Blackwell, 1984. Examines the connections between memory and personal identity, including the role of quasi-memory.
