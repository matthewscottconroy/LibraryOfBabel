## Primary Sources

- Nelson Goodman, *Fact, Fiction, and Forecast*, Harvard University Press, 1955. Introduces the concept of reflective equilibrium in the context of inductive logic, arguing that logical principles and particular judgments must be brought into coherent alignment.
- John Rawls, *A Theory of Justice*, Harvard University Press, 1971. Develops and popularizes reflective equilibrium as a general method for moral and philosophical theory construction, highly influential in epistemology.
- Jonathan Weinberg, Shaun Nichols, and Stephen Stich, "Normativity and Epistemic Intuitions," *Philosophical Topics* 29, 2001. The landmark experimental philosophy paper claiming that epistemic intuitions vary systematically across cultural groups, challenging the evidential weight of intuitions in epistemology.
- George Bealer, "Intuition and the Autonomy of Philosophy," in Michael DePaul and William Ramsey (eds.), *Rethinking Intuition*, Rowman & Littlefield, 1998. Defends the evidential status of rational intuitions against naturalist challenges.
- Timothy Williamson, *The Philosophy of Philosophy*, Blackwell, 2007. Argues that philosophical thought experiments employ a species of counterfactual reasoning rather than a distinctive faculty of intuition, defending the evidential value of philosophical methodology without positing special intuitive capacities.
- Alvin Goldman and Joel Pust, "Philosophical Theory and Intuitional Evidence," in DePaul and Ramsey (eds.), *Rethinking Intuition*, Rowman & Littlefield, 1998. Examines the epistemic status of intuitions as evidence in philosophy, arguing for a moderate account.
- Herman Cappelen, *Philosophy Without Intuitions*, Oxford University Press, 2012. Argues that mainstream analytic philosophy does not in fact rely on intuitions as evidence, directly challenging the experimental philosophy critique.
- Ernest Sosa, "Intuitions: Their Nature and Epistemic Efficacy," *Grazer Philosophische Studien* 74, 2007. Careful analysis of intuitions as intellectual seemings with prima facie evidential weight.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Michael DePaul and William Ramsey (eds.), *Rethinking Intuition*, Rowman & Littlefield, 1998. Essential anthology covering the nature and epistemic role of intuitions across multiple philosophical subdisciplines.
- Joshua Knobe and Shaun Nichols (eds.), *Experimental Philosophy*, Oxford University Press, 2008. Collects the foundational papers of the experimental philosophy movement challenging the reliance on intuitions.
- Joachim Horvath and Timothy Williamson (eds.), *Philosophical Methodology: The Armchair or the Laboratory?*, Routledge, 2013. Balanced collection presenting multiple perspectives on the methodological debate sparked by experimental philosophy.
- Stathis Psillos and Martin Curd (eds.), *The Routledge Companion to Philosophy of Science*, Routledge, 2008. Contains entries relevant to the use of thought experiments in science and their relation to philosophical intuitions.
- Frank Jackson, *From Metaphysics to Ethics*, Oxford University Press, 1998. Defends conceptual analysis and the evidential role of intuitions in philosophy against naturalist critiques.
- Jennifer Nagel, "Epistemic Intuitions," *Philosophy Compass* 2, 2007. Accessible overview of the role of intuitions in epistemology and the challenges posed by experimental philosophy.
- Dominic Murphy and Robert Stalnaker (eds.), *Conceptual Analysis and Philosophical Naturalism*, MIT Press, 2009. Contains important papers on the relationship between a priori conceptual analysis and empirical inquiry.
- Stephen Stich, *The Fragmentation of Reason*, MIT Press, 1990. Naturalistic challenge to the idea that philosophical intuitions track anything independently significant.
- Brian Weatherson, "What Good Are Counterexamples?" *Philosophical Studies* 115, 2003. Examines the methodological role of intuitive counterexamples in philosophical theorizing.
