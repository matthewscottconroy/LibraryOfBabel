# Further Reading: Coherentism

## Primary Sources

- W.V.O. Quine, "Two Dogmas of Empiricism," *The Philosophical Review* 60, 1951, pp. 20–43. A landmark paper arguing against the analytic-synthetic distinction and for a holism in which our beliefs form an interconnected web, none of which faces the tribunal of experience independently.

- W.V.O. Quine and J.S. Ullian, *The Web of Belief*, Random House, 1970. A short, accessible defense of the holistic view that beliefs are justified by their fit within a broader system, with attention to the virtues of simplicity, conservatism, and explanatory power.

- Otto Neurath, "Protocol Sentences," in *Logical Positivism*, ed. A.J. Ayer, Free Press, 1959. Contains the boat metaphor — we rebuild the ship of knowledge plank by plank while remaining afloat — which became the canonical image for coherentist epistemology.

- Laurence BonJour, *The Structure of Empirical Knowledge*, Harvard University Press, 1985. The most rigorous and sustained defense of coherentism in analytic epistemology, developing the account of coherence in terms of systematic interconnection and arguing that it can ground justification without foundations.

- Nicholas Rescher, *The Coherence Theory of Truth*, Oxford University Press, 1973. Defends a coherentist theory of truth as well as justification, exploring the relationship between the two and the role of practical considerations in belief-system selection.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Jonathan Dancy, *Introduction to Contemporary Epistemology*, Blackwell, 1985. Chapter 6 is one of the most accessible treatments of coherentism and the isolation objection, situating both within the broader debate about epistemic structure.

- Ernest Sosa, "The Raft and the Pyramid," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 5, 1980, pp. 3–25. Presses the coherentist on how a belief system can be both internally coherent and epistemically anchored to the external world, offering the foundationalist alternative.

- Keith Lehrer, *Theory of Knowledge*, Westview Press, 1990. Develops a sophisticated "coherentist" account based on the concept of a "truth-connected" acceptance system, with particular attention to the role of personal probability and the defeating of competing hypotheses.

- Keith Lehrer, *Self-Trust: A Study of Reason, Knowledge, and Autonomy*, Oxford University Press, 1997. Extends Lehrer's coherentist framework by emphasizing the role of self-trust in grounding justification within a coherent system.

- Susan Haack, *Evidence and Inquiry*, Blackwell, 1993. Offers a sustained critique of pure coherentism while defending foundherentism, a hybrid view that preserves coherentism's insight that justification is a system-wide property.

- Paul Moser, *Empirical Justification*, D. Reidel, 1985. Contains a critique of BonJour's coherentism on the grounds that it cannot escape the isolation objection without smuggling in a foundationalist element.

- Michael Williams, *Unnatural Doubts*, Blackwell, 1991. Challenges the presuppositions that make coherentism seem necessary, arguing that the isolation problem reveals not just a flaw in coherentism but a flaw in the theoretical framework that generates the regress in the first place.

- Catherine Elgin, *Considered Judgment*, Princeton University Press, 1996. Defends "reflective equilibrium" as a coherentist epistemological method in which beliefs and epistemic commitments are mutually adjusted to achieve systematic fit.

- BonJour, Laurence, "The Coherence Theory of Empirical Knowledge," *Philosophical Studies* 30, 1976, pp. 281–312. An important early statement of BonJour's coherentism that anticipates many of the moves developed in his 1985 book.

- John Pollock, *Contemporary Theories of Knowledge*, Rowman & Littlefield, 1986. Chapters on coherentism provide a critical survey and press the "alternative coherent systems" objection with particular sharpness.

- Erik Olsson, *Against Coherence*, Oxford University Press, 2005. Uses formal probabilistic tools to argue that coherence is not truth-conducive, mounting a systematic challenge to the coherentist claim that more coherent belief sets are more likely to be true.

- Erik Olsson, "The Coherence Theory of Knowledge," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2017 ed. A comprehensive and reliable survey of coherentism's main variants and the objections they face, suitable as supplementary reading.

- Crispin Wright, "Facts and Certainty," *Proceedings of the British Academy* 71, 1985, pp. 429–472. Explores the relationship between holism, certainty, and the role of "entitlements" that are not themselves beliefs, with implications for the coherentist approach to epistemic foundations.

- Timothy Williamson, *The Philosophy of Philosophy*, Blackwell, 2007. Chapters on reflective equilibrium and coherentism in philosophical methodology, challenging the idea that coherence-based methods are adequate for epistemological inquiry.
