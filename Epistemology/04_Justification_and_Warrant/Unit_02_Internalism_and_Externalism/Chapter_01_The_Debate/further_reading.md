# Further Reading: The Internalism/Externalism Debate

## Primary Sources

- Alvin Goldman, "What Is Justified Belief?" in *Justification and Knowledge*, ed. George Pappas, D. Reidel, 1979. The paper that launched process reliabilism and defined the externalist position: justification is a function of the reliability of the cognitive process that produces a belief, not of the believer's reflective access to that process.

- Roderick Chisholm, *Theory of Knowledge*, 3rd ed., Prentice-Hall, 1989. The paradigm internalist position: epistemic concepts are defined by reference to what the believer can discover through careful reflection, with no essential appeal to factors external to the mind.

- Laurence BonJour, "Externalist Theories of Empirical Knowledge," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 5, 1980, pp. 53–73. An early and influential critique of reliabilism and externalism generally, arguing that externalist accounts fail to capture the "guiding" function of epistemic justification.

- William Alston, *Epistemic Justification: Essays in the Theory of Knowledge*, Cornell University Press, 1989. Develops a subtle position distinguishing different "desiderata" for justification and arguing that different internalist and externalist conditions serve different legitimate epistemic purposes.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Earl Conee and Richard Feldman, "Internalism Defended," in *Epistemology: Internalism and Externalism*, ed. Hilary Kornblith, Blackwell, 2001. A clear defense of mentalist internalism — the view that justification supervenes on the mental states of the believer — with responses to externalist objections.

- Alvin Goldman, "Internalism Exposed," *Journal of Philosophy* 96, 1999, pp. 271–293. Goldman's critical response to internalism, arguing that it cannot fulfill the practical and theoretical functions epistemologists assign to the concept of justification.

- Richard Fumerton, *Metaepistemology and Skepticism*, Rowman & Littlefield, 1995. Defends access internalism — the view that justification requires the believer to have cognitive access to the factors that make a belief justified — against externalist rivals.

- John Greco, "Externalism and the Skeptical Threat," *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 55, 1995, pp. 843–864. Examines the relationship between externalist epistemology and the problem of skepticism, arguing that externalism does and should dissolve rather than solve skeptical puzzles.

- Hilary Kornblith, ed., *Epistemology: Internalism and Externalism*, Blackwell, 2001. An excellent anthology collecting the principal papers on both sides of the debate, including contributions by BonJour, Goldman, Conee, Feldman, and Alston.

- Stewart Cohen, "Justification and Truth," *Philosophical Studies* 46, 1984, pp. 279–295. An early paper arguing that the new evil demon intuition (that victims of an evil demon have justified beliefs) is a strong reason to prefer internalism over reliabilism.

- Juan Comesaña and Matthew McGrath, "Having False Reasons," in *Epistemic Justification and the Skeptical Threat*, Oxford University Press, 2016. A sharp analysis of how the internalism/externalism debate ramifies into the debate about how false beliefs function as reasons.

- Michael Bergmann, *Justification without Awareness*, Oxford University Press, 2006. Defends externalism against access internalism, arguing that beliefs can be justified without the believer being aware of what makes them justified.

- Ernest Sosa, "Reflective Knowledge in the Best Circles," *Journal of Philosophy* 94, 1997, pp. 410–430. Develops a virtue-theoretic position that occupies middle ground between pure internalism and externalism, with attention to the role of reflective meta-justification.

- Matthias Steup and Ernest Sosa, eds., *Contemporary Debates in Epistemology*, Blackwell, 2005. The internalism/externalism section contains paired papers by leading figures on each side, ideal for a structured reading of the debate.

- James Pryor, "Highlights of Recent Epistemology," *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 52, 2001, pp. 95–124. A reliable overview of the internalism/externalism landscape suitable for orienting students to the main positions and arguments.

- Jonathan Vogel, "Reliabilism Leveled," *Journal of Philosophy* 97, 2000, pp. 602–623. Presses objections to reliabilism that are relevant to the framing of the internalism/externalism debate, particularly the problem of how reliabilism handles bootstrapping and easy knowledge.

- Anthony Brueckner, "The Scope of Skepticism," *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 52, 1992, pp. 59–78. Examines how the new evil demon thought experiment should be used to adjudicate between internalism and externalism.

- Susanna Goldberg, *Relying on Others*, Oxford University Press, 2010. Extends the internalism/externalism debate to cover social sources of justification — testimony, peer disagreement, and collective epistemic practices.
