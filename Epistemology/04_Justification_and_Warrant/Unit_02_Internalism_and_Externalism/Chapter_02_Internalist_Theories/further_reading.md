# Further Reading: Internalist Theories

## Primary Sources

- Roderick Chisholm, *Theory of Knowledge*, 3rd ed., Prentice-Hall, 1989. The canonical systematic internalist epistemology, in which all epistemic concepts are defined deontologically by reference to what a reflective believer can determine about the epistemic status of her own doxastic states.

- Roderick Chisholm, *The Foundations of Knowing*, University of Minnesota Press, 1982. A collection of essays developing Chisholm's internalist foundationalism, with particularly important treatments of the self-presenting nature of basic beliefs.

- Earl Conee and Richard Feldman, *Evidentialism: Essays in Epistemology*, Oxford University Press, 2004. The definitive collection of papers developing and defending evidentialism — the view that a belief is justified if and only if it fits the believer's evidence — which is a sophisticated mentalist internalist position.

- Richard Fumerton, *Metaepistemology and Skepticism*, Rowman & Littlefield, 1995. Develops the most rigorous acquaintance-based internalism, arguing that non-inferential justification requires direct acquaintance with the truth-making fact and the relation between that fact and one's belief.

- Richard Fumerton, *Epistemology*, Blackwell, 2006. A comprehensive textbook developing Fumerton's acquaintance-based internalism and critically assessing alternatives, with sustained engagement with reliabilism and other externalist views.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Earl Conee and Richard Feldman, "Internalism Defended," in *Epistemology: Internalism and Externalism*, ed. Hilary Kornblith, Blackwell, 2001. The clearest statement of why the new evil demon intuition and other considerations strongly support mentalist internalism over externalism.

- Michael Bergmann, *Justification without Awareness*, Oxford University Press, 2006. The most systematic contemporary challenge to access internalism, pressing the regress argument against the claim that justification requires the believer to have reflective access to their justifiers.

- William Alston, "Internalism and Externalism in Epistemology," in *Epistemic Justification*, Cornell University Press, 1989. A careful taxonomic essay distinguishing multiple versions of internalism and showing which are defensible and which collapse under scrutiny.

- James Pryor, "Highlights of Recent Epistemology," *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 52, 2001, pp. 95–124. A survey article that situates the internalism debate within the broader contemporary landscape and explains the appeal of different internalist positions.

- Laurence BonJour and Ernest Sosa, *Epistemic Justification: Internalism vs. Externalism, Foundations vs. Virtues*, Blackwell, 2003. A paired debate format, with BonJour defending internalist foundationalism and Sosa defending virtue externalism; each responds to the other's position.

- Stewart Cohen, "Justification and Truth," *Philosophical Studies* 46, 1984, pp. 279–295. Develops the new evil demon case as the strongest argument for internalism and argues that reliabilism fails to accommodate the insight it captures.

- Peter Fumerton, "The Challenge of Refutation," in *Philosophical Studies* 85, 1997. Engages the metajustification regress — the worry that internalism generates an infinite regress of justification for the claim that one's evidence justifies one's belief.

- Hilary Kornblith, *Epistemology: Internalism and Externalism*, Blackwell, 2001. An anthology with excellent editorial framing of the debate, collecting the most important papers from both sides in one volume.

- Anthony Brueckner, "Exploring Access Internalism," *Philosophical Studies* 146, 2009, pp. 323–327. A careful examination of what "access" means in access internalism and which versions avoid the regress objection.

- John Greco, "Agent Reliabilism," *Philosophical Perspectives* 13, 1999, pp. 273–296. Develops a virtue-reliabilist position that incorporates internalist elements — the agent's character and perspective — while remaining broadly externalist.

- Jonathan Dancy, *Introduction to Contemporary Epistemology*, Blackwell, 1985. Chapters 7–9 survey internalist positions and their difficulties with particular attention to the deontological tradition descending from Chisholm.

- Selim Berker, "The Rejection of Epistemic Consequentialism," *Philosophical Issues* 23, 2013, pp. 363–387. Argues for an internalist-friendly view on which epistemic norms are "agent-focused" rather than consequentialist, with implications for how internalist deontology should be understood.

- Ralph Wedgwood, *The Nature of Normativity*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Develops a broadly internalist account of normativity — including epistemic normativity — grounded in the essential connection between normative judgments and a subject's perspective.

- Juan Comesaña, "A Well-Founded Solution to the Generality Problem," *Philosophical Studies* 129, 2006, pp. 27–47. Addresses the generality problem for reliabilism in a way that incorporates internalist elements about how belief-types are to be individuated.
