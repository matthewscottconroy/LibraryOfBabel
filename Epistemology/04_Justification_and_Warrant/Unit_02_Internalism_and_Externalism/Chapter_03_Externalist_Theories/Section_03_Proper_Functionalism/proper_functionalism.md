# Proper Functionalism: Plantinga on Warrant

Alvin Plantinga's *Warrant* trilogy (*Warrant: The Current Debate*, *Warrant and Proper Function*, and *Warranted Christian Belief*) develops an alternative externalist account that avoids some problems of reliabilism while addressing skeptical scenarios in a distinctive way. Plantinga introduces the term "warrant" to replace "justification" — a deliberate terminological choice to distance his account from the deontological connotations of "justification" while still capturing what makes a belief rise to the level of knowledge.

## The Core Account

Plantinga's account of warrant: a belief is warranted to the degree it is produced by cognitive faculties functioning properly, in an appropriate environment, according to a design plan successfully aimed at truth.

The four conditions:

**Proper function**: The cognitive faculties producing the belief must be functioning properly — working as they are supposed to, without malfunction. If your visual system has a defect that distorts color perception, beliefs produced through that defective system lack warrant (or have reduced warrant).

**Appropriate environment**: The faculties must be operating in the environment for which they were designed. A normally-functioning visual system in unusual optical conditions — fog, funhouse mirrors, extreme darkness — may not produce warranted beliefs, because it is not in its appropriate environment.

**Design plan aimed at truth**: The relevant part of the design plan must be aimed at producing true beliefs, not beliefs that are adaptive for other reasons. Some of our cognitive processes are designed to produce comforting beliefs, socially adaptive beliefs, or other beliefs that serve functions other than truth. These don't produce warranted beliefs.

**Success**: The design plan must in fact be successfully operating to produce true beliefs. It's not enough that the design plan is aimed at truth; it must actually succeed in doing so.

## The Design Plan

The concept of a "design plan" is central to Plantinga's account. He does not require that the designer be God — the design plan could be the product of natural selection or any other process that gives cognitive systems their functional profiles. But the normative concept of proper function — functioning as one is *supposed to* — requires some reference to what the system was designed to do.

This is the key innovation: unlike pure reliabilism, which evaluates processes by their actual reliability, proper functionalism evaluates processes by whether they are functioning as they are supposed to and achieving what they are aimed at. A process can be unreliable (due to adverse conditions) but still involved in warranted belief if it is functioning properly in an appropriate environment.

## Plantinga and the Evil Demon

Proper functionalism handles the evil demon case differently from pure reliabilism. The deceived victim is (by hypothesis) functioning properly — their faculties are working as they should. But they are in an inappropriate environment — their faculties were designed for the actual world, not for the demonic scenario. Hence, their beliefs have reduced warrant, not because their faculties are malfunctioning, but because they are in an alien environment.

This is a nuanced result: Plantinga can distinguish between the victim's *functioning well* (they are doing everything right) and the victim's *having warrant* (the conditions for warrant are not met). This captures the internalist intuition that the victim is blameless while vindicating the externalist intuition that they don't have knowledge.

## The Evolutionary Challenge

If the design plan is produced by evolution, it might be aimed primarily at survival and reproduction rather than truth. Evolution selects for traits that are adaptive, not necessarily for traits that produce true beliefs. If adaptive beliefs are often false — as some evolutionary debunking arguments suggest — then properly-functioning evolved cognitive systems may not reliably produce true beliefs.

This evolutionary argument is raised by Patricia Churchland and others as a challenge to naturalized epistemology. Plantinga uses a version of this argument (*Plantinga's Evolutionary Argument against Naturalism*) to argue that if naturalism is true and our cognitive faculties were produced by unguided evolution, we have no reason to trust them to be truth-aimed. Hence, a theistic account of design — one that includes God as the designer of truth-aimed faculties — provides a more secure foundation for knowledge.

## Evaluating Proper Functionalism

Proper functionalism is an elegant response to the generality problem that afflicts reliabilism: the relevant type is specified by the design plan, not by an arbitrary choice among possible type descriptions. It also captures the normative dimension of epistemology more naturally than pure reliabilism.

Critics challenge the concept of a design plan as philosophically obscure (when systems weren't literally designed by anyone), and they press the theological implications of grounding warrant in divinely-designed faculties. Whether proper functionalism succeeds depends significantly on whether the design plan concept can be given a satisfactory naturalistic interpretation.
