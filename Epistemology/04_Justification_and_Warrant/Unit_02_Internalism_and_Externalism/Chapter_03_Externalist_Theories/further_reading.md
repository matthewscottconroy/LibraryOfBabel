# Further Reading: Externalist Theories

## Primary Sources

- Alvin Goldman, "What Is Justified Belief?" in *Justification and Knowledge*, ed. George Pappas, D. Reidel, 1979. The founding paper of process reliabilism, arguing that a belief is justified if and only if it is produced by a reliable belief-forming process — one that tends to produce true beliefs.

- Alvin Goldman, *Epistemology and Cognition*, Harvard University Press, 1986. The book-length development of reliabilism, integrating epistemological norms with cognitive science and offering detailed treatments of the generality problem.

- Robert Nozick, *Philosophical Explanations*, Harvard University Press, 1981, Chapter 3. Develops the "tracking" theory of knowledge, requiring that a knowing belief be sensitive (if P were false, the agent would not believe P) and adherent (if P were true, the agent would believe P).

- Alvin Plantinga, *Warrant: The Current Debate*, Oxford University Press, 1993. The first of Plantinga's trilogy, critically surveying existing theories of knowledge and warrant before developing his own proper functionalist account.

- Alvin Plantinga, *Warrant and Proper Function*, Oxford University Press, 1993. Develops proper functionalism: a belief has warrant (the extra ingredient that converts true belief into knowledge) when it is produced by cognitive faculties functioning properly, in an appropriate environment, according to a design plan aimed at truth.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Frederick Dretske, "Epistemic Operators," *Journal of Philosophy* 67, 1970, pp. 1007–1023. Introduces the "relevant alternatives" framework, arguing that "knows" is not a fully penetrating operator and that knowledge is relativized to a set of relevant alternative possibilities.

- Frederick Dretske, *Knowledge and the Flow of Information*, MIT Press, 1981. Develops an information-theoretic account of knowledge that is broadly externalist, grounding epistemic relations in actual information flow rather than reflective access.

- John Greco, "Internalism and Epistemically Responsible Action," *Philosophical Studies* 93, 1999, pp. 21–39. Defends a version of agent reliabilism incorporating internalist elements, arguing that justified belief requires that reliability be attributable to the agent's character.

- Jonathan Vogel, "Reliabilism Leveled," *Journal of Philosophy* 97, 2000, pp. 602–623. A systematic critique of reliabilism, pressing bootstrapping and easy-knowledge objections that show reliabilism licenses unjustifiable inferential moves.

- Juan Comesaña, "A Well-Founded Solution to the Generality Problem," *Philosophical Studies* 129, 2006, pp. 27–47. The most technically precise attempt to solve the generality problem by individuating belief types through their functional role and context.

- Ernest Sosa, *A Virtue Epistemology*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Develops a virtue-reliabilist epistemology that combines externalist foundations (reliable faculties) with internalist elements (reflective competence), offering a sophisticated alternative to simple process reliabilism.

- Laurence BonJour, "Externalist Theories of Empirical Knowledge," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 5, 1980, pp. 53–73. The most influential early critique of reliabilism, deploying cases like the reliable clairvoyant to argue that reliability without internal access fails to capture justification.

- Timothy Williamson, *Knowledge and Its Limits*, Oxford University Press, 2000. Defends a knowledge-first epistemology that is broadly externalist while arguing against both internalism and standard reliabilism, placing knowledge rather than justification at the center.

- Stewart Cohen, "Justification and Truth," *Philosophical Studies* 46, 1984, pp. 279–295. Presses the new evil demon objection against reliabilism with particular clarity.

- Michael Bergmann, *Justification without Awareness*, Oxford University Press, 2006. Defends reliabilism and externalism against access internalism, arguing that the awareness requirement generates a vicious regress.

- Matthias Steup and Ernest Sosa, eds., *Contemporary Debates in Epistemology*, Blackwell, 2005. The section on reliabilism includes paired papers by Goldman and BonJour, ideal for focused engagement with the externalism debate.

- Peter Graham, "Conveying Information," *Synthese* 131, 2002, pp. 223–269. Develops a reliabilist account of testimony and the transmission of knowledge, extending Goldman's framework to social epistemology.

- Mylan Engel, "Is Epistemic Luck Compatible with Knowledge?" *Southern Journal of Philosophy* 30, 1992, pp. 59–75. Examines how reliabilist accounts handle Gettier cases and epistemic luck, arguing that modal conditions on knowledge need to supplement reliability.

- Alvin Plantinga, *Warranted Christian Belief*, Oxford University Press, 2000. Extends proper functionalism to the epistemology of religious belief, arguing that if Christianity is true, belief in God can be warranted by properly functioning faculties — illustrating the reach of the externalist framework.
