# Further Reading: Reliabilist Virtue Epistemology

## Primary Sources

- Ernest Sosa, *Knowledge in Perspective: Selected Essays in Epistemology*, Cambridge University Press, 1991. Collects Sosa's seminal papers developing virtue epistemology, including his influential treatment of animal vs. reflective knowledge and the distinction between intellectual virtues and reliable faculties.

- Ernest Sosa, *A Virtue Epistemology: Apt Belief and Reflective Knowledge*, Vol. 1, Oxford University Press, 2007. The first volume of Sosa's mature system, introducing the AAA model (accurate, adroit, apt) and developing the archery analogy in detail.

- Ernest Sosa, *Reflective Knowledge: Apt Belief and Reflective Knowledge*, Vol. 2, Oxford University Press, 2009. Extends the AAA model to reflective knowledge and engages debates about skepticism, Gettier, and the relationship between animal and human cognition.

- John Greco, "Agent Reliabilism," *Philosophical Perspectives* 13, 1999, pp. 273–296. Argues that knowledge requires beliefs produced by stable cognitive dispositions attributable to the agent as competencies, bridging reliabilism and virtue epistemology.

- John Greco, *Achieving Knowledge: A Virtue-Theoretic Account of Epistemic Normativity*, Cambridge University Press, 2010. A systematic defense of reliabilist virtue epistemology, arguing that knowledge is a kind of achievement that involves the exercise of intellectual virtues.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Alvin Goldman, "Epistemic Folkways and Scientific Epistemology," in *Liaisons: Philosophy Meets the Cognitive and Social Sciences*, MIT Press, 1992. Develops the idea of epistemic virtues as reliable cognitive faculties, connecting Goldman's reliabilism with Sosa's virtue-theoretic approach.

- Linda Zagzebski, *Virtues of the Mind*, Cambridge University Press, 1996. While primarily defending responsibilist virtue epistemology, Part I provides the clearest survey of reliabilist alternatives and the key distinctions between the two programs.

- John Greco and John Turri, eds., *Virtue Epistemology: Contemporary Readings*, MIT Press, 2012. A comprehensive anthology with essential papers by Sosa, Greco, Zagzebski, and their critics, organized by topic.

- Duncan Pritchard, *Epistemic Luck*, Oxford University Press, 2005. Develops a "safety" condition for knowledge — closely related to the APT condition — and argues that virtue-theoretic approaches need to be supplemented with modal conditions to handle epistemic luck.

- Mylan Engel, "Is Epistemic Luck Compatible with Knowledge?" *Southern Journal of Philosophy* 30, 1992, pp. 59–75. An early examination of how luck bears on knowledge, anticipating the virtue-theoretic treatment of Gettier problems.

- Wayne Riggs, "Reliability and the Value of Knowledge," *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 64, 2002, pp. 79–96. Argues that virtue epistemology's emphasis on achievement and credit provides the best account of why knowledge is more valuable than mere true belief.

- Jennifer Lackey, "Why We Don't Deserve Credit for Everything We Know," *Synthese* 158, 2007, pp. 345–361. A critical examination of the credit thesis in reliabilist virtue epistemology, arguing that testimonial knowledge poses a challenge for the view that knowledge must be an achievement of the knower.

- Matt Weiner, "Norms of Assertion," in *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 71, 2005. Relevant to the discussion of how Sosa's AAA model connects to the norms governing knowledge claims and assertion.

- Ernest Sosa, "How to Defeat Opposition to Moore," *Philosophical Perspectives* 13, 1999, pp. 141–153. Shows how reliabilist virtue epistemology responds to Moorean common-sense arguments against skepticism.

- Greco, John, "Knowledge as Credit for True Belief," in *Intellectual Virtue: Perspectives from Ethics and Epistemology*, eds. DePaul and Zagzebski, Oxford University Press, 2003. Provides the clearest statement of the credit-for-true-belief theory of knowledge.

- Duncan Pritchard, Alan Millar, and Adrian Haddock, *The Nature and Value of Knowledge*, Oxford University Press, 2010. A three-author study integrating virtue epistemology, safety conditions, and the value of knowledge.

- Christopher Kelp, *Good Thinking: A Knowledge First Virtue Epistemology*, Routledge, 2018. Attempts to integrate Williamson's knowledge-first framework with virtue-theoretic concerns about cognitive achievement.

- Virtue Epistemology: Contemporary Readings, eds. Greco and Turri — see above. A convenient single source for many of the most important papers in the field.

- Jason Baehr, *The Inquiring Mind*, Oxford University Press, 2011. Primarily responsibilist in orientation but provides an excellent survey of the reliabilist/responsibilist distinction and its implications.
