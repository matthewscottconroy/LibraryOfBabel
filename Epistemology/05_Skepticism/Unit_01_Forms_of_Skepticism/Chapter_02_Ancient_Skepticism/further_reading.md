# Further Reading: Ancient Skepticism

## Primary Sources

- Sextus Empiricus, *Outlines of Pyrrhonism*, trans. R.G. Bury, Harvard University Press, 1933. The primary source for Pyrrhonism; contains the ten modes of Aenesidemus (for suspending judgment about appearances) and the five modes of Agrippa (targeting any attempt at justification), along with Sextus's account of the Pyrrhonist life.

- Sextus Empiricus, *Against the Professors* (*Adversus Mathematicos*), trans. R.G. Bury, 4 vols., Harvard University Press, 1935–1949. Sextus's more detailed critiques of different domains of knowledge claim — grammar, rhetoric, geometry, arithmetic, astrology, and music — illustrating the scope of Pyrrhonist skepticism.

- Cicero, *Academica*, trans. H. Rackham, Harvard University Press, 1933. The principal source for Academic skepticism, including Arcesilaus and Carneades; Cicero defends a form of probabilism and records the debates about whether the Academics were genuine skeptics or merely anti-Stoic polemicists.

- Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of the Eminent Philosophers*, Vol. 2, trans. R.D. Hicks, Harvard University Press, 1925. Contains the life of Pyrrho (Book IX) and is an important source for the doxographic tradition on Pyrrhonism.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Julia Annas and Jonathan Barnes, *The Modes of Scepticism: Ancient Texts and Modern Interpretations*, Cambridge University Press, 1985. The standard scholarly analysis of the modes of Aenesidemus and Agrippa, combining textual scholarship with philosophical assessment.

- Myles Burnyeat, ed., *The Skeptical Tradition*, University of California Press, 1983. A collection of essays by leading scholars on ancient and early modern skepticism, covering Pyrrhonism, Academic skepticism, and their legacy.

- Richard Bett, *Pyrrho: His Antecedents and His Legacy*, Oxford University Press, 2000. A scholarly examination of the historical Pyrrho — showing that his original philosophy was quite different from later Pyrrhonism — and its transformation by Sextus and others.

- Jonathan Barnes, *The Toils of Scepticism*, Cambridge University Press, 1990. A careful philosophical analysis of the modes of Agrippa, examining the logical structure and implications of each mode and assessing Agrippa's case for suspension of judgment.

- Gisela Striker, "The Ten Tropes of Aenesidemus," in *Essays on Hellenistic Epistemology and Ethics*, Cambridge University Press, 1996. A precise philosophical analysis of the ten modes, examining their argumentative structure and their relationship to Pyrrhonist practice.

- Michael Frede, "The Sceptic's Beliefs," in *The Original Sceptics: A Controversy*, eds. Burnyeat and Frede, Hackett, 1997. Addresses the interpretive question of whether Pyrrhonists had any beliefs at all, defending the view that they had "appearances" that governed behavior without constituting beliefs.

- Myles Burnyeat, "Can the Sceptic Live His Scepticism?" in *The Skeptical Tradition*, University of California Press, 1983. A classic examination of the apraxia objection — the charge that the Pyrrhonist cannot act without forming beliefs — arguing that Pyrrhonism faces a serious practical objection.

- Harold Tarrant, *Scepticism or Platonism? The Philosophy of the Fourth Academy*, Cambridge University Press, 1985. Examines Academic skepticism and whether figures like Arcesilaus and Carneades were genuine skeptics or deploying skeptical arguments in service of a Platonic agenda.

- R.J. Hankinson, *The Sceptics*, Routledge, 1995. A comprehensive introduction to ancient skepticism covering both Pyrrhonism and Academic skepticism, combining philosophical analysis with historical scholarship.

- Anthony Long and David Sedley, *The Hellenistic Philosophers*, 2 vols., Cambridge University Press, 1987. The standard sourcebook for Hellenistic philosophy including Skepticism; provides Greek/Latin texts with translation and commentary on Pyrrhonism and Academic skepticism.

- Katja Vogt, *Belief and Truth: A Skeptic Reading of Plato*, Oxford University Press, 2012. Examines the relationship between Platonic epistemology and the Academic skeptical tradition, useful for understanding the intellectual context of Academic skepticism.

- Luciano Floridi, "The Skeptical Solutions to the Problem of Sextus Empiricus," *Phronesis* 47, 2002. A careful examination of whether Sextus's suspension of judgment is philosophically coherent as a response to the modes.

- Casey Perin, *The Demands of Reason: An Essay on Pyrrhonian Scepticism*, Oxford University Press, 2010. A philosophical reconstruction of Pyrrhonian skepticism that takes seriously both the theoretical arguments and the practical Pyrrhonist way of life.

- Benson Mates, *The Skeptic Way: Sextus Empiricus's Outlines of Pyrrhonism*, Oxford University Press, 1996. Translation with extensive philosophical commentary, useful as a guide to the philosophical content of Sextus's main text.
