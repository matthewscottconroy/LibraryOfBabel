# Further Reading: IBE and Abduction Against Skepticism

## Primary Sources

- Jonathan Vogel, "Cartesian Skepticism and Inference to the Best Explanation," *Journal of Philosophy* 87, 1990, pp. 658–666. The landmark defense of IBE as a response to Cartesian skepticism, arguing that the hypothesis that an external world exists is a better explanation of our experience than the evil demon/BIV hypothesis.

- Jonathan Vogel, "Reliabilism Leveled," *Journal of Philosophy* 97, 2000, pp. 602–623. While primarily about reliabilism, includes important discussion of how IBE connects to anti-skeptical argument and the question of bootstrapping.

- C.S. Peirce, "Deduction, Induction, and Hypothesis," *Popular Science Monthly* 13, 1878, pp. 470–482. The original formulation of "abduction" as a distinct form of reasoning — inferring to the best explanation — foundational for understanding IBE.

- Gilbert Harman, "The Inference to the Best Explanation," *Philosophical Review* 74, 1965, pp. 88–95. The paper that introduced "inference to the best explanation" into contemporary philosophy, providing the terminology and framework for subsequent debates.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Peter Lipton, *Inference to the Best Explanation*, 2nd ed., Routledge, 2004. The most comprehensive philosophical treatment of IBE, examining its structure, its relationship to Bayesianism, and its application in science and everyday reasoning.

- Gilbert Harman, *Thought*, Princeton University Press, 1973. Chapter 8 develops the connection between IBE and theory choice in science, relevant to understanding how IBE might respond to underdetermination-based skepticism.

- Jonathan Vogel, "E Pluribus Unum: IBE as a Basis for Justified Belief," *Philosophical Perspectives* 14, 2000, pp. 303–319. Develops the case for IBE as foundational to justification more generally, not just as a response to specific skeptical challenges.

- Bas van Fraassen, *Laws and Symmetry*, Oxford University Press, 1989. Chapter 6 ("The Pragmatics of Explanation") contains the most influential critique of IBE, arguing that "loveliness" (explanatory virtue) is not a guide to "likelihood" (truth).

- Paul Lipton, "Is the Best Good Enough?" *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 93, 1993, pp. 89–104. A careful examination of the relationship between explanatory goodness and truth, and whether IBE can bridge the gap between the two.

- John Vogel, "Skepticism and Inference to the Best Explanation," in *Contemporary Epistemology*, ed. Huemer, Routledge, 2002. Develops the IBE response to skepticism with attention to objections from question-begging and coherent alternatives.

- Igor Douven, "Inference to the Best Explanation," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2021 ed. A comprehensive and reliable survey of IBE, including the epistemological debate about its application to skeptical challenges.

- Brian Skyrms, *Choice and Chance: An Introduction to Inductive Logic*, 4th ed., Wadsworth, 2000. Provides background on the relationship between induction and IBE, clarifying how abductive and inductive forms of inference relate.

- Alexander Bird, *Philosophy of Science*, Routledge, 1998. Chapter 4 provides an accessible account of IBE in science and its philosophical justification, useful for contextualizing IBE within the broader epistemology of science.

- Arthur Merin, "Information, Evidence, and Peirce's Notion of Abduction," *Sprache und Datenverarbeitung* 23, 1999. Connects Peircean abduction to information-theoretic accounts of evidence, relevant for understanding the roots of IBE.

- Marc Lange, "Is Jeffrey Conditionalization Defective by Virtue of Being Non-Commutative? Remarks on the Relationship between Bayesian Epistemology and the Logic of 'Old Evidence'," *Synthese* 123, 2000, pp. 393–403. Examines the relationship between Bayesian epistemology and abduction, relevant to how IBE relates to formal epistemological frameworks.

- Michael Huemer, *Skepticism and the Veil of Perception*, Rowman & Littlefield, 2001. Defends direct realism and phenomenal conservatism partly against IBE-based indirect realism, with implications for the structure of IBE-based anti-skeptical arguments.

- Ted Poston, *Reason and Explanation: A Defense of Explanatory Coherentism*, Palgrave Macmillan, 2014. Defends "explanatory coherentism" — a view that incorporates IBE into a coherentist account of justification — as the best available response to skepticism.

- Quine, W.V.O., "Epistemology Naturalized," in *Ontological Relativity and Other Essays*, Columbia University Press, 1969. Argues for naturalizing epistemology and accepting IBE as a fundamental epistemic norm, with implications for how IBE is to be justified against the skeptic.
