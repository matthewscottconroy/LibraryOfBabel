## Primary Sources

- Aristotle, *Metaphysics*, Book IV (Gamma), trans. W.D. Ross, Oxford University Press, 1924. The locus classicus of correspondence: "To say of what is that it is, and of what is not that it is not, is true."

- Bertrand Russell, *The Problems of Philosophy*, Home University Library, 1912. Introduces the multiple-relation theory of judgment and develops correspondence intuitions within a realist framework.

- Bertrand Russell, *The Philosophy of Logical Atomism*, Open Court, 1985 (lectures 1918). Argues that facts, not things, are the primary truth-makers and develops the picture theory of propositions.

- Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*, Kegan Paul, 1922. The picture theory of meaning: propositions are logical pictures of facts, grounding a correspondence account.

- J.L. Austin, "Truth," *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society*, Supplementary Volume 24, 1950. Influential attempt to state correspondence without reliance on dubious facts about facts.

- J.L. Mackie, "Simple Truth," *Philosophical Quarterly* 20, 1970. Defends a version of correspondence against coherence and pragmatist rivals.

- D.M. Armstrong, *A World of States of Affairs*, Cambridge University Press, 1997. Systematic defense of states of affairs as truth-makers; distinguishes them from facts in a Fregean sense.

- D.M. Armstrong, *Truth and Truthmakers*, Cambridge University Press, 2004. The most thorough recent treatment of truthmaker theory, including the problems of negative and general truths.

- P.F. Strawson, "Truth," *Analysis* 9, 1949. Classic performative/redundancy challenge to correspondence that nonetheless clarifies the correspondence picture.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Michael Devitt, *Realism and Truth*, 2nd ed., Blackwell, 1991. Defends correspondence realism; contains a sustained critique of anti-realist rivals.

- Scott Soames, *What Is Meaning?*, Princeton University Press, 2010. Examines how semantic theory relates to correspondence, with attention to propositions and truth-conditions.

- Julian Dodd, *An Identity Theory of Truth*, Palgrave Macmillan, 2000. Argues that the correspondence relation collapses into identity, challenging robust correspondence views.

- Marian David, *Correspondence and Disquotation*, Oxford University Press, 1994. Careful analysis of whether disquotational accounts capture genuine correspondence intuitions.

- Barry Taylor, "Truth-maker Realism," *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 77, 1999. Assesses the prospects for grounding correspondence in a truth-maker ontology.

- Stephen Neale, "The Philosophical Significance of Gödel's Slingshot," *Mind* 104, 1995. Best exposition of the slingshot argument and its implications for fact-based correspondence theories.

- Kevin Mulligan, Peter Simons, and Barry Smith, "Truth-Makers," *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 44, 1984. The paper that launched the contemporary truth-maker program.

- Jennifer Saul and Mark Sprevak (eds.), *The Routledge Handbook of Philosophy of Language*, Routledge, 2021. Contains surveys of truth and reference essential for situating correspondence in broader semantic debates.
