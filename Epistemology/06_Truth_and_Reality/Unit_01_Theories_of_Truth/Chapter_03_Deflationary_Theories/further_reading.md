## Primary Sources

- Alfred Tarski, "The Semantic Conception of Truth and the Foundations of Semantics," *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 4, 1944. The indispensable source for the T-schema and the formal treatment of truth as a predicate; Tarski's own view is contested between deflationary and inflationary readers.

- W.V.O. Quine, "Philosophy of Logic," Harvard University Press, 1970. Develops disquotationalism: the truth predicate serves purely to disquote sentences, allowing semantic ascent without substantive semantic commitment.

- W.V.O. Quine, "Truth," in *Quiddities*, Harvard University Press, 1987. Brief, clear statement of Quine's deflationary view and its connection to the apparatus of semantic ascent.

- Paul Horwich, *Truth*, 2nd ed., Oxford University Press, 1998. The canonical statement of minimalism: every use of "true" is explained by instances of the equivalence schema, with no further nature for truth to have.

- Dorothy Grover, Joseph Camp, and Nuel Belnap, "A Prosentential Theory of Truth," *Philosophical Studies* 27, 1975. Introduces the view that "true" is not a predicate but a proform functioning anaphorically.

- Frank Ramsey, "Facts and Propositions," *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society*, Supplementary Volume 7, 1927. The earliest deflationary observation: "It is true that Caesar was murdered" adds nothing to "Caesar was murdered."

- Peter Strawson, "Truth," *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society*, Supplementary Volume 24, 1950. Performative theory; arguing that "true" performs the speech act of endorsing a claim rather than ascribing a property.

- Hartry Field, "Tarski's Theory of Truth," *Journal of Philosophy* 69, 1972. Argues that Tarski's definition is physicalistically inadequate and that a substantive theory of reference is needed—a challenge deflationists must answer.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Jody Azzouni, *Deflating Existential Consequence*, Oxford University Press, 2004. Develops a deflationary view in the context of ontological commitment and formal logic.

- Volker Halbach, *Axiomatic Theories of Truth*, Cambridge University Press, 2011. Systematic treatment of formal theories of truth; essential for understanding which deflationary axioms are logically consistent.

- Anil Gupta, "A Critique of Deflationism," *Philosophical Topics* 21, 1993. Influential objection arguing that deflationists cannot account for the role of truth in generalizations and explanations.

- Scott Soames, *Understanding Truth*, Oxford University Press, 1999. Argues that deflationary theories cannot account for the expressive or explanatory functions truth must perform.

- Michael Dummett, "Truth," *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 59, 1959. Argues that truth must be connected to verification conditions—a direct challenge to the deflationist's claim that the T-schema exhausts the concept.

- Marian David, *Correspondence and Disquotation*, Oxford University Press, 1994. Asks whether the disquotational account covertly relies on correspondence notions.

- Brad Armour-Garb and J.C. Beall (eds.), *Deflationary Truth*, Open Court, 2005. The best anthology of deflationary positions and objections; includes key papers by Horwich, Field, and critics.

- Graham Priest, *An Introduction to Non-Classical Logic*, Cambridge University Press, 2008. Background on the logical issues—especially the Liar paradox—that any theory of truth must confront.
