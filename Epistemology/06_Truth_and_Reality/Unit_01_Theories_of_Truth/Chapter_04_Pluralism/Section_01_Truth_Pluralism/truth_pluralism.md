# Truth Pluralism

Truth pluralism holds that there is no single property that all truths share in virtue of which they are true. Different domains of discourse — empirical science, mathematics, ethics, aesthetics — may have different truth properties. What makes a scientific statement true (correspondence to physical facts) might be different from what makes a moral statement true (perhaps coherence with a system of norms, or warranted assertibility under ideal conditions).

## Motivation

The main motivation for pluralism is the problem of the *unity of truth*. Traditional theories try to identify a single property — correspondence, coherence, warranted assertibility — that all true statements share. But each theory faces compelling counterexamples in some domain:

- Correspondence fits the sciences but seems strained for mathematics (what physical facts do mathematical truths correspond to?), ethics (what facts do moral truths correspond to?), and logic (what facts do tautologies correspond to?).
- Coherence works for some interpretations of mathematics and ethics but seems wrong for empirical science (the external world provides inputs that coherence alone cannot account for).
- Deflationism avoids these problems but seems to give up on explaining the explanatory role of truth.

Pluralism's solution: instead of finding a single property that fits all domains, recognize that different domains have different truth properties. There is no single "truth" property; there is a family of domain-specific truth properties, unified by a common functional role.

## Crispin Wright's Superassertibility

Crispin Wright's pluralism in *Truth and Objectivity* (1992) holds that all truths share a "cosmetic" common property (captured by the platitudes of the T-schema), but different domains may satisfy additional properties:

- **Superassertibility**: True if assertible under ideal conditions and would remain assertible under any further investigation. This is available in all domains but may be the *only* truth property in domains like ethics (where there are no further facts beyond ideal warranted assertibility).
- **Correspondence**: Available in domains with mind-independent facts — the physical sciences, paradigmatically.

Wright holds that debates about realism and anti-realism in specific domains (ethics, mathematics) turn on which of these richer properties applies. If ethical truths are only superassertible, ethics is in some sense less "thick" than physics, where truths are also correspondence truths.

## Alethic Pluralism: Lynch and Sher

Michael Lynch's *True to Life* (2004) and subsequent work develops a more systematic pluralism. Lynch holds that truth is a "functional" property — what it means to be true is captured by a role defined by the platitudes (sentences like "to assert is to present as true," "true beliefs tend to lead to success"). Different properties in different domains "realize" or "play the role of" this functional truth property.

This parallels functionalism in philosophy of mind: just as mental states are defined by their functional role (and different physical states can play the same functional role), truth is defined by its functional role (and different properties can play that role in different domains).

Gila Sher's "substantive pluralism" argues that truth is genuinely substantive but admits of multiple forms corresponding to the diverse ways that language connects to reality. Different types of sentences make contact with reality in different ways, and their truth consists in these different types of contact.

## Objections

**The unity problem**: If different domains have different truth properties, what do they have in common? If the answer is "the functional role," this seems like a form of deflationism about the common element, supplemented by substantive accounts of the domain-specific realizers.

**The mixing problem**: What about sentences that mix domains? "It's true that I believe (that the table is brown and that murder is wrong)" mixes psychology, physics, and ethics. Which truth property applies?

**Explanatory coherence**: We often explain events using both scientific and ethical vocabulary. If these use different truth properties, how do mixed explanations work?

**Simpler alternatives**: Defenders of global deflationism argue that pluralism is more complex than necessary — the deflationary account handles all domains without positing different truth properties, and the apparent differences between domains are differences in the character of the facts, not differences in what truth consists in.
