## Primary Sources

- Crispin Wright, *Truth and Objectivity*, Harvard University Press, 1992. The founding text of contemporary truth pluralism; introduces superassertibility and argues that different discourses may be governed by different truth properties.

- Crispin Wright, "Truth: A Traditional Debate Reviewed," *Canadian Journal of Philosophy* 28, 1998. Wright refines his pluralism and responds to early objections about the unity of truth.

- Michael Lynch, *Truth in Context: An Essay on Pluralism and Objectivity*, MIT Press, 1998. An early defense of pluralism about truth that focuses on the relationship between truth and objectivity in different domains.

- Michael Lynch, *True to Life: Why Truth Matters*, MIT Press, 2004. Accessible development of Lynch's functionalist pluralism; argues that different properties realize the functional role of truth in different domains.

- Michael Lynch, *Truth as One and Many*, Oxford University Press, 2009. The most systematic statement of Lynch's pluralism; addresses the mixing problem and the unity of truth through functionalism.

- Crispin Wright, "Varieties of Realism," in *Proceedings of the British Academy*, 2001. Develops the framework of "width of cosmological role" as a criterion distinguishing robust from minimal truth properties.

- Gila Sher, *Epistemic Friction: An Essay on Knowledge, Truth, and Logic*, Oxford University Press, 2016. Develops "substantive pluralism"—truth is always substantive and correspondence-like, but differently realized in different domains.

- Douglas Edwards, *The Metaphysics of Truth*, Oxford University Press, 2018. A careful monograph-length treatment of the main pluralist positions and their merits.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Nikolaj Pedersen and Cory Wright (eds.), *Truth and Pluralism: Current Debates*, Oxford University Press, 2013. The best anthology for the contemporary debate; includes papers by Lynch, Wright, Sher, and their critics.

- Nikolaj Pedersen, "Truth as Constitutively Pluralist," *Philosophers' Imprint* 10, 2010. Argues for a version of pluralism that avoids the mixing problem by treating truth as a family-resemblance concept.

- Panu Raatikainen, "On Alethic Pluralism," *Acta Analytica* 30, 2015. Critical survey of the main pluralist positions; presses difficulties about logical reasoning across discourse domains.

- Aaron Cotnoir, "Pluralism and Paradox," in Pedersen and Wright (eds.), 2013. Examines how truth pluralism interacts with the Liar paradox; argues pluralism may actually help with semantic paradoxes.

- Cory Wright, "Truth, Ramsification, and the Pluralist's Revenge," *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 83, 2005. Argues that functionalist pluralism faces a version of the Ramsey-Lewis problem for functional characterization.

- Paul Horwich, *Truth*, 2nd ed., Oxford University Press, 1998. The minimalist foil against which pluralists define themselves; contains important discussions of whether there is a single truth property.

- Douglas Edwards, "Simplifying Alethic Pluralism," *Southern Journal of Philosophy* 50, 2012. Argues for a streamlined version of pluralism that sidesteps some mixing-problem difficulties.
