## Crispin Wright (b. 1942)

Wright's *Truth and Objectivity* (1992) is the founding document of contemporary alethic pluralism. He argued that different areas of discourse—mathematics, ethics, aesthetics, science—may satisfy different, domain-specific truth properties while sharing a common "minimal" notion tied to the equivalence schema. Wright introduced superassertibility—truth as what would survive arbitrary increases in information and ideal reflection—as a truth property appropriate for some domains, particularly those where robustly mind-independent correspondence seems implausible. His framework distinguishes "thin" discourse (where minimalism suffices) from "thick" discourse (where more robust truth obtains).

## Michael Lynch (b. 1966)

Lynch developed the most systematic functionalist pluralism in a series of books culminating in *Truth as One and Many* (2009). His key move is to characterize truth by its functional role—the properties a thing must have to play the "truth role" in cognition and inquiry—and then argue that different properties (correspondence, coherence, superassertibility) realize this role in different domains. Lynch addresses the unity problem: despite pluralism about realizing properties, there is a single truth concept defined by its functional role. He also develops the most thorough treatment of the mixing problem.

## Gila Sher (b. 1943)

Sher defends substantive pluralism, arguing against both deflationism and domain-specific minimalism. For Sher, truth is always substantive—it always involves some kind of grounding in reality—but the manner of grounding differs across domains. Mathematical truths are grounded in abstract structural facts; physical truths in material states of affairs; moral truths in normative properties. Sher's account preserves the realist intuition that truth is never merely "what we say" while acknowledging genuine differences in how different truths hold.

## Nikolaj Pedersen (b. 1975)

Pedersen has been one of the most productive contributors to the formal development and critical evaluation of pluralism. His work on "pluralism and paradox" examines how different pluralist positions interact with the Liar and other semantic paradoxes, arguing that pluralism may actually provide resources that monist theories lack. He has also developed a "weakly classical" pluralism that assigns different truth values in different domains while maintaining classical logic for cross-domain reasoning.

## Douglas Edwards (b. 1981)

Edwards has systematized and critically evaluated pluralist positions in his monograph *The Metaphysics of Truth* (2018). He distinguishes between different senses in which truth might be plural—pluralism about truth properties, about truth predicates, about truth bearers—and argues that each raises distinct philosophical challenges. His work on "simplifying alethic pluralism" attempts to reformulate Lynch-style functionalism in a way that avoids the mixing problem while retaining the core pluralist insight.
