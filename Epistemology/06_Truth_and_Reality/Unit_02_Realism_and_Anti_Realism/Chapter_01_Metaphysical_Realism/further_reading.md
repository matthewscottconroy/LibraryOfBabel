## Primary Sources

- Hilary Putnam, *Meaning and the Moral Sciences*, Routledge, 1978. Contains the "model-theoretic argument" against metaphysical realism—Putnam's later work that complicates his earlier externalism.

- Michael Devitt, *Realism and Truth*, 2nd ed., Blackwell, 1991. The clearest sustained defense of metaphysical realism; distinguishes realism about existence from realism about independence.

- Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, trans. Guyer and Wood, Cambridge University Press, 1998 (orig. 1781). The primary source for the phenomena/noumena distinction that frames modern debates about whether reality is accessible to cognition.

- Hilary Putnam, *Representation and Reality*, MIT Press, 1988. Putnam's argument against functionalism and computational realism; part of his turn toward internal realism.

- W.V.O. Quine, "On What There Is," *Review of Metaphysics* 2, 1948. The canonical statement of ontological commitment; sets up modern realism/anti-realism debates in terms of existential quantification.

- Stathis Psillos, *Scientific Realism: How Science Tracks Truth*, Routledge, 1999. The most comprehensive defense of scientific realism; develops the no-miracles argument and responds to the pessimistic meta-induction.

- Penelope Maddy, *Realism in Mathematics*, Oxford University Press, 1990. Defends mathematical platonism on broadly naturalistic grounds; connects mathematical realism to epistemological questions about perception.

- Russ Shafer-Landau, *Moral Realism: A Defence*, Oxford University Press, 2003. Systematic defense of non-naturalist moral realism; argues for mind-independent moral facts.

- Larry Laudan, "A Confutation of Convergent Realism," *Philosophy of Science* 48, 1981. The most influential statement of the pessimistic meta-induction against scientific realism.

## Key Secondary Sources

- J.J.C. Smart, *Our Place in the Universe*, Blackwell, 1989. Defends a thoroughgoing physical realism; argues that the world is exactly as science describes it.

- Alexander Bird, *Philosophy of Science*, UCL Press, 1998. An accessible entry into scientific realism debates, including the no-miracles argument and pessimistic meta-induction.

- Paul Boghossian, *Fear of Knowledge*, Oxford University Press, 2006. Critique of relativist and constructivist challenges to realism; argues that epistemic relativism is self-undermining.

- Michael Dummett, *The Seas of Language*, Oxford University Press, 1993. Contains essays clarifying what is at issue between realism and anti-realism; essential background for both sides.

- John Bigelow and Robert Pargetter, *Science and Necessity*, Cambridge University Press, 1990. Defends scientific realism with attention to modality and dispositions.

- Peter Lipton, *Inference to the Best Explanation*, 2nd ed., Routledge, 2004. Defends the use of IBE—on which the no-miracles argument depends—as a rational inferential strategy.

- Bas van Fraassen, *The Scientific Image*, Oxford University Press, 1980. The principal anti-realist challenge to scientific realism; essential reading for understanding what is at stake.
