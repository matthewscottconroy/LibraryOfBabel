## Primary Sources

- Michael Dummett, "Truth," *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 59, 1959. Dummett's founding anti-realist argument: truth must be connected to verification or assertion conditions, not to potentially unknowable correspondence.

- Michael Dummett, *The Logical Basis of Metaphysics*, Harvard University Press, 1991. The most systematic statement of Dummett's anti-realism; argues that the logic appropriate to undecidable discourse is intuitionist, not classical.

- Michael Dummett, *Truth and Other Enigmas*, Harvard University Press, 1978. A collection of essays including "The Philosophical Basis of Intuitionistic Logic"—essential for understanding anti-realism's logical implications.

- George Berkeley, *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge*, Pepyat, 1710. The primary source for immaterialism: esse est percipi—to be is to be perceived.

- George Berkeley, *Three Dialogues between Hylas and Philonous*, Pepyat, 1713. More accessible than the *Principles*; Berkeley's immaterialism presented in dialogue form.

- Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, trans. Guyer and Wood, Cambridge University Press, 1998 (orig. 1781). The transcendental aesthetic and analytic develop transcendental idealism—the view that the structure of experience is mind-contributed.

- G.W.F. Hegel, *Phenomenology of Spirit*, trans. Miller, Oxford University Press, 1977 (orig. 1807). Absolute idealism: reality is the self-unfolding of Spirit; mind-independence dissolves into absolute self-knowing.

- Bas van Fraassen, *The Scientific Image*, Oxford University Press, 1980. Develops constructive empiricism—the view that the goal of science is empirical adequacy, not truth about unobservables.

- Bas van Fraassen, *The Empirical Stance*, Yale University Press, 2002. Van Fraassen's empiricism as an attitude; argues against metaphysics as a cognitive enterprise.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Neil Tennant, *Anti-Realism and Logic*, Oxford University Press, 1987. Develops Dummettian anti-realism with logical precision; argues for intuitionist logic on semantic grounds.

- Crispin Wright, *Realism, Meaning and Truth*, 2nd ed., Blackwell, 1993. Sustained engagement with Dummett's anti-realism; both sympathetic and critical.

- Paul Boghossian, *Fear of Knowledge*, Oxford University Press, 2006. Argues that epistemic relativism and constructivism about truth are self-undermining.

- Paul Snowdon, "Anti-Realism and Epistemological Idealism," *Aristotelian Society Supplementary Volume* 60, 1986. Distinguishes varieties of idealism and their epistemic implications.

- Kenneth Westphal, *Kant's Transcendental Proof of Realism*, Cambridge University Press, 2004. Argues that Kant's transcendental idealism is actually compatible with a robust empirical realism.

- Andrew Melnyk, *A Physicalist Manifesto*, Cambridge University Press, 2003. Defends physicalism against idealist and anti-realist alternatives.

- Bas van Fraassen, "Arguments Concerning Scientific Realism," in *The Scientific Image*, ch. 2. The canonical constructive empiricist statement of why theoretical commitment is unnecessary and epistemically unjustified.
