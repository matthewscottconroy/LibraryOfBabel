## Primary Sources

- Ernst Mach, *The Science of Mechanics*, Open Court, 1893. Classic statement of scientific instrumentalism: theories are economical descriptions of experience, not maps of hidden reality.

- Bas van Fraassen, *The Scientific Image*, Oxford University Press, 1980. Modern constructive empiricism as a refined form of instrumentalism about unobservables.

- Hartry Field, *Science Without Numbers*, Princeton University Press, 1980. The foundational text of mathematical fictionalism; argues that mathematics is useful fiction that need not be true.

- Hartry Field, *Realism, Mathematics and Modality*, Blackwell, 1989. Extends the fictionalist program and responds to objections about the indispensability argument.

- Richard Joyce, *The Myth of Morality*, Cambridge University Press, 2001. Argues that moral discourse presupposes the existence of moral facts that do not exist; moral fictionalism as the appropriate response.

- Richard Joyce, *The Evolution of Morality*, MIT Press, 2006. Evolutionary debunking argument for moral fictionalism; argues that moral beliefs are adaptive fictions with no cognitive authority.

- Gideon Rosen, "Modal Fictionalism," *Mind* 99, 1990. Introduces the view that possible worlds are useful fictions for analyzing modal claims without ontological commitment.

- Mark Balaguer, *Platonism and Anti-Platonism in Mathematics*, Oxford University Press, 1998. Evaluates fictionalism and Platonism in mathematics; argues both are defensible.

- Pierre Duhem, *The Aim and Structure of Physical Theory*, Princeton University Press, 1954 (orig. 1906). Classic instrumentalism about theoretical physics; theories are systems of mathematical propositions for classifying observations.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Otávio Bueno and Mark Colyvan, "An Inferential Conception of the Application of Mathematics," *Noûs* 45, 2011. Develops a nominalist-friendly account of applied mathematics that informs fictionalist debates.

- Penelope Maddy, *Second Philosophy*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Develops naturalistic alternatives to fictionalism in mathematics while engaging seriously with Field.

- Alan Musgrave, "Realism vs. Constructive Empiricism," in Churchland and Hooker (eds.), *Images of Science*, University of Chicago Press, 1985. A pointed realist critique of van Fraassen's instrumentalism.

- Mary Leng, *Mathematics and Reality*, Oxford University Press, 2010. Defends fictionalism about mathematics while addressing the applicability challenge.

- John Divers, *Possible Worlds*, Routledge, 2002. Comprehensive treatment of possible-worlds theories including modal fictionalism.

- Frederique Janssen-Lauret, "Meta-Ontology, Naturalism and the Quine-Putnam Indispensability Argument," *Journal for the History of Analytic Philosophy* 4, 2016. Situates indispensability arguments in relation to fictionalism.

- Cian Dorr, "There Are No Abstract Objects," in Sider, Hawthorne, and Zimmerman (eds.), *Contemporary Debates in Metaphysics*, Blackwell, 2008. Defends an error theory and fictionalism about abstracta.
