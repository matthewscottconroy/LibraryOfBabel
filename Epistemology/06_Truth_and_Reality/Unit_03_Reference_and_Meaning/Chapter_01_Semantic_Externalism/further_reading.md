## Primary Sources

- Hilary Putnam, "Meaning and Reference," *Journal of Philosophy* 70, 1973. Introduces Twin Earth and argues that "meaning" in the sense of extension is not in the head.

- Hilary Putnam, "The Meaning of 'Meaning'," in *Mind, Language and Reality*, Cambridge University Press, 1975. The full statement of semantic externalism; introduces the linguistic division of labor and the Twin Earth thought experiments.

- Tyler Burge, "Individualism and the Mental," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 4, 1979. The arthritis thought experiment; extends Putnam's externalism from natural kind terms to all intentional mental states.

- Tyler Burge, "Other Bodies," in Woodfield (ed.), *Thought and Object*, Oxford University Press, 1982. Extends externalism to thoughts about physical objects in the environment.

- Saul Kripke, *Naming and Necessity*, Harvard University Press, 1980. Although primarily about names, Kripke's arguments for rigid designation and the causal picture of reference underpin Putnam's Twin Earth scenario.

- David Kaplan, "Demonstratives," in Almog, Perry, and Wettstein (eds.), *Themes from Kaplan*, Oxford University Press, 1989. Indexical semantics; natural kind terms as indexicals connects to Kripke-Putnam externalism.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Jerry Fodor, *Psychosemantics*, MIT Press, 1987. Defends methodological solipsism against externalism; argues that psychological laws require narrow content.

- Brian Loar, "Social Content and Psychological Content," in Grimm and Merrill (eds.), *Contents of Thought*, University of Arizona Press, 1988. Argues for a phenomenal concept strategy compatible with internalism.

- Michael McKinsey, "Anti-Individualism and Privileged Access," *Analysis* 51, 1991. Argues that externalism is incompatible with privileged first-person access to mental content—the McKinsey paradox.

- Ned Block, "Advertisement for a Semantics for Psychology," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 10, 1986. Argues for the necessity of narrow content for psychological explanation.

- Christopher Peacocke, *A Study of Concepts*, MIT Press, 1992. Develops a theory of concept individuation that tries to capture both narrow and wide aspects of content.

- Frances Svensson, "Internalism, Externalism and Privileged Access," *Synthese* 180, 2011. Survey of the debate; examines the McKinsey paradox and proposed resolutions.

- Adam Pautz and Daniel Stoljar (eds.), *Themes from Block*, MIT Press, 2021. Contains papers that address semantic externalism in relation to intentionality and mental content.

- Jessica Brown, *Anti-Individualism and Knowledge*, MIT Press, 2004. Defends externalism against epistemological objections; argues externalism is compatible with self-knowledge.

- Francois Recanati, *Mental Files*, Oxford University Press, 2012. Develops a file-based theory of reference that incorporates externalist insights.
