## Primary Sources

- Saul Kripke, *Naming and Necessity*, Harvard University Press, 1980 (lectures 1970). The founding text of the causal theory of reference; argues that names are rigid designators fixed by initial baptism and causal-historical chains.

- Saul Kripke, "Identity and Necessity," in Munitz (ed.), *Identity and Individuation*, New York University Press, 1971. Early statement of Kripke's views on modality and the necessary a posteriori.

- Hilary Putnam, "The Meaning of 'Meaning'," in *Mind, Language and Reality*, Cambridge University Press, 1975. Applies the causal-historical picture to natural kind terms; argues for rigid designation for kind terms.

- Gareth Evans, "The Causal Theory of Names," *Aristotelian Society Supplementary Volume* 47, 1973. Early critique and refinement of the causal theory; introduces the "Madagascar" case showing that causal chains can "misfire."

- Michael Devitt and Kim Sterelny, *Language and Reality*, 2nd ed., MIT Press, 1999. Develops the causal theory with attention to the grounding problem and the role of descriptions.

- Ruth Barcan Marcus, "Essentialism in Modal Logic," *Noûs* 1, 1967. Important precursor to Kripke; develops quantified modal logic and discusses necessity of identity.

- Keith Donnellan, "Reference and Definite Descriptions," *Philosophical Review* 75, 1966. Distinguishes referential and attributive uses of descriptions; influences the causal-historical picture.

- Michael Dummett, *Frege: Philosophy of Language*, Harvard University Press, 1973. The most systematic defense of a descriptive theory; the main contrast position to Kripke.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Scott Soames, *Beyond Rigidity*, Oxford University Press, 2002. Extends Kripkean semantics and responds to objections about natural kind terms and the necessary a posteriori.

- Nathan Salmon, *Frege's Puzzle*, MIT Press, 1986. Defends direct reference theory (Millian view) and grapples with Frege's puzzle about cognitive significance.

- Scott Soames, *Philosophical Analysis in the Twentieth Century*, Volume 2, Princeton University Press, 2003. Historical and critical treatment of Kripke's revolution; chapters on naming, necessity, and rigid designation.

- Stephen Schwartz (ed.), *Naming, Necessity, and Natural Kinds*, Cornell University Press, 1977. Classic anthology; includes key papers by Kripke, Putnam, Donnellan, and early responses.

- Jason Stanley, "Names and Rigid Designation," in Hale and Wright (eds.), *A Companion to the Philosophy of Language*, Blackwell, 1997. Survey of the debate; clear on the distinction between semantic and epistemic necessity.

- Ásta Sveinsdóttir, "The Metaphysics of Sex and Gender," in Ásta, *Categories We Live By*, Oxford University Press, 2018. Applies reference-theoretic tools to social kinds; raises questions about the extension of the Kripkean framework.

- Timothy Williamson, *Modal Logic as Metaphysics*, Oxford University Press, 2013. Defends a necessitist modal metaphysics that builds on but also challenges Kripkean foundations.
