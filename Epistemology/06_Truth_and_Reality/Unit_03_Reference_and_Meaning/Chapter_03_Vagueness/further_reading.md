## Primary Sources

- Timothy Williamson, *Vagueness*, Routledge, 1994. The definitive statement of epistemicism: vague predicates have sharp extensions, and our ignorance of cut-off points is ineliminable but not semantically significant.

- Roy Sorensen, *Blindspots*, Oxford University Press, 1988. An early epistemicist treatment of vagueness; argues that vague predicates have sharp boundaries we are constitutionally unable to know.

- Kit Fine, "Vagueness, Truth and Logic," *Synthese* 30, 1975. The founding paper of supervaluationism; introduces the framework of completions and super-truth.

- Rosanna Keefe, *Theories of Vagueness*, Cambridge University Press, 2000. Comprehensive and balanced survey; defends supervaluationism as the most defensible approach.

- Dominic Hyde, *Vagueness, Logic and Ontology*, Ashgate, 2008. Survey with sympathy for dialethic approaches; includes discussion of metaphysical vagueness.

- Mark Sainsbury, "Concepts Without Boundaries," in Keefe and Smith (eds.), *Vagueness: A Reader*, MIT Press, 1997. Argues that vague predicates have no sharp extensions even in principle—an alternative to epistemicism.

- Crispin Wright, "Further Reflections on the Sorites Paradox," *Philosophical Topics* 15, 1987. Develops a "forced march" version of the sorites and argues for a logic that tolerates contradictions in the penumbra.

- Hartry Field, "No Fact of the Matter," *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 81, 2003. Argues for a metalinguistic approach to vagueness: there is no fact of the matter about borderline cases.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Rosanna Keefe and Peter Smith (eds.), *Vagueness: A Reader*, MIT Press, 1997. The essential anthology; includes classic papers by Fine, Wright, Williamson, Evans, and Tye.

- Gideon Rosen and J.J.C. Smith, "Worldly Indeterminacy: A Rough Guide," *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 82, 2004. Survey of positions on metaphysical (ontic) vagueness; distinguishes semantic and metaphysical approaches.

- Patrick Greenough, "Contextualism About Vagueness," *Philosophical Perspectives* 17, 2003. Develops and defends a contextualist approach to vagueness.

- Mark Colyvan, "Vagueness and Truth," in Dyke (ed.), *From Truth to Reality*, Routledge, 2009. Examines how theories of truth interact with theories of vagueness.

- Elizabeth Barnes and J.R.G. Williams, "A Theory of Metaphysical Indeterminacy," *Oxford Studies in Metaphysics* 6, 2011. Develops a formal account of ontic vagueness using indeterminate set membership.

- Nicholas Smith, *Vagueness and Degrees of Truth*, Oxford University Press, 2008. Develops a degree-theoretic approach; argues it handles the sorites and borderline cases better than competitors.

- Diana Raffman, *Unruly Words: A Study of Vague Language*, Oxford University Press, 2014. Defends a contextualist account of vagueness grounded in psychological findings about categorization.
