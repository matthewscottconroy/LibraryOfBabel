# Essay Questions: Unit 6 — Truth and Reality

---

**1. Deflationism about Truth: Statement and Assessment**

Deflationary theories of truth hold that there is no substantial metaphysical property of truth — the truth predicate serves merely a logical-expressive function. Carefully distinguish disquotationalism (Quine), minimalism (Horwich), and prosentential theories. What is the strongest argument for deflationism (the claim that substantive truth theories face insuperable difficulties)? What is the strongest objection (that deflationism cannot account for the role of truth in explanations of successful action and inquiry)? Is there a principled way to be a deflationist while accepting that truth matters for epistemology?

---

**2. The Correspondence Theory and Its Difficulties**

The correspondence theory holds that a proposition is true iff it corresponds to a fact. Critically examine the ontology required by this view: what are facts, and what is the relation of correspondence? Assess the truth-maker version of correspondence theory (Armstrong, Restall): for every truth, there is an entity whose existence necessitates it. Does truth-maker theory avoid the difficulties of classical correspondence (the problem of negative facts, general facts, modal facts)? Is the correspondence theory more metaphysically committal than the epistemological project of understanding truth requires?

---

**3. Dummett's Anti-Realism and the Rejection of Bivalence**

Michael Dummett argues that semantic realism — the view that statements have determinate truth values independently of our capacity to verify them — is incoherent. If the meaning of a statement is its use, then there can be no truth that transcends all possible evidence. Critically assess Dummett's argument. Does the rejection of the law of excluded middle for undecidable statements (such as statements about the distant past or abstract mathematical structures) follow from semantic anti-realism? What are the most serious objections to Dummett's position — the self-refutation objection, the objection from mathematical practice, or the objection from the theoretical costs of rejecting classical logic?

---

**4. Tarski's T-Schema: Content, Limits, and Philosophical Significance**

Tarski's material adequacy condition for a theory of truth requires it to entail all instances of the schema "'S' is true iff S." Assess the philosophical significance of Tarski's contribution. Is Tarski's formal definition of truth for formalized languages a philosophical theory of truth, or does it merely provide a technical apparatus that leaves the philosophical question open? Evaluate Hartry Field's argument that a genuinely naturalistic correspondence theory must go beyond Tarski by providing a theory of reference that connects language to the world.

---

**5. Coherence Theories of Truth: Prospects and Problems**

Coherence theories identify truth with membership in a maximally coherent and comprehensive belief system. Evaluate the coherence theory against the two most serious objections: (a) the alternative coherent systems objection — there may be multiple equally coherent but mutually inconsistent systems, each defining its own "truth"; (b) the isolation objection — a perfectly coherent system of beliefs that has no contact with the actual world would count as true. Can a coherence theorist about truth also be an anti-relativist? How does coherentism about truth relate to coherentism about justification?

---

**6. Semantic Externalism and the Epistemology of Reference**

Kripke's causal theory of reference and Putnam's semantic externalism both imply that the content of thoughts and utterances is partly determined by the external environment, not merely by intrinsic mental states ("meanings ain't in the head"). What are the epistemological implications of semantic externalism? Does the Twin Earth argument (Putnam) establish that water thoughts and twater thoughts differ in content even if the thinkers are internally identical? How does externalism about mental content interact with the epistemology of self-knowledge and introspection?

---

**7. Truth and Epistemic Justification: Are They Extensionally Identical?**

Epistemic theories of truth hold that truth is what would be believed under idealized epistemic conditions (Peirce, Putnam's internal realism, Dummett's warrant-assertibility). Critically assess whether epistemic and truth-conditional notions of truth can be identified. Does Putnam's internal realism — truth as idealized rational acceptability within a conceptual scheme — succeed as a response to both metaphysical realism and radical relativism? What are the most serious problems with identifying truth and ideal justification?
