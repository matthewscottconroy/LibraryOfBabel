## Primary Sources

- René Descartes, *Meditations on First Philosophy*, trans. Cottingham, Cambridge University Press, 1996 (orig. 1641). The primary text for substance dualism; the Sixth Meditation establishes the real distinction between mind and body.

- René Descartes, *The Passions of the Soul*, trans. Voss, Hackett, 1989 (orig. 1649). Descartes's attempted account of mind-body interaction through the pineal gland.

- Nicolas Malebranche, *The Search After Truth*, trans. Lennon and Olscamp, Cambridge University Press, 1997 (orig. 1674–75). Develops occasionalism as a solution to the interaction problem.

- G.W. Leibniz, "New System of Nature," in *Philosophical Essays*, trans. Ariew and Garber, Hackett, 1989 (orig. 1695). Introduces pre-established harmony as an alternative to occasionalism and interactionism.

- Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia, correspondence with Descartes (1643), in *The Correspondence between Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia and René Descartes*, ed. Shapiro, University of Chicago Press, 2007. The first and sharpest statement of the interaction problem.

- Frank Jackson, "Epiphenomenal Qualia," *Philosophical Quarterly* 32, 1982. The knowledge argument motivates property dualism; essential for distinguishing substance from property dualism.

- David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind*, Oxford University Press, 1996. Contemporary defense of property dualism and naturalistic dualism; motivates dualism from the hard problem.

## Key Secondary Sources

- John Cottingham, *Descartes*, Blackwell, 1986. Clear philosophical biography; chapter on the mind-body problem is the best entry point.

- Jaegwon Kim, *Mind in a Physical World*, MIT Press, 1998. Argues that dualist positions cannot account for mental causation; the most influential physicalist critique.

- Jaegwon Kim, *Philosophy of Mind*, 3rd ed., Westview Press, 2010. The best textbook treatment of the full range of mind-body positions.

- Eric Olson, *The Human Animal*, Oxford University Press, 1997. Develops animalism as an alternative to Cartesian substance dualism about personal identity.

- Geoffrey Madell, *Mind and Materialism*, Edinburgh University Press, 1988. Defends substance dualism against physicalist alternatives.

- Howard Robinson, "Dualism," in *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2020. Comprehensive survey of arguments for and against substance and property dualism.

- Brie Gertler, *Self-Knowledge*, Routledge, 2011. Defends a form of property dualism grounded in the special character of first-person authority.

- Richard Swinburne, *The Evolution of the Soul*, Oxford University Press, 1986. Defends a version of substance dualism that connects it to personal identity and religious philosophy.
