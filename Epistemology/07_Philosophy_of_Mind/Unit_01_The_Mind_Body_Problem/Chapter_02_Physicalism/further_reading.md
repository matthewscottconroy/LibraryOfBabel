## Primary Sources

- U.T. Place, "Is Consciousness a Brain Process?" *British Journal of Psychology* 47, 1956. Founding paper of the type identity theory; argues that consciousness is identical with a brain process.

- J.J.C. Smart, "Sensations and Brain Processes," *Philosophical Review* 68, 1959. Defends the type identity theory and introduces the "topic-neutral" analysis of mental terms.

- Hilary Putnam, "Psychological Predicates," in Capitan and Merrill (eds.), *Art, Mind and Religion*, University of Pittsburgh Press, 1967. The multiple realizability argument; argues that mental states can be realized in different physical substrates.

- Donald Davidson, "Mental Events," in *Essays on Actions and Events*, Oxford University Press, 1980 (essay 1970). Introduces anomalous monism; argues for token identity without type identity and for the irreducibility of the mental.

- Paul Churchland, "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes," *Journal of Philosophy* 78, 1981. Argues that folk psychological concepts are a false theory that should be replaced by neuroscience.

- Patricia Churchland, *Neurophilosophy*, MIT Press, 1986. The most comprehensive development of eliminative materialism; integrates neuroscience and philosophy.

- Andrew Melnyk, *A Physicalist Manifesto*, Cambridge University Press, 2003. A rigorous statement of physicalism as a supervenience thesis; responds to multiple challenges.

- Jaegwon Kim, *Supervenience and Mind*, Cambridge University Press, 1993. Essays developing and critically evaluating physicalist supervenience theses.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Sydney Shoemaker, *Physical Realization*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Develops a nuanced account of physical realization compatible with multiple realizability.

- David Lewis, "Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications," *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 50, 1972. Argues for functionalist reduction of mental states to physical states via Ramsey sentences.

- Ned Block and Jerry Fodor, "What Psychological States Are Not," *Philosophical Review* 81, 1972. Influential objection to type identity theory using multiple realizability.

- Jaegwon Kim, *Mind in a Physical World*, MIT Press, 1998. Argues that non-reductive physicalism is unstable and that the mind-body problem requires either reduction or elimination.

- Stephen Yablo, "Mental Causation," *Philosophical Review* 101, 1992. Proposes a proportionality account of mental causation that challenges the exclusion argument.

- Brian McLaughlin and Karen Bennett, "Supervenience," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2018. The most comprehensive survey of supervenience concepts; essential for understanding physicalism's commitments.
