## Primary Sources

- Hilary Putnam, "The Nature of Mental States," in *Mind, Language and Reality*, Cambridge University Press, 1975 (essay 1967). Classic statement of machine functionalism; mental states are defined by causal-computational roles.

- Ned Block, "Troubles with Functionalism," *Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 9, 1978. Introduces the China brain and inverted qualia objections; distinguishes absent and inverted qualia problems.

- Ned Block, "Psychologism and Behaviorism," *Philosophical Review* 90, 1981. Argues that functionalism risks behaviorism and that genuine psychology requires more than functional description.

- David Armstrong, *A Materialist Theory of the Mind*, Routledge, 1968. Causal functionalism: mental states are states apt to cause certain behaviors, identifiable with physical states.

- David Lewis, "Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications," *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 50, 1972. Uses Ramsey-sentence technique to give a reductive functionalist analysis.

- Jerry Fodor, *The Language of Thought*, Crowell, 1975. Develops computational functionalism; mental states are functional states involving representations in a language of thought.

- John Searle, "Minds, Brains and Programs," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 3, 1980. The Chinese Room argument against strong AI and computational functionalism.

- Sydney Shoemaker, "Absent Qualia Are Impossible," *Philosophical Review* 90, 1981. Defends functionalism against the absent qualia objection.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Ned Block (ed.), *Readings in Philosophy of Psychology*, Volumes 1–2, Harvard University Press, 1980–81. Canonical anthology; essential for understanding the functionalism debates of the 1970s–80s.

- Brian Loar, *Mind and Meaning*, Cambridge University Press, 1981. Develops a sophisticated functionalist semantics.

- Jaegwon Kim, *Philosophy of Mind*, 3rd ed., Westview Press, 2010. Best textbook treatment of functionalism and its rivals.

- Georges Rey, *Contemporary Philosophy of Mind*, Blackwell, 1997. Defends a broadly functionalist-computational view of mind.

- Jerry Fodor, *Psychosemantics*, MIT Press, 1987. Develops the functionalist account in terms of language of thought and narrow content.

- Joseph Levine, *Purple Haze: The Puzzle of Consciousness*, Oxford University Press, 2001. Argues that functionalism leaves an "explanatory gap" for phenomenal consciousness.

- David Chalmers, "Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia," in *The Conscious Mind*, Oxford University Press, 1996, ch. 7. A series of thought experiments against absent qualia functionalism.

- Alastair Noe and Evan Thompson (eds.), *Vision and Mind*, MIT Press, 2002. Connects functionalism to debates about visual consciousness and phenomenology.
