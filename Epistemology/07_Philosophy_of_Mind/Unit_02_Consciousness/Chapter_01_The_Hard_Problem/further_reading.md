## Primary Sources

- David Chalmers, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 2, 1995. The paper that introduced the "hard problem" as a distinct and central challenge for philosophy of mind.

- David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind*, Oxford University Press, 1996. The book-length development of the hard problem, zombie argument, and naturalistic dualism.

- Joseph Levine, "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap," *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 64, 1983. Introduces the explanatory gap; argues that even if mind-brain identity is true, we cannot explain why neural processes produce phenomenal experience.

- Ned Block, "On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 18, 1995. Distinguishes phenomenal consciousness from access consciousness; clarifies what the hard problem is a problem for.

- Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" *Philosophical Review* 83, 1974. Argues that the subjective character of consciousness resists objective, third-person scientific description.

- Colin McGinn, "Can We Solve the Mind-Body Problem?" *Mind* 98, 1989. Argues that the hard problem is cognitively closed to human minds—a mysterian position.

- Daniel Dennett, *Consciousness Explained*, Little, Brown, 1991. The most comprehensive attempt to dissolve rather than solve the hard problem; argues that phenomenal consciousness is a cognitive illusion.

- Daniel Dennett, "Quining Qualia," in Lycan (ed.), *Mind and Cognition*, Blackwell, 1990. Argues that the concept of qualia is incoherent and should be rejected.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Ned Block, Owen Flanagan, and Güven Güzeldere (eds.), *The Nature of Consciousness*, MIT Press, 1997. The essential anthology; covers the hard problem from multiple angles.

- Jonathan Shear (ed.), *Explaining Consciousness: The Hard Problem*, MIT Press, 1997. Responses to Chalmers's hard problem from across the discipline.

- Peter Carruthers and Andrew Jokic (eds.), *Consciousness: New Philosophical Perspectives*, Oxford University Press, 2003. Contemporary debates on phenomenal consciousness.

- John Levine, *Purple Haze: The Puzzle of Consciousness*, Oxford University Press, 2001. Book-length development of the explanatory gap; argues that physicalism cannot bridge it.

- Kathleen Akins, "What Is It Like to Be Boring and Myopic?" in Dahlbom (ed.), *Dennett and His Critics*, Blackwell, 1993. An empirical and philosophical examination of Nagel's bat case.

- Brian Loar, "Phenomenal States," *Philosophical Perspectives* 4, 1990. Defends the phenomenal concepts strategy as a response to the knowledge argument and hard problem.

- Keith Frankish, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23, 2016. Defends a "type-B illusionism"—the hard problem is a cognitive illusion created by the way we represent experience.
