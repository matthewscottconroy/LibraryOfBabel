## Primary Sources

- Frank Jackson, "Epiphenomenal Qualia," *Philosophical Quarterly* 32, 1982. The Mary's Room thought experiment; argues that physical knowledge leaves out phenomenal facts.

- Frank Jackson, "What Mary Didn't Know," *Journal of Philosophy* 83, 1986. Shorter, cleaner statement of the knowledge argument; responds to early objections.

- Frank Jackson, "Mind and Illusion," in O'Hear (ed.), *Minds and Persons*, Cambridge University Press, 2003. Jackson recants the knowledge argument and argues that Mary does not acquire new factual knowledge.

- David Chalmers and Frank Jackson, "Conceptual Analysis and Reductive Explanation," *Philosophical Review* 110, 2001. Defense of a priori conceptual analysis in philosophy of mind against Jackson's recantation.

- Lawrence Nemirow, "Physicalism and the Cognitive Role of Acquaintance," in Lycan (ed.), *Mind and Cognition*, Blackwell, 1990. Defends the ability hypothesis as a response to the knowledge argument.

- David Lewis, "What Experience Teaches," in Lycan (ed.), *Mind and Cognition*, Blackwell, 1990. The classical defense of the ability hypothesis.

- Brian Loar, "Phenomenal States," *Philosophical Perspectives* 4, 1990. Develops the phenomenal concepts strategy against the knowledge argument.

- Daniel Dennett, "Quining Qualia," in Lycan (ed.), *Mind and Cognition*, Blackwell, 1990. Argues that all four defining features of qualia are incoherent.

- Sydney Shoemaker, "Qualia and Consciousness," *Mind* 100, 1991. Defends the reality of qualia while arguing they are functional properties.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Peter Ludlow, Yujin Nagasawa, and Daniel Stoljar (eds.), *There's Something About Mary*, MIT Press, 2004. Comprehensive anthology on the knowledge argument; includes Jackson's original papers and major responses.

- Torin Alter and Sven Walter (eds.), *Phenomenal Concepts and Phenomenal Knowledge*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Best collection on the phenomenal concepts strategy.

- Michael Tye, *Ten Problems of Consciousness*, MIT Press, 1995. Develops a representationalist account of qualia; argues that phenomenal properties are wide representational properties.

- Martine Nida-Rümelin, "Qualia: The Knowledge Argument," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2015. Comprehensive survey of the debate.

- Ned Block, "Qualia," in Guttenplan (ed.), *A Companion to the Philosophy of Mind*, Blackwell, 1994. Clear survey of positions on qualia.

- Keith Frankish, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23, 2016. Develops illusionism: phenomenal consciousness as we conceive it doesn't exist; what exists is functional states we misrepresent.

- David Papineau, *Thinking About Consciousness*, Oxford University Press, 2002. Defends physicalism and the phenomenal concepts strategy; argues that intuitions against physicalism arise from features of phenomenal concepts, not phenomenal facts.
