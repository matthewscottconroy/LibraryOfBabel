## Primary Sources

- David Rosenthal, "Two Concepts of Consciousness," *Philosophical Studies* 49, 1986. Introduces the higher-order thought (HOT) theory; a mental state is conscious iff it is accompanied by a higher-order thought that one is in that state.

- David Rosenthal, *Consciousness and Mind*, Oxford University Press, 2005. Full development of HOT theory.

- Bernard Baars, *A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness*, Cambridge University Press, 1988. Global workspace theory: consciousness is the broadcast of information to a "global workspace" accessible to multiple cognitive processes.

- Stanislas Dehaene, *Consciousness and the Brain*, Viking, 2014. Develops and empirically supports global workspace theory with neuroscientific evidence.

- Giulio Tononi, "Consciousness as Integrated Information: A Provisional Manifesto," *Biological Bulletin* 215, 2008. Introduces IIT; consciousness is identical with integrated information (Phi).

- Giulio Tononi and Christof Koch, "Consciousness: Here, There, and Everywhere?" *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 370, 2015. The most accessible statement of IIT and its implications.

- David Armstrong, *A Materialist Theory of the Mind*, Routledge, 1968. Develops a higher-order perception theory of consciousness; introspection as inner perception.

- William Lycan, *Consciousness and Experience*, MIT Press, 1996. Develops a higher-order representation theory of consciousness; discusses the representationalist framework.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Ned Block (ed.), *Readings in Philosophy of Psychology*, Volume 1, Harvard University Press, 1980. Contains key historical papers on the structure of consciousness and its theories.

- Max Velmans and Susan Schneider (eds.), *The Blackwell Companion to Consciousness*, Blackwell, 2007. Comprehensive survey of all major theories.

- Ned Block, "Comparing the Major Theories of Consciousness," in Gazzaniga (ed.), *The Cognitive Neurosciences IV*, MIT Press, 2009. Critical comparative assessment of HOT, GWT, and IIT.

- Scott Frankish and William Ramsey (eds.), *The Cambridge Handbook of Cognitive Science*, Cambridge University Press, 2012. Situates theories of consciousness within the broader landscape of cognitive science.

- Christof Koch, *The Feeling of Life Itself*, MIT Press, 2019. Popular but rigorous treatment of IIT and the neuroscience of consciousness.

- Jesse Prinz, *The Conscious Brain*, Oxford University Press, 2012. Defends an attention-based, empirical theory of consciousness; critical of IIT and HOT theories.

- Richard Brown, Hakwan Lau, and Joseph LeDoux, "Understanding the Higher-Order Approach to Consciousness," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 23, 2019. Recent defense and refinement of HOT theory in response to empirical challenges.
