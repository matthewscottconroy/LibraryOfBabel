# Panpsychism

Panpsychism is the view that mentality or experience is a fundamental and ubiquitous feature of reality — that all physical entities, or entities at some fundamental physical level, have some form of inner life or experiential property. This is not the view that rocks have sophisticated thoughts, but that even fundamental physical entities have some form of proto-experiential properties.

## Motivation

Panpsychism is motivated primarily by the hard problem. Standard physicalism seems unable to explain why any physical process gives rise to phenomenal experience. The explanatory gap between physical description and experiential description cannot be closed by any account that treats experience as derived from or emergent from non-experiential properties. If consciousness cannot emerge from nothing experiential, perhaps experiential properties are present at the fundamental level.

Galen Strawson's "Realistic Monism" (2006) makes this argument forcefully: if you are a monist (all that exists is physical), and if you accept that consciousness exists (as surely you must, since you have it), then you must accept either that consciousness is identical to some physical property (which the hard problem shows is very difficult) or that consciousness was "present" in some form at the fundamental physical level all along. Emergence *ex nihilo* is impossible: you can't get experience from something completely non-experiential.

## Forms of Panpsychism

**Panpsychism (full)**: All entities, including fundamental physical entities (quarks, electrons), have experiential properties. These are "proto-experiential" or "micro-experiential" properties — not rich consciousness but some form of inner experiential property.

**Panprotopsychism**: Fundamental physical entities have properties that are neither purely physical nor fully mental, but proto-mental — disposed to give rise to experience when organized in the right ways. This is the view of some who want to avoid the explanatory gap without claiming that electrons have rich qualia.

**Cosmopsychism**: The cosmos as a whole is conscious, and individual consciousnesses are derivative from or parts of this cosmic consciousness. This is Schopenhauer's view (the Will is the cosmic reality) and has affinities with certain forms of mysticism.

## The Combination Problem

The most challenging objection to panpsychism is the *combination problem*: how do micro-experiential properties of fundamental particles combine to form the rich, unified phenomenal consciousness of humans?

The problem is not just additive: you can't simply sum up the micro-experiences of billions of particles and get a unified human consciousness. The unity and richness of conscious experience — the integration of information from many sources into a single stream — seems to require something beyond mere aggregation.

Proposed solutions:
- **Constitutive panpsychism**: Micro-experiences constitute (but are not simply summed to) macro-experiences, via some constitutional relation.
- **Emergent combination**: Macro-experience emerges from the interaction of micro-experiences in ways that the combination problem itself cannot anticipate.
- **Cosmopsychism as alternative**: If the cosmos is the fundamental conscious entity, the "combination problem" is replaced by a "decomposition problem" — how individual conscious states arise from the cosmic consciousness.

## Philip Goff's Defense

Philip Goff has become the leading contemporary defender of panpsychism, particularly in *Galileo's Error* (2019). Goff argues that Galileo's "error" was to define matter in purely quantitative terms (mass, charge, position) while leaving out quality entirely. This created the hard problem from the start: if the physical is defined as purely quantitative, experiential qualities can never be derived from it.

Goff's solution: experience is a fundamental quality of matter. Physics tells us about the quantitative structure of matter; consciousness tells us about the qualitative nature that underlies that structure.

## Panpsychism and Physics

Contemporary physics is silent about the intrinsic nature of fundamental physical entities. Quantum mechanics describes electron behavior in terms of mathematical structure; it says nothing about what electrons are intrinsically. Panpsychism proposes to fill this gap: the intrinsic nature of fundamental physical entities includes proto-experiential properties. This is not in conflict with physics (which describes structural/relational properties); it supplements physics with an account of intrinsic nature.

This "Russellian monism" — holding that consciousness is the intrinsic nature that underlies physical structure — has been developed by Chalmers, Strawson, and Goff as a potentially coherent account that navigates between substance dualism and eliminative materialism.
