## Primary Sources

- Franz Brentano, *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint*, trans. Rancurello, Terrell, and McAlister, Routledge, 1995 (orig. 1874). The founding text; introduces intentional inexistence as the mark of the mental.

- Alexius Meinong, "The Theory of Objects," in Chisholm (ed.), *Realism and the Background of Phenomenology*, Free Press, 1960 (orig. 1904). The most developed theory of non-existent objects; introduces the distinction between being and existence.

- Bertrand Russell, "On Denoting," *Mind* 14, 1905. Russell's attack on Meinong; develops the theory of descriptions as an alternative to a theory of non-existent objects.

- Edmund Husserl, *Logical Investigations*, trans. Findlay, Routledge, 2001 (orig. 1900–01). Phenomenological development of intentionality; introduces the notion of intentional content (noema) and the distinction between act and object.

- John Searle, *Intentionality*, Cambridge University Press, 1983. Develops a theory of intentionality in terms of "conditions of satisfaction" and the relationship between intentionality and the background.

- Roderick Chisholm, "Intentionality," in Edwards (ed.), *Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, Macmillan, 1967. Clear survey of intentionality's defining features.

- Tim Crane, "Intentionality as the Mark of the Mental," *Royal Institute of Philosophy Supplement* 43, 1998. Defends Brentano's thesis against objections.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Tim Crane, *Elements of Mind*, Oxford University Press, 2001. Accessible and philosophically rigorous introduction to intentionality and consciousness.

- Tim Crane, *The Objects of Thought*, Oxford University Press, 2013. Defends intentionalism and a neo-Meinongian approach to non-existent objects.

- Alex Byrne, "Intentionalism Defended," *Philosophical Review* 110, 2001. Systematic defense of the claim that phenomenal states are intentional states.

- Fred Dretske, *Knowledge and the Flow of Information*, MIT Press, 1981. Introduces the causal-informational framework for intentionality.

- Uriah Kriegel, *The Sources of Intentionality*, Oxford University Press, 2011. Develops a phenomenological theory of intentionality; argues that intentionality requires phenomenal consciousness.

- Gregory McCulloch, *The Mind and Its World*, Routledge, 1995. A philosophical survey of intentionality from Brentano through contemporary debates.

- Ned Block, "Mental Paint," in Hahn and Ramberg (eds.), *Reflections and Replies*, MIT Press, 2003. Distinguishes different senses of intentionality and their relationship to phenomenal consciousness.
