## Primary Sources

- Fred Dretske, *Knowledge and the Flow of Information*, MIT Press, 1981. Develops causal-informational semantics: a mental state represents F iff it carries the information that F is present.

- Fred Dretske, *Explaining Behavior*, MIT Press, 1988. Extends the informational account to explain intentional action; introduces the teleological element.

- Ruth Millikan, *Language, Thought, and Other Biological Categories*, MIT Press, 1984. Develops teleosemantics: a representation's content is determined by the function it evolved to fulfill.

- Ruth Millikan, "Biosemantics," *Journal of Philosophy* 86, 1989. Accessible statement of the biosemantic view; introduces the device-description distinction.

- Jerry Fodor, *The Meaning of Mental Representations*, MIT Press, 1990. Develops an asymmetric dependence theory of content to address the disjunction problem.

- Jerry Fodor, *Psychosemantics*, MIT Press, 1987. Argues for the indispensability of intentional psychology and develops the LOT framework.

- Colin McGinn, "The Structure of Content," in Woodfield (ed.), *Thought and Object*, Oxford University Press, 1982. Analyzes narrow and wide content; influential in fixing the conceptual landscape.

- Gabriel Segal, *A Slim Book About Narrow Content*, MIT Press, 2000. Defends the psychological relevance of narrow content against externalist critics.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Jerry Fodor, "A Theory of Content I and II," in *A Theory of Content and Other Essays*, MIT Press, 1990. Most systematic presentation of the asymmetric dependence theory.

- Karen Neander, *A Mark of the Mental*, MIT Press, 2017. Develops a teleological theory of mental content drawing on neuroscience and biology.

- David Papineau, *Philosophical Naturalism*, Blackwell, 1993. Defends teleosemantics; connects biological function to semantic content.

- Louise Antony and Norbert Hornstein (eds.), *Chomsky and His Critics*, Blackwell, 2003. Contains debates relevant to narrow content and the role of syntax in intentionality.

- Kim Sterelny, "Representation, Teleosemantics, and the Problem of Holism," in Loewer and Rey (eds.), *Meaning in Mind*, Blackwell, 1991. Survey of teleosemantic approaches and their challenges.

- Tyler Burge, *Origins of Objectivity*, Oxford University Press, 2010. Defends a perceptual-causal account of content; argues that representational content is grounded in objective perceptual relations.

- Daniel Dennett, *The Intentional Stance*, MIT Press, 1987. Develops interpretationism: mental content is ascribed from the "intentional stance" for predictive purposes, not discovered as an intrinsic feature.
