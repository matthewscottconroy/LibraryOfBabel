## Primary Sources

- Jerry Fodor, *The Language of Thought*, Crowell, 1975. The founding text; argues that thinking requires a system of structured mental representations with a combinatorial syntax.

- Jerry Fodor, *Psychosemantics*, MIT Press, 1987. Connects LOT to the theory of mental content; defends intentional psychology against eliminativism.

- Jerry Fodor, *The Modularity of Mind*, MIT Press, 1983. Argues for a modular architecture of the mind; limits LOT to central cognition, with modular input systems.

- Jerry Fodor, *The Mind Doesn't Work That Way*, MIT Press, 2000. Admits that LOT faces the "frame problem" and cannot explain central cognition as well as peripheral processing.

- John Searle, "Minds, Brains and Programs," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 3, 1980. The Chinese Room argument against the computational conception of mind that LOT requires.

- Paul Smolensky, "On the Proper Treatment of Connectionism," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 11, 1988. Develops a connectionist alternative to LOT; argues for distributed representations.

- Jerry Fodor and Zenon Pylyshyn, "Connectionism and Cognitive Architecture," *Cognition* 28, 1988. The classic defense of LOT against connectionist challenges; argues that productivity and systematicity require classical architecture.

- Andy Clark, *Associative Engines*, MIT Press, 1993. Defends connectionism against Fodor's systematicity argument.

## Key Secondary Sources

- José Luis Bermúdez, *Thinking Without Words*, Oxford University Press, 2003. Defends the view that animals and infants have genuine propositional thought without language; extends and challenges LOT.

- Tim Crane, "The Language of Thought: No Syntax Without Semantics," *Mind and Language* 5, 1990. Critical examination of the relationship between syntactic and semantic properties in LOT.

- Brian McLaughlin and Jonathan Cohen (eds.), *Contemporary Debates in Philosophy of Mind*, Blackwell, 2007. Contains debates on LOT and its rivals.

- Fred Adams and Kenneth Campbell, "Fodor and the Future of Cognitive Science," *Minds and Machines* 29, 2019. Assesses LOT's legacy after Fodor's later pessimism.

- Peter Gärdenfors, *The Geometry of Meaning*, MIT Press, 2014. Develops a conceptual spaces alternative to the LOT framework.

- Susan Schneider, *The Language of Thought*, MIT Press, 2011. The most recent and systematic defense of LOT; updates the framework to handle later challenges.

- Daniel Dennett, "The Language of Thought," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 3, 1980. The most influential early critique; argues that LOT is not needed to explain cognition.
