## Primary Sources

- Jaegwon Kim, "Epiphenomenal and Supervenient Causation," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 9, 1984. Early statement of the exclusion problem for non-reductive physicalism.

- Jaegwon Kim, *Mind in a Physical World*, MIT Press, 1998. Full development of the causal exclusion argument; argues non-reductive physicalism is unstable.

- Jaegwon Kim, *Physicalism, or Something Near Enough*, Princeton University Press, 2005. Kim's mature position; accepts reduction for some mental properties while acknowledging consciousness may resist it.

- Donald Davidson, "Mental Events," in *Essays on Actions and Events*, Oxford University Press, 1980. Anomalous monism; token identity without type-type laws; mental events causally efficacious qua physical.

- Stephen Yablo, "Mental Causation," *Philosophical Review* 101, 1992. Proportionality account; mental properties cause effects because they are proportional to the relevant effect.

- Ernest Sosa, "Mind-Body Interaction and Supervenient Causation," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 9, 1984. Defends the compatibility of supervenience and causation.

- Karen Bennett, "Why the Exclusion Problem Seems Intractable, and How, Just Maybe, to Tract It," *Noûs* 37, 2003. Systematic examination of responses to the exclusion problem.

- Peter Menzies, "The Causal Efficacy of Mental States," in McLaughlin (ed.), *Dretske and His Critics*, Blackwell, 1991. Counterfactual approach to mental causation.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Ned Block, "Do Causal Powers Drain Away?" *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 67, 2003. Argues against Kim's exclusion argument using metaphysical considerations.

- Sydney Shoemaker, *Physical Realization*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Argues that mental properties are causally efficacious in virtue of the physical properties that realize them.

- Dean Zimmerman, "Mental Causation and the Persistence of Intention," *Philosophical Topics* 26, 1999. Defends non-reductive physicalism against the exclusion argument.

- Sophie Gibb, E.J. Lowe, and Rögnvaldur Ingthorsson (eds.), *Mental Causation and Ontology*, Oxford University Press, 2013. Comprehensive collection on the exclusion problem and responses.

- Carl Gillett, *Reduction and Emergence in Science and Philosophy*, Cambridge University Press, 2016. Reassesses the exclusion argument in light of scientific practice and the metaphysics of realization.

- Ralph Wedgwood, "The Qualitative Content of Mental Causation," *Ratio* 13, 2000. Argues that qualitative mental properties can be genuinely causally efficacious.
