## Primary Sources

- Andy Clark and David Chalmers, "The Extended Mind," *Analysis* 58, 1998. The founding paper of the extended mind thesis; introduces the parity principle and Otto's notebook.

- Andy Clark, *Being There: Putting Brain, Body, and World Together Again*, MIT Press, 1997. Extended treatment of embodied and embedded cognition; critique of GOFAI and connectionism.

- Andy Clark, *Supersizing the Mind*, Oxford University Press, 2008. Full development of the extended mind thesis; responds to objections and extends the framework.

- Maurice Merleau-Ponty, *Phenomenology of Perception*, trans. Landes, Routledge, 2012 (orig. 1945). The classic statement of embodied cognition from phenomenology; the blind man's cane as an extension of the body schema.

- Hubert Dreyfus, *Being-in-the-World*, MIT Press, 1991. Commentary on Heidegger's *Being and Time* with application to cognitive science; argues for primacy of practical embodied engagement.

- Francisco Varela, Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch, *The Embodied Mind*, MIT Press, 1991. Introduces enactivism; cognition as enacted by an organism's structural coupling with its environment.

- Lawrence Barsalou, "Grounded Cognition," *Annual Review of Psychology* 59, 2008. Survey of empirical evidence for embodied cognition; argues that cognition depends on sensorimotor simulations.

- Evan Thompson, *Mind in Life*, Harvard University Press, 2007. Develops enactivism and its implications for biology, consciousness, and cognitive science.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Robert Rupert, "Challenges to the Hypothesis of Extended Cognition," *Journal of Philosophy* 101, 2004. Systematic challenge; argues that the parity principle does not establish genuine cognitive extension.

- Frederick Adams and Kenneth Aizawa, *The Bounds of Cognition*, Blackwell, 2008. Argues against the extended mind; the mark of the cognitive requires intrinsic, non-derived content.

- Mark Rowlands, *The New Science of the Mind*, MIT Press, 2010. Defends and clarifies the extended and enacted mind hypotheses.

- Alva Noë, *Action in Perception*, MIT Press, 2004. Develops enactivism for perception; argues that perceiving is a form of action, not passive reception.

- Richard Menary (ed.), *The Extended Mind*, MIT Press, 2010. Comprehensive anthology; contains the original Clark-Chalmers paper and major responses.

- Evan Thompson and Francisco Varela, "Radical Embodiment: Neural Dynamics and Consciousness," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 5, 2001. Develops the dynamical systems perspective on embodied consciousness.

- Shaun Gallagher, *How the Body Shapes the Mind*, Oxford University Press, 2005. Empirical and philosophical treatment of embodiment; draws on developmental psychology and phenomenology.
