## Primary Sources

- Karl Friston, "The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?" *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 11, 2010. The most comprehensive scientific statement of the free energy principle and active inference.

- Karl Friston et al., "Active Inference and Epistemic Value," *Cognitive Neuroscience* 6, 2015. Develops active inference and its relationship to curiosity and epistemic behavior.

- Andy Clark, *Surfing Uncertainty: Prediction, Action, and the Embodied Mind*, Oxford University Press, 2016. The best philosophical treatment of predictive processing; connects the framework to perception, action, and the extended mind.

- Jakob Hohwy, *The Predictive Mind*, Oxford University Press, 2013. Philosophical development of predictive processing; argues for a broadly internalist conclusion.

- Anil Seth, *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*, Dutton, 2021. Accessible popular treatment; argues that consciousness is a "controlled hallucination" and develops the predictive processing account of the self.

- Hermann von Helmholtz, "Concerning the Perceptions in General," in *Treatise on Physiological Optics*, Vol. 3, 1867. The original statement of perception as "unconscious inference"; the historical precursor to predictive processing.

- Rajesh Rao and Dana Ballard, "Predictive Coding in the Visual Cortex," *Nature Neuroscience* 2, 1999. Influential computational neuroscience paper; provides the mechanistic model underlying predictive processing.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Jakob Hohwy, "The Self-Evidencing Brain," *Noûs* 50, 2016. Develops the philosophical implications of the predictive brain for the self and agency.

- Thomas Metzinger, *The Ego Tunnel*, Basic Books, 2009. Develops the self-model theory; connects to predictive processing accounts of the self.

- Mark Miller, "Embodied Inference: Or How We Can Act in a World of Uncertainty," in Anderson et al. (eds.), *The Feeling of Life Itself*, MIT Press, 2019. Connects active inference to embodied cognition.

- Lisa Feldman Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made*, Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2017. Applies predictive processing to emotion; argues that emotions are constructed predictions, not read-outs of internal states.

- Sam Wilkinson, "Accounting for the Phenomenology and Varieties of Auditory Verbal Hallucination Within a Predictive Processing Framework," *Psychopathology* 47, 2014. Applies predictive processing to hallucination; illustrates the framework's clinical applicability.

- Chris Frith, *Making Up the Mind*, Blackwell, 2007. Accessible neuroscientific account of predictive processing; emphasizes the brain's role in constructing the perceived world.

- Ryan Smith, Philipp Schwartenbeck, and Karl Friston, "An Active Inference Approach to Modeling Structure Learning," *Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience* 14, 2020. Technical development of active inference; illustrates the breadth of the framework's applicability.
