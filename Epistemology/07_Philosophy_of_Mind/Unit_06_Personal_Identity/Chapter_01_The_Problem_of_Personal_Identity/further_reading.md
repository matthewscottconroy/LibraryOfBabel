## Primary Sources

- John Locke, *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, Book II, Chapter 27, ed. Nidditch, Oxford University Press, 1975 (orig. 1689). The memory theory of personal identity; a person is the same as any earlier person they can remember being.

- Thomas Reid, *Essays on the Intellectual Powers of Man*, Essay III, chapter 4, ed. Brookes, Edinburgh University Press, 2002 (orig. 1786). Classic objection to Locke's memory theory using the "brave officer" case.

- David Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature*, Book I, Part IV, Section 6, ed. Selby-Bigge, Oxford University Press, 1978 (orig. 1739). The bundle theory; no persistent self, only a bundle of perceptions.

- Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, Part III, Oxford University Press, 1984. The most important contemporary treatment; introduces fission cases, psychological continuity, reductionism, and "what matters" in survival.

- Sydney Shoemaker and Richard Swinburne, *Personal Identity*, Blackwell, 1984. Classic debate between a psychological continuity view (Shoemaker) and a simple view (Swinburne).

- Eric Olson, *The Human Animal*, Oxford University Press, 1997. Defends animalism: we are human animals, and personal identity is biological.

- Marya Schechtman, *The Constitution of Selves*, Cornell University Press, 1996. Narrative account of personal identity; we are constituted by the stories we tell about ourselves.

- Peter van Inwagen, *Material Beings*, Cornell University Press, 1990. Discusses the relationship between organisms, physical continuity, and personal identity.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Harold Noonan, *Personal Identity*, 2nd ed., Routledge, 2003. Best textbook on personal identity; comprehensive survey of positions.

- Raymond Martin and John Barresi (eds.), *Personal Identity*, Blackwell, 2003. Essential anthology covering the major positions and debates.

- Lynne Rudder Baker, *Persons and Bodies*, Cambridge University Press, 2000. Defends the constitution view; persons are constituted by but not identical to bodies.

- Galen Strawson, "Against Narrativity," *Ratio* 17, 2004. Argues against narrative theories of personal identity; challenges the claim that narrative is necessary for selfhood.

- Peter Unger, *Identity, Consciousness, and Value*, Oxford University Press, 1990. Develops a biological view of personal identity grounded in physical continuity.

- Jonathan Lowe, *Subjects of Experience*, Cambridge University Press, 1996. Defends a non-Cartesian but dualistic account of personal identity.

- Neil Levy, "Personal Identity," *Philosophy Compass* 3, 2008. Clear survey of contemporary debates; good on fission, animalism, and four-dimensionalism.
