## Primary Sources

- René Descartes, *Meditations on First Philosophy*, Meditation II, trans. Cottingham, Cambridge University Press, 1996 (orig. 1641). The cogito and the discovery of the thinking self as the foundation of knowledge.

- David Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature*, Book I, Part IV, Section 6, ed. Selby-Bigge, Oxford University Press, 1978 (orig. 1739). The bundle theory; introspection reveals no persistent self.

- Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, "The Paralogisms of Pure Reason," trans. Guyer and Wood, Cambridge University Press, 1998 (orig. 1781). The transcendental unity of apperception; the "I think" that accompanies all representations.

- Thomas Metzinger, *Being No One*, MIT Press, 2003. The self-model theory of subjectivity; the phenomenal self-model (PSM) as the basis of selfhood.

- Thomas Metzinger, *The Ego Tunnel*, Basic Books, 2009. Accessible version of the self-model theory; argues there is no self, only a self-model.

- Paul Ricoeur, *Oneself as Another*, trans. Blamey, University of Chicago Press, 1992. Narrative theory of the self; distinguishes idem-identity (sameness) from ipse-identity (self-hood).

- Antonio Damasio, *The Feeling of What Happens*, Harcourt, 1999. Neuroscience of the self; distinguishes core consciousness from extended consciousness.

- Antonio Damasio, *Self Comes to Mind*, Pantheon, 2010. Develops the somatic marker hypothesis and the three-level account of self.

## Key Secondary Sources

- Shaun Gallagher and Dan Zahavi, *The Phenomenological Mind*, 2nd ed., Routledge, 2012. Best philosophical survey of phenomenological approaches to the self.

- Mark Siderits, Evan Thompson, and Dan Zahavi (eds.), *Self, No Self?*, Oxford University Press, 2011. Cross-disciplinary debate between Buddhist no-self views and Western self-theories.

- Dan Zahavi, *Subjectivity and Selfhood*, MIT Press, 2005. Defends a minimal phenomenal self against bundle theories and no-self views.

- Galen Strawson, *The Self and Its Brain*, Routledge, 2011. Defends the reality of the momentary, episodic self against narrative theories.

- Marya Schechtman, *Staying Alive: Personal Identity, Practical Concerns, and the Unity of a Life*, Oxford University Press, 2014. Develops the narrative identity view in relation to practical concerns.

- Albahari Miri, *Analytical Buddhism: The Two-Tiered Illusion of Self*, Palgrave Macmillan, 2006. Connects Buddhist no-self doctrine to contemporary philosophy of mind.

- Patricia Churchland, *Touching a Nerve*, Norton, 2013. Neuroscientific perspective on the self; argues that our sense of self is a product of brain processes without a corresponding metaphysical entity.
