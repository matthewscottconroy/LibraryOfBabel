## Primary Sources

- Frank P. Ramsey, *The Foundations of Mathematics and Other Logical Essays*, Routledge, 1931. Foundational essay "Truth and Probability" introduces subjective probability and the betting-based representation of credences.
- Bruno de Finetti, *Theory of Probability*, 2 vols., Wiley, 1974. The definitive statement of subjective Bayesianism, arguing that probability is coherent degree of belief and developing the Dutch book framework systematically.
- Leonard J. Savage, *The Foundations of Statistics*, Wiley, 1954. Provides the decision-theoretic underpinning of subjective probability through axioms on preference, linking credences to expected utility.
- Rudolf Carnap, *Logical Foundations of Probability*, University of Chicago Press, 1950. Defends an objective, logically-grounded concept of credence through inductive logic and his system of confirmation functions.
- David Lewis, "A Subjectivist's Guide to Objective Chance," in *Studies in Inductive Logic and Probability*, vol. 2, University of California Press, 1980. Introduces the Principal Principle governing the relationship between credence and objective chance.
- Brian Skyrms, *The Dynamics of Rational Belief*, Westview Press, 1990. Explores how degrees of belief evolve over time and defends diachronic coherence norms using Dutch book-style arguments.
- Richard Jeffrey, *The Logic of Decision*, 2nd ed., University of Chicago Press, 1983. Develops the full Bayesian decision theory based on credences, including the framework for Jeffrey conditionalization.

## Secondary Sources

- Alan Hájek, "Dutch Book Arguments," in *The Oxford Handbook of Rational and Social Choice*, Oxford University Press, 2008. A careful critical survey of the Dutch book argument and its many variants, noting key objections and responses.
- Alan Hájek, "Interpretations of Probability," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2019. Comprehensive overview of all major interpretations of probability, essential for locating credences within the broader landscape.
- Joyce, James M., *The Foundations of Causal Decision Theory*, Cambridge University Press, 1999. Develops a scoring-rule argument for probabilism that does not rely on Dutch books but on the epistemic norm of accuracy.
- Colin Howson and Peter Urbach, *Scientific Reasoning: The Bayesian Approach*, 3rd ed., Open Court, 2006. A thorough and accessible defense of Bayesianism as a philosophy of science, with careful treatment of coherence norms.
- Patrick Maher, *Betting on Theories*, Cambridge University Press, 1993. A critical examination of the pragmatic Dutch book argument, arguing that epistemic norms should be justified epistemically rather than pragmatically.
- Lina Eriksson and Alan Hájek, "What Are Degrees of Belief?," *Studia Logica* 86, 2007. Critically examines the standard dispositional and numerical accounts of what credences are, raising challenges for each.
- Stephan Lewandowsky, Ullrich Ecker, and John Cook, "Beyond Misinformation: Understanding and Coping with the 'Post-Truth' Era," *Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition* 6(4), 2017. Though focused on applied contexts, this piece illuminates how human credence management departs from ideal Bayesian norms.
- Hartry Field, "A Note on Jeffrey Conditionalization," *Philosophy of Science* 45(3), 1978. Examines the relationship between conditionalization and Jeffrey conditionalization, clarifying scope conditions for each rule.
- Kenny Easwaran, "Bayesianism," in *Introduction to Formal Philosophy*, Springer, 2018. A clear pedagogical introduction to Bayesian epistemology covering credences, coherence, and updating for advanced students.
