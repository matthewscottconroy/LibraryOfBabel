## Primary Sources

- Thomas Bayes, "An Essay towards Solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances," *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society* 53, 1763. The posthumously published paper in which Bayes derives what is now called Bayes' theorem, applied to the problem of inferring a binomial probability from observed successes.
- Richard Jeffrey, *The Logic of Decision*, 2nd ed., University of Chicago Press, 1983. Introduces Jeffrey conditionalization for cases where evidence does not fix a proposition with certainty but only shifts probabilities, extending the standard conditionalization rule.
- Paul Teller, "Conditionalization and Observation," *Synthese* 26, 1973. An early and careful analysis of the circumstances under which standard conditionalization is the appropriate updating rule and where it may fall short.
- Brian Skyrms, "Dynamic Coherence and Probability Kinematics," *Philosophy of Science* 54(1), 1987. Argues for diachronic Dutch book arguments as a foundation for conditionalization as the uniquely rational updating rule.
- Ian Hacking, *An Introduction to Probability and Inductive Logic*, Cambridge University Press, 2001. An accessible textbook treatment of Bayesian updating, Bayes' theorem, and the medical test example of base rate neglect.
- Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011. Synthesizes decades of research on cognitive biases including base rate neglect, showing how actual human updating systematically deviates from Bayesian norms.
- Clark Glymour, *Theory and Evidence*, Princeton University Press, 1980. Contains the first systematic statement of the problem of old evidence as a challenge to Bayesian confirmation theory.

## Secondary Sources

- Colin Howson and Peter Urbach, *Scientific Reasoning: The Bayesian Approach*, 3rd ed., Open Court, 2006. The most thorough philosophical defense of Bayesian updating as a normative account of scientific reasoning, with careful attention to objections.
- Deborah Mayo, *Error and the Growth of Experimental Knowledge*, University of Chicago Press, 1996. Defends frequentist error-statistical methodology against Bayesian updating, arguing that Bayesianism cannot adequately account for severe testing.
- Bas van Fraassen, "Belief and the Will," *Journal of Philosophy* 81(5), 1984. Argues that rational constraints on diachronic belief change cannot be purely probabilistic, motivating reflection principles and the debate over epistemic voluntarism.
- Alan Hájek and Ned Hall, "Induction and Probability," in *The Blackwell Guide to the Philosophy of Science*, Blackwell, 2002. Surveys the relationship between inductive inference and Bayesian probability, covering conditionalization as a model of induction.
- Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman, "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases," *Science* 185(4157), 1974. The landmark paper documenting systematic failures of Bayesian reasoning in human subjects, including neglect of base rates.
- Branden Fitelson, "The Plurality of Bayesian Measures of Confirmation and the Problem of Measure Sensitivity," *Philosophy of Science* 66, 1999. Examines multiple Bayesian confirmation measures and argues that the choice between them is not trivial, affecting key epistemological conclusions.
- Stephan Lewandowsky et al., "Misinformation and Its Correction: Continued Influence and Successful Debiasing," *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 13(3), 2012. Reviews psychological evidence on the updating of beliefs in response to corrections, highlighting failures of ideal conditionalization.
- Franz Huber, "Formal Representations of Belief," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2016. A clear overview of various formal frameworks for representing and updating belief, placing conditionalization in context.
- David Christensen, *Putting Logic in Its Place*, Oxford University Press, 2004. Argues for a moderate Bayesian epistemology that takes seriously both formal norms of coherence and the practical constraints on belief revision.
