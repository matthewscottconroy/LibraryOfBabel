## Primary Sources

- Pierre-Simon Laplace, *A Philosophical Essay on Probabilities*, Dover, 1951 (original 1814). Contains the classic formulation of the principle of indifference (Laplace's "principle of insufficient reason"), assigning equal probabilities in the absence of grounds for preference.
- Rudolf Carnap, *The Continuum of Inductive Methods*, University of Chicago Press, 1952. Explores a family of inductive methods parametrized by a single constant, attempting to find objective criteria for selecting rational prior credences.
- Edwin T. Jaynes, *Probability Theory: The Logic of Science*, Cambridge University Press, 2003. A comprehensive defense of objective Bayesianism using maximum entropy as the principle for selecting prior distributions, arguing that probability theory extends Aristotelian logic to degrees of belief.
- Harold Jeffreys, *Theory of Probability*, 3rd ed., Oxford University Press, 1961. Introduces Jeffreys priors — invariant under reparametrization — as an objective method for specifying non-informative prior distributions in statistical inference.
- David Lewis, "Sleeping Beauty: Reply to Elga," *Analysis* 61, 2001. Defends the "thirder" position in the Sleeping Beauty problem, arguing that centered worlds require a principled extension of the standard Bayesian framework.
- Adam Elga, "Self-Locating Belief and the Sleeping Beauty Problem," *Analysis* 60, 2000. The paper that introduced the Sleeping Beauty problem into contemporary debate and argued for the thirder position by a particular application of the principle of indifference.
- David Christensen, "Clever Bookies and Coherent Beliefs," *Philosophical Review* 100(2), 1991. Examines constraints on rational prior probabilities and the relationship between coherence requirements and the choice of initial credences.

## Secondary Sources

- Colin Howson and Peter Urbach, *Scientific Reasoning: The Bayesian Approach*, 3rd ed., Open Court, 2006. Defends the subjective Bayesian view that there are no rational constraints on priors beyond coherence, responding to the demand for objective priors.
- Roger White, "Epistemic Permissivism and the Problem of Priors," *Philosophical Perspectives* 19, 2005. Argues against permissivism about priors: that the same evidence should compel the same doxastic response, challenging unconstrained subjectivism.
- José M. Bernardo and Adrian F. M. Smith, *Bayesian Theory*, Wiley, 1994. A technical textbook covering reference priors, Jeffreys priors, and other methods for specifying objective priors, essential background for the philosophical debate.
- Jon Williamson, *In Defence of Objective Bayesianism*, Oxford University Press, 2010. A sustained defense of the view that rational credences are constrained by both coherence and an objective calibration norm drawn from maximum entropy principles.
- Michael Titelbaum, *Quitting Certainties: A Bayesian Framework Modeling Degrees of Belief*, Oxford University Press, 2013. Develops a Bayesian framework that explicitly handles self-locating beliefs, addressing puzzles like Sleeping Beauty and indexical uncertainty.
- John D. Norton, "Ignorance and Indifference," *Philosophy of Science* 75(1), 2008. A careful critique of the principle of indifference, arguing that no formulation successfully identifies genuine epistemic symmetry.
- Jonathan Weisberg, "Commutativity or Holism? A Dilemma for Conditionalizers," *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 60(4), 2009. Raises the problem that conditionalization can produce different results depending on the order of evidence, challenging the coherence of Bayesian updating when priors are controversial.
- Bradley Monton, ed., *Images of Empiricism: Essays on Science and Stances*, Oxford University Press, 2007. Contains essays addressing the role of prior probabilities in scientific methodology, including debates between objective and subjective Bayesians.
- David Papineau, *The Roots of Reason*, Oxford University Press, 2003. Discusses the evolutionary and naturalistic underpinnings of prior probabilities, asking where initial credences come from if not from pure logic.
