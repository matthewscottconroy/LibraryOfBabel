## Primary Sources

- Carlos Alchourrón, Peter Gärdenfors, and David Makinson, "On the Logic of Theory Change: Partial Meet Contraction and Revision Functions," *Journal of Symbolic Logic* 50(2), 1985. The foundational paper establishing the AGM framework, introducing partial meet contraction and revision, and deriving the eight rationality postulates.
- Peter Gärdenfors, *Knowledge in Flux: Modeling the Dynamics of Epistemic States*, MIT Press, 1988. The first book-length exposition of the AGM approach, placing it within the broader context of belief dynamics and its philosophical implications.
- Adam Grove, "Two Modelings for Theory Change," *Journal of Philosophical Logic* 17(2), 1988. Introduces the sphere semantics for belief revision, providing an equivalent possible-worlds representation of the AGM postulates that connects belief revision to the Lewisian semantics for conditionals.
- Wolfgang Spohn, *The Laws of Belief: Ranking Theory and Its Philosophical Applications*, Oxford University Press, 2012. Presents ranking theory as a comprehensive alternative to both Bayesian probability and AGM belief revision, capable of handling iterated revision naturally.
- Sven Ove Hansson, *A Textbook of Belief Dynamics: Theory Change and Database Updating*, Kluwer, 1999. A comprehensive and pedagogically clear textbook covering contraction, revision, and expansion with detailed proofs and philosophical commentary.
- Hirofumi Katsuno and Alberto Mendelzon, "Propositional Knowledge Base Revision and Minimal Change," *Artificial Intelligence* 52(3), 1991. Extends the AGM framework to propositional databases and introduces the KM postulates for belief update (as distinct from belief revision), a distinction with important philosophical implications.
- Darwiche and Pearl, "On the Logic of Iterated Belief Revision," *Artificial Intelligence* 89(1–2), 1997. Introduces the DP postulates for iterated revision, showing that the original AGM postulates underdetermine the behavior of revision sequences and proposing additional constraints.

## Secondary Sources

- Krister Segerberg, "The Logic of Deliberate Belief Change," *Bulletin of the Section of Logic* 18, 1989. An early exploration of the relationship between AGM theory and dynamic logic, treating belief revision as an action that transforms epistemic states.
- Peter Gärdenfors and Hans Rott, "Belief Revision," in *Handbook of Logic in Artificial Intelligence and Logic Programming*, vol. 4, Oxford University Press, 1995. A comprehensive survey of AGM theory, iterated revision, and connections to nonmonotonic reasoning, essential for advanced students.
- Hans Rott, *Change, Choice and Inference: A Study of Belief Revision and Nonmonotonic Reasoning*, Oxford University Press, 2001. A thorough investigation of the connections between belief revision, choice theory, and nonmonotonic logic, placing AGM within a broader logical landscape.
- Maurice Pagnucco and Abhaya Nayak, "Iterated Belief Change and the Axiom of Conditional Preservation," in *Proceedings of the 6th International Workshop on Non-Monotonic Reasoning*, 1996. Examines the DP postulates for iterated revision and the role of conditional beliefs in guiding successive revisions.
- Isaac Levi, *The Fixation of Belief and Its Undoing*, Cambridge University Press, 1991. Defends a philosophical account of belief change based on deliberate choice among epistemic policies, offering an alternative to the AGM approach that emphasizes the role of inquiry.
- Johan van Benthem, "Dynamic Logic for Belief Revision," *Journal of Applied Non-Classical Logics* 17(2), 2007. Connects the AGM framework to dynamic epistemic logic, showing how belief revision operators can be expressed as programs in a dynamic modal language.
- Franz Huber, "Formal Representations of Belief," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2016. Situates AGM theory alongside Bayesian probability and ranking theory as three competing formal frameworks for belief and its dynamics.
- Sven Ove Hansson, "Logic of Belief Revision," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2017. An authoritative encyclopedic overview of the AGM framework, its extensions, and the main open problems in belief revision theory.
