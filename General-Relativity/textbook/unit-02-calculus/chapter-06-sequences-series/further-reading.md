# Chapter 6 Further Reading and References

---

## Primary Sources

**Euler, L. (1748).** *Introductio in analysin infinitorum*. Lausanne. English translation: Blanton, J.D. (1988–1990). *Introduction to Analysis of the Infinite*. Springer.  
The source of Euler's formula, the Basel problem solution, systematic power series development, and the product formula for sin x. One of the most important mathematical texts of the 18th century.

**Cauchy, A.L. (1821).** *Cours d'analyse de l'École Royale Polytechnique*. English translation: Bradley, R.E. and Sandifer, C.E. (2009). *Cauchy's Cours d'analyse*. Springer.  
Contains Cauchy's rigorous definition of convergence, the ratio test, and the systematic theory of series.

**Riemann, B. (1854/1868).** "Über die Darstellbarkeit einer Function durch eine trigonometrische Reihe." *Abhandlungen der Königlichen Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen*, 13.  
Source of the rearrangement theorem and the Riemann integral, arising from the question of which functions have Fourier series.

---

## Foundational Texts

**Rudin, W. (1976).** *Principles of Mathematical Analysis*, 3rd ed. McGraw-Hill.  
Chapters 3 (numerical sequences and series) and 7 (sequences and series of functions) are the canonical treatment. Chapter 3 includes the ratio and root tests, alternating series, and rearrangements. Chapter 7 covers uniform convergence with full proofs and counterexamples.

**Spivak, M. (1994).** *Calculus*, 3rd ed. Publish or Perish.  
Chapters 22–24: sequences and series, with exceptional discussion of convergence tests and the counterexamples that motivate the distinction between pointwise and uniform convergence. Chapter 26 on complex numbers and the derivation of Euler's formula is particularly beautiful.

**Knopp, K. (1951).** *Theory and Application of Infinite Series*, 2nd ed. Blackie (Dover reprint).  
The most comprehensive single-volume treatment of series, including Dirichlet series, double series, and applications. More detail than any other source.

---

## Analysis Textbooks

**Apostol, T.M. (1974).** *Mathematical Analysis*, 2nd ed. Addison-Wesley.  
Chapter 8 on infinite series; Chapter 9 on sequences of functions. Complementary to Rudin; somewhat more conversational in style.

**Abbott, S. (2015).** *Understanding Analysis*, 2nd ed. Springer.  
A gentle but rigorous introduction to real analysis, with excellent motivation and discussion of the key examples (Weierstrass function, counterexamples to limit interchange). An ideal preparation for Rudin.

---

## Connections to Physics

**Bender, C.M. and Orszag, S.A. (1978).** *Advanced Mathematical Methods for Scientists and Engineers*. Springer.  
Chapter 6 on asymptotic series: the definitive treatment of divergent perturbative expansions in physics. Covers Borel summation, Padé approximants, and other methods for extracting information from formally divergent series. Essential for anyone working with perturbative QED or post-Newtonian GR.

**Dyson, F.J. (1952).** "Divergence of Perturbation Theory in Quantum Electrodynamics." *Physical Review*, 85, 631–632.  
A short but profound paper arguing that the perturbative expansion in QED must diverge for any nonzero coupling constant. Argument: if the series converged for α > 0, it would also converge for α < 0 (since the radius of convergence is a property of the analytic function, not its sign) — but α < 0 is unphysical (repulsive rather than attractive charges) and the theory would be qualitatively different. This is a "proof by physics" of divergence, anticipating the later mathematical proof.

**Will, C.M. (2014).** "The Confrontation between General Relativity and Experiment." *Living Reviews in Relativity*, 17, 4.  
The post-Newtonian (PN) expansion of GR is a power series in v²/c² and GM/rc². Will's review shows how this perturbative series — which terminates for any finite-order approximation but diverges as a formal series — is used for precision tests of GR.

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973).** *Gravitation*. W.H. Freeman.  
Chapter 27 on the description of gravitational waves as Fourier modes; Chapter 36 on post-Newtonian approximations.

---

## Fourier Analysis

**Körner, T.W. (1988).** *Fourier Analysis*. Cambridge University Press.  
An accessible and fascinating account of Fourier analysis, including the history of convergence questions, the role of uniform convergence, and the pathological examples that drove the development of modern analysis. Highly recommended as supplementary reading.

**Stein, E.M. and Shakarchi, R. (2003).** *Fourier Analysis: An Introduction*. Princeton University Press.  
The first volume of the Princeton Lectures in Analysis; a rigorous but readable introduction with connections to number theory, physics, and complex analysis.
