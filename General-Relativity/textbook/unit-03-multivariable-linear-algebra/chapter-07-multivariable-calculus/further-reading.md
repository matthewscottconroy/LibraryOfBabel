# Chapter 7: Further Reading

---

## Primary Sources

- **Cauchy, A.L. (1823).** *Résumé des leçons données à l'École Royale Polytechnique sur le calcul infinitésimal.* Paris. [The first rigorous treatment of the differential in several variables. Cauchy introduces partial derivatives and anticipates the chain rule for composite functions.]

- **Riemann, B. (1854).** "Über die Hypothesen welche der Geometrie zu Grunde liegen." In *Abhandlungen der Königlichen Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen*, 13 (1867), 133–152. Translated in Spivak (1979). [The Habilitationsschrift that introduced the concept of an n-dimensional manifold and the Riemannian metric — a function that is exactly the kind of object studied in this chapter generalized to curved spaces.]

- **Jacobi, C.G.J. (1841).** "De determinantibus functionalibus." *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik*, 22, 319–359. [Introduces the Jacobian determinant and matrix. The Jacobian of a coordinate transformation is central to the change-of-variables formula for multiple integrals and the transformation law for tensor components in GR.]

- **Lagrange, J.L. (1788).** *Mécanique analytique.* Paris. [The first systematic treatment of constrained optimization via multipliers — the origin of Lagrange multipliers. Lagrange's formulation of mechanics was the model for the entire Lagrangian approach in physics.]

- **Clairaut, A.C. (1740).** "Sur les courbes que l'on forme en coupant une surface courbe quelconque par un plan qui passe par un point donné." *Histoire de l'Académie royale des sciences de Paris.* [Clairaut's theorem (equality of mixed partials) in its original geometric context.]

- **Schwarz, H.A. (1873).** "Communication." *Archives des sciences physiques et naturelles* 48, 38–44. [The modern statement and proof of Clairaut's theorem, with the necessary hypothesis of continuity of mixed partials. The theorem is now often called the "Schwarz theorem" in the European literature.]

## Standard Graduate References

- **Spivak, M. (1965).** *Calculus on Manifolds.* W.A. Benjamin. [An 85-page masterpiece: the inverse and implicit function theorems, the change-of-variables formula, and Stokes' theorem, all in the abstract manifold setting. The book to read before or alongside this chapter.]

- **Apostol, T.M. (1974).** *Mathematical Analysis*, 2nd ed. Addison-Wesley. [Chapters 12–15: rigorous treatment of the multivariable derivative, the chain rule, and multiple integrals with a clean functional-analysis perspective.]

- **Rudin, W. (1976).** *Principles of Mathematical Analysis*, 3rd ed. McGraw-Hill. [Chapter 9: the derivative as a linear map, the chain rule in the language of linear transformations, and the inverse function theorem. Terse but complete; essential reading.]

- **Fleming, W.H. (1977).** *Functions of Several Variables*, 2nd ed. Springer. [A thorough treatment of multivariable calculus with emphasis on analysis; excellent coverage of the implicit function theorem and integration in ℝⁿ.]

- **Munkres, J.R. (1991).** *Analysis on Manifolds.* Addison-Wesley. [A gentle, thorough development of multivariable analysis and Stokes' theorem; accessible to readers with one year of calculus.]

## Physics and GR Applications

- **Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973).** *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman. [§2.1–2.6 on the coordinate-free formulation of physics, §3 on the metric; the coordinate transformation of tensors is introduced concretely here.]

- **Wald, R.M. (1984).** *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press. [Appendix A: the inverse function theorem and implicit function theorem in the smooth manifold setting. Chapter 3: covariant differentiation, which is the GR generalization of the multivariable chain rule.]

- **Schutz, B.F. (1985).** *A First Course in General Relativity.* Cambridge University Press. [Chapters 3–4: the chain rule for coordinate transformations, tensor calculus. A clear and physically motivated introduction to the formalism of this chapter.]

- **Fubini, G. (1907).** "Sugli integrali multipli." *Rendiconti Accademia dei Lincei*, 16, 608–614. [The original proof that iterated integrals equal multiple integrals under appropriate conditions.]

---

## For the Curious

- **Morse, M. (1934).** *The Calculus of Variations in the Large.* American Mathematical Society. [Morse theory: using critical points of functions on manifolds to deduce topological information. The second derivative test of this chapter becomes, in Morse theory, a tool for understanding the topology of the space itself.]

- **Whitney, H. (1944).** "The self-intersections of a smooth n-manifold in 2n-space." *Annals of Mathematics*, 45, 220–246. [Contains the Whitney embedding theorem: every smooth n-dimensional manifold can be embedded in ℝ^{2n}. The Jacobian of the embedding map is exactly the kind of object studied in this chapter.]
