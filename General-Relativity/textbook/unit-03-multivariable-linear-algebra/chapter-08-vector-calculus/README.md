# Chapter 8: Vector Calculus

---

## Chapter Introduction

Vector calculus is the calculus of vector fields — assignments of vectors to points in space (or spacetime). Wind velocity, electric and magnetic fields, the gravitational acceleration vector, the fluid flow velocity in a river — these are all vector fields. The operations of vector calculus describe how these fields vary in space and how they interact with surfaces and volumes.

The three fundamental operations on vector fields in ℝ³ are:
- The **gradient** ∇f (of a scalar field), already introduced in Chapter 7: points in the direction of greatest increase.
- The **divergence** ∇ · **F** (of a vector field): measures the "outflow" at each point — how much the field spreads out from a point.
- The **curl** ∇ × **F** (of a vector field): measures the rotation of the field around a point — the "circulation" per unit area.

These three operators, together with the Laplacian ∇² = ∇ · ∇, are the building blocks of all classical field theories: Maxwell's equations, the Navier-Stokes equations, the heat and wave equations, and Newtonian gravity. They are also the seed forms of the **exterior derivative** — the unified operator d that extends gradient, curl, and divergence to differential forms of any degree on manifolds of any dimension.

The integral theorems — Green's theorem, Stokes' theorem, the divergence theorem — express deep relationships between these differential operators and boundary integrals. They are the key to deriving conservation laws from local differential equations.

This chapter is essential preparation for Chapter 28 (differential forms on manifolds) and Chapters 35–40 (the structure of the Einstein equations and conservation laws in GR).
