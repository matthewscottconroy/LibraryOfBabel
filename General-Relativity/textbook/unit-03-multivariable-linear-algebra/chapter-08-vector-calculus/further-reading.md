# Chapter 8: Further Reading

## Primary Sources

- **Green, G. (1828).** *An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism.* Privately printed, Nottingham. Reprinted in *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik*, 39–40 (1850). [The paper that introduces Green's theorem, Green's function, and the divergence theorem — all in the context of electrostatics. Green was a self-taught miller's son who submitted this to subscription publication because he couldn't afford Cambridge. One of the most remarkable papers in mathematical physics.]

- **Gauss, C.F. (1813).** "Theoria attractionis corporum sphaeroidicorum ellipticorum homogeneorum methodo nova tractata." *Commentationes Societatis Regiae Scientiarum Gottingensis Recentiores*, 2, 2–5. [Contains what is now called the divergence theorem, in the context of gravitational attraction of ellipsoids. Gauss states the result as a theorem about flux through a closed surface.]

- **Stokes, G.G. (1854).** Letter to William Thomson (Lord Kelvin), July 2, 1850. Published in Stokes' collected papers. [Stokes' theorem appeared as an examination question at Cambridge — set by Stokes as part of the Smith's Prize examination that Maxwell famously took. The original statement and proof are in Stokes' correspondence with Kelvin.]

- **Cartan, É. (1922).** *Leçons sur les invariants intégraux.* Hermann, Paris. [Cartan introduces differential forms as a unifying framework for vector calculus, generalizing the theorems of Green, Stokes, and Gauss to forms on manifolds. The exterior derivative d and the generalized Stokes' theorem ∫_M dω = ∫_{∂M} ω appear here in near-modern form.]

- **de Rham, G. (1931).** "Sur l'Analysis situs des variétés à n dimensions." *Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées*, 10, 115–200. [De Rham's theorem: the cohomology of closed/exact forms (de Rham cohomology) is isomorphic to singular homology with real coefficients. The topological content of differential forms is established here.]

- **Hodge, W.V.D. (1941).** *The Theory and Applications of Harmonic Integrals.* Cambridge University Press. [Hodge theory: every de Rham cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative. The Hodge star ★, the codifferential δ = ★d★, and the Laplace-Beltrami operator Δ = dδ + δd are introduced and applied to algebraic geometry and physics.]

## Standard Graduate References

- **Spivak, M. (1965).** *Calculus on Manifolds.* W.A. Benjamin. [Chapters 4–5: differential forms and Stokes' theorem in 80 pages. The cleanest treatment available; every physicist who uses GR should read this book.]

- **Flanders, H. (1963).** *Differential Forms with Applications to the Physical Sciences.* Academic Press. Reprinted by Dover, 1989. [Accessible and physically motivated. Chapters 2–3 on differential forms and integration; Chapter 6 on applications to electromagnetism and mechanics.]

- **Arnold, V.I. (1989).** *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics*, 2nd ed. Springer. [Chapter 7 on differential forms; Appendix on symplectic geometry and the Poincaré integral invariant. Arnold shows how the Hamiltonian formalism is differential forms.]

- **Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973).** *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman. [Chapter 4: differential forms and electromagnetism; §5.6 on Faraday 2-form; §15 on Bianchi identities as dR = 0.]

## On Maxwell's Equations as Differential Forms

- **Maxwell, J.C. (1873).** *A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism*, Vol. 1–2. Clarendon Press, Oxford. [Maxwell's original formulation uses quaternionic notation. The modern differential forms version reveals the geometric structure hidden in his equations.]

- **Misner, C.W. and Wheeler, J.A. (1957).** "Classical Physics as Geometry." *Annals of Physics*, 2, 525–603. [The paper that argues all of classical physics — electromagnetism, gravity — can be formulated as geometry. Faraday's F 2-form is the starting point.]

- **Hehl, F.W. and Obukhov, Y.N. (2003).** *Foundations of Classical Electrodynamics.* Birkhäuser. [A systematic treatment of electromagnetism using differential forms on spacetime, emphasizing the physical and conceptual clarity of the exterior calculus approach.]

## For the Curious

- **Aharonov, Y. and Bohm, D. (1959).** "Significance of electromagnetic potentials in the quantum theory." *Physical Review*, 115, 485–491. [The celebrated paper showing that the vector potential A (the 1-form whose exterior derivative is the magnetic 2-form B) has physical significance even where B = 0 — through its integral around loops, which measures an element of H¹ of the space. A beautiful application of de Rham cohomology to physics.]

- **Dirac, P.A.M. (1931).** "Quantised singularities in the electromagnetic field." *Proceedings of the Royal Society A*, 133, 60–72. [The Dirac monopole: if magnetic monopoles exist, de Rham cohomology forces the quantization of electric charge. The condition is that a certain 2-form (magnetic field of the monopole) not be globally exact — measuring H²(S²) = ℤ.]
