# Chapter 10: Further Reading

## Primary Sources

- **Picard, É. (1890).** "Mémoire sur la théorie des équations aux dérivées partielles et la méthode des approximations successives." *Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées*, 6, 145–210. [Introduces the method of successive approximations for ODEs — the Picard iteration — and proves existence and uniqueness. One of the founding papers of modern ODE theory.]

- **Lindelöf, E. (1894).** "Sur l'application de la méthode des approximations successives aux équations différentielles ordinaires du premier ordre." *Comptes rendus de l'Académie des sciences*, 118, 454–457. [Shows the Lipschitz condition is sufficient for uniqueness. The theorem is called Picard-Lindelöf in honor of both.]

- **Lyapunov, A.M. (1892).** "Obshchaya zadacha ob ustoychivosti dvizheniya" (The general problem of the stability of motion). Doctoral thesis, Kharkov University. Reprinted and translated in *International Journal of Control* 55 (1992), 531–773. [The founding paper of stability theory. Lyapunov functions, stability, asymptotic stability, and instability are all defined and analyzed here with extraordinary rigor and completeness.]

- **Hartman, P. (1960).** "A lemma in the theory of structural stability of differential equations." *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society*, 11, 610–620. [The Hartman-Grobman theorem: near a hyperbolic equilibrium, the nonlinear system is locally topologically conjugate to its linearization.]

- **Bessel, F.W. (1824).** "Untersuchung des Theils der planetarischen Störungen..." [listed in Section 10.4 references]. [Introduces Bessel functions in a GR-adjacent context: perturbations of planetary orbits, which are the Newtonian analogue of quasi-normal modes.]

- **Legendre, A.-M. (1782).** "Recherches sur l'attraction des sphéroïdes homogènes." [listed in Section 10.4 references]. [The foundational paper introducing Legendre polynomials for computing gravitational potentials of nearly spherical bodies — exactly the problem of GR perturbation theory in a Newtonian setting.]

## Standard Graduate Texts

- **Arnold, V.I. (1992).** *Ordinary Differential Equations*, 3rd ed. Springer. [The most geometrically insightful ODE text. Arnold builds the theory from the ground up as geometry: phase flows, vector fields, stability, and the behavior of trajectories in phase space. The chapters on linear systems and the matrix exponential are models of clarity.]

- **Coddington, E.A. and Levinson, N. (1955).** *Theory of Ordinary Differential Equations.* McGraw-Hill. [The classic rigorous reference. Thorough treatment of existence-uniqueness, continuation of solutions, linear systems, stability theory, and boundary value problems. More complete than Arnold but less geometric.]

- **Hirsch, M.W., Smale, S., and Devaney, R.L. (2013).** *Differential Equations, Dynamical Systems, and an Introduction to Chaos*, 3rd ed. Academic Press. [Excellent for geometric and qualitative theory: phase portraits, Poincaré-Bendixson theorem, bifurcations, chaos. The modern approach to ODEs as dynamical systems.]

- **Arfken, G.B., Weber, H.J., and Harris, F.E. (2013).** *Mathematical Methods for Physicists*, 7th ed. Academic Press. [Comprehensive treatment of all special functions: Legendre polynomials, spherical harmonics, Bessel functions, hypergeometric functions. The physicist's reference for power series solutions.]

- **Abramowitz, M. and Stegun, I.A., eds. (1965).** *Handbook of Mathematical Functions.* NIST. [The complete compendium of special functions. Every formula, recurrence relation, asymptotic expansion, and numerical table ever needed. Freely available at dlmf.nist.gov as the Digital Library of Mathematical Functions.]

## GR Applications

- **Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973).** *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman. [Chapter 25 on geodesics in Schwarzschild spacetime; §25.5 on the effective potential and orbit classification; Chapter 35 on gravitational wave generation — which requires solving linear ODEs for perturbations.]

- **Chandrasekhar, S. (1983).** *The Mathematical Theory of Black Holes.* Oxford University Press. [The definitive mathematical treatment of perturbation theory for black holes. The Regge-Wheeler equation (Chapter 4), the Teukolsky equation (Chapter 8), and their solutions by Frobenius series and asymptotic methods. For readers who want the complete story.]

- **Regge, T. and Wheeler, J.A. (1957).** "Stability of a Schwarzschild singularity." *Physical Review*, 108, 1063–1069. [The original paper on perturbations of the Schwarzschild metric — introducing the Regge-Wheeler equation, which is a second-order linear ODE with a Frobenius-type analysis at the horizon.]

- **Press, W.H. and Teukolsky, S.A. (1972).** "Floating orbits, superradiant scattering and the black-hole bomb." *Nature*, 238, 211–212; Teukolsky, S.A. (1973). "Perturbations of a rotating black hole." *Astrophysical Journal*, 185, 635–647. [The Teukolsky equation for perturbations of Kerr black holes — a more complicated ODE than Regge-Wheeler, but still amenable to power series methods. Its solutions describe gravitational wave emission and absorption by rotating black holes.]

## For the Curious

- **Poincaré, H. (1892–1899).** *Les méthodes nouvelles de la mécanique céleste*, 3 vols. Gauthier-Villars, Paris. [The founding work of dynamical systems theory and chaos theory. Poincaré invented the qualitative theory of ODEs — phase portraits, Poincaré sections, surface of section, stable and unstable manifolds — in the context of the three-body problem in celestial mechanics. This is the book that made "qualitative ODE theory" a subject.]

- **Lorenz, E.N. (1963).** "Deterministic nonperiodic flow." *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences*, 20, 130–141. [The paper that introduced the Lorenz attractor and made chaos theory visible. A 3×3 system of ODEs can produce bounded, non-periodic, sensitive-to-initial-conditions behavior — chaos. This is what makes long-term weather prediction impossible in principle.]
