# Chapter 11: Further Reading

---

## Primary Sources

- **Fourier, J.B.J. (1822).** *Théorie analytique de la chaleur.* Paris. English translation by Freeman (1878). [The founding work on heat conduction: introduces the heat equation, separation of variables, and the Fourier series. Fourier's physics is impeccable and the mathematics, while not fully rigorous, drove a century of developments in analysis.]

- **d'Alembert, J.L.R. (1747).** "Recherches sur la courbe que forme une corde tendue mise en vibration." *Mémoires de l'Académie Royale des Sciences et Belles Lettres de Berlin*, 214–249. [The first paper solving the wave equation via the traveling wave decomposition. D'Alembert also debates with Euler and Bernoulli about what functions can be "initial data," anticipating the problem of representing arbitrary functions as Fourier series.]

- **Hadamard, J. (1902).** "Sur les problèmes aux dérivées partielles et leur signification physique." *Princeton University Bulletin*, 49–52. [Introduces the concept of well-posedness and gives the canonical example of an ill-posed problem: Cauchy data for the Laplace equation.]

- **Schwartz, L. (1951).** *Théorie des distributions*, 2 vols. Hermann, Paris. [Rigorously founds the theory of distributions (generalized functions), making δ(x) and its derivatives precise mathematical objects. Schwartz received the Fields Medal for this work.]

- **Choquet-Bruhat, Y. (1952).** "Théorème d'existence pour certains systèmes d'équations aux dérivées partielles non linéaires." *Acta Mathematica*, 88, 141–225. [Proves the local well-posedness of the Einstein equations as a hyperbolic IVP — the foundational theorem of mathematical GR.]

- **Green, G. (1828).** *An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism.* [Introduces Green's functions and the method of images.]

## Standard Graduate References

- **Evans, L.C. (2010).** *Partial Differential Equations*, 2nd ed. American Mathematical Society. Graduate Studies in Mathematics Vol. 19. [The standard graduate PDE text. Comprehensive, rigorous, and well-written. Chapter 1: four fundamental examples; Chapter 2: representation formulas; Chapter 5: Sobolev spaces; Chapter 7: linear evolution equations. The reference for everything in this chapter.]

- **John, F. (1982).** *Partial Differential Equations*, 4th ed. Springer. [A classic and concise graduate treatment. Chapter 2 on the wave equation, including Huygens' principle; Chapter 3 on the heat equation; Chapter 4 on the Laplace equation. Excellent mathematical depth in a compact format.]

- **Taylor, M.E. (1996).** *Partial Differential Equations I: Basic Theory.* Springer. [The comprehensive modern reference. Chapter 2 on the wave equation; Chapter 3 on distribution theory; Chapters 5–6 on linear PDE theory.]

- **Courant, R. and Hilbert, D. (1962).** *Methods of Mathematical Physics*, 2 vols. Interscience/Wiley. [The classic reference for mathematical physics, particularly PDEs and eigenvalue problems. Volume II covers PDEs: characteristics, wave equation, heat equation, Laplace equation, and Sturm-Liouville theory. Essential background for anyone working in mathematical physics.]

## GR and PDE Theory

- **Wald, R.M. (1984).** *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press. [Chapter 10: the Einstein equations as a Cauchy problem; global existence results; constraints and evolution.]

- **Arnowitt, R., Deser, S., and Misner, C.W. (1962).** "The dynamics of general relativity." In L. Witten, ed., *Gravitation: An Introduction to Current Research.* [The ADM 3+1 formalism for GR: constraint equations (elliptic) and evolution equations (hyperbolic).]

- **Ringerstein, H. and Rendall, A. (2000).** *Theorems on Existence and Global Dynamics for the Einstein Equations.* Living Reviews in Relativity. [A comprehensive survey of mathematical results on well-posedness and global existence for GR.]

- **Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973).** *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman. [Chapter 35 on gravitational radiation: the quadrupole formula derived via the retarded Green's function of the wave equation.]

## For the Curious

- **Hamilton, R.S. (1982).** "Three-manifolds with positive Ricci curvature." *Journal of Differential Geometry*, 17, 255–306. [The founding paper on Ricci flow. Hamilton's parabolic PDE for the metric led eventually to Perelman's proof of the Poincaré conjecture.]

- **Perelman, G. (2002).** "The entropy formula for the Ricci flow and its geometric applications." arXiv:math/0211159. [The first and most important of Perelman's papers on Ricci flow and geometrization — using PDE analysis (monotonicity, maximum principle) to prove the deepest theorem in 3-manifold topology.]

- **Price, R.H. (1972).** "Nonspherical perturbations of relativistic gravitational collapse. I–II." *Physical Review D*, 5, 2419–2454. [Price's law: the late-time decay of gravitational wave perturbations of a Schwarzschild black hole as power laws in time. Derived by analyzing the wave equation on a curved background — one of the beautiful results of mathematical GR.]
