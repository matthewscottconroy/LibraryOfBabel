# Chapter 12: Further Reading

---

## Primary Sources

- **Cauchy, A.L. (1814).** "Mémoire sur les intégrales définies." Submitted to the Académie; published in *Mémoires présentés par divers savants* 1 (1827), 599–799. [Cauchy's first systematic treatment of complex integration; introduces what would become Cauchy's theorem, though the full clarity comes in later papers.]

- **Cauchy, A.L. (1825).** "Mémoire sur les intégrales définies, prises entre des limites imaginaires." Printed privately; reprinted in *Bulletin des sciences mathématiques* 7 (1874), 265–304. [The clearest early statement of Cauchy's theorem. The computation of ∮ dz/z = 2πi is here.]

- **Riemann, B. (1851).** "Grundlagen für eine allgemeine Theorie der Functionen einer veränderlichen complexen Größe." Doctoral dissertation, Göttingen. In *Gesammelte mathematische Werke* (1876), 3–45. [Riemann's thesis introduces the Riemann surface, the concept of a holomorphic function, the Riemann mapping theorem, and the connection between complex analysis and potential theory (the Cauchy-Riemann equations as conditions for the real and imaginary parts to be harmonic). One of the deepest doctoral theses ever written.]

- **Riemann, B. (1859).** "Über die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Größe." *Monatsberichte der Berliner Akademie*, 671–680. [Introduces the Riemann zeta function ζ(s) as a complex function, uses analytic continuation to define it for all s ∈ ℂ except s = 1, and states the Riemann hypothesis (all non-trivial zeros have Re(s) = 1/2). The connection between ζ(−1) = −1/12 (used in the Casimir effect) and analytic continuation is here.]

- **Weierstrass, K. (1876).** "Zur Theorie der eindeutigen analytischen Functionen." *Abhandlungen der Berliner Akademie*, 11–60. [The Weierstrass factorization theorem and the general theory of entire functions. Together with Riemann's work, establishes the modern theory of complex functions.]

- **Gibbons, G.W. and Hawking, S.W. (1977).** "Action integrals and partition functions in quantum gravity." *Physical Review D*, 15, 2752–2756. [The derivation of the Hawking temperature via analytic continuation to Euclidean signature and periodicity of imaginary time. The calculation in Section 12.3.4 follows this paper.]

- **Kruskal, M.D. (1960).** "Maximal extension of Schwarzschild metric." *Physical Review*, 119, 1743–1745. [Analytic continuation of Schwarzschild through the horizon to the full Kruskal-Szekeres manifold.]

## Standard Graduate References

- **Ahlfors, L.V. (1979).** *Complex Analysis*, 3rd ed. McGraw-Hill. [The classic graduate text. Rigorous, elegant, and comprehensive. Chapters 3–5: holomorphic functions, Cauchy's theorem, Laurent series, residues. The standard reference for everything in this chapter.]

- **Conway, J.B. (1978).** *Functions of One Complex Variable*, 2nd ed. Springer. [Slightly less terse than Ahlfors; excellent for self-study. Chapters 1–8 cover the core theory.]

- **Stein, E.M. and Shakarchi, R. (2003).** *Complex Analysis.* Princeton University Press. (Princeton Lectures in Analysis, Vol. 2.) [Beautifully written with excellent physical motivation. Chapter 5 on the Fourier transform and Chapter 9 on Theta functions and applications to number theory show the range of complex analysis.]

- **Lang, S. (1999).** *Complex Analysis*, 4th ed. Springer. [More algebraically flavored than Ahlfors; excellent treatment of Riemann surfaces in Chapter 9.]

## GR and Physics Applications

- **Hawking, S.W. (1975).** "Particle creation by black holes." *Communications in Mathematical Physics*, 43, 199–220. [The original derivation of Hawking radiation. Uses Bogoliubov transformations and analytic continuation of mode functions across the horizon. The calculation of T_H = ℏc³/(8πGMk_B) is here.]

- **Penrose, R. (1963).** "Asymptotic properties of fields and spacetimes." *Physical Review Letters*, 10, 66–68. [Conformal infinity (ℐ⁺, ℐ⁻) and the conformal compactification — the GR application of conformal maps.]

- **Hawking, S.W. and Ellis, G.F.R. (1973).** *The Large Scale Structure of Space-Time.* Cambridge University Press. [Chapter 5: conformal diagrams (Penrose diagrams) for all standard exact solutions. The conformal compactification technique is used systematically.]

- **Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973).** *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman. [§21.5 on the connection between complex analysis and gravitational radiation; the quadrupole formula derivation uses complex function techniques.]

## For the Curious

- **Needham, T. (1997).** *Visual Complex Analysis.* Oxford University Press. [The most geometrically intuitive treatment of complex analysis; uses visual reasoning throughout. Excellent for building understanding before approaching Ahlfors.]

- **Schwarz, H.A. (1869).** "Über einige Abbildungsaufgaben." *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik*, 70, 105–120. [The Schwarz-Christoffel mapping: a conformal map from the upper half-plane to a polygon. Used in electrostatics, fluid mechanics, and numerical methods. One of the most beautiful and practically useful results in conformal mapping theory.]

- **Casimir, H.B.G. (1948).** "On the attraction between two perfectly conducting plates." *Proceedings of the Koninklijke Nederlandse Akademie van Wetenschappen*, 51, 793–795. [The Casimir effect: quantum vacuum fluctuations produce an attractive force between uncharged plates. The zeta function regularization used in Section 12.4 gives a finite prediction that has been experimentally verified.]
