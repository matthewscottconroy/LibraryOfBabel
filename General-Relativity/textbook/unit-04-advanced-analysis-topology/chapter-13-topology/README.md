# Chapter 13: Topology and Metric Spaces

---

## Chapter Introduction

Topology is the study of properties preserved under continuous deformation. A coffee cup and a torus (donut) are topologically equivalent; a sphere and a torus are not. This may seem abstract, but the global topology of spacetime is a physically meaningful question in GR: is the universe spatially compact or non-compact? Are there wormholes (non-trivial spatial topology)? Can spacetime have closed timelike curves (topology that allows time travel)?

At the foundational level, topology provides the language for defining continuity, convergence, compactness, and connectedness in full generality — for spaces that need not be subsets of ℝⁿ. This generality is needed because smooth manifolds (the arenas of GR) are defined topologically.

Key results for GR:
- **Compactness** of closed bounded subsets of ℝⁿ (Heine-Borel): underlies the EVT and Bolzano-Weierstrass.
- **Completeness** of metric spaces (Banach fixed-point theorem): underlies Picard-Lindelöf and the IFT.
- **Hausdorff condition** and **second-countability**: the axioms that make manifolds well-behaved.
- **Covering spaces and fundamental group**: tools for classifying spatial topology; the fundamental group π₁(M) measures the "loops" in M.
- **De Rham cohomology** (recap from Chapter 8): topological invariants of differential-form character.
- **Geodesic completeness**: a spacetime is geodesically complete if every geodesic extends for all values of its affine parameter. Singularity theorems (Chapter 62) show that generic spacetimes are incomplete.
