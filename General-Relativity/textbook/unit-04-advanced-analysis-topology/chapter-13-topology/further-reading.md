# Chapter 13: Further Reading

## Primary Sources

- **Riemann, B. (1854).** "Über die Hypothesen, welche der Geometrie zu Grunde liegen." Habilitation lecture, Göttingen. In *Abhandlungen der Königlichen Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen*, 13 (1867), 133–152. [Riemann proposes the concept of an n-dimensional manifold — the founding document of differential geometry and, through it, of GR. He asks: what are the possible geometries? He introduces topological manifolds, Riemannian metrics, and curvature in one lecture.]

- **Hausdorff, F. (1914).** *Grundzüge der Mengenlehre.* Veit & Comp., Leipzig. [Introduces the modern concept of a topological space with separation axioms. The Hausdorff condition (the T₂ axiom) is named after him. This book also introduces the Hausdorff metric and develops the foundations of general topology.]

- **Poincaré, H. (1895).** "Analysis situs." *Journal de l'École Polytechnique*, 1, 1–121. [The founding paper of algebraic topology. Poincaré introduces the fundamental group π₁, homology groups, and the Euler characteristic as topological invariants. He also states the Poincaré conjecture: every simply connected compact 3-manifold is homeomorphic to S³.]

- **Gauss, C.F. (1827).** "Disquisitiones generales circa superficies curvas." *Commentationes Societatis Regiae Scientiarum Gottingensis Recentiores*, 6, 99–146. [Gauss's intrinsic differential geometry of surfaces: introduces the Gaussian curvature, the Gauss-Bonnet formula in local form, and the remarkable result that curvature is intrinsic (does not depend on embedding). The foundation of all subsequent differential geometry.]

- **Bonnet, P.O. (1848).** "Mémoire sur la théorie générale des surfaces." *Journal de l'École Polytechnique*, 19, 1–146. [The global form of the Gauss-Bonnet theorem: the total Gaussian curvature of a compact surface equals 2π times the Euler characteristic. The first major theorem connecting geometry (curvature) to topology (χ).]

- **Penrose, R. (1965).** "Gravitational collapse and space-time singularities." *Physical Review Letters*, 14, 57–59. [Three pages that contain the Penrose singularity theorem: using compactness of trapped surfaces and the Raychaudhuri equation to prove geodesic incompleteness. This paper, together with Hawking's theorems, established the inevitability of singularities in GR.]

## Standard References

- **Munkres, J.R. (2000).** *Topology*, 2nd ed. Prentice Hall. [The standard undergraduate and early graduate topology text. Part I: general topology (topological spaces, connectedness, compactness, metric spaces, Urysohn's theorem, Tychonoff). Part II: algebraic topology (fundamental group, covering spaces, classification of surfaces). Clear, complete, well-organized.]

- **Hatcher, A. (2002).** *Algebraic Topology.* Cambridge University Press. Freely available at https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html. [The standard graduate algebraic topology text. Chapter 0: cell complexes; Chapter 1: fundamental group; Chapters 2–3: homology and cohomology. The best modern treatment for the mathematics needed in GR and quantum field theory.]

- **Milnor, J. (1963).** *Morse Theory.* Princeton University Press. (Annals of Mathematics Studies 51.) [Morse theory: using the critical point structure of a smooth function to deduce topological information about a manifold. Chapter 2 on the Morse lemma; Chapter 7 on Bott periodicity. A model of mathematical writing.]

- **Bott, R. and Tu, L.W. (1982).** *Differential Forms in Algebraic Topology.* Springer. [De Rham cohomology from the differential forms perspective; connects to Chapter 8 of this text. Beautiful and modern; the natural follow-on from Spivak's *Calculus on Manifolds*.]

## GR and Spacetime Topology

- **Hawking, S.W. and Ellis, G.F.R. (1973).** *The Large Scale Structure of Space-Time.* Cambridge University Press. [The definitive reference for GR global structure. Chapters 2–5 on causal structure, singularity theorems, and the topology of spacetime. Chapter 6 on the singularity theorems and their proofs. Every chapter of this reference is essential for Units VIII–XV of this textbook.]

- **Wald, R.M. (1984).** *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press. [Chapter 8: global methods in GR; the Raychaudhuri equation; energy conditions; singularity theorems. Appendix B: topological spaces; Appendix C: the Baire category theorem in analysis. One of the clearest presentations of the mathematical underpinnings of GR.]

- **Geroch, R. (1967).** "Topology in general relativity." *Journal of Mathematical Physics*, 8, 782–786. [Shows that, if energy conditions hold, the spatial topology of spacetime cannot change. This is the mathematical basis for the topological censorship theorem.]

- **Friedman, J., Schleich, K., and Witt, D.M. (1993).** "Topological censorship." *Physical Review Letters*, 71, 1486–1489. [Proves that the exterior gravitational field cannot detect the topology of the interior: any causal curve going "through" a topological feature is hidden behind a horizon. Energy conditions + global hyperbolicity imply topological censorship.]

## For the Curious

- **Perelman, G. (2002–2003).** Three preprints: "The entropy formula for the Ricci flow and its geometric applications" (arXiv:math/0211159); "Ricci flow with surgery on three-manifolds" (arXiv:math/0303109); "Finite extinction time for the solutions to the Ricci flow on certain three-manifolds" (arXiv:math/0307245). [The proof of the Poincaré conjecture and the geometrization conjecture. The first preprint introduces the entropy monotonicity; together they constitute one of the greatest achievements in mathematics of this century.]

- **Milnor, J.W. (1956).** "On manifolds homeomorphic to the 7-sphere." *Annals of Mathematics*, 64, 399–405. [The discovery of exotic spheres: manifolds homeomorphic to S⁷ but not diffeomorphic to it. This shows that smooth structure is more subtle than topological structure — there are 28 distinct smooth structures on S⁷. Raises the question of whether spacetime's smooth structure is the "standard" one, and what physics might distinguish exotic structures.]

- **Wheeler, J.A. (1955).** "Geons." *Physical Review*, 97, 511–536. [Wheeler introduces the "geon" — a gravitational entity held together by its own gravitational field — and discusses the topology of spacetime at the Planck scale ("spacetime foam"). The topological aspects of this paper anticipate many ideas in quantum gravity.]
