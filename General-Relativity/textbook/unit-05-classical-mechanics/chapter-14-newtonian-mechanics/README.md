# Chapter 14: Newtonian Mechanics and the Problem of Gravity

---

## Chapter Introduction

Newton's mechanics is the theory GR replaces — and understanding it deeply makes the replacement comprehensible. Newton identified three laws of motion and a law of universal gravitation, formulated his theory in absolute space and time, and used it to explain Kepler's planetary laws, tides, the precession of equinoxes, and the shape of the Earth. For 200 years, no observation contradicted it.

But Newtonian gravity has a conceptual flaw that Newton himself identified: it requires instantaneous action at a distance. When the Sun moves, Earth "feels" the change instantaneously, regardless of distance. This violates causality once special relativity establishes a universal speed limit. And there is a deeper problem: the equivalence principle. In Newtonian mechanics, gravitational mass (how much gravity pulls on a body) and inertial mass (how much a body resists acceleration) are equal — to extraordinary precision — but this equality has no explanation. Einstein's insight was that this equality is not a coincidence but a deep identity: gravity is not a force at all, but the curvature of spacetime.

This chapter develops Newtonian mechanics in enough depth to understand:
1. What GR must reproduce in the appropriate limit (the weak-field, low-velocity limit).
2. What Newtonian mechanics cannot explain (perihelion precession of Mercury, gravitational time dilation, gravitational waves).
3. The conceptual foundations that GR replaces (absolute space and time, instantaneous action at a distance, force as a fundamental concept).

## Important Themes

**The equivalence principle in disguise**: In Newton's second law F = m_I a and the gravitational force F = m_G g, the equality m_I = m_G (inertial = gravitational mass) is an empirical fact with no theoretical explanation. Eötvös experiments confirm it to 1 part in 10¹³. Einstein's equivalence principle elevates this equality to a fundamental axiom: there is no local experiment that can distinguish uniform acceleration from a gravitational field. This is the conceptual seed of GR.

**Action at a distance and its discontents**: Newton's law F = GMm/r² acts instantaneously across arbitrary distances. Maxwell's electromagnetism (Unit VI) showed that electromagnetic forces propagate at c via fields. Analogy suggests gravity should also propagate at finite speed — which leads directly to gravitational waves. GR confirms this: gravitational influences propagate at c, and the linearized theory predicts gravitational waves carrying energy.

**The failure of Newtonian gravity**: Mercury's perihelion precesses at 574 arcseconds per century. Newtonian perturbation theory from other planets accounts for 531 arcseconds. The remaining 43 arcseconds per century — measured by Le Verrier in 1859 — had no Newtonian explanation. GR gives exactly 43 arcseconds per century. This "anomalous" perihelion precession was one of the three classical tests of GR (the others being gravitational light deflection and gravitational redshift).
