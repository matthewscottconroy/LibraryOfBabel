# Chapter 15: Further Reading and References

---

## Primary Sources

**Hamilton, W.R. (1834–1835). "On a general method in dynamics." *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society*, 124, 247–308; 125, 95–144.**
The papers in which Hamilton introduces the Hamiltonian function, derives Hamilton's equations from a variational principle (the "principal function"), and develops the Hamilton-Jacobi theory. One of the great achievements in mathematical physics. Hamilton was 29 years old. The notation is archaic but the ideas are transparently beautiful.

**Lagrange, J.L. (1788). *Mécanique analytique.* Paris. (2nd ed. 1811–1815.)**
The systematic development of analytical mechanics: generalized coordinates, the Lagrangian, the Euler-Lagrange equations, and the treatment of constraints. Lagrange's preface famously announces that the book contains no figures — all results are derived algebraically. The foundation of what we now call the Lagrangian formulation.

**Noether, E. (1918). "Invariante Variationsprobleme." *Nachrichten von der Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen, Math.-Phys. Klasse*, 235–257.**
One of the most important papers in theoretical physics, proving both Noether theorems in 12 pages: Theorem I (global symmetries → conserved currents) and Theorem II (gauge symmetries → identities between field equations). Translated by M.A. Tavel in *Transport Theory and Statistical Physics* 1 (1971), 183–207. Anyone serious about physics should read the original.

**Euler, L. (1744). *Methodus inveniendi lineas curvas maximi minimive proprietate gaudentes.* Bousquet, Lausanne.**
The founding work on the calculus of variations: Euler derives his equation (the Euler-Lagrange equation in its original form) for extremizing functionals of the form $\int F(y, y', x)\,dx$. Euler's method is geometric; Lagrange later gave the algebraic ($\delta$) approach that we use today.

**Arnol'd, V.I. (1978). *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics.* Springer. (2nd ed. 1989.)**
The geometric approach to mechanics: configuration space, phase space, symplectic forms, Poisson brackets, integrable systems, and the KAM theorem. Written in the language of differential geometry, this book is the best preparation for the geometric viewpoint of GR. Chapter 8 on Hamiltonian mechanics; Chapter 9 on integrable systems; Appendix on symplectic geometry.

## Textbooks

**Goldstein, H., Poole, C., and Safko, J. (2002). *Classical Mechanics*, 3rd ed. Addison-Wesley.**
The standard graduate reference. Thorough coverage of the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations, canonical transformations, the Hamilton-Jacobi equation, and action-angle variables. Chapter 8 is particularly good on the connection between classical and quantum mechanics.

**Landau, L.D. and Lifshitz, E.M. (1976). *Mechanics*, 3rd ed. Pergamon.**
The most elegant treatment. Derives everything from the Lagrangian. Shortest path from first principles to results. The introduction, deriving the Lagrangian $L = T - V$ from Galilean invariance and homogeneity of space, is a tour de force.

**Scheck, F. (2010). *Mechanics: From Newton's Laws to Deterministic Chaos*, 5th ed. Springer.**
More mathematically sophisticated than Goldstein but less terse than Arnol'd. Good on Lie groups in mechanics, canonical transformations, and chaos.

## Special Topics

**Dirac, P.A.M. (1964). *Lectures on Quantum Mechanics.* Belfer Graduate School of Science, Yeshiva University.**
The Hamiltonian theory of constrained systems — the framework for quantizing gauge theories and GR. The Dirac-Bergmann analysis identifies first-class and second-class constraints; first-class constraints generate gauge transformations. Essential for understanding the Hamiltonian formulation of GR (ADM formalism).

**Wald, R.M. (1993). "Black hole entropy is Noether charge." *Physical Review D*, 48, R3427–R3431.**
Noether's theorem applied to diffeomorphism-invariant theories: the conserved charge associated with the Killing horizon generator is the black hole entropy. For Einstein gravity: $S = A/(4G)$ (Bekenstein-Hawking). For higher-derivative theories: a modified formula. A beautiful example of Noether's theorem solving a deep problem in quantum gravity.

**Kolmogorov, A.N. (1954). "On conservation of conditionally periodic motions for a small change in Hamilton's function." *Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR*, 98, 527–530.**
The original paper of the KAM theorem: most invariant tori of an integrable system survive under small perturbations. Independently proven by Arnold (1963) and Moser (1962). Fundamental for understanding the long-term stability of planetary orbits.

## Connections to GR

**Arnowitt, R., Deser, S., and Misner, C.W. (1962). "The dynamics of general relativity." In L. Witten, ed., *Gravitation.* John Wiley.** [Reprinted in *General Relativity and Gravitation*, 40 (2008), 1997–2027.]
The ADM (Arnowitt-Deser-Misner) formalism: the Hamiltonian mechanics of GR. The phase space is the space of Riemannian 3-metrics $h_{ij}$ and their conjugate momenta $\pi^{ij}$. The Hamiltonian and momentum constraints generate diffeomorphisms. The ADM energy is the Hamiltonian value for asymptotically flat spacetimes. Foundation of numerical relativity and loop quantum gravity.

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973). *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman.**
Chapter 21 on the variational principle for GR; §25.3 on the Schwarzschild effective potential as an analog of classical orbital mechanics. The connection between geodesic motion and central force problems is made explicit.
