# Chapter 15: Important Researchers

---

**Joseph-Louis Lagrange (1736–1813)**
Born in Turin, died in Paris. His *Mécanique analytique* (1788) is the definitive work of analytical mechanics — the Lagrangian, the Euler-Lagrange equations, generalized coordinates, and the treatment of constraints, all in one systematic framework. He is credited with the statement (possibly apocryphal): "There are no figures in this work." Indeed, he replaced Newton's geometric arguments with pure algebra. Also made foundational contributions to number theory, algebra, group theory (via permutation groups), and celestial mechanics (the Lagrange points in the three-body problem). His work is characterized by extraordinary elegance.

**William Rowan Hamilton (1805–1865)**
Irish mathematician, born and died in Dublin. Appointed Astronomer Royal of Ireland at age 22, while still an undergraduate. Reformulated mechanics via the Hamiltonian and principal function (1834–1835), discovering the deep analogy between optics and mechanics that later inspired Schrödinger's wave mechanics. Discovered quaternions in 1843 — the first non-commutative algebra — by carving the multiplication rule into the stone of Broome Bridge in Dublin (the plaque is still there). He spent the last 22 years of his life working on quaternions, to the detriment of his physics. The Hamiltonian formulation of mechanics is his greatest legacy.

**Emmy Noether (1882–1935)**
See the entry in Chapter 14. Her 1918 theorem is the conceptual heart of this chapter.

**Vladimir Igoreovich Arnol'd (1937–2010)**
Russian mathematician and physicist, student of Kolmogorov. Proved the Arnol'd-Liouville theorem on completely integrable systems, and the Arnol'd component of the KAM theorem. His book *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics* (1978) set the standard for the geometric approach to mechanics. Made major contributions to topology, celestial mechanics, singularity theory, and the theory of ordinary differential equations. His lectures were famous for their range and wit.

**Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov (1903–1987)**
Soviet mathematician of towering breadth: probability theory, topology, turbulence, algorithmic information theory, and the KAM theorem. His 1954 paper sketched the proof that most invariant tori persist under small perturbations; the detailed proof required another decade of work by Arnol'd and Moser. He was also a major figure in Soviet education.

**Jürgen Moser (1928–1999)**
German mathematician, responsible for the "M" in KAM. Proved the twist theorem (1962), extending the KAM result to area-preserving maps. His book *Stable and Random Motions in Dynamical Systems* (1973) is a classic treatment of the KAM theorem and its implications for long-term stability and chaos.

**Paul Adrien Maurice Dirac (1902–1984)**
Formulated quantum mechanics algebraically, predicted the positron, discovered the Dirac equation (special relativistic quantum mechanics), and developed the theory of constrained Hamiltonian systems. His *Lectures on Quantum Mechanics* (1964) contains the Dirac-Bergmann theory of constraints, essential for the Hamiltonian formulation of gauge theories and GR. Described as the most unusual person he had ever met by Bohr — famous for extreme literalness and silence. Shared the 1933 Nobel Prize with Schrödinger.

**Brandon Carter (1942–)**
Australian physicist. Discovered the Carter constant (1968) — the fourth conserved quantity for Kerr geodesics, associated with a hidden Killing tensor. This discovery proved that Kerr geodesics are integrable, enabling separation of variables and analytic solutions. Also developed the theory of black hole mechanics (the four laws, with Bardeen and Hawking) and the anthropic principle in cosmology.

**Richard Arnowitt (1928–2014), Stanley Deser (1931–2023), Charles Misner (1932–)**
Three American physicists who developed the ADM (Arnowitt-Deser-Misner) formalism for Hamiltonian GR in 1962. Their paper "The dynamics of general relativity" is the foundation of numerical relativity (used to compute gravitational waveforms for LIGO) and of the canonical quantization approach to quantum gravity (loop quantum gravity). The ADM energy is the total energy of an asymptotically flat spacetime.

**Leonhard Euler (1707–1783)**
See the entry in Chapter 14. His calculus of variations (1744) is the direct precursor of the Euler-Lagrange equations and Hamilton's principle.
