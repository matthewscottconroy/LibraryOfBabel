# Chapter 16: Further Reading and References

---

## Primary Sources

**Kepler, J. (1609). *Astronomia Nova.* Prague.**
The first two laws: planetary orbits are ellipses (not circles) with the Sun at one focus, and the radius vector sweeps equal areas in equal times. A landmark of scientific reasoning: Kepler had to overcome his own Pythagorean preference for circular perfection to accept ellipses. The book records his struggles and failures alongside his successes — an unusually honest scientific document.

**Kepler, J. (1619). *Harmonices Mundi.* Linz.**
The third law: $T^2 \propto a^3$. Buried in a mystical book about the "music of the spheres." The law appears in Book V, which also contains Kepler's attempt to relate planetary intervals to musical harmonies — a reminder that even great scientists are products of their time.

**Newton, I. (1687). *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica*, Book I, Propositions I–XIII. London.**
The derivation of the conic section orbits from the inverse-square law, and of Kepler's three laws. Prop. I: Kepler's second law (equal areas) follows from central force. Prop. XI: inverse-square force gives elliptic orbits. Prop. XII–XIII: parabola and hyperbola. The derivations are geometric (no calculus notation), but logically equivalent to Binet's equation.

**Le Verrier, U.J.J. (1859). "Théorie du mouvement de Mercure." *Annales de l'Observatoire Impérial de Paris*, 5, 1–196.**
The paper in which Le Verrier identifies the anomalous perihelion precession of Mercury: 38 arcsec/century unexplained by Newtonian perturbations (modern value: 43). The most famous discrepancy in pre-relativistic astronomy, which stood for 56 years.

**Einstein, A. (1915). "Erklärung der Perihelbewegung des Merkur aus der allgemeinen Relativitätstheorie." *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 831–839.**
The paper in which Einstein computes the GR perihelion precession of Mercury and gets exactly 43 arcsec/century — matching Le Verrier's anomaly. He wrote of this result that it gave him palpitations of the heart. One of the most dramatic confirmations of a new physical theory in history.

**Bertrand, J. (1873). "Théorème relatif au mouvement d'un point attiré vers un centre fixe." *Comptes rendus de l'Académie des sciences*, 77, 849–853.**
The proof that only $F \propto r$ (harmonic oscillator) and $F \propto 1/r^2$ (Newtonian gravity) produce all closed orbits. A short, elegant paper with a profound result about the special status of the inverse-square law.

**Pauli, W. (1926). "Über das Wasserstoffspektrum vom Standpunkt der neuen Quantenmechanik." *Zeitschrift für Physik*, 36, 336–363.**
The quantum-mechanical treatment of the Kepler problem using the Runge-Lenz operator. Derived the complete spectrum of hydrogen algebraically (without solving the Schrödinger equation), demonstrating the $SO(4)$ symmetry that explains the $n^2$ degeneracy of hydrogen energy levels.

## Modern References

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973). *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman.**
Chapter 25: effective potential, circular orbits, ISCO. §40.5: perihelion precession derivation. The standard GR text treatment.

**Will, C.M. (2014). "The confrontation between general relativity and experiment." *Living Reviews in Relativity*, 17, 4.**
Section 5 on solar system tests including perihelion precession. Comprehensive review of current precision and status.

**Pireaux, S. and Rozelot, J.-P. (2003). "Solar quadrupole moment and purely relativistic gravitations effects on Mercury's perihelion advance." *Astrophysics and Space Science*, 284, 1159–1194.**
The modern precise measurement: $42.98 \pm 0.04$ arcsec/century for Mercury's GR precession.

## Binary Pulsars

**Hulse, R.A. and Taylor, J.H. (1975). "Discovery of a pulsar in a binary system." *Astrophysical Journal Letters*, 195, L51–L53.**
The discovery paper for PSR B1913+16 — the first binary pulsar, for which Hulse and Taylor received the 1993 Nobel Prize. The binary pulsar system is a test bed for GR: the perihelion advance (4.2 deg/year), the gravitational redshift, the Shapiro delay, and the orbital decay due to gravitational radiation have all been measured to high precision.

**Taylor, J.H. and Weisberg, J.M. (1982). "A new test of general relativity: gravitational radiation and the binary pulsar PSR 1913+16." *Astrophysical Journal*, 253, 908–920.**
The first measurement of the orbital period decay due to gravitational wave emission, in quantitative agreement with GR. The agreement is now $< 0.05\%$ after decades of monitoring.

## Historical and Conceptual

**Goldstein, H. (1975). "More on the prehistory of the Laplace or Runge-Lenz vector." *American Journal of Physics*, 44, 1123–1124.**
Historical reconstruction of who discovered the conserved vector: Laplace (1799), Hamilton (1845), Runge (1919), Lenz (1924). The standard "LRL" attribution is historically misleading.

**Chandrasekhar, S. (1983). *The Mathematical Theory of Black Holes.* Oxford University Press.**
Chapter 3: geodesics in the Schwarzschild metric. The standard treatment of the Schwarzschild effective potential and orbit classification.
