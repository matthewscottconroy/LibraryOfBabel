# Unit VI: Electromagnetism

---

## Unit Introduction

Electromagnetism holds a special place in the path to GR for two reasons. First, it is the first fundamental field theory — a theory in which the dynamical degrees of freedom are not particles but fields distributed throughout space. Maxwell's equations for the electromagnetic field (1865) are the prototype for all classical field theories, including GR. Second, electromagnetism in its relativistic formulation is the theory whose symmetry group drove Einstein to special relativity: the Lorentz transformations are exactly the symmetries of Maxwell's equations.

Maxwell's equations in differential form,

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}, \quad \nabla \times \mathbf{B} - \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J}$$
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0, \quad \nabla \times \mathbf{E} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t} = 0$$

predict electromagnetic waves traveling at speed c — which must be the same in all inertial frames (this is what motivates special relativity). In covariant 4D notation, they compress to:

$$\partial_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0 J^\mu, \quad \partial_{[\alpha} F_{\beta\gamma]} = 0$$

or, in the language of differential forms: dF = 0, d★F = ★J.

This compactness is not superficial — the 4D formulation reveals that electromagnetism is a **gauge theory**: the physics is invariant under A_μ → A_μ + ∂_μ χ. Gauge invariance is the defining symmetry of all fundamental interactions. GR is itself a gauge theory, with diffeomorphism invariance playing the role of the gauge group.

Understanding electromagnetism deeply — its field theory structure, its gauge invariance, its action principle, its coupling to matter — is indispensable preparation for GR.
