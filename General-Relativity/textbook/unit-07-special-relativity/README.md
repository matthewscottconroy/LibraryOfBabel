# Unit VII: Special Relativity

---

## Unit Introduction

Special relativity is Einstein's 1905 revolution: the abandonment of absolute time and the unification of space and time into a single 4-dimensional continuum called **spacetime**. The two postulates — (1) the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames, and (2) the speed of light is the same in all inertial frames — have consequences of extraordinary depth: time dilation, length contraction, the relativity of simultaneity, the equivalence of mass and energy (E = mc²), and the 4-dimensional spacetime interval ds² = −c²dt² + dx² + dy² + dz².

For the road to GR, special relativity is not merely a stepping stone — it is the flat-spacetime limit of GR, the theory that GR must reduce to when gravity is turned off. The mathematical language developed here:

- **4-vectors** (contravariant) and **4-covectors** (covariant): the natural objects of special relativity, transforming under the Lorentz group.
- **The Minkowski metric** ηᵤᵥ = diag(−1, +1, +1, +1): defines inner products, raises/lowers indices, and determines which vectors are timelike/spacelike/null.
- **Relativistic kinematics**: 4-momentum pᵘ = (E/c, **p**), proper time τ, 4-velocity uᵘ = dxᵘ/dτ.
- **Relativistic dynamics**: the 4-force equation dpᵘ/dτ = Fᵘ; energy-momentum relation E² = (pc)² + (mc²)².
- **Electromagnetic field tensor** Fᵤᵥ: unifies **E** and **B** into a single antisymmetric 2-tensor; Maxwell's equations become ∂_ν Fᵘᵛ = μ₀ Jᵘ.
- **The stress-energy tensor** Tᵘᵛ: the source of the gravitational field in GR. Contains energy density, momentum density, energy flux, and stress.

Special relativity is the arena in which tensor calculus first becomes necessary. Every student of GR should be completely fluent in SR before proceeding to curved spacetime.
