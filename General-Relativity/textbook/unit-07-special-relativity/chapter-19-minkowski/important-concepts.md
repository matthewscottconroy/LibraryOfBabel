# Chapter 19: Important Concepts

---

**The Two Postulates of Special Relativity**
(1) The laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames. (2) The speed of light in vacuum is the same in all inertial frames, regardless of the motion of source or observer. From these two postulates follow all the kinematic consequences of SR: Lorentz transformations, time dilation, length contraction, relativity of simultaneity, the invariant interval.

**Minkowski Spacetime**
4-dimensional spacetime with the indefinite metric $ds^2 = -c^2dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$ (signature $-+++$). Named for Hermann Minkowski, who gave the geometric formulation of SR (1908). Not a Riemannian manifold (positive-definite metric) but a pseudo-Riemannian manifold (indefinite metric). The geometry of all flat spacetimes and the local geometry of any curved spacetime (by the equivalence principle).

**Spacetime Interval**
$ds^2 = \eta_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu = -c^2dt^2 + d\mathbf{r}^2$: the Lorentz-invariant measure of "distance" in spacetime. Unlike the Euclidean distance, $ds^2$ can be positive (spacelike), zero (null), or negative (timelike). The invariant interval is the same in all inertial frames — it is the truly physical distance in spacetime.

**Timelike, Spacelike, and Null**
Three types of spacetime separation: timelike ($ds^2 < 0$): can be connected by a massive particle, causally related; null ($ds^2 = 0$): connected by a light ray; spacelike ($ds^2 > 0$): causally disconnected. The classification is Lorentz-invariant. In GR, the same classification applies at each point using the local metric $g_{\mu\nu}$.

**The Light Cone**
The set of events connected to a given event by null rays: $ds^2 = 0$. Divides spacetime into future timelike, past timelike, and spacelike (causally disconnected) regions. The causal structure of spacetime — which events can influence which — is encoded in the light cone structure. In GR, the light cone "tips" in curved spacetime, and at a black hole horizon it tips so that all future-directed paths lead inward.

**Proper Time**
$d\tau = \sqrt{-ds^2}/c = dt\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$: the time measured by a clock co-moving with the particle. Frame-independent: all observers agree on the reading of a co-moving clock. The geodesic equation maximizes proper time among paths between two events (the principle of maximal aging). GPS satellites require relativistic corrections to proper time.

**Lorentz Transformation**
$t' = \gamma(t - vx/c^2)$, $x' = \gamma(x - vt)$ (boost in $x$-direction with velocity $v$): the transformation between inertial frames that preserves $ds^2$. Parametrized by velocity $v$ or rapidity $\phi = \tanh^{-1}(v/c)$. The matrix form $\Lambda^\mu_{\ \nu}$ satisfies $\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda = \eta$.

**Lorentz Group**
$O(3,1)$: the group of $4\times4$ matrices preserving the Minkowski metric. Has 4 connected components. The physical subgroup (identity-connected) is $SO^+(3,1) = L^\uparrow_+$, generated by 3 rotations ($J_i$) and 3 boosts ($K_i$). The double cover is $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, which is required for spinor representations (fermions). In GR, the local symmetry group is the Lorentz group at each tangent space.

**Time Dilation**
Moving clocks run slow by factor $\gamma$: $\Delta t = \gamma\Delta\tau$. Confirmed by: muon lifetime measurement (Frisch-Smith 1963), flying atomic clocks (Hafele-Keating 1972), and GPS corrections (38 microseconds per day). Not a "trick" of perception — a real physical effect, confirmed whenever a moving clock returns to its starting frame.

**Length Contraction**
Moving rods are shorter by factor $\gamma$: $L = L_0/\gamma$. Not a physical compression — the rod is undamaged in its rest frame. The contraction is a consequence of the relativity of simultaneity: measuring both ends of the rod simultaneously (as required for "length") gives different results in different frames.

**Relativity of Simultaneity**
Two events that are simultaneous in one inertial frame are not simultaneous in another frame moving relative to the first (unless the events are at the same spatial location). This is the most counterintuitive aspect of SR and the key to resolving the "paradoxes" (ladder paradox, twin paradox).

**Twin Paradox**
The "paradox": if A stays home and B travels away and returns, each sees the other's clock running slow — so how can B be younger? Resolution: the situation is not symmetric. B's worldline is non-straight (it has a kink at the turnaround). The proper time elapsed is $\tau = \int\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}dt < T$ for any path that deviates from straight. The straight worldline (constant velocity) maximizes proper time — this is the principle of maximal aging, which in GR becomes the geodesic principle.

**Poincaré Group**
The 10-parameter symmetry group of flat Minkowski spacetime: 3 rotations + 3 boosts + 3 spatial translations + 1 time translation. By Noether's theorem, the corresponding conserved quantities are: angular momentum, boost momentum, linear momentum, and energy. The Poincaré group is the gauge group of special relativity. In GR, the global Poincaré symmetry is broken (curved spacetime has no global Killing vectors in general), but it is recovered asymptotically for isolated systems (the ADM 4-momentum).
