# Chapter 20: Further Reading and References

---

## Primary Sources

**Einstein, A. (1905). "Ist die Trägheit eines Körpers von seinem Energieinhalt abhängig?" *Annalen der Physik*, 18, 639–641.**
The $E = mc^2$ paper. A brief three-page follow-up to the SR paper, showing that if a body emits radiation of energy $L$, its mass decreases by $L/c^2$. The original statement of mass-energy equivalence. English translation: "Does the Inertia of a Body Depend upon its Energy-Content?" in *The Principle of Relativity* (Dover, 1952).

**Minkowski, H. (1908). "Die Grundgleichungen für die elektromagnetischen Vorgänge in bewegten Körpern."**
Introduces 4-vectors and the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor in their modern form.

**de Broglie, L. (1924). *Recherches sur la théorie des quanta.* Ph.D. thesis, University of Paris.**
Introduced the de Broglie relation $p = h/\lambda$ by analogy with photons: if photons have momentum $p = E/c = hf/c = h/\lambda$, then perhaps all particles have an associated wavelength. Verified by Davisson-Germer (1927). This is the first application of 4-momentum to matter waves.

**Compton, A.H. (1923). "A Quantum Theory of the Scattering of X-Rays by Light Elements." *Physical Review*, 21, 483–502.**
Derives the Compton formula using relativistic 4-momentum conservation, treating photons as particles with momentum $p = E/c = h/\lambda$. The agreement with experiment was decisive evidence that photons have momentum.

## Textbooks

**Taylor, E.F. and Wheeler, J.A. (1992). *Spacetime Physics.* 2nd ed. W.H. Freeman.**
Chapters 7–8 on 4-momentum and relativistic dynamics. The emphasis on invariant mass is particularly well-done.

**Landau, L.D. and Lifshitz, E.M. (1975). *The Classical Theory of Fields.* 4th ed. Butterworth-Heinemann.**
Chapter 2 on relativistic mechanics; Chapter 3 on collisions. The most elegant compact treatment. Also Chapters 7–11 on the stress-energy tensor and its connection to EM.

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973). *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman.**
Chapter 5 on stress-energy tensor. Box 5.1 on energy-momentum for dust, perfect fluid, and EM field. Chapter 22 on variational principle for the stress-energy tensor.

**Wald, R.M. (1984). *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press.**
Chapter 4 on the stress-energy tensor and energy conditions. The mathematically rigorous treatment.

## On Energy Conditions and Their Violations

**Curiel, E. (2017). "A Primer on Energy Conditions." In *Towards a Theory of Spacetime Theories*, Birkhäuser.**
A careful philosophical and physical analysis of what the energy conditions mean, why they are plausible for classical matter, and how quantum effects can violate them.

**Morris, M.S. and Thorne, K.S. (1988). "Wormholes in spacetime and their use for interstellar travel." *American Journal of Physics*, 56, 395–412.**
Shows that traversable wormholes require matter violating the WEC. Estimates the amount of exotic matter needed. A rigorous treatment that launched the modern study of traversable wormholes.

**Epstein, H. et al. (1965). "Nonpositivity of the Energy Density in Quantized Field Theories." *Il Nuovo Cimento*, 36, 1016–1022.**
Proves that quantum field theories allow local violations of the WEC — the vacuum state has fluctuations that can create negative energy density in a region, as long as positive energy compensates elsewhere.

## On $E = mc^2$ and Nuclear Energy

**Rhodes, R. (1986). *The Making of the Atomic Bomb.* Simon & Schuster.**
The definitive account of the Manhattan Project. Places the physics (fission, critical mass, bomb design) in its historical context. Chapter 5 on the discovery of fission; Chapter 11 on the chain reaction.

**Fermi, E. (1952). "Experimental Production of a Divergent Chain Reaction." *American Journal of Physics*, 20, 536–558.**
Fermi's own account of the first nuclear reactor (Chicago Pile-1, December 2, 1942). Describes the physics of the chain reaction and the experimental design.
