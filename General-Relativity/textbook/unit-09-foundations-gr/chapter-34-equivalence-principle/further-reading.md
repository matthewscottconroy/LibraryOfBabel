# Chapter 34: Further Reading and References

---

## Primary Sources

**Einstein, A. (1907). "Über das Relativitätsprinzip und die aus demselben gezogenen Folgerungen." *Jahrbuch der Radioaktivität und Elektronik*, 4, 411–462.**
Contains Einstein's first statement of the equivalence principle (§17): "We consider two systems of motion... which is equivalent to a gravitational field of homogeneous nature." The paper reviews SR and its applications, with the equivalence principle as the key new conceptual step toward GR.

**Eötvös, R., Pekár, D., and Fekete, E. (1922). "Beiträge zum Gesetze der Proportionalität von Trägheit und Gravität." *Annalen der Physik*, 68, 11–66.**
The main publication of the Eötvös torsion balance experiments, confirming the equality of inertial and gravitational mass to $\sim 10^{-9}$.

**Pound, R.V. and Rebka, G.A. (1959). "Gravitational Red-Shift in Nuclear Resonance." *Physical Review Letters*, 3, 439–441.**
First laboratory measurement of gravitational redshift. Uses the Mössbauer effect (recoil-free nuclear resonance absorption) to detect the frequency shift of $\gamma$-rays over a 22.5 m height difference.

**Pound, R.V. and Snider, J.L. (1965). "Effect of Gravity on Gamma Radiation." *Physical Review*, 140, B788–B803.**
Improved measurement to 1% accuracy.

**Shapiro, I.I. (1964). "Fourth Test of General Relativity." *Physical Review Letters*, 13, 789–791.**
Predicts the gravitational time delay of radar echoes from planets near conjunction with the Sun — the "Shapiro delay." The paper is short, elegant, and immediately suggested an experimentally feasible test.

**Bertotti, B., Iess, L., and Tortora, P. (2003). "A test of general relativity using radio links with the Cassini spacecraft." *Nature*, 425, 374–376.**
Measures the Shapiro delay during Cassini's solar conjunction, confirming GR's prediction to $2.3\times 10^{-5}$ — the most precise weak-field test of GR.

**MICROSCOPE Collaboration, Touboul, P. et al. (2017). "MICROSCOPE Mission: First Results of a Space Test of the Equivalence Principle." *Physical Review Letters*, 119, 231101.**
**Touboul, P. et al. (2022). "MICROSCOPE Mission: Final Results of the Test of the Equivalence Principle." *Physical Review Letters*, 129, 121102.**
First and final results from the space-based WEP test, constraining $|\eta(\text{Ti}, \text{Pt-Rh})| < 1.5\times 10^{-15}$.

## Textbooks

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973). *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman.**
Chapter 16 on the equivalence principle; Box 16.4 "Three meanings of the equivalence principle." Chapter 1 ("Geometrodynamics in brief") develops the equivalence principle as the entry point to GR. An unsurpassed overview.

**Will, C.M. (1993). *Theory and Experiment in Gravitational Physics.* Revised ed. Cambridge University Press.**
The definitive textbook on experimental tests of GR. Chapter 2 on the equivalence principle and the PPN (parametrized post-Newtonian) formalism; Chapter 3 on tests of the WEP; Chapter 4 on gravitational redshift and the EEP. The standard reference for experimental gravitation.

**Will, C.M. (2014). "The Confrontation between General Relativity and Experiment." *Living Reviews in Relativity*, 17, 4.**
Updated living review (regularly revised) covering all precision tests of GR. Available at livingreviews.springer.com.

**Wald, R.M. (1984). *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press.**
Chapter 4.1 on the equivalence principle. Clean, rigorous statement.

## On Mach's Principle

**Mach, E. (1883). *Die Mechanik in ihrer Entwickelung.* F.A. Brockhaus, Leipzig. (English: *The Science of Mechanics,* Open Court, 1893.)**
Mach's criticism of Newton's absolute space and the proposal that inertia is caused by distant matter. The text that shaped Einstein's thinking. Chapter 2, §6, on the bucket argument.

**Barbour, J. and Pfister, H. (eds.) (1995). *Mach's Principle: From Newton's Bucket to Quantum Gravity.* Birkhäuser.**
Collection of essays on whether GR realizes Mach's principle. Einstein's original intentions, the degree to which they were achieved, and modern perspectives.
