# Chapter 34: Important Concepts

---

**Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP)**
The trajectory of a freely falling test body is independent of its mass and internal composition. Equivalently, $m_i = m_g$ universally. Tested to $10^{-15}$ by the MICROSCOPE satellite (2017). The most precisely tested principle in science.

**Inertial Mass vs. Gravitational Mass**
Inertial mass $m_i$ measures resistance to acceleration ($\mathbf{F} = m_i\mathbf{a}$); gravitational mass $m_g$ measures coupling to the gravitational field ($\mathbf{F} = Gm_g M/r^2$). Their equality has no explanation in Newtonian mechanics — it is a coincidence. In GR, the geodesic equation contains no mass at all: the equality is a geometric fact.

**Eötvös Experiment**
Torsion balance test of WEP. Eötvös (1890–1909) tested $m_i = m_g$ to $10^{-9}$; Braginsky-Panov (1971) to $10^{-12}$; MICROSCOPE (2017) to $10^{-15}$. The parameter $\eta(A,B) = 2|a_A - a_B|/|a_A + a_B|$ measures WEP violation.

**Einstein Equivalence Principle (EEP)**
WEP holds, AND the outcome of any local non-gravitational experiment is independent of the velocity of the freely falling frame (Local Lorentz Invariance) and of location/time (Local Position Invariance). Implies that in a freely falling frame, physics is exactly SR. Basis of the minimal coupling prescription.

**Strong Equivalence Principle (SEP)**
EEP extends to all experiments — including those measuring gravitational physics. Self-gravitating bodies fall with the same acceleration as test bodies (Nordtvedt effect absent). Only GR among commonly-studied theories satisfies SEP fully.

**Normal Coordinates (Riemann Normal Coordinates)**
At any event $p$: coordinates in which $g_{\mu\nu}(p) = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$. The mathematical expression of EEP: at any point, there is a locally inertial (freely falling) frame. Curvature appears only in second derivatives of the metric, which cannot be set to zero in general.

**Light Bending by Gravity**
From EEP: light curves downward in a gravitational field (equivalently, follows null geodesics in curved spacetime). The equivalence-principle estimate gives $2GM/(bc^2)$; full GR gives twice this, $4GM/(bc^2)$, because both the spatial and temporal parts of the metric contribute. Measured by Eddington (1919), confirmed to $10^{-4}$ by modern radio astrometry (VLBI).

**Gravitational Redshift**
Photons lose energy climbing out of a gravitational potential: $f_{\rm obs}/f_{\rm emit} \approx 1 - g\Delta h/c^2$ (weak field). Equivalently, clocks run slower deeper in a gravitational well. Measured by Pound-Rebka (1959), Gravity Probe A (1976). Directly used in GPS satellite timing corrections: satellite clocks gain $45.9\,\mu$s/day due to weaker gravity minus $7.2\,\mu$s/day due to orbital velocity, net $+38.7\,\mu$s/day corrected in firmware.

**Minimal Coupling Prescription ("Comma Goes to Semicolon")**
To write any SR equation in curved spacetime: replace $\eta_{\mu\nu}\to g_{\mu\nu}$, replace $\partial_\mu\to\nabla_\mu$, replace $d^4x\to\sqrt{-g}d^4x$. Ensures local SR is recovered in every freely-falling frame. There are ambiguities at order $R$ (curvature-dependent non-minimal couplings).

**General Covariance**
The equations of physics have the same form in any coordinate system. Not merely a mathematical requirement (tensorial equations) but a physical one (no preferred coordinates = no absolute space). The gauge group of GR is $\text{Diff}(M)$, the group of diffeomorphisms of the spacetime manifold.

**Mach's Principle**
Inertia is determined by the large-scale matter distribution of the universe. Einstein was influenced by this; GR partially accommodates it (Lense-Thirring frame-dragging) but admits solutions (de Sitter, Minkowski) without matter, violating the strict Machian program.

**Shapiro Time Delay**
Light passing near a massive body takes longer than it would in flat space: $\Delta t \approx (2GM/c^3)\ln(4r_E r_P/b^2)$. Measured by radar ranging to Venus (Shapiro et al. 1971), confirmed to $2.3\times 10^{-5}$ by Cassini spacecraft (Bertotti et al. 2003).
