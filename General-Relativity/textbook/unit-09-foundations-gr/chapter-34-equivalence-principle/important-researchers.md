# Chapter 34: Important Researchers

---

**Galileo Galilei (1564–1642)**
Italian physicist, astronomer, and mathematician. First demonstrated (around 1590–1604) that all bodies fall with the same acceleration in a gravitational field, independent of their mass — the first experimental test of the weak equivalence principle. His inclined-plane experiments were systematic and quantitative, a prototype for the scientific method. Also discovered the moons of Jupiter, supported heliocentrism, and developed the kinematics of uniformly accelerated motion. Placed under house arrest by the Inquisition in 1633 for his astronomical views.

**Isaac Newton (1643–1727)**
See Chapter 14. Newton recognized the puzzle of inertial vs. gravitational mass and confirmed their equality to $\sim 10^{-3}$ using pendulums of different materials. His law of universal gravitation $F = Gm_g M/r^2$, combined with the equality $m_i = m_g$, gives the universal acceleration — but Newton had no explanation for why the two masses should be equal.

**Roland von Eötvös (1848–1919)**
Hungarian physicist. Developed the torsion balance into a precision instrument and from 1890 to 1922 (work continued by colleagues after his death) tested the equality $m_i = m_g$ to $10^{-9}$. His results are published in a posthumous 1922 paper with Pekár and Fekete. The Eötvös experiment is the prototype for all subsequent precision equivalence principle tests. The unit of gradient of gravitational acceleration (the Eötvös unit, $10^{-9}$ Gal/cm = $10^{-9}$ s$^{-2}$) is named for him.

**Albert Einstein (1879–1955)**
The equivalence principle was Einstein's "happiest thought" (1907): a freely falling person feels no gravity. This insight led, over eight years, to GR. He developed the principle into the full EEP: in a freely falling frame, all of SR holds. Then showed this implies light bends, clocks redshift, and general covariance is required. The EP is the soul of GR; everything else is consequence.

**Ernst Mach (1838–1916)**
Austrian physicist and philosopher. His criticism of Newton's absolute space and his proposal (1883) that inertia is caused by the distribution of distant matter ("Mach's principle") strongly influenced Einstein's thinking. Einstein initially hoped GR would be fully Machian — that the metric would be entirely determined by the matter distribution — but this hope was not realized. Mach was a strict positivist who rejected atomism (famously debating Boltzmann) until near the end of his life.

**Robert V. Pound (1919–2010) and Glen A. Rebka Jr. (1931–2015)**
American physicists at Harvard. Their 1959 experiment used the Mössbauer effect to measure the gravitational redshift of gamma rays over a 22.5-meter height difference in the Jefferson Physical Laboratory tower, confirming $\Delta f/f = gh/c^2$ to 10%. Pound and J.L. Snider (1965) improved this to 1%. The first laboratory test of gravitational redshift.

**Irwin Shapiro (1929–)**
American astrophysicist at Harvard/MIT Haystack Observatory. Predicted (1964) the gravitational time delay of radar signals passing near the Sun — the "fourth test of GR." Measured the delay using radar ranging to Venus and Mercury (1966–1971), confirming GR to 3%. The Cassini mission (2003) confirmed the Shapiro delay to $2.3\times 10^{-5}$.

**Leonard I. Schiff (1915–1971)**
American physicist. Proposed the tests of the equivalence principle using gyroscopes in space (1960) — the program that became Gravity Probe B. Derived the Schiff formula for geodetic precession of a gyroscope in orbit. Also showed (1960) that the EEP implies gravitational redshift to all orders. The "Schiff conjecture" is that WEP implies EEP (for consistent metric theories) — largely but not unconditionally established.

**Peter Bergmann (1915–2002)**
German-American physicist, student and collaborator of Einstein. Helped develop the constrained Hamiltonian formulation of GR (independently of Dirac) and clarified the meaning of general covariance. His 1942 textbook *Introduction to the Theory of Relativity* was a standard reference for a generation.

**The MICROSCOPE Science Team**
The MICROSCOPE (Micro-Satellite à traînée Compensée pour l'Observation du Principe d'Equivalence) mission (CNES, 2016–2018) tested WEP in space using two coaxial cylindrical test masses — one titanium, one platinum-rhodium — in free fall in orbit. Published in 2017 and 2022, the constraint is $|\eta| < 1.5\times 10^{-15}$, improving the best ground test by a factor of 10. The next planned test (MICROSCOPE 2 or STE-QUEST) aims at $10^{-17}$.
