# Chapter 35: Further Reading and References

## Primary Sources

**Einstein, A. (1915). "Die Feldgleichungen der Gravitation." *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 844–847.**
The November 25, 1915 paper presenting the correct Einstein field equations. Short (3 pages) and decisive. English translation in *The Principle of Relativity* (Dover, 1952).

**Einstein, A. (1915). "Erklärung der Perihelbewegung des Merkur aus der allgemeinen Relativitätstheorie." *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 831–839.**
The November 18, 1915 paper computing Mercury's perihelion precession from the (then-preliminary) field equations. The answer — 43 arcseconds per century — matched the long-standing observational anomaly exactly.

**Hilbert, D. (1915). "Die Grundlagen der Physik." *Nachrichten von der Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen*, 395–407.**
Hilbert's derivation of the Einstein field equations from the Einstein-Hilbert action. Submitted November 20, 1915 (five days before Einstein). The variational derivation is one of the most elegant in physics.

**Einstein, A. (1917). "Kosmologische Betrachtungen zur allgemeinen Relativitätstheorie." *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 142–152.**
Introduces the cosmological constant $\Lambda$ to allow a static universe. The paper that both introduced and (in retrospect) unnecessarily rejected the cosmological constant.

**Lovelock, D. (1971). "The Einstein Tensor and Its Generalizations." *Journal of Mathematical Physics*, 12, 498–501.**
The proof that the Einstein tensor (plus cosmological constant) is the unique symmetric, divergence-free tensor built from the metric and its first two derivatives in 4D. The uniqueness theorem for the Einstein equations.

**Choquet-Bruhat, Y. (1952). "Théorème d'existence pour certains systèmes d'équations aux dérivées partielles non linéaires." *Acta Mathematica*, 88, 141–225.**
Proof of the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the Einstein equations with standard matter. The mathematical foundation of GR as an initial value problem.

**Pretorius, F. (2005). "Evolution of Binary Black-Hole Spacetimes." *Physical Review Letters*, 95, 121101.**
First stable numerical simulation of binary black hole merger using the full nonlinear Einstein equations. The gravitational waveform template for what became GW150914.

## Textbooks

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973). *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman.**
Chapters 17–22 on the Einstein equations, their derivation, and solutions. Box 17.2 on the history of the field equations. Chapter 21 on linearized theory. The "bible" of GR.

**Wald, R.M. (1984). *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press.**
Chapter 4.3 on the Einstein equations. Chapter 10 on the initial value formulation. The most mathematically rigorous standard textbook.

**Landau, L.D. and Lifshitz, E.M. (1975). *The Classical Theory of Fields.* 4th ed. Butterworth-Heinemann.**
Chapters 14–17 on the Einstein equations, linearized gravity, and the post-Newtonian approximation. The most compact derivation.

**Arnowitt, R., Deser, S., and Misner, C.W. (1962). "The dynamics of general relativity." In L. Witten (ed.), *Gravitation: An Introduction to Current Research.* Wiley. Reprinted: *General Relativity and Gravitation*, 40, 1997–2027 (2008).**
The original ADM paper presenting the Hamiltonian formulation of GR.

## On the History of the Field Equations

**Pais, A. (1982). *"Subtle is the Lord...": The Science and Life of Albert Einstein.* Oxford University Press.**
The definitive scientific biography of Einstein. Chapters 11–15 on the path from the equivalence principle to the field equations, with careful attention to the mathematical development and the Hilbert priority dispute.

**Corry, L., Renn, J., and Stachel, J. (1997). "Belated Decision in the Hilbert-Einstein Priority Dispute." *Science*, 278, 1270–1273.**
Analysis of the corrected proofs of Hilbert's November 20 paper, showing that the original submission was incomplete (lacked the trace term). Settled (partially) the century-old priority dispute.
