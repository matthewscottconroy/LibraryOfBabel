# Chapter 36: The Stress-Energy Tensor in GR

---

## Chapter Introduction

In Newtonian gravity, the source of the gravitational field is a single scalar: the mass density $\rho$. Poisson's equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$ says that mass curves space (in the Newtonian sense — it creates a potential). A cloud of dust, a neutron star, the Earth — each characterized by its density distribution, each generating a gravitational field.

In GR, the source is richer: the stress-energy tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$. It encodes not just the energy density (the GR analog of $\rho$) but also the momentum density and the stress (pressure and shear). All forms of energy and momentum — not just rest mass, but kinetic energy, thermal energy, electromagnetic energy, the energy of nuclear binding — contribute to $T^{\mu\nu}$ and therefore curve spacetime.

This has profound consequences:

**Pressure gravitates.** In Newtonian gravity, pressure is the support mechanism that counteracts gravity: higher pressure means less compression. In GR, the pressure in the stress-energy tensor *adds* to the gravitational source. This leads to the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) equation for stellar structure — a GR correction to hydrostatic equilibrium in which pressure contributes positively to gravitational attraction. For a neutron star, this effect limits the maximum mass to $\sim 2$–$3\,M_\odot$ (the Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit). Above this, no equation of state can support the star against collapse.

**The cosmological constant is pressure.** A vacuum energy density with $p = -\rho c^2$ (equation of state $w = -1$) gravitates with *negative* effective pressure — it repels rather than attracts. This is the dark energy driving the current cosmic acceleration.

**Conservation of energy-momentum.** In SR, the conservation law $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ is an independent statement imposed on the theory. In GR, it follows from the field equations plus the Bianchi identity: $\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu} = 0$ identically, and $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$ then implies $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$. Energy-momentum conservation is *built into the geometry*.

This chapter develops the stress-energy tensor for the forms of matter most relevant to GR: dust, perfect fluids, and the electromagnetic field. We derive the conservation laws that follow from the equations of motion and the Bianchi identity, and apply them to the TOV equation for relativistic stellar structure.
