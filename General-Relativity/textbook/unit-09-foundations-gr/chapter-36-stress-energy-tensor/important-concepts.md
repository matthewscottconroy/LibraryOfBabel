# Chapter 36: Important Concepts

---

**Stress-Energy Tensor**
$T^{\mu\nu}$: symmetric rank-2 tensor encoding the distribution of energy and momentum in spacetime. $T^{00}$: energy density; $T^{0i}$: momentum density / energy flux; $T^{ij}$: stress (momentum flux). The source of the gravitational field in the Einstein equations. Definition via metric variation: $T_{\mu\nu} = -2/\sqrt{-g}\cdot\delta S_{\rm matter}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$.

**Dust**
$T^{\mu\nu} = \rho_0 u^\mu u^\nu$: pressureless perfect fluid. Models cold dark matter, pressureless gas. Conservation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ gives the continuity equation (rest-mass conservation) and the geodesic equation — free particles follow geodesics.

**Perfect Fluid**
$T^{\mu\nu} = (\varepsilon + p)u^\mu u^\nu/c^2 + pg^{\mu\nu}$: fluid with isotropic pressure, no viscosity or heat conduction. Parameterized by proper energy density $\varepsilon$, pressure $p$, and 4-velocity $u^\mu$. Conservation gives relativistic Euler equations.

**Equation of State**
Relation $p = p(\varepsilon)$ closing the system of fluid equations. Key cases: dust ($p = 0$), radiation ($p = \varepsilon/3$), dark energy ($p = -\varepsilon$), stiff fluid ($p = \varepsilon$). Determines the cosmological evolution and stellar structure.

**Pressure Gravitates**
In GR, the gravitational source is $(\varepsilon + 3p)$ (from the trace of the Einstein equations), not just $\varepsilon$. Pressure contributes positively to gravity. For a neutron star supported by nuclear pressure: the very pressure needed to support the star also adds to its gravitational pull, limiting the maximum mass.

**Weak Energy Condition (WEC)**
$T_{\mu\nu}t^\mu t^\nu \geq 0$ for all timelike $t^\mu$: energy density is non-negative in all frames. For perfect fluid: $\varepsilon \geq 0$ and $\varepsilon + p \geq 0$. Violated by quantum vacuum fluctuations and dark energy interpretations.

**Strong Energy Condition (SEC)**
$(T_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}T)t^\mu t^\nu \geq 0$: equivalent to $R_{\mu\nu}t^\mu t^\nu \geq 0$ via Einstein equations. For perfect fluid: $\varepsilon + p \geq 0$ and $\varepsilon + 3p \geq 0$. Required by Penrose-Hawking singularity theorems. Violated by $\Lambda > 0$ (dark energy, $p = -\varepsilon$).

**Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) Equation**
$dp/dr = -G(\varepsilon + p)(m(r)c^2 + 4\pi r^3 p)/[r^2c^2(1 - 2Gm(r)/(rc^2))]$: GR hydrostatic equilibrium for a spherically symmetric star. Contains three GR corrections beyond Newtonian ($\varepsilon + p$ instead of $\varepsilon$; pressure source inside star; metric factor). Determines neutron star structure and maximum mass.

**Oppenheimer-Volkoff Mass Limit**
Maximum mass for a neutron star (or any compact object in hydrostatic equilibrium), determined by the nuclear equation of state. For realistic equations of state: $\sim 2$–$3 M_\odot$. Maximum observed neutron star: $2.35 \pm 0.17 M_\odot$ (PSR J0952-0607). Stars above this limit collapse to black holes.

**Buchdahl Limit**
Universal compactness bound for stars in GR: $2GM/(Rc^2) \leq 8/9$ — more compact than this without becoming a black hole is impossible (for matter satisfying density decreasing outward + WEC). A Schwarzschild black hole has $2GM/(Rc^2) = 1$.

**Energy-Momentum Non-Conservation in Curved Spacetime**
$\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ is the covariant conservation law, but it does not imply a conserved total energy in general curved spacetimes (the Christoffel terms represent energy exchanged with the gravitational field). Global energy is defined only in spacetimes with timelike Killing vectors (Schwarzschild, asymptotically flat spacetimes). In cosmological spacetimes, photon energy is not conserved — it redshifts.

**Electromagnetic Stress-Energy Tensor**
$T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm EM} = F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu_{\ \alpha}/\mu_0 - g^{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}/(4\mu_0)$: symmetric, traceless ($T^\mu_{\ \mu,{\rm EM}} = 0$, reflecting masslessness of photons), divergence-free in vacuum. Sources the Einstein equations in electrovacuum solutions (Reissner-Nordström, Kerr-Newman).
