# Chapter 37: Further Reading and References

## Primary Sources

**Einstein, A. and Grommer, J. (1927). "Allgemeine Relativitätstheorie und Bewegungsgesetz." *Sitzungsberichte der Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 2–13.**
First attempt to prove that the geodesic equation follows from the Einstein field equations. Uses a singular-source approximation. The proof has gaps but established the program.

**Einstein, A., Infeld, L., and Hoffmann, B. (1938). "The Gravitational Equations and the Problem of Motion." *Annals of Mathematics*, 39, 65–100.**
Derives the equations of motion for point particles directly from the Einstein equations, without assuming geodesic motion. The resulting EIH (Einstein-Infeld-Hoffmann) equations are the 1PN equations of motion for N-body systems, used in solar system dynamics.

**Lense, J. and Thirring, H. (1918). "Über den Einfluß der Eigenrotation der Zentralkörper auf die Bewegung der Planeten und Monde nach der Einsteinschen Gravitationstheorie." *Physikalische Zeitschrift*, 19, 156–163.**
The Lense-Thirring effect: a rotating massive body drags the local inertial frames, causing precession of orbiting gyroscopes. English translation: *General Relativity and Gravitation*, 16, 711–750 (1984).

**Carter, B. (1968). "Global Structure of the Kerr Family of Gravitational Fields." *Physical Review*, 174, 1559–1571.**
Discovers the Carter constant — the hidden fourth integral of motion for geodesics in the Kerr spacetime. Makes Kerr geodesics completely integrable.

**Gralla, S.E. and Wald, R.M. (2008). "A rigorous derivation of gravitational self-force." *Classical and Quantum Gravity*, 25, 205009.**
Rigorous derivation of the geodesic hypothesis for small bodies: the center of mass follows a geodesic of the background metric to leading order, with systematic higher-order corrections.

**Everitt, C.W.F. et al. (2011). "Gravity Probe B: Final Results of a Space Experiment to Test General Relativity." *Physical Review Letters*, 106, 221101.**
Final results of the Gravity Probe B experiment: geodetic precession confirmed to $0.28\%$, Lense-Thirring precession confirmed to $19\%$.

## Textbooks

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973). *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman.**
Chapter 8 on geodesics; Chapter 25 on the Kerr metric and its geodesics (including Carter constant); Chapter 40 on solar system tests. Box 25.6 on the effective potential for Kerr geodesics.

**Wald, R.M. (1984). *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press.**
Chapter 3.3 on geodesics; Chapter 12 on the stationary axisymmetric solutions (Kerr metric and geodesics). Mathematical rigour throughout.

**Will, C.M. (1993). *Theory and Experiment in Gravitational Physics.* Revised ed. Cambridge University Press.**
The standard reference for experimental GR. Chapter 8 on the post-Newtonian expansion and the EIH equations; Chapter 9 on solar system tests; Chapter 10 on gravitational wave generation and the binary pulsar.

**Blanchet, L. (2014). "Gravitational Radiation from Post-Newtonian Sources and Inspiralling Compact Binaries." *Living Reviews in Relativity*, 17, 2.**
Comprehensive review of the post-Newtonian formalism for gravitational wave generation. The theoretical foundation for LIGO waveform templates. Available at livingreviews.springer.com.

## On the Geodesic Equation and Compact Binaries

**Buonanno, A. and Damour, T. (1999). "Transition from inspiral to plunge in binary black hole coalescences." *Physical Review D*, 59, 084006.**
Introduces the effective one-body (EOB) formalism — a resummation of the post-Newtonian expansion that remains accurate through merger. Used in LIGO parameter estimation.

**Peters, P.C. (1964). "Gravitational Radiation and the Motion of Two Point Masses." *Physical Review*, 136, B1224–B1232.**
Derives the orbital decay equation for a binary system emitting gravitational waves at 2.5PN order. Gives the merger time as a function of initial separation and eccentricity.
