# Chapter 37: Important Researchers

---

**Albert Einstein (1879–1955)**
The geodesic equation as the law of motion in GR is Einstein's insight: if the equivalence principle holds, then freely falling particles follow the "straightest possible" paths in spacetime. Einstein originally stated this as a postulate. The proof that it follows from the field equations came gradually, with contributions from Einstein and Grommer (1927), then from a long series of increasingly rigorous proofs culminating in Gralla and Wald (2008).

**Jacques Hadamard (1865–1963)**
French mathematician who developed the theory of well-posed problems in PDEs and the concept of "characteristics" (the propagation fronts of solutions). The geodesics of a Lorentzian metric are the bicharacteristics of the wave equation — the paths along which disturbances propagate. This deep connection between geometry and wave propagation is implicit in the fact that gravitational waves travel along null geodesics.

**Josef Lense (1890–1985) and Hans Thirring (1888–1976)**
Austrian physicists. Derived the frame-dragging effect of a rotating massive body on the orbits and gyroscopes of nearby objects (1918) — the Lense-Thirring effect. Their calculation used the weak-field, slow-rotation approximation. Thirring also calculated the "inside" problem: what is the gravitational field inside a slowly rotating massive shell? The Thirring-Lense effect (though they published separately) is the first post-Newtonian correction to Newtonian gravity that depends on angular momentum.

**William de Sitter (1872–1934)**
Dutch astronomer. Computed (1916) the geodetic precession (de Sitter precession) of a gyroscope in orbit around a massive body — the precession due to the curvature of the spatial metric. Predicted this effect for lunar orbit, as a test of GR. Confirmed by Gravity Probe B (2011). Also found the de Sitter solution of the Einstein equations (1917, a vacuum solution with $\Lambda > 0$) and contributed to the model of cosmic expansion.

**Gravity Probe B Team (W.W. Hansen Experimental Physics Laboratory, Stanford)**
Francis Everitt led the Gravity Probe B experiment from its conception in the 1960s to publication of the final results in 2011. The satellite flew four gyroscopes in polar orbit at 642 km altitude. Measured geodetic precession to $0.28\%$ and Lense-Thirring precession to $19\%$ (limited by systematic errors from electrostatic patch effects on the gyroscopes). A 50-year project — the longest experiment in NASA history.

**Luca Blanchet (1956–) and Thibault Damour (1951–)**
French physicists who developed the post-Newtonian formalism for compact binary systems to the high orders needed for LIGO data analysis. Blanchet (Institut d'Astrophysique de Paris) and Damour (Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques) are the primary architects of the PN expansion now embedded in every LIGO template. Their 3.5PN phase evolution and spin-orbit/spin-spin coupling terms enable matched-filter detection of binary inspiral signals.

**Sam Gralla (1983–) and Robert Wald (1947–)**
American physicists at the University of Chicago. Their 2008 paper "A Rigorous Derivation of Gravitational Self-Force" provided the most rigorous available proof of the geodesic hypothesis for finite-size bodies — that the center of mass of a small body follows a geodesic of the background spacetime to leading order, with systematic corrections at higher order in the mass. Their paper resolved long-standing ambiguities about the "self-force" problem (the back-reaction of a body on its own motion due to gravitational radiation).

**Brandon Carter (1942–)**
Australian-British physicist. Discovered the hidden symmetry of the Kerr metric — the Carter constant $C$, a fourth conserved quantity (beyond energy, angular momentum, and the mass-shell condition) for geodesics in Kerr spacetime (1968). This makes Kerr geodesics completely integrable — separable into four decoupled first-order ODEs. Without the Carter constant, geodesic equations in Kerr would be intractable. Carter also made foundational contributions to black hole thermodynamics (the four laws of black hole mechanics) and inflationary cosmology.
