# Unit IX: Foundations of General Relativity

---

## Unit Introduction

With the mathematical prerequisites developed in Units I–VIII, we are ready to construct general relativity.

Einstein's core insight (1915): **gravity is not a force but a manifestation of spacetime curvature**. A freely falling body — in the absence of non-gravitational forces — follows a geodesic of the spacetime metric. The metric is determined by the distribution of matter and energy through the Einstein field equations. This replaces Newton's action-at-a-distance theory of gravity with a local, geometric theory.

The foundations of GR rest on three pillars:

**The Equivalence Principle**: The local physics in a freely falling frame is that of special relativity (no gravity). This means GR reduces to SR locally, and any SR equation can be "made generally covariant" by replacing partial derivatives with covariant derivatives and the Minkowski metric with gᵤᵥ. This "minimal coupling" prescription is the bridge from SR to GR.

**General Covariance**: The laws of physics take the same form in all coordinate systems. This is not merely a mathematical convenience — it is a statement about the structure of physical law. It forces us to write all equations in tensor form, so that they transform consistently under diffeomorphisms.

**The Einstein Field Equations**: Gᵤᵥ = 8π(G/c⁴) Tᵤᵥ. The left side is the Einstein tensor — a combination of the Ricci tensor and Ricci scalar that satisfies ∇^μ Gᵤᵥ = 0 by the contracted Bianchi identity. The right side is the stress-energy tensor of matter. The equations are 10 independent second-order PDEs for the 10 independent components of gᵤᵥ. The contracted Bianchi identity ensures ∇^μ Tᵤᵥ = 0 automatically — energy-momentum is conserved as a consequence.

This unit also derives the equations of GR from the **Einstein-Hilbert action**, proves the Bianchi identities, and introduces the **initial value formulation** — the statement that GR is a deterministic theory given initial data.
