# Chapter 38: The Schwarzschild Solution

---

## Chapter Introduction

The Einstein field equations are ten coupled nonlinear partial differential equations. They are, in general, extraordinarily difficult to solve. Yet within months of Einstein's November 1915 paper — while fighting on the Eastern Front in World War I — Karl Schwarzschild found an exact solution. He sent it to Einstein from the Russian front in December 1915; Einstein replied that he "had not expected the exact solution to the problem could be formulated so simply." Schwarzschild died of a disease contracted at the front four months later, at 42.

The Schwarzschild metric is the solution for the gravitational field outside a spherically symmetric, non-rotating, uncharged mass — a star, a planet, a black hole. It is:
$$ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2GM}{rc^2}\right)c^2dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{2GM}{rc^2}\right)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)$$

Deceptively simple in appearance, this metric encodes:
- Newton's gravitational field (to lowest order)
- The exact 43-arcsecond/century perihelion precession of Mercury
- The exact deflection of light by the Sun (1.75 arcseconds)
- The exact Shapiro time delay
- The exact gravitational redshift
- The event horizon at $r = 2GM/c^2$ and the black hole spacetime
- The photon sphere at $r = 3GM/c^2$
- The innermost stable circular orbit at $r = 6GM/c^2$

And through its maximal analytic extension (the Kruskal-Szekeres manifold), a complete description of the geometry of a black hole spacetime.

The Schwarzschild metric is to GR what the Coulomb field is to electrostatics — the simplest nontrivial exact solution, the prototype for all other solutions, and the source of the most important physical predictions.

This chapter derives the Schwarzschild metric from the Einstein equations (using Birkhoff's theorem), analyzes its geometry (Schwarzschild radius, the radial and angular geometry, coordinate singularities vs. true singularities), and constructs the maximal extension (Kruskal coordinates and the Penrose diagram). Chapter 39 applies it to the classical tests of GR.
