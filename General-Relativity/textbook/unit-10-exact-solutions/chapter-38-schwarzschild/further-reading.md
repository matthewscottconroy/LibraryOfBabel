# Chapter 38: Further Reading and References

---

## Primary Sources

**Schwarzschild, K. (1916). "Über das Gravitationsfeld eines Massenpunktes nach der Einsteinschen Theorie." *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 189–196.**
The original derivation of the Schwarzschild metric, submitted from the Russian front. Available in German original and English translation.

**Droste, J. (1916). "The Field of a Single Centre in Einstein's Theory of Gravitation, and the Motion of a Particle in that Field." *Koninklijke Nederlandsche Akademie van Wetenschappen*, 19, 197–215.**
Independent derivation by Droste (also finding the geodesic equations), published in Dutch.

**Birkhoff, G.D. (1923). *Relativity and Modern Physics.* Harvard University Press.**
Contains the proof of Birkhoff's theorem. The theorem was actually proved by Jørgensen (1921) and independently by Birkhoff; Birkhoff's textbook made it widely known.

**Kruskal, M.D. (1960). "Maximal Extension of Schwarzschild Metric." *Physical Review*, 119, 1743–1745.**
The one-page paper presenting the Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates and the maximal extension. Remarkably concise; the entire paper is less than 2 pages.

**Szekeres, G. (1960). "On the Singularities of a Riemannian Manifold." *Publicationes Mathematicae Debrecen*, 7, 285–301.**
Szekeres's independent and more detailed analysis of the maximal Schwarzschild extension.

**Penrose, R. (1963). "Asymptotic Properties of Fields and Space-Times." *Physical Review Letters*, 10, 66–68.**
Introduction of the conformal compactification and the concept of null infinity $\mathscr{I}^\pm$. The origin of Penrose diagrams.

## Textbooks

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973). *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman.**
Chapters 31–34 on the Schwarzschild metric, orbits, and the maximal extension. Box 31.2 on the derivation of the Schwarzschild metric; Box 32.1 on Kruskal coordinates; Chapter 34 on black holes.

**Wald, R.M. (1984). *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press.**
Chapter 6.4 on the Schwarzschild solution; Chapter 12 on black holes and global structure. The most mathematically rigorous treatment.

**Chandrasekhar, S. (1983). *The Mathematical Theory of Black Holes.* Oxford University Press.**
The definitive mathematical treatment of the Schwarzschild and Kerr metrics and their perturbation theory. Chapter 2–5 on the Schwarzschild metric. A classic but demanding text.

**Hartle, J.B. (2003). *Gravity: An Introduction to Einstein's General Relativity.* Addison-Wesley.**
Chapters 9–11 on the Schwarzschild geometry. A pedagogically excellent introductory treatment, developing the physics before the full mathematical formalism.
