# Chapter 38: Important Researchers

---

**Karl Schwarzschild (1873–1916)**
German astronomer and mathematician. Found the first exact solution of Einstein's field equations (December 1915) — the Schwarzschild metric — while serving as an artillery officer on the Russian front during World War I. He developed the solution by assuming spherical symmetry and showed that the unique static, asymptotically flat vacuum solution has the form bearing his name. Sent the paper to Einstein from the front; Einstein replied that he had not expected the problem to be solved so simply. Schwarzschild died in May 1916 of a disease (pemphigus, an autoimmune condition) contracted at the front, four months after his discovery, at age 42. His second paper (also from the front) solved the interior equations for a uniform-density star. Without living to develop the implications, Schwarzschild could not know that his solution described black holes.

**Johannes Droste (1886–1963)**
Dutch physicist, student of Lorentz. Found essentially the same solution as Schwarzschild, independently, in early 1916 — and also derived the geodesic equations and identified the orbits and light deflection. His work was published later and is less well known, but he has legitimate priority in several aspects of the Schwarzschild analysis.

**David Hilbert (1862–1943)**
Hilbert was the first to analyze the coordinate singularity at $r = r_s$ rigorously (1917), and was misled into believing it represented a genuine singularity where the manifold ended. This misconception (that $r = r_s$ was a "real" singularity) persisted in the literature for decades.

**Arthur Stanley Eddington (1882–1944)**
British astrophysicist. Led the 1919 solar eclipse expedition to Principe Island (off West Africa) and Sobral (Brazil) to measure the deflection of starlight by the Sun — the first major observational test of GR. His announcement at the joint meeting of the Royal Society and Royal Astronomical Society in November 1919 made Einstein a world celebrity. Eddington was also among the first to study the mathematical structure of the Schwarzschild solution and proposed improved coordinates (Eddington-Finkelstein, 1924). His book *The Mathematical Theory of Relativity* (1923) was the first systematic English-language presentation of GR.

**Georges Lemaître (1894–1966)**
Belgian physicist and priest. Showed in 1933 that the Schwarzschild "singularity" at $r = r_s$ was a coordinate artifact and proposed regular coordinates that crossed the horizon smoothly. His analysis was essentially the ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein picture, though the significance was not appreciated at the time.

**Martin Kruskal (1925–2006) and György Szekeres (1921–2005)**
American mathematician Kruskal and Hungarian-Australian mathematician Szekeres independently found the maximal extension of the Schwarzschild spacetime in 1960 (Kruskal's result was communicated in a famous one-page paper in *Physical Review*; Szekeres published a longer analysis). Their coordinates revealed the full four-region structure: exterior, black hole interior, white hole interior, and second exterior. The Kruskal-Szekeres diagram is now the standard tool for understanding the global structure of the Schwarzschild spacetime.

**Roger Penrose (1931–)**
British mathematician and physicist. Developed the conformal compactification of spacetime (the Penrose diagram), which allows the global causal structure of any spacetime — including the Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes — to be represented in a finite diagram. Also proved the first singularity theorem (1965) and developed twistor theory. Nobel Prize in Physics 2020.

**Brandon Carter (1942–)**
Australian-British physicist. Made fundamental contributions to the global structure of black hole spacetimes, including the complete analysis of the Kerr geodesics, the discovery of the Carter constant, and the derivation of the laws of black hole mechanics. His 1973 paper with Bardeen, Carter, and Hawking on the four laws of black hole mechanics is a landmark.
