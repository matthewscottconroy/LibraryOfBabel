# Chapter 39: Classical Tests of General Relativity

---

## Chapter Introduction

A physical theory earns its authority not from its mathematical elegance alone but from its agreement with observation. General relativity in November 1915 had an immediate success: the 43 arcseconds per century anomaly in Mercury's perihelion precession — which had mystified astronomers for 60 years and prompted the hypothetical planet "Vulcan" — was explained exactly by the geodesic equations of the Schwarzschild metric. Einstein reported that his heart "fluttered" when he saw the number come out right.

The classical tests of GR are:

1. **Perihelion precession** of Mercury (and other planets, binary pulsars)
2. **Deflection of light** by the Sun (and other massive bodies)
3. **Gravitational redshift** of light and slowing of clocks in a gravitational field
4. **Shapiro time delay** (the "fourth test," Shapiro 1964)

All four are derived from geodesic motion in the Schwarzschild metric. Each is an instance of the same mathematical structure: a post-Newtonian correction to motion in the $1/r$ potential, with the GR correction proportional to $GM/(rc^2) \sim r_s/r$.

Modern measurements have added many more precision tests, and GR has passed all of them. But the four classical tests remain the clearest and most pedagogically transparent demonstrations of GR in action.
