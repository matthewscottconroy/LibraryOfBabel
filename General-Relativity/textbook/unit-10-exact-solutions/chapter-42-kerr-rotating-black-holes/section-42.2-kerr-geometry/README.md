# Section 42.2: The Geometry of the Kerr Spacetime

---

## Section Introduction

The Kerr spacetime has a richly layered geometry, made possible by rotation. Four distinct regions and surfaces characterize the Kerr exterior and interior: the **ergosphere**, the **outer event horizon**, the **inner Cauchy horizon**, and the **ring singularity**. Understanding each requires understanding a different physical effect — energy extraction from rotation, causal disconnection, failure of determinism, and curvature divergence.

The **ergosphere** is the region between the **static limit surface** $r_{\rm sl}(\theta) = r_s/2 + \sqrt{(r_s/2)^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}$ and the outer event horizon $r_+$. Inside the ergosphere, the Killing vector $\partial/\partial t$ (which is timelike outside the ergosphere) becomes spacelike: no observer can remain at rest relative to distant stars. All observers inside the ergosphere are dragged in the direction of rotation by the extreme frame dragging. But the ergosphere is outside the event horizon — observers can enter and exit it.

This makes possible the **Penrose process**: a particle entering the ergosphere can split into two, with one fragment falling into the black hole on a trajectory with negative energy (as measured at infinity), while the other escapes with energy greater than the original particle had. The net effect is extraction of rotational energy from the black hole. The spinning black hole loses angular momentum, and its area decreases by at most... zero. The area theorem (second law) is not violated: the area of the horizon is non-decreasing, which bounds how much energy can be extracted. The **maximum extractable energy** (via all Penrose processes) is $(1 - 1/\sqrt{2})Mc^2 \approx 29\%$ of the rest mass for an extremal Kerr black hole.

The **geodesics** of Kerr spacetime are completely integrable (Carter 1968): there are four constants of motion — energy $E$, angular momentum $L_z$, Carter constant $Q$, and rest mass $m$ — which together determine the geodesic. The Carter constant is a "hidden symmetry" not associated with any obvious Killing vector; it arises from a Killing tensor. Complete integrability means the geodesic equations can be separated and reduced to quadratures — a remarkable property that has no analogue in non-spherically-symmetric Newtonian systems.
