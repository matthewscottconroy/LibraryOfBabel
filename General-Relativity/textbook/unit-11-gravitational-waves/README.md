# Unit XI: Gravitational Waves

---

## Unit Introduction

Gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime curvature, propagating at the speed of light. Predicted by Einstein in 1916, they were directly detected for the first time on September 14, 2015, by the LIGO collaboration — a binary black hole merger at a distance of ~1.3 billion light years. This detection inaugurated **gravitational wave astronomy**: a new observational window on the universe, complementary to electromagnetic observations, probing the most extreme objects and events in the cosmos.

Gravitational waves emerge from the linearized Einstein equations. In the transverse-traceless gauge, the metric perturbation hᵤᵥ satisfies the wave equation □h̄ᵤᵥ = −16π Tᵤᵥ. Vacuum solutions are planar gravitational waves with two polarization modes: the "plus" polarization (h₊) and the "cross" polarization (h×). These modes stretch and compress space alternately, and the effect is measured by laser interferometers like LIGO and Virgo.

The power radiated in gravitational waves by a compact binary system is given by the **quadrupole formula** (first derived by Einstein, 1918):

$$P = -\frac{G}{5c^5} \langle \dddot{Q}_{ij} \dddot{Q}^{ij} \rangle$$

where Q_{ij} is the quadrupole moment tensor of the system. This formula predicts orbital decay — the binary loses energy and spirals inward. The Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar (1974) showed exactly this orbital decay, matching GR's prediction to better than 0.2%, providing the first indirect evidence for gravitational waves. [Hulse, R.A. and Taylor, J.H. (1975). *Astrophysical Journal Letters*, 195, L51.]
