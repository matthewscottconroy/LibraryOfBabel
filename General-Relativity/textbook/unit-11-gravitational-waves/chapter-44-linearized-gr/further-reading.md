# Chapter 44: Further Reading and References

---

## Primary Sources

**Einstein, A. (1916). "Näherungsweise Integration der Feldgleichungen der Gravitation." *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 688–696.**
Einstein's first derivation of gravitational waves from the linearized field equations. He showed that accelerating masses radiate at the speed of light. Available in English translation in the Collected Papers of Albert Einstein.

**Einstein, A. (1918). "Über Gravitationswellen." *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 154–167.**
The corrected 1918 paper, which fixed an error in the 1916 derivation and derived the quadrupole formula for the first time. The original derivation contained a factor-of-2 error that was corrected here.

**Bondi, H., van der Burg, M.G.J., and Metzner, A.W.K. (1962). "Gravitational Waves in General Relativity, VII. Waves from Axi-Symmetric Isolated Systems." *Proceedings of the Royal Society of London A*, 269, 21.**
Proved that gravitational waves carry genuine energy (using the "Bondi news" formalism) and showed the total mass of an isolated system decreases as it radiates. This resolved the decades-long controversy about whether GW energy is "real."

**Sachs, R.K. (1962). "Gravitational Waves in General Relativity, VIII. Waves in Asymptotically Flat Space-Time." *Proceedings of the Royal Society of London A*, 270, 103.**
Completed the Bondi program and identified the "Bondi-Metzner-Sachs" (BMS) symmetry group at null infinity. Recent decades have revealed deep connections between BMS symmetry, gravitational wave memory, and soft theorems in quantum gravity.

**Peters, P.C. and Mathews, J. (1963). "Gravitational Radiation from Point Masses in a Keplerian Orbit." *Physical Review*, 131, 435.**
The first computation of the gravitational wave spectrum from an elliptical Keplerian orbit. Derived the power formula $P = (32/5)(G^4/c^5)m_1^2 m_2^2(m_1+m_2)/a^5$ for circular orbits.

**Peters, P.C. (1964). "Gravitational Radiation and the Motion of Two Point Masses." *Physical Review*, 136, B1224.**
Derived the orbital decay equations including eccentricity corrections — the Peters equations. The formula for $\dot{P}_b$ that was later confirmed by the Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar.

**Taylor, J.H. and Weisberg, J.M. (1989). "Further Experimental Tests of Relativistic Gravity Using the Binary Pulsar PSR 1913+16." *Astrophysical Journal*, 345, 434.**
The definitive confirmation of GR's quadrupole formula using 15 years of timing data from PSR B1913+16. Agreement to 0.2%.

**Isaacson, R.A. (1968). "Gravitational Radiation in the Limit of High Frequency, I and II." *Physical Review*, 166, 1263 and 1272.**
Derived the effective stress-energy tensor for gravitational waves (the Isaacson tensor) that gives a covariant meaning to the energy carried by GWs. This resolved the question of energy localization for gravitational waves.

## Historical and Conceptual

**Feynman, R.P. (1957). "Chapel Hill Conference notes."**
The "sticky bead" argument for GW energy, presented informally at the 1957 Chapel Hill Conference on the Role of Gravitation in Physics. Feynman's argument convinced many physicists that gravitational waves carry real energy.

**Kennefick, D. (2007). *Traveling at the Speed of Thought: Einstein and the Quest for Gravitational Waves.* Princeton University Press.**
An excellent historical account of the gravitational wave controversy from Einstein's 1916 derivation through Weber's bar detectors and the founding of LIGO. Essential context for understanding the sociology of the field.

## Textbooks

**Misner, C.W., Thorne, K.S., and Wheeler, J.A. (1973). *Gravitation.* W.H. Freeman.**
Chapters 35–37 on linearized theory, gravitational waves, and the generation of GWs. Box 35.1 on the sticky bead argument; Chapter 36 on the quadrupole formula. The standard comprehensive reference.

**Wald, R.M. (1984). *General Relativity.* University of Chicago Press.**
Chapter 4 covers the linearized theory with exceptional mathematical clarity; the treatment of gauge transformations and degrees of freedom is the cleanest available.

**Maggiore, M. (2007). *Gravitational Waves, Vol. 1: Theory and Experiments.* Oxford University Press.**
Chapters 1–5: derivation of linearized GR, plane waves, TT gauge, energy-momentum tensor, generation of GWs. The most thorough modern textbook on the subject.

**Flanagan, É.É. and Hughes, S.A. (2005). "Measuring gravitational waves from binary black hole coalescences." *Physical Review D*, 57, 4566 and 4590.**
Review articles covering GW measurement theory and parameter estimation. Excellent technical reference.

**Carroll, S.M. (2004). *Spacetime and Geometry.* Addison-Wesley.**
Chapter 7 on linearized GR and gravitational waves. Accessible treatment with clear exposition of the TT gauge and gauge freedom.

**Schutz, B.F. (2009). *A First Course in General Relativity.* 2nd edition. Cambridge University Press.**
Chapter 9 on gravitational waves: clear pedagogical treatment of the linearization, plane waves, and interaction with test masses. Excellent for first-time readers.
