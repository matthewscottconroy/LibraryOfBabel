## Unit Introduction

Cosmology is the study of the universe as a whole: its large-scale structure, history, and ultimate fate. Modern cosmology rests on three observational pillars:

1. **The expansion of the universe** (Hubble 1929): galaxies recede from us at velocities proportional to their distances, v = H₀ d. This implies the universe began in a hot, dense state — the Big Bang.
2. **The cosmic microwave background (CMB)** (Penzias-Wilson 1965): thermal radiation at T ≈ 2.725 K, a relic of the epoch of recombination (z ≈ 1100), when the universe cooled enough for hydrogen atoms to form. The slight temperature fluctuations (ΔT/T ~ 10⁻⁵) encode the initial conditions for structure formation.
3. **Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN)**: the abundances of light elements (H, D, He, Li) produced in the first three minutes match theoretical predictions from the Standard Model of particle physics at the 1% level.

The mathematical framework is the **FLRW cosmology** with the Friedmann equations:

$$H^2 = \left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \frac{kc^2}{a^2} + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}$$

$$\frac{\ddot{a}}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}\left(\rho + \frac{3p}{c^2}\right) + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}$$

where a(t) is the scale factor, ρ the energy density, p the pressure, k the spatial curvature parameter, and Λ the cosmological constant.

The **discovery of accelerating expansion** (Riess et al. 1998; Perlmutter et al. 1999) showed that the universe is dominated by **dark energy** — currently modeled as a positive cosmological constant Λ. Combined with dark matter (~27% of the energy budget) and ordinary baryonic matter (~5%), the ΛCDM ("Lambda Cold Dark Matter") model describes observational data at all scales from CMB to large-scale structure.
