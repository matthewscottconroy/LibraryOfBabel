# Unit XIII: Advanced Formulations of General Relativity

---

## Unit Introduction

The Einstein field equations can be formulated in many ways. The standard tensor formulation is powerful but hides important structure: the action principle that generates the equations, the Hamiltonian formulation that enables canonical quantization, the spinor formalism that connects GR to quantum mechanics most naturally, and the conformal diagrams that encode the global causal structure of spacetime.

This unit develops these alternative formulations — not as redundant representations of the same physics, but as frameworks that illuminate different aspects of gravitational theory.

The **ADM (Arnowitt-Deser-Misner) 3+1 formulation** splits spacetime into spatial slices evolving in time, with a lapse function and shift vector encoding the gauge freedom. This is the foundation of numerical relativity — the only way to compute the gravitational waveforms from binary mergers that matched GW150914. The ADM Hamiltonian reveals that GR has no independent dynamics in the time coordinate, only constraint equations (the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints), leading to the "problem of time" in quantum gravity.

The **action principle** and the Einstein-Hilbert action $S_{\rm EH} = c^4/(16\pi G)\int R\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ generate the field equations from a variational principle, connect GR to gauge theory, and provide the framework for modified gravity theories (adding higher-curvature terms, scalar fields, etc.). The boundary terms (Gibbons-Hawking-York) are essential for a well-posed variational problem.

**Penrose diagrams** (conformal compactification) represent infinite spacetimes in finite diagrams, revealing the global causal structure, the locations of horizons, and the nature of spacetime singularities. They are indispensable for discussing black hole physics, cosmological horizons, and gravitational waves.

The **Newman-Penrose spinor formalism** uses complex null tetrads and spinors to simplify the field equations for spacetimes with special symmetries — particularly the Kerr black hole and gravitational waves. It led to the Petrov classification of gravitational fields and is the natural language for black hole perturbation theory.
