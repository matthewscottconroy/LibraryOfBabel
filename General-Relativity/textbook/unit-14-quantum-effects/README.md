# Unit XIV: Quantum Effects in Curved Spacetime

---

## Unit Introduction

Quantum mechanics and general relativity are the two pillars of 20th-century physics. Each is extraordinarily successful in its domain; a full quantum theory of gravity combining them remains the deepest open problem in theoretical physics. But even without a complete quantum gravity theory, we can study **quantum field theory in curved spacetime** — a semiclassical approximation in which the matter fields are treated quantum mechanically but the metric is treated as a fixed classical background.

The central results of this framework are:

**The Unruh Effect** (Unruh 1976): A uniformly accelerating observer in flat Minkowski spacetime observes a thermal bath of particles at temperature T_U = ℏa/(2πck_B), where a is the proper acceleration. Inertial observers see the same state as a vacuum; the accelerated observer sees it as a thermal state. This illustrates that "particle content" is observer-dependent — there is no absolute notion of the vacuum in quantum field theory.

**Hawking Radiation** (Hawking 1974): A black hole radiates as a blackbody at temperature T_H = ℏc³/(8πGMk_B). The radiation is thermal, with spectrum determined entirely by the mass M (and charge and angular momentum, for general black holes). This result has profound implications: black holes are not truly black; they evaporate over time (extremely slowly for astrophysical black holes); and the question of whether information is preserved during evaporation (the **black hole information paradox**) remains unresolved.

Both effects arise from the same mathematical structure: **Bogoliubov transformations** — the relationship between the particle definitions of two different observers, one inertial and one accelerated (or one asymptotically flat and one inside a collapsing star). The Bogoliubov coefficients mix positive and negative frequency modes, producing particle creation from the vacuum.

The **Casimir effect** — a force between closely spaced neutral conducting plates arising from vacuum fluctuations — is another manifestation of quantum effects in non-trivial geometries and has been precisely measured in the laboratory.
