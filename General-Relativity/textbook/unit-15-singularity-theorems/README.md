# Unit XV: Singularity Theorems and Global Structure

---

## Unit Introduction

One of the most striking predictions of GR is that singularities are inevitable under generic conditions. Penrose (1965) proved that any spacetime containing a **trapped surface** (a closed surface from which light cannot escape outward) must be **geodesically incomplete** — there are geodesics that cannot be extended to infinite affine parameter. This is the mathematical definition of a singularity in GR. Hawking (1967) proved the analogous result for cosmological singularities: any expanding universe satisfying energy conditions must have been geodesically incomplete in the past — the Big Bang is inevitable.

These **singularity theorems** use:
- **Raychaudhuri's equation**: the focusing equation for a congruence of geodesics. It shows that gravity (satisfying the energy conditions) always causes geodesics to converge — and convergence leads to caustic formation (crossing of geodesics), which signals geodesic incompleteness.
- **The energy conditions**: weak, dominant, strong, null. These constrain the stress-energy tensor to satisfy physically reasonable inequalities (positive energy, dominant energy flow, etc.). The strong energy condition guarantees gravitational focusing.
- **Global hyperbolicity**: the condition that spacetime has well-behaved causal structure — no closed timelike curves, a proper notion of "the future."
- **Cauchy surfaces and the Cauchy development**: the initial value problem for GR requires a globally hyperbolic spacetime.

The global structure of spacetime — its causal structure, topology, and boundary — is studied using **Penrose diagrams** (conformal diagrams): compact 2D representations of spacetime that preserve causal structure while compressing infinitely extended regions.
