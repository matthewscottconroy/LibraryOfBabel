# Chapter 59: Trapped Surfaces and Singularity Theorems

## Chapter Introduction

In 1965, Roger Penrose proved that black holes are not merely solutions to the Schwarzschild equations — they are generic. Any spacetime that contains a trapped surface and satisfies the null energy condition must be geodesically incomplete. This was the first singularity theorem: a rigorous mathematical proof that singularities are inescapable under physically reasonable conditions.

Penrose's theorem shocked the physics community. It implied that Einstein's equations, under conditions realized in nature (collapsing stars, the Big Bang), inevitably lead to breakdown — to regions where the equations no longer apply. The question of what actually happens at these singularities requires physics beyond GR: quantum gravity.

The proof is elegant in its strategy: rather than computing the full metric (which is impossible generically), Penrose used global topological methods. The key insight is that if geodesics are initially converging (as guaranteed by the energy conditions applied to trapped surfaces) and this convergence cannot be halted by the structure of the spacetime, then geodesics must eventually focus — and focused geodesics signal a singularity.

---

## Null Geodesic Congruences and the Raychaudhuri Equation

A **congruence** is a family of non-intersecting geodesics filling a region of spacetime. For null geodesics with tangent vector $l^\mu$ ($l^\mu l_\mu = 0$, $l^\mu\nabla_\mu l^\nu = 0$), define:

**Expansion** $\theta$: measures the rate of change of the cross-sectional area $A$ of a beam of geodesics:
$$\theta = \nabla_\mu l^\mu$$

A positive $\theta$ means the geodesics are spreading (diverging); negative $\theta$ means converging.

**Shear** $\hat{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}$: the traceless symmetric part of the transverse gradient of $l^\mu$ — measures the distortion of the cross-section.

**Twist** $\hat{\omega}_{\mu\nu}$: the antisymmetric part — measures rotation of the congruence.

For a hypersurface-orthogonal null congruence (twist-free, as for our purposes): the **Raychaudhuri equation**:
$$\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = -\frac{1}{2}\theta^2 - \hat{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}\hat{\sigma}^{\mu\nu} + \hat{\omega}_{\mu\nu}\hat{\omega}^{\mu\nu} - R_{\mu\nu}l^\mu l^\nu$$

For twist-free null congruences: $\hat{\omega} = 0$. Since $\hat{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}\hat{\sigma}^{\mu\nu} \geq 0$ and $\theta^2 \geq 0$:
$$\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} \leq -R_{\mu\nu}l^\mu l^\nu$$

**Focusing condition**: If $R_{\mu\nu}l^\mu l^\nu \geq 0$ (the **null energy condition** applied via Einstein's equations: NEC says $T_{\mu\nu}l^\mu l^\nu \geq 0$, which with $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$ implies $R_{\mu\nu}l^\mu l^\nu \geq 0$), then:
$$\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} \leq -\frac{1}{2}\theta^2$$

If $\theta_0 < 0$ (converging at some point), this ODE gives:
$$\theta^{-1}(\lambda) \geq \theta_0^{-1} + \frac{\lambda}{2}$$

So $\theta \to -\infty$ (caustic formation) within affine parameter $\lambda \leq 2/|\theta_0|$. This is the **focusing theorem**: converging null geodesics focus in finite affine parameter under the NEC.

## Trapped Surfaces

**Definition**: A **trapped surface** is a compact, orientable, spacelike 2-surface $S$ such that both families of null geodesics orthogonal to $S$ (inward and outward) have negative expansion: $\theta_+ < 0$ and $\theta_- < 0$.

In flat Minkowski spacetime, a sphere $S^2$ has $\theta_+ > 0$ (outgoing light rays spread) and $\theta_- < 0$ (ingoing light rays converge). In Schwarzschild, at $r < r_s$: a 2-sphere has $\theta_+ < 0$ and $\theta_- < 0$ — both families of null geodesics converge. The 2-sphere at $r < r_s$ is a trapped surface.

**Marginal surface**: At $r = r_s$ (the horizon), $\theta_+ = 0$ (marginally trapped). The horizon is the boundary of the trapped region.

**Apparent horizon**: The outermost marginal trapped surface (where $\theta_+ = 0$). In spherical symmetry, this coincides with the event horizon in stationary spacetimes. In dynamical spacetimes (binary mergers), the apparent horizon forms before the event horizon and is detectable in numerical relativity.

## The Penrose Singularity Theorem (1965)

**Theorem** (Penrose 1965): Let $(M, g)$ be a spacetime satisfying:
1. $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu \geq 0$ for all null vectors $k^\mu$ (NEC)
2. $(M, g)$ is globally hyperbolic
3. There exists a non-compact Cauchy surface $\Sigma$
4. There exists a trapped surface $S$ in $(M, g)$

Then $(M, g)$ is future null geodesically incomplete — there exists a null geodesic that cannot be extended to arbitrarily large affine parameter.

**Proof sketch**: 
1. The future of the trapped surface, $J^+(S)$, has a boundary $\partial J^+(S)$ which (if $M$ is complete) would be a smooth achronal null hypersurface generated by null geodesics orthogonal to $S$.

2. By the Raychaudhuri equation + NEC, these null geodesics focus within finite affine parameter $\lambda_{\rm max} \leq 2/|\theta_0|$.

3. Beyond the caustic, the generators leave $\partial J^+(S)$ — they are no longer on the future boundary of $J^+(S)$.

4. In a globally hyperbolic spacetime, the Cauchy surface $\Sigma$ must intersect $\partial J^+(S)$. For $\Sigma$ non-compact and $S$ compact, a topological argument shows $\partial J^+(S)$ must intersect $\Sigma$ and be non-compact.

5. But the generators cannot extend beyond the caustic while remaining on $\partial J^+(S)$. Therefore, some generators must terminate — they are incomplete null geodesics. $\square$

**Physical interpretation**: Inside the horizon ($r < r_s$), trapped surfaces exist. The NEC is satisfied by all known classical matter. Therefore, the classical spacetime must be geodesically incomplete — there is a singularity.

## The Hawking-Penrose Theorem (1970)

The Penrose theorem applies to black holes (future singularities). Hawking proved analogous theorems for cosmological singularities (past incomplete geodesics — the Big Bang). The most general result:

**Theorem** (Hawking-Penrose 1970): Let $(M, g)$ be a spacetime satisfying:
1. $R_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu \geq 0$ for all causal vectors $V^\mu$ (strong + null energy conditions)
2. Every complete causal geodesic contains a point where $V^{[\mu}R_{\nu]\rho[\sigma\tau]}V^{\rho}V^{\tau} \neq 0$ (generic condition: every geodesic is deflected)
3. There are no closed timelike curves
4. At least one of: (a) compact Cauchy surface, (b) trapped surface, (c) point $p$ with convergence of all null geodesics through $p$

Then $(M, g)$ is causal geodesically incomplete — either past or future incomplete.

**Cosmological implication**: An expanding universe satisfying the SEC and the generic condition must have been geodesically incomplete in the past — the Big Bang singularity is inevitable.

However: the energy conditions (especially SEC) are violated by dark energy/cosmological constant and by quantum fields (Casimir effect, Hawking radiation). This is why inflation can "bounce" the universe through a singularity in some models, and why quantum gravity may resolve the Big Bang singularity.

## The Cosmic Censorship Conjecture

The singularity theorems prove singularities exist but say nothing about whether they are hidden inside horizons (and thus causally disconnected from observers) or naked (visible to distant observers).

**Penrose's Cosmic Censorship Conjecture** (1969): *All singularities formed from physically reasonable initial data are hidden inside event horizons — there are no naked singularities visible to distant observers.*

This is the **weak cosmic censorship conjecture** (WCCC). It is not a theorem. It is not even precisely stated — "physically reasonable initial data" must be made precise. Counterexamples exist for special initial conditions (like infinitely fine-tuned critical collapse at the threshold between black hole formation and dispersal — Choptuik criticality), but these are measure-zero.

The **strong cosmic censorship conjecture** (SCCC): *Spacetime is globally hyperbolic — there are no Cauchy horizons* (beyond which evolution is non-unique). This would rule out the inner horizon of Kerr and Reissner-Nordström black holes.

SCCC is also unproven. Recent work (Dafermos-Luk 2017) showed that the Cauchy horizon of a Kerr black hole is stable under small perturbations in the $C^0$ topology, but unstable (blows up) in the $C^1$ topology — suggesting that SCCC may hold in a weaker sense than originally conjectured.

## Penrose Diagrams and the Structure of Singularities

Penrose diagrams represent the complete causal structure of spacetime in a finite diagram. The conformal compactification maps the infinite spacetime to a compact region while preserving the causal structure (null geodesics remain at $45°$).

**Schwarzschild**: The singularity at $r = 0$ is a spacelike surface in the future — it lies entirely in the future of any observer who falls past the horizon. An infalling observer's entire future lies in the region $r < r_s$, and the singularity is inevitable.

**Reissner-Nordström** (charged BH, $Q \neq 0$): The singularity is timelike rather than spacelike (similar for Kerr). An infalling observer can, in principle, avoid it. But the inner horizon ($r_-$) is a Cauchy horizon — evolution beyond it is not uniquely determined by initial data. Strong cosmic censorship predicts the inner horizon is unstable.

**Cosmological singularity**: The Big Bang is a spacelike singularity in the past — no observer could have come from before it. Future singularities (Big Crunch for $k = +1$, $\Lambda = 0$) are also spacelike.

## Important Figures

**Roger Penrose (born 1931)**: Proved the first singularity theorem (1965), developed conformal diagrams for spacetime causal structure, formulated the cosmic censorship conjectures, and invented the spinor/twistor formalism. Nobel Prize in Physics 2020 "for the discovery that black hole formation is a robust prediction of the general theory of relativity."

**Stephen Hawking (1942–2018)**: Extended Penrose's methods to cosmological singularities (1967) and proved (with Penrose, 1970) the most general singularity theorem. Also proved the area theorem (black holes don't shrink classically) and the four laws of black hole mechanics.

**Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar (1910–1995)**: Proved in 1930–35 that white dwarfs have a maximum mass ($\sim 1.4 M_\odot$) — the first theoretical prediction that stellar collapse must proceed beyond any stable degenerate star, leading to the singularity theorems. Nobel Prize 1983.

**Robert Geroch (born 1942)**: Made foundational contributions to global GR — the topology of spacetime, singularities, and the definition of global hyperbolicity.

**Thought Experiment T59.1.** *What lies beyond a singularity?*

The singularity theorems prove that classical GR breaks down. But "breakdown" is different from "end" — the singularity is where our classical description fails, not necessarily where physics stops.

Consider two views:
1. The singularity is a physical event — a real boundary of spacetime. Beyond it, nothing exists. Time and space simply end.
2. The singularity is a symptom of the failure of classical GR. Quantum gravity resolves the singularity and replaces it with a quantum region. The universe continues.

In loop quantum cosmology (LQC), the Big Bang singularity is resolved into a "quantum bounce" — the universe contracts, reaches a minimum density ($\rho_{\rm Pl}$), and bounces. In string theory, some singularities are resolved by winding modes and D-branes.

What would it look like, observationally, for the Big Bang singularity to be replaced by a quantum bounce? Could any observable in the CMB be sensitive to pre-bounce physics?

**Thought Experiment T59.2.** *Cosmic censorship as a physical principle.*

Naked singularities — singularities not hidden inside horizons — would allow regions of "infinite curvature" to be visible to distant observers. Such regions are unpredictable: they cannot be modeled by any known physics, and they could in principle emit arbitrary radiation.

Is cosmic censorship a fundamental principle or a property of GR that might fail in certain situations? The Choptuik critical collapse suggests that at the exact threshold between black hole formation and dispersal, a naked singularity forms briefly. Is this a counterexample to WCCC, or a measure-zero exception that doesn't count?

What would the existence of a naked singularity imply for physics? Would it destroy the predictability of spacetime? Would it have observable consequences?
