# Unit XVI: Frontiers of General Relativity and Quantum Gravity

---

## Unit Introduction

This final unit surveys the frontier — the open problems and active research directions that will define the next generation of physics. We have come a long way: from propositional logic and the ε-δ definition of a limit, through calculus, linear algebra, differential geometry, and classical mechanics, to the Einstein field equations and their solutions. Now we stand at the edge of what is known.

**Numerical Relativity**: Solving Einstein's equations on a computer for the most dynamical situations — binary black hole mergers, neutron star collisions, supernova core collapse. The first successful numerical binary black hole merger simulation (Pretorius 2005) was a breakthrough that made possible the theoretical templates needed to interpret LIGO observations.

**Post-Newtonian and Post-Minkowskian Approximations**: Systematic expansions in v/c and G respectively, used for precision tests of GR and for computing gravitational wave templates for compact binary inspiral. Current theoretical work extends these to high post-Newtonian order using techniques from quantum field theory (e.g., NRGR: non-relativistic GR as an effective field theory).

**The Information Paradox and Black Hole Entropy**: Hawking's calculation shows that black holes evaporate thermally, apparently destroying information. But quantum mechanics requires information conservation (unitarity). The resolution — if one exists — likely requires a quantum theory of gravity. Recent work (Page time, island formula, entanglement entropy of Hawking radiation) has made progress but the paradox is not fully resolved.

**Loop Quantum Gravity (LQG)**: A background-independent approach to quantizing GR. Space is quantized in units of the Planck length; the kinematic Hilbert space is spanned by spin network states. Spin foam models give a covariant path integral formulation. LQG predicts a discrete spectrum for the area and volume operators.

**String Theory and the AdS/CFT Correspondence**: String theory embeds GR into a consistent quantum theory in 10 or 11 spacetime dimensions. The AdS/CFT correspondence (Maldacena 1997) equates a gravitational theory on Anti-de Sitter space with a conformal field theory on its boundary — a duality between gravity and non-gravitational physics. It has produced deep insights into black hole entropy, the information paradox, and strongly coupled field theories.

**Gravitational Wave Astronomy**: LIGO/Virgo have detected ~90+ events (binary black holes, neutron star mergers) as of 2023. LISA (Laser Interferometer Space Antenna) will detect massive black hole mergers, extreme mass ratio inspirals, and possibly a stochastic gravitational wave background. Pulsar timing arrays have recently found evidence for a gravitational wave background.
