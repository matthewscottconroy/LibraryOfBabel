# References: Logic and Proof

## Primary Sources and Core Texts

**Aristotle. *Prior Analytics*. ca. 350 BCE.**
The original source of syllogistic logic. Book I catalogs valid syllogistic forms; Book II addresses probability, necessity, and the theory of demonstration. Loeb Classical Library edition (1938) provides facing Greek-English text. Read it to understand what Frege was reacting against — and to be surprised by how much Aristotle got right.

**Frege, G. *Begriffsschrift, eine der arithmetischen nachgebildete Formelsprache des reinen Denkens*. Halle: Louis Nebert, 1879.**
Translated by S. Bauer-Mengelberg in J. van Heijenoort, ed., *From Frege to Gödel* (Harvard, 1967). The founding document of modern logic. Frege introduces quantifiers, predicate abstraction, and formal inference rules. The two-dimensional notation is eccentric but the ideas are revolutionary. Essential reading for anyone who wants to understand where modern logic comes from.

**Gentzen, G. "Untersuchungen über das logische Schließen." *Mathematische Zeitschrift* 39 (1935): 176–210, 405–431.**
Translated as "Investigations into Logical Deduction" in M. E. Szabo, ed., *The Collected Papers of Gerhard Gentzen* (North-Holland, 1969). The founding document of natural deduction and the sequent calculus. Gentzen proves cut-elimination (the Hauptsatz) and uses it to prove the consistency of propositional logic. The introduction-elimination duality for each connective is defined here for the first time.

**Gödel, K. "Die Vollständigkeit der Axiome des logischen Funktionenkalküls." *Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik* 37 (1930): 349–360.**
Translated in van Heijenoort (1967). The completeness theorem: every valid first-order formula is provable in Hilbert's calculus. The proof introduces the Henkin model construction. This paper — Gödel's doctoral dissertation — is one of the most important papers in the history of logic, and it is readable.

**Gödel, K. "Über formal unentscheidbare Sätze der *Principia Mathematica* und verwandter Systeme I." *Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik* 38 (1931): 173–198.**
Translated in van Heijenoort (1967). The incompleteness theorems. This is the paper that ended Hilbert's program as originally conceived. Read the introduction and the informal overview even if the technical sections require background.

**Prawitz, D. *Natural Deduction: A Proof-Theoretical Study*. Stockholm: Almqvist & Wiksell, 1965. Reprinted by Dover, 2006.**
The definitive treatment of natural deduction: normalization theorem, proof-theoretic semantics, and the connections to category theory. Prawitz is careful and precise. The Dover reprint is affordable and widely available.

**Martin-Löf, P. "An Intuitionistic Theory of Types: Predicative Part." In *Logic Colloquium '73*, ed. H. E. Rose and J. C. Shepherdson, 73–118. Amsterdam: North-Holland, 1975.**
The first published account of what would become Intuitionistic Type Theory. Martin-Löf gives the inductive definition of types, the identity type, and the natural numbers type. Dense but foundational.

**Enderton, H. B. *A Mathematical Introduction to Logic*. 2nd ed. San Diego: Academic Press, 2001.**
The clearest modern introduction to propositional and first-order logic, with full proofs of completeness and compactness. Enderton's notation is standard in North American logic courses. The exercises are well-designed. Start here for a rigorous but accessible treatment.

**van Dalen, D. *Logic and Structure*. 5th ed. Berlin: Springer, 2013.**
A comprehensive introduction covering propositional logic, predicate logic, intuitionistic logic, and modal logic. More complete than Enderton and includes a clear treatment of intuitionistic natural deduction, which is directly relevant to HoTT.

**Troelstra, A. S., and D. van Dalen. *Constructivism in Mathematics*, 2 vols. Amsterdam: North-Holland, 1988.**
The authoritative reference on constructive mathematics: intuitionism, realizability, and connections to type theory. Volume I covers the basics; Volume II goes deep into formal systems. Not light reading, but indispensable for anyone serious about the constructive perspective.

**Howard, W. A. "The Formulae-as-Types Notion of Construction." In *To H. B. Curry: Essays on Combinatory Logic, Lambda Calculus, and Formalism*, ed. J. P. Seldin and J. R. Hindley, 479–490. New York: Academic Press, 1980.**
Howard's 1969 manuscript, finally published in 1980. The explicit formulation of the Curry-Howard correspondence: propositions as types, proofs as programs. This paper named and formalized the idea that natural deduction proofs are lambda terms. Read alongside the Gentzen paper.
