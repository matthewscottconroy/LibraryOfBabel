Path composition is not strictly associative. Two paths that traverse the same sequence of subpaths, but at different speeds, are different functions — yet they are homotopic, connected by a continuous deformation. The homotopy is itself a path of paths, a two-dimensional object with its own structure: it can be composed with other homotopies, reversed, and deformed. The associativity law holds not as an equation but as a witness, and that witness participates in further structure that must itself be accounted for. This is not a technical annoyance to be patched over. It is the central organizing phenomenon of Homotopy Type Theory.

The fundamental group is the truncated version of the full story: you collapse homotopies by taking homotopy classes, and the resulting quotient is a group. But the uncollapsed structure — loops, homotopies between loops, homotopies between homotopies — forms an *infinity-groupoid*, a structure with coherent associativity, identity, and inverse laws at every dimension. Homotopy Type Theory's central theorem (or axiom schema, depending on how you read it) is that every type has the structure of an infinity-groupoid. The identity type a = b is the space of paths from a to b. The identity type p = q (for p, q : a = b) is the space of homotopies between paths. And so on, indefinitely.

This section makes that structure visible in the classical topological setting, before the type-theoretic formalization. By the time you encounter identity types, J-elimination, and higher inductive types, the pictures here — paths composing, homotopies filling squares, coherences filling cubes — should be the images behind the formal definitions.
