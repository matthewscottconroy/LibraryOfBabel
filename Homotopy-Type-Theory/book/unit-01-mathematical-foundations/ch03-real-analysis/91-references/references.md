# References: Real Analysis

## Core Textbooks

**Rudin, W. *Principles of Mathematical Analysis*. 3rd ed. New York: McGraw-Hill, 1976.**
The classic graduate analysis text, universally known as "Baby Rudin." Covers metric spaces, sequences and series, continuity, differentiation, integration, and sequences of functions. The proofs are dense and elegant. Excellent for developing rigor and speed. The chapters on metric spaces (Chapters 2–4) and sequences (Chapter 3) are directly relevant to this chapter. The exercises are demanding and essential.

**Rudin, W. *Real and Complex Analysis*. 3rd ed. New York: McGraw-Hill, 1987.**
"Papa Rudin" — the advanced sequel. Covers measure theory, Hilbert and Banach spaces, Fourier analysis, and complex analysis. The treatment of Lebesgue integration and Lp spaces provides the completion context mentioned in the convergence section. For the mathematically mature reader.

**Munkres, J. R. *Topology*. 2nd ed. Upper Saddle River: Prentice Hall, 2000.**
The standard topology textbook. Part I covers point-set topology: topological spaces, continuous functions, connectedness, compactness, and metric spaces. Part II introduces algebraic topology: the fundamental group, covering spaces, and the van Kampen theorem. Exceptionally clear exposition. The compactness and connectedness chapters are directly relevant here.

**Sutherland, W. A. *Introduction to Metric and Topological Spaces*. 2nd ed. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2009.**
More accessible than Munkres, with careful treatment of metric spaces and their topology. Good for building intuition before the more abstract material.

**Pugh, C. C. *Real Mathematical Analysis*. 2nd ed. New York: Springer, 2015.**
A more geometric and intuitive treatment than Rudin. Excellent for building intuition alongside rigor. The treatment of compactness and connectedness is particularly good, with emphasis on examples and pictures.

## For the Connection to Homotopy Theory

**Hatcher, A. *Algebraic Topology*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2002.**
Chapter 0 (CW complexes and homotopy) and Chapter 1 (the fundamental group) are directly relevant to this chapter. Hatcher develops the path-homotopy theory from scratch in a clear and well-motivated way. The connection between van Kampen's theorem and the Seifert-van Kampen theorem for fundamental groups is the bridge between Chapters 2 and 3 of this curriculum.

**Bredon, G. E. *Topology and Geometry*. New York: Springer, 1993.**
A comprehensive treatment connecting point-set topology, differential topology, and algebraic topology. The treatment of homotopy theory in Chapter IV is rigorous and complete. Good for seeing how the analytic and algebraic perspectives integrate.

## On the Constructive Real Numbers

**Bishop, E., and D. Bridges. *Constructive Analysis*. Berlin: Springer, 1985.**
The definitive treatment of analysis from a constructive perspective. Bishop and Bridges show that most of classical analysis can be developed constructively, with explicit witnesses for existence statements. The Cauchy real numbers are the starting point. Essential reading for understanding the type-theoretic approach to the real numbers.

**Univalent Foundations Program. *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics*. Institute for Advanced Study, 2013. (Available at homotopytypetheory.org)**
Chapter 11 of the HoTT Book develops the real numbers in HoTT: both the Cauchy reals (as a higher inductive type) and the Dedekind reals (as a set of cuts), and the proof that they are equivalent. This is the type-theoretic resolution of the identity problem for the real numbers.

## Primary Sources

**Cantor, G. "Über unendliche, lineare Punktmannigfaltigkeiten." *Mathematische Annalen* 21 (1883): 545–586.**
The paper where Cantor defines the Cantor set (in a different form) and develops the theory of perfect sets and derived sets. Translated in part in Ewald (1996). Historical context for the origins of point-set topology.

**Weierstrass, K. "Über continuirliche Functionen eines reellen Arguments, die für keinen Werth des letzteren einen bestimmten Differentialquotienten besitzen." 1872.**
The paper introducing the continuous nowhere-differentiable function. Read for historical context — the shock of the example and its reception by the mathematical community.

**Poincaré, H. "Analysis Situs." *Journal de l'École Polytechnique* 1 (1895): 1–123.**
The founding paper of algebraic topology. Poincaré introduces the fundamental group, Betti numbers (a precursor to homology groups), and the beginnings of the machinery that connects analysis and algebra. Translated by John Stillwell in *Papers on Topology: Analysis Situs and Its Five Supplements* (AMS, 2010).
