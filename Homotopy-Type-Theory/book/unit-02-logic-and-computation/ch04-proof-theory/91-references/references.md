# References: Proof Theory

## Primary Sources

**Gentzen, G. (1935). Untersuchungen über das logische Schließen (Investigations into Logical Deduction).** *Mathematische Zeitschrift*, 39, 176–210 and 405–431. Translated and reprinted in Szabo (1969). The founding document of structural proof theory: introduces natural deduction and sequent calculus, proves cut elimination and normalization, and proves the consistency of Peano Arithmetic. Essential primary source. Read the first half (natural deduction and sequent calculus); the consistency proof requires separate study of ordinal arithmetic.

**Prawitz, D. (1965). *Natural Deduction: A Proof-Theoretical Study*. Almqvist & Wiksell, Stockholm. (Reprinted by Dover, 2006.)** The definitive treatment of normalization for natural deduction. Proves weak and strong normalization, establishes the subformula property, and develops the theory of reduction as a formal system. The Dover reprint includes a new preface. This is the standard modern reference for natural deduction proof theory.

**Gentzen, G. (1936). Die Widerspruchsfreiheit der reinen Zahlentheorie (The Consistency of Elementary Number Theory).** *Mathematische Annalen*, 112, 493–565. Translated in Szabo (1969). The proof of consistency of Peano Arithmetic using transfinite induction up to $\varepsilon_0$. Harder than the 1935 paper; requires background in ordinal arithmetic. Historically essential.

**Tait, W. W. (1967). Intensional Interpretations of Functionals of Finite Type I.** *Journal of Symbolic Logic*, 32(2), 198–212. Introduces the logical relations method for proving strong normalization of the simply typed lambda calculus. Short, dense, and technically demanding, but essential for understanding the proof method used throughout type theory. The method is more accessible through the textbook expositions below.

## Secondary Sources and Textbooks

**Troelstra, A. S. and Schwichtenberg, H. (2000). *Basic Proof Theory*. Cambridge University Press. (2nd edition.)** The standard graduate textbook for proof theory. Covers natural deduction, sequent calculus, cut elimination, normalization, proof nets, and the proof theory of arithmetic and analysis. Mathematically rigorous throughout. Essential for serious study.

**Buss, S. R. (ed.) (1998). *Handbook of Proof Theory*. Elsevier.** A comprehensive reference covering all major areas of proof theory: structural proof theory (Buss), propositional proof complexity, ordinal analysis, reverse mathematics, and more. Not for beginners, but invaluable as a reference. The introductory chapter by Buss gives an excellent overview of the field.

**Girard, J.-Y., Lafont, Y., and Taylor, P. (1989). *Proofs and Types*. Cambridge University Press.** An accessible introduction to the Curry-Howard correspondence, typed lambda calculus, and the computational content of proofs. Written by Girard, the inventor of System F and linear logic, with characteristic depth and occasionally impenetrable prose. Available free online. Chapters 1–5 are directly relevant here.

**Gallier, J. (1986). *Logic for Computer Science: Foundations of Automatic Theorem Proving*. Harper and Row.** A computer-science-oriented treatment of proof theory covering propositional and predicate logic, resolution, natural deduction, and sequent calculus. Good for the computational perspective on proof search. Available free online from the author.

**Negri, S. and von Plato, J. (2001). *Structural Proof Theory*. Cambridge University Press.** Focuses on the structural properties of sequent calculus, particularly the analysis of admissible rules and the proof theory of non-classical logics. Excellent treatment of proof-theoretic methods for modal and intuitionistic logics.

**Szabo, M. E. (ed. and trans.) (1969). *The Collected Papers of Gerhard Gentzen*. North-Holland.** The English translations of all of Gentzen's major papers, including the 1935 proof theory papers and the consistency proofs. Essential for historical study. The translations are excellent.

## For the Connection to Type Theory

**Martin-Löf, P. (1984). *Intuitionistic Type Theory*. Bibliopolis, Naples.** Notes from Martin-Löf's 1980 lectures in Padova. The foundational document for MLTT as a proof-theoretic system. Develops the four judgment forms, the type constructors, and the computation rules from first principles. Essential reading for the connection between proof theory and type theory.

**Pfenning, F. and Davies, R. (2001). A Judgmental Reconstruction of Modal Logic.** *Mathematical Structures in Computer Science*, 11(4), 511–540. An example of proof-theoretic methods applied to the design of type systems: derives a type theory for modal logic by carefully analyzing the judgment structure. Demonstrates how proof theory guides type theory design.
