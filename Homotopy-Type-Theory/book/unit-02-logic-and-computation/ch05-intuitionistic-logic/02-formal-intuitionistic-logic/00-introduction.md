The BHK interpretation is compelling but not a proof system — it tells you what proofs *mean* without specifying which proofs are *valid*. To have a formal logic you need inference rules, and those rules must reflect the constructive commitment. The Intuitionistic Propositional Calculus (IPC) is natural deduction with the classical additions removed. No Law of Excluded Middle, no double negation elimination, no *reductio ad absurdum* in the full classical sense. What remains is exactly what the BHK clauses sanction.

The non-obvious fact is how much survives this restriction. IPC proves every classical tautology whose proof does not essentially invoke LEM or its equivalents. Most of ordinary mathematical reasoning — forward arguments, case analysis where cases are explicitly decided, proof by contradiction within a single branch — is available. What fails is a specific and identifiable class of non-constructive moves. IPC also has properties classical logic lacks: the *Disjunction Property* (if $A \vee B$ is provable, one of $A$ or $B$ is provable) and the *Existence Property* (if $\exists x, P(x)$ is provable, some specific witness is provable). These properties capture the computational content of constructive proofs.

IPC is the logical core of every dependently typed proof assistant. Lean, Coq, and Agda all implement intuitionistic logic at their foundations, with classical logic optionally available as an axiom. The reason is Curry-Howard: a proof assistant needs its proof terms to compute, and computation requires normalization, and normalization fails in classical logic. IPC is not a weakened logic that proof assistants must apologize for — it is the exactly right logic for a system where proofs are programs.
