# References: Intuitionistic Logic

## Primary Sources

**Heyting, A. (1930). Die formalen Regeln der intuitionistischen Logik.** *Sitzungsberichte der Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften*, 42–56, 57–71, 158–169. Translated in Mancosu (1998). The first formal axiomatization of intuitionistic propositional logic and predicate logic. Three papers: the first gives propositional rules, the second predicate logic, the third arithmetic. Dense but historically essential.

**Brouwer, L.E.J. (1975). *Collected Works, Vol. 1: Philosophy and Foundations of Mathematics*.** Edited by A. Heyting. North-Holland. The philosophical and foundational papers, including the important 1908 "Unreliability of the Logical Principles" and later papers on the creating subject. Essential for understanding Brouwer's views. Not mathematics in the modern sense — read as philosophy.

**Kripke, S. (1965). Semantical Analysis of Intuitionistic Logic I.** In J.N. Crossley and M.A.E. Dummett (eds.), *Formal Systems and Recursive Functions*, North-Holland, 92–130. The original Kripke semantics paper. Gives the forcing definition, proves soundness and completeness for IPC. A model of clarity.

**Gödel, K. (1933). Zur intuitionistischen Arithmetik und Zahlentheorie (On Intuitionistic Arithmetic and Number Theory).** In *Collected Works Vol. I*. Oxford University Press. Contains the double-negation translation of classical arithmetic into intuitionistic arithmetic. Short and beautiful.

**Bishop, E. (1967). *Foundations of Constructive Analysis*.** McGraw-Hill. (Second edition with Bridges, 1985, Springer.) The most important work of 20th-century constructive mathematics. Chapters 1–3 are accessible immediately; the rest requires analysis background. The preface and introduction are worth reading as essays on constructivism.

## Secondary Sources and Textbooks

**Troelstra, A.S. and van Dalen, D. (1988). *Constructivism in Mathematics: An Introduction*. 2 vols.** North-Holland. The comprehensive reference for constructive mathematics. Covers intuitionism, formal systems, realizability, Kripke semantics, constructive algebra and analysis. Mathematically rigorous throughout. Volume 1 covers foundations; Volume 2 covers advanced topics.

**van Dalen, D. (2013). *Logic and Structure*. 5th edition.** Springer. An excellent introductory logic text that covers both classical and intuitionistic logic, with Kripke semantics and the double-negation translation. Accessible to advanced undergraduates.

**Dummett, M. (1977). *Elements of Intuitionism*. Clarendon Press. (2nd edition, 2000.)** Dummett's philosophical treatment of intuitionism, combining formal logic with philosophical analysis. Harder than van Dalen but philosophically deeper. Dummett was one of the major philosophical defenders of intuitionism.

**Sorensen, M.H. and Urzyczyn, P. (2006). *Lectures on the Curry-Howard Isomorphism*.** Elsevier. Part I develops the BHK interpretation and intuitionistic logic in connection with type theory. An excellent bridge between the material of this chapter and the Curry-Howard correspondence.

**Beeson, M. (1985). *Foundations of Constructive Mathematics*.** Springer. A comprehensive mathematical treatment focusing on formal systems, realizability, and the metatheory of constructive logic. Less philosophical than Dummett, more formal than Bishop.

## For the Connection to HoTT

**Martin-Löf, P. (1984). *Intuitionistic Type Theory*.** Bibliopolis. (Available online.) The lectures that established MLTT as a foundation for constructive mathematics. The meaning explanations in the introduction are the clearest account of the BHK-to-type-theory connection.

**Hedberg, M. (1998). A coherence theorem for Martin-Löf's type theory.** *Journal of Functional Programming*, 8(4), 413–436. Proves Hedberg's theorem: types with decidable equality are h-sets. Short, elegant, and directly relevant to the HoTT h-level hierarchy.

**Univalent Foundations Program. (2013). *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics*.** Institute for Advanced Study. Available at homotopytypetheory.org. Chapter 3 (Sets and Logic) develops the h-level hierarchy and the relationship between propositions, decidability, and truncation in HoTT.
