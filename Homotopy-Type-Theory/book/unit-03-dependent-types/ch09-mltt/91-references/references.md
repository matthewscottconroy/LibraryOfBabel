# References: Chapter 9

## Primary Sources

**Martin-Löf, Per.** *Intuitionistic Type Theory* (Bibliopolis Notes). Bibliopolis, 1984.
— The foundational document of MLTT. Covers the four judgments, all type formers, the identity type with J, and the meaning explanations. Essential primary reading.

**Martin-Löf, Per.** "An Intuitionistic Theory of Types: Predicative Part." In *Logic Colloquium '73*, 73–118. North-Holland, 1975.
— The first published version of MLTT. Introduces the eliminator for ℕ as a primitive and the identity type.

**Hofmann, Martin, and Thomas Streicher.** "The Groupoid Interpretation of Type Theory." In *Twenty-Five Years of Constructive Type Theory*, edited by G. Sambin and J. Smith, 83–111. Oxford University Press, 1998.
— The paper that showed UIP is not derivable and introduced the groupoid model. Essential.

**Voevodsky, Vladimir.** "Univalent Foundations Project." Available at math.ias.edu/vladimir.
— Voevodsky's notes and presentations introducing the univalence axiom and the homotopy interpretation. The 2010 IAS lectures are the primary source.

## Textbooks and Lecture Notes

**The Univalent Foundations Program.** *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics*. IAS, 2013.
— Chapter 1 (MLTT, identity types, transport, ap) and Appendix A (rules of HoTT) are the standard reference. Free at homotopytypetheory.org.

**Awodey, Steve, and Michael Warren.** "Homotopy Theoretic Models of Identity Types." *Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society* 146 (2009): 45–55.
— The model-theoretic perspective on identity types as path spaces. One of the papers that made the homotopy interpretation precise.

**Streicher, Thomas.** "Investigations into Intensional Type Theory." Habilitation thesis, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität, 1993.
— Contains the proof that UIP is not derivable from J, and Axiom K. The source of "Axiom K."

**Jacobs, Bart.** *Categorical Logic and Type Theory*. North-Holland, 1999.
— The categorical semantics of dependent type theory, including fibered category theory and comprehension categories. Advanced but thorough.

## On the Identity Type

**Streicher, Thomas.** "Semantics of Type Theory." Progress in Theoretical Computer Science. Birkhäuser, 1991.
— A careful treatment of the semantics of intensional and extensional type theory, including the first rigorous account of the distinction.

**Gambino, Nicola, and Richard Garner.** "The Identity Type Weak Factorisation System." *Theoretical Computer Science* 409, no. 3 (2008): 94–109.
— Shows that the identity type corresponds to a weak factorization system in a model category. Connects MLTT to abstract homotopy theory.

**Warren, Michael A.** "Homotopy Theoretic Aspects of Constructive Type Theory." PhD thesis, Carnegie Mellon University, 2008.
— One of the thesis-length treatments of the homotopy interpretation before the HoTT Book. Readable and careful.

## On Intensional vs. Extensional MLTT

**Hofmann, Martin.** "Extensional Concepts in Intensional Type Theory." PhD thesis, University of Edinburgh, 1995.
— The definitive study of the relationship between intensional and extensional type theory. Shows how to recover extensionality principles within intensional type theory.

**Altenkirch, Thorsten.** "Extensional Equality in Intensional Type Theory." *LICS 1999*, 412–420.
— Shows how setoids (types with explicit equality relations) provide an extensional layer on top of intensional MLTT. Practical approach to working in intensional type theory.

## On Cubical Type Theory

**Cohen, Cyril, Thierry Coquand, Simon Huber, and Anders Mörtberg.** "Cubical Type Theory: A Constructive Interpretation of the Univalence Axiom." In *TYPES 2015*, LIPIcs, 2016.
— The foundational paper for cubical type theory, which gives univalence a computation rule.

**The Cubical Agda Documentation.** Available at agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/cubical.html.
— The practical interface to cubical type theory in Agda.

## Historical and Philosophical

**Martin-Löf, Per.** "Constructive Mathematics and Computer Programming." In *Logic, Methodology and Philosophy of Science VI*, 153–175. North-Holland, 1982.
— Martin-Löf's philosophical account of the propositions-as-types principle and its significance for constructive mathematics.

**Martin-Löf, Per.** "Truth of a Proposition, Evidence of a Judgement, Validity of a Proof." *Synthese* 73, no. 3 (1987): 407–420.
— The philosophical grounding for the four judgments, from Martin-Löf himself.

**Constable, Robert L., et al.** *Implementing Mathematics with the Nuprl Proof Development System*. Prentice-Hall, 1986.
— The first book on a working proof assistant based on type theory. Historical document for the extensional approach.
