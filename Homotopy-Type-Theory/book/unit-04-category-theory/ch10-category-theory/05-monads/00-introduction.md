Every adjunction gives a monad, and every monad comes from an adjunction. This is a theorem, not a slogan, and it means that monads and adjunctions are two presentations of the same structure — one more abstract, one more concrete. A monad on a category $\mathcal{C}$ is an endofunctor $T$ equipped with a unit and a multiplication satisfying axioms that look exactly like the axioms for a monoid. The slogan, due to Mac Lane, is precise: a monad is a monoid in the category of endofunctors of $\mathcal{C}$.

What is non-obvious is the scope of what this packages. Free constructions — free groups, free algebras, free monoids — all arise as monads. The propositional truncation $\|-\|$ in HoTT is a monad on types. Computationally, monads are the categorical abstraction of "effect types": the maybe monad models partiality, the list monad models nondeterminism, the state monad models stateful computation. The same mathematical object models algebraic theories, computational effects, and logical modalities.

The Eilenberg-Moore category makes this precise: the algebras for a monad $(T, \eta, \mu)$ are exactly the "structured sets" that the monad describes. For the free group monad, the algebras are groups. For the propositional truncation monad, the algebras are propositions (mere propositions in HoTT). For you, the key consequence is this: every type former in HoTT that satisfies a universal property can be analyzed as a monad, and every monad gives an adjunction between the base category and a category of algebras. The categorical and type-theoretic perspectives are not parallel descriptions — they are the same mathematics, in different notation.
