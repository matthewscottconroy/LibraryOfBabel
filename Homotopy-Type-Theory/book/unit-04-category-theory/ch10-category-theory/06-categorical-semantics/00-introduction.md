The rules of type theory are not arbitrary syntactic choices — they are the rules that hold in every category of a certain kind. This is the central claim of categorical semantics, and it is a theorem. The beta-reduction rule $(\lambda x. t)\, a = t[a/x]$ is the counit equation of the product-exponential adjunction. The eta-rule $\lambda x. (f\,x) = f$ is the uniqueness clause of the universal property. Every rule in the derivation system corresponds to a categorical fact that holds in all models simultaneously.

What is surprising is the precision and completeness of the correspondence. The simply typed lambda calculus and cartesian closed categories are not merely analogous — they are interchangeable. The classifying CCC of STLC is the "universal model": any model of STLC in any CCC corresponds to a unique CCC-functor out of the classifying CCC. Going one level up: dependent type theory corresponds to locally cartesian closed categories, where substitution becomes pullback and the dependent type formers $\Sigma$ and $\Pi$ become left and right adjoints to the pullback functor. Each extension of the type theory corresponds to an additional categorical axiom.

For HoTT, the table of correspondences reaches its apex: the correct semantic model is an $\infty$-topos. Types are interpreted as objects in an $\infty$-topos, identity types as path objects, dependent types as fibrations, and the univalence axiom as a property that holds in all $\infty$-toposes satisfying a certain condition. Understanding categorical semantics is not optional background — it is the framework within which the correctness, consistency, and computational content of HoTT can be made mathematically precise.
