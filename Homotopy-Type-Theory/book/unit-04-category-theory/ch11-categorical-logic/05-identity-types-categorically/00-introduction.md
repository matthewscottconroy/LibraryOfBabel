The identity type is where classical categorical semantics breaks down — and where the homotopy-theoretic perspective becomes unavoidable. The naive categorical interpretation of $a =_A b$ as the equalizer of the two projections from $A \times A$ forces the identity type to be a proposition: either $a = b$ or not, with at most one proof. This validates the axiom of uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP). But UIP is not provable in MLTT and fails in HoTT — which means that any categorical model of HoTT must do something more subtle than equalizers.

The correct interpretation requires *path objects*: an object $\mathsf{Path}(A)$ that fits into a factorization $A \to \mathsf{Path}(A) \to A \times A$ of the diagonal, where the left map is an acyclic cofibration and the right map is a fibration. The identity type $a =_A b$ is then the fiber of the path-space map over the pair $(a, b)$. This factorization is the categorical version of the contractibility of the space of paths starting at a fixed point — and the $J$ eliminator, path induction, is exactly the lifting property of the weak factorization system.

Hofmann and Streicher's groupoid model (1994) was the first model in which UIP fails: types are interpreted as groupoids, and morphisms in the groupoid are proofs of equality. Their construction proved that UIP is genuinely independent of MLTT. Voevodsky's simplicial set model extended this to all higher dimensions, where types are ∞-groupoids and identity types are path spaces. The categorical analysis of identity types is not a technical footnote — it is the conceptual pivot point between classical type theory and HoTT.
