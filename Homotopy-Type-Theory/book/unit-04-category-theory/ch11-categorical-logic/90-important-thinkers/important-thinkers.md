# Important Thinkers: Categorical Logic

## Joachim Lambek (1922–2014)

Lambek made foundational contributions to the correspondence between logic and category theory. His 1968 paper *Deductive Systems and Categories* identified the category-theoretic content of the Curry-Howard correspondence: a proof in natural deduction corresponds to a morphism in a CCC, and cut elimination corresponds to composition. The *Curry-Howard-Lambek correspondence* bears his name as the three-way identification of proofs, programs, and morphisms. Lambek's later work (with P.J. Scott) on *Introduction to Higher Order Categorical Logic* (1986) established the precise relationship between CCCs and STLC, and between toposes and higher-order logic. He also made major contributions to linguistics (Lambek calculus, a type-logic for natural language).

## F. William Lawvere (born 1937)

Lawvere's contributions to categorical logic are pervasive. His doctoral thesis (1963, published 1969) introduced *functorial semantics* — the idea that algebraic structures are functors from a "theory" category to $\mathbf{Set}$ — and *Lawvere theories*, providing a categorical treatment of universal algebra. His 1969 paper *Adjointness in Foundations* showed that quantifiers are adjoints. His work with Tierney on *elementary toposes* (1971) established the axioms for a topos as the categorical foundation for higher-order intuitionistic logic. Lawvere has continued to develop *cohesive type theory* and the categorical treatment of differential geometry.

## Myles Tierney (1937–2017)

Tierney worked with Lawvere to develop elementary topos theory in 1971. His contribution was the precise formulation of the subobject classifier axiom and the proof that elementary toposes satisfy the requirements for higher-order logic. He also introduced *Lawvere-Tierney topologies* — a generalization of Grothendieck topologies to the elementary setting — and proved the key theorem that sheafification in a topos (with respect to a Lawvere-Tierney topology) produces a new topos. This theorem underlies the construction of new models of MLTT by sheafification over existing models.

## Martin Hofmann (1966–2019)

Hofmann made fundamental contributions to the categorical semantics of dependent type theory. His 1994 joint paper with Streicher on *The Groupoid Interpretation of Type Theory* proved the independence of UIP from MLTT — the result that launched HoTT. Hofmann's doctoral thesis and subsequent work on *syntax and semantics of dependent types* provided precise categorical models for MLTT using comprehension categories and categories with families. He proved that the category of contexts of MLTT is an initial model (classifying category), establishing completeness of MLTT for its categorical semantics. He was killed in an avalanche in 2019.

## Thomas Streicher (born 1958)

Streicher co-authored with Hofmann the groupoid model paper and has made extensive contributions to the categorical semantics of type theory. His 1991 dissertation *Semantics of Type Theory: Correctness, Completeness, and Independence Results* remains a comprehensive reference for the LCCC semantics of dependent types. Streicher's work on *domain-theoretic* and *realizability* models of MLTT has provided a broad landscape of categorical models, illuminating which axioms (UIP, function extensionality, LEM) are independent of the core MLTT rules.

## Andrew Pitts (born 1957)

Pitts has contributed to both the theory of categorical semantics and its application to programming language theory. His work on *categorical logic* (lecture notes, free online) provides an accessible treatment of the internal language of toposes. He also developed *tripos theory* (with Hyland) — a generalization of the effective topos to a wider class of "realizability" models — and *nominal sets*, a categorical approach to syntax with variable binding. More recently, Pitts has contributed to the foundations of Nominal and Fresh-Name type theories.

## J. Martin E. Hyland (born 1948)

Hyland constructed the *effective topos* (1982), one of the most important non-Grothendieck toposes. The effective topos models the logic of computable mathematics: Church's Thesis holds (all functions $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ are recursive) and Markov's Principle holds, but LEM fails. Hyland's proof that the effective topos satisfies the topos axioms was a major technical achievement. He has also contributed to realizability semantics, game semantics, and the theory of monads.

## Steve Awodey (born 1959)

Awodey contributed to the categorical foundations of HoTT through two important results. First, with Ricky Pollard, he proved that the *homotopy-theoretic models of identity types* (what became the Awodey-Warren framework) provide models of MLTT in any Quillen model category. Second, with Michael Warren, the precise formulation in terms of weak factorization systems. Awodey's book *Category Theory* (2010) provides an excellent treatment of the connections to logic, and his philosophical work has argued that HoTT provides a better foundation for mathematical practice than classical set theory.
