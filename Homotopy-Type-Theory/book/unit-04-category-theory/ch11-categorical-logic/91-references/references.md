# References: Categorical Logic

## Primary References

**Lambek, J. and Scott, P.J. (1986). *Introduction to Higher Order Categorical Logic*.** Cambridge University Press. The definitive treatment of the Curry-Howard-Lambek correspondence, proving the precise equivalence between CCCs and STLC, and between toposes and higher-order intuitionistic logic. Part I covers the CCC-STLC correspondence; Part II covers higher-order logic and toposes. Technical but complete.

**Jacobs, B. (1999). *Categorical Logic and Type Theory*.** North-Holland. (Free online.) The most comprehensive reference for fibered categories, comprehension categories, and the categorical semantics of dependent type theory. Chapters 1–4 cover fibrations; Chapters 5–7 cover the internal language of fibrations; Chapter 10 covers the semantics of dependent types. Essential for anyone formalizing the categorical semantics.

**Pitts, A. (1995). *Categorical Logic*.** (Course notes, available online from Cambridge.) A concise and clear treatment of the internal language of toposes, the subobject classifier, and the relationship between geometric logic, coherent logic, and propositional logic. Very accessible as a complement to the larger references.

## For Toposes

**Johnstone, P. (1977). *Topos Theory*.** Academic Press. The original systematic treatment. More accessible than the *Elephant*. Still a good first reference.

**McLarty, C. (1992). *Elementary Categories, Elementary Toposes*.** Clarendon Press. The most accessible treatment of elementary topos theory, written for non-specialists. Emphasizes the logical perspective. Good for those coming from logic.

**Bell, J.L. (1988). *Toposes and Local Set Theories: An Introduction*.** Clarendon Press. Develops the internal logic of a topos as a "local set theory" — a set theory where truth is local (in the sheaf-theoretic sense). Connects topos theory to intuitionistic set theories.

## For the Identity Type and HoTT Semantics

**Hofmann, M. and Streicher, T. (1998). The Groupoid Interpretation of Type Theory.** In *Twenty-Five Years of Constructive Type Theory*, Oxford University Press, 83–111. The landmark paper proving the independence of UIP from MLTT. Constructs the groupoid model, shows MLTT holds in it, and shows UIP fails. Essential reading for anyone working on HoTT foundations.

**Awodey, S. and Warren, M. (2009). Homotopy-Theoretic Models of Identity Types.** *Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society*, 146(1), 45–55. Establishes the general framework: any Quillen model category provides a model of MLTT in which the identity type is interpreted via path objects. The paper that initiated the homotopy-theoretic approach to identity types.

**Streicher, T. (1991). *Semantics of Type Theory: Correctness, Completeness, and Independence Results*.** Birkhäuser. Hofmann's doctoral advisor's monograph on the categorical semantics of MLTT. Proves the classifying property of the term category, the completeness theorem, and the independence of function extensionality and other axioms.

## For Fibered Categories and Models

**Gray, J.W. (1966). Fibred and Cofibred Categories.** In *Proceedings of the Conference on Categorical Algebra (La Jolla 1965)*, Springer, 21–83. One of the earliest systematic treatments of Grothendieck fibrations. Historical.

**Johnstone, P. (2002). *Sketches of an Elephant*, Vol. I.** Clarendon Press. Part B covers fibred category theory in depth: fibrations, indexed categories, and the correspondence between the two. The most complete reference.

**Dybjer, P. (1996). Internal Type Theory.** In *Proceedings of BRA Workshop TYPES 1995*, LNCS 1158, Springer, 120–134. Introduces Categories with Families as a categorical semantics for dependent type theory. Short and elegant. The CwF framework is widely used in implementations.

## Historical

**Lawvere, F.W. (1963/1969). Functorial Semantics of Algebraic Theories.** *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 50(5), 869–872 (1963); republished with author commentary in *Reprints in Theory and Applications of Categories*, 5 (2004). The introduction of Lawvere theories and functorial semantics.

**Lawvere, F.W. (1969). Adjointness in Foundations.** *Dialectica*, 23, 281–296. Reprinted in *Reprints in Theory and Applications of Categories*, 16 (2006). The paper arguing that adjunctions are the fundamental concept of mathematics, with quantifiers as adjoints as a central example.
