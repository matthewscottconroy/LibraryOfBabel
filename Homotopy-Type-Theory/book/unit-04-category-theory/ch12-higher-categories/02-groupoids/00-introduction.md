Groupoids are categories where every morphism is an isomorphism, and they occupy a precise middle ground between sets and spaces. A set is a groupoid with only identity morphisms — no non-trivial paths between points. A group is a groupoid with one object — all paths start and end at the same place. A topological space has a fundamental groupoid whose objects are points and whose morphisms are homotopy classes of paths. Groupoids are the 1-dimensional homotopy types.

What is non-obvious is that groupoids fix a well-known deficiency of the fundamental group. The fundamental group $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ requires a basepoint, which means it fails to be natural for spaces with non-trivial topology between components. The fundamental *groupoid* $\Pi_1(X)$ works with all basepoints simultaneously: it is the category whose objects are all points of $X$ and whose morphisms are homotopy classes of paths between them. The fundamental group at $x_0$ is the automorphism group of $x_0$ in $\Pi_1(X)$ — one object in a richer structure.

In MLTT and HoTT, every type is already a groupoid. The objects are the terms, the morphisms from $a$ to $b$ are the proofs of $a =_A b$, path concatenation is composition, reflexivity is the identity, and path inversion is the categorical inverse. The groupoid laws — associativity, unit, inverse — hold propositionally. UIP is exactly the claim that the groupoid is discrete (only identity morphisms), which fails in HoTT. Groupoids thus encode precisely the 1-dimensional homotopy content of a type, and understanding them is the prerequisite for understanding the full ∞-groupoid structure that HoTT assigns to every type.
