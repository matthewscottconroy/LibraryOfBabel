Grothendieck's homotopy hypothesis, formulated in his 1983 manuscript *Pursuing Stacks*, identifies topology and algebra at the deepest level: the ∞-category of homotopy types is equivalent to the ∞-category of ∞-groupoids. A topological space, up to weak homotopy equivalence, carries no more and no less information than an algebraic object — an ∞-groupoid — that can be described combinatorially. Geometry and algebra, usually separate domains, are the same thing.

This has been proved rigorously in multiple formulations. The most precise: there is a Quillen equivalence between the category of topological spaces (with the Quillen model structure) and the category of simplicial sets (with the Kan-Quillen model structure), where the Kan complexes are the ∞-groupoids. The functors realizing this equivalence are the singular simplicial set $\mathsf{Sing}(X)$ (turning a space into a combinatorial object) and the geometric realization $|K|$ (turning a combinatorial object into a space). The adjunction $|{-}| \dashv \mathsf{Sing}$ is a Quillen equivalence.

For HoTT, the hypothesis is not just a theorem about topological spaces — it is the philosophical foundation of the entire enterprise. Types in HoTT *are* ∞-groupoids: the identity types provide the 1-morphisms (paths), the identity types of identity types provide the 2-morphisms (homotopies between paths), and so on to all dimensions. The homotopy hypothesis says this is not just an analogy — the algebraic structure encoded in iterated identity types is exactly the structure of a homotopy type. HoTT is the internal language of the ∞-category of ∞-groupoids.
