∞-Groupoids capture homotopy types perfectly — but they model only spaces, only invertible morphisms. Mathematics also contains directed structure: functions between types are not invertible in general; morphisms in a category need not have inverses. The right generalization is the (∞,1)-category: an ∞-categorical structure where all $k$-morphisms for $k \geq 2$ are invertible (homotopies are reversible), but 1-morphisms may not be. This is the homotopy-coherent version of an ordinary category, and it is the natural home of most of modern mathematics.

The problem is making this precise without drowning in coherence data. If you try to specify an (∞,1)-category by listing its objects, morphisms, composition operations, associators, pentagon identities for the associators, and so on, you encounter an infinite tower of coherence conditions with no clear stopping point. The solution — due to Boardman, Vogt, and later Joyal and Lurie — is to encode all the coherence data *implicitly* in a single combinatorial object: a simplicial set satisfying the inner horn filling condition. This is a quasi-category, also called an ∞-category.

The inner horn filling condition is elegant: given two composable 1-simplices $f : a \to b$ and $g : b \to c$, the inner horn $\Lambda^1[2]$ is the "U-shape" and a filler gives the composite $g \circ f$. Higher inner horns give associativity data, and the fillers at all dimensions simultaneously encode all the higher coherence. No explicit coherence is specified; all of it is implicit in the filling. For you, quasi-categories are the model of (∞,1)-categories in which the ∞-topos of Kan complexes lives, and the ∞-topos of Kan complexes is the semantic model for HoTT.
