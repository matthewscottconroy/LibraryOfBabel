# Important Thinkers: Higher Category Theory

## Alexander Grothendieck (1928–2014)

Grothendieck formulated the homotopy hypothesis in his 1983 manuscript *Pursuing Stacks*. He proposed that homotopy types should be modeled by ∞-groupoids, and that the right notion of ∞-groupoid should be defined axiomatically in terms of the globular structure. Though he did not complete this program — withdrawing more completely from mathematics after *Pursuing Stacks* — the manuscript inspired an entire generation of mathematicians. His earlier work created sheaf theory (as part of his algebraic geometry program), Grothendieck toposes, and the theory of ∞-stacks that today connects algebraic geometry with homotopy theory. The Fields Medal (1966) does not capture the scope of his contribution.

## Daniel Kan (1927–2013)

Kan invented simplicial homotopy theory. His 1958 paper *A Combinatorial Definition of Homotopy Groups* introduced Kan complexes (simplicial sets satisfying the extension condition, which he called *fibrant* simplicial sets) and showed that they model homotopy theory. He proved that the geometric realization and singular functor form a Quillen equivalence (before Quillen's general theory existed). Kan also invented the notion of an adjoint functor (independently of Mac Lane) and contributed to the algebraic K-theory of spaces (the A-theory of Waldhausen and Segal).

## Daniel Quillen (1940–2011)

Quillen invented *model categories* in his 1967 monograph *Homotopical Algebra* — the abstract framework for homotopy theory. A model category is a category with three classes of morphisms (weak equivalences, fibrations, cofibrations) satisfying axioms that allow the construction of a homotopy category by formally inverting weak equivalences. The Quillen equivalence between simplicial sets and topological spaces (proving the homotopy hypothesis for Kan complexes) is the centerpiece of his work. Quillen also invented *algebraic K-theory* (earning him the Fields Medal in 1978) and made major contributions to rational homotopy theory.

## André Joyal (born 1943)

Joyal introduced *quasi-categories* as a model for (∞,1)-categories in unpublished notes from the 1980s (published later in collaboration with Lurie). A quasi-category is a simplicial set satisfying the inner horn filling condition — the starting point for Lurie's vast development of ∞-category theory. Joyal proved the *Joyal model structure* on simplicial sets, with quasi-categories as the fibrant objects and categorical equivalences as the weak equivalences. He also developed the theory of *species* (a categorical approach to enumerative combinatorics) and has contributed to the theory of ∞-toposes.

## Jacob Lurie (born 1977)

Lurie is the primary architect of modern ∞-category theory and ∞-topos theory. His two major works — *Higher Topos Theory* (800+ pages, 2009) and *Higher Algebra* (900+ pages, 2017) — established the foundations of the subject. *Higher Topos Theory* developed the theory of quasi-categories as ∞-categories, proved the ∞-categorical Yoneda lemma, defined ∞-toposes and proved their characterization, and connected ∞-toposes to Grothendieck toposes. *Higher Algebra* developed the theory of ∞-operads, structured ∞-categories (stable, symmetric monoidal), and ∞-categorical algebra. Lurie has also developed the *Lurie-Sullivan* factorization for algebraic K-theory and the foundations of derived algebraic geometry.

## Emily Riehl (born 1983)

Riehl has made major contributions to making higher category theory accessible and model-independent. Her book *Category Theory in Context* (2016) is the best introduction to ordinary category theory. Her joint work with Dominic Verity on *∞-cosmoi* provides a framework for doing ∞-category theory without choosing a specific model: the theory works for quasi-categories, complete Segal spaces, or any other equivalent model. Her work on *Reedy model structures*, *algebraic model structures*, and *enriched homotopy theory* has provided the technical foundations underlying many constructions in higher category theory. She is also known for making the field accessible through excellent expository writing.

## Vladimir Voevodsky (1966–2017)

Voevodsky constructed the *simplicial set model* of HoTT, proving the consistency of MLTT + Univalence. His proof showed that every closed term of type $\mathbb{N}$ in HoTT reduces to a numeral — establishing the canonicity that was initially in doubt. He invented the *Univalent Foundations* program, arguing that HoTT should replace set theory as the foundation of mathematics. He contributed to algebraic K-theory and motivic homotopy theory before turning to foundations. His Fields Medal (2002) was for his proof of the Milnor and Bloch-Kato conjectures in algebraic K-theory. He died unexpectedly in 2017, leaving the Univalent Foundations program as his legacy.

## Charles Rezk (born 1969)

Rezk introduced *complete Segal spaces* as a model for (∞,1)-categories and proved that they are Quillen equivalent to quasi-categories. His paper *A model for the homotopy theory of homotopy theory* (2001) is a foundational reference. Complete Segal spaces are simplicial spaces (bisimplicial sets) satisfying Segal conditions (expressing that composition is well-defined up to homotopy) and a completeness condition (expressing that equivalences are homotopy coherent). Rezk's model is sometimes preferred in foundational work because it makes the "homotopy theory of (∞,1)-categories" particularly transparent.
