# References: Higher Category Theory

## Primary References

**Lurie, J. (2009). *Higher Topos Theory*.** Princeton University Press. (Free online.) The foundational reference for ∞-categories and ∞-toposes. Chapter 1 (on quasi-categories as ∞-categories) is accessible after a background in ordinary category theory. Chapters 2–7 develop the theory systematically. This is the reference for all of ∞-category theory.

**Riehl, E. and Verity, D. (2022). *Elements of ∞-Category Theory*.** Cambridge University Press. (Free online.) A more accessible approach to ∞-categories that is model-independent (using the ∞-cosmos framework). Covers ∞-limits, adjunctions, and the Yoneda lemma at the ∞-categorical level. Recommended for those who want the content of Lurie's book in a more accessible form.

**Goerss, P. and Jardine, J.F. (1999). *Simplicial Homotopy Theory*.** Birkhäuser. The standard reference for the technical foundations of simplicial homotopy theory: Kan complexes, Quillen model structure, homotopy groups of simplicial sets, and the Quillen equivalence with topological spaces. Technical but comprehensive.

## For the Homotopy Hypothesis

**Quillen, D. (1967). *Homotopical Algebra*.** Springer Lecture Notes in Mathematics 43. The original model category paper. Short by modern standards (150 pages), but introduces all the key ideas: model categories, Quillen functors, derived functors, the Quillen equivalence between simplicial sets and topological spaces. A classic.

**May, J.P. (1967). *Simplicial Objects in Algebraic Topology*.** University of Chicago Press. (Free online.) The standard reference for the homotopy theory of simplicial sets. Chapters 1–6 cover Kan complexes, homotopy groups, and the adjunction $|{-}| \dashv \mathsf{Sing}$.

**Friedman, G. (2012). An Elementary Illustrated Introduction to Simplicial Sets.** *Rocky Mountain Journal of Mathematics*, 42(2), 353–423. (Available on arXiv.) The most accessible introduction to simplicial sets, with many pictures. Recommended as a first reading before the more technical references.

## For 2-Categories and Bicategories

**Kelly, G.M. and Street, R. (1974). Review of the Elements of 2-Categories.** *Lecture Notes in Mathematics 420*, Springer, 75–103. The foundational reference for 2-categories, introducing the basic definitions and key theorems.

**Bénabou, J. (1967). Introduction to Bicategories.** *Reports of the Midwest Category Seminar*, Springer, 1–77. The original paper on bicategories, introducing the weak version of 2-categories and the coherence conditions.

**Leinster, T. (1998). *Basic Bicategories*.** (arXiv: math/9810017.) A concise introduction to bicategories, including the coherence theorem and examples.

## For Higher Category Theory in HoTT

**Voevodsky, V. (2014). The equivalence axiom and univalent models of type theory.** *Talk at CMU*, available on the Voevodsky memorial website. The original presentation of the simplicial set model and the proof that it validates univalence.

**The Univalent Foundations Program. (2013). *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics*.** Chapter 2 (on homotopy type theory) and Chapter 6 (on higher inductive types). The HoTT Book treats types as ∞-groupoids and develops the consequences, though without using the language of ∞-category theory explicitly.

**Lumsdaine, P.L. (2010). Weak ω-Categories from Intensional Type Theory.** *Logical Methods in Computer Science*, 6(3). Proves formally that every type in MLTT gives an ∞-groupoid, providing the rigorous version of the claim that "types are ∞-groupoids."

**van den Berg, B. and Garner, R. (2011). Types are Weak ω-Groupoids.** *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society*, 102(2), 370–394. An independent proof (with different technique) that MLTT types are ∞-groupoids. Together with Lumsdaine's paper, establishes the type-theoretic homotopy hypothesis.

## For Applications to Algebraic Geometry

**Lurie, J. (2004). *Derived Algebraic Geometry*.** PhD thesis, MIT. The foundational work on derived algebraic geometry: schemes, stacks, and derived schemes in the ∞-categorical framework. This is where Lurie first developed the key tools later systematized in *Higher Topos Theory*.

**Toën, B. and Vezzosi, G. (2005). Homotopical Algebraic Geometry I: Topos Theory.** *Advances in Mathematics*, 193(2), 257–372. An alternative (and earlier) approach to ∞-topos theory in the context of algebraic geometry, using the Segal space model.
