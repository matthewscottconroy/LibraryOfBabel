Topology is not the study of spaces in isolation — it is the study of spaces together with the maps that respect their structure. A topological space is defined by its open sets; a continuous map is one that pulls open sets back to open sets. Everything else in the subject follows from the interplay between these two ideas.

What is non-obvious is how much the preimage definition encodes. Continuity via preimages feels abstract at first — you are not measuring how close outputs are to each other, you are asking about the structure of a set. But this abstraction is precisely what gives the definition its power: it generalizes instantly to any topological space, without requiring metrics, without requiring convergent sequences, without requiring any structure beyond the topology itself. The homeomorphism — the isomorphism of topological spaces — is equally elegant: a bijection that is continuous in both directions. Yet continuous bijections need not be homeomorphisms, and this failure is not a defect but a feature, one that forces you to think carefully about what "sameness" really means for spaces.

The category **Top** of topological spaces and continuous maps is the natural home of this theory, and learning to think categorically here pays dividends throughout HoTT. Homotopy type theory is, at its core, a story about what kinds of maps between types deserve to be called equivalences — and that story is already visible in miniature in the distinction between continuous bijections and homeomorphisms. When you later encounter the notion of a homotopy equivalence, or the univalence axiom's claim that equivalent types are equal, you will recognize the same conceptual pressure at work.
