# References: Point-Set Topology

## Primary Texts

**Munkres, James R.** *Topology.* 2nd ed. Prentice Hall, 2000.
The standard reference for point-set topology at the graduate level. Chapters 1–4 cover set theory and topological spaces; Chapters 5–8 cover connectedness, compactness, countability, and separation axioms; Part II covers the fundamental group and covering spaces. The presentation is clear, the exercises are well-graded, and the coverage of CW complexes (in the algebraic topology supplement) is excellent.

**Kelley, John L.** *General Topology.* Van Nostrand, 1955; reprinted by Springer, 1975.
The other classic reference, more encyclopedic and less pedagogically structured than Munkres. Comprehensive coverage of nets, filters, uniform spaces, and function spaces. Essential for understanding the more abstract aspects of point-set topology.

**Engelking, Ryszard.** *General Topology.* Revised and completed edition. Heldermann Verlag, 1989.
The most comprehensive reference on point-set topology, covering over 400 pages of theorems with full proofs and extensive historical notes. The appendix on problems (open and solved) is a valuable resource.

**Willard, Stephen.** *General Topology.* Addison-Wesley, 1970; reprinted by Dover, 2004.
A clean, readable account that bridges Munkres and Kelley. Particularly good on metrization theorems and the structure of compact spaces.

## For the HoTT Connection

**The HoTT Book.** *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics.* Institute for Advanced Study, 2013. Available free at homotopytypetheory.org.
Chapter 2 (Homotopy Type Theory) and Chapter 6 (Higher Inductive Types) are the primary references for the HoTT analog of the constructions in this chapter. Chapter 8 (Homotopy Theory) contains the synthetic proofs of classical results.

**Shulman, Michael.** "Homotopy Type Theory: A Synthetic Approach to Higher Equalities." In *New Spaces in Mathematics: Formal and Conceptual Reflections.* Cambridge University Press, 2021.
An excellent overview of how HoTT captures topological intuition, accessible to readers with a topology background.

## On CW Complexes

**Hatcher, Allen.** *Algebraic Topology.* Cambridge University Press, 2002. Available free at pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher.
Chapter 0 is the essential reference on CW complexes: definition, examples, and the key properties. Chapters 1–2 cover the fundamental group and homology for CW complexes.

**Milnor, John.** "On the Existence of a Connection with Curvature Zero." *Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici* 32 (1958): 215–223.
Not directly on CW complexes, but Milnor's work on the homotopy theory of CW complexes (including the comparison of the different notions of CW structure) is foundational for the section on Whitehead's theorem.

## Historical Sources

**Poincaré, Henri.** "Analysis Situs." *Journal de l'École Polytechnique* 2, no. 1 (1895): 1–123.
The founding paper of algebraic topology. Introduces the fundamental group, Betti numbers, and the homological methods that dominate Chapter 14.

**Hausdorff, Felix.** *Grundzüge der Mengenlehre.* Veit, 1914. Translated as *Set Theory.* Chelsea, 1957.
The book that put topology on rigorous foundations. Part III contains the original axiomatization of topological spaces.

**Brouwer, L.E.J.** "Beweis der Invarianz des $n$-dimensionalen Gebiets." *Mathematische Annalen* 71 (1911): 305–313.
The paper proving invariance of domain and establishing degree theory for maps of spheres.

## Survey Articles

**Steen, Lynn Arthur, and J. Arthur Seebach Jr.** *Counterexamples in Topology.* Springer, 1978; reprinted by Dover.
An invaluable catalog of topological spaces and the properties they do or do not satisfy. Organized as a table of spaces vs. properties, with detailed descriptions. The Sorgenfrey line, the topologist's sine curve, and many other examples from this chapter are documented here.

**Arhangel'skii, A.V., and L.S. Pontryagin, eds.** *General Topology I.* Springer, 1990.
A collection of survey articles covering the main areas of general topology, including encyclopedic treatments of compactness, connectedness, and separation axioms.
