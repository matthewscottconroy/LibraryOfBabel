A covering space lays multiple copies of a space's local structure over each of its points. Walk around a loop in the base space, and you find yourself at a different sheet of the cover than where you started — this is monodromy, the action of the fundamental group on the fiber. The staircase covering $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$, defined by $t \mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$, is the canonical picture: infinitely many copies of a small arc lying over each small arc of the circle, with the integers indexing which level of the staircase you occupy.

What is surprising is the depth of the Galois correspondence. Covering spaces of a connected, locally path-connected, semi-locally simply-connected base $B$ are classified, up to isomorphism, by conjugacy classes of subgroups of $\pi_1(B, b_0)$. This is a perfect analogue of the correspondence between field extensions and subgroups of the Galois group in classical algebra. The universal cover — the unique simply-connected covering space — corresponds to the trivial subgroup, just as the splitting field corresponds to the trivial subgroup of the Galois group. Topology and algebra are doing the same thing, and the covering-space machine makes this visible.

In HoTT, the correspondence becomes definitional. A covering space over $B$ is precisely a map $B \to \mathsf{Set}$ — a type family over the base type, where each fiber is a set. Path lifting corresponds to transport along identities: if $p : b_0 = b_1$ is a path (proof of equality) in $B$, then the covering space gives a function from the fiber over $b_0$ to the fiber over $b_1$, computed by transporting along $p$. Monodromy is transport around loops. The entire theory of covering spaces is, from the HoTT perspective, the theory of set-valued type families, and it is fully internal to the type-theoretic formalism.
