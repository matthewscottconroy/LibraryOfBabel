A fibration is a map that lets you lift paths. Given a path in the base and a starting point in the total space above the path's initial endpoint, you can always continue that path into the total space — and for a fibration, you can lift homotopies too. Covering spaces are the special case where the fibers are discrete; fibrations are the generalization where fibers are arbitrary topological spaces, and the lifting properties hold in full generality. The result is the most powerful computational tool in homotopy theory: the long exact sequence of a fibration, which ties together the homotopy groups of the fiber, total space, and base in a single exact algebraic chain.

What is striking about fibrations is how much algebraic output they produce from a single geometric condition. Given $p : E \to B$ with fiber $F$, you obtain a sequence $\cdots \to \pi_n(F) \to \pi_n(E) \to \pi_n(B) \to \pi_{n-1}(F) \to \cdots$ that is exact at every term. This turns the problem of computing homotopy groups — generally intractable — into a structured inductive exercise: if you know the homotopy groups of any two of the three spaces, exactness forces the third. The Hopf fibration $S^1 \to S^3 \to S^2$ is the canonical example: its long exact sequence immediately computes $\pi_3(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$ and several other groups.

In HoTT, fibrations are dependent types. A fibration $p : E \to B$ corresponds to a type family $P : B \to \mathcal{U}$, and the homotopy lifting property corresponds to the elimination rule for the identity type: given a path $p : b_0 = b_1$ and a term in the fiber over $b_0$, transport produces a term in the fiber over $b_1$. The path-loop fibration $\Omega(X) \to PX \to X$, with contractible total space and fiber the loop space, becomes in HoTT the statement that the path space from a basepoint is contractible — a foundational fact about identity types.
