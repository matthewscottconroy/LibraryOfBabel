# References: Homotopy Theory

## Primary Texts

**Hatcher, Allen.** *Algebraic Topology.* Cambridge University Press, 2002. Available free at pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher.
The standard graduate text. Chapter 1 covers the fundamental group; Chapter 2 covers homology; Chapter 3 covers cohomology; Chapter 4 covers homotopy theory (fibrations, higher homotopy groups, Eilenberg-MacLane spaces, Postnikov towers). Exceptionally clear exposition with many examples and exercises. The appendix on the topology of CW complexes is essential background.

**May, J. Peter.** *A Concise Course in Algebraic Topology.* University of Chicago Press, 1999. Available free online.
More advanced than Hatcher, covering model categories, spectra, and equivariant homotopy theory. Essential for the connections to modern homotopy theory.

**tom Dieck, Tammo.** *Algebraic Topology.* European Mathematical Society, 2008.
A comprehensive treatment with emphasis on functorial methods. Excellent on homotopy groups, fibrations, and the relationship between homotopy theory and category theory.

**Bredon, Glen E.** *Topology and Geometry.* Springer, 1993.
Strong on geometric intuition. Good treatment of fibrations and the long exact sequence. Useful for the connection between homotopy theory and differential geometry.

## For the Fundamental Group and Covering Spaces

**Massey, William S.** *A Basic Course in Algebraic Topology.* Springer, 1991.
The definitive textbook treatment of the fundamental group, covering spaces, and their algebraic classification. Clear proofs with good examples.

**Rotman, Joseph J.** *An Introduction to Algebraic Topology.* Springer, 1988.
Accessible treatment with careful attention to set-theoretic foundations. Good on van Kampen's theorem and the classification of surfaces.

## For Higher Homotopy Groups and Fibrations

**Whitehead, George W.** *Elements of Homotopy Theory.* Springer, 1978.
The comprehensive reference for classical homotopy theory. Covers homotopy groups, fibration sequences, spectral sequences, and much more. Dense but authoritative.

**Switzer, Robert M.** *Algebraic Topology: Homology and Homotopy.* Springer, 1975.
Strong treatment of the long exact sequence of a fibration, the Hurewicz theorem, and Eilenberg-MacLane spaces.

**Mosher, Robert E., and Martin C. Tangora.** *Cohomology Operations and Applications in Homotopy Theory.* Harper & Row, 1968; reprinted by Dover.
Essential for the stable homotopy theory that underlies modern computations.

## For the Hopf Fibration

**Steenrod, Norman.** *The Topology of Fibre Bundles.* Princeton University Press, 1951.
The classic reference on fiber bundles. The Hopf fibration is treated in detail, along with the classification theory and characteristic classes.

**Milnor, John W., and James D. Stasheff.** *Characteristic Classes.* Princeton University Press, 1974.
The standard reference on vector bundles and their characteristic classes. Develops the theory of fiber bundles needed to understand the Hopf fibration in its broader context.

## For Model Categories

**Quillen, Daniel G.** *Homotopical Algebra.* Springer Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 1967.
The founding text. Dense and technical, but essential for understanding the formal structure of homotopy theory.

**Dwyer, William G., and Jan Spaliński.** "Homotopy Theories and Model Categories." In *Handbook of Algebraic Topology.* North-Holland, 1995.
An accessible survey of model categories with many examples.

**Hovey, Mark.** *Model Categories.* American Mathematical Society, 2007.
The modern comprehensive reference. Clear proofs with emphasis on examples.

## For the HoTT Connection

**The HoTT Book.** *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics.* IAS, 2013.
Chapter 6 (Higher Inductive Types) and Chapter 8 (Homotopy Theory) contain synthetic proofs of the classical results: $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$, the Hopf fibration, van Kampen's theorem, and the long exact sequence of a fibration.

**Brunerie, Guillaume.** "On the Homotopy Groups of Spheres in Homotopy Type Theory." PhD thesis, Université de Nice, 2016.
The synthetic proof that $\pi_4(S^3) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ in HoTT. An extraordinary piece of synthetic homotopy theory.

## Historical

**Hopf, Heinz.** "Über die Abbildungen der dreidimensionalen Sphäre auf die Kugelfläche." *Mathematische Annalen* 104 (1931): 637–665.
The paper introducing the Hopf fibration and computing $\pi_3(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$.

**Hurewicz, Witold.** "Beiträge zur Topologie der Deformationen. I–IV." *Proceedings of the Akad. Amsterdam* 38-39 (1935-36).
Four papers defining the higher homotopy groups and proving the Hurewicz theorem.

**Serre, Jean-Pierre.** "Homologie singulière des espaces fibrés." *Annals of Mathematics* 54 (1951): 425–505.
The doctoral thesis introducing spectral sequences to homotopy theory and computing homotopy groups of spheres in a range.
