Model categories are Quillen's answer to a foundational question: what is the minimal structure needed to do homotopy theory? The question matters because homotopy theory was developed for topological spaces, but the same constructions — homotopy groups, fibrations, cofibrations, the homotopy category — appear in chain complexes, simplicial sets, categories, and many other settings. A model structure formalizes the common pattern: three classes of maps (cofibrations, fibrations, weak equivalences) satisfying axioms that guarantee the machinery of homotopy theory works. Any category with a model structure is a context in which you can do homotopy theory.

The Quillen model structure on $\mathbf{sSet}$ is clean and elegant. Cofibrations are just monomorphisms — injective maps. Fibrations are Kan fibrations — the horn-lifting condition. Weak equivalences are maps whose geometric realization induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups. The fibrant objects are precisely the Kan complexes, the good combinatorial models of spaces. Every simplicial set can be replaced by a weakly equivalent Kan complex (fibrant replacement), and two maps between Kan complexes that are homotopic in the simplicial sense are identified in the homotopy category.

The deepest theorem here is that the model structure on $\mathbf{sSet}$ is Quillen equivalent to the model structure on topological spaces. This means the two settings compute the same homotopy theory: the homotopy category of Kan complexes and the homotopy category of topological spaces are equivalent categories. Homotopy theory can be done entirely combinatorially, with no metric, no continuity in the classical sense, no point-set topology — just sets organized by face and degeneracy operations. This combinatorial freedom is what makes simplicial sets the right setting for Voevodsky's model, where types, terms, and identity proofs all live in a world of sets organized by the simplex category.
