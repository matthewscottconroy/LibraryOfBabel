Once you accept that paths are elements of types, a further question becomes unavoidable: what are the paths between paths? Given two paths p and q from a to b, the identity type `p =_{a=_Ab} q` is itself a type — the type of 2-paths, or homotopies between paths. There is no reason for this tower to stop. Between 2-paths there are 3-paths, and so on indefinitely. Every type carries, in principle, an infinite hierarchy of higher path structure.

This is the infinity-groupoid structure of a type, and it is not an addition to the theory — it falls out automatically from the definitions. You do not choose to have higher paths. They are there, whether you use them or not, as a consequence of taking the identity type seriously.

What makes higher paths more than a curiosity is that they interact. Two 2-paths can be composed in two distinct ways — horizontally and vertically — and the Eckmann-Hilton argument shows these compositions must coincide and must be commutative. This is the type-theoretic version of a classical result in algebraic topology: the second homotopy group is abelian. The fact that you can prove this from the J eliminator alone, without any appeal to geometry, is a sign that the foundations are exactly right.
