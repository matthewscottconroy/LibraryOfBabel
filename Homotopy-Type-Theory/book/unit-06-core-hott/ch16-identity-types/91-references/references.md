# References: Identity Types and Paths

## Primary Sources

**Martin-Lof, P.** (1984). *Intuitionistic Type Theory* (Notes by Giovanni Sambin of a series of lectures given in Padova, June 1980). Bibliopolis, Naples. The canonical source for Martin-Lof type theory, including the identity type and J rule. Still the best introduction to the philosophical underpinnings.

**Martin-Lof, P.** (1975). "An Intuitionistic Theory of Types: Predicative Part." In *Logic Colloquium '73*, H. Rose and J. Shepherdson (eds.), North-Holland, 73–118. The original introduction of the identity type.

**The Univalent Foundations Program.** (2013). *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics*. Institute for Advanced Study. Available free at homotopytypetheory.org/book. Chapter 2 covers path operations, transport, and ap in full formal detail. The definitive reference for the material in this chapter.

**Voevodsky, V.** (2010). "Univalent Foundations Project." Unpublished grant application. Available at math.ias.edu/vladimir/sites/math.ias.edu.vladimir/files/univalent_foundations_project.pdf. Voevodsky's original statement of the program.

## Foundational Models

**Hofmann, M. and Streicher, T.** (1994). "The Groupoid Interpretation of Type Theory." In *Twenty-Five Years of Constructive Type Theory*, Oxford University Press. The paper that established that identity types need not be propositional.

**Kapulkin, C. and Lumsdaine, P. L.** (2012, revised 2016). "The Simplicial Model of Univalent Foundations." Available at arxiv.org/abs/1211.2851. The rigorous construction of the simplicial set model in which J corresponds to contractibility of path spaces.

## Technical References

**Awodey, S. and Warren, M.** (2009). "Homotopy Theoretic Models of Identity Types." *Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society* 146(1): 45–55. Early rigorous treatment of the connection between identity types and path spaces.

**Garner, R.** (2009). "On the Strength of Dependent Products in the Type Theory of Martin-Lof." *Annals of Pure and Applied Logic* 160(1): 1–12. Technical analysis of how path induction interacts with the rest of type theory.

**Streicher, T.** (1993). "Investigations into Intensional Type Theory." Habilitationsschrift, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universitat München. Early investigation of the independence of UIP from J.

## For Further Reading

**Licata, D. R. and Shulman, M.** (2013). "Calculating the Fundamental Group of the Circle in Homotopy Type Theory." *Proceedings of LICS 2013*. A direct companion to this chapter, showing how path computations enable the fundamental group calculation.

**Coquand, T.** (2012). "Equality and Dependent Type Theory." Lecture notes. Available at cs.cmu.edu/~cmartens/equality.pdf. A careful treatment of the distinction between definitional and propositional equality.
