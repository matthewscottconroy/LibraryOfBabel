# References: H-Levels and Truncations

## Primary Sources

**The Univalent Foundations Program.** (2013). *Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics*. Chapter 3 covers mere propositions, sets, and the h-level hierarchy in full detail. Chapter 6 covers truncations as HITs. This is the canonical reference.

**Voevodsky, V.** (2010). "Univalent Foundations Project." The original description of the h-level hierarchy and its role in univalent foundations.

**Voevodsky, V.** "Foundations" (multiple Coq files, available at math.ias.edu/vladimir). The original Coq formalization of the h-level hierarchy, including contractibility, propositions, sets, and their properties.

## On Hedberg's Theorem

**Hedberg, M.** (1998). "A Coherence Theorem for Martin-Lof's Type Theory." *Journal of Functional Programming* 8(4): 413–436. The original proof that decidable equality implies UIP (sets in HoTT terminology).

**Kraus, N., Escardó, M., Coquand, T., and Altenkirch, T.** (2013). "Generalizations of Hedberg's Theorem." In *Typed Lambda Calculi and Applications*, Springer LNCS 7941. Generalisations showing when and how UIP can fail, and what conditions are needed.

## On Truncations

**Lumsdaine, P. L. and Shulman, M.** (2017). "Semantics of Higher Inductive Types." *Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society*. The foundational treatment of truncations as HITs.

**Rijke, E., Shulman, M., and Spitters, B.** (2020). "Modalities in Homotopy Type Theory." *Logical Methods in Computer Science* 16(1). A comprehensive treatment of truncation as a special case of a general "modality" — an idempotent operation on types.

## On the Postnikov Tower

**Licata, D. R. and Finster, E.** (2014). "Eilenberg-MacLane Spaces in Homotopy Type Theory." *Proceedings of LICS 2014*. Constructs the Eilenberg-MacLane spaces K(G,n) as HITs and uses them to study the Postnikov tower in HoTT.

**Brunerie, G.** (2016). *On the Homotopy Groups of Spheres in Homotopy Type Theory.* PhD thesis, Université Nice Sophia Antipolis. The most advanced application of h-level theory, computing π₄(S³) = Z/2Z.

## For Further Reading

**Awodey, S.** (2012). "Type Theory and Homotopy." In *Epistemology versus Ontology*, Springer. A philosophical treatment of why the h-level hierarchy provides the right notion of "type" for foundations.

**Escardó, M.** Various lecture notes on homotopy type theory in Agda. Available at cs.bham.ac.uk/~mhe/HoTT-UF-in-Agda-Lecture-Notes. Detailed worked examples of h-level arguments in a proof assistant.
