Before you can state the Univalence Axiom, you need to know what an equivalence is. The naive answer — a bijection, a function with a two-sided inverse — turns out to be insufficient, and the reason it fails is illuminating. The type of quasi-inverses of a function is not, in general, a proposition. A function can have distinct quasi-inverses, and this multiplicity would make univalence incoherent: paths in the universe would not correspond one-to-one with equivalences, they would correspond one-to-many.

The fix requires finding a notion of equivalence that is propositional — a type that says "f is an equivalence" in the way that a proposition should, with no extra data to choose. There are two clean solutions. The first is bi-invertibility: require a left inverse and a right inverse separately, without demanding they coincide. The second is the half-adjoint equivalence: require a quasi-inverse together with a coherence condition on the two homotopies. Both notions are equivalent to each other and to the contractibility of fibers, and all of them are propositions.

The fact that there are multiple equivalent definitions of equivalence is not redundancy — it is evidence that you have found the right concept. A robust mathematical notion looks the same from multiple angles. Equivalence in HoTT is the type-theoretic notion of isomorphism that is closed under all the operations of the theory, stable under composition, and exactly what the universe needs its paths to correspond to. Everything that follows in Chapter 18 depends on having this definition correct.
