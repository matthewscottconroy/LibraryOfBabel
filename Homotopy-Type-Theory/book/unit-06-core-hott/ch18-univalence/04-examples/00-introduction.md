Axioms become real through their computations. The Univalence Axiom tells you that paths in the universe are equivalences, but what does that actually mean when you sit down and ask: what are the loops at a specific type? The answer, for the boolean type, is striking: there are exactly two loops at Bool in the universe — the trivial one and the one corresponding to the swap equivalence — and they form the two-element group. The loop space of the universe at Bool is Z/2Z.

This is a concrete computation about the universe itself. Not about an abstract space, not about a topological model — about the type-theoretic universe, the object that contains all types. The universe has a non-trivial homotopy structure. The types inside it are connected by paths, and the paths form groups. Voevodsky saw this computation and understood it as confirmation that types genuinely are spaces, and that homotopy theory and type theory had found common ground.

The examples extend. The automorphisms of Fin(n) — the equivalences from the n-element type to itself — form the symmetric group S_n, and by univalence these are exactly the loops at Fin(n) in the universe. The integers and the type of natural numbers are equivalent as sets, so there is a path between them in the universe. These are not word games. They are computations, as rigorous as any calculation in algebra, and they show univalence doing exactly what it promises: making the formal behavior of types in mathematics precise.
