All the spheres — the circle, the ordinary sphere, and every higher-dimensional analog — can be defined by a single recursive construction: suspension. The suspension of a type A adds two new points, north and south, and for every element of A introduces a path from north to south. Starting from the two-point type Bool as the 0-sphere, each application of suspension raises the dimension by one: the suspension of Bool is the circle, the suspension of the circle is the 2-sphere, and the n-sphere is Bool suspended n times.

This is remarkable for several reasons. It means you do not need to write down a new definition for each sphere — a potentially unbounded collection of fundamental objects is captured by a single two-line HIT together with a single recursive step. It also makes the relationship between spheres structurally visible. The suspension is a left adjoint to the loop space functor, and the interaction between ΣA and ΩA governs the stable homotopy theory of spheres, which is one of the deepest and most studied areas in topology.

Concretely, the suspension tells you not only how to define spheres but how to prove things about them. The eliminator for ΣA requires you to specify behavior at the north pole, the south pole, and along every meridian. This is the type-theoretic analogue of defining a map out of a sphere by specifying where the boundary goes. The coherence conditions that the eliminator imposes are exactly the topological coherence conditions you would impose if you were thinking geometrically. Higher inductive types do not just encode spaces — they encode the right way to reason about maps out of them.
