Gluing is one of the most basic operations in geometry and topology. You take two spaces and attach them along a common piece, and the result is a new space whose properties depend on the properties of the pieces and the attachment. A sphere is a hemisphere glued to another hemisphere along their shared equator. A torus is a rectangle with its edges identified. The figure-eight is two circles glued at a single point. Every compact surface can be built this way.

The pushout is the categorical formalization of this gluing operation, and in HoTT it is a higher inductive type. Given a span — two types A and B with a common type C mapping into each — the pushout is the type generated by the elements of A and B, together with a path for each element of C that identifies the two images of that element. The path constructor of the pushout literally enacts the identification: `glue(c)` is a path in the pushout from the image of c in A to the image of c in B.

The power of the pushout as a HIT is that its universal property is not imposed from outside but built into the eliminator. A map out of a pushout is exactly a pair of maps from A and B that agree on C, and "agree" means they are connected by paths indexed by the elements of C. This is the type-theoretic expression of the universal property of colimits, and it comes for free from the HIT formulation. With pushouts available, you can build virtually any CW complex as a type, and the van Kampen theorem — computing fundamental groups of pushouts — becomes a direct consequence of the universal property.
