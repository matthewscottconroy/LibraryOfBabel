The computation that the fundamental group of the circle is the integers is the benchmark theorem of synthetic homotopy theory. It is to HoTT what the halting problem is to computability: the result that defines the territory, that shows the framework is strong enough to prove something genuinely non-trivial about a genuinely interesting mathematical object.

Everything in this unit has been building toward this proof. The circle is a HIT (Chapter 19). Paths are elements of identity types (Chapter 16). Transport gives the mechanism for moving data along paths (Chapter 16). Univalence gives a way to define type families with specific transport behavior (Chapter 18). The encode-decode method provides the proof strategy (Chapter 20, Section 1). Stack these tools and the computation goes through: the loop space of the circle at its basepoint is equivalent to the integers, with the winding number as the explicit equivalence.

The proof is a formal verification of something that classical topology proved by appeal to the theory of covering spaces. In HoTT, the universal cover of the circle is the code family — the type family over S^1 that assigns the integers to the basepoint and transports by successor. The covering space is not a separate topological construction; it is a type family, already internal to the theory. This is what "synthetic" means in synthetic homotopy theory: the spaces and their coverings are not set-theoretic objects studied by a theory, they are the theory, and reasoning about them is just type-theoretic reasoning.
