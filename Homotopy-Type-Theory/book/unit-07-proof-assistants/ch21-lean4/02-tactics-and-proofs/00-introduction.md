Lean 4 gives you two languages for writing proofs, and the choice between them is not merely stylistic — it reflects two different ways of relating to a proof. In *term mode*, you construct the proof object directly, in one expression, the way you might write a lambda term or a data structure. You know what you are building, and you build it. In *tactic mode*, you work backwards from the goal: you read the proof state, apply a tactic that transforms it, and iterate until no goals remain. Term mode is mathematics as programming; tactic mode is mathematics as dialogue with the type checker.

Both modes elaborate into the same kernel terms. Tactics are not magic — they are programs that construct terms, and the kernel verifies those terms without caring how they were produced. But tactic mode scales in a way that term mode does not, because the proof state externalizes the bookkeeping. Instead of holding the entire proof structure in your head, you hold a single goal and ask: what tactic reduces this? Learning to read the proof state — hypotheses above the turnstile, goal below it — is the central skill this section develops.

You will work through the core tactic vocabulary: `intro`, `apply`, `exact`, `rw`, `simp`, `constructor`, `cases`, `induction`, and the automation tactics that can close large classes of goals in one step. The point is not to memorize a list. It is to build the reflex of reading a goal and recognizing which move fits. With that reflex, the complexity of the proofs you can write grows far faster than the complexity of the tactics you need to know.
