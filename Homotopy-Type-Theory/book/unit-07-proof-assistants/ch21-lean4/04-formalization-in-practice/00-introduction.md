Formalizing mathematics is not transcription. The informal proof is a sketch — it gestures at a logical skeleton, papers over definitional details, and trusts the reader to fill in the gaps. Lean 4 trusts no one. When you formalize, you find every implicit assumption, every "it is clear that," every appeal to a fact the author did not bother to state. Sometimes what you find is merely overhead. Occasionally, as when Georges Gonthier formalized the four-color theorem and found errors that thirty years of informal review had missed, what you find is more significant.

The formalization workflow has a shape: state the theorem precisely enough that Lean 4 accepts the type, identify what Mathlib already provides, locate the gaps that need original proofs, fill those gaps with tactics, and assemble the pieces. Each stage has its own pitfalls. Stating the theorem forces a precision that informal mathematics often defers; searching Mathlib requires knowing its naming conventions; writing original proofs requires recognizing when to use automation and when to reason by hand. This section walks through a complete project — the image of a group homomorphism is a subgroup, then the first isomorphism theorem — at enough detail that you can reproduce the process independently.

What you are developing here is the capacity to carry a mathematical argument to completion in a proof assistant, not just write individual tactics. That means managing a multi-file project, structuring helper lemmas, and making the judgments about abstraction and reuse that determine whether a formalization is maintainable or a tangle of special cases. The technique is learnable, and this section is where you learn it.
