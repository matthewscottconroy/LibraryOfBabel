Agda occupies a precise position in the proof assistant landscape, and understanding that position matters before you write a line of code. Where Lean 4 invests heavily in ergonomics — a rich tactic language, aggressive automation, a library of 150,000 theorems — Agda invests in foundational transparency. You see more. You control more. You do more manually. The tradeoff is real, but so is the reward: when Agda accepts a proof, you understand it at a level of detail that the corresponding Lean 4 proof would not require you to articulate. For the purposes of HoTT — and specifically for Cubical Agda — this transparency is not a drawback. It is the point.

Agda's core mechanism is *pattern matching on dependent types*. A definition in Agda is a collection of clauses, each specifying the output for a particular pattern of the input. When those inputs are dependent types — types that mention terms — the pattern match can inspect the structure of a type-level object, enabling proofs by induction that look exactly like the mathematical induction principles they implement. The `with` construct extends this to allow intermediate computations to influence the case split. Together, these tools give you a direct expression of recursive mathematical reasoning.

This section builds the vocabulary you need before entering Cubical mode: universes and universe polymorphism, the `data` and `record` declarations that define types, how definitional equality works in vanilla Agda, and the interactive workflow in Emacs or VS Code where Agda's response to each definition guides your next step. The interactive feedback loop — write a clause, check the goal, refine — is Agda's version of the Lean 4 infoview, and getting comfortable with it is the prerequisite for everything in Cubical Agda that follows.
