Propositional truncation was always a hint. When you form $\|A\|$, you are not just squashing a type into a proposition — you are reflecting it into a subcategory of propositions. The reflection has a universal property: any map from $A$ into a proposition factors uniquely through $\eta : A \to \|A\|$. The truncation is the left adjoint to the inclusion of propositions into all types. This is a modality, and once you see it as such, you see it everywhere: $n$-truncation for any $n$, localization at a set of primes, sheafification, completion. The unifying concept is the reflective subcategory.

A modality in HoTT is an idempotent, accessible localization functor: an operation $\bigcirc$ on types, a unit $\eta_A : A \to \bigcirc A$, and the requirement that the modal types — those where $\eta$ is an equivalence — form a reflective subcategory with $\bigcirc$ as the reflector. The universal property says that $\bigcirc A$ is the closest modal approximation to $A$: any map from $A$ into a modal type factors uniquely through $\bigcirc A$. Idempotency says that reflecting twice adds nothing. These are not disparate axioms but facets of a single structural property.

What is technically significant here is the type-theoretic packaging. Modal types in homotopy type theory are not sets with structure; they are types that may have non-trivial path structure, and the modality must interact coherently with that structure. The subcategory of modal types is closed under all homotopy-type-theoretic constructions that preserve the relevant invariants. Understanding modalities precisely — not just as operations but as reflective subcategories with their full homotopy-theoretic content — is the foundation for everything in the remaining two sections, where specific modality systems encode differential geometry and gauge theory.
