A principal $G$-bundle over $M$ is, in cohesive HoTT, simply a map $P : M \to BG$. Not an atlas of transition functions satisfying a cocycle condition. Not a local trivialization with a gluing law. A single map into the classifying type. The classical classification theorem — that principal $G$-bundles over $M$ are classified by homotopy classes $[M, BG]$ — becomes, via univalence, a literal identity between the type of bundles and the type of maps $M \to BG$. Homotopy classes collapse to maps. Transition functions and cocycle conditions are recovered as the computational content of this map, not as additional data to be supplied.

This compression is the entry point to Urs Schreiber's program of *differential cohomology in a cohesive topos*, and it is technically significant for a concrete reason: the gauge symmetry of physics is, in the type-theoretic account, just the equivalence structure of the bundle map. A gauge transformation is a path in the mapping space $M \to BG$; gauge-invariant quantities are those that depend only on the equivalence class. In a type theory where equivalences are paths and paths are native objects, gauge invariance is not a condition you impose — it is a consequence of stating your physics in the right language. The action of a gauge group on fields is the action of the loop space of $BG$ on maps $M \to BG$, which is just the computation of homotopy groups.

What you encounter in this section is the outer edge of what cohesive HoTT currently achieves: a synthetic, coordinate-free formulation of Yang-Mills theory, Chern-Simons theory, and the classification of anomalies, all expressed as type-theoretic constructions. The formalism does not yet replace the physical intuition required to select which theory to write down — but once you have written it down in cohesive HoTT, the mathematics is done inside the type theory, with the full force of univalence and the cohesive modalities available as computational tools.
