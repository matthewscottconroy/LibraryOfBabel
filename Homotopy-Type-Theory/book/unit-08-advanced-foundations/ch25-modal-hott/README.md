# Chapter 25: Modal HoTT and Cohesive Geometry

## Differential Geometry Was Stuck in Coordinates for a Century.

Differential geometry studies smooth spaces — manifolds with tangent bundles, curvature, and connection. General relativity is a theory of differential geometry. It says that spacetime is a 4-manifold with a metric tensor, and gravity is the curvature of that metric. For a century, differential geometry lived in the world of coordinates and local charts, epsilon-delta arguments and tensors. Not because coordinate-based methods are the right language for the mathematics — everyone knew they weren't — but because there was no alternative.

The alternative took two forms. The first: sheaf theory and topos theory, developed by Grothendieck and his collaborators, which gave differential geometry a language in terms of spaces of sections and local data. The second: synthetic differential geometry, developed by Lawvere and Kock, which gave differential geometry a language in terms of infinitesimal objects — nilsquare elements, microlinear spaces — added directly to the foundation.

Both approaches had limitations. Sheaf theory required set-theoretic foundations and was not easily internalized. Synthetic differential geometry required a non-classical logic (the presence of nilsquare elements is inconsistent with classical Boolean logic).

Urs Schreiber and Michael Shulman discovered that you can do all of differential geometry — and gauge theory, and string theory — in a type theory. Not by encoding coordinates. Not by adding nilsquare elements. By adding three modalities: shape $\int$, flat $\flat$, and sharp $\sharp$. These three modalities, related by the adjoint triple $\int \dashv \flat \dashv \sharp$, capture the relationship between continuous spaces and their discrete shadows, between geometric structure and combinatorial structure.

The result is *Cohesive HoTT*, and it is one of the most beautiful things in contemporary mathematics.

---

### What Cohesion Captures

The word "cohesion" refers to the way a space hangs together — the continuous structure that makes it more than just a set of disconnected points. A smooth manifold $M$ is not just its set of points $|M|$. It has a topology, a smooth structure, a differential structure. The manifold *coheres* — its points are held together by their relationships.

Cohesive HoTT captures this by adding three modalities that distinguish between different levels of geometric structure:

**Shape $\int A$**: The underlying homotopy type of $A$ — the space $A$ regarded only up to continuous deformation, forgetting all smooth or differential structure. For a smooth manifold $M$: $\int M$ is the underlying topological space considered as an ∞-groupoid. For $\mathbb{R}^n$: $\int \mathbb{R}^n \simeq \mathbf{1}$ — the real $n$-space is contractible, and its shape knows nothing about its smooth structure.

**Flat $\flat A$**: The underlying discrete space of $A$ — the same elements, but with no continuous structure. Points of $\flat A$ are completely isolated; there are no paths between distinct points of $\flat A$ unless they come from actual equalities in the underlying set. For $\mathbb{R}$: $\flat \mathbb{R}$ is the set of real numbers with the discrete topology — no paths, no geometry, just a set.

**Sharp $\sharp A$**: The codiscrete space on $A$ — a type into which all maps from all cohesive spaces are automatically "continuous." $\sharp A$ has the indiscrete structure: too much structure for geometry, but useful as a target for formal constructions.

The three modalities satisfy the adjoint triple $\int \dashv \flat \dashv \sharp$: shape is left adjoint to discrete, flat is both left and right adjoint (the discrete functor), and sharp is right adjoint to flat.

---

### Real Cohesion: Pinning It Down

The abstract cohesion axioms admit many models. To get differential geometry specifically, you need to pin down that the cohesion is *generated by the real line* $\mathbb{R}$. This is the *real cohesion axiom* (Shulman 2018):

**Axiom.** A type $A$ is $\sharp$-modal (codiscrete) if and only if every continuous map $\mathbb{R} \to A$ is constant.

This axiom says: the cohesive structure is detected by mapping from the real line. A type with interesting geometric structure has non-constant $\mathbb{R}$-paths. A type with no geometric structure (a discrete type) has only constant $\mathbb{R}$-paths.

With this axiom:
- $\int \mathbb{R} \simeq \mathbf{1}$ (the real line is contractible as a cohesive space — it can be continuously deformed to a point)
- $\int S^1 \simeq S^1$ (the circle's shape is the HoTT circle)
- $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ follows from the covering space $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$

These are theorems, not axioms. Real cohesion is the single axiom from which all of continuous mathematics follows.

### The Physics Connection

Urs Schreiber's research program — formalizing gauge theory and string theory in cohesive HoTT — represents one of the most ambitious applications of type theory to mathematical physics.

The key objects of physics are:
- **Gauge fields**: connections on principal bundles
- **Gauge transformations**: bundle isomorphisms
- **The moduli stack of gauge fields**: a space whose points are gauge fields and whose morphisms are gauge transformations

In cohesive HoTT, all three are types. A gauge field is an element of a type. A gauge transformation is a path in that type. The moduli stack is literally a type, not a quotient space or a stack defined by a complex presentation.

The formalization proceeds:
1. Define smooth manifolds as cohesive types (types with appropriate flatness properties)
2. Define principal $G$-bundles as types with a $G$-action
3. Define connections using the flat modality: a connection on $P \to M$ is a map $P \to \flat P$ satisfying a compatibility condition
4. Define the moduli stack $\mathsf{Conn}(P)$ as the type of connections
5. Compute gauge transformations as the automorphisms of this type

The Chern-Weil homomorphism — the classical map from connections to characteristic classes — becomes a map between types in cohesive HoTT. Its properties (it is a ring homomorphism, it is natural, it doesn't depend on the connection) are all provable from the cohesion axioms.
