The problems in this section do not have answers at the back of the book — because no one knows them yet.

That is a simple fact, but it takes a moment to fully register. Everything in this curriculum up to now has been settled mathematics: results proved, checked, sometimes formalized down to the term level. You have been learning a subject that exists. What follows is different. The open problems in HoTT are genuinely open: no proof, no consensus on the right approach, no guarantee that the question is even well-posed in the form it is currently stated. When you engage with the Brunerie number or the question of canonicity for Book HoTT, you are working in the same epistemic position as every other researcher in the field.

This is not a pedagogical device. The problems are organized here by type — gaps, anomalies, voids — because that distinction shapes how you should work on them. A gap problem calls for a new proof technique. An anomaly problem calls for a diagnosis: why does the expected result fail? A void problem calls for something harder, a new framework or a new question. You will find all three kinds here. Some are accessible to a prepared newcomer within a year of focused work. Others have resisted the field's best efforts for a decade. The honest assessment of each is given plainly. Your job now is to read carefully, pick a thread, and pull.
