Formalization is not transcription — it is excavation.

When you take a proof you think you understand and force every step to typecheck, something unexpected reliably happens: the proof is not quite what you thought it was. An elision that seemed innocent turns out to carry real mathematical content. A definition that looked clean develops hidden dependencies when the universe levels are made explicit. Occasionally you find something sharper: a step that seemed to require one lemma actually requires a weaker one, and the gap between them is a theorem worth proving on its own. This is why formalization is listed here as a research frontier rather than a separate practical skill — the act of formalizing HoTT mathematics is itself how the field discovers things about HoTT mathematics.

The Cubical Agda library is where most of this excavation is currently happening, and its current state is a precise picture of the frontier. What is fully formalized tells you what the infrastructure can support. What is absent — π₅(S⁴), algebraic K-theory, the cobordism hypothesis — tells you where the next generation of results will come from. If you have worked through this curriculum seriously, you are in a position to contribute to this library: not by cleaning up comments, but by formalizing results that are not yet there. The sections that follow give you the current map, with its blank regions clearly marked.
