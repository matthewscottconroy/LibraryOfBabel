HoTT does not sit at the edge of mathematics — it sits at an intersection, and the roads leading away from it go deep into territories that most of its practitioners have not yet explored.

There is a difference worth holding onto as you read this section: an analogy helps you think, but a connection generates theorems. The connections described here are, in the best cases, formal correspondences — functors, equivalences, transport maps — that have already carried results across field boundaries and that promise to carry more. Algebraic K-theory has a natural synthetic formulation in HoTT that may turn out to be cleaner than its classical set-theoretic one. The cobordism hypothesis, Lurie's massive classification theorem for topological field theories, is exactly the kind of result that synthetic homotopy theory was built to handle — and no one has yet tried to prove it in a proof assistant. Condensed mathematics and motivic homotopy theory both point toward HoTT in ways that are not yet precisely articulated.

These connections are not offered here as curiosities. They are offered as research programs. Each one represents a direction where the formal machinery you have developed — univalence, HITs, dependent types, the synthetic treatment of homotopy — can engage with mathematics that has been done, until now, in purely classical foundations. The question is not whether these connections exist. The question is what you can do with them.
