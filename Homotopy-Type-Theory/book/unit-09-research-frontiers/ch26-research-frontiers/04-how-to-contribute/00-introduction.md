You are not too late, and you are not too small — HoTT is a field where a prepared newcomer can do real work, and this section tells you how.

The community is unusually accessible by the standards of modern mathematics. The researchers whose names appear throughout this curriculum are, most of them, reachable: they post questions and answers on the HoTT Zulip, they give freely available talks through HoTTEST, they respond to thoughtful emails from students they have never met. This is partly a consequence of the field's size — a few hundred active researchers worldwide — and partly a consequence of its culture, which has consistently valued openness and treated formalization infrastructure as a shared project rather than a competitive asset. When you open a pull request against the Cubical Agda library, you are not knocking on a closed door. You are contributing to something that the field's senior researchers actively maintain and care about.

What this section gives you is the practical shape of that contribution: where the community lives, how the conversations work, what "a good first issue" actually looks like in this context, and how to move from completing this curriculum to doing original work. The advice is specific because vague encouragement is not useful — you do not need to be told that HoTT is exciting or that the field welcomes newcomers. You need to know which Zulip stream to post in, how to frame a question so it gets a useful answer, and what a year of serious engagement actually looks like from the inside. That is what follows.
