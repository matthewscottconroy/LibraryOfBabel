# Exercises

These exercises range from direct applications of definitions to deeper investigations. Some are guided toward specific techniques; others are open-ended and invite exploration.

---

## Section 1: Groups

**Exercise 1.1.** Prove that every group of prime order $p$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.

*Hint:* Pick any non-identity element $g$. Consider $\langle g \rangle$. Use Lagrange's theorem to determine $|\langle g \rangle|$.

---

**Exercise 1.2.** Show that $S_3 \cong D_3$. Construct an explicit isomorphism.

*Guidance:* $D_3$ is the symmetry group of an equilateral triangle. Label the vertices 1, 2, 3. Show that each symmetry of the triangle determines a permutation of the vertices, and that this correspondence is a group isomorphism.

---

**Exercise 1.3.** Show that every subgroup of index 2 is normal.

*Hint:* If $[G : H] = 2$, there are exactly two left cosets: $H$ and $G \setminus H$. Similarly for right cosets. What does this force?

---

**Exercise 1.4.** Prove the cancellation laws directly from the group axioms:
- If $ab = ac$, then $b = c$.
- If $ba = ca$, then $b = c$.
Do not assume the existence of multiplicative inverses beyond what the axioms provide.

---

**Exercise 1.5.** Prove the "one-sided axioms are sufficient" claim: if $G$ is a set with an associative operation, a left identity $e$ (with $ea = a$ for all $a$), and left inverses (for each $a$, exists $b$ with $ba = e$), then $G$ is a group.

*Hint:* First show $a \cdot b = e \Rightarrow b \cdot a = e$ using the left inverse of $b$. Then show $ae = a$.

---

**Exercise 1.6.** The *commutator subgroup* $[G, G]$ is generated by all commutators $[a, b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.

(a) Show $[G, G] \unlhd G$.

(b) Show $G / [G, G]$ is abelian.

(c) Show that if $N \unlhd G$ and $G/N$ is abelian, then $[G, G] \leq N$.

(d) Conclude that $G/[G,G]$ is the "largest abelian quotient" of $G$ (the abelianization).

---

**Exercise 1.7.** Classify all groups of order 4.

*Hint:* There are exactly two: $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ and the Klein four-group $V_4 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. Use the possible orders of elements (each divides 4) to distinguish them.

---

**Exercise 1.8.** Classify all groups of order 6.

*Hint:* Show that any group of order 6 either has an element of order 6 (giving $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$), or has specific combinations of elements of orders 2 and 3 (giving $S_3$).

---

## Section 2: Free Groups and Presentations

**Exercise 2.1.** In $F(\{a, b\})$, determine which of the following hold:
- Is $aba^{-1} = b$? (i.e., do $a$ and $b$ commute in $F_2$?)
- Is $[a, b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1} = e$?

Then re-examine these questions in $\mathbb{Z}^2 = \langle a, b \mid [a,b] \rangle$. What changes?

---

**Exercise 2.2.** Show explicitly that $F(\{a, b\})$ is non-abelian by finding two elements that don't commute.

---

**Exercise 2.3.** Prove the universal property of free groups in detail. Specifically:

Given $f: S \to G$ (a function to a group), define $\bar{f}: F(S) \to G$ by $\bar{f}(s_1^{\epsilon_1} \cdots s_n^{\epsilon_n}) = f(s_1)^{\epsilon_1} \cdots f(s_n)^{\epsilon_n}$. Verify:
(a) $\bar{f}$ is well-defined on reduced words.
(b) $\bar{f}$ is a homomorphism.
(c) $\bar{f}$ is unique among homomorphisms extending $f$.

---

**Exercise 2.4.** The *word problem for free groups*: given two words $w_1, w_2$ over an alphabet, determine whether $w_1 = w_2$ in $F(S)$.

(a) Show this is decidable: describe an algorithm.
(b) What is the time complexity of your algorithm?

---

**Exercise 2.5.** Show that the free group $F(\{a\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. What is the explicit isomorphism? What is the universal property of $\mathbb{Z}$ as a group?

---

**Exercise 2.6.** Compute the fundamental group of a figure-eight (bouquet of two circles) using the van Kampen theorem: $\pi_1$ of a space glued from two pieces is the pushout of their $\pi_1$'s.

The figure-eight is two circles glued at a point. What is the pushout of $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}$ over the trivial group? This should give you $F_2$.

---

**Exercise 2.7 (Challenge).** Show that every subgroup of a free group is free (Nielsen-Schreier theorem). 

*Approach via Cayley graphs:* The Cayley graph of $F(S)$ with generators $S$ is a tree (why?). A subgroup $H$ acts on this tree. A group acting freely on a tree is free. (This last fact is Bass-Serre theory — state it without proof and use it.)

## Section 3: Group Actions

**Exercise 3.1.** Burnside's Lemma states: if $G$ acts on $X$, the number of orbits is $\frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} |X^g|$ where $X^g = \{x \in X \mid g \cdot x = x\}$.

Use Burnside's Lemma to count:
(a) The number of distinct necklaces with 4 beads, each colored with one of 3 colors (where two necklaces are the same if one is a rotation of the other).
(b) The number of distinct colorings of the faces of a cube with 2 colors (rotations identified).

---

**Exercise 3.2.** The class equation is $|G| = |Z(G)| + \sum_i [G : C_G(g_i)]$.

(a) Verify the class equation for $G = S_3$.
(b) Use it to prove that every group of order $p^2$ (prime $p$) is abelian.

---

**Exercise 3.3.** Show that any group of order $2p$ (prime $p > 2$) is either cyclic or isomorphic to the dihedral group $D_p$.

*Hint:* Use Sylow's theorems (which you may state without proof) to show there is a normal subgroup of order $p$, and analyze the possible semidirect products.

---

**Exercise 3.4.** The *orbit-counting theorem* (Burnside's Lemma) is actually a special case of a more general principle. Show: $\sum_{g \in G} |X^g| = \sum_{x \in X} |\text{Stab}(x)|$.

*Hint:* Count pairs $(g, x)$ with $g \cdot x = x$ in two ways.

---

## Section 4: Topology and HoTT Connections

**Exercise 4.1.** Verify that the following topological spaces have the stated fundamental groups by describing explicit generators and relations:

(a) $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$

(b) $\pi_1(\text{torus}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$

(c) $\pi_1(\text{Klein bottle}) = \langle a, b \mid abab^{-1} \rangle$

(d) $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$

---

**Exercise 4.2.** The *word problem for groups* is undecidable in general. Relate this to the following:

(a) Why is the word problem decidable for free groups?

(b) Why is decidability an obstruction for automated theorem proving about arbitrary groups?

(c) In HoTT, proofs of $a = b$ are terms of a type. Why doesn't the undecidability of the word problem prevent HoTT from being a usable proof system?

---

**Exercise 4.3 (Open-Ended).** Describe, in your own words, the "dictionary" between group theory and topology outlined in Section 5. For each row of the correspondence table, give a specific example illustrating both sides.

---

**Exercise 4.4 (Challenge).** Let $X$ be a topological space and $x_0 \in X$. The group $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ depends on the basepoint $x_0$.

(a) Show that if $X$ is path-connected, then $\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \pi_1(X, x_1)$ for any two basepoints $x_0, x_1$.

(b) Is this isomorphism canonical (independent of choices)? Explain.

(c) In HoTT, the corresponding statement is: for a connected type $A$ and any two terms $a, b : A$, the loop spaces $\Omega(A, a)$ and $\Omega(A, b)$ are equivalent. What extra structure would make this equivalence canonical?

---

**Exercise 4.5 (Research Direction).** The *Eilenberg-MacLane spaces* $K(G, 1)$ are topological spaces with $\pi_1 = G$ and all higher homotopy groups trivial. In HoTT, these correspond to the "1-truncation" of a type.

(a) What is $K(\mathbb{Z}, 1)$? What is $K(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, 1)$?

(b) The *classifying space* $BG = K(G, 1)$ classifies principal $G$-bundles. How does this connect group theory to the geometry of fiber bundles?

(c) In HoTT, the type $BG$ can be constructed as a HIT. What generators and relations would you need?
