# Applied Exercises

Simplicial sets may look like pure mathematics, but they carry a surprising amount of computational content. The face and degeneracy maps formalize something ubiquitous in computing: how structured data with boundary information decomposes and reassembles. Commit histories in version control, dependency graphs in build systems, and schema migrations in databases are all, at their core, simplicial structures. The exercises below move between the abstract theory of this chapter and these concrete domains, using each to illuminate the other.

---

## Exercise A.1: Computing the Nerve of a Small Category
*Domain: Graph Theory / Category Theory / Computational Topology*

**Setup:** The nerve construction $N(\mathcal{C})$ assigns to every small category $\mathcal{C}$ a simplicial set. The $n$-simplices of $N(\mathcal{C})$ are the composable strings of $n$ morphisms in $\mathcal{C}$. This is one of the most important constructions in this chapter: it embeds ordinary category theory into the combinatorial world of simplicial sets, and the quasi-category structure on a nerve tells you when the original category has nice properties.

Consider the category $\mathcal{P}$ with three objects $\{a, b, c\}$ and morphisms $f : a \to b$, $g : b \to c$, $h : a \to c$ with $h = g \circ f$ (plus identity morphisms).

**Questions:**

1. Write out all the simplices of $N(\mathcal{P})$ in dimensions 0, 1, and 2. What is the unique 2-simplex, and what are its three faces (given by $d_0$, $d_1$, $d_2$)? Draw the simplicial set as a "filled triangle."

2. Now consider the category $\mathcal{Q}$ with the same objects and morphisms $f$ and $g$, but where $h$ does not exist (there is no composite morphism $a \to c$). Write out all simplices of $N(\mathcal{Q})$ in dimensions 0, 1, and 2. Does $N(\mathcal{Q})$ have a 2-simplex? Draw the simplicial set and compare it to $N(\mathcal{P})$.

3. The inner horn $\Lambda^2_1$ is the simplicial set consisting of two edges $f : 0 \to 1$ and $g : 1 \to 2$ with no 2-simplex filling. Show that a map $\Lambda^2_1 \to N(\mathcal{Q})$ must exist (it picks out the composable pair $f, g$), but that no extension to $\Delta[2] \to N(\mathcal{Q})$ exists. What does this failure mean in categorical terms? Conclude that $N(\mathcal{Q})$ is not a Kan complex, but is still a quasi-category only if you add the composite.

*Abstract concept illustrated: The nerve construction $N : \mathbf{Cat} \to \mathbf{sSet}$ embeds categories into simplicial sets; $N(\mathcal{C})$ is a quasi-category (inner horn filler exists) if and only if the original category is a genuine category (composites exist and are unique). Outer horn filling fails for nerves of non-groupoids, because outer horns ask for inverses.*

## Exercise A.2: Implementing the Singular Chain Complex
*Domain: Computational Algebraic Topology / Scientific Computing*

**Setup:** Given a simplicial set $X$, the singular chain complex $C_*(X)$ is defined by taking $C_n(X) = \mathbb{Z}[X_n]$ (the free abelian group on the $n$-simplices) and boundary maps $\partial_n = \sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i d_i^* : C_n(X) \to C_{n-1}(X)$, where $d_i^* : \mathbb{Z}[X_n] \to \mathbb{Z}[X_{n-1}]$ is the map induced by the face map $d_i$. The homology groups $H_n(X) = \ker(\partial_n)/\mathrm{im}(\partial_{n+1})$ are topological invariants.

Consider the simplicial model of the circle $S^1$ with one 0-simplex $v$, one non-degenerate 1-simplex $e$ (a loop from $v$ to $v$), and only degenerate simplices in higher dimensions.

**Questions:**

1. Write out the chain complex $C_*(S^1_\Delta)$ explicitly for dimensions 0, 1, and 2. What are the free generators of each $C_n$, taking into account degenerate simplices? (Use the fact that the boundary of a degenerate $n$-simplex $s_i(\sigma)$ involves cancellations by the simplicial identities: $d_j s_i = s_{i-1} d_j$ for $j < i$, etc.)

2. Compute $\partial_1(e)$: which 0-simplices does $e$ bound? (Apply $d_0$ and $d_1$ to $e$, using the simplicial set structure of $S^1_\Delta$.) Conclude that $\partial_1(e) = 0$ and hence $H_1(S^1_\Delta) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $[e]$.

3. Write a pseudocode or actual code (Python or Haskell) for a function `homology(X, n)` that takes a finite simplicial set $X$ (given as a list of sets $X_0, X_1, \ldots$ with face maps encoded as functions) and computes $H_n(X)$ as an abelian group (i.e., computes the Smith normal form of the boundary matrices). Test on $S^1_\Delta$ and on the simplicial torus.

*Abstract concept illustrated: The singular chain complex is the composite $\mathbb{Z}[-] \circ (-)_n : \mathbf{sSet} \to \mathbf{Ab}$; the simplicial identities $d_i d_j = d_{j-1} d_i$ (for $i < j$) are exactly what guarantee $\partial \circ \partial = 0$, making this a chain complex.*

## Exercise A.3: Checking the Kan Condition Algorithmically
*Domain: Algorithm Design / Automated Verification*

**Setup:** A simplicial set $X$ (given by finite sets $X_0, \ldots, X_N$ and face maps $d_i : X_n \to X_{n-1}$) is a Kan complex if and only if every horn has a filler. A horn $(\sigma_0, \ldots, \hat{\sigma}_k, \ldots, \sigma_n)$ — a collection of $(n-1)$-simplices that are mutually compatible (i.e., $d_j \sigma_i = d_{i-1} \sigma_j$ for $j < i$, $i, j \neq k$) — must extend to an $n$-simplex $\tau$ with $d_i(\tau) = \sigma_i$ for all $i \neq k$. This is a constraint-satisfaction problem: does a compatible collection of faces extend to a simplex?

**Questions:**

1. Write a function `is_horn(simplices, face_maps, n, k, horn)` that checks whether a given collection `horn = [s_0, ..., None, ..., s_n]` (with a `None` at position $k$) is a valid horn: the given simplices are $(n-1)$-simplices, and $d_j(s_i) = d_{i-1}(s_j)$ for all $i < j$ with neither equal to $k$.

2. Write a function `has_filler(X, face_maps, n, k, horn)` that searches $X_n$ for an $n$-simplex $\tau$ satisfying $d_i(\tau) = s_i$ for all $i \neq k$. For the simplicial circle $S^1_\Delta$, show computationally that the horn $\Lambda^2_1 \to S^1_\Delta$ (given by the pair $(e, e)$ where both edges are the non-degenerate loop $e$) has a filler — namely the degenerate 2-simplex $s_0(e)$ or $s_1(e)$ — and determine which degenerate simplex fills which horn.

3. For the simplicial set $\partial \Delta[2]$ (the boundary of the 2-simplex: three vertices, three edges, no 2-simplex), show that the inner horn $\Lambda^2_1$ does not have a filler. What does this tell you about whether $\partial \Delta[2]$ is a Kan complex? Is it a quasi-category? Compare with $\Delta[2]$ (which has a filler, namely the unique 2-simplex).

*Abstract concept illustrated: The Kan condition is a lifting property — a diagram-filling condition — and it can be checked algorithmically for finite simplicial sets. The fact that it holds for $\mathsf{Sing}(X)$ of any space $X$ is the theorem that makes Kan complexes model homotopy types.*

## Exercise A.4: Commit Histories as Simplicial Sets
*Domain: Software Engineering / Version Control Systems*

**Setup:** A version control repository (like a git repository) has a commit history: a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of commits, where each commit has a set of parent commits and represents a "state" of the codebase. Merges create commits with multiple parents; linear chains create commits with one parent; the root commit has no parents. This DAG structure can be organized as a simplicial set, where composability of patches corresponds to horn-filling.

Model the commit history as follows: 0-simplices are commits, 1-simplices are "transition records" (pairs of a source commit and a target commit with a patch applied), and 2-simplices are "commutativity witnesses" (triangles saying that two sequences of patches produce the same result). The face maps $d_0, d_1 : X_1 \to X_0$ give the target and source of a transition.

**Questions:**

1. Consider a simple linear history: $c_0 \xrightarrow{p_1} c_1 \xrightarrow{p_2} c_2$. Define the simplicial set $X$ corresponding to this history. What are $X_0$, $X_1$? Is there a 2-simplex $\sigma$ with $d_1(\sigma) = p_1$, $d_0(\sigma) = p_2$, and $d_2(\sigma)$ a "direct patch" from $c_0$ to $c_2$? What would it mean for this 2-simplex to exist?

2. In git, applying $p_2 \circ p_1$ gives the same result as the direct transition from $c_0$ to $c_2$ only if the patches are "composable" in the same working tree. Describe, in simplicial terms, what a "merge conflict" corresponds to: is it a horn that has no filler, or a horn with multiple non-equivalent fillers? Discuss the relationship to the uniqueness condition in a nerve (which has at most one filler for inner horns).

3. The Kan condition for this simplicial set says: given any two composable patches, a composite exists. This is a strong condition: real-world patch application may not always compose without conflicts. Propose a weakening of the simplicial set structure that models "patches that may conflict" — perhaps replacing the simplicial set by a partial simplicial set (where face maps are partial functions) or by adding "conflict" as a distinguished 2-simplex indicating a failed horn fill. How does this relate to the theory of partial algebraic structures in higher category theory?

*Abstract concept illustrated: Nerves of categories are simplicial sets where inner horn fillers exist and are unique; real commit histories generalize this by allowing non-unique or missing fillers. The Kan/quasi-category distinction mirrors the distinction between invertible (git revert) and non-invertible operations.*

## Exercise A.5: The Nerve Construction and Database Queries
*Domain: Database Theory / Categorical Data*

**Setup:** The categorical approach to databases (Ryan Wisnesky, David Spivak) models a database schema as a small category $\mathcal{C}$ (objects are tables, morphisms are foreign key constraints), and a database instance as a functor $\mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}$. A query is a natural transformation or a functor between schema categories. The nerve $N(\mathcal{C})$ of the schema category encodes the "join structure" of the database: $n$-simplices of $N(\mathcal{C})$ correspond to $n$-fold joins.

Consider a small database schema with three tables: `Employee`, `Department`, and `Project`, with foreign keys `Employee.dept : Employee → Department` and `Employee.project : Employee → Project` (each employee belongs to one department and one project).

**Questions:**

1. Model this schema as a category $\mathcal{C}$ with three objects and two non-identity morphisms. Write out the simplices of $N(\mathcal{C})$ in dimensions 0, 1, and 2. What is the geometric shape of $N(\mathcal{C})$? (It should look like two edges sharing a common vertex, with no 2-simplex, since there is no morphism `Department → Project`.)

2. A 2-simplex in $N(\mathcal{C})$ would correspond to a composable chain of two foreign key constraints — a "transitive join." For the schema above, does $N(\mathcal{C})$ have a non-degenerate 2-simplex? If you add a foreign key `Project.lead_dept : Project → Department` (each project has a lead department), does a non-degenerate 2-simplex appear? What query does this 2-simplex represent?

3. The horn $\Lambda^2_1$ in $N(\mathcal{C})$ asks: given two composable foreign keys $f : A \to B$ and $g : B \to C$, fill in the composite $g \circ f : A \to C$. In database terms, this is asking: does the schema include the transitive join as a first-class foreign key? Argue that a schema whose nerve is a Kan complex must be a groupoid of tables (every foreign key is invertible). In practice, what kind of schema has this property, and is it useful?

*Abstract concept illustrated: The nerve functor $N : \mathbf{Cat} \to \mathbf{sSet}$ and the Segal condition (a quasi-category's horn-filling property) encode composability. In database contexts, this corresponds to whether a schema "closes under joins."*

## Exercise A.6: Geometric Realization and Shape Recognition
*Domain: Computer Vision / Computational Geometry*

**Setup:** The geometric realization $|X|$ of a simplicial set $X$ is the topological space obtained by gluing geometric simplices (points, line segments, triangles, tetrahedra, ...) according to the face and degeneracy maps. This construction is the bridge between combinatorial data and continuous geometry. In computational geometry and computer vision, meshes are represented as simplicial complexes — the "finite CW-complex" version — and algorithms on them often implicitly compute the geometric realization.

**Questions:**

1. A 3D mesh for a tetrahedron consists of 4 vertices, 6 edges, and 4 triangular faces. Describe this mesh as a simplicial set: give $X_0$, $X_1$, $X_2$, $X_3$, and the face maps $d_i : X_n \to X_{n-1}$. (You may label vertices $0, 1, 2, 3$ and describe each $n$-simplex as an ordered tuple.) Are there any degenerate simplices you need to include?

2. The Euler characteristic $\chi = V - E + F$ (vertices minus edges plus faces) is a topological invariant of the geometric realization, computable from the simplicial set data. For the tetrahedron mesh above, verify that $\chi = 2$ (the Euler characteristic of $S^2$). Now add a handle to the tetrahedron: add one extra edge connecting two non-adjacent vertices and fill in two new triangles. Recompute $\chi$ and identify the resulting surface.

3. In computer vision, "shape matching" often reduces to checking whether two meshes have the same homotopy type. Given two finite simplicial sets $X$ and $Y$ (representing 3D scans of objects), describe an algorithm to decide whether $|X|$ and $|Y|$ are homotopy equivalent using simplicial homotopy theory. What invariants would you compute, and in what order (from cheapest to most expensive)? Discuss the role of $\pi_0$, $H_1$, and $\pi_1$ in distinguishing shapes.

*Abstract concept illustrated: The adjunction $(|\text{-}|, \mathsf{Sing})$ between geometric realization and the singular complex functor; the simplicial set $\mathsf{Sing}(|X|)$ is the fibrant replacement of any simplicial set $X$ in the Quillen model structure.*
