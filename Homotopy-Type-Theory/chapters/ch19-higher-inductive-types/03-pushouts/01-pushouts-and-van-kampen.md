# 3.1 Pushouts and the van Kampen Theorem

## The Universal Gluing Construction

In classical topology, you often build spaces by "gluing" simpler pieces together. The torus is a square with opposite sides identified. The real projective plane is a disc with its boundary identified antipodally. The circle is an interval with its two endpoints identified.

In homotopy theory, this gluing is captured by the *pushout* — the universal construction that "glues" two types together along a common subtype.

## Pushouts

**Definition 3.1 (Pushout).** Given types $A$, $B$, $C$ and maps $f : C \to A$ and $g : C \to B$, the *pushout* $A \sqcup_C B$ (also written $A \sqcup^f_g B$ or just the pushout of $f$ and $g$) is defined by:
- Point constructor: $\mathsf{inl} : A \to A \sqcup_C B$
- Point constructor: $\mathsf{inr} : B \to A \sqcup_C B$
- Path constructor: $\mathsf{glue}(c) : \mathsf{inl}(f(c)) =_{A \sqcup_C B} \mathsf{inr}(g(c))$ for each $c : C$

The path constructor $\mathsf{glue}$ is the "gluing" — it identifies $f(c)$ (the image of $c$ in $A$) with $g(c)$ (the image of $c$ in $B$).

**The universal property of pushouts.** A function $h : A \sqcup_C B \to D$ is the same as:
- $h_A : A \to D$ (the restriction to $A$)
- $h_B : B \to D$ (the restriction to $B$)
- $H : \prod_{c:C} h_A(f(c)) = h_B(g(c))$ (the two functions agree on $C$)

This is the categorical pushout universal property: a map out of the pushout is a pair of maps out of $A$ and $B$ that agree on $C$.

**The diagram.** The pushout $A \sqcup_C B$ is the colimit of the span:
$$A \xleftarrow{f} C \xrightarrow{g} B$$

It's the "best type that makes the square commute":
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
C & \xrightarrow{g} & B \\
f \downarrow & & \downarrow \mathsf{inr} \\
A & \xrightarrow{\mathsf{inl}} & A \sqcup_C B
\end{array}$$

The pushout makes this square a "homotopy pushout square" (commuting up to homotopy, and universal with this property).

## Special Cases of Pushouts

**Coproduct:** $C = \mathbf{0}$ (the empty type). Then $A \sqcup_\mathbf{0} B = A + B$ (disjoint union). There are no gluing paths since $\mathbf{0}$ has no elements.

**Suspension:** $A = B = \mathbf{1}$ (the unit type), $f$ and $g$ are the unique maps $C \to \mathbf{1}$. Then $\Sigma C = \mathbf{1} \sqcup_C \mathbf{1}$ — the suspension is the pushout of $C$ along the two constant maps to a point.

**Cofiber:** Given $f : C \to A$ and $g : C \to \mathbf{1}$, the pushout $A \sqcup_C \mathbf{1}$ is the *cofiber* of $f$ — the space $A$ with the image of $f$ collapsed to a point. For $f : \mathsf{N} \hookrightarrow A$ (north pole of a suspension), the cofiber is $\Sigma A$ (the reduced suspension).

**Join:** $A * B = A \sqcup_{A \times B} B$ where the maps are the two projections $\pi_1 : A \times B \to A$ and $\pi_2 : A \times B \to B$.

**Mapping cone:** For $f : A \to B$, the mapping cone $Cf = B \sqcup_A (A \times \mathbb{I})$ where $A \times \mathbb{I} \to A$ is the projection and $A \times \mathbb{I} \to B$ is $\mathsf{pr}_2 \circ (\mathsf{id}_A \times \{1\})$... this is the "cone" over $A$ attached to $B$ via $f$.

## The Seifert-van Kampen Theorem

The van Kampen theorem is one of the most important theorems in algebraic topology: it computes the fundamental group of a pushout in terms of the fundamental groups of the pieces.

**Classical statement:** If $X = U \cup V$ (a topological space covered by open sets $U$ and $V$) with $U, V, U \cap V$ path-connected, then:
$$\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \pi_1(U, x_0) *_{\pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)} \pi_1(V, x_0)$$

(the amalgamated free product of groups).

**HoTT version:** Van Kampen holds for the pushout HIT:

**Theorem 3.2 (van Kampen in HoTT).** For a pushout $X = A \sqcup_C B$ with $A$, $B$, $C$ path-connected and basepoint-preserving maps $f : C \to A$, $g : C \to B$:
$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(A) *_{\pi_1(C)} \pi_1(B)$$

The right side is the *amalgamated free product* of groups: the group generated by $\pi_1(A)$ and $\pi_1(B)$ subject to the relation that $f_*(\gamma) = g_*(\gamma)$ for each $\gamma \in \pi_1(C)$.

**Proof strategy.** Use the encode-decode method:
1. Define $\mathsf{code} : X \to X \to \mathsf{Type}$ — for each pair of points $x, y \in X$, $\mathsf{code}(x, y)$ is the group of "formal words" in $\pi_1(A)$ and $\pi_1(B)$.
2. Use the pushout universal property to define $\mathsf{code}$ from $\mathsf{code}_A$ and $\mathsf{code}_B$, glued along $\mathsf{code}_C$.
3. Show encode and decode are inverses.

The full proof is in the HoTT Book (Chapter 8.7). The key insight is that the "formal words" in the amalgamated free product exactly match the paths in the pushout.

## Examples of van Kampen

**The circle.** $S^1 = \mathsf{D}^1_+ \sqcup_{S^0} \mathsf{D}^1_-$ (two arcs glued along their endpoints). By van Kampen:
$$\pi_1(S^1) \cong \pi_1(\mathsf{D}^1_+) *_{\pi_1(S^0)} \pi_1(\mathsf{D}^1_-) \cong \mathbf{1} *_{\mathbf{1} \sqcup \mathbf{1}} \mathbf{1}$$

Since $S^0$ has two components with fundamental group $\mathbf{1}$, and the two arcs are contractible (trivial fundamental group), the amalgamated product is the free group on one generator (the gluing relation gives one generator $\mathsf{loop}$). So $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$. ✓

**The torus.** $T^2 = S^1 \sqcup_{S^1 \vee S^1} S^1 \times \mathsf{D}^2$... the torus can be seen as a circle crossed with a circle. A cleaner decomposition: $T^2 = A \sqcup_C B$ where $A$ and $B$ are the "top hemisphere" and "bottom hemisphere" (both cylinders $S^1 \times [0,1]$) and $C = S^1$ is the equator.

By van Kampen: $\pi_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z} *_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ (the free abelian group on two generators, because the relation says the two generators commute). ✓

**The wedge $S^1 \vee S^1$.** The wedge is the pushout $S^1 \sqcup_{\mathbf{1}} S^1$ (identifying one point of each circle). By van Kampen:
$$\pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} *_{\mathbf{1}} \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} = F_2$$
the free group on two generators. ✓

**The real projective plane $\mathbb{RP}^2$.** $\mathbb{RP}^2 = \mathsf{D}^2 \sqcup_{S^1} \mathsf{D}^2$ where the gluing map doubles the winding. By van Kampen:
$$\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$$
generated by the gluing relation $a^2 = 1$. ✓

## Higher-Dimensional van Kampen

The van Kampen theorem in HoTT can be generalized beyond $\pi_1$:

**Theorem 3.3 (Higher van Kampen).** For pushouts, the higher homotopy groups $\pi_n$ satisfy a pushout-like formula involving $\pi_n(A)$, $\pi_n(B)$, $\pi_n(C)$, and $\pi_{n-1}$ of all three (the "Mayer-Vietoris sequence" in homotopy theory).

The precise statement involves the homotopy pushout of groups, which is more complex than the simple amalgamated product that appears for $\pi_1$.

In HoTT, this can be derived from the *Blakers-Massey theorem* (Chapter 20), which gives connectivity estimates for pushout squares.

## Pushouts and Colimits

Pushouts are a special case of *colimits* — the "gluing" or "combining" operations in category theory.

**General colimits as HITs.** Any colimit can be defined as a HIT:
- Objects (nodes in the diagram): point constructors
- Morphisms (edges in the diagram): path constructors
- Commutativity: 2-path constructors
- Higher coherences: higher path constructors

The pushout $A \sqcup_C B$ is the colimit of the span diagram $A \leftarrow C \rightarrow B$. More complex diagrams give more complex HITs.

**Sequential colimits.** The *sequential colimit* $\mathsf{colim}(A_0 \to A_1 \to A_2 \to \cdots)$ is a HIT with:
- Point constructors: $\mathsf{inj}_n : A_n \to \mathsf{colim}$ for each $n$
- Path constructors: $\mathsf{seq}(n, a) : \mathsf{inj}_n(a) = \mathsf{inj}_{n+1}(f_n(a))$ for each $a : A_n$

Sequential colimits are used in defining spectra (stable homotopy theory) and in constructing the classifying spaces of groups.

## Pushouts in Proof Assistants

In Cubical Agda, the pushout is defined as:
```agda
data Pushout {A B C : Type} (f : C → A) (g : C → B) : Type where
  inl : A → Pushout f g
  inr : B → Pushout f g
  push : (c : C) → inl (f c) ≡ inr (g c)
```

The computation rule for the `push` constructor is handled by the cubical interval, giving definitional computation.

In Lean 4, pushouts are available in Mathlib as `Pushout` or can be defined using `Quot`.

## Summary

The pushout HIT is the fundamental "gluing" construction in HoTT:
- It implements the homotopy-theoretic pushout (colimit) of a span
- Suspension, join, coproduct, and cofiber are all special cases
- The van Kampen theorem computes $\pi_1$ of a pushout
- Higher-dimensional van Kampen follows from Blakers-Massey
- Any colimit diagram gives a HIT

The power of HITs is that they make the "gluing" that topologists do informally into a precise type-theoretic operation with a universal property. The van Kampen theorem is not just a theorem in HoTT — it's a direct consequence of the universal property of the pushout, made rigorous by the HIT eliminator.
