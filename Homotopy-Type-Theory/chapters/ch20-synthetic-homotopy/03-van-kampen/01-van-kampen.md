# 3.1 The Seifert-van Kampen Theorem in HoTT

## Computing π₁ of Glued Spaces

The van Kampen theorem tells us how to compute the fundamental group of a pushout:

If $X = A \sqcup_C B$ (with path-connected pieces), then $\pi_1(X) = \pi_1(A) *_{\pi_1(C)} \pi_1(B)$ — the amalgamated free product.

In HoTT, this theorem has a clean synthetic proof using the universal property of the pushout HIT and the encode-decode method.

## Amalgamated Free Products

**Definition 3.1 (Amalgamated free product).** For groups $G_1$, $G_2$, and a group $H$ with homomorphisms $\phi_1 : H \to G_1$ and $\phi_2 : H \to G_2$, the *amalgamated free product* $G_1 *_H G_2$ is the group generated by $G_1 \sqcup G_2$ subject to the relations $\phi_1(h) = \phi_2(h)$ for all $h : H$.

Equivalently: $G_1 *_H G_2$ is the pushout of $G_1 \leftarrow H \rightarrow G_2$ in the category of groups.

**Examples:**
- $H = \mathbf{1}$ (trivial group): $G_1 *_\mathbf{1} G_2 = G_1 * G_2$ (free product)
- $G_1 = G_2 = G$ and $\phi_1 = \phi_2 = \mathsf{id}$: $G *_G G = G$ (trivial amalgam)
- $G_1 = G_2 = \mathbb{Z}$ and $H = \mathbb{Z}$ with $\phi_1(n) = 2n$, $\phi_2(n) = 3n$: various interesting groups

## The Theorem

**Theorem 3.2 (Seifert-van Kampen, HoTT version).** Let $f : C \to A$ and $g : C \to B$ with $X = A \sqcup_C B$ the pushout. Suppose $A$, $B$, $C$ are path-connected. Then for any basepoint $x_0 \in X$:

$$\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \pi_1(A, x_0) *_{\pi_1(C, x_0)} \pi_1(B, x_0)$$

where the amalgam is over the induced homomorphisms $f_* : \pi_1(C) \to \pi_1(A)$ and $g_* : \pi_1(C) \to \pi_1(B)$.

## The Proof Strategy

The proof in HoTT uses the encode-decode method generalized to the pushout:

**Step 1: Define a "code groupoid."** Instead of a type family $\mathsf{code} : X \to \mathsf{Type}$, define a type family $\mathsf{code} : X \to X \to \mathsf{Type}$ — a "path code" for paths between any two points.

$\mathsf{code}(x, y)$ should be the "group element of the amalgamated free product corresponding to paths from $x$ to $y$."

**Step 2: Use the pushout universal property.** To define $\mathsf{code}(x, y)$ using the pushout, we need compatible definitions on $A$ and $B$:
- $\mathsf{code}_A : A \to A \to \mathsf{Type}$: the code for paths within $A$
- $\mathsf{code}_B : B \to B \to \mathsf{Type}$: the code for paths within $B$
- Compatibility: $\mathsf{code}_A(f(c), f(c')) \simeq \mathsf{code}_C(c, c') \simeq \mathsf{code}_B(g(c), g(c'))$ for $c, c' : C$

The pushout universal property then gives $\mathsf{code} : X \to X \to \mathsf{Type}$.

**Step 3: Encode and decode.** Define encode and decode maps (as before), and show they're inverses.

**The "formal words" in the amalgam.** The code type $\mathsf{code}(x_0, x_0)$ at the basepoint is the type of "formal words" in $\pi_1(A)$ and $\pi_1(B)$, subject to the amalgam relations. This is exactly the amalgamated free product $\pi_1(A) *_{\pi_1(C)} \pi_1(B)$.

## Examples of Van Kampen Computations

**The circle.** $S^1 = \mathsf{arc}_+ \sqcup_{S^0} \mathsf{arc}_-$ where both arcs are contractible and $S^0 = \mathsf{Bool}$ has two components.

By van Kampen: $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \pi_1(\mathsf{arc}_+) *_{\pi_1(S^0)} \pi_1(\mathsf{arc}_-)$.

Since each arc is contractible: $\pi_1(\mathsf{arc}_\pm) = \mathbf{1}$.
Since $S^0$ has two components, $\pi_1$ of the image... this needs more care. The "correct" van Kampen decomposition for $S^1$ uses a different covering.

Alternatively: $S^1 = \Sigma \mathsf{Bool}$ and van Kampen for the suspension gives $\pi_1(\Sigma A) \cong (\pi_1(A) * \pi_1(A)) / \text{(relations from }A)$... which for $A = \mathsf{Bool}$ gives $\pi_1(\Sigma \mathsf{Bool}) = \mathbb{Z}$. ✓

**Torus.** $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$ can be decomposed as a pushout. The van Kampen theorem gives:
$$\pi_1(T^2) = \pi_1(S^1 \times S^1) = \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}^2$$

(Using the product formula $\pi_1(A \times B) = \pi_1(A) \times \pi_1(B)$.)

**Wedge of circles.** $S^1 \vee S^1 = S^1 \sqcup_{\mathbf{1}} S^1$ (pushout identifying one point in each circle). Van Kampen:
$$\pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1) = \mathbb{Z} *_{\mathbf{1}} \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} = F_2$$

the free group on two generators. ✓

**Genus-$g$ surface.** The genus-$g$ surface $\Sigma_g$ can be decomposed as a polygon with edge identifications. Van Kampen gives:
$$\pi_1(\Sigma_g) = \langle a_1, b_1, \ldots, a_g, b_g \mid [a_1, b_1] \cdots [a_g, b_g] = 1 \rangle$$

where $[a, b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ is the commutator. ✓

## The Proof at a High Level

The full proof of van Kampen in HoTT follows these steps:

1. **Connectivity.** Assume $A$, $B$, $C$ are 0-connected (path-connected). The pushout $X = A \sqcup_C B$ is also 0-connected.

2. **Code family.** Define a "path code" groupoid on $X$ using the pushout eliminator. The code at $(x_0, x_0)$ is the amalgamated free product $G = \pi_1(A) *_{\pi_1(C)} \pi_1(B)$.

3. **Encode.** For any path $p : x_0 = y$ in $X$, $\mathsf{encode}(p) : \mathsf{code}(x_0, y)$ is defined by induction on the path, using the HIT eliminator for the pushout.

4. **Decode.** For any $w : \mathsf{code}(x_0, y)$, $\mathsf{decode}(y, w) : x_0 = y$ is defined by interpreting words in the amalgam as paths in $X$.

5. **Inverse.** Show the composites are homotopic to identities using J and the HIT computation rules.

6. **Conclude.** $\Omega(X, x_0) \simeq G = \pi_1(A) *_{\pi_1(C)} \pi_1(B)$.

## Van Kampen and Higher Homotopy

The van Kampen theorem generalizes to higher homotopy groups via the *higher van Kampen theorem* (proven by Brown, Loday, and others classically; and in HoTT using the Blakers-Massey theorem):

**Long exact sequence of a pushout.** For $X = A \sqcup_C B$, there is a long exact sequence (Mayer-Vietoris sequence):
$$\cdots \to \pi_{n+1}(X) \to \pi_n(C) \to \pi_n(A) \times \pi_n(B) \to \pi_n(X) \to \pi_{n-1}(C) \to \cdots$$

This is the higher-dimensional analogue of van Kampen. The exact sequence is a consequence of the Blakers-Massey theorem (Chapter 20.4) and the long exact sequence of a fibration.

## Why Synthetic Proofs Matter

The HoTT proof of van Kampen is cleaner than the classical proof in several ways:

**1. No need for open sets.** The classical proof requires the technical condition "the interiors of $A$ and $B$ cover $X$" (or "the maps $C \to A$, $C \to B$ are cofibrations"). In HoTT, the pushout is defined with exact equality conditions (the path constructor $\mathsf{glue}$), avoiding this technicality.

**2. Constructive.** The HoTT proof gives an explicit isomorphism $\pi_1(X) \cong G$ — a computable bijection, not just a proof of isomorphism.

**3. Works for higher $n$.** With the Blakers-Massey theorem, the encode-decode method generalizes to higher $n$, giving higher van Kampen. The classical proof only directly gives the $\pi_1$ case.

**4. Machine-verifiable.** The proof has been formalized in Agda and Lean, giving certainty at the highest level.

Van Kampen is one of the great theorems of algebraic topology — and its synthetic proof in HoTT is one of the great achievements of the program. It shows that HoTT can not only state and prove classical theorems, but do so with new clarity and generality.
