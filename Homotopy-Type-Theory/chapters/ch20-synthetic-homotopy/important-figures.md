# Important Figures

## Daniel R. Licata (dates not public)
*Type theorist; co-developer of the encode-decode method; pioneer of formalized synthetic homotopy theory.*

Daniel Licata received his PhD from Carnegie Mellon University under Robert Harper and is currently a professor at Wesleyan University. His research spans the computational content of HoTT proofs, the design of modal and directed type theories, and the formalization of homotopy theory in proof assistants. He was one of the most active contributors to the HoTT effort during the crucial period 2010–2015, when the main techniques of synthetic homotopy theory were being developed.

Licata's defining contribution to this chapter is the encode-decode method, developed jointly with Michael Shulman and first applied in their 2013 LICS paper "Calculating the Fundamental Group of the Circle in Homotopy Type Theory." The method works as follows: to compute the loop space $\Omega(X, x_0)$ of a HIT $X$, define a "code" type family $\mathsf{code} : X \to \mathsf{Type}$ by specifying $\mathsf{code}(x_0)$ to be the expected group $G$ and transporting along the path constructors of $X$ using the expected group operations. Then show that $\Omega(X, x_0) \simeq G$ via explicit encode and decode maps. In the circle case, $\mathsf{code}(\mathsf{base}) = \mathbb{Z}$ and the encode map sends a loop $p$ to the winding number $\mathsf{transport}^\mathsf{code}(p, 0)$. Licata and Shulman proved encode and decode are inverse, establishing $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$. Licata subsequently extended the encode-decode method to compute $\pi_n(S^n)$ for all $n$ (each using the corresponding Hopf fibration) and applied it to Eilenberg-MacLane spaces. He has also developed "2D type theory" (a directed type theory for encoding 2-dimensional categorical diagrams) and has worked on modal type theory for cohesion and other modalities.

## Michael Shulman (1980–present)
*Type theorist and category theorist; co-developer of encode-decode; contributor to Freudenthal and Blakers-Massey in HoTT.*

Michael Shulman is a professor at the University of San Diego; his background and broad contributions to HoTT are described in Chapter 19. For this chapter, beyond the encode-decode method developed with Licata, Shulman contributed to the formalized proof of the Freudenthal suspension theorem in HoTT and to the Seifert-van Kampen theorem. He also contributed to the HoTT Book's Chapter 8, which is the standard reference for the theorems of this chapter.

Shulman's most foundational contribution to synthetic homotopy theory is arguably his proof (2019) that every $(\infty,1)$-topos models Book HoTT with universes. This establishes that synthetic homotopy theory takes place in a fully general mathematical universe: any theorem provable in HoTT holds in every $(\infty,1)$-topos, not just in simplicial sets. The synthetic proofs of $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$, Freudenthal, and Hopf are therefore not just "computations in a specific model" but genuinely model-independent homotopy-theoretic theorems. This perspective — that HoTT is a logic for $\infty$-toposes, and synthetic homotopy theory is topos-theoretic algebraic topology — is Shulman's most significant contribution to the conceptual foundations of the field.

## Guillaume Brunerie (1988–present)
*Type theorist; author of the $\pi_4(S^3)$ synthetic computation; pioneer of the Brunerie number; central figure in the HoTT synthesis program.*

Guillaume Brunerie completed his PhD at Université Nice Sophia-Antipolis in 2016 and has held positions at the Institute for Advanced Study and Stockholm University, where he works on the formalization of higher mathematics and the homotopy groups of spheres. His PhD thesis "On the Homotopy Groups of Spheres in Homotopy Type Theory" is the most ambitious single piece of work in synthetic homotopy theory to date, proving synthetically $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_2(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_3(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$ (via the Hopf fibration), and reducing $\pi_4(S^3) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ to a single numerical computation.

The technical heart of Brunerie's thesis is the construction of the *James construction* $J(A)$ (the free associative multiplication on a pointed type $A$) as a HIT, and the *EHP sequence* — a long exact sequence $\cdots \to \pi_n(S^{k-1}) \xrightarrow{E} \pi_{n+1}(S^k) \xrightarrow{H} \pi_{n+1}(S^{2k-1}) \xrightarrow{P} \pi_{n-1}(S^{k-1}) \to \cdots$ — which inductively computes homotopy groups of spheres. Using this machinery, Brunerie showed that $\pi_4(S^3)$ is generated by the composition of the Hopf fibration $\eta : S^3 \to S^2$ with its suspension $\Sigma\eta : S^4 \to S^3$, and that the order of this generator is a specific integer, which he called the "Brunerie number" $\beta$. He conjectured $\beta = 2$, which would prove $\pi_4(S^3) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The verification of this conjecture — that the HoTT-defined integer $\beta$ normalizes to 2 — was completed in Cubical Agda by Ljungström and Mörtberg in 2023, making the proof complete. Brunerie has also contributed to the development of the `agda/cubical` library and to synthetic cohomology theory.

## Egbert Rijke (dates not public)
*Type theorist; developer of synthetic homotopy theory techniques; author of the preeminent HoTT textbook.*

Egbert Rijke completed his PhD under Thierry Coquand at the University of Gothenburg and subsequently held positions at the University of Ljubljana and the University of Illinois at Chicago, where he is now a professor. His research focuses on the foundations of HoTT, synthetic homotopy theory, and the formalization of mathematics in proof assistants. He is the author of the most comprehensive modern textbook on HoTT, *Introduction to Homotopy Type Theory* (Cambridge University Press, to appear), which has been widely used as lecture notes for HoTT courses.

Rijke's contributions to this chapter include his work on the Freudenthal suspension theorem in HoTT. In his master's thesis and subsequent work, he developed a clean synthetic proof of Freudenthal using the join construction and connectivity arguments — a proof that is both fully constructive and more streamlined than earlier approaches. He also developed the theory of *truncated types* and their interaction with synthetic homotopy (showing, for instance, that the $n$-truncation of a type is its $n$th Postnikov section), and he worked on the theory of *connected types* and their characterization via the suspend-loop adjunction. His textbook treatment of these topics is the clearest available exposition.

## Axel Ljungström (dates not public) and Anders Mörtberg (dates not public)
*Type theorists; authors of the machine-checked verification of $\pi_4(S^3) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ in Cubical Agda.*

Axel Ljungström is a PhD student at Stockholm University under Anders Mörtberg. Anders Mörtberg is a professor at Stockholm University, one of the primary developers of Cubical Agda, and a contributor to cubical type theory from its inception (as a co-author of the "Cubical Type Theory" paper with Cohen, Coquand, and Huber). Their 2023 LICS paper "Formalizing π₄(S³) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ and Computing a Brunerie Number in Cubical Agda" completes the program Brunerie initiated.

Their technical contribution is to take Brunerie's proof sketch — which reduced $\pi_4(S^3) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ to the statement that a specific HoTT-defined integer $\beta$ equals 2 — and verify the entire argument in Cubical Agda. This required not only formalizing the Brunerie number as a computable term in cubical type theory (possible because univalence is computational in cubical type theory, giving `ua` a reduction rule), but also simplifying Brunerie's original proof at several points where the original argument was too complex to formalize directly. In particular, they found a simplified computation of the Brunerie number that normalizes faster, making the Cubical Agda typechecking feasible. The result is a complete, machine-checked proof — with no remaining lemmas left to the reader — that $\pi_4(S^3) = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

## Evan Cavallo (dates not public) and Robert Harper (dates not public)
*Type theorists; developers of higher inductive types and synthetic homotopy theory in cubical computational type theory.*

Evan Cavallo completed his PhD at Carnegie Mellon University under Robert Harper and is currently at Stockholm University. Robert Harper is a professor at Carnegie Mellon University and one of the most influential type theorists of the past thirty years, responsible for foundational work on Standard ML, logical frameworks (LF), and computational type theory.

Their primary contribution to this chapter is the 2019 paper "Higher Inductive Types in Cubical Computational Type Theory," which establishes that HITs in cubical type theory satisfy strict (definitional) computation rules for all constructors, including path constructors. This is crucial for synthetic homotopy theory: the winding number computation in $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ requires transporting along `loop`, and in Book HoTT this transport is only propositionally equal to `succ`. In cubical type theory with Cavallo-Harper HITs, it is *definitionally* equal to `succ`, making the computation truly computational. They also developed the theory of *parametric cubical type theory* and have worked on higher-dimensional type theories suited to synthetic homotopy computations.

## Heinz Hopf (1894–1971)
*Classical homotopy theorist; discoverer of the Hopf fibration; founder of modern algebraic topology.*

Heinz Hopf was a German-Swiss mathematician who made foundational contributions to algebraic topology and differential geometry. He was a student of Ludwig Bieberbach and Erhard Schmidt, and spent most of his career at ETH Zurich. His work on the classification of maps between spheres, beginning in the 1920s and 1930s, established the foundations of what would become homotopy theory.

Hopf discovered the fibration $S^1 \to S^3 \xrightarrow{h} S^2$ in 1931, now called the Hopf fibration. This was a revelation: it showed that $\pi_3(S^2) \neq 0$, i.e., there are non-contractible maps from $S^3$ to $S^2$. Before Hopf's discovery, it was not known whether maps between spheres of different dimensions could be non-trivial. The Hopf fibration is constructed by viewing $S^3 \subset \mathbb{C}^2$ and $S^2 \subset \mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{R}$, and defining $h(z_1, z_2) = (2z_1\bar{z}_2, |z_1|^2 - |z_2|^2)$ — a smooth surjection whose fibers are exactly the orbits of the $S^1$-action $(z_1, z_2) \mapsto (e^{i\theta}z_1, e^{i\theta}z_2)$. This gives a fiber bundle $S^1 \to S^3 \to S^2$, whose long exact sequence of homotopy groups shows $\pi_3(S^2) \supseteq \mathbb{Z}$.

The Hopf fibration appears in this chapter as the central object in Brunerie's computation of $\pi_3(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$. In HoTT, it is constructed synthetically: $S^3$ is defined as the join $S^1 * S^1$, and the Hopf fibration is a map $S^3 \to S^2$ constructed using the $S^1$-action on itself by multiplication. The long exact sequence in HoTT is derived from the fiber sequence of the Hopf fibration. Hopf's 90-year-old discovery thus lives at the heart of contemporary synthetic homotopy theory.
