# Chapter 26: Research Frontiers — Open Problems and the Path Forward

## The End of the Curriculum, the Beginning of Research

This is the final chapter of a long curriculum. You have spent many chapters building mathematical foundations: logic, set theory, algebra, analysis, category theory. Then the core of HoTT: identity types, h-levels, univalence, higher inductive types. Then the advanced material: synthetic homotopy theory, proof assistants, cubical type theory, simplicial type theory, modal HoTT.

This chapter is different from all the others. It doesn't teach settled mathematics. It maps the *frontier* — the open problems, the research programs, the questions that remain unanswered. It's also a guide to the practical side of research: how to read the literature, how to find your problem, how to engage with the community.

The HoTT community is small and young. The HoTT Book was published in 2013. Many of the papers in this curriculum are from 2015–2024. Some of the researchers whose work appears in these pages are in their 30s. The field is new enough that a motivated person, working through this curriculum seriously, has a realistic chance of contributing something genuinely new within a few years.

This chapter tells you what the "something new" could look like.

## Chapter Roadmap

**Section 1: Open Problems** — The canonical open problems in HoTT, synthetic homotopy theory, and foundations. Which problems are approachable and which are deep?

**Section 2: Formalization Frontiers** — What remains to be formalized in Cubical Agda, Lean 4/Mathlib, and Rzk? How to contribute.

**Section 3: How to Contribute** — Reading the literature, engaging with the community, writing and publishing results.
