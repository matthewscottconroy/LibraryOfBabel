# Categories of Being

The world contains many different kinds of things: individual substances (this horse, this human), qualities (redness, courage), quantities (five meters, twice as large), relations (being to the left of, being married to), places, times, actions, and passions. Are these genuinely different in some fundamental ontological sense, or are the apparent differences artifacts of grammar and convention? And if there are genuinely different categories of being, what are they, how many are there, and what unifies them?

Aristotle was the first to give a systematic answer. His ten categories — substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, position, state, action, passion — represent his attempt to enumerate the most general classes under which anything that exists can fall. Substance has priority among these: qualities, relations, and the rest exist only as features of substances and are ontologically dependent on them. This hierarchical structure — with substance as the primary kind of being and everything else as derivative — defines Aristotelian ontology.

This section traces the theory of categories from Aristotle through its post-Aristotelian transformations. Kant reinvented categories as pure concepts of the understanding — conditions of the possibility of experience rather than most general features of being — and his table of twelve categories structured transcendental idealism. Hegel sought to derive the categories dialectically, showing how each category generates its own negation and how the sequence of categories culminates in the Absolute. Contemporary analytic ontology has returned to something closer to the Aristotelian spirit, asking which categories are genuinely fundamental and how they relate.

The contemporary debate about objects, properties, relations, facts, events, and processes is not merely a taxonomy of the world's furniture; it determines what kinds of things can figure in causal explanations, what kinds of things can ground modal facts, and what kinds of things our best scientific theories are committed to. Understanding categories of being is understanding the architecture of ontology itself.
