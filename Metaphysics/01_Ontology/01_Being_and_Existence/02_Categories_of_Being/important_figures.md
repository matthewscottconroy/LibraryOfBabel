# Important Figures

## Aristotle (384–322 BCE)
The originator of category theory in ontology. His *Categories* and *Metaphysics* remain foundational texts. Aristotle's insistence that individual substances are ontologically primary, combined with his theory of immanent universals, defined the terms of debate for two millennia.

## Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)
Kant's table of categories in the *Critique of Pure Reason* transformed the project by relocating categories from mind-independent reality to the cognitive structure of the understanding. His influence on all subsequent discussion of ontological categories—including both neo-Kantian and anti-Kantian responses—is incalculable.

## G. W. F. Hegel (1770–1831)
Hegel's *Science of Logic* proposed a radically dynamic conception of categories, arguing that they develop dialectically from one another through their internal contradictions. His influence has been felt primarily in Continental philosophy but is increasingly engaged in analytic circles.

## Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)
Husserl's phenomenological analysis of the intentional structures of consciousness opened a new approach to ontological categories—one that begins from the structures of experience rather than from science or logic. His *Logical Investigations* and *Ideas I* are the central texts.

## Martin Heidegger (1889–1976)
Heidegger's *Being and Time* (1927) argued that traditional ontological categories presuppose an artificially theoretical attitude and that the primary structures of existence—being-in-the-world, care, temporality—are not captured by the substance-property scheme. His "destruction" of the history of ontology opened up a distinctly Continental approach to the question of Being.

## D. M. Armstrong (1926–2014)
Armstrong's systematic development of a naturalist, immanent-realist ontology—organized around states of affairs, universals, and laws of nature—has been one of the most influential contributions to analytic ontology of the late twentieth century. His trilogy *Universals and Scientific Realism* (1978) and *A World of States of Affairs* (1997) are essential reading.

## Donald Davidson (1917–2003)
Davidson's arguments for an event ontology in essays like "The Individuation of Events" (1969) established events as a central category in analytic ontology and provided the framework for his influential theories of action and mental causation.
