# Important Terms and Ideas: Categories of Being

**Ontological category**: A most general kind of entity — a division of being that is fundamental and not reducible to more basic kinds. Examples include: substance, property, relation, event, set, proposition.

**Substance**: In the Aristotelian tradition, a concrete, independently existing individual — the paradigm category of being. A substance exists in its own right, while other entities (properties, events) depend on substances for their existence.

**Accident**: In Aristotelian ontology, a property or feature that belongs to a substance but is not essential to it — something the substance could lose or never have had while remaining what it is. Contrasts with essential properties.

**Attribute**: A property or characteristic that can be predicated of a subject. In Lowe's four-category ontology, attributes are universals (repeatable entities), contrasting with modes, which are particular property-instances.

**Mode (trope)**: A particular, non-repeatable property-instance: this particular whiteness of this particular surface. Distinguished from universals by being unrepeatable and from substrata by being property-like rather than thing-like.

**Category mistake**: Ryle's term for the error of attributing to something a property that belongs to a different logical or ontological category — e.g., asking what the "average plumber" weighs as if "the average plumber" referred to a particular individual.

**Formal ontology**: The study of the most general structure of reality — the a priori laws governing all possible objects and their combinations, independently of any specific material content.

**Dependence**: An ontological relation in which one entity's existence or nature requires another entity. Ontological dependence is distinct from causal dependence: a property-instance depends on its bearer without being caused by it.
