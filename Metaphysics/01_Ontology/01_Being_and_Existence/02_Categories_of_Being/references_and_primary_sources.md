# References and Primary Sources: Categories of Being

## Foundational Texts

Aristotle. *Categories*. Trans. Ackrill. Oxford: Clarendon Press. The original systematic theory of ontological categories: substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, position, state, action, passion.

Kant, Immanuel. *Critique of Pure Reason*. Trans. Guyer and Wood. Cambridge University Press, 1998. The "Table of Categories" in the Analytic attempts a systematic derivation of the pure concepts of the understanding.

Husserl, Edmund. *Logical Investigations*. Trans. Findlay. Routledge, 2001. Vol. 2 develops a formal ontology of categories based on the a priori laws governing combinations of meanings and objects.

## Contemporary Works

Lowe, E.J. *The Four-Category Ontology*. Oxford University Press, 2006. Defends a systematic neo-Aristotelian ontology with four fundamental categories: universal kinds, individual substances, attributes, and modes.

Thomasson, Amie. *Ontology Made Easy*. Oxford University Press, 2015. Argues that many disputes about categories are best resolved by careful attention to the ordinary rules governing our use of category terms.

Koslicki, Kathrin. "Categories." In Tahko (ed.), *Contemporary Aristotelian Metaphysics*. Cambridge University Press, 2012. An overview of the methodology and results of categorial ontology.
