# Exercises and Thought Experiments

## Exercise 1: Existence vs. Non-Existence
For each of the following, determine whether it exists and, if you say it does not exist, explain what is going on when we make seemingly true claims about it:
- Sherlock Holmes
- The largest prime number
- The average American voter
- Wednesday (the day)
- Phlogiston
- Hamlet's father's ghost

## Exercise 2: Meinong's Challenge
Meinong argued that the golden mountain, though non-existent, has properties: it is golden and mountainous. Russell replied that this violates the law of non-contradiction. Evaluate this exchange. Does Russell's theory of descriptions adequately handle all the cases Meinong was worried about? Are there cases where we seem to make true statements about nonexistent objects that Russell's paraphrase strategy cannot handle naturally?

## Exercise 3: Free Logic in Practice
In classical logic, from "Fa" we can infer "∃x(Fx)." In free logic, we cannot—we also need the premise "E!a" (a exists). Apply this distinction to the following arguments:
- "Pegasus is a winged horse; therefore, there exists a winged horse."
- "God is omnipotent; therefore, there exists something omnipotent."
- "The current Prime Minister of Canada is Canadian; therefore, someone is Canadian."
Which inferences are valid in classical logic but invalid in free logic? Is this the right result?

## Thought Experiment: The Library of Babel
Borges's Library of Babel contains every possible book. Consider: do the characters in books that have never been read, in editions that have never been printed, exist? If a possible book describes a character with unique properties, does that character—considered as a Meinongian nonexistent object—have those properties? How does your answer connect to the debate between Meinong and Russell?

## Exercise 4: Modes of Being
Suppose you are convinced by ontological pluralism—that there are different modes of being. Try to articulate what criteria would distinguish one mode of being from another. Are modes of being distinguished by: causal efficacy? Spatiotemporal location? Being knowable a priori vs. a posteriori? Being necessary vs. contingent? Which criterion seems most plausible, and why?
