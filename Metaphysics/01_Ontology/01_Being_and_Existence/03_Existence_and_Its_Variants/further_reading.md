# Further Reading and Primary Sources

## Primary Sources

**Alexius Meinong, "On the Theory of Objects" (1904)** — The foundational Meinongian text. Available in English in *Realism and the Background of Phenomenology*, ed. Roderick Chisholm (Free Press, 1960).

**Bertrand Russell, "On Denoting" (1905)** — The definitive response to Meinong. Published in *Mind* 14: 479–493 and reprinted countless times. Short, dense, and essential.

**Bertrand Russell, *The Problems of Philosophy* (1912), Ch. 9–10** — Russell's introduction of the existence/subsistence distinction for general audiences; still readable and illuminating.

**W. V. O. Quine, "On What There Is" (1948)** — Reprinted in *From a Logical Point of View*. States the univocal, quantificational analysis of existence.

**Terence Parsons, *Nonexistent Objects* (Yale, 1980)** — The fullest development of nuclear/extranuclear neo-Meinongianism.

## Secondary Literature

**Graham Priest, *Towards Non-Being: The Logic and Metaphysics of Intentionality* (2nd ed., Oxford, 2016)** — A sophisticated paraconsistent approach to nonexistent objects and intentionality; challenging but rewarding.

**Maria Reicher, "Nonexistent Objects," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*** — An excellent, up-to-date survey of all positions on nonexistent objects.

**Kris McDaniel, *The Fragmentation of Being* (Oxford, 2017)** — The most developed contemporary case for ontological pluralism.

**Stephen Neale, *Descriptions* (MIT, 1990)** — A thorough treatment of Russell's theory of descriptions and its subsequent development, assessing how well it handles Meinongian puzzles.

**Dale Jacquette, *Meinongian Logic* (de Gruyter, 1996)** — Formalizes Meinong's object theory and explores its logical consequences.
