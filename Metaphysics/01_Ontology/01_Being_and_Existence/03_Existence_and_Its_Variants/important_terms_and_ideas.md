# Important Terms and Ideas: Existence and Its Variants

**Existence as a second-order predicate**: Frege's thesis that "exists" does not attribute a property to objects but says of a concept that at least one object falls under it. On this view, "unicorns exist" means "the concept unicorn is instantiated."

**Meinongianism**: The view, associated with Alexius Meinong, that some objects do not exist — golden mountains, round squares, the present king of France — while still having a kind of being (Sosein: being thus-and-so) that is distinct from existence.

**Subsistence vs. existence**: Meinong's distinction between two modes of being. Concrete physical objects exist; abstract objects and possibilities subsist. Non-existent objects may have their properties without existing or subsisting.

**Nuclear vs. extranuclear properties**: In neo-Meinongian theories, nuclear properties (being red, being round) constitute the nature of an object, while extranuclear properties (existing, being possible, being thought about) do not.

**Negative existentials**: True statements asserting that something does not exist, e.g., "Sherlock Holmes does not exist." These are philosophically puzzling: they seem to be about something, but that something supposedly does not exist.

**Existential quantifier and being**: Quine's slogan "to be is to be the value of a bound variable" ties existence to the ranges of quantifiers in our canonical scientific theories — a deflationary view that avoids positing non-existent entities.

**Possibilism vs. actualism**: Possibilism holds that merely possible objects (things that could have existed but do not) are real in some sense; actualism holds that only actual objects exist.

**Ontological parsimony (Ockham's Razor)**: The methodological principle that entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity — preferring simpler ontologies when they are explanatorily adequate.
