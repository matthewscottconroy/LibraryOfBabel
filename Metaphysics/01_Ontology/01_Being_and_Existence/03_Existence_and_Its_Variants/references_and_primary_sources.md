# References and Primary Sources: Existence and Its Variants

## Foundational Texts

Frege, Gottlob. *The Foundations of Arithmetic*. Trans. Austin. Blackwell, 1950. Contains Frege's influential argument that existence is not a first-level predicate but a second-level one — it says of a concept that it is satisfied.

Meinong, Alexius. "On the Theory of Objects." In Chisholm (ed.), *Realism and the Background of Phenomenology*. Free Press, 1960. The classic source for the view that there are non-existent objects (golden mountains, round squares) that nonetheless have being (Sein) or subsistence (Bestand).

Russell, Bertrand. "On Denoting." *Mind* 14 (1905): 479-493. The theory of descriptions that eliminates apparent reference to non-existents by paraphrasing definite descriptions as quantified claims.

## Contemporary Works

van Inwagen, Peter. "Being, Existence, and Ontological Commitment." In Chalmers, Manley, and Wasserman (eds.), *Metametaphysics*. Oxford University Press, 2009. Defends the Fregean view that existence is univocal and the existential quantifier is our primary tool.

Priest, Graham. *Towards Non-Being*. Oxford University Press, 2005. A contemporary defense of a Meinongian view using paraconsistent logic to handle impossible objects.

Lewis, David. "Noneism or Allism?" *Mind* 99 (1990): 23-31. Critical discussion of Meinongianism; defends the view that all quantification ranges over everything that exists.
