# Further Reading and Primary Sources — History of Substance

*Foundational texts and recommended secondary literature for the history of substance theory.*

---

## Primary Sources

**Aristotle**, *Categories* — The foundational text for Aristotle's initial account of substance, the ten categories, and the priority of primary substances. Begin with chapters 2–5.

**Aristotle**, *Metaphysics*, Books VII–IX (Zeta, Eta, Theta) — The mature and more complex treatment of substance, form, matter, essence, and actuality. Especially Books VII and VIII are essential.

**Aquinas, Thomas**, *On Being and Essence* (*De Ente et Essentia*) — A short but dense text articulating the real distinction between essence and existence in creatures.

**Scotus, John Duns**, *Ordinatio* II, d. 3 — The locus classicus for the haecceity doctrine. Also available in translated selections in Peter King, *John Duns Scotus: Selected Writings* (Oxford, 2008).

**Ockham, William of**, *Summa of Logic*, Part I — Ockham's nominalist theory of universals and categories. Translated by Michael Loux (Notre Dame, 1974).

**Descartes, René**, *Meditations on First Philosophy* (1641) — Especially Meditations I, II, and VI for the cogito argument and substance dualism.

**Spinoza, Baruch**, *Ethics* (1677) — Part I (*On God*) for the argument for substance monism. Part II (*On the Nature and Origin of the Mind*) for the theory of attributes and modes.

**Leibniz, Gottfried Wilhelm**, *Monadology* (1714) and *Discourse on Metaphysics* (1686) — The two most accessible introductions to Leibniz's mature substance theory.

## Secondary Literature

Loux, Michael J. *Substance and Attribute: A Study in Ontology*. Dordrecht: Reidel, 1978.

Pasnau, Robert. *Metaphysical Themes 1274–1671*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2011. — Exceptionally thorough treatment of late medieval and early modern substance theory.

Hoffman, Paul, and Howard Rosencrantz, eds. *Substance: Its Nature and Existence*. London: Routledge, 1997.

Cover, J. A., and Martin O'Leary-Hawthorne. *Substance and Individuation in Leibniz*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1999.
