# Important Figures — History of Substance

*Key thinkers who shaped the development of substance theory from antiquity through the early modern period.*

---

**Aristotle (384–322 BCE)** — Developed the foundational account of substance in the *Categories* and *Metaphysics*. His analysis of primary substance, the form-matter composite, and essence as the core of what it is to be a thing established the framework within which substance theory was conducted for over two millennia.

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — The preeminent medieval Aristotelian. Aquinas integrated Aristotelian hylomorphism with Christian theology and introduced the real distinction between essence and existence, arguing that only in God are these identical. His synthesis remains the official metaphysics of Roman Catholic philosophy.

**John Duns Scotus (1266–1308)** — Subtle Doctor of the Franciscan tradition. Proposed *haecceity* as the principle of individuation, challenging Aquinas's matter-based account. His formal distinction between aspects of a single entity opened new conceptual space between real and merely conceptual distinctions.

**William of Ockham (c. 1287–1347)** — Franciscan nominalist who wielded the famous Razor against metaphysical excesses. Rejected the Scotist common nature and argued for a spare ontology of individual substances and qualities, anticipating empiricist and nominalist traditions that followed.

**René Descartes (1596–1650)** — Inaugurated modern philosophy with the cogito and the dualist distinction between thinking and extended substance. His *Meditations on First Philosophy* established the terms of substance debate for the seventeenth century and beyond.

**Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677)** — Responded to Cartesian dualism with a rigorous monism: there is exactly one substance (God or Nature) with two attributes (thought and extension). The *Ethics* remains a landmark of rationalist metaphysics.

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)** — Proposed the monadology as an alternative to both Cartesian dualism and Spinozistic monism. Developed the doctrines of pre-established harmony, the identity of indiscernibles, and the theory of possible worlds — ideas that remain influential in contemporary analytic metaphysics.
