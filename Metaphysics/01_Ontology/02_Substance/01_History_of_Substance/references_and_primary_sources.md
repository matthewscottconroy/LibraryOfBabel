# References and Primary Sources: History of Substance

## Foundational Texts

Aristotle. *Metaphysics*, Books VII-IX. Trans. Ross. Oxford: Clarendon Press. The central treatment of ousia (substance): what it is to be a primary substance, the role of form and matter, and the criteria for substancehood.

Descartes, René. *Principles of Philosophy*. Trans. Miller and Miller. Reidel, 1983. Part I contains Descartes' definition of substance as that which requires nothing else for its existence — the basis for his substance dualism.

Leibniz, G.W. *Monadology*. Trans. Latta. Oxford University Press, 1898. The doctrine that the ultimate substances are simple, immaterial, perceiving monads — the culmination of early modern substance metaphysics.

## Contemporary Works

Pasnau, Robert. *Metaphysical Themes 1274-1671*. Oxford University Press, 2011. The most comprehensive scholarly treatment of substance metaphysics from Aquinas through Descartes and Leibniz.

Hoffman, Joshua and Rosenkrantz, Gary. *Substance: Its Nature and Existence*. Routledge, 1997. A systematic survey of the concept of substance from antiquity to the present.

Robinson, Howard. "Substance." *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2023. An authoritative overview covering ancient, medieval, and modern conceptions.
