# Exercises and Thought Experiments — Contemporary Substance Theory

*Problems for engaging with contemporary substance, persistence, and identity debates.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Bundle Theory's Binding Problem**
The bundle theorist says objects are bundles of properties. What is it that makes a bundle a single object rather than a scattered plurality of properties? Propose two different answers to this binding problem and evaluate each. Is there any answer available to the bundle theorist that the substance-and-attribute theorist cannot also appropriate?

**Exercise 2 — Tropes vs. Universals**
Explain the difference between a trope and a universal using a specific example. If all objects have their own individual property-instances (tropes), what explains why two pieces of gold *behave the same way* in chemical reactions? Can a trope theory ground genuine natural laws, or does it require supplementing with a theory of resemblance?

**Exercise 3 — Bradley's Regress**
State Bradley's regress as precisely as you can. Does it show that relations are unreal? Does it show that instantiation cannot be a relation? Or does it show something else? Evaluate at least two responses to the regress and assess whether any of them fully dissolve the problem.

**Exercise 4 — Endurantism vs. Perdurantism**
Consider a statue made of clay. The clay existed before the statue; the statue came into existence when the clay was shaped. On the endurantist view, what exists when the sculptor finishes? On the perdurantist view? Which view handles the following case better: at time t, the statue can survive being repainted but not being squashed; the clay can survive being squashed but not (as a statue) being repainted. What are the identity implications?

**Exercise 5 — Fission and What Matters**
Describe the fission case in your own words. Why does it create a problem for psychological continuity theories of personal identity? Do you find Parfit's conclusion — that identity is not what matters — persuasive? What would have to be true for identity to *fail* to matter in the way Parfit suggests?

**Thought Experiment — Teleporter Identity**
A teleporter disintegrates your body atom by atom, transmits the information to a receiving station, and assembles an exact physical duplicate from new matter. Does the teleported person survive? Does the answer depend on whether your original body is destroyed? Construct a metaphysical position — bundle theory, bare-particular theory, animalism, psychological continuity — and use it to answer these questions consistently.
