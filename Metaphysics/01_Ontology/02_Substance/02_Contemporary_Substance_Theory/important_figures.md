# Important Figures — Contemporary Substance Theory

*Key philosophers shaping substance theory in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.*

---

**David Hume (1711–1776)** — Though an early modern rather than a contemporary figure, Hume's bundle theory remains the ancestor of contemporary bundle-theoretic approaches. His critique of the substance substratum set the agenda for subsequent empiricist metaphysics.

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — Developed and defended a bundle theory based on qualities and their compresence, influencing the subsequent trajectory of analytic metaphysics.

**Gustav Bergmann (1906–1987)** — Systematic defender of bare particulars as the individuators of substance. His *Realism: A Critique of Brentano and Meinong* develops the ontological framework of facts, particulars, and universals that influenced Armstrong.

**Donald Williams (1899–1983)** — Pioneer of trope theory in analytic philosophy. His paper "The Elements of Being" (1953) introduced tropes (he called them "abstract particulars") as an alternative ontological base.

**Keith Campbell (1938–)** — Developed the most systematic contemporary defense of trope bundle theory in *Abstract Particulars* (1990).

**David Armstrong (1926–2014)** — The most influential defender of a naturalistic states-of-affairs ontology in analytic philosophy. His trilogy *Universals and Scientific Realism* (1978) and *A World of States of Affairs* (1997) are landmark texts.

**Derek Parfit (1942–2017)** — Transformed debates about personal identity with *Reasons and Persons* (1984), arguing that identity is not what matters and drawing radical ethical consequences from Reductionist views about the self.

**E. J. Lowe (1950–2014)** — Developer of the four-category ontology and leading neo-Aristotelian in analytic metaphysics. His *The Possibility of Metaphysics* (1998) and *The Four-Category Ontology* (2006) are essential reading.

**Kit Fine (1946–)** — Revived the Aristotelian notion of essence against purely modal analyses, with significant impact on contemporary neo-Aristotelian metaphysics.
