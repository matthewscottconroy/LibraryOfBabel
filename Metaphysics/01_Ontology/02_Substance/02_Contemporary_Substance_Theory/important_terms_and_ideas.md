# Important Terms and Ideas: Contemporary Substance Theory

**Substratum theory**: The view that a substance consists of a "bare particular" or thin substance — a substanceless subject — together with the properties it exemplifies. The bare particular is the individuator; it is what makes two qualitatively identical objects numerically distinct.

**Bundle theory**: The view that a substance is nothing more than a bundle or collection of properties (universals or tropes) co-instantiated or compresent with each other. There is no underlying substratum; the object just is its properties.

**Thin vs. thick particular**: Lewis's distinction between the bare subject of predication (thin particular) and the same subject considered together with all its intrinsic properties (thick particular). The thin particular is the individuating component; the thick particular is the complete object.

**Neo-Aristotelian substance theory**: The contemporary revival of Aristotle's account of substance as essentially characterized by its natural kind, having a real essence, and persisting through accidental change. Associated with Lowe, Wiggins, and others.

**Compresence**: The relation that holds among the properties that constitute a single substance, on bundle theories. Properties are co-located or simultaneous in a way that bundles them into one object rather than many.

**Individual essence**: A set of properties that are sufficient for the identity of exactly one individual. An individual essence picks out one object and no other across all possible worlds in which that object exists.

**Natural kind**: A grouping of things that share a real, objective nature — not merely a convenient human classification. Membership in a natural kind grounds objective similarity and supports law-like generalizations.

**Sortals**: Concepts (like "person," "river," "statue") that supply criteria of identity and counting for the things they apply to. Wiggins argued that substances must fall under sortal concepts for their persistence conditions to be determinate.
