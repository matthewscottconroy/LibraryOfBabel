# Neo-Aristotelian Substance Theory

The revival of Aristotelian substance theory in contemporary analytic philosophy is one of the most interesting developments in recent metaphysics. After decades in which the dominant frameworks were Humean (substance as bundle), logical atomist (substance as fact-constituent), or broadly Kantian, philosophers including E. J. Lowe, Jonathan Lowe, Michael Loux, and Kit Fine returned to explicitly Aristotelian frameworks in which substances are basic, irreducible individuals that ground the existence and nature of properties, kinds, and relations.

The neo-Aristotelian revival is not mere antiquarianism. It responds to genuine problems with the alternatives. Bundle theory faces the individuation problem; bare particular theory faces the opacity problem; process ontology seems to abandon the commonsense picture of a world of enduring things. Neo-Aristotelianism proposes that individual substances — horses, humans, trees — are ontologically basic: not constructed from more fundamental elements, not reducible to events or processes, but the irreducible starting points of ontological analysis.

On this view, substances have two key features. First, they are essentially of a kind: this human being is essentially human; it could not exist without being human; its identity conditions are determined by what kind of thing it is. Second, substances are the ground of predication: when we say that Socrates is pale, we are attributing a property to a substance; the substance is ontologically prior to the property attribution, not derived from it.

The distinction between essential and accidental properties is particularly important for neo-Aristotelianism. A substance has its essential properties necessarily — it could not exist without them; its accidental properties are ones it could have lacked while remaining the same substance. Recovering this distinction in a philosophically rigorous way is one of the central projects of neo-Aristotelian substance theory, and it connects directly to the revival of essentialism in philosophy of biology, natural kinds, and modality.
