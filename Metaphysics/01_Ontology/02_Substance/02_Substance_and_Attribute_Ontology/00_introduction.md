# Substance and Attribute Ontology

Substance-attribute ontology is perhaps the most intuitive general ontological framework: the world consists of substances (individual things) and attributes (properties and relations), where attributes are borne by substances and substances are the primary existents. This framework is presupposed by the ordinary grammar of subject and predicate: "Socrates is wise" attributes the property of wisdom to the substance Socrates. The question is what the ontological relationship between substance and attribute amounts to — what "instantiation" or "exemplification" is.

The relationship between a substance and the properties it has is not identical to the relationship between a part and a whole (properties are not parts of the substance that has them), nor is it the same as the identity relation (Socrates is not identical to wisdom). It seems to be a distinctive, perhaps primitive, ontological relation. Armstrong called it "instantiation"; others call it "exemplification." Whether this relation is a genuine addition to the ontology (a universal relating other universals to particulars) or whether it can be understood without positing a further relational entity is a question that connects directly to Bradley's regress.

Within the substance-attribute framework, a major dispute concerns whether attributes are universals (the same property genuinely present in multiple substances) or tropes (particular, non-repeatable property instances). Both positions accept the substance-attribute structure; they differ about the nature of the attributes. This distinction has implications for the analysis of natural laws, causal relations, and the semantics of predicates.

Bare particular theory, as examined in the section on substrata, is one specific development of substance-attribute ontology: it holds that the "substance" in the framework is a bare particular, entirely propertyless in itself. Neo-Aristotelian views hold instead that substances are essentially of a kind, so that what the substance-attribute framework tracks is not bare particulars bearing properties but natural individuals of kinds being characterized by their properties and relations. This section examines these options and their implications.
