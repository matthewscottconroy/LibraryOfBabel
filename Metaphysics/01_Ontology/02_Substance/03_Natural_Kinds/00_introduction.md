# Natural Kinds and Substance

When a biologist says that tigers are carnivores, she does not mean merely that all actual tigers are carnivores — a claim that might be falsified by a single vegetarian tiger. She means something stronger: that it is in the nature of tigers to be carnivores, that carnivorous behavior is part of what being a tiger consists in, that a creature relevantly similar to tigers in all respects except for dietary preference might not count as a tiger at all. This is an essentialist claim about a natural kind: it holds that tiger is a natural kind with a real essence that determines the necessary and sufficient conditions for membership.

The connection between substance theory and natural kinds is tight. Aristotle's substance theory was explicitly a theory of natural kinds: the form of a substance is precisely its nature, the essential organization that makes it the kind of thing it is. To be a human being is to be a rational animal; to be a horse is to be a certain kind of animate body; and so on. The form is not merely a description we apply to the individual but a real, determining principle that makes the individual what it is.

Contemporary essentialism about natural kinds — associated with Kripke and Putnam's work on natural kind terms — holds that kinds like gold, water, and tiger have real essences that scientific inquiry progressively discovers. "Water" rigidly designates H₂O; whatever is water is necessarily H₂O; a substance that is not H₂O is not water, regardless of its superficial appearance. This is the essentialist picture: natural kind membership is determined by intrinsic, non-superficial properties that things have as a matter of necessity.

Anti-essentialists challenge this picture, arguing that natural kind boundaries are often indeterminate, interest-relative, or multiply realizable, and that the demand for real essences imposes a tidiness on nature that nature does not possess. The debate about natural kinds in the context of substance theory is thus a debate about whether the world carves itself at joints that our categories can track — or whether the joints are of our own making.
