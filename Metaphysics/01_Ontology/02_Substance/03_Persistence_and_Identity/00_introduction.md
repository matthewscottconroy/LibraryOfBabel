# Persistence and Identity of Substances

Every substance exists at a time — is present at one moment and then at the next. But what is it for a substance to persist through time? This question is not trivial. A substance that persists must be, in some sense, the same substance at different times. But at different times it may have different properties: the tree is leafy in summer and bare in winter; the person is young and then old. How can a single thing have incompatible properties?

The puzzle generates the two main theories of persistence examined in the Persistence chapter of Unit 3: endurantism and perdurantism. But substance theory adds its own dimension to the debate. If substances are fundamental individuals — the basic units of ontological analysis — then their persistence cannot be reduced to something more fundamental; it must be primitively determined by what the substance is. On the neo-Aristotelian view, each natural kind has determinate persistence conditions built into its essence: the persistence conditions of a biological organism are different from those of a river, which are different from those of a nation. What makes a substance persist is its nature as the kind of substance it is.

The problem of coincident objects — the lump and the statue that share all their parts but have different persistence conditions — bears directly on substance theory. If substances are individuated by their persistence conditions, and if the lump and the statue have different persistence conditions, then they are distinct substances: they are co-located but numerically distinct. But what kind of substance is each? The lump is a material substance of a certain chemical kind; the statue is an artifact, a substance of a functional kind. This invites reflection on whether both are genuine substances, and if so, what distinguishes the ontological status of natural substances from the ontological status of artifacts.

These questions receive sustained attention in this section, which connects the abstract theory of substance to the concrete puzzles about material objects, persons, and artifacts.
