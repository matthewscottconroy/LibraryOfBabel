# Whitehead and Process Philosophy

Classical substance metaphysics begins with the assumption that the fundamental entities of reality are *things* — enduring particulars that persist through time and undergo changes while remaining numerically the same. A rock, an atom, a person: these are the basic furniture of the world, and events, changes, and processes are what happen *to* them. Alfred North Whitehead thought this picture was exactly backwards. What we call things — rocks, atoms, persons — are not enduring substances but relatively stable patterns of ongoing events. The error of treating them as basic, self-subsistent entities is what Whitehead called the "fallacy of misplaced concreteness": taking a genuine abstraction — the stable pattern extracted from the process — and treating it as the concrete reality that the process is an abstraction from.

Whitehead came to this view from two directions: philosophy of physics and philosophy of experience. From physics — he co-authored *Principia Mathematica* with Russell and engaged deeply with relativity and quantum mechanics — he concluded that the fundamental entities of nature are events and fields, not particles enduring through time. From philosophy of experience — his engagement with Descartes, Hume, and Kant — he diagnosed the "bifurcation of nature" as the root philosophical error: the Cartesian splitting of reality into subjective experience and objective matter, leaving experience unaccounted for in the scientific picture of the world. His alternative, developed in *Process and Reality* (1929), replaces both enduring substances and the bifurcated Cartesian picture with a process ontology in which experience and process are fundamental at every level of nature.

The basic entity in Whitehead's ontology is the *actual occasion* (or *actual entity*): a brief, indivisible event of becoming. Each actual occasion arises by integrating data from its causal past, reaches its determinate character (*satisfaction*), and then perishes — ceasing to be an active subject and becoming objective data for subsequent occasions to respond to. The rock on my desk is not a single enduring substance; it is an enormously complex pattern of actual occasions, temporarily stable but constitutively processual. At the most basic level, even the apparently inert objects of the physical world are composed of events of becoming.

## Concrescence, Prehension, and the Structure of Process

The process by which an actual occasion comes into being is called *concrescence* — the growing together of data into a unified experiential event. Concrescence proceeds through *prehension*: each actual occasion grasps, selects, and integrates data from prior occasions and from what Whitehead calls eternal objects — the unchanging possibilities (the analogs of Platonic forms) that can be realized in actual events. Prehensions can be positive (genuine integration of data, what Whitehead calls feelings) or negative (deliberate exclusion of data). The selectivity and weighting of prehensions gives each actual occasion its unique perspective on the universe.

Notice what this replaces. In substance ontology, causation is a relation between two enduring things: the fire causes the water to boil. In Whitehead's process ontology, causation is the inheritance of felt data: a new occasion of becoming integrates the completed prior occasion into its own concrescence. There is no push-pull relation between substances; there is responsive becoming that takes the past as its material. The world does not consist of static entities that interact; it consists of occasions of responsive becoming, each inheriting from the past and contributing to the future.

God plays an indispensable structural role. God is a unique actual entity — not a substance in the classical sense but an everlasting actual entity with two natures. The primordial nature is God's grasp of all eternal objects and their possible interrelations, which provides each new becoming occasion with relevant possibilities (what Whitehead calls God's "initial aim"). The consequent nature is God's responsive reception of all completed actual occasions — the world's experiences enriching God's experience. This is the theological implication of a process ontology: God is not the immutable, omnipotent deity of classical theism but a supremely responsive participant in the universal process of becoming.

## Panpsychism and Contemporary Resonances

Whitehead's claim that actual occasions are "drops of experience" is an explicit form of panpsychism. This does not mean that electrons are conscious in the way that humans are. It means that "experience" — in the minimal sense of responsive integration of data, of taking account of the past and orienting toward a future — is a feature of natural events at every level, not only at the level of complex nervous systems. What distinguishes human consciousness is not that it is something entirely different from nature but that it is the highest expression of experiential integration built on layers of simpler occasions.

Contemporary philosophy of mind has found renewed interest in this position. Panpsychists like Philip Goff and Giulio Tononi are motivated by the same problem Whitehead was: if physical processes are purely objective, mechanical, and third-personal, there is a genuine explanatory gap between them and the first-personal character of conscious experience. Whitehead's solution — make first-personal responsiveness (in a minimal sense) fundamental at every level — avoids the gap by ensuring that experience is never something entirely new that needs to be generated from non-experiential materials.

Whether this solution is coherent — whether the panpsychist faces an equally serious *combination problem* (how do simple occasions of experience combine to yield rich human consciousness?) — is a central debate in contemporary philosophy of mind that process ontology has not fully resolved. But the Whiteheadian framework remains the most thoroughly worked-out systematic alternative to substance ontology, and its engagement with both physics and philosophy of experience gives it resources that purely analytic event ontologies have not matched.
