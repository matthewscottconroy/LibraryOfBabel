# Exercises and Thought Experiments — Special Substances

*Problems for engaging with the metaphysics of persons, artifacts, and kinds.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Criteria for Personhood**
List five candidate criteria for personhood (e.g., rationality, self-consciousness, sentience, moral agency, narrative continuity). For each criterion, identify a case where the criterion is satisfied but personhood seems absent, and a case where personhood seems present but the criterion is not satisfied. What do these test cases reveal about the adequacy of the criterion?

**Exercise 2 — Animalism and the Transplant**
On Olson's animalism, you are a biological organism. Now suppose your brain is transplanted into a new body, the brain being the seat of all your memories, personality, and psychological states. Where are you after the transplant — in the body that has your brain, or in the body that has the rest of your original animal? Olson has a specific (counterintuitive) answer. What is it, and do you find it convincing?

**Exercise 3 — Ship of Theseus Variations**
Consider three variations: (a) the original planks are not collected — each is burned as it is replaced; (b) the original planks are collected and reassembled simultaneously with the gradual repair; (c) the ship is disassembled all at once and immediately reassembled from the same planks. Does the same theory give the same answer to all three? Should it?

**Exercise 4 — Natural Kinds and Twin Earth**
Putnam argues that the word "water" as used by an eighteenth-century English speaker refers to H₂O even though the speaker had no idea what H₂O was. Is this plausible? Consider the case of jade: "jade" turns out to refer to two different minerals (jadeite and nephrite) that are superficially similar. Does this show that "jade" was never a natural kind term, or that natural kinds can be disjunctive?

**Thought Experiment — Social Construction and Reality**
Searle argues that money is real but socially constructed. A committed metaphysical realist might worry: if money's existence depends on our collective attitudes, then money is just a fiction we all believe in — and fictions are not real. Is this a good objection? Can something be both socially constructed and genuinely real? Defend a position.
