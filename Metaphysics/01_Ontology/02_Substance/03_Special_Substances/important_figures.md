# Important Figures — Special Substances

*Key thinkers on persons, artifacts, and natural and social kinds.*

---

**John Locke (1632–1704)** — Introduced the influential psychological criterion for personal identity (consciousness and memory) in *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, Book II, Chapter 27. Distinguished personal identity from the identity of the man (human animal) and the identity of substance.

**Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)** — Grounded personhood and moral status in rational autonomy. His distinction between persons (ends in themselves) and things (mere means) in the *Groundwork for the Metaphysics of Morals* established the Kantian framework for debates about moral status.

**Derek Parfit (1942–2017)** — Transformed personal identity debates with *Reasons and Persons*. His thought experiments about fission and teleportation and his Reductionist conclusion that identity is not what matters have been the starting point for all subsequent discussion.

**Eric Olson (1964–)** — Principal defender of animalism, the view that we are biological organisms. *The Human Animal* (1997) and *What Are We?* (2007) are the key texts.

**Saul Kripke (1940–2022)** — With *Naming and Necessity* (1980), established the essentialist framework for natural kind terms. His account of rigid designation and a posteriori necessity transformed philosophy of language and metaphysics.

**Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)** — The Twin Earth thought experiment and the slogan "meanings ain't in the head" developed alongside Kripke's essentialism. *Mind, Language and Reality* (1975) collects the relevant papers.

**John Searle (1932–)** — *The Construction of Social Reality* (1995) provided the most systematic account of how institutional and social kinds are created and sustained by collective intentionality.

**Sally Haslander (1955–)** — Developed a structuralist account of gender and race as social kinds that are real but socially constructed by power relations. *Resisting Reality* (2012) collects her influential papers.

**Amie Thomasson (1968–)** — Developed the deflationary "easy ontology" approach and a careful account of artifact identity in *Ordinary Objects* (2007).
