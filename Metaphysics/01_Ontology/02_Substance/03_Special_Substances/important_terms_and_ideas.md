# Important Terms and Ideas: Special Substances

**Animalism**: The view that human persons are fundamentally biological organisms — animals. Our persistence conditions are those of a living human animal, not those of a stream of consciousness or psychological continuity.

**Artifact**: An object produced by intentional human activity for a purpose — hammers, statues, sentences. Whether artifacts are genuine substances (with their own identity and persistence conditions) or merely conventional constructs is disputed.

**Natural kind substance**: A substance whose identity and persistence conditions are fixed by its membership in a natural kind (such as being a gold atom or a tiger). The kind provides the criterion of identity: this is the same tiger as that one iff the same tiger-kind grounds the comparison.

**Divine simplicity**: The doctrine that God has no parts, aspects, or distinguishable properties — that in God essence and existence, power and knowledge, are absolutely identical. Defended by Aquinas and the classical theistic tradition.

**Immaterial substance**: A substance that lacks material composition and is not located in physical space — the mind (on Cartesian dualism), angels, God. Immaterial substances raise distinctive questions about individuation and interaction with matter.

**Necessary being**: A being that could not have failed to exist — whose non-existence is impossible. Often attributed to God and to abstract objects like numbers. Contrasts with contingent beings, which exist but might not have.

**Haecceity**: The "thisness" of an individual — a primitive, qualitatively characterizable property that makes it the very individual it is, distinct from all its qualitative properties. Used to individuate substances that might otherwise be qualitatively indistinguishable.

**Persistence conditions**: The conditions under which a substance at one time is identical to a substance at another time. Different kinds of substance have different persistence conditions: persons, organisms, artifacts, and abstract objects may persist in quite different ways.
