# References and Primary Sources: Special Substances

## Foundational Texts

Aquinas, Thomas. *Summa Theologiae*, Ia, QQ. 3-13. The classic treatment of divine substance: God as simple, necessary being whose essence is identical with his existence.

Wiggins, David. *Sameness and Substance Renewed*. Cambridge University Press, 2001. Argues that persons are biological animals — a view known as animalism — whose persistence conditions are given by the biological continuity of the organism.

## Contemporary Works

Olson, Eric. *The Human Animal*. Oxford University Press, 1997. A systematic defense of animalism: the view that we are essentially human animals, not persons or streams of consciousness.

van Inwagen, Peter. *Material Beings*. Cornell University Press, 1990. Argues that the only composite material objects that exist are living organisms; artifacts and other "ordinary" objects do not strictly exist.

Thomasson, Amie. *Fiction and Metaphysics*. Cambridge University Press, 1999. Treats fictional characters as abstract artifacts — a kind of substance distinct from both concrete individuals and standard abstracta.

Hoffman, Joshua and Rosenkrantz, Gary. *The Divine Attributes*. Blackwell, 2002. A systematic treatment of what it would be for God to be a substance with specific divine attributes.
