# Exercises: The Nature of Properties

1. Distinguish universals from tropes. What difference does it make to how we explain resemblance between red objects?

2. Armstrong holds that only instantiated universals exist. What problems arise if we admit uninstantiated universals? Are there reasons to accept them anyway?

3. Shoemaker argues that properties are identical iff they have identical causal roles. Construct an objection involving two properties that share all their causal roles but are still intuitively distinct.

4. Is the categorical/dispositional distinction itself principled, or does it collapse under scrutiny? Defend your answer with at least one example.

5. Suppose scientific essentialism is true: natural properties have their causal roles essentially. What consequences does this have for the possibility of laws of nature that could have been different?

6. Compare nominalism, trope theory, and realism about universals as responses to the problem of the one over the many. Which do you find most defensible, and why?
