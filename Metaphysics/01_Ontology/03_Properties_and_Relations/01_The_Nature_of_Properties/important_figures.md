# Important Figures: The Nature of Properties

**David Armstrong** (1926–2014) defended a naturalist theory of universals, arguing that properties are repeatable features of the physical world. His multi-volume *Universals and Scientific Realism* remains the touchstone for contemporary discussions.

**D.H. Mellor** (1938–2020) contributed extensively to the theory of dispositions and categorical properties, arguing that many apparently dispositional predicates describe perfectly intrinsic categorical features of their bearers.

**Sydney Shoemaker** (b. 1931) proposed that properties are individuated by their causal roles — two properties are identical if and only if they confer exactly the same causal powers on their bearers, a view that bridges the categorical/dispositional divide.

**George Molnar** (1934–1999) left an unfinished but influential manuscript on powers that was published posthumously as *Powers: A Study in Metaphysics* (2003), defending a robust ontology of irreducibly dispositional properties.

**Brian Ellis** (b. 1929) developed scientific essentialism, the view that natural properties have their causal roles essentially, lending structure to scientific laws and natural necessity.
