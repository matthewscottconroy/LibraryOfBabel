# Important Terms and Ideas: The Nature of Properties

**Universal**: A repeatable entity that can be wholly present in numerically distinct individuals simultaneously. The universal redness is one and the same entity in each red object. Contrasts with tropes, which are particular property-instances.

**Trope (abstract particular)**: A non-repeatable, particular property-instance: this specific redness of this specific rose, numerically distinct from any other redness. Tropes are abstract in being aspects of their bearers, particular in being unrepeatable.

**Nominalism about properties**: The view that there are no property-like entities beyond particular objects — either predicates apply without any corresponding ontological entity, or resemblance among particulars is the fundamental fact.

**Natural properties**: Lewis's term for the properties that "carve nature at its joints" — those that ground genuine objective similarities and figure in the fundamental laws of physics. Contrasts with gerrymandered, "unnatural" properties.

**Instantiation**: The relation between a particular and a universal whereby the particular has or exemplifies the property. Instantiation is the fundamental connection between the world of particulars and the world of universals.

**Causal role of a property**: The characteristic causal contribution a property makes to the behavior of its bearers. Shoemaker argued that properties are individuated by their causal roles: two properties are identical iff they confer identical causal powers.

**Immanent universals**: Armstrong's view that universals exist only where instantiated — they are wholly present in their instances, not in a separate Platonic realm. Contrasts with transcendent universals, which exist independently of instantiation.

**Qualitative vs. numerical identity**: Two objects are qualitatively identical if they share all their properties; they are numerically identical only if they are one and the same object. Properties are repeatable (numerically the same universal in many instances) or non-repeatable (tropes that only qualitatively resemble).
