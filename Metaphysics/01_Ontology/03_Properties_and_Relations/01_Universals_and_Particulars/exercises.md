# Exercises and Thought Experiments — Universals and Particulars

*Problems for engaging with the problem of universals.*

---

**Exercise 1 — The One Over Many**
Two roses are both red. List three different explanations of what grounds their similarity: (a) Platonic realism, (b) Armstrongian immanent realism, (c) resemblance nominalism. What does each explanation gain and what does it cost? Which do you find most compelling?

**Exercise 2 — Predicates and Properties**
Not every predicate corresponds to a genuine property (Lewis, Armstrong). Consider the predicate "is grue" (green before time T, blue after). Does "grue" pick out a genuine property? What criterion would distinguish genuine properties (natural kinds, fundamental physical properties) from gerrymandered predicates? Is any such criterion available without circularity?

**Exercise 3 — The Regress Arguments**
State Bradley's regress about relations. Then state the Third Man argument about Platonic universals. Are these essentially the same regress? Are the available responses structurally similar? Which response is more satisfying?

**Exercise 4 — Laws and Universals**
Armstrong holds that laws of nature are relations of necessitation between universals: N(F, G) means that being F necessitates being G. Lewis holds that laws are regularities that figure in the best systematic description of the world. Construct a case that distinguishes the two views — a situation that Armstrong would count as a genuine law but Lewis would not (or vice versa). Which view fits your intuitions better?

**Thought Experiment — A World of Two Red Things**
Imagine a world containing exactly two things: two perfectly red spheres, alike in every quality. Does the universal REDNESS exist in this world? The Platonic says yes (universals exist necessarily); the Armstrongian immanent realist says yes (it is instantiated by both); the resemblance nominalist says there is no universal but the two spheres genuinely resemble each other. Does the distinction matter here? Would it matter if one of the spheres ceased to exist?
