# Important Figures — Universals and Particulars

*Key philosophers in debates about universals, nominalism, and properties.*

---

**Plato (c. 428–348 BCE)** — Developed the original theory of Forms: transcendent universals that exist independently and are the objects of genuine knowledge. The *Republic*, *Phaedo*, and *Parmenides* are the key texts.

**Aristotle (384–322 BCE)** — Criticized Platonic realism and developed an immanent view of universals existing only in their instances. His *Metaphysics* (especially Books VII and XIII) is essential.

**William of Ockham (c. 1287–1347)** — The most influential medieval nominalist. Argued against the real existence of universals and defended a sparse ontology of individual substances and qualities.

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — In *Problems of Philosophy* (1912) and *The Problems of Philosophy* defended universals on the grounds that relations, in particular, cannot be explained without them.

**David Armstrong (1926–2014)** — The most influential contemporary defender of immanent realism about universals, combined with states-of-affairs ontology and a naturalistic theory of laws. His two-volume *Universals and Scientific Realism* (1978) and *What Is a Law of Nature?* (1983) are landmarks.

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Defended extreme modal realism (all possible worlds are real) and a regularity theory of laws supplemented by the concept of *naturalness*. *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986) and *Philosophical Papers* Vol. II collect the relevant work.

**Gonzalo Rodriguez-Pereyra (1966–)** — Developer of the most systematic contemporary resemblance nominalism. *Resemblance Nominalism: A Solution to the Problem of Universals* (2002).

**Keith Campbell (1938–)** — Pioneer of contemporary trope theory in *Abstract Particulars* (1990).
