# Important Terms and Ideas: Universals and Particulars

**The one over many problem**: The challenge of explaining how numerically distinct things can share a property. If two things are both red, there must be some account of what their sharing amounts to — either a shared universal, a resemblance, or something else.

**The many over one problem**: The related challenge of explaining how one and the same thing can have multiple, apparently incompatible properties in different relations or at different times.

**Ostrich nominalism**: The view (attributed by Armstrong to Quine) that the one-over-many problem is a pseudo-problem and can be dismissed. There are no universals; particular things just are the way they are.

**Predicate nominalism**: The view that two things share a "property" simply in virtue of both falling under the same predicate. Properties are nothing more than the semantic contents of predicates — not genuinely existing entities.

**Class nominalism**: The view that a property is identified with the set (class) of its instances. To have property P is simply to be a member of the class of P-things. Faces the coextension problem: co-extensive properties collapse into one.

**Resemblance nominalism**: The view that property-talk is grounded in primitive, objective resemblance relations among particulars. Things are red because they resemble each other and paradigm cases of redness — no universal redness is needed.

**Realism about universals**: The view that universals are genuine entities, either immanent (in their instances, as Armstrong holds) or transcendent (existing independently, as Plato holds).

**Principle of Instantiation**: Armstrong's requirement that every genuine universal must be instantiated somewhere in the actual world. Uninstantiated universals are metaphysically inadmissible on his view.
