# References and Primary Sources: Universals and Particulars

## Foundational Texts

Plato. *Phaedo* and *Republic* (Book VI). Trans. Grube, revised Cooper. Hackett, 1997. The original statement of the theory of Forms as eternal, non-sensible universals that ground the intelligibility of particular things.

Russell, Bertrand. *The Problems of Philosophy*. Williams & Norgate, 1912. Chapters 9-10 give a clear introduction to universals and their distinction from particulars; defends the reality of both.

## Contemporary Works

Armstrong, D.M. *Universals: An Opinionated Introduction*. Westview Press, 1989. A concise guide to the main positions: ostrich nominalism, predicate nominalism, class nominalism, resemblance nominalism, immanent realism.

Rodriguez-Pereyra, Gonzalo. *Resemblance Nominalism: A Solution to the Problem of Universals*. Oxford University Press, 2002. The most sophisticated contemporary defense of resemblance nominalism.

Price, H.H. *Thinking and Experience*. Hutchinson, 1953. Chapters 1-2 develop a careful resemblance nominalism that influenced subsequent debate.

Moreland, J.P. *Universals*. McGill-Queen's University Press, 2001. A comprehensive treatment of universals with attention to both analytic and continental perspectives.
