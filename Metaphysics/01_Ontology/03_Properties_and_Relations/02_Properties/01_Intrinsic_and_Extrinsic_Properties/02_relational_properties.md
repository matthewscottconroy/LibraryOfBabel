# Relational Properties

*Properties that an object has in virtue of its relations to other things.*

---

Relational properties seem ubiquitous in both ordinary thought and scientific theory: being to the left of, being heavier than, being the parent of, being electrically attracted to. Yet there is a long tradition in metaphysics of treating relational properties with suspicion, viewing them as somehow less fundamental than intrinsic or monadic properties.

Bradley argued that relations are ultimately unreal, generating the famous regress. If the relation of *being larger than* relates A to B, then this relation must itself relate to A and B, requiring a further relation, and so on infinitely. Bradley concluded that relations cannot ultimately be real; reality at its deepest level is a single undifferentiated unity (his Absolute). Few contemporary philosophers follow Bradley this far, but the regress he identified remains a challenge for any account of relations.

A less extreme position holds that relational properties supervene on the intrinsic properties of the relata. If A is heavier than B, this is grounded in A's having a certain mass (intrinsic to A) and B's having a certain mass (intrinsic to B). On this view, relations are not a further primitive over and above intrinsic properties. This view is plausible for some relations but faces challenges for others: spatio-temporal relations between things do not obviously supervene on anything intrinsic to the things related.

Leibniz held that all apparently external relations reduce to intrinsic properties of the relata: for A to be larger than B is for A to have a certain intrinsic magnitude and B to have a different intrinsic magnitude. This *reductionism about relations* is motivated by the principle of sufficient reason: for a relation to exist, there must be a reason, and the reason must lie in the intrinsic natures of the things related. Russell challenged this reduction, arguing that asymmetric relations — A is to the north of B — cannot be reduced to intrinsic properties, since the intrinsic properties of A and B do not by themselves determine the direction of the relation.

Contemporary metaphysics is largely pluralist about relations: both intrinsic properties and relations are real, and the attempt to reduce one to the other is abandoned. The interesting questions become which relations are fundamental and how they connect to the causal powers and dispositions of the things that stand in them.

## Russell's Case for Real Relations

Russell's argument against the reduction of relations to intrinsic properties rests on the irreducibility of *order* and *direction*. Consider the relation *A is to the north of B*. The Leibnizian reductionist must identify some intrinsic property of A (a "northerliness" quality) and some intrinsic property of B (a "southerliness" quality) from which the relational fact can be derived. But:

- P1: If the relational fact *aRb* reduces to intrinsic properties of a and b, then the same intrinsic properties that make *aRb* true would also make *bRa* true.
- P2: But *aRb* and *bRa* differ in truth value for asymmetric relations (if a is north of b, b is not north of a).
- Conclusion: The relational fact cannot reduce to the intrinsic properties of the relata alone.

This argument shows that at least *external* relations — relations whose holding is not determined by the intrinsic natures of the relata — must be admitted as genuine ontological posits. The structure of reality is not simply a collection of things with intrinsic natures; it includes irreducibly relational facts.

## Internal vs. External Relational Properties

Not all relational properties are external. *Internal* relational properties are those whose holding supervenes on the intrinsic natures of the relata: given what a and b are intrinsically, their internal relations are fixed.

- *Being taller than* is internal: it supervenes on the heights of the relata, and heights are intrinsic.
- *Being a brother of* is internal in one sense (requires that both are male siblings) but depends on external facts about family structure.
- *Being five meters from* is external: two objects could have exactly the same intrinsic properties while being five meters apart or five miles apart.

This distinction matters for Lewis's Humean supervenience thesis: the claim that all facts about the world supervene on the distribution of *local*, *intrinsic* properties plus the pattern of *spatiotemporal* (external) relations. On this view, external spatiotemporal relations are fundamental posits; all other relational properties supervene on intrinsic plus spatiotemporal facts.

## Relations in Contemporary Debate

Contemporary discussions have focused on whether relational properties are irreducibly real or can be eliminated in favor of a richer account of intrinsic properties. Structural realists in philosophy of physics (Ladyman, French) argue that quantum mechanics and general relativity reveal that what physics tracks is fundamentally structural — patterns of relations — rather than the intrinsic natures of relata. On the ontic structural realist view, relational properties are not derivative from intrinsic properties but are ontologically prior.

Dispositionalists face a distinctive challenge: if intrinsic properties are causal powers essentially directed toward manifestation, then their nature is partly constituted by their relational potential. Whether this makes intrinsic dispositional properties covertly relational is a matter of active dispute. Mumford and Anjum's *powers ontology* holds that causation consists in the mutual manifestation of complementary powers — making causal relations partly internal to the natures of the powers involved.
