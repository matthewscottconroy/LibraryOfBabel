# Important Figures — Properties

*Key philosophers in debates about intrinsicality, relations, essences, and dispositions.*

---

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)** — Argued for the reducibility of all relations to intrinsic properties and for the identity of indiscernibles. His monadology grounds a complete dispositionalist ontology avant la lettre.

**David Hume (1711–1776)** — The Humean tradition of treating properties as categorical and denying irreducible necessary connections between distinct existences motivates much of the contemporary debate.

**Saul Kripke (1940–2022)** — Introduced rigidly designating names and the notion of necessary a posteriori truth, transforming the treatment of essential properties. *Naming and Necessity* (1980).

**Kit Fine (1946–)** — Disentangled essence from necessity, arguing that essential properties reveal what things are rather than merely what they cannot fail to have. "Essence and Modality" (1994) and subsequent work.

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Developed the account of intrinsicality in terms of duplicates, defended Humean supervenience, and pioneered the use of *naturalness* to distinguish genuine from gerrymandered properties.

**C. B. Martin (1924–2008)** — The finkish disposition counterexample undermined conditional analyses and revived categorical vs. dispositional debates. His later work developed an ontology of "powerful qualities."

**George Molnar (1934–1999)** — His posthumous *Powers: A Study in Metaphysics* (2003) is the foundational text for the contemporary revival of powers as irreducible physical intentionality.

**Brian Ellis (1929–)** — Developed *scientific essentialism* — the view that the fundamental properties of physics have essential dispositional natures — in *Scientific Essentialism* (2001).

**Alexander Bird (1964–)** — *Nature's Metaphysics* (2007) argues for a pure dispositional monism and necessary laws of nature grounded in property identities.
