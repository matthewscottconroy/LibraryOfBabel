# Important Terms and Ideas: Properties

**Sparse vs. abundant properties**: Lewis's distinction between the elite few properties that genuinely cut nature at its joints (sparse), used in physics and metaphysics, and the profuse properties corresponding to every predicate (abundant), used in semantics.

**Property individuation**: The question of when two predicates express one property versus two distinct properties. Candidates for the individuation criterion include: necessary coextension, identical causal roles, or syntactic/conceptual structure.

**Higher-order properties**: Properties of properties (or of other abstract entities). For example, being a natural property or being an intrinsic property are higher-order properties. The coherence of higher-order predication raises questions about property hierarchies.

**Structural properties**: Properties that are constituted by the having of other properties and relations — e.g., being water (being H₂O) or being methane (CH₄). Armstrong analyzed structural universals as complex universals whose instances structurally mirror their composition.

**Determinable and determinate**: A determinable is a generic property (like color, shape, mass); a determinate is a fully specific value under a determinable (scarlet, circular, 5kg). Determinates are maximally specific; determinables are ranges of possible specificity.

**Accidental vs. essential properties**: A property is essential to an object if the object could not exist without having it; accidental if the object could exist without it. Socrates' being human is essential; his being snub-nosed is accidental.

**Intrinsic vs. extrinsic properties**: Intrinsic properties are those an object has independently of its relations to other things; extrinsic properties depend on its relational context. Mass is intrinsic; being the tallest building in the city is extrinsic.
