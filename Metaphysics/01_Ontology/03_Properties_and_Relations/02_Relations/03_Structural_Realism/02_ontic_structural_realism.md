# Ontic Structural Realism

Ontic structural realism (OSR), developed primarily by James Ladyman (with Don Ross and others), takes the next step beyond epistemic structural realism. Where epistemic structural realism says we can know only the structure of the world, ontic structural realism says the world just is structure. The fundamental ontology is not objects with properties but patterns of relations.

The motivation comes from physics. In quantum mechanics, particles appear to lack the kind of individuality that traditional object-ontology assumes. Bosons and fermions are indistinguishable in a sense that goes beyond mere qualitative similarity: there is no fact about which boson is which, because the quantum state of a multi-boson system is symmetric under exchange. This suggests that "particle" may be a misleading term — what we have are excitation modes of quantum fields, more naturally understood as structural features of the field than as individual objects.

Similarly, in general relativity, spacetime points have no intrinsic identity beyond their positional relations. Hole argument considerations show that if spacetime points are taken as genuine individuals, then the field equations underdetermine the physical state of the world. This has pushed many philosophers toward relationist views that reduce the importance of individual spacetime points relative to the structural relations among them.

OSR's most radical form eliminates objects entirely: there are no things, only structures. A less radical form retains a thin notion of objects as nodes in the structural network — they exist, but they are entirely constituted by their structural roles. Critics, including Stathis Psillos, argue that the nodeless version is incoherent: relations must relate something.

Ladyman and Ross connect OSR to a broader naturalist program, arguing that the special sciences also discover structures and that the object-ontology of folk metaphysics is simply inappropriate for serious scientific inquiry.

## The Ladder of OSR Positions

OSR comes in a spectrum from moderate to eliminativist:

**Moderate OSR** (French, Ladyman in some formulations): Objects exist but are "weakly" individual — their identity is entirely constituted by their structural positions. There is a thin notion of object (a node, a place in the structure), but it carries no intrinsic haecceistic identity beyond the structural role.

**Eliminativist OSR** (Ladyman and Ross in *Every Thing Must Go*): There are no objects — not even thin ones. The fundamental ontology consists purely of relational structures. What we call "objects" are emergent patterns in these structures, useful for certain levels of description but not part of the fundamental metaphysical picture.

**Priority OSR** (Esfeld and Deckert in *A Minimalist Ontology*): A minimal substrate of "matter points" provides the relata for structural relations, but these matter points have no intrinsic properties — all their properties are relational. This is a compromise: there are relata (so the "no relata" objection fails), but the relata are entirely constituted by their relational roles.

## The Hole Argument and Spacetime Points

The *hole argument* (Earman and Norton, 1987) is a technical argument in general relativity that poses a serious problem for spacetime substantivalism. The argument exploits the *diffeomorphism invariance* of GR — the fact that the physical content of GR is invariant under smooth permutations of spacetime points.

More precisely:
1. General relativity describes the world using a manifold M equipped with a metric tensor g and a stress-energy tensor T.
2. The field equations Gμν = 8πTμν relate the geometry (g) to the matter distribution (T).
3. If we take any region H ("the hole") of the manifold and apply a diffeomorphism d that is the identity outside H but differs inside H, we get a new metric d*g related to g by a smooth mapping.
4. Both (M, g, T) and (M, d*g, d*T) satisfy the field equations and agree outside H but disagree inside.
5. If spacetime points are genuine individuals, these are *distinct physical states* that agree on all observable facts (outside H) — violating determinism.

The OSR response: spacetime points are not genuine individuals. The "same" spacetime manifold described differently by a diffeomorphism is *one* physical state, not two. The physical content is encoded in the relational structure (the metric field relations), not in which points play which role. The diffeomorphism invariance of GR reflects the fact that the individual points of the manifold carry no intrinsic identity.

## OSR and the Special Sciences

Ladyman and Ross argue in *Every Thing Must Go* (2007) that OSR is not just a philosophy of fundamental physics but the correct metaphysics for science generally. The special sciences — chemistry, biology, economics, psychology — also discover structures. What makes something a protein, an organism, a market, a mental state is its structural-relational role, not any intrinsic categorical character.

This naturalist extension of OSR connects to debates in philosophy of mind (multiple realizability: mental states are defined by their functional-relational roles), philosophy of biology (biological categories are defined by phylogenetic and functional relations), and philosophy of social science (social structures are irreducible to the properties of individuals).

The broader OSR program is thus a version of scientific structuralism applied across all levels of the scientific hierarchy, with the proviso that structures at different levels are related by *inter-level relations* (constitution, emergence, reduction) that are themselves structural.
