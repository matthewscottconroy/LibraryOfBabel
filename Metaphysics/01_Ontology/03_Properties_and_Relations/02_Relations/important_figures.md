# Important Figures: Relations

**Bertrand Russell** (1872–1970) argued forcefully for the irreducibility of relations against the British Idealists who held that all relations were internal. His analysis of mathematics depended on accepting external relations.

**F.H. Bradley** (1846–1924) denied that relations can be real: every apparent relation, he argued, generates a vicious regress. His arguments set the stage for twentieth-century discussions.

**Kit Fine** (b. 1946) has done influential work on the nature of relations, distinguishing positionalist from anti-positionalist approaches and analyzing how asymmetric relations can be construed.

**Cian Dorr** has argued for a sparse view of relations in which the genuine relational facts are far fewer than surface grammar suggests.

**James Ladyman** (b. 1969) defends ontic structural realism: the view that fundamental reality consists not of objects with properties but of structures — systems of relations — in which objects are derivative or merely apparent.
