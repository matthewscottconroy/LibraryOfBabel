# Important Terms and Ideas: Relations

**Relation**: An entity that holds between (or among) two or more objects. Like properties (which relate objects to predicates), relations can be construed as universals (repeatable across instances) or as particular relational tropes.

**Internal relation**: A relation whose holding is necessitated by the intrinsic natures of its relata — given what the relata are, the relation must hold. Mathematical relations (greater than, part of) are paradigm internal relations.

**External relation**: A relation whose holding is not determined by the intrinsic natures of its relata. Spatial relations (being five meters from) are paradigm external relations: knowing everything about two objects intrinsically does not settle how far apart they are.

**Relational property**: A property that an object has in virtue of standing in some relation to something else. Being to the left of the chair is a relational property of the table; it is extrinsic to the table.

**Symmetric/asymmetric relations**: A relation R is symmetric if whenever aRb then bRa (being siblings); asymmetric if whenever aRb then not bRa (being taller than); non-symmetric if neither condition holds universally.

**Bradley's regress**: The argument that any attempt to ground a relation in a further connecting relation generates an infinite regress, suggesting that genuine relational facts cannot be reduced to further relational facts.

**Ontic structural realism (OSR)**: The view that fundamental reality consists of relational structures rather than objects with intrinsic properties. Objects, on this view, are at most nodes in the structural network, defined entirely by their relational roles.

**Adicity (arity)**: The number of argument positions a relation has. Binary (2-place) relations hold between pairs; ternary (3-place) relations hold among triples; and so on. Some relations may be variably polyadic.
