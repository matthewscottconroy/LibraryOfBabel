# Supervenience of Internal Relations

*How internal relations are grounded in the intrinsic properties of their relata.*

---

The key thesis about internal relations is that they *supervene* on the intrinsic properties of the things they relate: fix the intrinsic properties of A and B, and you automatically fix all the internal relations that hold between them. This means that internal relations do not add to the ontological inventory — they are not further facts over and above the monadic facts about the relata.

Supervenience is a relation of ontological dependence that can be defined as follows: A-facts supervene on B-facts just in case there cannot be a difference in the A-facts without a corresponding difference in the B-facts. The internal/external distinction is then: a relation R is internal iff the R-facts supervene on the intrinsic-property facts about the relata.

David Lewis made extensive use of the internal/external distinction in his defense of Humean supervenience — the thesis that all facts about the world supervene on the distribution of intrinsic properties at spatio-temporal points. If resemblance and other internal relations supervene on intrinsic properties, then the Humean mosaic (the pattern of local, intrinsic, particular facts) determines everything else about the world, including modal facts, nomic facts, and causal facts.

The supervenience of internal relations also has implications for the debate about whether tropes or universals are needed to ground property-talk. If resemblance supervenes on the intrinsic natures of the relata, and if the intrinsic natures are themselves tropes (particular property-instances), then resemblance relations among tropes are determined by the tropes' natures — no further entity (the universal they share) is required. This is one reason why trope bundle theories can be combined with a nominalist rejection of universals without falling into complete arbitrariness about resemblance.

Critics of the supervenience claim note that *some* resemblance relations seem to hold across qualitatively dissimilar objects — two objects can resemble each other in some respects while differing dramatically in others, and the partial resemblance relations among diverse objects do not obviously supervene on any one intrinsic property. This suggests that resemblance may require relational facts not fully determined by monadic properties.

## Supervenience Defined

Several varieties of supervenience are relevant:

- **Local supervenience**: For any possible worlds w₁ and w₂ and any objects a (in w₁) and b (in w₂) that are duplicates (share all intrinsic properties), a and b also share all their internal relations to their respective counterparts.
- **Global supervenience**: If two possible worlds agree on all the intrinsic property facts, they agree on all internal relational facts.
- **Strong supervenience**: Necessarily, for any possible objects a and b that are duplicates, a stands in the same internal relations as b.

The strong supervenience formulation most directly captures the intended notion: internal relations are those that cannot differ between intrinsic duplicates. This is Lewis's preferred formulation in "Extrinsic Properties" (1983).

The crucial question is what counts as a "duplicate." Lewis's account relies on *perfectly natural* properties: duplicates are objects that share all their perfectly natural properties and stand in all the same perfectly natural relations (to their own parts). Internal relations are then those shared by all such duplicates.

## The Humean Supervenience Thesis

Lewis's Humean supervenience (HS) thesis holds that the entire distribution of facts about the actual world supervenes on the *Humean mosaic* — the pattern of local, intrinsic, perfectly natural facts distributed across spacetime points. HS can be stated:

> **HS**: Necessarily, any two worlds alike in the distribution of local, intrinsic, perfectly natural properties and perfectly natural external (spatiotemporal) relations are alike in all other respects.

If HS is true, then:

1. Internal relations supervene on intrinsic properties — by definition.
2. *External* spatiotemporal relations are the only primitive external relations.
3. All nomic, causal, and dispositional facts supervene on the Humean mosaic.

This last claim is what makes HS controversial. Dispositionalists deny it: dispositional properties are not reducible to categorical intrinsic properties. If charge is essentially a power to attract and repel, then charge-facts cannot supervene on non-dispositional intrinsic properties.

## Resemblance and Supervenience

The claim that resemblance is internal — that it supervenes on the intrinsic properties of the resembling objects — is central to nominalist projects. If two objects resemble because they both have a certain intrinsic feature (redness), then resemblance supervenes on that intrinsic feature and is not a further primitive relational entity.

However, degrees of resemblance complicate this picture. Overall resemblance is a matter of degree: two objects can resemble each other greatly (sharing many intrinsic properties) or slightly (sharing few). Overall resemblance does not supervene on any single intrinsic property but on the full distribution of intrinsic properties across the compared objects. This is still supervenience on intrinsic properties — just a complex, multidimensional supervenience rather than a simple one.

The deeper challenge is *partial resemblance* — resemblance in a specific respect. Two objects resemble in their color but not in their shape. To fix resemblance in a specific respect, we need to identify the respect — which is itself a property. But identifying the relevant property may reintroduce universals (the property of redness grounds color resemblance), which is exactly what the nominalist hoped to avoid. Supervenience of resemblance on intrinsic properties is thus harder to maintain in a purely nominalist framework than it first appears.
