# Important Figures — Relations

*Key philosophers in debates about the ontology of relations, structural realism, and grounding.*

---

**F. H. Bradley (1846–1924)** — His regress argument against the reality of relations — that any relation requires a further relating relation, *ad infinitum* — was the starting point for twentieth-century debates. *Appearance and Reality* (1893).

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — Refuted Bradley's idealism about relations and argued that asymmetric relations (particularly spatial ones) cannot be reduced to intrinsic properties. *The Problems of Philosophy* (1912) and *Principles of Mathematics* (1903).

**G. E. Moore (1873–1958)** — Distinguished internal from external relations and used this distinction to refute the monism of Bradley and the idealists.

**John Worrall (1946–)** — Introduced *epistemic structural realism* as a response to the pessimistic meta-induction. "Structural Realism: The Best of Both Worlds?" (1989).

**James Ladyman (1969–)** — Developed *ontic structural realism* (with Don Ross), arguing that structure is all there is and individual objects with intrinsic natures are a fiction. *Every Thing Must Go* (2007).

**Kit Fine (1946–)** — Introduced the grounding relation as a fundamental metaphysical primitive and distinguished it from supervenience. "The Question of Realism" (2001) and subsequent papers.

**Jonathan Schaffer (1970–)** — Developed and defended the concept of grounding extensively. "On What Grounds What" (2009) in *Metametaphysics* (ed. Chalmers, Manley, Wasserman) is the key statement.

**E. J. Lowe (1950–2014)** — Used ontological dependence as the central organizing concept of his four-category ontology.
