# Important Terms and Ideas: Relations (II)

**Supervenience**: A relation of determination between levels of description. The mental supervenes on the physical if there can be no mental difference without a physical difference. Supervenience is weaker than reduction or identity.

**Truthmaking relation**: The relation between a fact or state of affairs and the propositions it makes true. The truthmaking relation is not causal but metaphysical: the obtaining of a state of affairs necessitates the truth of the corresponding proposition.

**Grounding relation**: The metaphysical relation of non-causal explanation: A grounds B if B obtains because A does. Grounding is irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive — a partial ordering of facts from fundamental to derivative.

**Part-whole relation (parthood)**: The mereological relation of being a part of something. Classical mereology specifies axioms governing parthood, overlap, and fusion. Whether parthood is a primitive relation or analyzable in other terms is debated.

**Instantiation**: The relation between a particular and a universal (or between an object and a property) whereby the object has the property. Instantiation is the fundamental tie in the realist framework.

**Resemblance**: A relation of similarity between objects or tropes. For resemblance nominalists, resemblance is primitive and unanalyzable; for universalists, resemblance is grounded in shared universals.

**Causal relation**: The relation between causes and their effects. Theories differ on whether causation is a genuine relation between distinct events (Hume: regularity; Lewis: counterfactual) or a relation grounded in the natures of the events (powers ontology).
