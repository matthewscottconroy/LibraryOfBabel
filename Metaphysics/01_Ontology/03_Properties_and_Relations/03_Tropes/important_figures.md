# Important Figures: Tropes

**D.C. Williams** (1899–1983) is credited with giving trope theory its modern form in his 1953 paper "On the Elements of Being." He called them "abstract particulars" and argued they were the fundamental constituents of reality.

**Keith Campbell** (b. 1938) developed Williams's trope theory into a comprehensive metaphysical system in *Abstract Particulars* (1990), arguing that tropes could replace both universals and particular substrata.

**Peter Simons** (b. 1950) analyzed the bundling of tropes into objects via nuclear and peripheral trope dependencies, developing a nuanced account of individual substances as trope structures.

**Gonzalo Rodriguez-Pereyra** (b. 1970) has argued for trope theory from the perspective of resemblance nominalism, developing a careful account of how tropes and their resemblances ground property attribution.

**John Heil** (b. 1942) defends a type-type identity view on which every property is both a quality and a power, using trope-friendly language while resisting full trope-bundle reductionism.
