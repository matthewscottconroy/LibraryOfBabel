# Important Terms and Ideas: Tropes

**Trope**: A particular property-instance — this specific redness of this specific rose. Tropes are non-repeatable: numerically distinct across different objects, even if qualitatively identical. Also called "abstract particulars," "modes," or "unit properties."

**Trope bundle theory**: The view that ordinary particular objects are bundles or collections of co-present tropes. The object is nothing over and above its tropes considered together; there is no substratum or bare particular.

**Compresence**: The bundling relation that holds among tropes constituting a single individual. Tropes that are co-located, spatiotemporally coincident, or mutually dependent are said to be compresent.

**Nuclear vs. peripheral tropes**: Simons's distinction between the essential tropes that constitute an object's core nature (nuclear) and the accidental tropes that the object has contingently (peripheral). Nuclear tropes are mutually dependent; peripheral tropes depend on the nuclear core.

**Resemblance among tropes**: The relation that grounds property predication on trope theories. Two objects are both red because their redness-tropes resemble each other. The challenge is explaining this resemblance without invoking universals.

**Trope causation**: The use of tropes as causal relata. Rather than whole events causing each other, it is specific tropes — this particular heat, this particular molecular configuration — that cause specific effects. Trope causation supports fine-grained causal explanations.

**Powerful qualities**: Heil's view that every genuine property is simultaneously a quality (it is what it is, categorically) and a power (it has a specific causal role). The categorical/dispositional distinction does not track a real difference in properties.

**Abstract particular**: Williams's preferred term for tropes. "Abstract" because tropes are aspects or aspects of their bearers, not concrete substances; "particular" because they are numerically unrepeatable and non-shareable.
