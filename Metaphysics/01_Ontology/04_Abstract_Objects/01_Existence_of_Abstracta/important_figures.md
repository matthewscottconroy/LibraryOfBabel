# Important Figures: Existence of Abstracta

**Plato** (428-348 BCE) provided the original and most enduring case for abstract objects: the Forms are eternal, unchanging, non-spatial entities that ground the intelligibility and order of the sensible world.

**Gottlob Frege** (1848-1925) argued that numbers are abstract objects — objective, non-mental, non-physical entities — and that arithmetic truths are analytic truths about these objects. His work established the modern framework for the debate.

**W.V.O. Quine** (1908-2000) developed the indispensability argument (with Putnam): since mathematics is indispensable to science, and we should be ontologically committed to the entities that scientific theories quantify over, we should believe in mathematical objects.

**Hartry Field** (b. 1946) wrote *Science Without Numbers* (1980), arguing that mathematics is conservative but not necessary: science can be nominalized, and we need not believe in mathematical objects.

**Paul Benacerraf** (b. 1931) identified the central epistemological challenge: if mathematical objects are non-causal and causally inert, how can we have knowledge of them? His twin challenges (from 1965 and 1973) have shaped the debate ever since.
