# Important Terms and Ideas: Existence of Abstracta

**Abstract object**: An object that is not located in space or time and does not enter into causal relations. Paradigm examples: numbers, sets, propositions, universals, types, possible worlds (on some views).

**Platonism about abstracta**: The view that abstract objects exist independently of minds and the physical world — that mathematical and logical truths are truths about a mind-independent abstract realm.

**Causal inertness**: The property of not being able to cause or be caused. Abstract objects are typically held to be causally inert: the number 7 does not cause anything and is not produced by anything.

**Benacerraf's challenge**: The epistemological problem for Platonism. If abstract objects are causally inert, and knowledge requires causal connection between knower and known, then abstract objects are unknowable. Yet mathematical knowledge seems paradigmatically secure.

**Indispensability argument**: The Quine-Putnam argument that we should believe in abstract mathematical objects because they are indispensable to our best scientific theories. If physics quantifies over numbers, we are ontologically committed to numbers.

**Nominalism about abstracta**: The view that there are no abstract objects. All that exists is the concrete physical world. Mathematical statements either describe concrete objects (in unusual ways) or are not strictly true.

**Fictionalism**: The view that mathematical statements are not literally true but are useful fictions. We speak as if there are numbers, but there are none; the fiction is useful for deriving truths about the concrete world.

**Immanent vs. transcendent abstracta**: Immanent abstract objects (if they exist) are somehow "in" the concrete world; transcendent abstract objects exist wholly independently of it. The debate between Armstrong's immanent universals and Platonic Forms illustrates this distinction.
