# References and Primary Sources: Existence of Abstracta

## Foundational Texts

Plato. *Phaedo*, 74a-78e. Trans. Grube. Hackett, 1977. The argument from the Forms: abstract entities (equal itself, beautiful itself) are required to explain the deficient participation of sensible things.

Frege, Gottlob. *The Foundations of Arithmetic*. Trans. Austin. Blackwell, 1950. §§55-69 argue that numbers are abstract objects, neither mental nor physical; one of the most influential arguments for Platonism.

## Contemporary Works

Benacerraf, Paul. "Mathematical Truth." *Journal of Philosophy* 70 (1973): 661-679. The epistemological challenge for Platonism: if abstract objects are causally inert, how can we know about them?

Field, Hartry. *Science Without Numbers*. Princeton University Press, 1980. The most influential nominalist program; argues that mathematics is conservative over physics.

Balaguer, Mark. *Platonism and Anti-Platonism in Mathematics*. Oxford University Press, 1998. Surveys and assesses both Platonist and anti-Platonist positions with unusual even-handedness.

Rosen, Gideon. "Abstract Objects." *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2020. The definitive overview of what abstract objects are and the main debates about their existence.
