# Types, Tokens, and Kinds

*The distinction between abstract types and their concrete instantiations.*

---

The type/token distinction, introduced by Peirce, separates abstract patterns or kinds from their concrete instantiations. A *type* is an abstract category; a *token* is a concrete particular that instantiates the type. The word "the" is a type; this occurrence of "the" in this sentence is a token. Beethoven's Fifth Symphony is a type; the performance you attended last week was a token. The letter "e" is a type; each printed "e" is a token.

Types and tokens serve importantly different roles. Tokens are spatiotemporally located events or objects; types are not. The question "where is Beethoven's Fifth Symphony?" does not admit of a simple answer: the symphony is wherever performances of it are, but it is not *identical* with any particular performance and is not tied to any particular location. The symphony persists through the addition or deletion of one performance in a way that no particular performance does.

The type/token distinction raises immediate ontological questions. Are types abstract objects — genuine entities in addition to the tokens? Or is type-talk merely a convenient way of grouping tokens? The Platonist about types holds that Beethoven's Fifth genuinely exists as an abstract object, discovered rather than created by Beethoven. The nominalist holds that there is no type-object, only the class of tokens and our practice of treating them as performances of "the same piece."

Music provides a productive case study. Is the work identical to its score (Goodman)? Is it the class of all its performances? Is it a type that can be fully instantiated by a correct performance (Platonist view)? Composers can revise works, and performances can diverge; these complications put pressure on all simple accounts.

The type/token distinction is also central to philosophy of mind: is a mental state type (pain) identical to a physical state type (C-fiber firing), or only the particular tokens? Type identity theory and token identity theory are the two main versions of the mind-brain identity theory, and their differences have significant implications for functionalism and the multiple realizability argument.

## Types as Abstract Objects

If types are genuine entities, they appear to be abstract: they lack determinate spatiotemporal location, they can be multiply instantiated, and they are not causally efficacious in the way concrete particulars are.

Three accounts of types as abstract objects:

**Platonic types**: Types exist in a mind-independent abstract realm. The word-type "the" exists necessarily and unchangingly; tokens are its concrete instantiations in particular written or spoken contexts. On this view, when we coin a new word, we are not creating a type but discovering one — or at least determining that a certain type is to be used in language.

**Type-kinds**: Types are natural kinds — the kind *tiger*, the kind *electron*, the kind *water*. Natural kinds, on the Kripke-Putnam view, are discovered by science; their membership is determined by the intrinsic nature of kind members (the microstructure of water is H₂O) rather than by stipulation. If natural kinds are genuine abstract entities, they are types in a philosophically demanding sense.

**Constructed types**: Types are constructed by cognitive or social processes — they are concepts or collections organized by human thought and practice. The "type" corresponding to a symphony is determined by the norms of musical performance and the conventions of the musical community. This view treats types as less robustly abstract than Platonic objects: they are mind-dependent in the sense of being constituted by practices.

## Tokens and Individuation

Tokens are individuated by their spatiotemporal properties and by their causal histories. Two tokens of the same type are numerically distinct despite sharing type membership:

- This written "e" and that written "e" are numerically distinct tokens of the same letter-type, even if they look identical.
- This performance of the Fifth Symphony and yesterday's performance are numerically distinct tokens of the same musical work.

The individuation of tokens is relatively unproblematic: tokens are concrete particulars, and concrete particulars are individuated by spatiotemporal location and causal history. But the individuation of types raises harder questions.

**What makes two tokens tokens of the same type?** 

- *Resemblance*: tokens of the same type resemble each other in relevant respects. But resemblance is too coarse: two different letter-types might resemble each other more than two tokens of the same type printed in different fonts.
- *Causal history*: tokens of the same type have the appropriate kind of causal connection to the type — they are produced by processes that aim to instantiate or reproduce the type. A performance of the Fifth Symphony is produced by musicians following Beethoven's score.
- *Normative correctness*: tokens of the same type are those that correctly instantiate the type according to relevant norms. A performance that does not follow Beethoven's score sufficiently closely is not a performance of the Fifth Symphony at all.

## Natural Kinds

Natural kinds are a philosophically significant subset of types. Where conventional types (word-types, musical works) are defined by human conventions and intentions, natural kinds are defined by objective features of nature.

**The Kripke-Putnam account**: Natural kinds like *gold*, *water*, *tiger*, and *electron* have essences determined by their intrinsic nature. "Water" refers to the natural kind whose actual instances are H₂O; it refers to H₂O in all possible worlds, even worlds where humans call H₂O something else. The essence of a natural kind is discovered by science, not stipulated by convention.

This account has important ontological implications:
- Natural kinds are rigid — they pick out the same kind in all possible worlds.
- Natural kind membership is not purely phenomenological — something that looks like water but is XYZ (Putnam's "Twin Earth") is not water.
- The identity of a natural kind is determined by its microstructure or fundamental nature, not by its superficial properties.

**Are natural kinds abstract?**: Natural kinds (gold, water, the electron) are not concrete particulars, yet they seem different from Platonic mathematical objects. They are intimately connected to the concrete instances that constitute them. Armstrong treats natural kinds as universals — immanent rather than transcendent abstract objects. Lewis identifies the natural kind *tiger* with a perfectly natural property. Kripke and Putnam treat them as requiring direct reference without giving a detailed ontological analysis.

## The Metaphysics of Artworks

The ontology of artworks provides the most concrete illustration of the type/token framework. Artworks present a challenging case because they seem to be types — distinct from any particular physical object — but also seem to be created, modified, and potentially destroyed.

**Goodman's view** (*Languages of Art*, 1968): Musical works are defined by their scores; a performance instantiates the work iff it conforms completely to the score in the relevant respects (note for note, instrument for instrument, dynamic for dynamic). On this *allographic* account of musical works (in contrast to *autographic* works like paintings, where authenticity requires the original physical object), the work is a set of correct performances.

*Problem*: Musicians intentionally deviate from scores; performances include elements (tempo, dynamics, expression) not fully specified by scores. Goodman's strict conformity criterion is too demanding for actual musical practice.

**Wollheim's view** (*Art and Its Objects*, 1968): Artworks are types in a Platonic sense. Beethoven's Fifth is an abstract object; performances are its tokens. The type exists independently of any particular token, which is why we can speak of hearing "the symphony" by attending any correct performance.

**Levinson's view**: Musical works are *indicated types* — not pre-existing Platonic objects but types created by composers through a complex act of indication involving the relevant musical tradition and performance norms. This splits the difference: types are not fully mind-independent Platonic objects, but they are not reducible to classes of tokens either.

## Types, Tokens, and Multiple Realizability

The type/token distinction has direct implications for the mind-body problem. The debate between type and token identity theory:

**Type identity theory**: Mental state types are identical with physical state types. Pain-type = C-fiber-firing-type. Every pain is a C-fiber firing; the type of state that pain is is the same type as the state of having C-fibers fire.

*Objection* (multiple realizability, Putnam): The same mental state type (pain) can be realized by different physical state types in different organisms. An octopus has pain without having C-fibers. So pain-type cannot be identical with C-fiber-firing-type — because the octopus's pain is not a C-fiber firing.

**Token identity theory**: Each particular mental event (token) is identical with some particular physical event (token), but there is no requirement that the same mental type be realized by the same physical type across different organisms. My current pain is token-identical with some neural event; an octopus's pain is token-identical with a different neural event. The mental and physical types are not identified.

Token identity theory is compatible with functionalism — the view that mental state types are defined by their functional role (causal relations to inputs, outputs, and other mental states) rather than by any particular physical realization. Functional types are abstract types; their tokens can be physically diverse.
