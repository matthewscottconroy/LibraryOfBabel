# Exercises and Thought Experiments — Ontology of Abstracta

*Problems for engaging with abstract objects, mathematics, and their epistemology.*

---

**Exercise 1 — What Is Abstract?**
Classify the following as abstract or concrete, and explain your reasoning: (a) the number π; (b) a performance of Beethoven's Fifth Symphony; (c) the law of gravity; (d) the property of being red; (e) the color red; (f) the University of Oxford (the institution, not the buildings). Are there any cases where the abstract/concrete distinction seems to break down?

**Exercise 2 — Benacerraf's Identification Problem**
Explain why there is no fact about which sets the natural numbers are, on Benacerraf's argument. What follows for the ontology of numbers? Compare two responses: (a) numbers are abstract objects that are not sets (there is a further fact, just not a set-theoretic one); (b) numbers are positions in structures, not objects at all. Which response do you find more convincing?

**Exercise 3 — The Indispensability Argument**
State the Quine-Putnam indispensability argument. Field responds by attempting to show that mathematics is conservative — that nominalist physics plus mathematics yields no new nominalist conclusions. Why would conservatism undermine the indispensability argument? Is conservatism the same as dispensability? What would it mean for mathematics to be genuinely indispensable (not merely conservative)?

**Exercise 4 — Benacerraf's Epistemological Challenge**
State the epistemological challenge to Platonism about mathematics. Evaluate two responses: (a) we have a faculty of mathematical intuition (Gödel); (b) we know mathematics indirectly through science (Quine). Does either response adequately explain how we can have a priori mathematical knowledge? Is there a third option?

**Thought Experiment — The Vanishing Universe**
Suppose the physical universe were destroyed — all matter and energy ceased to exist. Would the number 7 still exist? Would it still be true that 7 is prime? If you think mathematical objects would survive the destruction of the physical universe, does this commit you to Platonism? If you think they would not, what are the consequences for mathematical truth?
