# Important Figures — Ontology of Abstracta

*Key philosophers in debates about abstract objects, mathematics, and their ontological status.*

---

**Gottlob Frege (1848–1925)** — Developed the logicist program — the reduction of arithmetic to logic — and articulated the concept of an objective, non-mental *thought* (Gedanke) as the bearer of truth. His *Foundations of Arithmetic* (1884) and *Basic Laws of Arithmetic* (1893–1903) are foundational.

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — Discovered the paradox in Frege's set theory (1902), developed type theory as a response, and proposed *Russellian* propositions (structured by objects and properties). *Introduction to Mathematical Philosophy* (1919).

**Kurt Gödel (1906–1978)** — The incompleteness theorems showed the limits of formalism. Gödel himself was a committed mathematical Platonist who believed in mathematical intuition as a faculty for perceiving abstract objects.

**W. V. O. Quine (1908–2000)** — The indispensability argument for mathematical objects and the criterion of ontological commitment (what we quantify over, we're committed to). *From a Logical Point of View* (1953) and *Word and Object* (1960).

**Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)** — Extended the indispensability argument to include physical applications of mathematics, arguing mathematics is genuinely indispensable rather than a notational convenience.

**Paul Benacerraf (1931–)** — "What Numbers Could Not Be" (1965) and "Mathematical Truth" (1973) set the agenda for subsequent philosophy of mathematics with the identification problem and the epistemological challenge.

**Hartry Field (1946–)** — *Science Without Numbers* (1980) attempted to show that mathematics can be nominalized, mounting the most rigorous anti-Platonist challenge.

**Stewart Shapiro (1951–)** — Developer of *ante rem* structuralism in *Philosophy of Mathematics: Structure and Ontology* (1997).
