# Important Terms and Ideas: Ontology of Abstracta

**Encoding vs. exemplifying**: Zalta's distinction between two ways of having a property. Concrete objects exemplify properties (a ball exemplifies sphericity); abstract objects encode them (the abstract object representing Sherlock Holmes encodes the property of being a detective without exemplifying it).

**Abstract artifact**: Thomasson's term for abstract objects that are created by human intentional activity — fictional characters, corporations, legal statuses, musical works. Unlike mathematical objects, they depend on human practices for their existence.

**Type vs. token**: A type is an abstract kind or pattern (the word "cat"); a token is a concrete instance of it (a particular inscription of "cat"). This distinction applies to words, sentences, musical works, and other abstract entities.

**Abstracta as maximally consistent sets**: One approach to abstract objects defines them as maximal sets of properties; an abstract object exists whenever a consistent set of properties can be specified. Used in Zalta's and Parsons's neo-Meinongian theories.

**The Fregean third realm**: Frege's description of abstract objects as belonging to a "third realm" distinct from the external physical world (first realm) and the internal mental world (second realm). Abstract objects are objective but non-physical.

**Structural description**: An abstract object is sometimes characterized by a structural description — a specification of the formal relations it stands in to other objects. Mathematical structures are abstract objects of this kind.

**Existence-independence**: The property of not depending on any mind or mental act for one's existence. Typically attributed to abstract mathematical objects: the number 7 would exist even if no one had ever counted or thought about it.
