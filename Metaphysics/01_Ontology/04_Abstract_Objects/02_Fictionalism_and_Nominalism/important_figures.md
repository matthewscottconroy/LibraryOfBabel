# Important Figures — Fictionalism and Nominalism

*Key philosophers in nominalist and fictionalist traditions about abstract objects and properties.*

---

**William of Ockham (c. 1287–1347)** — The founding figure of nominalism, whose rejection of universals and insistence on ontological parsimony established the tradition.

**Nelson Goodman (1906–1998)** — Systematic nominalist who (with Quine) attempted to reconstruct mathematics nominalistically. His *The Structure of Appearance* (1951) and nominalist program were influential.

**W. V. O. Quine (1908–2000)** — Both the indispensability argument (for abstract objects in mathematics) and, with Goodman, the nominalist program. Quine's final position reluctantly accepted abstract objects on indispensability grounds. *Word and Object* (1960).

**Hartry Field (1946–)** — *Science Without Numbers* (1980), the most rigorous modern nominalist program for mathematics. Also "Realism, Mathematics and Modality" (1989) on fictionalism.

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Developed the appeal to *naturalness* to explain the distinction between genuine and gerrymandered properties within a broadly nominalist framework (accepting classes but not universals).

**Mark Balaguer (1963–)** — *Platonism and Anti-Platonism in Mathematics* (1998) defends both full-blooded Platonism and fictionalism as equally coherent, arguing for underdetermination between them.

**Amie Thomasson (1968–)** — Deflationary "easy ontology" developed in *Ordinary Objects* (2007) and *Ontology Made Easy* (2015).

**Gonzalo Rodriguez-Pereyra (1966–)** — Contemporary defender of resemblance nominalism. *Resemblance Nominalism: A Solution to the Problem of Universals* (2002).
