# Important Terms and Ideas: Fictionalism and Nominalism

**Mathematical fictionalism**: The view that mathematical statements like "7 is prime" are not literally true because there are no mathematical objects. Mathematics is a useful fiction — like novels, it is fruitful to engage with even if its existential claims are false.

**Conservativeness**: Field's claim that mathematics is a conservative extension over nominalist physics — it does not prove any nominalistic consequences that cannot be proved without it. If true, mathematics is useful shorthand, not a source of new physical truths.

**Hermeneutic fictionalism**: The view that mathematical discourse is already understood (even by ordinary practitioners) as fictional rather than literal. Contrasts with revolutionary fictionalism, which proposes we should start treating it as fictional.

**Revolutionary fictionalism**: The revisionary view that, even if mathematical discourse purports to describe real abstract objects, we should recognize this as false and adopt a fictionalist understanding.

**Nominalization**: The process of reformulating a theory that quantifies over abstract objects into one that does not. Field nominalized Newtonian mechanics; whether all of physics can be nominalized is an open question.

**Indispensability and dispensability**: The question of whether abstract objects are indispensable to science. Field argues they are dispensable; if so, the indispensability argument for Platonism fails.

**Modal nominalism**: Hellman's strategy of replacing mathematical claims with modal claims — claims about what would hold in any possible omega-sequence satisfying the Peano axioms — avoiding commitment to actual abstract objects.

**Pretense theory**: A version of fictionalism on which mathematical discourse involves a kind of cooperative make-believe, governed by implicit pretense that numbers exist.
