# Ante Rem Structuralism

Ante rem structuralism, associated primarily with Stewart Shapiro and Michael Resnik, holds that abstract mathematical structures exist independently of and prior to any systems that might instantiate them. The natural number structure — with its specific pattern of elements and successor relations — exists as an abstract object even if no concrete system satisfies it. Mathematical objects are positions in these structures, and their only properties are the structural ones: the number 2 has no intrinsic nature beyond its structural position as the successor of the successor of 0.

This is an explicitly Platonist view. Ante rem structuralism agrees with traditional Platonism that mathematical objects are abstract and mind-independent. Where it differs is in insisting that the fundamental mathematical entities are structures, not individual objects. Numbers, sets, and other mathematical entities are not free-floating abstract objects; they are positions in abstract structures.

Shapiro argues that ante rem structuralism avoids the arbitrariness problem Benacerraf raised. Since numbers are positions in the natural number structure rather than particular objects (like particular sets), there is no question about which sets the numbers are — numbers are just natural number positions, period. Arithmetic truths hold of those positions, not of any particular set-theoretic representatives.

The view also has a natural account of mathematical ontology: a mathematical theory is satisfiable if it has a coherent model, and every coherent mathematical theory describes a genuine abstract structure. This gives a generous ontology of mathematical structures while grounding their existence in the coherence of the theories that characterize them.

Critics argue that ante rem structuralism faces the structuralist's own identity problem: if mathematical objects are defined purely by their structural roles, then the number 2 in the natural number structure seems to be the same as the number 2 in the integer structure. Shapiro responds that these are different positions in different structures that happen to share structural features at certain levels of abstraction.

## The Metaphysics of Structures

On Shapiro's ante rem account, a mathematical structure is an abstract object consisting of:

- A collection of *places* or *positions* (the elements of the structure)
- A collection of *relations* and *functions* on those positions (the structural features)
- The *relations hold* among positions in the abstract structure itself, not just among any concrete instantiations

Formally: a structure ⟨D, R₁, R₂, ...⟩ consists of a domain D of positions and relations R₁, R₂, ... defined on D. The natural number structure is ⟨ω, 0, S, +, ×, <⟩ where ω is a countably infinite domain, 0 is a designated initial element, S is the successor function, and the operations +, ×, < are defined on ω.

The key claim of ante rem structuralism: this structure *exists as an abstract object*, independently of any concrete omega-sequences. The positions in the structure — the natural numbers — have their properties (being prime, being a successor, being an even number) by virtue of the structural relations, not by virtue of any intrinsic non-structural properties.

**Structuralist constraint on objects**: Mathematical objects (numbers, groups, topological spaces) have no intrinsic properties beyond their structural properties. The number 3 is not intrinsically anything — it has no color, no mass, no location, no membership relations except those defined by its structural role (being prime, being odd, being between 2 and 4, etc.).

## The Epistemology of Ante Rem Structures

One of the advantages Shapiro claims for ante rem structuralism is its epistemology. Mathematical knowledge does not require non-causal contact with a mysterious Platonic realm; it requires recognition of structural patterns.

We learn arithmetic by manipulating concrete objects — counting fingers, adding stones, dividing pebbles. Through this concrete practice, we come to grasp the abstract structure that all such collections share. The structure is then studied in its own right, independently of any particular instantiation.

Shapiro's epistemological model:
1. We encounter concrete systems that exhibit structural features.
2. Through pattern recognition, we extract the abstract structure from its concrete instantiation.
3. We then study the abstract structure directly — proving theorems about it that hold of all instantiations.

This model explains how finite, embodied creatures can gain knowledge of infinite abstract structures: through pattern recognition in concrete instantiations and abstraction to the general pattern.

**The challenge**: Pattern recognition can give us knowledge of the structural features we observe in concrete instantiations. But ante rem structures exist necessarily and have structural properties not visible in any finite instantiation (for example, structural facts about all infinite omega-sequences). How does pattern recognition from finite concrete cases give us knowledge of infinite abstract structures?

Shapiro's response: mathematical practice includes not just pattern recognition but also proof — deductive reasoning from structural axioms. The axioms capture the abstract structure, and proof extends our knowledge of the structure's properties beyond what pattern recognition alone could provide.

## The Identity Problem Across Structures

A distinctive challenge for ante rem structuralism: the same mathematical object appears in multiple structures. The natural number 2, the integer 2, the rational 2, the real 2, and the complex 2 are all called "2" and share certain structural properties (being even, being a predecessor of 3, etc.). Are they the same object or different objects?

**Option 1 (Distinct objects)**: Each "2" is a distinct position in a distinct structure. The natural number 2 in ℕ is numerically distinct from the rational 2 in ℚ. The structures are connected by *embedding morphisms* — structure-preserving maps that identify the natural number 2 with a corresponding position in ℚ — but the objects remain distinct.

*Problem*: This multiplies objects unnecessarily and seems to conflict with mathematical practice, where "2" is used without qualification to denote the same thing across contexts.

**Option 2 (Identical objects)**: The "2" in ℕ is the same object as the "2" in ℚ. But this requires that a single position can belong to multiple structures, which raises questions about what individuates positions if not their membership in a single structure.

Shapiro opts for Option 1 with qualifications: positions are individuated within their structures, but we have linguistic conventions that allow us to use the same numeral "2" for corresponding positions in different structures. The structures are not identical but are *coherently related* through embedding morphisms, so the same numeral can be used systematically.

## Ante Rem Structuralism and the Coherence of Mathematics

Shapiro's ante rem structuralism grounds mathematical existence in structural coherence: a mathematical structure exists if and only if its characterizing axioms are satisfiable (have a model in standard mathematical terms, or are logically coherent). This gives a principled ontology: not every description generates an existing structure, but every coherent description does.

This is sometimes called the *coherence criterion*: if the axioms for a mathematical structure are satisfiable, the structure exists. Non-Euclidean geometries exist as structures because their axioms are satisfiable (even though Euclid's parallel postulate is denied). The quaternions exist as a structure because Hamilton's axioms for them are satisfiable.

**The connection to full-blooded Platonism**: Balaguer's full-blooded Platonism (FBP) holds that all logically possible mathematical structures exist. This is close to Shapiro's view, with the difference that Shapiro requires *coherent satisfiability* (there must be models) rather than mere logical possibility. On both views, the mathematical universe is very large — nearly every consistent mathematical description corresponds to an existing structure.
