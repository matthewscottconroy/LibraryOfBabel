# References and Primary Sources: Numbers and Sets

## Foundational Texts

Dedekind, Richard. *What Are Numbers and What Should They Be?* Trans. Pogorzelski, Ryan, and Wood. Research Institute for Mathematics, 1995. The foundational analysis of natural numbers as positions in a simply infinite system — a precursor to structuralism.

Frege, Gottlob. *The Foundations of Arithmetic*. Trans. Austin. Blackwell, 1950. The logicist reduction of arithmetic to logic via the concept of number as the extension of an equivalence class of concepts.

## Contemporary Works

Benacerraf, Paul. "What Numbers Could Not Be." *Philosophical Review* 74 (1965): 47-73. The decisive argument that numbers cannot be identified with any particular set-theoretic construction.

Shapiro, Stewart. *Philosophy of Mathematics: Structure and Ontology*. Oxford University Press, 1997. The canonical statement of ante rem structuralism about numbers.

Boolos, George. "The Iterative Conception of Set." *Journal of Philosophy* 68 (1971): 215-231. A philosophical foundation for the iterative (cumulative) conception of the set-theoretic hierarchy.

Maddy, Penelope. *Realism in Mathematics*. Oxford University Press, 1990. A naturalist Platonism that grounds access to sets in perception of physical aggregates.
