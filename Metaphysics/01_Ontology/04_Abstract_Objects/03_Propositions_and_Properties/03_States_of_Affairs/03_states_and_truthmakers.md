# States of Affairs and Truthmakers

The theory of states of affairs is intimately connected to truthmaker theory — the view that for every true proposition, there is something in the world (a truthmaker) in virtue of which it is true. Truth is not merely a matter of propositions; it is grounded in how things are. The state of affairs composed of Socrates and wisdom is what makes "Socrates is wise" true.

This connection gives states of affairs a dual role: they are both the fundamental ontological constituents of the world and the truthmakers for true propositions. If we want to know what makes a proposition true, we look for the state of affairs that grounds it.

The truthmaker project raises immediate questions. Not every true proposition seems to be made true by a single, obvious state of affairs. What makes "there are no unicorns" true? There is no unicorn-state of affairs for this negative existential to correspond to. Armstrong responds by positing "totality facts" — higher-order states of affairs that record what states of affairs there are in total — which then ground negative existentials.

What makes a counterfactual true? The truthmaking for counterfactuals is especially difficult, since counterfactuals seem to describe situations that do not obtain. Lewis's possible-worlds analysis makes counterfactuals true in virtue of facts about similar possible worlds, but this requires either that possible worlds are abstract objects (Platonism) or that they are concrete worlds (Lewis's extreme realism).

States of affairs also connect to Armstrong's theory of laws. A law of nature is a necessitation relation N(F, G) between universals F and G — itself a state of affairs. This higher-order state of affairs is the truthmaker for the law statement "It is a law that all Fs are G" and, in turn, grounds the truth of all the instances of the law.

The truthmaker project, with states of affairs at its center, represents one of the most systematic attempts to give a comprehensive account of how truth depends on reality.

## Truthmaker Theory: The Core Principle

The truthmaker principle has several formulations of varying strength:

**Weak truthmaker principle**: Every contingent truth has a truthmaker — some entity whose existence necessitates the truth of the proposition.

**Strong truthmaker principle**: Every truth has a truthmaker — including necessary truths (mathematical truths, logical truths) and negative existential truths.

**Maximalism**: For every true proposition, there is a minimal truthmaker — a truthmaker that is not a truthmaker in virtue of containing a proper part that is also a truthmaker.

Armstrong defends the strong truthmaker principle: every truth, including mathematical truths, is made true by something in the world. Mathematical truths are made true by states of affairs involving mathematical structures or by the intrinsic features of the mathematical realm (on Platonism) or by the logical structure of possibilities (on modalist accounts).

**The formal principle**: For any proposition P, there exists an entity x such that:
- x exists, and
- Necessarily, if x exists, then P is true.

The entity x is the truthmaker for P.

## The Truthmaking Relation

What is the relationship between a truthmaker and the proposition it makes true? Several characterizations:

**Entailment**: x makes P true iff x's existence necessitates P — iff in every possible world in which x exists, P is true. This is the standard characterization.

*Problem*: Socrates's existence seems to necessitate "there are human beings" (given that Socrates is necessarily a human being). But it seems odd to say that Socrates is the truthmaker for "there are human beings" — the relevant truthmaker seems to be the state of affairs of Socrates being human, not Socrates himself.

**Grounding-based**: x makes P true iff P's truth is *grounded* in x — P is true *because* x exists, and this because-relation is a metaphysically fundamental relation. This account uses grounding rather than mere necessitation to characterize truthmaking.

**State-of-affairs accounts**: States of affairs are the natural truthmakers. The state of affairs [Socrates, Wisdom] is the truthmaker for "Socrates is wise": its existence necessitates the truth of the proposition, and its existence consists in Socrates instantiating Wisdom.

## Problematic Truthmakers

Several categories of true propositions raise challenges for truthmaker theory:

**Negative existentials** ("there are no unicorns"): No positive state of affairs can serve as the truthmaker, since any positive state is compatible with the existence of unicorns. Armstrong's totality state of affairs — the state that the world contains exactly the facts it does — grounds negative existentials. The totality state combined with the absence of unicorn-states entails that there are no unicorns.

**Universal generalizations** ("all ravens are black"): Made true by the conjunction of individual raven-facts plus the totality fact that those are all the ravens. Without the totality fact, the individual facts are compatible with additional non-black ravens.

**Modal truths** ("water could have been colorless"): What makes it possible that water is colorless? If the actual world contains only actual states of affairs, there seem to be no truthmakers for merely possible states. Possible-worlds theories locate the truthmakers in other possible worlds; Armstrong locates them in the combinatorial possibilities among actual universals and particulars.

**Mathematical truths** ("7 is prime"): Platonists posit mathematical states of affairs as truthmakers. Anti-Platonists must find truthmakers in the physical world (Field), in modal facts (Hellman), or must deny that mathematical truths need truthmakers (deflationism).

## Grounding and Truthmaking

Contemporary metaphysics distinguishes *truthmaking* from *grounding*, and the distinction matters:

**Truthmaking** is a relation between a thing (entity) and a proposition: x makes proposition P true.

**Grounding** is a relation between facts, propositions, or states: the fact that P is grounded in (or holds in virtue of) the fact that Q.

Truthmaking: [Socrates, Wisdom] makes "Socrates is wise" true.
Grounding: The fact that Socrates is wise holds *in virtue of* the fact that Socrates instantiates the universal Wisdom.

The grounding relation is more fine-grained: it can hold between facts at the same or different ontological levels (the biological grounded in the physical, the chemical grounded in the physical, etc.). Truthmaking is a special case of grounding: a proposition's truth is grounded in the existence and obtaining of certain states of affairs.

**Fine's analysis**: Kit Fine's account of essence and grounding separates the *real definition* of things from merely necessary connections. "Socrates is wise" is made true by the state of affairs [Socrates, Wisdom] because Socrates's wisdom is a real constituent of the world. This is stronger than mere necessary connection: it is a constitutive dependence relation.

The integration of truthmaker theory with grounding theory represents one of the most active areas of contemporary metaphysics, connecting the theory of truth to the theory of ontological dependence and to the project of giving a complete account of which facts are fundamental and which are derivative.
