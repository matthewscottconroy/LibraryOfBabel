# Exercises: Propositions and Properties

1. What is a proposition? How does Frege's account differ from Russell's? What are the main advantages and disadvantages of each?

2. Possible-worlds semantics identifies propositions with sets of possible worlds. What are the consequences of this identification for the individuation of propositions?

3. Frege's context principle says that words have meaning only in the context of a sentence. How does this connect to his account of abstract objects?

4. Are properties abstract objects? What is the difference between a universal and a proposition, and could one be reduced to the other?

5. States of affairs are sometimes called "objective facts." How do they differ from propositions? Can there be false states of affairs?

6. Armstrong argues that states of affairs are the fundamental ontological category. What problems does this view face, and how might they be answered?
