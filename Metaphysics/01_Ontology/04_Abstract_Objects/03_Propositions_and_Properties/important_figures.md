# Important Figures: Propositions and Properties

**Gottlob Frege** distinguished the sense (Sinn) of an expression from its reference, with senses being abstract objects — the thoughts expressed by sentences. Frege's thoughts are the paradigm of abstract propositions.

**Bertrand Russell** developed a competing account in which propositions are composed of actual objects and properties, not abstract senses. The difference between Frege's and Russell's accounts has shaped all subsequent work on propositions.

**George Bealer** (b. 1943) developed a systematic theory of propositions and properties as primitive intensional entities, arguing that they are required by natural language semantics and by the logic of attitude reports.

**Robert Stalnaker** (b. 1940) identified propositions with sets of possible worlds, providing a technically tractable account with a clear connection to probability and decision theory.

**David Armstrong** developed the theory of states of affairs (facts) as the fundamental truthmakers, arguing that the world's structure is given by states of affairs rather than by objects, properties, or propositions alone.
