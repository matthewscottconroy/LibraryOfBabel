# Important Terms and Ideas: Propositions and Properties

**Proposition**: The content expressed by a sentence — what is believed, asserted, doubted, or denied. Propositions are the bearers of truth and falsity and the objects of propositional attitudes.

**Structured proposition**: A proposition with internal structure that mirrors the subject-predicate (or more complex) structure of the sentence expressing it. Russellian structured propositions contain the objects and properties the sentence is directly about.

**Possible-worlds proposition**: Stalnaker's account on which propositions are sets of possible worlds — the set of worlds at which the sentence is true. This is technically tractable but too coarse-grained to distinguish necessarily co-true propositions.

**Frege's Thought (Gedanke)**: Frege's term for propositions — abstract, objective, non-mental, non-physical entities that are expressed by sentences and grasped by thinkers. Thoughts are neither in the physical world nor in any mind.

**Propositional attitude**: A mental state that involves a proposition as its content: belief that P, desire that P, hope that P, fear that P. The same proposition can be the content of different types of attitude.

**State of affairs**: An entity composed of a particular and a property (or relation and its relata) — the metaphysical correlate of an atomic sentence. A state of affairs obtains iff the corresponding proposition is true.

**Properties as abstract objects**: The view (Platonic realism, Bealer's intensionalism) that properties are abstract entities existing independently of their instances. Properties on this view are the constituents of propositions and the objects of conceptual thought.

**Hyperintensional context**: A context in which necessarily equivalent propositions can be distinguished — for example, belief contexts. John can believe that Hesperus is a planet without believing that Phosphorus is a planet, even though they are the same body.
