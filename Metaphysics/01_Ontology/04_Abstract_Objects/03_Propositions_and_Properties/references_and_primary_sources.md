# References and Primary Sources: Propositions and Properties

## Foundational Texts

Frege, Gottlob. "On Sense and Reference." In *Translations from the Philosophical Writings of Gottlob Frege*, ed. Geach and Black. Blackwell, 1952. Introduces the sense/reference distinction; senses (thoughts/propositions) are abstract objects.

Russell, Bertrand. *The Principles of Mathematics*. Norton, 1903. Part I develops Russell's account of propositions as complexes of objects and properties, anticipating structured proposition theories.

## Contemporary Works

Stalnaker, Robert. "Propositions." In MacKay and Merrill (eds.), *Issues in the Philosophy of Language*. Yale University Press, 1976. The possible-worlds account of propositions as sets of possible worlds.

King, Jeffrey. *The Nature and Structure of Content*. Oxford University Press, 2007. A detailed defense of structured propositions as facts about expression-use.

Soames, Scott. *What Is Meaning?* Princeton University Press, 2010. Argues against possible-worlds propositions and for structured Russellian propositions.

Armstrong, D.M. *A World of States of Affairs*. Cambridge University Press, 1997. The canonical treatment of states of affairs as the fundamental ontological category, closely related to propositions.
