# Exercises and Thought Experiments — Historical Background

*Problems for engaging with the history of the problem of universals.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Formulating the Problem**
State the one-over-many problem as a formal argument: premises leading to the conclusion that there must be universals. Then identify which premise a nominalist would reject. Is there a third option: accept the conclusion but interpret "universals" in a deflationary way?

**Exercise 2 — The Third Man**
Explain the Third Man regress in your own words. Suppose a Platonist responds: Forms do not instantiate themselves — LARGENESS is not large; it is what makes other things large. Does this response fully block the regress? Can you reformulate a version of the regress that survives this move?

**Exercise 3 — Aristotle vs. Plato on Forms**
Aristotle says universals exist only in particulars. Plato says they exist independently. Identify three specific philosophical issues where this difference in view makes a difference: (a) to the ontology of uninstantiated properties; (b) to mathematical objects; (c) to the epistemology of abstract knowledge. In each case, does Aristotle or Plato have the better position?

**Exercise 4 — Ockham's Razor**
Ockham claims we should not multiply entities beyond necessity. Evaluate this methodological principle: is it a methodological preference or a metaphysical claim about reality? Does it favor nominalism over realism, or could a realist invoke it against the nominalist (e.g., by arguing that tropes multiply entities where universals would do)?

**Thought Experiment — The Fire**
Suppose every red thing in the universe is destroyed. On Platonic realism, the universal REDNESS still exists; on Aristotelian immanent realism, it ceases to exist; on nominalism, there is nothing to cease. Is this a genuine disagreement? What evidence could settle it? Does the disagreement depend on further, independent commitments about what existence means?
