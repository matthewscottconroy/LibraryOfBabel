# Important Figures — Historical Background

*Key thinkers in the history of the problem of universals from antiquity through the twentieth century.*

---

**Plato (c. 428–348 BCE)** — Developed the theory of Forms as eternal, non-spatial universals that are the objects of genuine knowledge and the standards for sensory things. The *Republic*, *Phaedo*, and *Parmenides* are the key texts.

**Aristotle (384–322 BCE)** — Developed immanent realism in response to Plato: universals exist only in particulars, not in a separate realm. The *Metaphysics*, especially Books VII and XIII, and the *Categories* are essential.

**Porphyry (c. 234–305 CE)** — His *Isagoge* (Introduction to Aristotle's *Categories*) posed the three questions (real or conceptual? corporeal or incorporeal? in things or separate?) that defined medieval debate.

**Boethius (c. 480–524)** — Latin commentary on Porphyry transmitted the problem of universals to the medieval West.

**Peter Abelard (1079–1142)** — Developed the most sophisticated pre-thirteenth-century account of universals as sermones with objective grounds (status), resisting both extreme realism and crude nominalism.

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — Integrated Aristotelian moderate realism with Christian theology, articulating the threefold existence of universals (ante res, in rebus, post res).

**William of Ockham (c. 1287–1347)** — The supreme nominalist of the medieval period, developing the critique of universals via the Razor and replacing them with natural mental signs.

**Gottlob Frege (1848–1925)** — Transformed the problem by introducing the concept/object distinction and treating concepts as logical functions.

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — Revived realism about universals in analytic philosophy, especially via the argument from relations.

**David Armstrong (1926–2014)** — Revived the traditional debate in contemporary analytic metaphysics with a comprehensive defense of immanent universals.
