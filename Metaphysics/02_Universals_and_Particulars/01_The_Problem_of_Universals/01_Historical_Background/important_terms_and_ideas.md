# Important Terms and Ideas: Historical Background (Problem of Universals)

**The problem of universals**: The question of whether there are genuinely shared features (universals) in addition to particular individual things, and if so, what their nature is. The problem arises because many things appear to share properties (redness, humanity), yet particulars are numerically distinct.

**Platonic realism (extreme realism)**: The view that universals exist independently of and prior to particular things — in a separate, non-physical realm (the "third realm" or "Platonic heaven"). The Forms are the paradigm case.

**Aristotelian realism (moderate realism)**: The view that universals are real but immanent — they exist only in (and through) their particular instances. Universals are the forms of things, not separate entities.

**Medieval nominalism**: The view (associated with Ockham, Roscelin) that only particular individuals exist. Universals are merely words (voces) or mental concepts — they have no independent reality.

**Conceptualism**: The intermediate view that universals exist as mental concepts — they are real, but their reality is in the mind rather than in the external world. Abelard's account of universals is often interpreted as a conceptualism.

**Porphyry's three questions**: (1) Do universals exist or are they merely mental? (2) If they exist, are they corporeal or incorporeal? (3) Are they in sensible things or separate from them? These questions structured medieval debate about universals.

**Realism vs. nominalism**: The fundamental divide in the problem of universals. Realists hold that there are mind-independent, extra-linguistic entities corresponding to general terms; nominalists deny this, holding that generality is a feature of language or thought, not of reality.

**Haecceity (thisness)**: A term from Duns Scotus — the individual "thisness" that makes a thing the very individual it is, as opposed to its "whatness" (quiddity) or qualitative nature. Haecceities individuate things beyond their qualitative properties.
