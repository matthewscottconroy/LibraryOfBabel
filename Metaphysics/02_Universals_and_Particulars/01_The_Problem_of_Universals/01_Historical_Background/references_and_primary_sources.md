# References and Primary Sources: Historical Background (Problem of Universals)

## Foundational Texts

Plato. *Phaedo* and *Republic*, Book VI. Trans. Grube. Hackett, 1977. The original argument for the Forms as universals: abstract, eternal entities that particular things imperfectly participate in.

Aristotle. *Categories* and *Metaphysics* Book Z. Trans. Ackrill and Ross. Oxford: Clarendon Press. Aristotle's immanent realism: universals are real but exist only in their instances; they are the forms of things.

Porphyry. *Isagoge*. Trans. Barnes. Oxford University Press, 2003. The introduction to Aristotle's *Categories* that posed the three questions — whether universals exist, whether they are corporeal or incorporeal, whether they are in sensible things or separate — that dominated medieval discussion.

## Contemporary Works

Spade, Paul Vincent (ed.). *Five Texts on the Mediaeval Problem of Universals*. Hackett, 1994. Key texts from Porphyry, Abelard, Duns Scotus, William of Ockham, and others.

Penner, Terry. *The Ascent from Nominalism*. Reidel, 1987. A study of the problem of universals in the context of Plato's dialogues.

Tweedale, Martin. *Abailard on Universals*. North Holland, 1976. A scholarly study of Abelard's contribution to the medieval debate.
