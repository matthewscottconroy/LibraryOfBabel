# Exercises — Realism About Universals

*Problems for engaging with realist positions on universals.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Transcendent vs. Immanent**
Both Platonic and Aristotelian realists posit universals, but they differ on whether universals depend on instances. Identify at least three philosophical questions where this difference matters (e.g., the status of mathematical objects, the epistemology of properties, the nature of possibility). For each, say which view you find more defensible.

**Exercise 2 — Armstrong's Argument from Failure**
Armstrong argues that nominalism fails because no nominalist account can simultaneously handle objective similarity, natural laws, and the non-arbitrariness of natural kinds. Choose the nominalist account you find strongest (predicate nominalism, class nominalism, or resemblance nominalism) and try to defend it against Armstrong's critique. Where does the defense break down?

**Exercise 3 — Naturalness as Primitive**
Lewis treats naturalness as a primitive, brute feature of certain classes (or properties). An opponent says: if naturalness is just a primitive, then Lewis's account offers no more insight into why some properties are natural than the realist's claim that some universals exist. Evaluate this objection. Is accepting a primitive really equivalent to saying nothing?

**Exercise 4 — Structural Universals**
Armstrong accepts structural universals: the property of being a methane molecule is partly constituted by simpler universals (CARBON, HYDROGEN, BONDED-TO). How does a structural universal differ from a complex state of affairs? Is there a problem with the "wholly present" claim for structural universals? Can Armstrong's account handle the fact that the same simple universal (HYDROGEN) must be instantiated multiple times in the structural universal METHANE?

**Thought Experiment — Alien Universals**
Lewis posits that some possible worlds contain *alien* properties — perfectly natural properties not instantiated in the actual world. Can we think about alien universals? If we cannot conceive of them, does that show they are impossible? If we can conceive of them, what does that show about the relationship between what we can think about and what is possible?
