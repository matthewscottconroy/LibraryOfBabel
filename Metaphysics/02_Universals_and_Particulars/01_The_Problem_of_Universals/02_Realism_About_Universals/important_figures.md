# Important Figures — Realism About Universals

*Key philosophers who developed and defended various forms of realism about universals.*

---

**Plato (c. 428–348 BCE)** — Transcendent realism about Forms: universals as eternal, mind-independent paradigm entities. The *Republic* and *Phaedo* develop the positive theory; the *Parmenides* raises the self-criticism.

**Aristotle (384–322 BCE)** — Immanent realism: universals exist in their instances, not apart from them. The foundational Aristotelian framework for all subsequent moderate realism.

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — Synthesized Aristotelian moderate realism with Augustinian divine ideas, arguing universals have a threefold status: in God's mind, in things, and in human cognition.

**Gottlob Frege (1848–1925)** — Defended objectivity of concepts (logical functions) against psychologism, establishing the lineage of abundant realism. *Foundations of Arithmetic* and the Grundgesetze series.

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — Defended universals via the argument from relations and the epistemology of acquaintance. *Problems of Philosophy* (1912), *The Philosophy of Logical Atomism* (1918).

**Gustav Bergmann (1906–1987)** — Systematic analytic defense of universals within a facts-based ontology.

**David Armstrong (1926–2014)** — The preeminent contemporary defender of immanent universals. *Universals and Scientific Realism* (1978) and *A World of States of Affairs* (1997).

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Developed naturalness as a primitive to do the work of universals without full commitment to them. "New Work for a Theory of Universals" (1983).
