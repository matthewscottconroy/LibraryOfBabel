# Important Terms and Ideas: Realism About Universals

**Immanent realism**: The view that universals exist within (and only within) their particular instances — they are not abstract entities in a separate Platonic realm. Armstrong is the contemporary paradigm of an immanent realist.

**Transcendent realism**: The view that universals exist independently of whether they are instantiated — they are abstract entities in a Platonic realm, not dependent on particular physical things for their existence.

**Principle of Instantiation**: Armstrong's requirement that every genuine universal must be instantiated by at least one particular in the actual world. He rejects uninstantiated universals as ontologically profligate.

**States of affairs**: Armstrong's fundamental ontological category: a particular's instantiating a universal constitutes a state of affairs (fact). States of affairs are the truth-makers for atomic propositions and the basic constituents of the world.

**Multiple location**: The feature of universals on immanent realism — the same universal (redness) is wholly present at each location where a red thing is. This distinguishes universals from particulars, which are in at most one location.

**Thick vs. thin particulars**: Armstrong's distinction: a thick particular is an object together with all its intrinsic properties (the full concrete individual); a thin particular is the bare subject of predication, stripped of all its properties.

**Sparse universals**: Armstrong's view that not every predicate corresponds to a genuine universal. Only those properties that figure in the fundamental laws of nature are real universals; gerrymandered predicates do not pick out universals.

**Truthmaking**: The relation in which a real-world entity (a particular, a universal, or a state of affairs) makes a proposition true. Armstrong's realism is motivated partly by the need to identify truthmakers for true predications.
