# Exercises — Nominalism

*Problems for engaging with nominalist positions on universals.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Evaluating Predicate Nominalism**
State the two most serious objections to predicate nominalism. For each, say whether the resemblance nominalist or the class nominalist does better at handling it. Is there any advantage predicate nominalism has over its rivals?

**Exercise 2 — The Resemblance Regress**
The objection to resemblance nominalism says: resemblance is itself a universal; if two things resemble, they share the universal RESEMBLANCE. Formulate this as a precise argument. Then formulate the resemblance nominalist's best response. Does the response succeed?

**Exercise 3 — Class Nominalism and Coextension**
"Has a heart" and "has a kidney" are coextensive in the actual world. Explain why this creates a problem for actual-world class nominalism. How does Lewis's modal class nominalism resolve the problem? What ontological commitment does the resolution require?

**Exercise 4 — Naturalness as Primitive**
Lewis takes naturalness as a primitive feature of some classes. An alternative is to say that natural classes are those that correspond to Armstrong's immanent universals. Has Lewis gained any theoretical economy by replacing universals with natural classes? Or has he just shifted the mystery from "why are some universals fundamental?" to "why are some classes natural?"

**Thought Experiment — Grue**
Goodman's predicate "grue" applies to things examined before time T and found to be green, or not so examined and blue. "Grue" is perfectly well-defined. Does anything in nature correspond to grue-ness? If not, why not? What would it mean for something to really be grue? Use this case to test each of the nominalist accounts: predicate nominalism, resemblance nominalism, class nominalism.
