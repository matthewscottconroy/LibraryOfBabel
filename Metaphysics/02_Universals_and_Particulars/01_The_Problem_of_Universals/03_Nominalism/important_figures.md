# Important Figures — Nominalism

*Key defenders of nominalist positions from Ockham to the present.*

---

**William of Ockham (c. 1287–1347)** — The foundational medieval nominalist. Replaced universals with natural mental signs; introduced Ockham's Razor as the methodological principle of parsimony.

**Thomas Hobbes (1588–1679)** — Early modern nominalist who held that universals are merely names — spoken sounds that we apply to many things — with no corresponding reality in the world.

**John Stuart Mill (1806–1873)** — Empiricist approach to general terms: general names apply to things by resemblance and shared attributes, but the attributes are themselves particulars. A transitional figure between classical nominalism and more modern resemblance-based views.

**Nelson Goodman (1906–1998)** — Systematic nominalism applied to logic, science, and the philosophy of art. Co-authored (with Quine) the first serious modern attempt to reconstruct mathematics nominalistically.

**W. V. O. Quine (1908–2000)** — Argued that predicates do not carry ontological commitment; only quantification does. Moved toward reluctant acceptance of abstract objects on indispensability grounds.

**H. H. Price (1899–1984)** — Developed an early analytic version of resemblance nominalism in *Thinking and Experience* (1953).

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Developed naturalness as a primitive to replace universals in a class-nominalist framework. *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986).

**Gonzalo Rodriguez-Pereyra (1966–)** — The most systematic contemporary resemblance nominalist. *Resemblance Nominalism* (2002).
