# Important Terms and Ideas: Nominalism

**Nominalism**: In the metaphysics of universals, the view that only particular individuals exist — there are no universals, no shared abstract entities corresponding to general terms. "Nominalism" derives from nomen (name).

**Ostrich nominalism**: The view that the problem of universals is a pseudo-problem to be dismissed rather than solved. Quine is the paradigm: "a is F" is primitively true of particular a, and no analysis in terms of universals or resemblance is needed.

**Predicate nominalism**: The view that all that is required for "a is F" to be true is for the predicate "F" to apply to a. Properties are identified with predicates (or with their extensions); there are no mind-independent property-entities.

**Class nominalism**: The view that properties are identified with sets of their actual and possible instances. To be red is to be a member of the set of red things. Faces the problem that co-extensive properties collapse into one.

**Resemblance nominalism**: The view that property-talk is grounded in primitive, objective resemblance relations among particular objects. No universal redness is needed: red things are all those that sufficiently resemble paradigm cases of red.

**Ockham's Razor**: The methodological principle attributed to William of Ockham: do not multiply entities beyond necessity. Applied to universals: if we can account for predication without positing universals, we should not posit them.

**The companionship problem**: A difficulty for resemblance nominalism: if two properties always accompany each other (every cordate is a renate and vice versa), the resemblance class of one will be identical to that of the other, collapsing them into one "property."

**The coextension problem**: A difficulty for class nominalism: properties with the same extension (e.g., being a creature with a heart and being a creature with kidneys, if they always co-occur) would be identical, which seems wrong.
