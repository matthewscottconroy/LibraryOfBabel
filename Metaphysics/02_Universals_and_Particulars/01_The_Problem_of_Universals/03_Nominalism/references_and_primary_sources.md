# References and Primary Sources: Nominalism

## Foundational Texts

Ockham, William of. *Philosophical Writings*. Trans. Boehner. Hackett, 1990. The classic source for medieval nominalism: only individuals exist; universals are merely signs or mental concepts.

Goodman, Nelson. *The Structure of Appearance*. Harvard University Press, 1951. A systematic program of nominalistic construction using mereology rather than set theory to avoid positing abstract entities.

## Contemporary Works

Rodriguez-Pereyra, Gonzalo. *Resemblance Nominalism*. Oxford University Press, 2002. The most sophisticated contemporary nominalist program, grounding property-talk in primitive resemblance among particulars.

Price, H.H. *Thinking and Experience*. Hutchinson, 1953. An influential early discussion of resemblance nominalism.

Lewis, David. "New Work for a Theory of Universals." *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 61 (1983): 343-377. Though not itself nominalist, this paper clarifies what nominalists must say about natural properties.

Armstrong, D.M. *Nominalism and Realism*, vol. 1 of *Universals and Scientific Realism*. Cambridge University Press, 1978. The most comprehensive critical survey of all forms of nominalism; essential for understanding the debate.
