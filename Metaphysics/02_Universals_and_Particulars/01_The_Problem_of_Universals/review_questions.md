# Review Questions — The Problem of Universals

1. The chapter presents the one-over-many problem as arising from a simple observation about shared redness. Reconstruct the core argument for universals from that observation. Which premise do nominalists reject, which do trope theorists reject, and why is the realist entitled to think the argument is sound?

2. Plato's Third Man regress is generated by three assumptions: One Over Many, Self-Predication, and Non-Identity. Explain how all three are needed to produce the regress. Which assumption does Aristotle's immanent realism deny, and does that denial avoid the regress without undermining what Forms were supposed to accomplish?

3. Ockham argues that positing universals is unnecessary because mental concepts can do all the explanatory work as natural signs. Evaluate how well the mental-language doctrine handles the objectivity objection against predicate nominalism — the worry that properties existed before any language users did.

4. Armstrong's sparse realism and the abundant realism of the Fregean tradition give sharply different answers to the question of which predicates correspond to real properties. What does each view say about a disjunctive predicate like "is either an electron or the Sydney Opera House"? What are the theoretical costs each view pays for its answer?

5. Russell argues that relations are the hardest case for nominalism. Explain his argument from relations in your own words, and assess Quine's response — that predicates do not carry ontological commitment — as a possible reply. Is that response adequate for the relational case?

6. Resemblance nominalism and class nominalism are both nominalist alternatives to universals, but they handle the coextension problem differently. Explain how Rodriguez-Pereyra appeals to possible objects to distinguish necessarily coextensive properties, and identify what ontological commitment that move requires. Does it seem a genuine economy over universals realism?

7. Armstrong's account of natural laws invokes higher-order universals and the necessitation relation N(F, G), while Lewis offers the Best Systems Account. What is van Fraassen's inference problem for Armstrong's view, and how does the dispute between these accounts bear on whether laws have a modal force of their own or merely describe patterns?

8. Both Benacerraf's epistemological challenge and Aristotle's separation argument press Platonic realism from epistemological and causal directions. Explain each objection, then consider: does moving to immanent realism (Armstrong's position) fully answer both, or does it simply shift the location of the problem?
