# Important Terms and Ideas: Tropes and Universals

**Trope vs. universal**: A universal is a repeatable entity wholly present in each of its instances; a trope is a non-repeatable particular instance of a property. Two roses share the universal redness; each rose has its own redness-trope that merely resembles the other's.

**Abstract particular**: Williams's term for tropes — they are "abstract" in being aspects or parts of their bearers (not free-standing substances), and "particular" in being numerically unrepeatable.

**Middle position**: Trope theory occupies a middle ground between universals realism (which posits repeatable abstract or immanent universals) and simple nominalism (which denies any property-like entities). Tropes are property-like but particular.

**Compresence and bundling**: The problem of explaining why multiple tropes constitute a single object. Various bundling relations have been proposed: spatiotemporal coincidence, mutual ontological dependence, or a primitive compresence relation.

**Dependence of tropes on substances**: The question of whether tropes can exist without a substance to bear them, or whether each trope necessarily belongs to a particular individual. The view that tropes depend on substances (not vice versa) is sometimes called "mode realism."

**Resemblance among tropes**: The relation that grounds property-predication on trope theories. Two objects are both F because their F-tropes resemble each other. The challenge is explaining this without invoking a universal resemblance relation.

**Trope nominalism**: The view that tropes (particular property-instances) are the fundamental entities, replacing both universals and bare particulars. Objects are bundles of tropes; universals are resemblance classes of tropes.
