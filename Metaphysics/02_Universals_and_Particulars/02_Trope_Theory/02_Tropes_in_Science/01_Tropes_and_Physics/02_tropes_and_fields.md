# Tropes and Fields

---

Trope theory was initially developed with discrete particulars in mind — individual objects with individual property-instances, one trope per property per object. But contemporary physics is at least as much a physics of fields as of particles. The electromagnetic field, the gravitational field, the Higgs field — these are continuous physical entities spread over regions of spacetime, not bundles of tropes belonging to discrete objects. If the trope framework is to serve as a general ontology for physics, it must accommodate this continuity.

The natural extension treats each spacetime point (or small region) as a bearer of field-tropes. The electromagnetic field at point x has its own field-strength-trope at x, distinct from the field-strength-trope at adjacent point y. The field as a whole is the continuous distribution of such tropes across spacetime — a "trope field" rather than a collection of tropes belonging to discrete particles. When the field value varies smoothly — the electric field strength changes gradually from one region to another — the tropes at adjacent points are not exactly resembling but inexactly resembling, with the degree of resemblance inversely proportional to the gradient of the field. The resemblance relations among field-tropes constitute a continuous spectrum rather than the simple two-value structure of exactly-resembling/not-resembling.

In classical electromagnetism, the electromagnetic tensor F_μν(x) characterizes the field state at each spacetime point. On the trope interpretation, at each point x there is a trope T(x) with qualitative character F_μν(x); the field is the collection {T(x) : x ∈ spacetime}; and the field's dynamical evolution is expressed as causal relations among tropes across time: T(x) at time t causes T(x) at time t + dt through Maxwell's equations. Charged particles interact with the field through their charge-trope interacting with the local field-trope at their location. This preserves the trope framework's commitment to singular causation — it is this field-trope at this point that acts on this charge-trope — while accommodating the continuous structure of field theory.

## Quantum Fields

Quantum field theory (QFT) is the most fundamental physical theory currently available, and its ontology is deeply contested. In QFT, what naive physics calls "particles" are excitations of underlying quantum fields. The electron field permeates all of spacetime; an electron is a localized excitation of that field, a ripple propagating through it. Prior to excitation, the field is in its vacuum state — no particles, but not nothing either, since the vacuum has determinate properties including energy.

The trope interpretation of QFT runs as follows: the electron field is a collection of electron-field-tropes at each spacetime region; an electron is a bundle of tropes corresponding to a localized excitation — a region where the electron-field has a specific non-vacuum value; the creation and annihilation of electrons are trope-creation and trope-annihilation events. This is coherent in outline. But it faces deep difficulties. In QFT, particles can be created from the vacuum through vacuum fluctuations, the Unruh effect, and Hawking radiation. Tropes are thereby coming into existence without a prior discrete object to bear them — the field itself generates them. This challenges the intuitive picture of tropes as properties of independently identifiable objects; the object is constituted by the trope-cluster rather than existing prior to it.

More troublingly, quantum field states are represented as superpositions, and particle number is not always determinate. A state can be a superposition of different numbers of particles, which means it is a superposition of different numbers of trope-clusters. Whether this indeterminacy reflects indeterminacy in which tropes exist, or whether the field-tropes themselves are determinate while particle-number is a derived and sometimes indeterminate quantity, is genuinely unclear. Both the trope theorist and the universals realist face this puzzle, and neither has a fully worked-out answer.

## Holism and Composite-System Tropes

The deepest challenge for trope theory from field physics is holism. In entangled quantum systems, global properties of the composite system are not determined by the properties of its parts. Two entangled electrons may have a determinate total spin of zero while neither electron has any determinate spin individually. If all properties are tropes of individual objects, holistic properties seem to have no place. The trope theorist must posit composite-system tropes — tropes belonging to the two-electron system as a whole, not to either electron individually. The total-spin-trope is a trope of the pair.

This extension is coherent but complicates the ontology significantly. If composite systems can have their own tropes, and if the composites are arbitrarily large (the entire universe has holistic properties in some interpretations of quantum mechanics), then tropes of composite systems at every scale must be admitted alongside individual-particle tropes. The parsimony that made trope theory attractive begins to dissolve as the inventory of tropes expands to cover every level of composition. The universals theorist faces an analogous expansion — SPIN-0 is a universal instantiated by composite systems — but neither theory offers a clearly simpler account of quantum holism.
