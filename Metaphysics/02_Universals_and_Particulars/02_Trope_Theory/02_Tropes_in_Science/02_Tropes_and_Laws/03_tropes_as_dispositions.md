# Tropes as Dispositions

---

The Humean trope theorist treats individual property-instances as "bare": they have their qualitative characters without any intrinsic directedness toward specific effects. A charge-trope just is the specific quality of being negatively charged; its electromagnetic effects are governed by external laws, contingent regularities that could in principle have been different. This picture leaves causation unexplained at the level of individual tropes, grounding causal connections in the Humean mosaic rather than in the intrinsic natures of the things that cause. C. B. Martin's challenge to this picture is sharp: if properties have no intrinsic powers, what exactly does causal work? Surely it is the specific charge of this electron — this trope — that pushes that proton. If the trope has no intrinsic push, causation is either nowhere or everywhere, but never here in the property itself.

The alternative is to treat tropes as dispositions — or better, as *powerful qualities* in Martin and Heil's sense. A powerful quality is a single ontological entity with two inseparable aspects: a specific qualitative character (what it is to be this charge-trope) and an intrinsic directedness toward specific manifestations (the disposition to attract protons, repel electrons, deflect in electromagnetic fields). These are not two separate tropes bundled together, nor two aspects of a complex entity; they are one trope that is constitutively both. The quality just is the power; the power just is the quality. Just as a knife's blade and its sharpness are not two things but one thing described from two perspectives, the qualitative character of the charge-trope and its directedness are one entity.

When tropes are understood as powerful qualities, laws of nature are not external constraints imposed on the world from outside but necessary consequences of the intrinsic natures of the relevant tropes. If every charge-trope is essentially disposed to attract opposite charges, then the law of electromagnetic interaction follows from what charge-tropes are, with the same necessity as "triangles have three sides" follows from what triangles are. This is a form of *scientific essentialism* — the view that the fundamental properties of physics have essential natures that necessitate their causal roles — applied at the level of individual trope-instances rather than universals. Laws become metaphysically necessary, counterfactuals are grounded in natures rather than patterns, and inductive inference is secured by the fact that tropes of the same kind share the same essence.

## Pure Categoricalism vs. Pure Dispositionalism

The powerful-qualities view navigates between two extremes. Pure categoricalism treats properties as "quiddities" — they have specific qualitative characters but are not intrinsically directed toward any manifestation. The same categorical quality could, on this view, have been associated with different causal roles if the laws had been different. Mass could in principle have caused repulsion rather than attraction; charge could have had different electromagnetic behavior. The identity of a property is fixed by its qualitative character alone, independently of its causal role. This view faces the challenge that it makes the connection between properties and their causal roles utterly inexplicable — why does this quality happen to produce these effects?

Pure dispositionalism treats properties as pure powers: they are exhausted by their directedness toward manifestations, with no qualitative character beyond that directedness. Mass just is the disposition to resist acceleration and attract other masses; there is nothing more to it. This view faces Alexander Bird's *manifestation regress*: if every property is a power directed toward a manifestation, and the manifestation is itself a property, then we get an infinite chain of powers pointing toward further powers, never reaching a terminus in an actual categorical quality. Bird's response is that the regress is benign (possibly circular or infinite without being vicious), but many find this unsatisfying.

The powerful-qualities view avoids both problems. The ground floor consists of tropes that are genuinely qualitative — they are something, here and now, not merely a pointing toward future manifestations — and genuinely dispositional — this qualitative character just is a disposition toward specific effects, not a bare quality with external causal role attached. The manifestation regress does not arise because each trope is already a terminus in its qualitative character; the contingency of causal roles does not arise because each trope's causal role is essential to what it is.

## Necessity, Essentialism, and the Law-Trope Connection

Adopting powerful tropes moves the entire account of laws in a strongly anti-Humean direction. Laws are not patterns discovered in the mosaic; they are necessary consequences of what tropes intrinsically are. This connects trope theory to Brian Ellis's scientific essentialism (*Scientific Essentialism*, 2001), which argues that the fundamental properties of physics have real essences discovered by science. Ellis works within a universals framework, but the trope version is structurally parallel: each fundamental trope-kind has an essence constituted by its dispositional nature, and science progressively discovers these essences.

The practical consequence is that induction is epistemically secured in a way that Humeanism cannot match. We can inductively project from observed charge-tropes to unobserved ones precisely because they share the same essential nature — they are tropes of the same kind, with the same intrinsic powers. The uniformity of nature that induction presupposes is grounded in the fact that tropes of the same kind are essentially alike, not in the brute fact that the mosaic happens to be regular. Whether this neo-Aristotelian picture is ultimately more satisfying than the Humean alternative depends on whether one finds primitive essences or primitive regularities more metaphysically tolerable — a question that goes to the heart of the dispute between Humean and anti-Humean metaphysics.
