# Tropes and Grounding

---

Grounding has become one of the central concepts of contemporary analytic metaphysics. The idea is that some facts hold *in virtue of* other facts, in a metaphysically explanatory sense that is distinct from causation, logical entailment, and mere supervenience. This table is brown in virtue of its surface having the molecular composition it has; this molecular composition obtains in virtue of the arrangement of its constituent atoms; those atoms have their properties in virtue of their electron and nuclear configurations; and so on down. Grounding captures the order of ontological priority — the way the world is structured into more and less fundamental layers — without identifying higher-level facts with lower-level ones or requiring causal explanations at every level.

For trope theory, grounding raises both an opportunity and a challenge. The opportunity is that it provides a principled vocabulary for articulating how macro-level tropes depend on micro-level tropes without being identical to them. The wetness-trope of a body of water is a genuine trope — not a fiction, not merely a manner of speaking — but it obtains in virtue of the molecular tropes of its constituent H₂O molecules and their arrangement. The solidity-trope of the table obtains in virtue of the atomic tropes and their bonding relations. Grounding lets us affirm the reality of macro-level tropes while insisting on their metaphysical dependence on the physical level. This is a form of non-reductive physicalism expressed in trope-theoretic terms: all tropes are either physical or grounded in physical tropes, but higher-level tropes are not identical to any physical tropes.

The challenge is that grounding is itself a relation — a specific relation holding between specific facts — and the trope theorist must decide what to say about its ontological status. If relations are tropes (relational tropes), then grounding is a grounding-trope. But what grounds the grounding-trope? A second-order grounding relation between the grounding-trope and the facts it connects? And what grounds that? The regress threatens to be vicious.

## The Structure of Grounding

The contemporary grounding framework, associated with Fine, Rosen, and Schaffer, gives grounding several structural features. It is irreflexive: no fact grounds itself. It is asymmetric: if [A] is grounded in [B], then [B] is not grounded in [A] — the relation runs one way. It is transitive: if [A] is grounded in [B] and [B] in [C], then [A] is grounded in [C]. Together these features ensure that grounding generates a partial order on facts, with ungrounded facts at the bottom and progressively more grounded facts above.

In trope terms, the grounding order runs from fundamental physical tropes upward: physical tropes are ungrounded (or self-grounding, if one prefers), chemical tropes are grounded in physical tropes, biological tropes in chemical tropes, psychological tropes in biological tropes. The asymmetry ensures there is no circularity; the transitivity ensures that psychological tropes are ultimately grounded in physical tropes even if only through intermediate levels.

Grounding is distinct from causation because it does not require temporal precedence and is not a contingent connection: the molecular tropes ground the wetness-trope necessarily, given that configuration. It is distinct from supervenience because supervenience is a modal claim (no A-difference without a B-difference), whereas grounding is an explanatory claim (A holds because B holds). Two theories can agree on the supervenience of mental properties on physical properties while disagreeing about whether the mental is grounded in the physical.

## Grounding as Ideology

Most trope theorists who engage seriously with grounding treat the grounding relation as part of the theoretical framework's ideology rather than its ontology — a formal or structural posit that organizes the theory without being itself an entity of any kind, neither a trope nor a universal. This parallels Armstrong's treatment of instantiation as a formal nexus rather than a genuine relation: instantiation connects particulars to universals without itself being a further entity that must be connected to both.

The alternative is to treat "A is grounded in B" as expressing a methodological judgment about ontological priority rather than describing a metaphysical relation in the world. On this view, grounding talk is a way of organizing metaphysical explanations, not a report on real worldly structure. This deflationary option avoids the ontological difficulties but may undercut the ambition of grounding theory: if grounding is not a real relation, what exactly is the content of the claim that mental facts are "grounded in" physical facts?

The interaction between trope theory and grounding remains an active frontier. Whether the two can be coherently combined — with tropes as the fundamental entities and grounding as the structural relation that organizes them into layers — or whether grounding requires theoretical resources beyond the trope framework is a question that systematic metaphysics has not yet settled. What is clear is that the grounding framework, however it ultimately fits with trope theory, has given trope theorists a much sharper vocabulary for articulating the ontological relationships that their view posits, and has connected the theory of properties to some of the most important disputes in contemporary metaphysics.
