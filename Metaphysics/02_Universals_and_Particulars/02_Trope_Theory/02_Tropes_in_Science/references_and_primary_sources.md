# References and Primary Sources: Tropes in Science

## Foundational Texts

Heil, John and Mele, Alfred (eds.). *Mental Causation*. Oxford University Press, 1993. Includes essays using trope theory to analyze mental causation and the exclusion problem.

Martin, C.B. "The Need for Ontology: Some Choices." *Philosophy* 68 (1993): 505-522. Argues for a powerful-qualities ontology using trope-like entities.

## Contemporary Works

Mumford, Stephen and Anjum, Rani Lill. *Getting Causes from Powers*. Oxford University Press, 2011. Uses a powers ontology (employing trope-like entities) to analyze causation in science.

Schrenk, Markus. "Antidotes for Dispositional Essentialism." In Bird, Ellis, and Sankey (eds.), *Properties, Powers, and Structures*. Routledge, 2012. Discusses how tropes and dispositions function in scientific laws.

Heil, John. *The Universe as We Find It*. Oxford University Press, 2012. Defends a naturalist ontology combining tropes (as "modes") with a powerful-qualities view of properties.

Morrison, Margaret. *Unifying Scientific Theories*. Cambridge University Press, 2000. Examines how structural features of scientific theories (often involving relational properties) are unified — relevant to the relational vs. intrinsic dimension of trope theory.
