# Review Questions — Trope Theory

1. Trope theory is described as a "middle ground" between universals realism and nominalism. Articulate exactly what tropes are supposed to inherit from each side, and where the genuine novelty lies. Does the position successfully avoid the characteristic problems of both rivals, or does it relocate them?

2. Williams argues that tropes can serve as the basis for a one-category ontology, deriving both concrete particulars (as bundles) and repeatable properties (as resemblance classes) from a single kind of entity. What are the compresence and resemblance relations supposed to do in this account, and why do critics argue that they quietly reintroduce the categories the theory was meant to eliminate?

3. Armstrong contends that resemblance among tropes must itself be a universal — since two pairs of red tropes involve the same kind of resemblance — and that the trope theorist therefore cannot sustain a genuinely one-category ontology. Reconstruct and assess Campbell's response that resemblance is an internal relation. Is the response fully satisfying for cases of inexact resemblance?

4. Trope theory is claimed to handle singular causation more naturally than universals-based accounts. Explain the distinction between singular and nomic causation, and evaluate whether the trope account genuinely grounds this distinction or merely describes it in a different vocabulary. How does the causal exclusion problem figure in this comparison?

5. The compresence problem shows that the central constructive move of bundle theory — assembling tropes into objects — generates a regress if compresence is itself a trope. Assess the three main responses (compresence as primitive formal relation, mutual ontological dependence, set-theoretic bundling) against one another. Which handles the circularity worry most effectively, and at what cost?

6. The view of tropes as powerful qualities (Martin and Heil) is presented as a way of navigating between pure categoricalism and pure dispositionalism. Explain Bird's manifestation regress against pure dispositionalism and the quiddity objection against pure categoricalism, then assess whether the powerful-qualities position actually avoids both rather than combining their difficulties.

7. Quantum mechanics presents test cases for trope theory — superposition, entanglement, and the statistics of identical particles — that go beyond the classical picture. Choose one of these cases and explain precisely what difficulty it poses for the trope account. Does the difficulty bear equally on universals realism, or is it distinctive to the trope framework?

8. How should the dispute between trope theory and Armstrong's immanent universals be adjudicated, given that both frameworks invoke irreducible primitives — primitive resemblance on one side, primitive instantiation on the other? What methodological standards would be needed to settle the question, and what does the debate about grounding add to it?
