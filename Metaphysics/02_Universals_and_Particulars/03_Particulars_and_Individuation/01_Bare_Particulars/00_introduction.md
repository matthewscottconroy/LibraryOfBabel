# Bare Particulars

Strip away all the properties of a particular thing — its size, color, shape, mass, charge, and every other qualitative feature — and what remains? According to the bare particular theory, something does remain: the bare particular, the ontological subject of predication, the entity that bears all the properties but is itself entirely propertyless. This is the "thin" object stripped of all qualitative clothing, the pure this-ness that makes an individual the very individual it is without being qualitatively different from any other bare particular.

The motivation for bare particulars comes from two directions. First, the bundle theory — the rival view that an individual is nothing over and above the bundle of its properties — seems to face serious difficulties with individuation: if two individuals had exactly the same properties (numerically identical in all qualitative respects), the bundle theory would have to say they are the same individual, since a bundle just is its members. But this seems wrong: two perfectly similar particles at different locations seem numerically distinct. Second, we need some account of what it is that instantiates the properties of a thing — what is the subject that is red, round, and sweet, as opposed to the properties themselves?

The bare particular answers both concerns: it is the entity that bears properties, and it is numerically distinct from any other bare particular even if both are qualitatively identical — its individuality does not consist in any qualitative difference. Bare particulars are instances of "primitive thisness" (haecceity) — they are this rather than that, and that is all there is to say.

The objections to bare particulars are formidable. Locke himself described the substratum as "something I know not what" — a purely theoretical posit with no independent content. If bare particulars have no properties themselves, how can we refer to them, know them, or even think about them? And if they are entirely featureless, what distinguishes one bare particular from another? The theory that was introduced to solve the problem of individuation seems to generate a further problem of individuation for the bare particulars themselves.

This section examines the bare particular theory in depth, assessing whether it is a coherent and necessary posit or an explanatory fiction that generates more problems than it solves.
