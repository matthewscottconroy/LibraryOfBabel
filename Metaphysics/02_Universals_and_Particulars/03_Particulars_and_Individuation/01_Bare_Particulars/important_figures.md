# Important Figures: Bare Particulars

## Aristotle (384–322 BCE)
His concept of prime matter—the ultimate substratum of all change, which has no properties in itself—anticipates bare particular theory. While Aristotle did not clearly endorse bare particulars, his hylomorphism raises the question of what underlies form and grounds individuality.

## Gustav Bergmann (1906–1987)
The most influential twentieth-century defender of bare particulars (or "bare individuals"): the substratum of a concrete particular is a featureless individual—a "bare" entity that bears properties without itself having any nature. Bergmann's ontology distinguishes bare particulars sharply from the properties they instantiate.

## David Armstrong (1926–2014)
Armstrong defended states of affairs as the fundamental ontological category but rejected bare particulars as ontologically mysterious. His critique of substratum theory and defense of the bundle theory as an alternative shaped the contemporary debate.

## Peter van Inwagen (born 1942)
Has contributed to debates about individual substances and their identity conditions, defending positions on composition and individuation that bear on the bare particular vs. bundle theory dispute.
