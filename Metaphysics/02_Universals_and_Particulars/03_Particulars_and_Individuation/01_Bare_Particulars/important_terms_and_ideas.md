# Important Terms and Ideas: Bare Particulars

**Bare particular (thin particular)**: A posited entity that is the bare subject of predication — a "this" that has properties but is not itself characterized by any of those properties essentially. It is the individuating component of an object, stripped of all its qualities.

**Substratum**: Locke's term for the "something I know not what" that underlies and supports observable qualities. The substratum is posited as the bearer of properties but is not itself known through any quality it possesses.

**The regress problem for substrata**: If a bare particular is what bears properties, then does the bare particular itself have the property of being a bare particular? And does that property require a further bearer? The regress threatens to make substratum theory incoherent.

**Featurelessness**: The allegedly paradoxical feature of bare particulars: they are the bearers of all properties but themselves have no qualitative properties. Critics argue that a featureless entity is indistinguishable from nothing at all.

**Bundle theory vs. substratum theory**: The two main options for the metaphysics of particulars. Bundle theory says objects are bundles of properties (universals or tropes) with no substratum; substratum theory says there is a thin particular over and above the properties.

**Primitive thisness (haecceity)**: A bare particular can serve as the source of numerical identity and distinctness: two qualitatively identical objects are different because they have distinct bare particulars. This is a primitive individuation, not grounded in any qualitative difference.

**Substance-attribute ontology**: The traditional two-category ontology of substances (bare particulars or thick individuals) and their attributes (properties or universals). Often contrasted with event ontologies or property ontologies.
