# References and Primary Sources: Bare Particulars

## Foundational Texts

Locke, John. *Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, Book II, ch. 23. Ed. Nidditch. Oxford University Press, 1975. The famous passage on the "substratum" — the "something I know not what" that underlies and supports observable qualities.

Russell, Bertrand. *An Inquiry into Meaning and Truth*. Allen & Unwin, 1940. Introduces "particulars" as the entities that instantiate universals and serve as the logical subjects of predication.

## Contemporary Works

Sider, Theodore. "Bare Particulars." *Philosophical Perspectives* 20 (2006): 387-397. A careful defense of bare particulars (thin particulars) against standard objections.

Van Cleve, James. "Three Versions of the Bundle Theory." *Philosophical Studies* 47 (1985): 95-107. A careful analysis of bundle theory alternatives to substratum theory.

Moreland, J.P. and Pickavance, Timothy. "Bare Particulars and Individuation: Reply to Noonan." *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 81 (2003): 1-13. A defense of bare particular theory against critics.

Armstrong, D.M. *A World of States of Affairs*. Cambridge University Press, 1997. Discusses the role of particulars in states of affairs; Armstrong accepts thin particulars within states of affairs.
