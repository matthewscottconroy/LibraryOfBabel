# Important Figures: Identity of Indiscernibles

## Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)
The originator of the principle: there cannot be two substances that are perfectly alike—if two things share all their properties, they are one. Leibniz used this principle (together with the principle of sufficient reason) to argue against Newtonian absolute space.

## Max Black (1909–1988)
Provided the canonical counterexample to the strong version of the Identity of Indiscernibles: two qualitatively identical spheres in a symmetrical universe share all their intrinsic and relational (non-haecceitist) properties yet seem to be numerically distinct. This is the "two spheres" argument.

## Robert Adams (born 1937)
Defended primitive haecceities and argued against the Identity of Indiscernibles; his work on "primitive thisness" argues that numerical identity cannot be reduced to qualitative similarity and that the principle is false for metaphysical reasons.

## Peter French, Theodore Uehling, and Howard Wettstein
Editors of influential anthologies on the metaphysics of individuation; their collections brought together the major positions on the Identity of Indiscernibles and haecceitism that define contemporary debate.
