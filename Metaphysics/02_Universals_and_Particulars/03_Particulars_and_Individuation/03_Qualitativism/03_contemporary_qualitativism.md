# Contemporary Qualitativism

---

Contemporary qualitativism holds that all metaphysical facts — including facts about individual identity and the numerical distinctness of objects — are fully determined by qualitative facts. There are no primitive haecceities, no bare particulars, no non-qualitative thisnesses floating free of qualitative character. The world is the totality of qualitative reality, and numerical distinctions either supervene on qualitative differences or do not obtain. This is both a substantive metaphysical claim and a methodological one: we should not posit individuating elements beyond qualitative properties unless forced to, and we are not forced to if qualitative individuation suffices.

The most systematic contemporary qualitativism is Lewis's Humean Supervenience:

**HS**: The world is the totality of the distribution of intrinsic perfectly natural properties across spacetime points and the spatiotemporal relations among those points. All other facts supervene on this distribution.

If two worlds agree on the distribution of natural properties across spacetime, they agree on everything — there are no further non-qualitative facts that could differ. Individual identity in the actual world is determined by an object's unique pattern of properties across its spatiotemporal career; in cases where the pattern is not unique (like the two spheres in Black's universe), qualitativists must either deny that Black's scenario is genuinely possible or accept some supplementation. Lewis's response tends toward denial: genuinely symmetric universes with perfectly indistinguishable individuals may not be genuinely possible for the qualitativist, or they may require spatiotemporal individuation via substantivalism.

HS is defended not by direct argument for qualitativism but by showing that plausible analyses of causation, mental content, meaning, modality, and personal identity can all be given within the qualitativist framework. If all the phenomena can be accommodated qualitatively, the framework earns its parsimony. The main challenges are well-known: quantum mechanics threatens the picture at the particle level, and consciousness threatens it at the mind level. If phenomenal facts are not determined by physical-qualitative facts — if there is a genuine explanatory gap between neural descriptions and phenomenal descriptions — then HS fails, at least for that domain.

## Structural Realism: A Radical Qualitativism

Structural realism offers a more radical form of qualitativism that dissolves the individuation problem rather than solving it. The basic idea, developed from different angles by Worrall, Ladyman, and Ross, is that what science tells us about the world is its relational structure, not the intrinsic natures of the individual entities in that structure. The electromagnetic field is characterized by Maxwell's equations; quantum mechanics is characterized by its symmetry groups and state-space structure. The structure is what physics discovers, and the individual "nodes" of the structure — the supposed concrete entities instantiating the structure — have no scientifically accessible intrinsic natures.

*Ontic structural realism* draws the stronger conclusion: not merely that we cannot know the intrinsic natures of individuals, but that there are no genuine individuals at the fundamental level. There are only structural relations, and what we call "particles" or "fields" are nodes in that structure, defined entirely by their relational role. The individuation problem dissolves because there are no genuine individuals to individuate: the question "what makes this electron numerically distinct from that one?" has no answer because there are no electrons as primitive individuals — only the structural web of which electrons are patterns.

The objection "no relations without relata" cuts deep: relations require things that stand in them. If there are only relations and no individuals, what are the relata? Structural realists have developed various responses — relations can be conceived as primitive, without requiring individual relata; or "individuals" are merely thin nodes in the structure, defined by the structure rather than prior to it — but none has fully silenced the objection. The gain is a dissolution of the individuation problem; the cost is a severely reconceived ontology that departs radically from the pre-theoretic picture of discrete concrete individuals.

## Qualitativism and Modal Semantics

The debate between qualitativism and haecceitism has a precise formal expression in the semantics of modal logic. Qualitativism is committed to the thesis that possible worlds are individuated by their qualitative profiles: if two worlds are qualitatively identical, they are the same world. This entails that for any qualitative scenario, there is exactly one possible world that realizes it (up to isomorphism). Haecceitism, by contrast, allows qualitatively identical worlds to be numerically distinct — differing only in which individuals play which qualitative roles. The set of possible worlds is correspondingly larger on haecceitism.

These different commitments have consequences for modal claims: "it is possible that there are two qualitatively identical iron spheres" is necessarily false on qualitativism if PII is taken as a necessary truth; it is true on haecceitism. Work by Forbes, Hazen, and others has developed the technical semantics that distinguish qualitativist and haecceitist modal logics, making the formal differences precise. The choice between them is not made by the formal tools alone but by the prior metaphysical commitments about whether individual identity is a further fact beyond qualitative distribution — which is ultimately the choice between viewing the world as qualitatively "transparent" or as containing primitive thisness as an irreducible structural feature.
