# Important Figures: Qualitativism

## Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)
Defended a bundle theory of particulars in *An Inquiry into Meaning and Truth* (1940): ordinary physical objects are bundles of universals, not substrata bearing properties. This is a form of qualitativism—individuals are constituted entirely by their qualitative characters.

## John Locke (1632–1704)
His concept of "something we know not what" (the substratum) as the bearer of qualities led bundle theorists to reject substrata altogether in favor of identifying objects with their qualities. Locke's difficulties with substratum motivated qualitativist alternatives.

## Harvey Brown (born 1950) and various philosophers of physics
Philosophers of quantum mechanics have argued that quantum particles may lack haecceities and be "weakly discerned" only by their relational properties—motivating a form of qualitativism for the fundamental entities of physics.

## David Lewis (1941–2001)
Lewis developed a sophisticated account of "natural properties" and defended Humean supervenience partly by arguing that the world is constituted by the pattern of qualitative intrinsic properties at spacetime points—a qualitativist picture at the most fundamental level.
