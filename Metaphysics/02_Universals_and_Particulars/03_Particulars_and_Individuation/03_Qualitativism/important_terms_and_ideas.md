# Important Terms and Ideas: Qualitativism

**Qualitativism**: The view that the identity and distinctness of individuals are fully grounded in their qualitative properties — their intrinsic and relational features. No primitive, non-qualitative "thisnesses" are needed to distinguish distinct individuals.

**Anti-haecceitism**: The view that there are no primitive haecceities — non-qualitative individual essences that ground numerical identity. All facts about which individual is which are qualitative facts.

**Haecceitism**: The opposing view that individuals have primitive "thisnesses" — identities not reducible to any qualitative properties. Even qualitatively indistinguishable scenarios can differ in which individual plays which role.

**Qualitative duplication**: Two objects are qualitative duplicates if they share all their intrinsic qualitative properties. Qualitativism entails that duplicates are identical; anti-qualitativism allows duplicate objects to be numerically distinct.

**Spatiotemporal location as a qualitative individuator**: One qualitativist strategy: things that are qualitatively identical in intrinsic properties can still be individuated by their different spatiotemporal locations, which are relational but qualitative properties.

**Counterpart theory and qualitativism**: Lewis's counterpart theory is anti-haecceitistic: statements about what an individual could have been are analyzed as statements about qualitatively similar individuals in other possible worlds, not about the individual itself transworld.

**Modal profile and individual essence**: An individual's modal profile is the totality of modal facts about it — what it could and could not have been. Qualitativism says modal profiles are determined by qualitative features; haecceitism says some modal facts are primitively about the individual.
