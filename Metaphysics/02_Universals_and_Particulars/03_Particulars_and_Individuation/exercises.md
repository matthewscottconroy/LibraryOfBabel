# Exercises — Particulars and Individuation

*Problems for engaging with debates about substrata and individuation.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Bare Particulars and Their Defenses**
State the three main arguments for bare particulars (individuation, subject of predication, persistence). For each argument, construct a response available to a bundle theorist. Which argument is hardest to answer from the bundle-theorist's position?

**Exercise 2 — Black's Two Spheres**
Present Black's thought experiment as precisely as you can. Then evaluate three responses: (a) the scenario is incoherent; (b) the spheres differ in relational properties (being-not-identical-to the other); (c) haecceitism is true, so their distinctness is primitive. Which response do you find most convincing, and what does it commit you to?

**Exercise 3 — Haecceitism and Counterpart Theory**
Adams holds that Humphrey has a primitive identity that persists across possible worlds. Lewis holds that cross-world identity claims are really claims about counterparts (qualitatively similar individuals in other worlds). Construct a case where the two views give different verdicts — a case where our intuitions seem to side with one against the other. Which theory captures the intuition better?

**Exercise 4 — Quantum Individuality**
Electrons of the same spin, charge, and mass are empirically indistinguishable. Some philosophers (French, Krause) argue this shows that quantum particles are not genuine individuals — they violate PII and cannot be individuated even by location. Assess this argument. Does quantum indistinguishability really show that electrons are non-individuals, or does it only show something about our epistemic access to them?

**Thought Experiment — The Identity Machine**
A machine perfectly duplicates any object, creating a second object qualitatively identical in every respect to the original. After duplication, are there two objects or one? The machine operator says "two" — he sees two distinct objects coming out of the machine. The metaphysician must explain how they are distinct. Run through bare-particular theory, haecceitism, spatiotemporal individuation, and bundle theory and assess which gives the most satisfying account.
