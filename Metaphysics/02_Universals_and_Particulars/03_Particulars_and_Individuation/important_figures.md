# Important Figures — Particulars and Individuation

*Key philosophers in debates about substrata, haecceities, and the identity of indiscernibles.*

---

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)** — Originated PII and grounded it in the principle of sufficient reason. His denial of primitive thisnesses in favor of qualitative individuation is the starting point for qualitativism.

**John Locke (1632–1704)** — Posited a "something, I know not what" — a substratum underlying and unifying a thing's properties — in *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*. Inspired both bare-particular theories and trope theories.

**Gustav Bergmann (1906–1987)** — Systematic analytic defender of bare particulars as the individuating constituent of complex particulars. His *Realism* (1967) is the key text.

**Max Black (1909–1988)** — "The Identity of Indiscernibles" (1952) is the canonical thought experiment challenging PII.

**Robert Merrihew Adams (1937–)** — "Primitive Thisness and Primitive Identity" (1979) is the canonical contemporary defense of haecceitism.

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Developed counterpart theory as an anti-haecceitist account of de re modal claims. *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986).

**Peter French, Theodore Uehling, and Howard Wettstein** — Editors of *Midwest Studies in Philosophy*, Vol. 11 (1986), which collects key papers on individuation.

**Steven French and Décio Krause** — Developed the quantum mechanical challenge to individuality in *Identity in Physics* (2006).
