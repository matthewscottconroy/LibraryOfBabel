# Exercises — Metaontological Issues

*Problems for engaging with the methodology of the universals debate.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Internal vs. External Questions**
Carnap distinguishes asking "are there properties?" within a property-theoretic framework (trivially yes) from asking it as a framework-external question (potentially meaningless). Give two arguments for Carnap's position and two for Quine's response (that there is no principled internal/external distinction). Which do you find more compelling?

**Exercise 2 — Ontological Commitment**
Quine says we are committed to entities our best theories quantify over. Suppose our best chemistry quantifies over molecules. Does this commit us to molecules as genuine entities? What about abstracta like molecular orbitals (mathematical constructs)? How does Quine's criterion handle entities that appear in theories in a functional rather than straightforwardly referential way?

**Exercise 3 — Quantifier Variance**
Hirsch argues that the same language can be interpreted under different quantifier meanings, so ontological disputes (do tables exist? do properties exist?) are verbal rather than substantive — the two sides are using "exists" with different meanings. Sider responds that some ways of using "exists" carve reality at its joints better than others, making the dispute substantive. Whose view do you find more convincing, and what would it take to settle the dispute?

**Exercise 4 — Grounding vs. Existence**
Schaffer argues that the fundamental question of metaphysics is not "what exists?" (everything does, including tables, numbers, and properties) but "what grounds what?" (what is fundamental, and what is derivative?). Compare this priority monism approach to the traditional existence-focused approach. Does the grounding approach dissolve traditional ontological disputes or transform them?
