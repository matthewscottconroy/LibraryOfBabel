# Important Terms and Ideas: Metaontological Issues

**Metaontology**: The philosophical inquiry into the nature of ontological questions — what they are asking, what methods are appropriate, and whether ontological debates are genuine or merely verbal.

**Quantifier variance**: The view (Hirsch) that the existential quantifier can be used with different meanings, and many ontological disputes simply reflect different conventions for "exists" rather than genuine disagreement about what there is.

**Ontological deflationism**: The view that ontological questions are not deep or theoretically interesting — they can be settled by relatively trivial inferences from ordinary truths. Associated with Thomasson and Easy Ontology.

**Ontological realism**: The opposing view that ontological questions are substantive and may not be settled by conceptual analysis alone — they require serious metaphysical theorizing. Associated with Sider, Fine, and others.

**Neo-Carnapianism**: The contemporary version of Carnap's view that external ontological questions (Does God exist? Are there numbers?) are not factual but pragmatic — matters of choosing a linguistic framework, not discovering truths.

**Truthmaker theory and ontological commitment**: The view that our ontological commitments are determined not just by what our theories quantify over but by what entities are required to make the true statements of our theories true.

**Ontological pluralism**: The view that "exists" can mean different things for different categories of entity — existence for concrete objects may be a different concept from existence for abstract objects or fictional characters.

**First-order vs. metaontological debates**: First-order debates ask "are there universals?"; metaontological debates ask "what kind of question is that, and what would count as answering it?" The two levels interact but can be distinguished.
