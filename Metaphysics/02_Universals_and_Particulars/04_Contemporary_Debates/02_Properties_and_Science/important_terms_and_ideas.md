# Important Terms and Ideas: Properties and Science

**Natural properties**: Properties that correspond to genuine divisions in nature — those that figure in true laws and support inductive inference. Contrasted with gerrymandered, artificial predicates that do not track real similarities.

**Dispositional essentialism**: The view that natural properties are essentially dispositional — their identities are constituted by the causal powers they confer. Laws of nature are necessary because they follow from what the properties essentially are.

**Causal structuralism**: The view that properties are individuated by and constituted by their structural-causal roles — the pattern of causes and effects they participate in. Structuralist properties have no "quiddity" beyond their role.

**Quidditism**: The view that natural properties have primitive, non-structural identities — "quiddities" — that are not constituted by their causal roles. Quidditistic properties could in principle swap their causal roles across possible worlds.

**Nomological necessity**: The kind of necessity that attaches to laws of nature — weaker than logical or metaphysical necessity but stronger than mere contingency. On some views, nomological necessity is grounded in the natures of natural properties.

**Natural kind essentialism**: The view that members of a natural kind (water, gold, tiger) share an essential nature — a microstructural property that is necessary and sufficient for kind membership.

**Categorical vs. hypothetical necessity**: Categorical necessity is absolute (nothing can change it); hypothetical or nomological necessity is conditional on the actual laws of nature. The distinction maps onto the debate between Humean contingency and essentialist necessity about laws.
