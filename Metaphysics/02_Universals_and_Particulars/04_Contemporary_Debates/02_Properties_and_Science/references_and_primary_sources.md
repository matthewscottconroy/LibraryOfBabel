# References and Primary Sources: Properties and Science

## Foundational Texts

Armstrong, D.M. *What Is a Law of Nature?* Cambridge University Press, 1983. Argues that laws of nature are necessitation relations between universals — a view that ties the ontology of properties to the metaphysics of natural law.

Shoemaker, Sydney. "Causality and Properties." In van Inwagen (ed.), *Time and Cause*. Reidel, 1980. The causal-role theory of property identity: properties are individuated by their causal contributions.

## Contemporary Works

Ellis, Brian. *Scientific Essentialism*. Cambridge University Press, 2001. Defends the view that natural properties have their causal roles essentially, making laws necessary and explaining natural kinds.

Bird, Alexander. *Nature's Metaphysics*. Oxford University Press, 2007. Argues that all natural properties are dispositional; the laws of nature are necessary given the nature of the properties they relate.

Ladyman, James and Ross, Don. *Every Thing Must Go*. Oxford University Press, 2007. Argues that fundamental ontology should be read from physics, not from intuition — leading to an ontology of structures rather than intrinsic properties.

Cartwright, Nancy. *The Dappled World*. Cambridge University Press, 1999. Argues against universal laws and for a more local, ceteris paribus view of how properties and powers operate in science.
