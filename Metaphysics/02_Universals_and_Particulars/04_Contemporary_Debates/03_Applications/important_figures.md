# Important Figures — Applications

*Key thinkers on color, mental, and moral properties.*

---

**G. E. Moore (1873–1958)** — Non-naturalist moral realism: goodness is a genuine non-natural property, open question argument against naturalistic analyses. *Principia Ethica* (1903).

**John Mackie (1917–1981)** — Error theory: moral properties do not exist; all moral claims are false. *Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong* (1977).

**Simon Blackburn (1944–)** — Quasi-realism and expressivism about moral claims, developed in *Spreading the Word* (1984) and *Essays in Quasi-Realism* (1993).

**Frank Jackson (1943–)** — The knowledge argument about Mary (color perception and qualia). *Perception: A Representative Theory* (1977) and subsequent work.

**Richard Boyd (1942–)** — Cornell moral realism, identifying goodness with natural properties. "How to Be a Moral Realist" (1988).

**Jaegwon Kim (1934–2019)** — Systematic work on mental properties, supervenience, and the mind-body problem. *Supervenience and Mind* (1993) and *Philosophy of Mind* (1996).

**Sydney Shoemaker (1931–)** — Functionalism about mental properties and causal theories of properties. *Identity, Cause, and Mind* (1984).

**Jonathan Dancy (1946–)** — Particularism in ethics: moral properties are context-dependent; there are no exceptionless moral universals. *Moral Reasons* (1993).
