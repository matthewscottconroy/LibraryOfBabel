# Important Terms and Ideas: Applications (Universals and Particulars)

**Supervenience**: A relation between sets of properties: the A-properties supervene on the B-properties if any two things indistinguishable in their B-properties are also indistinguishable in their A-properties. Used to articulate mind-body relations, moral supervenience, and more.

**Multiple realizability**: A property F is multiply realizable if it can be instantiated by things with very different lower-level physical properties. Often used to argue against type-type identity theories of mind: mental properties are not identical to unique physical properties.

**Token identity**: The view that each individual mental event (token) is identical to a particular physical event, even though mental properties may not be identical to physical properties. Token identity is compatible with multiple realizability.

**Grounding and the mental**: The question of what grounds mental properties — whether they are grounded in physical properties (physicalism) or whether they are fundamental (property dualism, panpsychism).

**Truthmakers for moral claims**: Whether moral truths require distinctively moral properties as truthmakers (moral realism) or whether they are made true by natural properties (naturalism) or have no truthmakers (anti-realism).

**Functionalism about properties**: In philosophy of mind, the view that mental properties are functional properties — defined by their causal roles rather than by their physical substrate. This parallels dispositional/structural accounts of natural properties.

**Laws and property instantiation**: Armstrong's view that laws are higher-order relations between universals: the law "All Fs are G" is the necessitation relation N(F, G). When F is instantiated by some individual, it follows that G is instantiated by that individual.
