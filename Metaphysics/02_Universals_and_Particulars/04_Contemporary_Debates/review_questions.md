# Review Questions — Contemporary Debates

1. Carnap's distinction between internal and external existence questions is designed to show that debates like the one about universals are either trivially resolved within a framework or meaningless when asked from outside one. Quine rejects this distinction by attacking the analytic/synthetic divide. Assess which view is more defensible: does the universals debate look like a substantive question about the world's structure, a choice between frameworks, or something else entirely once the metaontological issues are on the table?

2. Sider's joint-carving criterion and Schaffer's priority-based reformulation of the existence question are both neo-Quinean alternatives to flat existential debate. Explain how each reformulates the question "do universals exist?" and identify what additional resources (beyond standard Quinean ontological commitment) each requires. Do they genuinely sharpen the debate or shift it to equally contested terrain?

3. Armstrong restricts genuine universals to perfectly determinate properties, while the determinable/determinate hierarchy suggests that higher-level properties like PAIN or RED do important explanatory work that no perfectly determinate universal can fully capture. Explain Yablo's account of determinable causation, and assess whether it requires determinables to be genuine universals in order to succeed, or whether it can work even on a sparse-realist ontology that denies them full property-hood.

4. The color case shows that properties apparent to everyday perception may not survive scientific scrutiny as perfectly natural universals. Explain the specific difficulties that metamerism and illumination-dependence pose for color physicalism. What does the sparse realist conclude from these difficulties, and is that conclusion too quick — could redness be a genuine higher-level natural property without being perfectly fundamental?

5. Multiple realizability was supposed to show that mental properties cannot be identified with any specific physical property. Kim's exclusion argument then suggests that if mental properties are distinct from physical properties, they are causally inert. Trace the dialectic carefully: does token-identity theory (Davidson's anomalous monism) escape the exclusion argument, or does it preserve mental causation at the cost of making mental types causally irrelevant?

6. Moore's Open Question Argument concludes that GOODNESS is a non-natural universal, while Cornell realism argues that moral properties are a posteriori identical with natural ones. Assess both positions against the supervenience of the moral on the natural. Does the supervenience relation support, undermine, or leave neutral each of these forms of moral realism?

7. Higher-order properties — such as Armstrong's necessitation relation N(F, G) or the second-order functional property of being in PAIN — are required by several accounts examined in this chapter. Explain van Fraassen's inference problem for the necessitation relation, and compare it with Kim's exclusion problem for functional mental properties. Is there a common structural difficulty that afflicts both higher-order posits, or are the two problems fundamentally different in kind?

8. The chapter argues that the universals debate bears directly on the foundations of natural science, moral theory, and philosophy of mind. Having worked through the module, assess this claim: which downstream domain — science, ethics, or mind — is most tightly constrained by which answer one gives to the problem of universals, and what does this suggest about whether the metaphysics of properties should be settled independently of these applications or in light of them?
