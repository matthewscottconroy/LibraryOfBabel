# Important Figures — Numerical and Qualitative Identity

*Key thinkers on identity, Leibniz's Law, and the logic of identity.*

---

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)** — Formulated both the Indiscernibility of Identicals and the Identity of Indiscernibles. His principle of sufficient reason grounded the PII.

**Gottlob Frege (1848–1925)** — Frege's puzzle about the informativeness of identity statements ("Hesperus = Phosphorus" vs. "Hesperus = Hesperus") and his sense/reference distinction to resolve it. *On Sense and Reference* (1892).

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — Attempted to define identity in second-order terms; *Principia Mathematica* treats identity as defined from the Leibniz schema.

**Peter Geach (1916–2013)** — Relative identity doctrine: "a is the same F as b" is not reducible to absolute identity. *Reference and Generality* (1962).

**Saul Kripke (1940–2022)** — Argued for the necessity of identity (if a = b, then necessarily a = b) in the context of rigid designators. *Naming and Necessity* (1980).

**David Wiggins (1933–)** — Developed a theory of identity as sortal-relative and connected to persistence conditions. *Sameness and Substance* (1980) and *Sameness and Substance Renewed* (2001).

**Harold Noonan** — *Personal Identity* (1989); extensive survey of identity puzzles and solutions.
