# Important Terms and Ideas: Numerical and Qualitative Identity

**Numerical identity**: The strictest sense of identity — being one and the same thing. "Superman is Clark Kent" expresses numerical identity. Nothing is more fundamental; it is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

**Qualitative identity**: Complete likeness in all properties — indistinguishability. Two things can be qualitatively identical (exact duplicates) while being numerically distinct (two different things).

**Leibniz's Law (Indiscernibility of Identicals)**: If a = b, then for all properties P, Pa iff Pb. This is uncontroversial and logically valid. The converse (PII) is the contested Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles.

**Relative identity**: Geach's view that identity is always relative to a sortal concept — "a is the same F as b" where F is a sortal like "river" or "person." There may be cases where a is the same river as b but not the same body of water.

**Absolute identity**: The standard view opposing Geach's: identity is a single relation, not relative to any sortal. "a = b" is either true or false, not true-relative-to-F and false-relative-to-G.

**Criteria of identity**: Principles specifying the conditions under which two things of a given kind are identical — for example, sets are identical iff they have the same members; numbers are identical iff they are in one-one correspondence with the same things.

**Sortal concept**: A concept (like "river," "ship," "person") that supplies both criteria of application (what falls under it) and criteria of identity (when two things falling under it are the same). Distinguished from adjectives and mass terms.
