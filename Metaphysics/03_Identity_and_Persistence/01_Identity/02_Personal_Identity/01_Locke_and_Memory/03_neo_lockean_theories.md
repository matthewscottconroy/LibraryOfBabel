# Neo-Lockean Theories

*Psychological continuity theories that refine Locke's memory account.*

---

Locke's memory theory cannot survive Reid's objections in its naive form. But the Lockean insight — that personal identity is constituted by psychological rather than bodily facts — remains compelling. The neo-Lockean tradition, running from H. P. Grice (1941) through Sydney Shoemaker (1963) to Derek Parfit (1984), retains that insight while rebuilding the theory on sturdier logical foundations. The key refinements are two: replacing strict memory with causal continuity, and replacing direct connections with overlapping chains.

*Psychological connectedness* describes the direct relations between person-stages: A remembers (in the quasi-memory sense) B's experiences; A has beliefs, desires, and intentions inherited from B; A has character traits continuous with B's; A's intentions are executed by B's actions. Connectedness is a matter of degree — it can be stronger or weaker depending on how many direct psychological links obtain. *Psychological continuity* is the ancestral of connectedness: two person-stages are psychologically continuous iff there is a chain of overlapping direct connections linking them. The chain requirement is essential. Even if A at t₃ has no direct psychological connections to B at t₁, A and B can be psychologically continuous if there is an intermediate stage C at t₂ such that A is directly connected to C and C is directly connected to B.

The distinction between connectedness and continuity is Parfit's: *connectedness* is the direct relation; *continuity* is the transitive closure. Identity through time requires continuity rather than mere connectedness — which is why the general in Reid's story is psychologically continuous with the flogged boy (via intermediate stages) even though no direct memory connects them.

## The Causal Condition

Shoemaker's key contribution was a *causal condition*. Psychological continuity must proceed through the right kind of causal process — the normal mechanisms of memory, character formation, and personality development. Merely qualitative psychological similarity does not suffice; the psychological states of the later stage must be causally derived from the earlier stage in the appropriate way.

This handles teletransportation cases. If my brain is destroyed and a perfect replica is built elsewhere with all my psychological states reconstructed from a blueprint, the resulting person is *not* psychologically continuous with me in the causal sense — the right causal process was interrupted. Psychological qualitative similarity holds; psychological continuity (with the causal condition) does not. The formal statement:

> **(NL)** Person P at t₂ is the same person as person Q at t₁ iff there is a chain of overlapping psychological connections from Q at t₁ to P at t₂, and these connections are causally sustained by the normal operation of brain-based memory and personality processes.

## The Uniqueness Clause

A further refinement addresses fission cases. If person Q at t₁ is psychologically continuous with two distinct persons P and P' at t₂ — as in brain bisection — then neither P nor P' is identical with Q, because identity is one-one, not one-many. The uniqueness clause:

> **(NL+)** P at t₂ = Q at t₁ iff (i) there is a chain of overlapping psychological connections from Q to P, (ii) causally sustained by normal processes, and (iii) there is no person P' ≠ P at t₂ who is also psychologically continuous with Q at t₁.

But the uniqueness clause has a troubling consequence: whether you survive depends on facts *extrinsic* to you — namely, whether anyone else happens to also be psychologically continuous with your earlier self. If fission is possible, then the mere *possibility* of fission changes whether you survive in the ordinary one-continuation case. Your survival, it seems, should be determined by what happens to *you*, not by what happens elsewhere.

## Parfit's Conclusion: What Matters is Not Identity

Parfit uses the neo-Lockean framework but draws a conclusion that undercuts it from within. Given the uniqueness problem, he argues we should abandon the view that what matters in survival *is* personal identity. Instead, what matters is *psychological continuity and connectedness* — Relation R — whether or not it is accompanied by strict identity.

In normal survival, Relation R holds with uniqueness — and this is what we care about. In fission, Relation R holds without uniqueness — identity does not hold, but something equally good obtains. Therefore identity is not what matters. This is revisionary: it implies that self-concern should be calibrated to the *degree* of Relation R, not to the all-or-nothing of identity. Strong psychological connections give more reason to care about a future stage than weak connections do, even if both stages are (or both fail to be) strictly identical with me.

The key texts of the neo-Lockean tradition: Grice (1941) introduced the causal connection requirement and the chain condition; Shoemaker (1963, *Self-Knowledge and Self-Identity*) developed the causal theory of memory and applied it to personal identity; Parfit (1971, "Personal Identity"; 1984, *Reasons and Persons*) argued that identity is not what matters and drew revolutionary ethical implications; Lewis (1976, "Survival and Identity") offered a four-dimensionalist interpretation that preserves conventional identity-talk by allowing overlapping temporal worms to both be "the original" up to the moment of fission.
