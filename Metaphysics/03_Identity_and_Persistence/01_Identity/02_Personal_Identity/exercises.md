# Exercises — Personal Identity

*Problems for engaging with theories of personal identity.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Reid's Objection**
State Reid's brave officer objection carefully as a formal argument. How does the neo-Lockean revision (replacing memory with causal psychological continuity) handle the objection? Does the neo-Lockean revision fully resolve the problem, or does it face a version of the same difficulty?

**Exercise 2 — Animalism and Transplants**
If animalism is correct, then in a brain transplant case, where do *you* go? Evaluate Olson's answer (you stay with your body, the brain-transplant case does not preserve you). Do you find this counterintuitive? If so, does your intuition show that animalism is false, or does it show that you have confused intuitions?

**Exercise 3 — Fission and Identity**
Present Parfit's fission case. Why does it seem to show that identity is not what matters in survival? Construct a response: perhaps identity *is* what matters, and fission is a case of death followed by two new people, which is worse than ordinary survival. Evaluate both positions.

**Exercise 4 — The Non-Identity Problem**
Parfit argues that if a future person's existence is caused by a policy (even a harmful one), the person cannot be harmed by the policy (since without it, they would not exist). Does this seem right to you? Formulate the strongest objection to Parfit's position and assess how he might respond.

**Thought Experiment — Teletransportation**
You step into a teleporter that disintegrates your body and transmits the information to a receiver that assembles an exact physical duplicate. You survive (from the inside experience). Now: the teleporter malfunctions; it transmits your information but fails to disintegrate your original body. There are now two of "you." Who is the real you? On psychological continuity theory, neither has a prior claim. On animalism, the original body is you. Which view gives the right answer, and does your answer depend on facts about causal continuity, or about physical continuity, or about something else?
