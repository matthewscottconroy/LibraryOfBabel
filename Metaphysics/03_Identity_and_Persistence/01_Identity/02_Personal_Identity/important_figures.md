# Important Figures — Personal Identity

*Key thinkers in debates about what constitutes personal identity through time.*

---

**John Locke (1632–1704)** — Founding account of personal identity through consciousness and memory. *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, Book II, Ch. 27.

**Joseph Butler (1692–1752)** — Argued that Locke's memory theory presupposes personal identity rather than constituting it. *Dissertation of Personal Identity* (1736).

**Thomas Reid (1710–1796)** — The "brave officer" transitivity objection to Locke. *Essays on the Intellectual Powers of Man* (1786).

**Sydney Shoemaker (1931–)** — Neo-Lockean psychological continuity theory. *Self-Knowledge and Self-Identity* (1963) and *Personal Identity: A Materialist's Account* (1984, with Swinburne).

**Derek Parfit (1942–2017)** — *Reasons and Persons* (1984): Reductionism, the non-identity problem, and the ethical consequences of revisionary personal identity theory.

**Peter Unger (1942–)** — Defended a version of psychological continuity theory in *Identity, Consciousness and Value* (1990).

**Eric Olson (1964–)** — Animalism: we are animals and personal identity is animal identity. *The Human Animal* (1997).

**David Wiggins (1933–)** — Identity as sortal-relative, with personal identity grounded in the biological sortal PERSON. *Sameness and Substance Renewed* (2001).
