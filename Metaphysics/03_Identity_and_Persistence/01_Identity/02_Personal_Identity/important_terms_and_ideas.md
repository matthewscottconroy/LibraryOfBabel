# Important Terms and Ideas: Personal Identity

**Psychological continuity theory**: The view that personal identity consists in overlapping chains of psychological connections — memory, belief, intention, character — linking temporal stages of a person.

**Physical/biological continuity theory (animalism)**: The view that personal identity is constituted by the continuous existence of a living human animal. What makes you the same person over time is what makes you the same organism over time.

**Narrative identity**: The view (Ricoeur, MacIntyre) that personal identity is constituted by the self's self-understanding through narrative — the story one tells about oneself across time.

**Fission cases**: Thought experiments in which a person splits into two (e.g., by brain-bisection into two transplants), each of which is psychologically continuous with the original. Such cases are used to argue against the necessity of identity for survival.

**What matters in survival**: Parfit's claim that psychological continuity — not strict identity — is what matters practically and morally. Even if you survive in the strict numerical sense, what matters is whether you are psychologically continuous with your future self.

**Four-dimensionalism and persons**: On the worm view, a person is a four-dimensional entity extended through time as well as space. Personal identity is identity of the worm; survival is having a temporal part in the future.

**Soul theory**: The view that personal identity consists in the persistence of an immaterial soul or Cartesian ego. The soul is the subject of experience, and its continuity is what makes a person over time the same person.

**The closest-continuer theory**: Nozick's view that x at t₁ is the same person as y at t₂ iff y is x's closest continuer — the entity most psychologically continuous with x at t₁ — provided some continuity threshold is met.
