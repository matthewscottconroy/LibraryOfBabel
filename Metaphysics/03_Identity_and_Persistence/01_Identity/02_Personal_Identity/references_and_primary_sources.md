# References and Primary Sources: Personal Identity

## Foundational Texts

Locke, John. *Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, Book II, ch. 27. Ed. Nidditch. Oxford University Press, 1975. Locke's psychological continuity theory: personal identity consists in continuity of memory and consciousness.

Hume, David. *A Treatise of Human Nature*, Book I, Part IV, §6. Ed. Selby-Bigge. Oxford: Clarendon Press. Hume's bundle theory of the self: no impression of a simple, continuous self; the self is a bundle of perceptions.

## Contemporary Works

Parfit, Derek. *Reasons and Persons*, Part III. Oxford University Press, 1984. The most influential modern treatment; argues that personal identity is not what matters in survival — psychological continuity without identity can matter as much.

Olson, Eric. *The Human Animal*. Oxford University Press, 1997. Defends animalism: we are essentially human animals; personal identity is biological continuity.

Shoemaker, Sydney and Swinburne, Richard. *Personal Identity*. Blackwell, 1984. Two contrasting essays — Shoemaker defending psychological continuity, Swinburne defending soul-based identity.

Johnston, Mark. "Human Beings." *Journal of Philosophy* 84 (1987): 59-83. A careful treatment of the relationship between persons and human organisms.
