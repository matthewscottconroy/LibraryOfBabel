# Review Questions — Identity

1. The distinction between numerical and qualitative identity seems obvious once stated, yet Max Black's two-sphere thought experiment shows that the Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles (PII) is genuinely contested. What exactly is at stake in that dispute, and why can't the bundle theorist simply accept that the two spheres are one?

2. Leibniz's Law (the Indiscernibility of Identicals) is described as universally accepted, while PII is a contested metaphysical thesis. Yet both are often lumped together as "Leibniz's Law." Why does conflating the two directions lead to philosophical errors, and can you identify a case in the chapter where the conflation causes confusion?

3. Reid's brave officer case is usually presented as a transitivity objection. But Reid also raises a circularity objection against Locke. Which objection cuts deeper, and why does Shoemaker's quasi-memory concept address only the circularity problem rather than solving the transitivity problem directly?

4. Animalism and the neo-Lockean psychological continuity theory generate opposite verdicts on the brain-transplant case. Both claim to be tracking something metaphysically important — biological life versus conscious psychological continuity. Assess what each view loses by accepting its own verdict on that case: what cost does the animalist pay, and what cost does the neo-Lockean pay?

5. Parfit argues that what matters in survival is Relation R (psychological continuity and connectedness), not strict numerical identity. Yet he derives this conclusion from the fission puzzle rather than from an independent account of what matters. Does his argument establish that identity is not what matters, or only that identity cannot be what grounds survival in branching cases?

6. Geach's relative identity and Wiggins's sortal-based absolute identity arrive at different logical conclusions but seem to agree on a crucial point: identity-tracking always requires a sortal concept. What exactly do they disagree about, and does accepting sortal-dependence of tracking commit one to Geach's radical thesis about there being no absolute identity?

7. The circularity in Locke's memory theory (genuine memory presupposes personal identity) and the circularity Evans's argument reveals in claiming vague identity (the property λx.∇(x = a) is description-sensitive) are structurally similar. What does each circularity reveal about the limits of using a concept to define itself, and are the remedies — quasi-memory in one case, semantic ascent in the other — analogous?

8. If Parfit's reductionism about persons is correct and self-concern should be calibrated to the degree of Relation R rather than to all-or-nothing identity, what becomes of the transitivity of identity as a formal logical property? Does Parfit's position require abandoning transitivity, or does it only recommend abandoning the ethical weight we attach to it?
