# The Problem of Persistence

The problem of persistence is, at its core, a problem about how change is possible without contradiction. If a leaf is green in summer and red in autumn, and if the same leaf — numerically one individual — exists at both times, then it seems that the very same thing has incompatible properties: green and not-green, red and not-red. But contradictions are impossible. Something must give — and what gives, in the various theories of persistence, is one of the central issues of this chapter.

The naive view — the view held before the problem has been posed clearly — is that material objects simply persist through time, being present at one time, then the next, and so on. What makes this naive view puzzling is the observation that objects change their properties over time. The leaf changes color; the person changes height and opinion and character. But if the leaf is present at t₁ with property G and present at t₂ with property R, and if G and R are incompatible, then the leaf has incompatible properties — which is a contradiction.

Three main strategies have been developed to dissolve or solve this apparent contradiction. Presentism dissolves it by holding that only present objects exist: when the leaf is red, it is not also green, because there simply is no green leaf — the green leaf has ceased to exist. Endurantism holds that the leaf is wholly present at each time it exists, and handles incompatible properties by relativizing them to times: the leaf has the property being-green-at-t₁ and the property being-red-at-t₂, and these are not incompatible. Perdurantism (or four-dimensionalism) holds that the leaf is a temporally extended entity that has different temporal parts at different times: the summer temporal part is green, the autumn temporal part is red, and these parts are numerically distinct, so there is no contradiction.

Each strategy has philosophical costs. This section diagnoses the problem with care, introduces the three main strategies, and prepares the reader for the detailed examination of endurantism and perdurantism that follows.
