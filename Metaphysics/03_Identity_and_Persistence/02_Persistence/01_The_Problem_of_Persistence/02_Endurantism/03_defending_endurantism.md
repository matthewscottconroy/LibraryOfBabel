# Defending Endurantism

*The main arguments in favor of three-dimensionalism and responses to perdurantist challenges.*

---

Endurantism is often presented on the back foot — responding to Lewis's temporary intrinsics argument, or to the colocation problem, or to pressure from special relativity. But there are positive reasons to accept the view, and responses to the main perdurantist challenges have been developed. We should consider both.

## Positive Arguments for Endurantism

*Ordinary language and phenomenology* are the first line of support. We ordinarily refer to persisting objects without temporal indices — "this is the same table that was here yesterday." Perdurantism requires a wholesale revision of this practice, treating "this table" as picking out a stage rather than an enduring object. Endurantism preserves the ordinary conception. When I greet a friend after a long absence, I experience myself as greeting *the same person* — not a new person who is a temporal counterpart of the earlier friend. Perdurantism seems to say this experience is systematically misleading — a cost that must be reckoned.

*Parsimony* provides a second argument. If I exist from birth to death, perdurantism posits an uncountable infinity of temporal stages — one for each instant. What work do these stages do that the single enduring object does not? Endurantism is more parsimonious: one object rather than infinitely many stages. This is Occam's Razor applied to the temporal dimension.

*Action and agency* provide a third argument. Our concept of agency seems to require an enduring agent who acts over time. When I deliberate about what to do tomorrow, it is *I* — one entity across time — who deliberates and who will later act. The deliberating-now and the acting-later are the same subject. On temporal-parts theories, the deliberating stage and the acting stage are numerically distinct objects. It becomes unclear how the deliberating stage's reasons connect to the acting stage's behavior. The fragmentation of agency across stages cuts against the unity of practical rationality.

*Moral responsibility* presses the same point. Responsibility requires that the person who acted in the past is the very same person held responsible in the present. On perdurantism, these are distinct temporal stages. The connection between the past-stage that acted and the present-stage held responsible is one of temporal overlap in a common worm — but why should this temporal overlap generate moral responsibility? Endurantism provides a simpler answer: it is literally the same person, numerically identical, who acted then and is held responsible now.

## Response to Lewis's Temporary Intrinsics Objection

The time-indexing strategy need not collapse the intrinsic/extrinsic distinction. Haslanger (1989) articulates the key distinction: *relational properties*, whose instantiation essentially involves a relation to a distinct entity (like "being taller than Napoleon," which essentially involves Napoleon), versus *temporally located properties*, instantiated at a time, where the time specifies *when* the property is had without making the property itself dependent on the time as a relatum.

Being spherical at t₁ is, on this view, a matter of the property of sphericality being instantiated at t₁ — not a relation between the ball and t₁ in the way that "taller than" is a relation between Socrates and Theaetetus. The "at t" locates the instantiation in time without building the time into the property's content. This distinction may be contested, but it provides a conceptual framework within which endurantism can resist the collapse of intrinsic into relational properties.

## Response to the Coincidence Problem

The coincidence of distinct endurant objects (statue and clay) is a challenge, but several responses are available. *Constitutionalism* (Baker, Rea) accepts that two distinct objects can coincide: the clay constitutes the statue without being the statue. Coincidence is a genuine but non-identity relation. *Sortal relative identity* (Wiggins) holds that the clay and statue are the same *bronze* but not the same *artifact* — one object, differently sorted. *Eliminativism* holds that only one of the two objects genuinely exists; the other is a conceptual scheme projected onto the single underlying material. Each response has costs, but none is obviously worse than the perdurantist's positing of infinitely many temporal stages.

## Mereological Considerations

Four-dimensionalism seems committed to treating time and space as metaphysically symmetric: both are dimensions over which objects have parts. But there is an asymmetry: spatial parts can be physically separated from their wholes (a leg detached from a table), while temporal stages cannot be detached — the concept of "removing" yesterday's stage from an object is incoherent. This disanalogy between spatial and temporal parts is a feature of endurantism, not a bug: spatial parts are genuine parts; temporal "stages" are not parts at all, but just the same enduring object at different times. Endurantism captures the asymmetry between spatial and temporal dimensions that four-dimensionalism tends to obscure.
