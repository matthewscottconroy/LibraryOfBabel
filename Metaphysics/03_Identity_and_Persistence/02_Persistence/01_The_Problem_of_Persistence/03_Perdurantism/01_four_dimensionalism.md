# Four-Dimensionalism

*The view that objects persist by having temporal parts spread through time.*

---

Consider how we understand spatial extension. It does not puzzle us that the left half of a table exists while the right half also exists — they are different spatial parts of one table. Four-dimensionalism extends this to time: the table-yesterday and the table-today are different temporal parts of one four-dimensional table-worm. Just as spatial extension does not require the table to be in two places simultaneously, temporal extension does not require the table to be "present at" multiple times in any mysterious sense — it simply spans them. The temporal dimension is, on this view, just another dimension over which objects extend.

Four-dimensionalism holds that persisting objects are four-dimensional entities extended through both space and time, with distinct temporal parts at each time. The spatial analogy motivates the view:

- **(SA)** For any spatially extended object O, O has distinct spatial parts at each spatial location it occupies.
- **(TA)** For any temporally extended object O, O has distinct temporal parts at each temporal location it occupies. [By analogy with SA]

The argument from analogy is not conclusive — there may be relevant disanalogies between space and time — but it provides powerful motivation for taking temporal parts seriously, and Lewis's defense connects the analogy to solutions to several deep problems.

## Lewis's Defense

Lewis's defense of four-dimensionalism in *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986) and his papers on persistence connects the view to several theoretical advantages.

*The temporary intrinsics problem*: a spherical temporal-part at t₁ and a dented temporal-part at t₂ are distinct objects, each having its intrinsic properties without contradiction. Intrinsic properties are instantiated *simpliciter* by the relevant stage, not time-indexed by the whole enduring object.

*Coincident objects*: the statue and the clay share all their temporal stages at times when both exist, but they have different temporal stages before and after those times (the clay-worm begins and ends at different times than the statue-worm). They are numerically distinct four-dimensional objects that happen to share temporal parts during the period of coincidence. No mystery about spatial coincidence: two worms share a segment.

*Fission*: fission creates two worms that share an initial segment. There were always two overlapping worms — they just became distinguishable after fission. The question "which worm is the original person?" has a precise answer: both worms were "the original" up to the moment of fission (each has the relevant pre-fission stages as genuine parts), but they are distinct worms, and neither is identical with the other post-fission.

*Special relativity*: four-dimensionalism fits naturally with the four-dimensional spacetime of special relativity. In Minkowski spacetime, physical objects are worldlines — four-dimensional entities extended through spacetime. Endurantism's claim that objects are wholly present at a time requires a privileged simultaneity, which special relativity denies. Four-dimensionalism is compatible with the relativity of simultaneity because it does not require a privileged "now."

## Sider's Argument

Theodore Sider (*Four-Dimensionalism*, 2001) provides the most systematic contemporary defense. His main argument is from *composition*: given a principle of *unrestricted temporal composition* (analogous to unrestricted spatial composition), temporal parts exist for all objects at all intervals. He defends unrestricted temporal composition by showing that any restriction would be arbitrary — there is no principled stopping point for restricting which temporal intervals yield genuine temporal parts, just as there is no principled stopping point for restricting which spatial regions yield genuine spatial parts.

Formally, let P(x, y) abbreviate "x is a part of y" and E(x, t) abbreviate "x exists at t." Four-dimensionalism holds:

> **(4D)** For any persisting object O and time t at which O exists, there exists a temporal part Oₜ such that: P(Oₜ, O), E(Oₜ, t), and Oₜ is spatially coincident with O throughout its existence.

## Objections

*The rotating homogeneous sphere*: consider a perfectly homogeneous sphere rotating uniformly. The four-dimensionalist must say there are temporal stages at each instant. But adjacent stages are qualitatively identical (the sphere looks the same). What distinguishes one stage from another? What grounds the claim that it is *rotating*?

*Causal overdetermination*: if temporal stages are distinct objects that (at a moment) occupy the same region as the whole object, then at any moment there are two objects in the same place — the stage and the worm. Both seem to cause the same effects (the stage supports my coffee mug; the table-worm also supports my mug). Causal overdetermination results.

*Arbitrary worms*: universal composition principles allow arbitrary "worms" composed of scattered temporal stages with no natural connection. The worm composed of my-Monday-morning-stage and the Eiffel Tower's-Thursday-afternoon-stage is a four-dimensional object on Lewis's view. Four-dimensionalism thus populates the world with an enormous array of arbitrary, gerrymandered objects that seem to have no natural status.

Despite these objections, four-dimensionalism remains a powerful and systematic framework for the metaphysics of persistence, with decisive advantages in handling change, coincidence, and fission.
