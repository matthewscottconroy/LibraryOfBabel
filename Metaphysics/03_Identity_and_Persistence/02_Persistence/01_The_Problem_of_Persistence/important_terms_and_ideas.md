# Important Terms and Ideas: The Problem of Persistence

**Persistence**: The property of existing at more than one time — of continuing to exist through time. The philosophical question is how objects (and persons) persist: what makes them the same object at different times.

**Endurance (three-dimensionalism)**: The view that objects persist by being wholly present at each moment of their existence — they are entirely present at t₁ and entirely present (numerically the same) at t₂. There are no temporal parts.

**Perdurance (four-dimensionalism)**: The view that objects persist by having distinct temporal parts at each time — they are extended through time as well as space. The object as a whole is a four-dimensional spacetime worm.

**Stage theory**: The view that ordinary object-names refer to instantaneous temporal stages rather than extended four-dimensional worms. Stages persist by having counterparts at other times rather than by being identical to them.

**Temporal parts**: The parts of a four-dimensional object that exist at specific times. On perdurance, my current temporal part exists now; my past and future temporal parts exist at other times. The whole person is the sum of all temporal parts.

**Problem of temporary intrinsics**: Lewis's argument for four-dimensionalism: intrinsic properties like shape can change over time; if the person is wholly present at t₁ (bent) and t₂ (straight), how does the same person have incompatible intrinsic properties? Four-dimensionalism solves this by relativizing properties to temporal parts.

**Perdurance vs. endurance and identity**: Perdurance makes diachronic identity strict: the same worm. Endurance treats diachronic identity as genuine numerical identity of the wholly present object across times.
