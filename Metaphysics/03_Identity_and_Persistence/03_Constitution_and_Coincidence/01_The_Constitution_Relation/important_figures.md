# Important Figures: The Constitution Relation

**Lynne Rudder Baker** (b. 1944) developed the most systematic contemporary account of constitution as a genuine metaphysical relation distinct from identity. Her book *Persons and Bodies* (2000) argues that persons are constituted by but not identical to their bodies.

**Kit Fine** (b. 1946) analyzed coincident objects and the constitution relation using his theory of variable embodiment, arguing that ordinary objects are made of matter but are not identical to it.

**Mark Johnston** has written influentially on the distinction between constitution and identity, arguing that coincident objects are a real phenomenon requiring a non-reductive explanation.

**David Wiggins** (b. 1933) developed sortalism: the identity conditions of an object are fixed by the sortal concept it falls under. His work on constitution emphasizes that "same F" relations are sortal-relative.

**Peter van Inwagen** (b. 1942) offers a more deflationary view — arguing that ordinary objects like statues and lumps either do not both exist or are identical — a foil for the constitution view.
