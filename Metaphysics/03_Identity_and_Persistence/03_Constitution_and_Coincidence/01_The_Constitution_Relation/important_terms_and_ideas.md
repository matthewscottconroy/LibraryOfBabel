# Important Terms and Ideas: The Constitution Relation

**Constitution**: The relation holding when one thing is "made of" another without being identical to it. The statue is constituted by the lump of clay; they share the same matter at the same time but are distinct objects with different persistence conditions.

**Material coincidence**: The phenomenon of two numerically distinct objects occupying the same spatial location at the same time — sharing all their matter. The statue and the lump are co-located, co-material, but distinct.

**Persistence conditions**: The conditions under which an object of a given kind continues to exist through change. Statues and lumps differ in their persistence conditions: the statue cannot survive radical squashing; the lump can.

**Sortal-relative identity**: Wiggins's view that "same F" is a genuine identity relation only when relativized to a sortal concept F. The statue and lump may be the same lump while being different statues — because they fall under different sortals.

**Variable embodiment (Fine)**: Fine's account of how ordinary objects are related to their material realizers: an object is "embodied" by different parcels of matter at different times, and its identity conditions are given by its kind, not its matter.

**Dominant sortal**: The claim (Burke) that when multiple candidate sortals apply to the same region, one is dominant and determines which object exists there. Only the dominant object genuinely exists; the others are "not objects" strictly speaking.

**The grounding problem**: A challenge for constitution theory: if the statue and the lump are co-located, share all their matter, and have the same microphysical properties, what grounds their distinctness? What makes it the case that they are two things, not one?
