# References and Primary Sources: The Constitution Relation

## Foundational Texts

Wiggins, David. *Sameness and Substance Renewed*. Cambridge University Press, 2001. Develops the theory that identity is sortal-relative and that statues and lumps are genuinely distinct objects falling under different sortals.

Baker, Lynne Rudder. *Persons and Bodies*. Cambridge University Press, 2000. The most systematic defense of constitution theory: objects can constitute each other without being identical to each other.

## Contemporary Works

Fine, Kit. "Coincidence and the Grounds of Identity." Manuscript circulated widely; an influential analysis distinguishing constitution from identity.

Sider, Theodore. *Four-Dimensionalism*. Oxford University Press, 2001. Discusses how coincident objects relate to the four-dimensionalist framework.

Burke, Michael. "Preserving the Principle of One Object to a Place." *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 72 (1994): 258-273. Defends the view that only one object (the dominant sortal) exists at each spatiotemporal region.

Heller, Mark. *The Ontology of Physical Objects*. Cambridge University Press, 1990. Defends a four-dimensionalist approach that dissolves puzzles about constitution by identifying coincident objects with temporal stages.
