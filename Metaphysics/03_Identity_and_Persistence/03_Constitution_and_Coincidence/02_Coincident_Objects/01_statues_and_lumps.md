# Statues and Lumps

*The paradigm case for coincident objects.*

---

Suppose a sculptor shapes a lump of bronze into a statue. We now seem to have two things before us — the lump, and the statue — though we can lift only one object, measure only one mass, and point to only one location. The puzzle is that both cannot be true: either we have two objects or we have one, and neither answer is comfortable. Call the lump LUMP and the statue GOLIATH. LUMP and GOLIATH occupy the same place, are made of the same matter, and are otherwise physically indistinguishable. Yet they seem to be numerically distinct.

The case for non-identity rests on several property-differences. Temporally: LUMP existed before GOLIATH was created (before the shaping). LUMP has the property "existed in 2000"; GOLIATH does not (if created in 2005). By Leibniz's Law, LUMP ≠ GOLIATH. Modally: if the bronze were melted down, GOLIATH would cease to exist but LUMP would survive (as a shapeless mass). GOLIATH has the property "would be destroyed by melting"; LUMP does not. Again by Leibniz's Law, GOLIATH ≠ LUMP. Historically: GOLIATH was created by an act of sculpting, with artistic intention; LUMP was created by combining two smaller lumps, without artistic intention.

The Leibniz's Law argument runs:

- P1. If GOLIATH = LUMP, then ∀F(F GOLIATH ↔ F LUMP).
- P2. GOLIATH would be destroyed by squashing into a ball; LUMP would not.
- P3. ∃F(F GOLIATH ∧ ¬F LUMP).
- C. GOLIATH ≠ LUMP.

## The Coincidence Puzzle

Yet GOLIATH and LUMP share their spatiotemporal location, their mass, their chemical composition, and during the period of the statue's existence, every physical property. How can two distinct objects be in exactly the same place at the same time, made of exactly the same matter?

*Friends of coincidence* (Baker, Rea, Thomson, Wiggins, Fine) accept this conclusion: GOLIATH and LUMP are two numerically distinct objects that coincide during the period of the statue's existence. Each has its own persistence conditions and modal profile. Coincidence is a genuine metaphysical phenomenon. Baker's constitutionalism: LUMP constitutes GOLIATH. GOLIATH "borrows" its properties from LUMP during coincidence — is bronze, is heavy, is in the studio — because LUMP is all these things. But GOLIATH and LUMP differ in their intrinsic modal and sortal properties.

The four main positions:

*Coincidentalism* holds that both GOLIATH and LUMP exist, they coincide, and they are numerically distinct. The coincidence is a brute metaphysical fact about the modal profiles of different kinds of objects.

*Four-dimensionalism* holds that GOLIATH and LUMP are four-dimensional worms with different temporal extents. They share temporal stages during the coincidence period but are distinct worms. No mystery about spatial coincidence: two worms share a segment.

*Monism* holds that there is only one object — the bronze in a certain configuration — and our different descriptions ("lump," "statue") pick out different aspects of the same thing. Relative identity: it is the same bronze but different sortals apply.

*Eliminativism* holds that one (or both) of them does not really exist. Van Inwagen eliminates statues (only the bronze-simples arranged statue-wise exist). Others eliminate lumps (only the statue — a structured entity with form — is a genuine object).

## Thought Experiment Extensions

The gradual replacement variant presses the case further. Suppose GOLIATH's bronze is gradually replaced, atom by atom, with gold. At some point it is entirely gold. Is the resulting gold statue GOLIATH? Is the original bronze still LUMP (if removed)? These extensions test the robustness of the coincidence framework under material change.

The modal variant is illuminating: in a world where LUMP is never sculpted, does GOLIATH exist? The constitutionalist says no — GOLIATH is contingent on the act of sculpting. LUMP exists in that world; GOLIATH does not. This confirms the modal non-identity and shows that LUMP has a larger modal range than GOLIATH: LUMP can exist in worlds where GOLIATH cannot.

The statue-lump case thus serves as a test case for virtually every position in the metaphysics of coincidence, constitution, and material persistence. The positions it generates — and the responses and counter-responses they elicit — are examined in the following sections.
