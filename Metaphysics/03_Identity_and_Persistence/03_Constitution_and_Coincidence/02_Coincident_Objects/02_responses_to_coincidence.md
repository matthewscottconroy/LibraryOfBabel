# Responses to Coincidence

*The main philosophical strategies for handling or avoiding coincident objects.*

---

The challenge of coincident objects admits several systematic responses, each reflecting broader metaphysical commitments. The challenge can be put starkly: if GOLIATH (the statue) and LUMP (the bronze) are both wholly present at the same location from t₁ to t₂, made of the same matter, and differ only in modal and temporal properties, what distinguishes them as two objects rather than one? Five main strategies have been developed.

## Constitution Monism (Wiggins)

The first response accepts that the statue and the lump coincide but denies that two distinct *objects* are present. There is one object — the bronze in a certain configuration — and our different descriptions ("lump," "statue") pick out different *aspects* or *ways of describing* the same thing. On Wiggins's relative identity theory, the bronze is the same lump as before but a different statue, without there being two distinct objects. The same individual falls under both the concept LUMP and the concept STATUE; what differs is not the individual but the sortal applied. The persistence conditions associated with different sortals yield different verdicts about survival under different scenarios — but these are verdicts about the individual under different descriptions, not verdicts about two distinct objects.

The problem: if there is only one object and it falls under both LUMP and STATUE, it has both the property "would survive squashing" (as a lump) and "would not survive squashing" (as a statue). These seem contradictory. Wiggins must say that modal properties are sortal-relative: what it is for something to survive squashing *as a lump* is different from surviving squashing *as a statue*. This is a form of relative modality that not all philosophers accept.

## Constitution Dualism (Baker, Rea, Thomson)

The second response accepts both the distinction in modal and temporal properties *and* the numerical distinctness of the objects. There are genuinely two coincident objects: the lump and the statue. Baker's account: the lump constitutes the statue. The statue *derives* its physical properties from the lump in a non-symmetric, asymmetric dependency relation. Two distinct objects can coincide because being a statue involves a different set of persistence conditions from being a lump — conditions not reducible to physical co-location.

The pressing problem: if two distinct objects are wholly present in the same spatial region at the same time, do they have twice the mass? Baker responds: they share their physical properties by a derivation relation — the statue's mass is not *additional* to the lump's mass but is *the same mass* derived from the lump. But this derivation relation requires careful articulation, and it is not obvious it can be made fully coherent.

## Four-Dimensional Solution (Lewis, Sider)

The lump and the statue are four-dimensional worms that overlap during the period of the statue's existence. LUMP-WORM extends from t₀ (creation of lump) to t₃ (destruction of lump), passing through the sculpting at t₁ and the destruction of the statue at t₂. GOLIATH-WORM extends from t₁ (sculpting) to t₂ (melting). During [t₁, t₂], both worms share temporal stages.

These two worms are distinct four-dimensional objects that happen to share a temporal segment. There is no mystery about two things being in the same place at the same time — they share *the same thing* at those times (the shared temporal stage) while differing in their other temporal parts. The problem: the region is occupied by both LUMP-WORM and GOLIATH-WORM during [t₁, t₂]. If both are genuine material objects, the region contains twice the matter — but intuition says it contains only one portion of bronze. The four-dimensionalist responds: the two worms *overlap* in the temporal stages, and the mass of the region is just the mass of the shared temporal stages (counted once). The worms coincide without doubling the matter.

## Eliminativism

Only one of the two objects exists — either the material lump (van Inwagen-style: only organisms really exist) or a primitive undifferentiated material thing. Talk of "statues" and "lumps" is useful shorthand without ontological commitment. Van Inwagen's version: there are bronze atoms arranged statue-wise and lump-wise, but no statue and no lump as additional objects over and above the atoms. The quantifier "there is a statue" is contextually acceptable shorthand but does not commit to a statue as an object.

The cost: if statues do not exist, then art, property, and cultural institutions built around statues are built on ontological fictions. Ordinary practice resists eliminativism.

## Sortal Relative Identity (Geach)

LUMP and GOLIATH are the same *bronze* but different *statues*. There is no further absolute identity question. This avoids both the coincidence and the elimination strategies by denying that numerical identity is an absolute relation. Each response reflects a broader metaphysical commitment: to constitution, to four-dimensionalism, to eliminativism about ordinary objects, or to relative identity. The puzzle of coincident objects thus serves as a touchstone for comprehensive metaphysical theories.
