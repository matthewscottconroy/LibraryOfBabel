# Coincidence and Modality

*How modal differences between coincident objects ground their numerical distinctness.*

---

The primary evidence for the numerical distinctness of coincident objects comes from modal differences. The statue has certain modal properties — it would be destroyed by squashing — that the lump lacks. Since identical things must share all their properties (Leibniz's Law), the modal difference establishes non-identity. But this is only half the story. Once we have accepted that the statue and the lump are numerically distinct, we face a deeper question: *what grounds* their modal differences? Two physically indistinguishable objects — sharing the same location, the same mass, the same chemical composition — seem to differ only in their modal profiles. What could explain this?

The modal argument itself has a clear structure:

- P1. a = b → ∀F(Fa ↔ Fb). [Leibniz's Law]
- P2. GOLIATH has the property "would be destroyed by squashing into a ball." [Modal claim about the statue]
- P3. LUMP does not have the property "would be destroyed by squashing into a ball." [Modal claim about the lump]
- P4. ∃F(F GOLIATH ∧ ¬F LUMP). [From P2, P3]
- C. GOLIATH ≠ LUMP. [By modus tollens on P1 with P4]

## What Grounds the Modal Differences?

The natural first answer is *sortal-based grounding*. The modal differences are grounded in the different *kinds* or *sortals* the objects instantiate. GOLIATH is a statue, and statues qua statues cannot survive radical deformation. LUMP is a lump of bronze, and lumps qua lumps can survive such deformation. The modal properties flow from what kind of thing the object is — from its sortal. This connects to debates about essential properties: statues have different essential properties than lumps. A statue is essentially a certain shape and kind of object; a lump is essentially some matter in some spatial configuration.

A second answer is *haecceitism about kinds*. The modal difference is grounded in the specific individual's thisness combined with its kind membership. GOLIATH, as *this particular statue*, has essential properties that flow from its artistic origin and intended form. LUMP, as *this particular lump*, has essential properties that flow from its material composition. The same piece of matter harbors two different individuals with different thisnesses, each with different essential properties.

## The Essentiality of Origin

The modal grounding issue connects to the necessity of origin. If GOLIATH necessarily originates in a certain sculpting process — because that process is part of what makes it *this statue* — then in worlds where the bronze is not sculpted that way, GOLIATH does not exist. Since LUMP can exist in those worlds (as an unsculpted lump of bronze), they are modally distinct objects even before the sculpting: GOLIATH could only have come into being through sculpting; LUMP need not have been sculpted.

We can capture this in possible worlds terms. Let @ be the actual world:

- In world W₁ (where the bronze is squashed after sculpting): LUMP exists (as a squashed lump), but GOLIATH does not.
- In world W₂ (where the bronze is never sculpted): LUMP exists, but GOLIATH does not.
- In world W₃ (where the bronze is sculpted differently, into a different statue): LUMP exists, but GOLIATH does not.

The set of possible worlds in which LUMP exists is a proper superset of the set in which GOLIATH exists. This captures the modal difference: LUMP has a larger modal range than GOLIATH.

## Challenges to Modal Grounding

Notice that a grounding regress threatens. The modal difference is grounded in which kind of thing the object is (its sortal). But what grounds its sortal membership? If GOLIATH's being a statue is what grounds its modal sensitivity to squashing, what grounds GOLIATH's being a statue? The sculpting process, the artistic context, the maker's intention. But then the modal difference is grounded in contingent historical facts — which means the modal difference is itself contingent. Is there a non-contingent ground for the modal difference?

A further challenge: during the period of coincidence, GOLIATH and LUMP share all their categorical (non-modal) properties. Only their modal profiles differ. Can two physically indistinguishable objects *at a time* have genuinely different modal profiles? Some philosophers (following Lewis) are skeptical: modal properties supervene on categorical properties, so physically identical objects cannot differ modally.

The constitution theorist's response: modal properties do not supervene on *intrinsic* categorical properties but on *origin* and *kind*. Two qualitatively identical artifacts can differ modally if they have different origins — one handmade (with the kind "original"), one mass-produced (with the kind "replica"). GOLIATH and LUMP differ in origin (GOLIATH originates in an act of sculpting; LUMP originates in the combining of bronze pieces) and in kind. Origin-dependent modal profiles are compatible with categorical indistinguishability at a moment.

This shows that coincidence cases are not only about persistence through time but about the modal structure of individual existence — connecting the problem of coincident objects to the broader theory of essentialism, de re modality, and the necessity of origin.
