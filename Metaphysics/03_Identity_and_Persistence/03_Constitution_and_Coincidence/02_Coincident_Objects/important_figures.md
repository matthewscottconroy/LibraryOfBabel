# Important Figures: Coincident Objects

**Judith Jarvis Thomson** (1929–2020) defended the view that spatially coincident objects are genuinely distinct, arguing that ordinary sortal concepts generate distinct persistence conditions for objects in the same location.

**David Lewis** (1941–2001) proposed that coincident-object puzzles are solved by temporal parts: coincident objects are identical four-dimensional worms that share temporal stages. His perdurance view eliminates genuine coincidence at a time.

**Peter Geach** (1916–2013) introduced the notion of relative identity, used to analyze cases where objects are the same F but different Gs — relevant to dissolving apparent coincidences without positing distinct co-located objects.

**Michael Burke** has defended the dominant-kinds view: apparent coincident objects are not genuinely distinct; the dominant sortal determines which single object occupies a location.

**Ernest Sosa** has contributed to understanding how ordinary object-sortal concepts generate the coincidence puzzles and how a non-reductionist ontology can handle them.
