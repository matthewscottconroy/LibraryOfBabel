# Important Terms and Ideas: Coincident Objects

**The statue and the clay**: The canonical puzzle: a sculptor creates a statue from a lump of clay. The statue and the lump seem numerically distinct (they have different modal and historical properties) but are spatiotemporally co-located.

**Co-location**: Two objects being in exactly the same place at the same time. Ordinarily, distinct objects do not share a location — this is a principle of impenetrability. Coincident objects apparently violate it.

**Temporal coincidence**: The phenomenon where objects coincide only temporarily — for example, the statue and the lump coincide while the sculpture exists but may diverge before and after (if the lump existed before or survives the statue's destruction).

**Modal distinctness**: Two coincident objects are often argued to be modally distinct — they differ in what would be true of them in counterfactual situations. The statue would not survive squashing; the lump would. This modal difference grounds their numerical distinctness.

**Historical distinctness**: Coincident objects may have different origins or different endpoints. The lump came into existence before the statue; the lump may survive the statue's destruction. These historical differences are part of what makes them distinct.

**Eliminativist responses**: The strategy of denying that one (or both) of the apparently coincident objects exists. Van Inwagen denies that statues exist as things over and above organized matter; on this view, the puzzle dissolves.

**Four-dimensionalist responses**: Lewis's strategy: the statue and the lump are identical as four-dimensional worms but have some distinct temporal stages. This explains how they can be "the same" at a time without being absolutely identical.
