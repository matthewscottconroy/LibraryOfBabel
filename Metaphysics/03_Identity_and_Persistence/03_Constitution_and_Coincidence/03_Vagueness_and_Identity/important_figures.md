# Important Figures: Vagueness and Identity

**Gareth Evans** (1946–1980) published a famous short proof purporting to show that vague objects are impossible: if it were indeterminate whether a = b, one could derive a contradiction. The proof sparked enormous discussion.

**Timothy Williamson** (b. 1955) defends epistemicism: apparent vagueness is merely epistemic — there are precise facts of the matter but we are unable to know them. His book *Vagueness* (1994) is the canonical source.

**Rosanna Keefe** defends supervaluationism — the view that vague predicates have multiple precisifications, and a sentence is true (false) only if true (false) on all precisifications.

**Michael Dummett** contributed to the analysis of vagueness through his discussions of indefinite truth-values and the limits of classical logic.

**Crispin Wright** has analyzed vagueness in terms of tolerance: vague predicates tolerate small differences — a grain of sand cannot make the difference between a heap and a non-heap — leading to sorites susceptibility.
