# References and Primary Sources: Kripke on Identity

## Foundational Texts

Kripke, Saul. *Naming and Necessity*. Harvard University Press, 1980. The seminal work arguing that identity statements using rigid designators (including names) are, if true, necessarily true.

Kripke, Saul. "Identity and Necessity." In Munitz (ed.), *Identity and Individuation*. New York University Press, 1971. An earlier, more compressed presentation of the argument.

## Contemporary Works

Marcus, Ruth Barcan. "Essentialism in Modal Logic." *Noûs* 1 (1967): 90-96. An early formal argument for the necessity of identity in modal logic.

Salmon, Nathan. *Reference and Essence*. Princeton University Press, 1981. A careful development and defense of Kripke's argument, with responses to objections.

Hawthorne, John. *Metaphysical Essays*. Oxford University Press, 2006. Contains essays on the necessity of identity and its consequences.

Fine, Kit. "Essence and Modality." *Philosophical Perspectives* 8 (1994): 1-16. Argues that essential truths are more fundamental than modal truths — relevant to understanding Kripke's claims.
