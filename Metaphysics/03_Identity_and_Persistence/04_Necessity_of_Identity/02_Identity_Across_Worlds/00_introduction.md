# Identity Across Worlds

What does it mean to say that I might have been a musician? The most natural reading is that there is a possible world in which I exist — the very same individual who exists in the actual world — and in that world, I pursued a career in music rather than philosophy. Identity across possible worlds — transworld identity — is the relation that holds between an individual in the actual world and its counterpart (or its very self) in other possible worlds.

Kripke's framework for understanding modality deploys exactly this notion of transworld identity. When we ask what might have been true of Aristotle, we are asking about the very individual Aristotle — the man who taught Alexander — and what properties he could have had. There is a range of possible worlds that contain Aristotle, and in some of those worlds, Aristotle did not study philosophy. "Aristotle might not have studied philosophy" is true because there is a possible world containing Aristotle (the same Aristotle, not a mere counterpart) in which he did not study philosophy.

David Lewis challenged this picture with counterpart theory. For Lewis, possible worlds are concrete universes as real as the actual world; no individual exists in more than one world; what we call "transworld identity" is really similarity between distinct individuals in distinct worlds. When I say "I might have been a musician," I am saying that a counterpart of me — an individual in another world who is similar to me in the relevant respects — is a musician. This counterpart-theoretic account avoids the metaphysical puzzlement about how one individual can be present in multiple concrete worlds; but it requires that de re modal claims be relativized to counterpart relations, which raises its own difficulties.

The choice between genuine transworld identity and counterpart theory has implications for the analysis of essential properties, for the logic of iterated modal claims, and for the metaphysics of persons. This section examines the debate carefully, with attention to the considerations that each side marshals.
