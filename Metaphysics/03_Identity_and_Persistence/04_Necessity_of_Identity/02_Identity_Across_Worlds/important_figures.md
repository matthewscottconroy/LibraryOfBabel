# Important Figures: Identity Across Worlds

**David Lewis** (1941–2001) developed counterpart theory as an alternative to transworld identity: individuals do not exist in multiple possible worlds, but have counterparts in other worlds — things similar enough to represent possibilities for the individual.

**Saul Kripke** (b. 1940) defends transworld identity directly: we can stipulate that we are talking about the same individual in a counterfactual scenario. Possible worlds are not discovered but described.

**Alvin Plantinga** (b. 1932) defends transworld identity against counterpart theory, arguing that individuals have genuine individual essences (haecceities) that ground their identity across worlds.

**Kit Fine** (b. 1946) has analyzed essence and modal facts in ways that bear on transworld identity, arguing that essence is more fundamental than de re modality.

**David Kaplan** (b. 1933) developed the logic of demonstratives and proposes "dthat" as a way to rigidify descriptions — relevant to how we refer to individuals across possible worlds.
