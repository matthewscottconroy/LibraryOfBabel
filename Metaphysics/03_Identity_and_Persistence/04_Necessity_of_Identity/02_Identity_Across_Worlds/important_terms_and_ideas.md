# Important Terms and Ideas: Identity Across Worlds

**Counterpart theory**: Lewis's alternative to transworld identity: a statement about what an individual could have been is made true by counterparts of that individual in other possible worlds — things that resemble the individual closely enough to represent its possibilities.

**Transworld identity**: The view that one and the same individual can be a member of multiple possible worlds. Kripke: we simply stipulate that we are talking about the same individual when we describe counterfactual scenarios.

**De re modality**: Modal statements that are about specific individuals — "Necessarily, Socrates is human" makes a modal claim about Socrates himself. De re modality requires some account of transworld identity or counterparts to be evaluated.

**Individual essence (haecceity)**: A property that is uniquely and necessarily possessed by one individual — it is essential to that individual and to no other. Plantinga uses haecceities to ground transworld identity.

**Counterpart relation**: Lewis's technical notion: the relation that holds between an individual in one world and its counterpart in another. Counterparts are similar in relevant respects, but which similarity-respects are relevant can vary by context.

**Serious actualism**: The view that every property is had only by existing things — an individual cannot have properties in worlds where it does not exist. Serious actualism bears on how transworld individuals can be described.

**Possible world semantics for de re modality**: Formal semantics for statements like "Necessarily, Aristotle is a philosopher" requires specifying truth conditions at worlds with respect to Aristotle. Different semantics handle this differently (rigid designation vs. counterparts).
