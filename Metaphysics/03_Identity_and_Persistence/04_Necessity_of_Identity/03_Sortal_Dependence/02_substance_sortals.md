# Substance Sortals

*Sortals that specify the most fundamental kind an individual belongs to and its essential persistence conditions.*

---

Not all sortal concepts work the same way. Some — *phase sortals* — apply to an individual for only a period of its existence: LARVA, INFANT, PRESIDENT, PRISONER. Others — *substance sortals* — apply for the whole life of an individual: HORSE, PERSON, OAK TREE, RIVER, GOLD NUGGET. Wiggins's central claim in *Sameness and Substance* (1980) is that this distinction is not merely grammatical but metaphysically fundamental. The substance sortal of an individual specifies its most fundamental kind and thereby determines its persistence conditions — the conditions under which it continues to exist through change.

A phase sortal picks out a *phase* of an individual's existence — a period defined by certain properties or roles the individual temporarily has. An individual can gain and lose phase-sortal predicates without ceasing to exist: the caterpillar becomes a larva and then ceases to be a larva (while becoming a butterfly) but the organism persists; the man becomes President and then ceases to be President but the man persists; the person is an infant for a period and then ceases to be an infant but the person persists.

A substance sortal, by contrast, picks out the fundamental *kind* of the individual — what it essentially is. An individual cannot gain or lose a substance sortal while remaining the same individual: this horse cannot cease to be a horse while remaining this individual; if it ceases to be a horse, it ceases to exist.

## Persistence Conditions and Substance Sortals

The substance sortal of an individual determines its persistence conditions — the conditions under which it continues to exist through change. The substance sortal HORSE carries with it certain persistence conditions: a horse continues to exist so long as it remains biologically alive and continuous; it ceases to exist when it dies or is destroyed in a way that ends its biological organization.

A phase sortal like ADULT HORSE does not carry independent persistence conditions — it borrows them from the underlying substance sortal HORSE. What makes an adult horse persist is just what makes the horse persist simpliciter. This is Wiggins's principle: *each individual has a unique substance sortal that governs its persistence*. When we trace an individual through change, we apply the criteria associated with its substance sortal. Different substance sortals carry different criteria:

- **PERSON** (on psychological theories): psychological continuity and connectedness.
- **HORSE/ORGANISM**: biological continuity (continuation of life processes).
- **RIVER**: continuity of channel and hydrological function.
- **STATUE**: continuity of form and artistic/social function.
- **GOLD NUGGET**: continuity of material composition.

## The Unity of Substance Sortals with Individuals

Wiggins holds that every individual falls under *exactly one* substance sortal (its primary or fundamental kind). This generates a clean account of identity: to ask whether x at t₂ is the same individual as y at t₁, one must determine under which substance sortal x and y fall, and then apply that sortal's criteria of identity.

Apparent cases of an individual falling under multiple substance sortals are resolved by determining which sortal is the fundamental one (providing the governing persistence conditions) and treating other sortals as phase sortals or characterizing concepts that do not generate distinct identity criteria. A person is also an organism, an animal, and a rational being. Which is the substance sortal? Wiggins leans toward PERSON or HUMAN ORGANISM as the fundamental sortal, with psychological and biological criteria both being relevant aspects of the human person's persistence conditions. The animal vs. person debate — animalism vs. psychological theories — is in part a debate about which substance sortal governs human individual identity.

## Creation and Destruction

Substance sortals also determine what counts as the *creation* and *destruction* of an individual. A HORSE comes into existence when a new biological organism of the right kind is formed; it ceases to exist when that organism dies. These boundaries are fixed by the substance sortal HORSE and its associated biological criteria. Under the substance sortal PERSON, when does a person begin to exist? If PERSON involves psychological criteria, perhaps not until psychological states are first present. If PERSON is identified with HUMAN ORGANISM, perhaps at biological beginning. The sortal chosen determines the boundaries — with significant implications for the morality of abortion and the ethics of end-of-life care.

Fission cases: when a person's brain is divided, the original individual falls under PERSON with certain psychological criteria. If two individuals emerge, both falling under PERSON, the original person may cease to exist even if neither successor is identical with the original — because the individual's substance sortal PERSON cannot be instantiated by a one-many relation.

Wiggins's substance sortals are explicitly Aristotelian in inspiration. For Aristotle, a primary substance — Socrates, this horse — falls under a species (*eidos*) that specifies its nature and persistence conditions. The species is a real feature of the world, not a conceptual projection. Wiggins's substance sortals play the same role: they are real kinds, not merely human conventions, that govern the identity and persistence of the individuals that fall under them. This framework allows Wiggins to argue that questions of personal identity are questions about the persistence conditions of the substance sortal under which persons fall — which avoids both the subjectivist tendencies of psychological theories and the excessive physicalism of crude animalism.
