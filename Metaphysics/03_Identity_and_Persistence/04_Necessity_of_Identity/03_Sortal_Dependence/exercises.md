# Exercises — Necessity of Identity and Sortal Dependence

*Problems for engaging with Kripkean identity, counterpart theory, and sortals.*

---

**Exercise 1 — Necessity of Identity**
Present Kripke's argument for the necessity of identity. Evaluate the following apparent counterexample: "The Morning Star is the Evening Star" seems contingent (the two celestial bodies might not have been the same). But on Kripke's view, if they are identical, the statement is necessary. Has Kripke changed the meaning of "necessity"? Or has he shown that our pre-theoretical intuition is wrong?

**Exercise 2 — Counterpart Theory**
Lewis says "I could have been a plumber" means "my counterpart is a plumber in some world." Kripke says it means *I myself* exist as a plumber in some world. Construct a case where the two views give different verdicts. Which verdict is more intuitively correct?

**Exercise 3 — Essentiality of Origin**
Kripke argues that I could not have had different parents. Test this: could Einstein have been born in France instead of Germany? Could a table made from this oak have instead been made from a different oak of the same species? Where does the essentiality of origin end?

**Exercise 4 — Substance Sortals**
Wiggins distinguishes substance sortals (HORSE, PERSON, OAK TREE) from phase sortals (FOAL, INFANT, SAPLING). Evaluate the claim that substance sortals determine persistence conditions. Does PERSON or HUMAN ORGANISM provide the relevant substance sortal for human beings? What are the consequences for personal identity?

**Thought Experiment — Necessary A Posteriori**
Water is necessarily H₂O (if true, true in all possible worlds) even though this was discovered empirically. Construct three more examples of plausible necessary a posteriori truths (not Kripke's examples). For each, explain why it seems a posteriori despite being (allegedly) necessary. Is the notion of necessary a posteriori truth coherent, or does it rest on conflating metaphysical and epistemic modality?
