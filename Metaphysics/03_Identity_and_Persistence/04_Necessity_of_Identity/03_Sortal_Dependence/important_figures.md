# Important Figures: Sortal Dependence

**David Wiggins** (b. 1933) is the leading theorist of sortalism: his books *Identity and Spatio-Temporal Continuity* (1967) and *Sameness and Substance Renewed* (2001) argue that identity facts are sortal-relative and that persistence conditions are given by natural kinds.

**Peter Geach** (1916–2013) introduced the doctrine of relative identity: there is no absolute, sortal-independent identity, only "same F" for various sortals F. This is a more radical claim than Wiggins's sortalism.

**Eli Hirsch** (b. 1940) has used sortalism in his arguments for quantifier variance: different communities can use quantifiers governed by different sortal-relative identity conditions.

**Mark Jubien** has argued that ordinary object sortal terms do not correspond to genuine entities, pressing a deflationary view of sortal-relative identity.

**Kit Fine** has analyzed how essences and sortal kinds interact to fix the identity and existence conditions of objects.
