# Important Terms and Ideas: Sortal Dependence

**Sortalism**: The view that identity facts are sortal-relative: there is no identity tout court, only "same F" for specific sortals. Two things may be the same river but different bodies of water.

**Relative identity**: Geach's thesis that "is the same as" is not an absolute relation but always relative to a sortal: "a is the same F as b." This allows apparent contradictions (same statue, different lumps) without material coincidence.

**Absolute identity**: The standard opposing view: identity is a single, non-relative relation. "a = b" is simply true or false, not relative to any sortal.

**Persistence conditions via sortals**: Wiggins's claim that persistence conditions are given by the sortal concept an object falls under. What it takes for a river to persist is different from what it takes for a lump to persist, because they fall under different sortals.

**The NN thesis**: Wiggins's thesis that it is necessary that Aristotle is (falls under) the natural kind human being — that natural kind membership is essential. This is used to argue that sortal membership grounds modal facts about individuals.

**D-relation (criteria of individuation)**: Wiggins's formal schema: x=y iff (∃f)(x is an f and y is an f and x stands in the f-appropriate spatiotemporal continuity relation to y). The sortal f fixes the criteria.

**Anti-sortalism**: The view that identity is absolute and that sortal concepts play no constitutive role in fixing identity conditions. Objects have their identity conditions independently of how we classify them.
