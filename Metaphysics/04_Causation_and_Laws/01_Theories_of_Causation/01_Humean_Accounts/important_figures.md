# Important Figures: Humean Accounts of Causation

**David Hume** (1711–1776) gave the first systematic empiricist analysis of causation: causation is nothing more than constant conjunction plus the mind's inference from one object to another. *A Treatise of Human Nature* and *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* are the key texts.

**John Stuart Mill** (1806–1873) developed Hume's regularity view into systematic methods for discovering causes — the methods of agreement, difference, and concomitant variation. His *System of Logic* (1843) remains influential.

**J.L. Mackie** (1917–1981) analyzed singular causation using the INUS condition: a cause is an insufficient but necessary part of a sufficient but unnecessary condition. *The Cement of the Universe* (1974) is the key work.

**David Lewis** (1941–2001) developed counterfactual theories of causation, grounding causal claims in what would have happened in nearby possible worlds. His papers in *Philosophical Papers* vol. 2 are foundational.

**Ernest Sosa** and **Michael Tooley** have contributed systematic analyses of the regularity and counterfactual traditions.
