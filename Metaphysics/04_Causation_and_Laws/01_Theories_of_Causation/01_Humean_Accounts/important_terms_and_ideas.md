# Important Terms and Ideas: Humean Accounts of Causation

**Regularity theory**: The view that causation consists in nothing more than constant conjunction: c causes e iff events of type C are always followed by events of type E. There is no necessary connection in nature beyond the regularity.

**INUS condition**: Mackie's analysis: a cause is an insufficient but necessary part of a sufficient but unnecessary condition for the effect. Striking a match is INUS for a fire: striking is not sufficient (needs oxygen, dry paper) but is necessary for the specific sufficient condition it is part of.

**Counterfactual theory**: Lewis's analysis: c causes e iff if c had not occurred, e would not have occurred (where the counterfactual is evaluated at the closest possible worlds where c is absent).

**Causal chain**: A sequence of events c₁, c₂, ..., cₙ where each is a cause of the next. Lewis uses causal chains to handle transitivity and to define indirect causation.

**Preemption**: A case where two causes compete, and one pre-empts the other from producing the effect. Billy's throw breaks the window before Suzy's arrives — Billy's throw is the cause, not Suzy's, even though Suzy's would have caused the same effect.

**Overdetermination**: A case where two independent causes each sufficient to produce an effect simultaneously produce it. Neither seems to be "the" cause, and standard counterfactual analysis has trouble handling this.

**Hume's two definitions**: Hume gave two definitions of cause: (1) "A constant conjunction such that..." (objective, third-personal); (2) "An object followed by another such that the mind is carried..." (subjective, involving the mental transition). Whether these are equivalent is debated.
