# Exercises and Thought Experiments: Humean and Regularity Theories

## Comprehension Questions

1. State Hume's two definitions of causation. What is the difference between them, and why has the relationship between them been contested?

2. Explain the INUS condition analysis. Construct an original example (not from the text) of a cause that is an INUS condition and identify all four features (insufficient, necessary, unnecessary, sufficient).

3. What is the problem of accidental regularities? Why does this pose a difficulty for the regularity theory that Mill's methods alone cannot resolve?

## Discussion Questions

4. Hume claims that the impression of necessary connection is internal rather than external — it is a feeling of expectation in us rather than a feature of the world. Is this a satisfying answer? What would a dissatisfied interlocutor say?

5. Russell argued that science has no use for causation. Do you think this is right? Consider whether concepts like "gene expression causes disease" or "CO2 causes warming" are genuinely causal or merely functional/correlational.

6. Consider the claim: "The regularity theory cannot distinguish causal laws from accidental regularities, so it must be false." Evaluate this argument. Is it a knockdown objection or can the regularity theorist respond?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Preempted Assassin**: Two assassins independently decide to poison the king's wine. Assassin A acts first, and the king dies from A's poison. B's poison was ready but never reached the king. Was A the cause of the king's death? Was B? How does the regularity theory handle this?

8. **Night and Day**: Night regularly follows day. Does day cause night? Formulate the regularity theory's answer and then identify what additional condition would be needed to rule out this case as genuine causation.

9. **The Witch Doctor**: A witch doctor dances and rain follows. This has happened fifty times. According to the regularity theory, is the witch doctor causing rain? What does your answer reveal about the theory?

## Advanced Problems

10. Develop the most charitable version of the regularity theory you can. What additional resources — probabilistic, counterfactual, or structural — would you need to add to handle the standard objections? At what point does your modified theory cease to be a "regularity" theory in any meaningful sense?
