# Important Terms and Ideas: Humean and Regularity Theories

**Humean supervenience**: Lewis's thesis that all facts about the world supervene on the "Humean mosaic" — the distribution of perfectly natural intrinsic properties across spacetime points. There are no modal or causal facts over and above this distribution.

**Best System Analysis (BSA) of laws**: Lewis's view that laws are the axioms and theorems of the simplest and strongest systematization of the Humean mosaic. Laws are not basic features of reality but descriptions of patterns.

**Necessary and sufficient conditions**: In simple regularity analysis, c causes e iff c is a necessary and sufficient condition for e in the relevant circumstances. More sophisticated versions (Mill, Mackie) analyze more complex relationships.

**Mill's methods**: Five inductive methods for discovering causes: agreement (one common preceding factor), difference (what distinguishes presence from absence), joint agreement and difference, residues, and concomitant variation.

**Regularity vs. singular causation**: A challenge for regularity theories: they analyze causation in terms of general patterns, but particular causal claims ("the short circuit caused this fire") seem to be about a specific instance, not the pattern.

**The problem of spurious causation**: Regularity theories must distinguish genuine causal regularities from mere correlations. Two things can be constantly conjoined without one causing the other (e.g., both caused by a common cause).

**Determinism**: The thesis that every event is necessitated by prior events and the laws of nature. Humean regularity theories entail that determinism, if true, is a matter of the pattern of the Humean mosaic, not of any deeper metaphysical necessity.
