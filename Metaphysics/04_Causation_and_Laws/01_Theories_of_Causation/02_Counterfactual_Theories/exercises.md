# Exercises and Thought Experiments: Counterfactual Theories

## Comprehension Questions

1. State Lewis's counterfactual analysis of causation. What role do possible worlds and similarity play in evaluating causal counterfactuals?

2. Explain the problem of early preemption for the counterfactual analysis. How does Lewis's appeal to causal chains attempt to handle it, and what is the residual problem?

3. What is Woodward's key distinction between the interventionist theory and standard counterfactual analyses? What is an intervention, and why does it matter?

## Discussion Questions

4. Lewis's analysis requires a notion of world-similarity. But world-similarity seems to presuppose causal structure (worlds with the same causal laws are more similar). Does this make the analysis circular, or can similarity be specified independently?

5. Woodward's interventionism has been criticized for being unable to handle causation in closed systems (like the entire universe) where interventions are impossible. Is this a fatal objection? What might Woodward say?

6. Causal graphs are widely used in statistics and machine learning. Does their practical success give us reason to believe in the causal realism they presuppose, or are they just useful representational tools?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Double Preemption**: Both A and B shoot simultaneously at the victim. Both bullets hit. Counterfactually, if A had not shot, B's bullet would have killed the victim anyway. How does the counterfactual theory handle this case? Is A a cause of the death?

8. **The Desert Traveler**: A traveler's water supply is contaminated by enemy X and then poured out by enemy Y. The traveler dies of thirst. Who caused the death? Use the counterfactual framework to analyze this.

9. **The Absent Cause**: My failure to water the plant caused it to die. Can absences be causes? How should the counterfactual theory handle causation by omission?

## Advanced Problems

10. Design a causal graph for the following scenario: Smoking causes cancer; exercise reduces cancer risk; income causes both smoking and exercise. Draw the DAG, write structural equations, and explain how you would estimate the effect of smoking on cancer controlling for the confounding effect of income.
