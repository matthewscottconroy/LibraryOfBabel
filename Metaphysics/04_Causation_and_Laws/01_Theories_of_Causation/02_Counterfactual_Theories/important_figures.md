# Important Figures: Counterfactual Theories

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** is the central figure. His 1973 paper "Causation" set the agenda for counterfactual analyses, and his subsequent refinements in "Causation as Influence" (2000) and the posthumously published papers show the extraordinary range of his engagement with hard cases. His possible worlds framework provided the semantic machinery for the analysis.

**David Hume (1711–1776)** again: Lewis saw himself as systematizing Hume's insight that causation is not a primitive necessary connection but something constructible from patterns of events. Lewis's Humean supervenience thesis was an attempt to show that all causally relevant properties supervene on the distribution of intrinsic properties and spatiotemporal relations.

**James Woodward (b. 1946)** developed the interventionist theory in *Making Things Happen* (2003), connecting philosophical theories of causation to the practice of causal modeling in science. His work has been influential across philosophy, statistics, and cognitive science.

**Judea Pearl (b. 1936)** developed the theory of causal graphical models and the do-calculus. His book *Causality* (2000, 2nd ed. 2009) is a foundational text for the formal treatment of causation in statistics and AI.

**Peter Spirtes, Clark Glymour, and Richard Scheines** developed the algorithmic approaches to causal discovery in *Causation, Prediction, and Search* (1993), which is foundational for the field of causal inference.

**Jonathan Bennett (1930–2018)** was a close critic of Lewis's counterfactual framework, pressing objections about the context-sensitivity of similarity judgments and the backtracking problem.

**Christopher Hitchcock (b. 1965)** has worked extensively on preemption, overdetermination, and token causation, offering refinements of the counterfactual framework.
