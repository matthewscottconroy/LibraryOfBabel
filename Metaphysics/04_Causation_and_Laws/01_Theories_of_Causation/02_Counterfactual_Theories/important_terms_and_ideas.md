# Important Terms and Ideas: Counterfactual Theories

**Counterfactual conditional**: A conditional of the form "If it were the case that A, it would be the case that B" — evaluated by considering the closest possible worlds where A holds and asking whether B holds there.

**Closest possible world**: In Lewis's semantics, the world most similar to the actual world in which the antecedent of a counterfactual is true. Similarity is measured by a context-sensitive metric.

**Counterfactual dependence**: e counterfactually depends on c iff if c had not occurred, e would not have occurred. Lewis's original analysis: c causes e iff there is a chain of counterfactual dependences from c to e.

**Transitivity of causation**: The principle that if A causes B and B causes C, then A causes C. Counterfactual theories aim to capture this via causal chains.

**Late pre-emption**: A problematic case for counterfactual theories: Billy's rock reaches the bottle first; Suzy's would have, had Billy not thrown. In the actual world, e counterfactually depends on Billy's throw; but it is harder to handle when the pre-empted cause is equally close.

**Trumping**: Schaffer's counterexample to Lewis: two sorcerers cast equally powerful spells at the same moment; only the senior's spell takes effect because of a rule giving priority to seniority. The junior's spell is pre-empted, but both would have produced the effect independently.

**Interventionism**: Woodward's alternative: a cause is a variable that, if manipulated by an ideal intervention, would change the probability (or value) of the effect variable. This handles many pre-emption problems.
