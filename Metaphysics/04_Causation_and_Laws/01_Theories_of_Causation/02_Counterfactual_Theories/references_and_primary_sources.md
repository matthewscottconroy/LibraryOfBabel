# References and Primary Sources: Counterfactual Theories of Causation

## Foundational Texts

Lewis, David. "Causation." *Journal of Philosophy* 70 (1973): 556-567. The original statement of the counterfactual analysis; proposes that causation reduces to counterfactual dependence.

Lewis, David. *Counterfactuals*. Harvard University Press, 1973. The formal semantics for counterfactual conditionals using closest-worlds analysis.

## Contemporary Works

Collins, John, Hall, Ned, and Paul, L.A. (eds.). *Causation and Counterfactuals*. MIT Press, 2004. The best anthology; includes Lewis's revised account and many critical responses.

Paul, L.A. and Hall, Ned. *Causation: A User's Guide*. Oxford University Press, 2013. A comprehensive and accessible treatment of counterfactual and other theories.

Woodward, James. *Making Things Happen*. Oxford University Press, 2003. The interventionist theory: a cause is a variable whose manipulation changes the probability of the effect.

Schaffer, Jonathan. "Counterfactuals of Absences." *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 85 (2004): 149-162. Discusses the problem of absences and omissions for counterfactual theories.
