# Important Figures: Powers and Mechanisms

**C.B. Martin** (1924–2008) was the most important developer of powers ontology. His posthumous *The Mind in Nature* (2008) presents his mature view that properties are "powerful qualities" — simultaneously categorical and dispositional.

**George Molnar** (1934–1999) left the posthumously published *Powers: A Study in Metaphysics* (2003), a systematic defense of irreducibly dispositional properties and their role in causation.

**Rom Harré** (b. 1927) and **E.H. Madden** articulated a causal powers theory in *Causal Powers* (1975) drawing on realist philosophy of science.

**Stephen Mumford** (b. 1968) and **Rani Lill Anjum** have developed a powers-based account of causation: causal relations are manifestations of reciprocal powers, not mere regularities or counterfactual dependences.

**Wesley Salmon** (1925–2001) developed the mechanistic and process theory of causation, arguing that causes and effects are connected by causal processes that transmit a conserved quantity.
