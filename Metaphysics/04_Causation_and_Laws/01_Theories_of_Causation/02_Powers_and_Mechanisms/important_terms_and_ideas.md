# Important Terms and Ideas: Powers and Mechanisms

**Powers ontology**: The view that fundamental properties are essentially dispositional — they are powers directed at specific manifestations. The causal structure of the world is constituted by the interplay of powers, not by external laws imposing patterns on inert properties.

**Mutual manifestation**: Mumford and Anjum's analysis of causation: a cause and its effect are related by the mutual manifestation of complementary partners (the power to push and the susceptibility to be pushed). Causation is intrinsic to the powers involved.

**Mechanistic explanation**: The explanation of a phenomenon by identifying the mechanism — the set of entities and activities organized in such a way as to produce the phenomenon. Mechanisms operate according to causal principles involving the powers of their components.

**Causal process**: Salmon's notion: a causal process is a continuous physical process (a moving object, a light wave) that transmits a mark (an intrinsic modification) through time. Processes, not events, are the primary causal relata.

**Mark transmission**: Salmon's criterion for genuine causal processes: a process is causal if a mark introduced at one point persists throughout the process (without a separate local interaction). Non-causal pseudo-processes (shadows) cannot transmit marks.

**Conserved quantity theory**: Dowe's refinement of Salmon's account: a causal process is one that possesses a conserved physical quantity (energy, momentum). Causation involves the transfer of such quantities between processes.

**Interventionist causation**: Woodward's account: X causes Y iff an ideal intervention on X (holding all other factors fixed) changes Y. This captures the manipulability intuition central to scientific practice.
