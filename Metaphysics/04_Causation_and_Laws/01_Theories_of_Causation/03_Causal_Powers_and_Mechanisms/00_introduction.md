# Causal Powers and Mechanisms: Synthesis and Applications

The debate between Humean and non-Humean approaches to causation is not merely an academic dispute; it shapes how we understand scientific explanation, the nature of dispositional properties, and the possibility of a genuinely realist metaphysics of nature. This section examines how the powers and mechanisms approaches work together as a unified alternative to the Humean framework, and considers their applications to specific scientific and philosophical problems.

On the powers-and-mechanisms synthesis: powers ground the activities of mechanisms, and mechanisms explain how powers are realized and combined in nature. A mechanism is not a bare list of regularities; it is an organized system whose parts have powers — tendencies to behave in specific ways — and whose activities are the manifestations of those powers in the context of the mechanism's organization. The solubility of salt is a power of sodium chloride; the mechanism by which it dissolves in water involves the electromagnetic interactions between water molecules and salt ions — interactions that are themselves manifestations of powers. Understanding the mechanism gives us explanatory depth that mere regularity cannot provide.

Applications to specific problems include: (1) Mental causation — how can mental properties be causally efficacious if they are not identical to physical properties? The powers theorist can hold that mental properties are genuine powers in their own right, not merely physical powers redescribed. (2) Biological function — what is it for a trait to have a function? The mechanism-oriented view holds that a trait has a function if it participates in a mechanism that maintains or produces an important biological property. (3) Social causation — can social structures cause individual behavior? The mechanisms approach provides tools for distinguishing macro-social regularities from the micro-level mechanisms that produce them.

This section synthesizes the powers and mechanisms traditions and shows how the synthesis illuminates a range of problems in the philosophy of science and the philosophy of mind.
