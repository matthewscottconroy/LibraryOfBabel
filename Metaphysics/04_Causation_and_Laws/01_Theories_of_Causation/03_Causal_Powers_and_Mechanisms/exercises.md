# Exercises and Thought Experiments: Causal Powers and Mechanisms

## Comprehension Questions

1. What is the difference between a dispositional property and a categorical property? Give an example of each and explain why the distinction matters for the metaphysics of causation.

2. What is a finkish disposition? Explain Martin's original case and explain why it poses a problem for the simple conditional analysis of dispositions.

3. How does the mechanism theory of Machamer, Darden, and Craver differ from regularity theories? What does it mean to explain a phenomenon by identifying the mechanism responsible for it?

## Discussion Questions

4. Pure dispositionalists hold that every fundamental property is dispositional. Critics respond with the "always packing, never arriving" objection — dispositions all the way down leads to a regress with nothing categorical to ground the chain. Is this objection decisive? How might a pure dispositionalist respond?

5. Salmon distinguished causal processes from pseudo-processes by their capacity to transmit conserved quantities. Can you think of cases where this criterion gives the wrong verdict? Does the framework handle mental causation adequately?

6. Is the mechanism approach a theory of causation or a theory of causal explanation? Is there a difference?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Perfect Mask**: A fragile glass is surrounded by a perfectly responsive cushioning device that activates exactly when the glass would break, preventing breakage every time. The glass possesses the disposition to break but never manifests it. Does the glass have the property of fragility? What does your answer imply about the relationship between dispositions and conditionals?

8. **The Causal Chain Relay**: Process A transmits energy to B, which transmits energy to C, which transmits energy to D. At each step there is a genuine exchange of conserved quantities. Is each transmission a cause? Is A the cause of D's final state, or only the cause of B's state? What does the causal process theory say?

9. **The Missing Mechanism**: We observe a perfect regularity — every time condition C obtains, effect E follows — but we have no idea what mechanism connects them. Is this enough for genuine causation? What does the mechanism theorist say?

## Advanced Problems

10. Compare the powers theory and the mechanism theory as accounts of biological causation. Take as your test case: "ATP synthase causes the synthesis of ATP." Which account better captures what is happening here, and why?
