# Important Terms and Ideas: Causal Powers and Mechanisms (II)

**Directedness**: The intentional-like feature of powers: a power is directed toward its manifestation. A fragile object is "pointed at" breaking; a soluble substance is "pointed at" dissolving. This directedness makes powers more like intentional states than ordinary physical properties.

**Manifestation**: The event or state produced when a power is activated by a suitable stimulus or reciprocal partner. The manifestation of fragility is breaking; the manifestation of combustibility is burning.

**Stimulus**: The triggering condition for a power's manifestation. A power is not always manifested; it requires a suitable stimulus (e.g., striking a fragile glass) to exercise. The stimulus plus power produce the manifestation.

**Reciprocal powers**: Pairs of powers that are jointly directed at a shared manifestation. The solubility of salt and the dissolving capacity of water are reciprocal: they manifest together in the event of dissolution.

**Categorical basis**: On Humean/Armstrong views, the categorical property that underlies and "realizes" a disposition — the molecular structure that makes glass fragile. The disposition reduces to the categorical property plus the laws connecting it to the manifestation.

**Pandispositionalism**: Bird's thesis that all natural properties are irreducibly dispositional — there are no categorical properties. This has consequences for laws (they are necessary), modality (possibility is constituted by powers), and causation.

**Emergence and powers**: A question about whether higher-level powers (biological capacities, mental powers) are reducible to or emergent from lower-level physical powers.
