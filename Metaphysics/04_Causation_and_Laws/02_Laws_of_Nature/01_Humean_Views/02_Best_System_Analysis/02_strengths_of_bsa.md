# Strengths of the Best System Analysis

What does the Best System Analysis get right? The question matters because the BSA is not merely a technical proposal — it represents a sustained attempt to account for laws of nature without departing from a Humean ontology of local, intrinsic, contingent facts. If it succeeds, it vindicates the program of doing serious metaphysics without postulating modally loaded primitives. The virtues of the account are real, and understanding them is necessary for evaluating whether the objections are decisive.

## Distinguishing Laws from Accidents

The BSA provides a principled distinction between laws and accidental regularities, which was precisely what the naive regularity theory lacked. The accidental regularity "all coins in my pocket are silver" does not appear in the best system because it contributes negligible strength — it covers only coins in one person's pocket — at a cost in specificity that reduces the system's simplicity. "All copper conducts electricity" does appear because including it contributes substantial strength efficiently, and it connects to further laws about conductivity, band structure, and quantum mechanics.

The uranium/gold asymmetry that bedeviled the naive regularity theory is handled directly. "All gold spheres are less than one mile in diameter" probably does not appear in the best system — there is no simple theoretical reason why gold spheres must be small; the fact holds contingently due to scarcity and the practical difficulty of assembly, and including it separately would add nothing to the system's theoretical power. "All uranium spheres are less than one mile in diameter" does appear in the best system, because nuclear physics provides the simple theoretical grounding: any uranium sphere larger than critical mass would self-detonate. The generalization earns its place because it is a consequence of nuclear physics, which is itself in the best system. The BSA gives the right verdict on both cases, where the naive regularity theory gave the wrong verdict on both.

## Alignment of Scientific Methodology and Metaphysics

The BSA captures something that competing accounts struggle to explain: why scientists care about simplicity and unification in identifying laws. On accounts that make laws into primitive necessitation relations or essential dispositional properties, the scientific preference for simple, unified theories is merely epistemic — we prefer simple theories because they are easier to use, or because simplicity is a guide to truth. But this leaves unexplained why simplicity is a guide to what laws are. On the BSA, simplicity is built into what laws are. A simpler system that achieves equal strength is genuinely a better system; its generalizations are better candidates for laws. The methodological preference for simplicity is not merely a heuristic but a direct response to what laws are.

This gives the BSA a non-accidental alignment between scientific practice and metaphysics. Scientists are not merely finding convenient summaries of data; they are finding the correct laws, and the correct laws just are those that appear in the optimal systematization. The epistemic and metaphysical come apart only in the derivative sense that our best current theory may not yet be the ideal best system.

## Probabilistic Laws

Lewis's extension of the BSA to include probabilistic laws is one of its most significant achievements. In the actual world, if quantum mechanics is correct, laws assign probabilities to outcomes rather than determining them. The BSA accommodates this by adding fit as a third theoretical virtue: how well the system's probability assignments match the actual pattern of outcomes. The best system for an indeterministic world balances simplicity, strength, and fit; probabilistic laws are the chance-assigning generalizations in this optimal system.

Non-Humean accounts face genuine difficulty here. The Armstrong-Dretske-Tooley necessitation framework is most naturally suited to deterministic laws where N(F, G) guarantees that all F's are G's. Extending this to probabilistic laws requires a probabilistic necessitation relation — perhaps N(F, G, p) meaning F necessitates G with probability p — whose nature is far from transparent. The BSA handles the probabilistic case by the same mechanism as the deterministic case, which is a structural advantage.

## Modal Humility and Empirical Discovery

The BSA embodies appropriate modal humility: laws of nature are contingent. Different distributions of particular facts would yield different best systems and thus different laws. Laws are discovered empirically because they are determined by the actual mosaic, which we learn about through observation. If laws were metaphysically necessary — as some dispositional essentialist accounts hold — it would be mysterious why we need observation at all; we should be able to reason to them a priori. The BSA coheres with the scientific practice of empirical discovery in a way that strongly necessitarian accounts do not.

## Uninstantiated Generalizations

Unlike the naive regularity theory, the BSA can in principle include generalizations with no actual instances. If a generalization "all particles of type X behave thus" appears in a simple and informative theory, it may be a law even if no particle of type X has ever been observed. The criterion is theoretical role, not actual instantiation. This gives the BSA a genuine advantage over its naive predecessor: it handles the problem of uninstantiated laws without postulating Armstrongian universals that exist independent of their instances.

Recent work by Loewer, Albert, and others has extended the BSA to quantum mechanics through the "mentaculus" framework, in which the laws of statistical mechanics — including probabilistic laws about thermodynamic behavior — appear in the best system as part of the fundamental physical description. Bohmian mechanics and GRW collapse theories are themselves candidates for the best-system description of quantum reality. This suggests the BSA is scientifically viable in ways that matter: it can accommodate the probabilistic, holistic, quantum-mechanical character of the actual world while maintaining Humean principles. Whether it can fully succeed is contested; but the fact that serious engagement with quantum physics is possible within the BSA framework is a genuine point in its favor.
