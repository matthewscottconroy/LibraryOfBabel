# Laws and Counterfactuals

One of the most important tests for any account of laws is how it handles counterfactuals. "If this rod were copper, it would conduct electricity" is licensed by the law connecting copper to conductivity. "If this coin were in my pocket, it would be silver" is not licensed by the accidental generalization that all my coins are silver. Laws support counterfactuals; accidents do not. This asymmetry is not incidental — it is central to why we care about laws in the first place. Understanding how laws and counterfactuals are related, and which is prior to which, is one of the more difficult questions in the metaphysics of laws.

## The Counterfactual Test and Lewis's Account

The counterfactual test is widely used to distinguish laws from accidents: L is a law only if L supports the corresponding counterfactual, while an accidental generalization A does not support its corresponding counterfactual. This captures an important asymmetry. Laws range over the real nature of things and constrain what those things would do in counterfactual circumstances; accidents describe contingent facts that need not hold in nearby possible scenarios.

Lewis's counterfactual semantics makes the connection precise. "If C had not occurred, E would not have occurred" is true at world W iff at the nearest W-accessible world where ¬C, we also have ¬E. The "nearest" worlds are assessed by a similarity metric that gives priority, in order, to: avoiding widespread violations of the actual laws; maximizing the spatiotemporal region of exact match in particular matters of fact; and avoiding small local violations of the actual laws. Laws play an essential role in evaluating counterfactuals: the nearby counterfactual worlds are those that share the actual laws with only minor local violations and differ from actuality in the antecedent.

On this account, laws ground counterfactuals. We first identify the laws as the theorems of the best system, and then use those laws to evaluate what happens in nearby possible worlds. The laws are determined by the mosaic independently of any counterfactual assessment; counterfactuals are then derived from the laws together with similarity considerations. This is the natural Lewisian ordering.

## The Priority Question

But the ordering raises a deep question. Are laws genuinely prior to counterfactuals, or does counterfactual support partly constitute what makes something a law?

The worry about circularity is real. The BSA identifies laws as the theorems of the best system, where "best" is assessed partly by simplicity and strength. Assessing simplicity requires knowing which properties are natural. The naturalness of properties is connected to their causal roles. Causal roles are partly determined by counterfactuals — what a property would do under various interventions. And counterfactuals depend on laws. The circle is not obviously vicious — Lewis maintained it was virtuous — but it is undeniable.

The alternative ordering gives counterfactuals priority. We know what would happen in various counterfactual situations through manipulation, experiment, and inference; from this knowledge we identify the laws as the generalizations that underlie the counterfactuals. On this view, laws are not the independent grounding for counterfactuals but are abstracted from counterfactual knowledge. This is epistemologically natural — it describes how we actually discover laws — but it threatens to make laws constitutively dependent on counterfactuals, rather than the other way round.

A third position, developed by Lange, holds that neither is prior. Laws are those generalizations whose members are stable — each would remain true under any counterfactual supposition consistent with the set as a whole. "All copper conducts" remains true under counterfactual suppositions about individual copper samples, unusual conditions, and time reversal, because it is a law. "All my coins are silver" would be false under the supposition that I added a gold coin, because it is merely accidental. Laws and counterfactuals are part of a holistic network of modal concepts, none of which is fully prior to the others; nomological stability is characterized by the network, not derived from any single base.

## Non-Humean Accounts and the Priority of Laws

Non-Humean accounts can give a more direct answer to the priority question. Laws have genuine modal force — they are not merely patterns but necessities — and this modal force is precisely what makes them support counterfactuals.

On the Armstrong-Dretske-Tooley account, the law N(F, G) says that the universal F stands in a necessitation relation to G. This necessary connection holds in all worlds where the relevant universals exist with the same relationship. Therefore, in the nearest possible world where something is copper, it conducts — because the same universals standing in the same necessitation relation require it. The counterfactual is supported directly by the modal force of the law.

On dispositional essentialism (Bird), laws follow from the essential natures of properties. The law that copper conducts holds in all worlds where the properties constituting copper's nature exist. In any counterfactual scenario where something genuinely is copper — has the same essential properties — it conducts. The counterfactual support comes from the essential nature of the properties. Both accounts give priority to the laws over the counterfactuals: laws support counterfactuals because they have modal force that extends beyond the actual world.

## Epistemology and Methodology

Epistemologically, laws and counterfactuals are discovered together: learning what would happen if we intervened is learning about laws, and learning about laws gives us counterfactual knowledge. This mutual interdependence in epistemology need not reflect vicious circularity in metaphysics. Woodward's interventionism makes this explicit: we discover causal laws by conducting interventions — manipulating variables and observing outcomes. Interventions are a form of counterfactual testing. The laws discovered are precisely the generalizations invariant under interventions, which is another way of saying: the generalizations that support intervention counterfactuals. The epistemological interdependence is a feature of good scientific methodology; the metaphysical question of which grounds which is separate and remains contested.
