# Important Figures: Humean Views of Laws

**David Hume** (1711–1776) inaugurated the Humean tradition by denying necessary connections in nature. Laws, on his view, are simply descriptions of regularities — there is nothing more to being a law than being part of the Humean mosaic.

**John Stuart Mill** (1806–1873) developed a systematic empiricist account of laws as the most general regularities derivable from a comprehensive systematization of nature — a precursor to the Best System Analysis.

**David Lewis** (1941–2001) gave the most sophisticated Humean account in his Best System Analysis: laws are the axioms of the simplest and strongest systematization of the Humean mosaic. *Philosophical Papers* vol. 2 contains his key papers.

**Frank Ramsey** (1903–1930) anticipated the Best System Analysis in his posthumous paper "General Propositions and Causality," arguing that laws are distinguished regularities within an ideal systematic account.

**Barry Loewer** (b. 1947) has developed and defended the Best System Analysis in response to non-Humean critics.
