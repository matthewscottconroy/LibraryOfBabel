# Important Terms and Ideas: Humean Views of Laws

**Best System Analysis (BSA)**: Lewis's account: a generalization is a law iff it is an axiom or theorem of the best system — the systematization of all particular facts that achieves the best balance of simplicity and informativeness (strength).

**Humean mosaic**: Lewis's image of the world as a vast arrangement of local, intrinsic, non-modal facts: particular properties at particular spacetime points, with no necessary connections or laws as fundamental entities.

**Regularity**: A universal generalization that is true but has no modal force. On Humean views, laws are distinguished regularities — those that feature in the best systematization — not regularities with any further metaphysical status.

**Systematization**: The organization of all particular facts into a coherent system, ideally with few axioms (simplicity) and with many facts deducible from those axioms (strength).

**Accidental generalization**: A generalization that happens to be true but is not a law — e.g., "All the coins in my pocket are copper" (if that happens to be true). Laws support counterfactuals; accidental generalizations do not.

**Governing vs. describing**: A key question about laws: do laws govern what happens (metaphysically constrain events) or merely describe what happens? Humeans favor the describing view; non-Humeans favor the governing view.

**Ceteris paribus laws**: Laws qualified with "other things being equal" — they hold unless disturbed by interfering factors. How such laws are compatible with Humean supervenience, and whether they are genuine laws, is contested.
