# Important Figures: Regularity and Necessity in Laws of Nature

## David Hume (1711–1776)
The source of the regularity view: causation consists in regular succession—constant conjunction—not in any necessary connection between events. Laws of nature are summaries of observed regularities, not necessary connections. All "necessity" in nature is projected by the mind.

## David Lewis (1941–2001)
Developed the most sophisticated version of Humean regularity theory—the Best Systems Account (BSA): laws are the axioms of the strongest, simplest systematization of the Humean mosaic. Lewis's account is the standard Humean view in contemporary philosophy of science.

## Frank Ramsey (1903–1930)
Anticipated the Best Systems Account: laws are the generalizations that feature in an ideally simple and complete deductive system for organizing our knowledge of the world. The Ramsey-Lewis account is named for both.

## John Carroll (born 1959)
A persistent critic of Humean regularity theories; argues that no regularity account can adequately distinguish laws from accidental generalizations, and defends a primitivist view of laws as fundamental features of reality.
