# Important Terms and Ideas: Regularity and Necessity in Laws

**Necessitation relation (N)**: Armstrong's account: a law "All Fs are G" is grounded in a higher-order relation N(F, G) — a necessitation relation holding between universals F and G. The law is not merely a regularity but a modal fact.

**Dretske-Tooley-Armstrong view**: The shared thesis that laws are relations between universals, not regularities among particulars. The law relates universals F and G; particular instances fall under the law by instantiating F and G.

**Nomic necessity**: The kind of necessity attaching to laws: nomologically necessary truths hold in all physically possible worlds (worlds with the same laws) but not in all metaphysically possible worlds.

**Logical strength and metaphysical strength**: A law can have logical strength (generating many particular truths) without being metaphysically necessary. The question of whether laws are metaphysically necessary or only nomologically necessary is central.

**Primitivism about laws**: The view (Carroll, Maudlin) that laws are fundamental, irreducible facts about the world — they cannot be reduced to Humean regularities, universals-necessitation, or any other more basic ontological category.

**Psillos's dilemma for Armstrong**: If N(F, G) necessitates that all Fs are Gs, this seems to require a further principle — but any further principle seems to be another law. Either the necessitation is brute or it requires an infinite regress.

**Nomological possibility**: What is possible given the actual laws of nature. A world is nomologically possible if the same laws hold there; it may be metaphysically possible but nomologically impossible (e.g., a world without gravity).
