# Exercises and Thought Experiments: Armstrong and Necessitarianism

## Comprehension Questions

1. State Armstrong's DTA account of laws. What is the necessitation relation N, and how does it connect to first-order regularities?

2. Explain the inference problem. Why does Lewis think Armstrong's account faces a deep explanatory gap?

3. How does scientific essentialism (Ellis, Bird) differ from Armstrong's necessitation account? What is the role of dispositional essences?

## Discussion Questions

4. Armstrong defends immanent realism about universals, which means universals exist only if instantiated. Does this create problems for his account of laws — for example, laws governing particles of a type that never actually exists?

5. Scientific essentialism makes laws metaphysically necessary: the laws could not have been different given the nature of the properties. Is this claim empirically testable? What would count as evidence for or against it?

6. Lewis's Best System Analysis is designed to vindicate Humean supervenience: everything supervenes on the distribution of local qualitative facts. If Armstrong is right about necessitation relations among universals, does Humean supervenience fail? What are the implications?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Swapped World**: Imagine a world physically indistinguishable from ours (the same distribution of events) but where the necessitation relation N holds between different pairs of universals. Lewis says there is no such possible world — Humean supervenience entails that if the events are the same, the laws are the same. Armstrong disagrees. Who is right, and what hangs on the answer?

8. **The Alien Laws**: Could there be a possible world where a law of the form "All F are G" holds, even though no F's ever exist in that world? Armstrong's account makes this difficult (since universals are immanent). Does this count against his view?

9. **The Essential Charge**: On scientific essentialism, it is essential to an electron that it has negative charge. Could there be a world with a particle exactly like an electron in every way except that it has positive charge? Is it still an electron? What does your answer reveal about the essentialist view?

## Advanced Problems

10. Compare and contrast three accounts of laws: the Humean BSA, Armstrong's necessitation account, and Ellis's scientific essentialism. For each, identify (a) what laws are, (b) how they differ from accidental regularities, (c) whether they hold necessarily, and (d) the main objection. Which account do you find most defensible, and why?
