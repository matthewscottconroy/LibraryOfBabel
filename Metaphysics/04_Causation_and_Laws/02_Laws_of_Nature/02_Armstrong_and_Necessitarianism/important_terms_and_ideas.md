# Important Terms and Ideas: Armstrong and Necessitarianism

**Armstrong's necessitation**: The law "All Fs are G" is not a universal generalization but the obtaining of a higher-order state of affairs: the universals F and G standing in the necessitation relation N. When N(F,G) obtains, any particular that is F must be G.

**Tooley's uninstantiated universals**: Tooley noted that Armstrong's view requires universals to necessitate each other even if uninstantiated. This may require accepting uninstantiated universals, which Armstrong resists — a tension in the view.

**Van Fraassen's identification problem**: If laws are relations between universals, how do we identify which relations are the law-making relations? Van Fraassen argues no non-circular answer is available.

**Inference from the particular**: Armstrong's claim that the necessitation relation N(F,G) entails the corresponding regularity (all Fs are Gs) — the inference from the universal to the particular. This inference is itself a nomological fact.

**Essentialism and necessity**: Bird's version of necessitarianism grounds necessity in the essential nature of properties, not in external relations between them. Properties essentially have their powers; the laws follow from what the properties are.

**Eliminativism about laws (Mumford)**: The view that laws as a distinct ontological category are redundant — the work done by laws can be done entirely by the intrinsic powers of objects. Laws are not needed as a separate fundamental category.

**Induction and laws**: If laws are necessitation relations between universals rather than mere regularities, this may provide a non-Humean ground for inductive inference: we infer the necessity, not just the regularity.
