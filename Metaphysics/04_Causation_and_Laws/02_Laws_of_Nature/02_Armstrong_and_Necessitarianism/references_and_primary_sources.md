# References and Primary Sources: Armstrong and Necessitarianism

## Foundational Texts

Armstrong, D.M. *What Is a Law of Nature?* Cambridge University Press, 1983. Armstrong's systematic necessitation theory: laws are higher-order relations N(F,G) between universals.

Armstrong, D.M. *Universals: An Opinionated Introduction*. Westview Press, 1989. Contains the relevant material on universals and their role in laws.

## Contemporary Works

Bird, Alexander. *Nature's Metaphysics*. Oxford University Press, 2007. Extends Armstrong's necessitarianism in a dispositionalist direction: necessity flows from the essences of properties.

Ellis, Brian. *Scientific Essentialism*. Cambridge University Press, 2001. An alternative necessitarianism based on essential powers rather than relations between universals.

Mumford, Stephen. *Laws in Nature*. Routledge, 2004. Argues that laws are not needed as a separate ontological category — powers and dispositions do the relevant work.

van Fraassen, Bas. *Laws and Symmetry*. Oxford University Press, 1989. A thorough critique of both Humean and necessitarian accounts of laws.
