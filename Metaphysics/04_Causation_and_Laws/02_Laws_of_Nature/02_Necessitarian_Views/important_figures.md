# Important Figures: Necessitarian Views of Laws

## David Armstrong (1926–2014)
The leading defender of the necessitarian view: laws of nature are second-order relations of nomic necessitation between universals (N(F,G)). The law that Fs are G consists in the universal F's standing in the necessitation relation to the universal G.

## Michael Tooley (born 1941)
Independently developed a necessitarian account of laws as irreducible relations between universals; his version differs from Armstrong's in placing laws in a Platonic realm of uninstantiated universals.

## Fred Dretske (1932–2013)
Also independently developed a necessitarian view: laws are singular statements about relations between properties (universals), not generalizations about individuals. The law-statement "Salt dissolves in water" states a relation between the property salthood and the property solubility-in-water.

## Brian Ellis (born 1929)
Developed scientific essentialism as an alternative to both regularity and necessitation views: natural kinds have essential dispositional properties; laws of nature flow necessarily from these essences. Necessity in laws comes from the essences of properties, not from a brute necessitation relation.
