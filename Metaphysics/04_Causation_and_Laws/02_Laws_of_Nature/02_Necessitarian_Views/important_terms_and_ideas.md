# Important Terms and Ideas: Necessitarian Views of Laws

**Natural necessity**: A mode of necessity intermediate between logical/mathematical necessity (holding in all possible worlds) and mere actuality. Laws of nature are naturally necessary: they hold in all physically possible worlds.

**Metaphysical necessitation by essences**: The view that the laws of nature are metaphysically necessary — not just nomologically necessary — because they flow from the essential natures of natural properties. Water must be H₂O; electrons must repel like charges.

**Dispositional essentialism (Bird)**: All fundamental natural properties are dispositional; their identities are constituted by their causal roles. The laws are just descriptions of what the properties necessarily do — their essences.

**Quidditism revisited**: The anti-essentialist view that properties have primitive identities ("quiddities") independent of their causal roles — and so the laws could have been different while the properties remained the same. Necessitarians deny this.

**Causal structuralism**: Properties are individuated by and constituted by their structural-causal roles — the web of causal relations they participate in. On this view, swapping the causal roles of charge and mass across possible worlds would just be swapping the properties, not giving them different laws.

**Natural vs. logical possibility**: For the necessitarian, some scenarios that are logically coherent (electrons that repel unlike charges) are not genuinely possible, because they violate the essential natures of the properties involved.

**Laws as descriptions vs. prescriptions**: On regularity views, laws describe; on necessitarian views, laws prescribe (or, rather, they reflect the metaphysical necessity of what happens). The prescriptive view is associated with the folk conception of laws as governing.
