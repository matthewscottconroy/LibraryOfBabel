# Exercises and Thought Experiments: Probabilistic Causation

## Comprehension Questions

1. What is the probability-raising account of causation? State it formally and explain its motivation.

2. Explain the problem of probability-lowering causes. Construct an original example and explain why it poses a challenge for the probability-raising account.

3. What is Reichenbach's principle of the common cause, and how does it underpin Suppes's screening-off condition?

## Discussion Questions

4. RCTs establish population-level causal effects. Can they establish token causal claims — that this particular patient was cured by this particular drug? What would be needed to establish token causation?

5. Bradford Hill's criteria for causal inference are widely used in epidemiology, but philosophers have noted that they cannot establish causation in a single study. What is the right framework for thinking about causal evidence accumulation?

6. Nancy Cartwright argues that probabilistic causation ultimately requires appeal to capacities or powers. Is she right? Or can we give a purely probabilistic account without invoking capacities?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Probability Paradox**: Suppose that taking aspirin raises the probability of surviving a heart attack from 50% to 70%. A patient takes aspirin and survives. Did the aspirin cause the patient's survival? What if a patient takes aspirin and still dies — did the aspirin cause the death?

8. **The Preempted Rain**: A drought-prone region has a 90% chance of no rain. A cloud-seeder intervenes, raising the probability of rain to 50%. It rains. Did the cloud-seeding cause the rain? Would your answer change if the probability had been raised only to 10%?

9. **The Quantum Trigger**: An atom has a 50% probability of decaying in one second. A single click of a Geiger counter triggers a mechanism that kills a cat. The atom decays; the cat dies. Was the atomic decay the cause of the cat's death? What does this say about causation in a genuinely indeterministic world?
