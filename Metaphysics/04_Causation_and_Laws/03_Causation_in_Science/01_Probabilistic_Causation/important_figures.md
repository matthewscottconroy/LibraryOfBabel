# Important Figures: Probabilistic Causation

**Hans Reichenbach (1891–1953)** introduced the principle of the common cause and the concept of screening off, which became foundational for probabilistic theories of causation. His *The Direction of Time* (1956) contains the key ideas.

**Patrick Suppes (1922–2014)** gave the first systematic probabilistic theory of causation in *A Probabilistic Theory of Causality* (1970). His formal framework using probability-raising and screening off influenced subsequent work.

**Wesley Salmon (1925–2001)** moved from a statistical relevance model of explanation (connected to Suppes's probabilistic causation) to a causal process account, recognizing that probability-raising alone is insufficient. *Statistical Explanation and Statistical Relevance* (1971) and *Scientific Explanation and the Causal Structure of the World* (1984) chart this progression.

**Judea Pearl (b. 1936)** developed the most comprehensive framework for probabilistic causal inference, including the do-calculus and counterfactual analysis within causal graphs. *Causality* (2000) is essential.

**Christopher Hitchcock (b. 1965)** has written extensively on token causation in probabilistic contexts, probability-lowering causes, and the distinction between actual and generic causation.

**Bradford Hill (1897–1991)** established the epidemiological criteria for causal inference from observational data. His 1965 paper "The Environment and Disease: Association or Causation?" remains a standard reference.

**Nancy Cartwright (b. 1944)** has argued that probabilistic causation requires appeal to capacities (a form of causal power) rather than regularities. Her *Nature's Capacities and Their Measurement* (1989) is an important alternative perspective.
