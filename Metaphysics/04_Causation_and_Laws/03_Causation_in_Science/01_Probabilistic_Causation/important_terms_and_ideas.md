# Important Terms and Ideas: Probabilistic Causation

**Probabilistic causation**: The thesis that causes raise the probability of their effects — c causes e iff P(e|c) > P(e). A reformulation of causation appropriate for indeterministic settings where causes do not necessitate effects.

**Positive correlation**: A correlation where the probability of e given c is greater than the probability of e given not-c. Probabilistic causal theories require positive correlation, but positive correlation alone is insufficient for causation.

**Screening off**: A causal criterion: c screens off b from e if P(e|c,b) = P(e|c). Screening off relations help identify genuine causes versus spurious correlates caused by a common cause.

**Common cause principle (Reichenbach)**: If A and B are correlated, either A causes B, B causes A, or there is a common cause C that produces both. If C screens off A from B, C is the common cause.

**Probabilistic relevance**: Salmon's condition: c is causally relevant to e iff c is positively relevant (probability-raising) to e, after controlling for confounders.

**Deterministic vs. probabilistic laws**: Deterministic laws guarantee that given the initial conditions and laws, the outcome is fixed. Probabilistic laws specify the probability of an outcome given the initial conditions. Quantum mechanics is the paradigm of irreducibly probabilistic laws.

**Objective single-case probability**: The probability that a specific individual event of type C will produce an event of type E — not a frequency but a disposition of the individual. Frequentism and propensity theories analyze this differently.
