# Exercises and Thought Experiments: Causal Explanation

## Comprehension Questions

1. State the DN model's four conditions for an adequate scientific explanation. Give an example of an explanation that satisfies all four and one that fails at least one.

2. Explain Kitcher's unification account of explanation. What is an "argument pattern," and how does the use of argument patterns produce explanatory unification?

3. What is Fodor's multiple realizability argument, and how does it support the autonomy of the special sciences?

## Discussion Questions

4. The DN model is sometimes said to "run in the wrong direction": we can derive the height of a flagpole from the shadow, but the shadow does not explain the height. What does this reveal about the adequacy of the DN model? How do alternative accounts handle this asymmetry?

5. Kim argues that higher-level causes are either identical to lower-level causes (reducible) or causally inert (epiphenomenal). Can a non-reductive physicalist escape this dilemma? What additional resources are needed?

6. Woodward argues that to explain an event is to show how it would change under interventions. Does this capture all cases of scientific explanation, including historical explanations and constitutive explanations?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Flagpole and the Shadow**: The shadow of a flagpole can be used to calculate the height of the pole. Does the shadow explain the height? Does the height explain the shadow? Use the DN model, the unification account, and the mechanistic account to answer, and compare the results.

8. **The Temperature and Solubility**: Sugar dissolves in water because it is soluble. Solubility is explained by the molecular structure of sugar. Which of these is the "real" explanation? Is the molecular explanation always more explanatory than the higher-level chemical explanation?

9. **The Functional Explanation**: A heart pumps blood. Is "pumping blood" an explanation of the heart's function? Is this a causal explanation, a teleological explanation, or both?

## Advanced Problems

10. Design an experiment that would test the hypothesis "Stress causes cardiovascular disease." Specify the study design, the causal model you are testing, the confounds you would need to control, and the inference you would draw if the hypothesis is supported.
