# Important Figures: Causal Explanation

**Carl Hempel (1905–1997)** developed the DN model of explanation, which set the agenda for philosophical discussions of scientific explanation for decades. His collected essays in *Aspects of Scientific Explanation* (1965) are foundational.

**Michael Friedman (b. 1947)** proposed the unification account of explanation in "Explanation and Scientific Understanding" (1974).

**Philip Kitcher (b. 1947)** elaborated the unification account in terms of argument patterns in "Explanatory Unification and the Causal Structure of the World" (1989).

**Wesley Salmon (1925–2001)** developed the statistical relevance model and then the causal process model as alternatives to the DN account. *Scientific Explanation and the Causal Structure of the World* (1984) is essential.

**Jerry Fodor (1935–2017)** argued for the autonomy of the special sciences in "Special Sciences" (1974), grounding this in multiple realizability. His work connects philosophy of science to philosophy of mind.

**Jaegwon Kim (1934–2019)** developed the causal exclusion problem and argued against non-reductive physicalism in *Supervenience and Mind* (1993) and *Mind in a Physical World* (1998).

**Carl Craver (b. 1967)** has applied the mechanistic account of explanation to neuroscience in *Explaining the Brain* (2007), distinguishing etiological from constitutive explanations.

**James Woodward (b. 1946)** developed the manipulationist account of explanation: to explain is to show how the phenomenon would change under interventions. *Making Things Happen* (2003) is the key text.
