# Important Terms and Ideas: Causal Explanation

**Deductive-Nomological (D-N) model**: Hempel's account: an explanation is a valid deductive argument whose premises include at least one law of nature (nomic premise) and statements of initial conditions, with the explanandum as conclusion.

**Inductive-Statistical (I-S) model**: Hempel's probabilistic extension: an explanation of a particular event cites a law of the form "Pr(B|A) = r" and states that A occurred, allowing a high-probability induction to B.

**Explanation vs. prediction**: The D-N model makes explanation and prediction symmetric: the same argument that explains an event could (if known in advance) have predicted it. Critics argue explanation and prediction can come apart.

**Why-questions and explanatory contrast**: Explanations are always relative to a contrast class. "Why did x die of this disease?" has different answers depending on the contrast: why rather than surviving? why this disease rather than another?

**Mechanistic explanation**: Explanation by identifying the mechanism responsible for a phenomenon — the organized set of entities and activities that, together, produce the effect. Standard in biology and neuroscience.

**Covering law**: A general law that "covers" the case being explained — under which the particular event falls. The D-N model requires at least one covering law in every explanation.

**Kairetic account (Strevens)**: A causal explanation is complete when it specifies only causally relevant differences — factors whose variation would make a difference to the outcome — abstracting away causally irrelevant details.

**Explanatory depth**: The degree to which an explanation tracks the deep causal structure of the world rather than surface regularities. A deeper explanation subsumes more phenomena under fewer general principles.
