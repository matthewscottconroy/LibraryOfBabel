# What Are Dispositions?

Salt is soluble: it has a disposition to dissolve when placed in water. Glass is fragile: it has a disposition to shatter when struck. These dispositional properties are ubiquitous in our description of the natural world, yet they have a peculiar character that has puzzled philosophers since the concept was first examined systematically. A disposition is "multi-track" in the sense that it would manifest differently in different stimulus conditions; it can exist even when it is not manifesting (the salt is soluble even when it is not dissolving); and it is inherently connected to a counterfactual conditional (if the salt were placed in water, it would dissolve).

This counterfactual connection is what makes dispositions philosophically interesting and philosophically troublesome. On a pure Humean view, there are no real dispositions in the world — there are only actual events and regularities. What we call a disposition is a predicate that we apply to things on the basis of a regularity: "soluble" applies to x if x, whenever placed in water, dissolves. The disposition does not name a real property over and above the regularity.

The anti-Humean — the powers theorist — holds that dispositions are real intrinsic properties of things, not mere regularities or supervenient patterns. Salt has a real property of solubility that consists in its molecular structure — the way its ions are bound and their tendency to separate in the presence of polar solvents. This property is causally responsible for the dissolving events; the regularity is not the disposition but its manifestation. Real dispositions ground counterfactuals: the salt would dissolve in water because it has the disposition, not merely because there is a regularity.

Two further puzzles are the "fink" and "mask" cases. A fink is a device that removes the disposition precisely when the stimulus condition is met: a fragile glass protected by a spell that makes it hard whenever it would be struck. The glass has the disposition (it is intrinsically fragile) but will not manifest it. A mask prevents manifestation without removing the disposition. These cases show that the simple counterfactual analysis of dispositions fails and require a more sophisticated account of the disposition-manifestation relationship.
