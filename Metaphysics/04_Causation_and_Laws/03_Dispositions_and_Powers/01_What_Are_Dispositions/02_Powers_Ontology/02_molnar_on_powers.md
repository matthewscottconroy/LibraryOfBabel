# Molnar on Powers

George Molnar's posthumous *Powers: A Study in Metaphysics* (2003), edited by Stephen Mumford, offers one of the most careful and systematic defenses of the view that irreducible causal powers are genuine features of the world. Molnar does not merely assert this — he develops a positive ontology of what powers are, how they are individuated, and why accepting them is both scientifically well-motivated and philosophically coherent.

## The Challenge to Humean Orthodoxy

Molnar's starting point is the Humean picture's hidden cost. If all fundamental properties are categorical — lacking any intrinsic dispositional character — then laws of nature must be posited as additional facts that connect properties to their effects. The property of charge, on the Humean view, has no intrinsic connection to repulsion; it is connected to repulsion only because a law says so. But why does the law hold? On the Humean account, this is a brute fact: the law is a regularity in the Humean mosaic, and there is no further explanation of why it holds. The regularity is just there.

Powers ontology avoids this by building causal roles into the properties themselves. The power of charge to repel like charges is not an external connection imposed by a law; it is what charge is. The law "like charges repel" is not an additional fact about the world; it is a consequence of the essential nature of charge. Compare the two explanations:

- **Categorical + law**: Charged objects have categorical property P, and there is a law that objects with P repel like-charged objects. Why does the law hold? Brute fact.
- **Pure powers**: Charge is constitutively the power to repel like charges. The repulsion is an expression of what charge essentially is. No further "why" is available or needed.

Molnar argued that the pure powers account is explanatorily superior. Both accounts bottom out somewhere; the question is where. For the Humean, it bottoms out in unexplained laws. For Molnar, it bottoms out in the essential natures of properties — and this is a genuine advance, because the essential nature of a property gives an internal explanation of why it behaves as it does.

## Physical Intentionality

The distinctive philosophical contribution of Molnar's account is his analysis of physical intentionality. He identifies four features that powers share with mental intentional states:

1. **Directedness**: Both beliefs and powers are directed toward objects or states — beliefs toward their contents, powers toward their manifestations.
2. **Independence from existence of the object**: A belief can be about a non-existent state; a power can be directed toward a manifestation that never occurs. The fragile glass's fragility is real even if the glass is never struck.
3. **Independence from actuality**: The belief need not be true for the belief to exist; the manifestation need not occur for the power to exist.
4. **Intrinsicality**: The directedness is a feature of the power itself, not derived from its relations to other things. This distinguishes powers from mere dispositions-as-regularities: the power is intrinsic; the regularity is relational.

Powers differ from mental intentionality in important ways: they lack semantic content, are not subject to norms of correctness, and cannot be satisfied or frustrated in the way beliefs can be false. Physical intentionality is a simpler, more basic form of directedness. But Molnar's key claim is that acknowledging this simpler directedness does not commit us to smuggling minds into physics — physical intentionality is genuine and irreducible without being mentalistic.

## The Asymmetry of Power and Manifestation

Molnar argues for a fundamental asymmetry between powers and their manifestations. Powers are ontologically independent: the fragility of the glass exists regardless of whether the glass is ever struck. Manifestations are dependent: the breaking depends on the power's exercise. If the glass lacked the power, it would not break in the relevant way even when struck. The power is prior; the manifestation is derivative.

This inverts the Humean ordering. On the regularity view, the "power" is just the pattern of manifestations — fragility is the regularity of breaking-when-struck, and the disposition is derivative on the regularity. For Molnar, the power is prior and the manifestation (and hence the regularity) is derivative. We observe regularities because powers consistently manifest under the right conditions; we should not mistake the observable trace for the underlying reality.

## Laws as Expressions of Powers

If powers are real and fundamental, what role do laws play? Molnar's answer is that the so-called laws of nature are not additional facts about the world over and above the powers of things. The law "like charges repel" is the expression of the power of charge. Laws describe what powers do; they are not independent constraints on how powers must behave. This connects Molnar to Mumford's eliminativism about laws: if powers fully explain the regularities we call "laws," laws are ontologically redundant. The laws are descriptive summaries of how powers work, not additional governing features of reality.

The consequence is a significant simplification of ontology: we do not need both powers and laws. Powers suffice. The laws are not additional features of the world that we must explain; they are consequences of the powers of things.

## Objections

The **causal deviance objection** asks how Molnar's account distinguishes genuine manifestation of a power from an accidentally co-occurring event. If charge repels like charges, and two like-charged particles move apart — but for a reason unconnected to their charge — how is this non-manifestation distinguished from genuine manifestation? The response requires an account of what it is for the power to play a causally sufficient role in producing the manifestation, which connects to process theories of causation and to the question of whether the exercise of directedness is an additional primitive or can be understood in other terms.

The **extensionality objection** notes that dispositional descriptions seem intensional: "charge repels like charges" and "charge repels particles with the same sign" may differ in what they are "about" even if they refer to the same objects. Physical science is normally expected to be extensional. Whether physical intentionality violates extensionality or whether the expectation of extensionality is itself a Humean assumption requiring justification is an ongoing debate.
