# Important Figures: What Are Dispositions?

**Gilbert Ryle** (1900–1976) introduced "dispositions" into modern analytic philosophy in *The Concept of Mind* (1949), analyzing mental concepts dispositionally to avoid Cartesian dualism.

**Nelson Goodman** (1906–1998) posed the problem of counterfactual conditionals and disposition terms in *Fact, Fiction, and Forecast* (1955), highlighting the difficulty of analyzing dispositions without circularity.

**C.B. Martin** (1924–2008) argued against the conditional analysis of dispositions using "finks" — cases where the disposition disappears just when its stimulus is applied. His work is collected in *The Mind in Nature* (2008).

**David Armstrong** contributed a categorical analysis: dispositions reduce to categorical properties plus laws of nature. This is the "standard" Humean/Armstrong view challenged by powers theorists.

**Sydney Shoemaker** argued for the causal theory of properties: a property's identity is constituted by its causal powers, making every genuine property a power of some kind.
