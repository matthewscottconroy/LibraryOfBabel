# Important Terms and Ideas: What Are Dispositions?

**Disposition**: A property characterized by what its bearers would do in suitable conditions — a tendency, power, or liability. Fragility, solubility, conductivity, and irascibility are paradigm dispositions.

**Conditional analysis of dispositions**: The traditional analysis: x is disposed to M when S iff if x were in conditions S, x would M. Fragility: if struck, would break. Martin's finks are designed to defeat this analysis.

**Finkish dispositions**: Martin's counterexample: a fragile glass is protected by a guardian who always removes the disposition just as the triggering condition is met. The glass is fragile but the conditional is false (if struck, it would not break).

**Mimicking**: The reverse of finking: x is not disposed to M, but if conditions S occurred, a guardian would give x the disposition and trigger it simultaneously. The conditional is true, but x lacks the disposition.

**Masked dispositions**: Cases where the disposition is present, the stimulus occurs, but the manifestation is prevented by a further interfering factor. The masked glass does not break when struck because it is padded.

**Categorical basis**: The intrinsic, non-dispositional property that "underlies" and "realizes" the disposition. The glass's fragility has a categorical basis in its molecular structure. On the categorical-primitivist view, the disposition reduces to this basis plus the laws.

**Irreducible dispositions (powers)**: The view that at least some dispositions are not reducible to categorical properties plus laws — they are fundamental, directed properties that essentially "point toward" their manifestations.
