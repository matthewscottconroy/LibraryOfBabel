# Important Figures: Objective Probability

**Richard von Mises** (1883–1953) developed the frequentist account: probability is the limiting relative frequency in an infinite hypothetical sequence of trials. His *Probability, Statistics, and Truth* (1928) is the key text.

**Karl Popper** (1902–1994) defended the propensity interpretation: probabilities are real, objective dispositions of chance set-ups to produce outcomes. His *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1959) and later papers develop this view.

**David Lewis** (1941–2001) developed the Principal Principle connecting objective chances to rational credences: one's credence in an event should match the objective chance, given complete information about the laws and history.

**Bas van Fraassen** (b. 1941) has written critically on objective probability and the interpretation of quantum mechanics, arguing for empiricist constraints on probability interpretations.

**Antony Eagle** has written systematic work on objective probability, defending a modified frequentism and analyzing the propensity view.
