# Important Terms and Ideas: Objective Probability

**Objective probability (chance)**: A probability that is a feature of the world itself — not merely of our degrees of belief. Quantum mechanics posits irreducible objective probabilities for measurement outcomes.

**Frequentism**: The interpretation that probability is limiting relative frequency: the probability of an outcome is the limit of the ratio of favorable to total trials in a long (possibly infinite) sequence.

**Propensity interpretation**: Popper's view that probabilities are real dispositions or tendencies of chance set-ups to produce outcomes with specific frequencies. A biased coin has a propensity of 0.6 to land heads.

**Principal Principle**: Lewis's principle: a rational agent's credence in event A should equal the objective chance of A, given complete information about the laws and history and nothing else. Connects chance to rationality.

**Chance and time**: Lewis's "new principal principle" addresses how credences should be updated as objective chances are discovered. Chances are time-indexed: the chance of rain tomorrow is different at noon from at midnight.

**Determinism and chance**: Whether objective probabilities can exist in a deterministic world. Lewis (following Loewer) argues that high-level objective chances can be grounded in the deterministic Humean mosaic through the Best System Analysis.

**Single-case probability**: The probability of a specific individual event (this coin landing heads this time) rather than a long-run frequency. Frequentism has trouble accounting for single-case probabilities; propensity views do better.
