# Quantum Chance and Fundamental Indeterminism

Quantum mechanics presents the most compelling case for objective, irreducible chance. According to standard interpretations, the outcome of a quantum measurement is not determined by the prior state of the system plus the laws — it has an objective probability that reflects genuine indeterminism at the fundamental level. What this means, and whether it really is irreducible, depends on which interpretation of quantum mechanics one accepts. But the empirical success of the Born Rule — the principle that assigns probabilities to measurement outcomes — is beyond serious dispute, and explaining what kind of probability it expresses is one of the central tasks in the philosophy of physics.

## The Born Rule

The Born Rule gives the probability that a measurement of quantum system S in state |ψ⟩ will yield outcome O_i corresponding to eigenvalue λ_i of observable Â:

**P(O_i) = |⟨φ_i|ψ⟩|²**

where |φ_i⟩ is the eigenstate corresponding to λ_i. The probability is the squared modulus of the amplitude — the inner product of the state vector with the eigenstate. This rule is empirically extraordinary: it predicts the statistics of quantum measurements with precision across all of physics, chemistry, and quantum information theory. The Born Rule may be the most precisely confirmed quantitative law in science. Whether it represents genuine objective chance or something else depends on how the measurement process is interpreted.

## Bell's Theorem and Non-Locality

Bell's theorem (1964) showed that any hidden variable theory reproducing the predictions of quantum mechanics must be non-local: the hidden variables of one particle are influenced by measurements on distant particles. **Bell inequalities** state constraints that the correlations between measurement outcomes on entangled particles must satisfy in any local hidden variable theory. Quantum mechanics predicts violations of these inequalities for entangled states.

Bell test experiments — beginning with Aspect et al. (1982) and confirmed with improving loophole closure in subsequent decades — have established that the Bell inequalities are violated in nature. Local hidden variable theories are ruled out. Either quantum mechanics involves genuine non-locality (as Bohmian mechanics explicitly does) or it involves genuine indeterminism at the fundamental level. There is no way to restore determinism and locality simultaneously while reproducing the quantum mechanical predictions.

## The Interpretations and Their Probability Claims

The **Copenhagen interpretation** holds that the wave function provides a complete description of the quantum state, and that measurement "collapses" the wave function — the system transitions from a superposition to a definite outcome, with probability given by the Born Rule. Before measurement, there is no fact of the matter about which outcome will occur: quantum probability is irreducible and objective. This interpretation supports a propensity or primitive chance reading of Born Rule probabilities.

The **many-worlds interpretation** (Everett) holds that the wave function never collapses but evolves deterministically according to the Schrödinger equation. All measurement outcomes occur in different "branches" of the universal wave function. Probability enters not as fundamental indeterminism but as a measure of how likely an observer is to find themselves in a given branch. The interpretation is deterministic at the level of the wave function; the appearance of probability reflects the branching structure. Whether the Born Rule probabilities can be derived from the Everettian structure without circularity is intensely debated. Work by Deutsch and Wallace attempts to derive the Born Rule from decision-theoretic principles within the Everettian framework — arguing that a rational agent who accepts many-worlds should set credences according to the Born Rule amplitudes.

**GRW collapse theory** (Ghirardi-Rimini-Weber) modifies quantum mechanics by adding a stochastic collapse mechanism: at random times, the wave function spontaneously collapses to a localized state. The collapse is genuinely random — not determined by hidden variables — and the probabilities of collapse outcomes are given by the Born Rule. GRW is explicitly stochastic, with irreducible, objective probability at the fundamental level.

**Bohmian mechanics** (pilot wave theory) is deterministic: particles have definite positions and momenta at all times, guided by a pilot wave (the wave function). All quantum randomness reflects ignorance of initial particle positions. The Born Rule probabilities are recovered from an equilibrium distribution over initial conditions — analogous to the thermodynamic use of probability. Bohmian mechanics restores determinism at the cost of explicit non-locality and by making the initial particle positions unobservable (hidden variables).

## Philosophical Implications

For frequentism, quantum probabilities present the hardest case. The Born Rule assigns probabilities to individual quantum events — specific measurements on specific systems — in ways that are not obviously analyzable as frequencies in reference classes. Frequentism struggles with the single-case character of these probabilities and with the non-classical features of quantum probability: contextuality, interference effects, and entanglement correlations that violate classical probability constraints.

For propensity theory, GRW and Copenhagen interpretations align most naturally: quantum probabilities are genuine dispositional features of physical systems or setups, manifesting as particular outcomes when measurements are made. The single-case character of quantum probabilities is exactly what propensity theory was designed to accommodate.

For Humean accounts, the Born Rule can appear in the Best System as a probabilistic law, and Lewisian chances for quantum systems can be defined accordingly. But whether Humean chances capture the single-case, contextual character of quantum probability remains contested. Quantum mechanics also challenges Humean supervenience directly: entangled quantum states are not local, intrinsic features of individual spacetime points but holistic features of multi-particle systems. Recent Humeans have explored whether quantum mechanics can be reformulated in a locally supervenient form, but no consensus has emerged.

For the metaphysics of laws more generally, the Born Rule illustrates that probabilistic laws are genuine features of the world that resist reduction to their extensions — the actual pattern of measurement outcomes. A law that assigns probability 0.5 to an outcome has content that cannot be read off from any finite sequence of outcomes, because any finite sequence is compatible with many different probability values. The irreducibility of probabilistic laws to their actual instances is among the strongest evidence that laws are genuinely modal and not merely descriptive.
