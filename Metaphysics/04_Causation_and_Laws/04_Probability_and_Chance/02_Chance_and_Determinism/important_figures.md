# Important Figures: Chance and Determinism

**Pierre-Simon Laplace** (1749–1827) articulated the deterministic picture: an intellect knowing all forces and positions at any moment could calculate all future (and past) events. His *Philosophical Essay on Probabilities* is the key text.

**David Lewis** (1941–2001) argued that objective chances can exist in a deterministic world through his Best System Analysis: probability is a fundamental posit of the best system, even if the underlying dynamics are deterministic.

**Barry Loewer** has developed Lewis's Humean account of chance, arguing that the apparently irreducible probabilities of statistical mechanics can be understood as Humean chances.

**Karl Popper** introduced the indeterminism/determinism debate into modern philosophy of science and defended propensity as the correct account of quantum probabilities.

**Albert, David Z.** (b. 1956) has argued for a Bohmian (deterministic) interpretation of quantum mechanics, which recovers apparent probabilities from ignorance of initial conditions.
