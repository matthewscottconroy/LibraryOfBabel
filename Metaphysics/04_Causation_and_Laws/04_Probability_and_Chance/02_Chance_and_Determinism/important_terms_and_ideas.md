# Important Terms and Ideas: Chance and Determinism

**Determinism**: The thesis that the state of the world at any time, together with the laws of nature, uniquely determines its state at every other time. A deterministic world has no objective chanciness; all apparent probability reflects epistemic ignorance.

**Laplacean demon**: Laplace's hypothetical intellect knowing all particle positions and momenta at a time — and hence able to calculate all future and past events. The demon represents the ideal of determinism.

**Indeterminism**: The thesis that the laws of nature leave the future underdetermined by the present state. Quantum mechanics is the primary example of a well-confirmed indeterministic theory.

**Epistemic probability**: Probability as a measure of rational credence given partial information. Epistemic probability is compatible with determinism: we assign probabilities to outcomes because we do not know the exact initial conditions.

**Ontic (objective) probability**: Probability as a feature of the world itself — not a measure of ignorance but a genuine propensity or chance. Indeterminism is the view that ontic probabilities are irreducible.

**Many-worlds interpretation**: Everett's interpretation of quantum mechanics: all outcomes occur in branching parallel worlds; the apparent probabilities are branch weights, not objective chances in any single world.

**Humean chance under determinism**: Lewis's (and Loewer's) view: even in a deterministic world, objective chances can exist as features of the Best System. Statistical mechanical probabilities, for instance, are objective Humean chances.
