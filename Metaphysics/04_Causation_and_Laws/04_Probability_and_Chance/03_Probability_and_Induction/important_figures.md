# Important Figures: Probability and Induction

**Rudolf Carnap** (1891–1970) developed inductive logic: a formal theory of logical or objective probability as the degree to which evidence supports a hypothesis. His *Logical Foundations of Probability* (1950) is the key text.

**Karl Popper** (1902–1994) famously argued that induction is not a valid form of inference and that science proceeds by conjectures and refutations. His *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1959) is essential.

**Nelson Goodman** (1906–1998) posed the "new riddle of induction" with the grue predicate, arguing that the problem of induction has not been solved even by probabilistic approaches.

**Frank Ramsey** (1903–1930) and **Bruno de Finetti** (1906–1985) developed the subjective interpretation of probability as coherent degrees of belief, with Bayesian updating as the normative standard.

**David Hume** (1711–1776) gave the classical formulation of the problem of induction in *A Treatise of Human Nature* and the *Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*.
