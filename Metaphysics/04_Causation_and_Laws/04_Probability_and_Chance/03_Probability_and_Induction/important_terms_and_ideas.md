# Important Terms and Ideas: Probability and Induction

**The problem of induction**: Hume's challenge: what justifies inferring regularities about unobserved cases from observed cases? No deductive argument from the past to the future is valid; any inductive argument seems circular.

**Confirmation**: The relation between evidence and hypothesis: evidence E confirms hypothesis H iff E increases the probability of H. Bayesian confirmation theory analyzes this in terms of updating prior probabilities.

**Prior probability**: In Bayesian terms, the probability assigned to a hypothesis before considering specific evidence. Priors encode initial plausibility assessments and are updated by evidence through Bayes' theorem.

**Likelihood**: The probability of the evidence given the hypothesis: P(E|H). In Bayesian updating, hypotheses with higher likelihoods receive more confirmation from the evidence.

**Bayes' theorem**: P(H|E) = P(E|H) × P(H) / P(E). The formal rule for updating degrees of belief in a hypothesis in light of new evidence.

**The grue paradox (Goodman)**: "Grue" means "green before time T or blue after T." All observed emeralds confirm "All emeralds are grue" as well as "All emeralds are green" — but only the latter seems a genuinely projectible regularity. The problem is explaining the asymmetry.

**Projectibility**: The property of a predicate or hypothesis that makes it apt for inductive projection. "Green" is projectible; "grue" is not. Explaining projectibility is one of the central problems of induction.

**Dutch book argument**: An argument that rational agents must have coherent degrees of belief (satisfying the probability axioms) or they can be exploited by a series of bets they would accept but must collectively lose.
