# References and Primary Sources: Probability and Induction

## Foundational Texts

Hume, David. *A Treatise of Human Nature*, Book I, Part III, §VI. Ed. Selby-Bigge. Oxford: Clarendon Press. The problem of induction: no logical justification for inferring future regularities from past observations.

Goodman, Nelson. *Fact, Fiction, and Forecast*. Harvard University Press, 1955. The new riddle of induction: why do some predicates project better than others? Introduces "grue."

## Contemporary Works

Carnap, Rudolf. *Logical Foundations of Probability*. University of Chicago Press, 1950. Develops inductive logic as a formal system.

Jaynes, E.T. *Probability Theory: The Logic of Science*. Cambridge University Press, 2003. Defends Bayesian probability as a generalization of logic.

Sober, Elliott. *Evidence and Evolution*. Cambridge University Press, 2008. A careful treatment of how probabilistic reasoning works in science.

Vineberg, Susan. "Dutch Book Arguments." *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, 2022. The coherence constraints on probabilistic degrees of belief.
