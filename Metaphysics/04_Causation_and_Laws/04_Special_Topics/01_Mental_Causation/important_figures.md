# Important Figures: Mental Causation

**Donald Davidson** (1917–2003) proposed anomalous monism: each mental event is identical to a physical event, but mental types are not identical to physical types. Mental events can be causes, but under their mental descriptions they fall under no strict (physical) laws.

**Jaegwon Kim** (1934–2019) developed the exclusion argument: if physical events have sufficient physical causes, mental causes are excluded unless mental properties are identical to (or realized by) physical properties. His *Mind in a Physical World* (1998) is essential.

**Jerry Fodor** (1935–2017) argued for the causal efficacy of intentional states via the computational theory of mind. *Psychosemantics* (1987) addresses how mental states can causally explain behavior.

**Stephen Yablo** (b. 1957) proposed proportionality as a solution to exclusion: the mental cause is the more "proportionate" cause — it is at the right level of abstraction to explain the effect.

**Tim Crane** (b. 1962) has argued for a dualist-friendly account of mental causation that accepts irreducible mental properties without abandoning causal closure.
