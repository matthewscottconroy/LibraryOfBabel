# Important Terms and Ideas: Mental Causation

**The exclusion problem**: If every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, and mental events are not identical to physical events, then mental causes are excluded from causal efficacy. Adding mental causes would create overdetermination.

**Causal closure of the physical**: The thesis that every physical event that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause. This is the key premise generating the exclusion problem for non-reductive views.

**Anomalous monism (Davidson)**: Mental events are identical to physical events (token identity), but there are no strict psychophysical laws correlating mental and physical types. Mental descriptions are "anomalous" — not covered by strict laws.

**Non-reductive physicalism**: The view that mental properties are not identical to physical properties (not type-type reduction) but that mental events are realized by physical events (token physicalism). Vulnerable to the exclusion argument.

**Supervenience and causation**: Mental properties supervene on physical properties; if supervenience is not constitutive, the question arises of whether supervenient properties can contribute to causal explanation beyond what the subvenient properties explain.

**Proportionality (Yablo)**: A mental cause is the right level of causation because it is proportionate to the effect: it abstracts from causally irrelevant physical detail. The mental cause "explains" the effect at the appropriate level of generality.

**Epiphenomenalism**: The view that mental properties are effects of physical processes but cause nothing — they are epiphenomenal byproducts of the physical. The exclusion problem may force non-reductive physicalists toward this uncomfortable position.
