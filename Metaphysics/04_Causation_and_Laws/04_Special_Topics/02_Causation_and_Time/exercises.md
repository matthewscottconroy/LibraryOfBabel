# Exercises and Thought Experiments: Special Topics in Causation

## Comprehension Questions

1. State Kim's causal exclusion argument. What are the key premises, and what is the conclusion? Which premise do you think is most questionable?

2. What is Yablo's solution to the exclusion problem using the determinable/determinate distinction? How does it attempt to vindicate non-reductive physicalism?

3. Explain the philosophical argument for the conceptual coherence of backward causation. What is the standard objection involving causal loops, and how might a defender of backward causation respond?

## Discussion Questions

4. If physical causal closure is true, does it follow that mental causation is epiphenomenal? Or are there ways to accept causal closure while denying epiphenomenalism?

5. Is the temporal direction of causation a necessary feature (causation must run from past to future), a contingent feature (it happens to run that way in our universe), or a conceptual feature (the concept of causation includes temporal direction)? What are the implications for physics?

6. Causal structuralists argue that the structure of causal relations is more fundamental than the relata (objects and events). Does this view require that causation be atemporal, or can temporal structure be part of the causal structure?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Delayed Choice Experiment**: In quantum mechanics, a "delayed choice" experiment appears to allow the observer to retroactively affect which path a photon took in a double-slit experiment. Does this constitute evidence for backward causation? What else might explain the result?

8. **The Perfect Epiphenomenon**: Imagine a zombie world physically identical to ours in which mental properties exist but have no causal power. Could we tell the difference? What does this thought experiment reveal about the relationship between causation and mental properties?

9. **The Structural Equilibrium**: In an economic model, supply and demand simultaneously determine price and quantity. Is this genuine simultaneous causation, or is the "causation" here just a structural relationship without temporal direction? Does it matter?

## Advanced Problems

10. Kim presents the exclusion problem as a dilemma: either reduce the mental to the physical or accept epiphenomenalism. Construct the strongest possible defense of non-reductive physicalism that avoids both horns. What additional metaphysical resources does your defense require?
