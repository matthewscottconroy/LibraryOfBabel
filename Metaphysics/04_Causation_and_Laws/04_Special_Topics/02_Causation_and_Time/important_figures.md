# Important Figures: Causation and Time

**Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)** argued that causation is a pure concept of the understanding that structures our experience. His discussion of simultaneous causation in the *Critique of Pure Reason* raised important questions about the relationship between causation and temporal order.

**Michael Dummett (1925–2011)** argued for the conceptual coherence of backward causation in "Bringing About the Past" (1964), examining whether it is possible to cause past events.

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** derived the asymmetry of causation from the asymmetry of counterfactual dependence, which he explained in terms of the thermodynamic arrow of time.

**Huw Price (b. 1953)** has argued extensively for retrocausation as a possible solution to puzzles in quantum mechanics, particularly in joint work with Ken Wharton. His *Time's Arrow and Archimedes' Point* (1996) is an important treatment of the arrow of time.

**John Bell (1928–1990)** and later work by various physicists on Bell inequalities and quantum entanglement raised the question of whether quantum correlations involve any form of causal influence that is independent of temporal order.

**Judea Pearl** has noted that causal graphs can represent atemporal structural equations, raising questions about whether causation requires temporal order or is fundamentally about structural dependence.
