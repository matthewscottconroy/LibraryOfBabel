# Important Terms and Ideas: Causation and Time

**Causal asymmetry**: The feature of causation whereby causes precede their effects (in standard cases). The puzzle is explaining why causation is temporally asymmetric if the fundamental physical laws are time-symmetric.

**The arrow of time**: The directionality of time from past to future, manifested in thermodynamic processes, the growth of information, and the asymmetry of causation. The arrow is not derivable from time-symmetric fundamental laws alone.

**Retrocausation (backward causation)**: Causation in which the cause is later in time than the effect. Standard physics forbids this; but some quantum mechanical interpretations and philosophical thought experiments have proposed it.

**The fork asymmetry**: Reichenbach's observation that causes typically produce correlated effects (a common cause creates a fork), while distinct effects of a common cause are screened off by their common cause. This fork structure is temporally asymmetric.

**The block universe and causation**: If the four-dimensional block universe is metaphysically real, the asymmetry of causation must be explained without appeal to an objective "passage" of time — purely in terms of the structural features of the Humean mosaic.

**Causal direction and counterfactuals**: Lewis's counterfactual analysis grounds causal asymmetry in the asymmetry of overdetermination: past events are recorded by many subsequent states (many counterfactually depend on the past), while future events are anticipated by few.

**Time travel and causal loops**: A time-traveler going to the past creates a causal loop: the effect is earlier than the cause. Causal loops may or may not be coherent; their possibility depends on whether the causal structure of spacetime permits closed timelike curves.
