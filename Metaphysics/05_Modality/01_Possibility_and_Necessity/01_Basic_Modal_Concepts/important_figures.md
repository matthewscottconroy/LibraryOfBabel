# Important Figures: Basic Modal Concepts

## Aristotle (384–322 BCE)
The first systematic philosopher to distinguish necessity, possibility, and contingency as logical and metaphysical categories. His modal concepts shaped the subsequent tradition through their incorporation into medieval scholasticism and early modern philosophy.

## Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)
Introduced the concept of possible worlds as a framework for understanding necessity and contingency: necessary truths hold in all possible worlds, while contingent truths hold only in some. His framework became the dominant idiom for twentieth-century modal semantics.

## Rudolf Carnap (1891–1970)
Developed early formal treatments of modal logic and modal semantics using state-descriptions. His work influenced Kripke's later possible-worlds semantics and set the stage for rigorous analytic treatments of modality.

## Saul Kripke (1940–2022)
Provided the standard possible-worlds semantics for modal logic, introducing accessibility relations between worlds. His technical framework revolutionized both modal logic and analytic metaphysics, making precise the distinctions between different modal systems.
