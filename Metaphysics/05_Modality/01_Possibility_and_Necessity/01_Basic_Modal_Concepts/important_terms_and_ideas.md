# Important Terms and Ideas: Basic Modal Concepts

**Necessity**
A proposition is necessary if it could not have been false—it holds in every possible world. Philosophers distinguish metaphysical necessity (what must be given the nature of reality) from logical necessity (what follows from logic alone) and nomological necessity (what must be given the laws of nature).

**Possibility**
A proposition is possible if it could be true—if there is at least one possible world where it holds. Possibility is the dual of necessity: P is possible if and only if it is not necessary that not-P.

**Contingency**
A proposition is contingent if it is both possible and possibly false—it holds in some worlds but not others. Contingent truths are neither necessary nor impossible; they depend on how things happen to be.

**Actuality**
The actual world is the world we inhabit—the totality of what is genuinely the case. Modal claims relate propositions to the actual world and to other possible worlds, with actuality serving as the reference point for distinguishing what is from what merely could be.

**De Dicto vs. De Re Modality**
De dicto modality attaches to a proposition (the proposition that water is H₂O is necessarily true). De re modality attaches to an object itself (this water is necessarily H₂O). The distinction matters because de re necessity is linked to the essences of individual things.

**Epistemic vs. Metaphysical Modality**
Epistemic possibility concerns what is compatible with our knowledge (it is epistemically possible that there is life on Mars). Metaphysical possibility concerns what could genuinely be the case (it is metaphysically necessary that water is H₂O). Kripke showed these can come apart: there are necessary truths that are known only a posteriori.

**Modal Logic**
The formal study of necessity and possibility using operators □ (necessarily) and ◇ (possibly). Different systems (T, S4, S5) correspond to different constraints on the accessibility relation between possible worlds and capture different conceptions of what necessity requires.
