# Important Figures: Modal Logic

## C.I. Lewis (1883–1964)
Dissatisfied with the paradoxes of material implication, Lewis developed the first modern systems of modal logic (S1–S5), introducing strict implication and the modern symbolism for necessity and possibility. His work laid the formal foundation for all subsequent modal logic.

## Saul Kripke (1940–2022)
Developed the relational (possible-worlds) semantics for modal logic in the early 1960s while still a teenager. His Kripke frames—sets of worlds with an accessibility relation—gave rigorous mathematical models for Lewis's systems and transformed modal logic into a productive formal discipline.

## Ruth Barcan Marcus (1921–2012)
Pioneer of quantified modal logic, she derived the Barcan formula (◇∃xFx → ∃x◇Fx) and its converse, stimulating decades of debate about the interaction between modality and quantification. Her work raised fundamental questions about possibilist versus actualist interpretations of quantification.

## Jaakko Hintikka (1929–2015)
Independently developed possible-worlds semantics and applied it to epistemic and deontic logic as well as alethic modality. His work extended the Kripke framework to a broad family of intensional logics and influenced formal semantics in linguistics.
