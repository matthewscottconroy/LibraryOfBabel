# Important Figures: Lewis's Modal Realism

## David Lewis (1941–2001)
The most influential analytic metaphysician of the late twentieth century and the originator of modal realism. His *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986) argues that possible worlds are concrete, spatiotemporally isolated universes as real as our own, providing a reductive analysis of modality in purely non-modal terms.

## David Armstrong (1926–2014)
Lewis's main interlocutor on the theory of universals and modality; defended combinatorialism as an alternative reductive account of possibility, arguing that possible worlds are recombinations of actual states of affairs rather than distinct concrete universes.

## Robert Stalnaker (born 1940)
Defended a moderate realism about possible worlds—treating them as abstract maximal consistent propositions or ways things could be—as an alternative to Lewis's extreme realism. His account preserves the technical benefits of possible-worlds semantics without the ontological costs.

## Saul Kripke (1940–2022)
While not a modal realist, Kripke's possible-worlds semantics gave Lewis the formal framework his realism was meant to ground metaphysically. Lewis's realism is in part an attempt to explain what possible worlds really are such that the Kripkean semantics is literally true.
