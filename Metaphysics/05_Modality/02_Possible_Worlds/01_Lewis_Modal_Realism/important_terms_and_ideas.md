# Important Terms and Ideas: Lewis's Modal Realism

**Modal Realism**
The thesis that possible worlds are real, concrete, spatiotemporally isolated universes, each as fully actual as our own—differing in what they contain but not in ontological status. "Actual" on this view is an indexical term: every world is actual from its own perspective.

**Concrete Possible Worlds**
On Lewis's account, worlds are not abstract objects, propositions, or sets—they are concrete totalities of physical objects and events. Our world is one among infinitely many such totalities, distinguished by the laws and particular facts that hold within them.

**Counterpart Theory**
Since individuals exist in only one world (they are not transworld entities), de re modal claims about an individual are analyzed as claims about that individual's counterparts in other worlds—objects that resemble it in the relevant ways. What is possible for me is what is true of my counterparts.

**Indexical Actuality**
"Actual" functions like "here" or "now"—it picks out whichever world is used as the context of utterance. There is no metaphysically privileged actual world; each world is actual from within itself. This defuses the objection that modal realism fails to respect the special status of actuality.

**Humean Supervenience**
Lewis's broader thesis that everything supervenes on the mosaic of local, non-modal, physical facts arranged across spacetime. Modal realism supports this program by reducing modal truths to truths about the distribution of properties across many worlds.

**Recombination Principle**
Any recombination of elements from existing worlds—provided it satisfies certain size constraints—corresponds to a genuine possible world. This principle generates the plenitude of possibilities Lewis needs without ad hoc restrictions.
