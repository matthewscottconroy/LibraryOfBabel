# References and Primary Sources: Actualism

## Foundational Texts

- Plantinga, Alvin, *The Nature of Necessity* (1974). The canonical statement of actualist possible-worlds theory; introduces maximal possible states of affairs as the ontological basis for modal semantics while remaining within an actualist ontology.

- Adams, Robert, "Theories of Actuality," *Noûs* 8 (1974). Distinguishes actualist from possibilist theories and defends an actualist account using world-stories, arguing that actuality is an irreducible primitive.

- Fine, Kit, "Essence and Modality," *Philosophical Perspectives* 8 (1994). Argues that necessity should be grounded in essence rather than possible worlds, reversing the standard order of explanation and providing an actualist alternative grounding for modal truths.

## Contemporary Works

- Rosen, Gideon, "Modal Fictionalism," *Mind* 99 (1990). Proposes that possible-worlds talk is a useful fiction—we speak as if there are possible worlds without actually being committed to their existence, whether concrete or abstract.

- Sider, Theodore, "Reductive Theories of Modality," in *The Oxford Handbook of Metaphysics* (2003). Surveys actualism, possibilism, and fictionalism with attention to the trade-offs between ontological economy and explanatory power.
