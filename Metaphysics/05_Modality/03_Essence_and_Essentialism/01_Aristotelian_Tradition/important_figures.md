# Important Figures: The Aristotelian Tradition of Essence

## Aristotle (384–322 BCE)
The originator of essentialist metaphysics; distinguished what a thing is (its essence or "what-it-is-to-be") from its accidental properties. For Aristotle, essences are tied to natural kinds and constitute the proper objects of scientific knowledge.

## Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)
Developed Aristotelian essentialism within a theological framework, distinguishing essence from existence and arguing that in God alone are essence and existence identical. His synthesis shaped the scholastic tradition's treatment of substantial forms and essences.

## Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677)
Transformed Aristotelian essentialism radically: each thing's essence is its conatus or striving to persist in being. His approach raises questions about whether essences are individuating properties or more dynamic, power-based features.

## W.V.O. Quine (1908–2000)
Although a critic of essentialism, Quine's challenge—"Aristotelian essentialism" as he called it—forced essentialists to clarify what it means to say an object has a property essentially versus accidentally. His objections helped sharpen the contemporary debate.
