# Important Terms and Ideas: The Aristotelian Tradition of Essence

**Essence (To Ti Ēn Einai)**
Aristotle's phrase, literally "the what-it-was-to-be" for a thing, referring to the defining character that makes something the kind of thing it is. An essence answers the question "what is it?" in a way that reveals the nature of the thing, not merely its accidental features.

**Substantial Form**
The form that makes a parcel of matter a member of a particular natural kind—the principle of organization that constitutes a thing as what it is. Substantial forms are the metaphysical cores of Aristotelian essences and the basis for natural kind membership.

**Accident**
A property that a thing has but could lack without ceasing to be the thing it is. Aristotle contrasts accidents with essential or necessary properties: Socrates is essentially a human but accidentally snub-nosed.

**Primary vs. Secondary Substance**
In the *Categories*, primary substances are individual things (this horse, this man); secondary substances are the species and genera (horse, animal) to which they belong. Secondary substances express the essential nature that individuals instantiate.

**Real Definition**
An Aristotelian real definition specifies the essence of a kind by stating its genus and specific difference: "human being is a rational animal" defines the essence of humanity. Real definitions contrast with nominal definitions, which merely record how a word is used.

**Hylomorphism**
Aristotle's view that physical substances are composites of matter (hyle) and form (morphe). The form provides the essential structure; the matter provides the substratum that receives form. This framework underlies the Aristotelian account of change and essence.
