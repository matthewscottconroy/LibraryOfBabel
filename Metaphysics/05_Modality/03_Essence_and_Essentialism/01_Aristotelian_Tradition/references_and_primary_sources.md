# References and Primary Sources: The Aristotelian Tradition of Essence

## Foundational Texts

- Aristotle, *Metaphysics*, Books Zeta and Eta (c. 350 BCE). The canonical analysis of substance, essence, and form; introduces the "what-it-is-to-be" (to ti ēn einai) as the core of what makes a thing the kind of thing it is.

- Aristotle, *Categories* (c. 350 BCE). Distinguishes substance from accident, setting up the ontological framework for essentialism; primary and secondary substances represent the two levels at which essence operates.

- Aquinas, Thomas, *On Being and Essence* (c. 1252). A compact, influential treatise distinguishing essence, existence, and the different senses of essence in simple and composite beings; draws on Aristotle while developing the doctrine in a theological direction.

## Contemporary Works

- Loux, Michael, *Substance and Attribute* (1978). A careful analytical reconstruction of the Aristotelian theory of substance and essence, situating it within contemporary debates about universals and particulars.

- Cohen, Marc, "Aristotle's Metaphysics," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2020). A thorough survey of Aristotle's ontological views with detailed treatment of substance, essence, and form.
