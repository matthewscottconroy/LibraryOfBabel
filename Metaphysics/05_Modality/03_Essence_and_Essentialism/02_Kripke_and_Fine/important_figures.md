# Important Figures: Kripke and Fine on Essence

## Saul Kripke (1940–2022)
Revolutionized the theory of essence and essentialism in *Naming and Necessity* (1980). By distinguishing metaphysical necessity from epistemic necessity and a priori knowledge, Kripke rehabilitated essentialism and argued for origin essentialism—that things necessarily originate from the matter and parents they actually came from.

## Kit Fine (born 1946)
Transformed the foundations of essence theory in a series of papers arguing that essence is prior to and more fundamental than necessity. His core claim: modal notions should be explained in terms of essence, not the other way around. Objects have essences that ground their necessary properties.

## Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)
Collaborated with Kripke in developing the causal-historical theory of reference and the associated essentialism about natural kinds. Putnam argued that the meaning of natural-kind terms is partly constituted by the essential nature of the kind (water is essentially H₂O).

## Nathan Salmon (born 1951)
Extended and defended Kripkean essentialism, particularly origin essentialism, against objections. His work clarifies the metaphysical and semantic dimensions of essential properties and the relationship between transworld identity and essentialist claims.
