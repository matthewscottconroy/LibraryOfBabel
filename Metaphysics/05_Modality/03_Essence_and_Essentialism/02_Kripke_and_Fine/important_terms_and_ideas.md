# Important Terms and Ideas: Kripke and Fine on Essence

**Origin Essentialism**
Kripke's thesis that the origin of a thing is essential to it: a person necessarily comes from the sperm and egg they actually came from, and an artifact necessarily originates from the material it actually originated from. Different origins would yield a different individual, not the same one with a different history.

**Natural Kind Essentialism**
The view that natural kinds like water, gold, and tigers have real essences—underlying natures (H₂O, atomic number 79, genetic structure)—that are metaphysically necessary. Kripke and Putnam use this to argue that many scientific identities are necessary a posteriori truths.

**Necessary A Posteriori**
A truth that is metaphysically necessary yet can only be known through empirical investigation. "Water is H₂O" is Kripke's canonical example: necessarily true (water couldn't fail to be H₂O) but discoverable only by science, not by armchair reasoning.

**Rigid Designator**
A term that designates the same object in every possible world (where that object exists). Proper names and natural-kind terms are rigid designators on Kripke's account; this rigidity explains why identity statements using them are necessarily true if true at all.

**Fine's Essentialist Reversal**
Fine argues that the standard analysis defining essential properties as those an object has in every world where it exists is incorrect. The number 2 has every property of Socrates necessarily (since Socrates is not a number), but this does not make those properties essential to 2. Essence must be defined independently of necessity.

**Constitutive Essence**
Fine's category of those properties that directly constitute what a thing is—the essential properties in the core sense. These are distinguished from consequential essence (logical consequences of constitutive essence) and immediate essence (constitutive properties not derived from others).
