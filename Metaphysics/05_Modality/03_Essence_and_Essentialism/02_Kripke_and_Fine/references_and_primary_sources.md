# References and Primary Sources: Kripke and Fine on Essence

## Foundational Texts

- Kripke, Saul, *Naming and Necessity* (1980). The landmark work rehabilitating essentialism: argues for the necessity of identity, origin essentialism, and natural kind essentialism, distinguishing metaphysical from epistemic modality throughout.

- Fine, Kit, "Essence and Modality," *Philosophical Perspectives* 8 (1994). Argues that the modal analysis of essence is inadequate and that essence is an irreducible notion that should ground our understanding of necessity, not be defined by it.

- Fine, Kit, "Senses of Essence," in *Modality, Morality and Belief* edited by Sinnott-Armstrong et al. (1995). Distinguishes constitutive, consequential, and immediate essence, building a comprehensive theory of essentialist claims.

- Putnam, Hilary, "The Meaning of 'Meaning'," in *Mind, Language and Reality* (1975). Develops the causal-historical semantics for natural-kind terms and argues that natural kinds have real essences discoverable by science rather than meaning-constituting definitions.

## Contemporary Works

- Salmon, Nathan, *Reference and Essence* (1981). Systematic defense of origin essentialism and Kripkean essentialism about material objects against a range of objections.

- Robertson Ishii, Teresa, "Essential vs. Accidental Properties," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2020). A thorough contemporary survey of the main positions in the debate.
