# Important Figures: Anti-Essentialism

## W.V.O. Quine (1908–2000)
The most influential critic of essentialism in the analytic tradition. Quine argued that essential properties make sense only relative to a description or mode of presentation of an object, not absolutely, rendering Aristotelian essentialism "invidious." His critique forced essentialists to articulate what grounds the essential/accidental distinction.

## David Lewis (1941–2001)
While not a thoroughgoing anti-essentialist, Lewis's counterpart theory relativizes de re modal claims to contextually determined counterpart relations, allowing that whether a property is essential to an object depends on which counterpart relation is salient—a position that softens essentialism significantly.

## Alan Sidelle (born 1956)
A prominent conventionalist about modality who argues that essences are not discovered features of mind-independent reality but are instead artifacts of the concepts and conventions we bring to bear. On his view, what counts as essential is determined by our linguistic and conceptual practices.

## Eli Hirsch (born 1940)
Argues for a pluralism about persistence and identity conditions, suggesting that alternative conceptual schemes would carve reality differently. His work challenges the idea that there is a unique, language-independent fact about what is essential to things.
