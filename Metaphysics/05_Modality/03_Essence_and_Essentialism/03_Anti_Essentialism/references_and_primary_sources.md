# References and Primary Sources: Anti-Essentialism

## Foundational Texts

- Quine, W.V.O., "Reference and Modality," in *From a Logical Point of View* (1953). The locus classicus of Quinean anti-essentialism; argues that quantified modal logic requires Aristotelian essentialism and that this commits one to distinctions that cannot be made sense of.

- Quine, W.V.O., "Three Grades of Modal Involvement," *Proceedings of the XIth International Congress of Philosophy* (1953). Distinguishes degrees of commitment to modality and explains why only the weakest—metalinguistic modality—is acceptable without ontological cost.

- Sidelle, Alan, *Necessity, Essence, and Individuation* (1989). A systematic conventionalist critique of essentialist metaphysics; argues that modal facts are constituted by our conceptual practices rather than mind-independent necessities in nature.

## Contemporary Works

- Sider, Theodore, *Writing the Book of the World* (2011). Contains important discussion of anti-essentialist positions and the debate about whether there is a uniquely correct way to carve reality.

- Bird, Alexander, "Essences and Natural Kinds," *The Routledge Companion to Metaphysics* (2009). Surveys essentialist and anti-essentialist positions about natural kinds and evaluates the main objections to scientific essentialism.
