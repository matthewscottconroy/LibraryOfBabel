# Important Figures: How Do We Know Modality?

## Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)
Argued that necessary truths are known a priori through the structure of pure reason and pure intuition, not through experience. His account of synthetic a priori knowledge raised the problem of how such knowledge is possible and influenced all subsequent modal epistemology.

## Saul Kripke (1940–2022)
By distinguishing metaphysical necessity from epistemic a priority, Kripke showed that the epistemology of modality is not simply a matter of armchair reasoning. Some necessary truths are known only a posteriori, requiring empirical investigation despite their modal status.

## George Bealer (1944–2019)
Defended modal intuitions as a genuine source of evidence about metaphysical necessity. On his view, philosophical intuitions—including modal intuitions—have evidential weight that is not reducible to mere psychological dispositions; they are truth-tracking responses to modal reality.

## Timothy Williamson (born 1955)
Developed an influential account of modal knowledge in *Modal Logic as Metaphysics* (2013), arguing that our knowledge of metaphysical modality is an extension of our capacity for counterfactual reasoning, itself grounded in ordinary cognitive abilities.
