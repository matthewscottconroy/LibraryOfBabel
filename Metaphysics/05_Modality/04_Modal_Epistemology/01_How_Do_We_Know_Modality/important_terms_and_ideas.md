# Important Terms and Ideas: How Do We Know Modality?

**Modal Epistemology**
The philosophical study of how we know or have justified beliefs about what is necessary, possible, or impossible. Key questions include whether modal intuitions are reliable, whether conceivability is a guide to possibility, and whether modal knowledge requires distinctive cognitive capacities.

**Conceivability**
The epistemic relation of being able to coherently imagine or suppose that something is the case without explicit contradiction. Traditionally cited as evidence for possibility: if we can conceive of P, then P is (prima facie) possible. Kripke's work showed this inference is problematic for certain necessary truths.

**Conceivability-Possibility Thesis**
The claim that conceivability is a reliable guide to metaphysical possibility. Strong versions hold that what is conceivable is possible; weaker versions hold only that conceivability is pro tanto evidence for possibility. Kripke's necessary a posteriori creates pressure against the strong version.

**Two-Dimensional Semantics**
Chalmers's framework distinguishing two ways an expression can determine a referent: the primary intension (what the term picks out given the actual world as considered actual) and the secondary intension (what it picks out given a world as counterfactually considered). The framework aims to preserve conceivability as a guide to possibility at the primary-intension level.

**A Priori vs. A Posteriori Modal Knowledge**
A priori modal knowledge is knowledge of necessity and possibility accessible through pure reason (e.g., arithmetic necessities). A posteriori modal knowledge requires empirical investigation (e.g., that water is necessarily H₂O). Kripke's work showed these categories are logically independent.

**Rational Intuition**
The proposed cognitive faculty by which we directly grasp necessary truths or modal facts without inference. Rationalists defend such a faculty as explaining our knowledge of logic, mathematics, and metaphysical necessities; empiricists are skeptical that any faculty tracks modal reality in this way.
