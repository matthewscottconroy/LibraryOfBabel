# References and Primary Sources: How Do We Know Modality?

## Foundational Texts

- Kripke, Saul, *Naming and Necessity* (1980). Distinguishes epistemic from metaphysical modality and argues that conceivability does not straightforwardly track possibility; some necessary truths are empirically discovered.

- Chalmers, David, "Does Conceivability Entail Possibility?" in *Conceivability and Possibility* edited by Gendler and Hawthorne (2002). Defends a two-dimensional framework distinguishing primary from secondary intensions to salvage the evidential value of conceivability for modal epistemology.

- Williamson, Timothy, *Modal Logic as Metaphysics* (2013). Argues that modal knowledge derives from the general human capacity for counterfactual reasoning and that the gap between modal and counterfactual knowledge is much smaller than commonly assumed.

## Contemporary Works

- Gendler, Tamar Szabó and Hawthorne, John (eds.), *Conceivability and Possibility* (2002). A landmark anthology collecting the main positions on whether conceivability is a guide to possibility, including papers by Chalmers, Yablo, and others.

- Vaidya, Anand, "The Epistemology of Modality," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2017). Comprehensive survey of the main epistemological approaches to modal knowledge.
