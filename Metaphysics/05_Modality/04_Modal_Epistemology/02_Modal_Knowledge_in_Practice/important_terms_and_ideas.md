# Important Terms and Ideas: Modal Knowledge in Practice

**Thought Experiments**
Hypothetical scenarios designed to test intuitions or elicit modal knowledge. In philosophy, thought experiments function as instruments for probing what is possible or necessary: if we can clearly conceive the scenario and judge its outcome, this provides evidence about modal facts.

**Intuition Pumps**
Dennett's term for thought experiments that work by priming certain intuitive responses. In modal epistemology, this raises the question of when intuition pumps genuinely illuminate modal structure versus merely manipulating psychological responses. Distinguishing reliable from misleading intuition pumps is a central methodological challenge.

**Imaginative Simulation**
Yablo's account: modal knowledge is gained by imagining a world and asking whether the relevant proposition obtains within it. This is more demanding than mere logical consistency—one must simulate the world in enough detail to judge whether P holds, which provides stronger evidence for possibility.

**Conceptual Analysis**
The philosophical method of analyzing the contents of concepts to extract necessary truths about what falls under them. Jackson uses conceptual analysis to show how a priori reflection on ordinary concepts can yield knowledge of what is possible or necessary in a given domain.

**Reflective Equilibrium**
The methodological standard for evaluating philosophical theories through iterative adjustment between principles and intuitions about cases. In modal epistemology, reflective equilibrium governs how we balance modal intuitions against theoretical considerations when building a modal metaphysics.

**Zombie Conceivability**
Chalmers's thought experiment: it is conceivable that there exist beings physically identical to us but lacking consciousness (philosophical zombies). If this conceivability tracks possibility, physicalism about consciousness is false. Modal epistemological debates about this case illustrate the broader stakes of conceivability arguments.
