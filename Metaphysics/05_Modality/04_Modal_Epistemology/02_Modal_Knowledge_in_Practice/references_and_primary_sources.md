# References and Primary Sources: Modal Knowledge in Practice

## Foundational Texts

- Yablo, Stephen, "Is Conceivability a Guide to Possibility?" *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 53 (1993). Develops an account of conceivability as imagining that P is true, distinguishing this from mere supposing, and argues this makes conceivability a reliable guide to possibility.

- Jackson, Frank, *From Metaphysics to Ethics* (1998). Defends conceptual analysis and the two-dimensional approach as a way of connecting a priori philosophical reflection to modal truths about the extension of ordinary concepts.

- Williamson, Timothy, "Armchair Philosophy, Metaphysical Modality and Counterfactual Thinking," *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 105 (2005). Argues that modal thinking is a species of counterfactual thinking, grounding modal knowledge in practical reasoning skills.

## Contemporary Works

- Bealer, George, "Modal Epistemology and the Rationalist Renaissance," in *Conceivability and Possibility* edited by Gendler and Hawthorne (2002). Defends the evidential weight of modal intuitions against skeptical challenges, arguing they are truth-tracking responses to modal reality.

- Hanrahan, Rebecca, "Imagination and Possibility," *The Philosophical Forum* 40 (2009). Examines the relationship between imaginative exercise and modal knowledge, with attention to where imaginative exercises can mislead.
