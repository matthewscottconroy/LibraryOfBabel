# Important Figures: Applications of Modal Epistemology

## David Chalmers (born 1966)
Applied modal epistemology most influentially in philosophy of mind, using conceivability arguments to argue for the possibility of phenomenal consciousness without physical realization (the zombie argument), raising deep questions about the relationship between conceivability and metaphysical possibility.

## Alvin Plantinga (1932–present)
Applied modal reasoning extensively in philosophy of religion, most notably in his ontological argument for God's existence, which moves from the conceivability (and hence possibility) of a maximally great being to its necessary existence via S5 modal logic.

## Derek Parfit (1942–2017)
Applied modal reasoning to personal identity and ethics, using thought experiments about fission, teleportation, and spectrum cases to probe the modal structure of personal identity and to argue for a reductionist view of persons.

## Judith Jarvis Thomson (1929–2020)
Developed and used thought experiments—including the famous trolley problem—to probe moral necessities and contingencies, illustrating how modal epistemology applies to ethics as well as metaphysics.
