# References and Primary Sources: Applications of Modal Epistemology

## Foundational Texts

- Chalmers, David, *The Conscious Mind* (1996). The most sustained application of modal epistemology in philosophy of mind; argues from the conceivability of zombies to the possibility of consciousness without physical realization, challenging physicalism.

- Plantinga, Alvin, *The Nature of Necessity* (1974). Applies modal logic and modal epistemology to natural theology, developing a version of the ontological argument using possible-worlds semantics and the S5 principle.

- Parfit, Derek, *Reasons and Persons* (1984), Part III. Uses thought experiments about personal identity—fission, teletransportation, spectrum cases—to establish modal claims about the nature of persons that have consequences for ethics and rationality.

## Contemporary Works

- Chalmers, David, "Does Conceivability Entail Possibility?" in *Conceivability and Possibility* edited by Gendler and Hawthorne (2002). A more careful defense of the conceivability-possibility inference, using two-dimensional semantics to handle apparent counterexamples.

- Sorensen, Roy, *Thought Experiments* (1992). A general treatment of thought experiments in philosophy with attention to their epistemic role in establishing modal claims across domains.
