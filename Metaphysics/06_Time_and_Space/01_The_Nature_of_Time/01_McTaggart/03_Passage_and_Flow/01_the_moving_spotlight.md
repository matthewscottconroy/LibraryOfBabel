# The Moving Spotlight Theory

Suppose we accept that all times exist — that past, present, and future are all equally real parts of a four-dimensional block universe, as special relativity seems to suggest. The B-theorist is satisfied. But many philosophers feel that something important has been left out: the fact that the present moment is not merely the time at which we happen to be located, but the time at which things are *actually happening*, at which the world is *alive and ongoing*. Can we have the block universe and also the genuine present?

The moving spotlight theory says yes. It accepts that all times exist in the four-dimensional block, but holds that one time — the present — is objectively special, marked by the "spotlight" of nowness that moves from earlier to later times. The spotlit moment is genuinely present, not merely the time one happens to occupy.

C.D. Broad introduced a version of this view, and Bradford Skow has defended it in detail in *Objective Becoming* (2015). What makes it attractive is that it tries to avoid the problems besetting its two rivals: unlike presentism, it has straightforward truthmakers for past-tense sentences (past events are real parts of the block); unlike pure B-theory, it gives the present a genuine metaphysical privilege.

## Formal Characterization

We can characterize the moving spotlight view more precisely. The fundamental ontology consists of three elements: a four-dimensional block of spacetime events, related by the permanent B-series relations; an additional monadic property — Nowness or Presentness — that is instantiated by exactly one temporal slice of the block at each "meta-time"; and a meta-temporal series along which the instantiation of Nowness changes, so that at meta-time M₁, Nowness is instantiated by block-time t₁, at M₂ by t₂, and so on. The moving spotlight theorist thus accepts both the block universe of the B-theorist and the objective present of the A-theorist. Passage consists in the movement of the spotlight through the block.

Skow distinguishes "thick" from "thin" presentism. Thick presentism holds that only the present exists; thin presentism merely holds that there is an objective present. The moving spotlight is a form of thin presentism: it adds an objective present to the block without removing the reality of past and future times. This is what allows it to solve the truthmaker problem while affirming the phenomenological datum.

## Skow's Defense

Skow's central datum is what he calls "the vividness of the present" — the phenomenological fact that the present moment feels more vivid, real, and alive than other times. On the B-theory's pure block universe, there is no explanation of why the present should be experienced differently from other times; the B-theorist can only say that this is a perspectival illusion, an artifact of being located at the time in question. On the moving spotlight view, the vividness of the present reflects a genuine metaphysical difference: the present time instantiates Nowness and the others do not.

Skow adopts four-dimensionalism about persistence — objects persist by having temporal stages spread across the block — while adding that the spotlight picks out which stages are presently occurring. This does not change the fact that all stages exist; it adds an objective, moving distinction between the spotlit and the non-spotlit.

## The Meta-Temporal Regress

The most serious objection to the moving spotlight is a regress that mirrors McTaggart's original argument. If the spotlight "moves" — if its position changes — then there must be a temporal structure in which this change occurs. This temporal structure is itself a series of moments, and it is natural to ask whether there is an objective present in this series too. If there is, a second spotlight moves through the meta-series; and then we need a meta-meta-time for that movement, and so on.

Skow's response: the movement of the spotlight is a primitive fact about the world — it is not analyzed in terms of a meta-time, but is simply a brute feature of reality. The world is characterized by both block-structure and genuine becoming, and the becoming is not further reducible. "Movement" is being used loosely; what is really meant is that the distribution of Nowness across block-times is not uniform but concentrated at a single time that changes in a way that is itself primitive and unanalyzed.

Paul Horwich pressed the simpler objection that "movement" is literally incoherent: motion requires moving through something, and the spotlight's "movement through time" requires a second time dimension. Without this second dimension, the spotlight has no position to move from or to. Skow's reply is that this objection applies to a naive reading of the "movement" metaphor, not to the technical view: the technical view says only that Nowness is distributed across the block in a certain non-uniform way, and this distribution is a primitive fact.

Critics find this unsatisfying precisely because it makes the core thesis of the view — the genuine passage of time — into a brute, unexplained posit. Whether this is better or worse than the B-theorist's dismissal of passage as mere illusion is a judgment call, and both sides have genuine resources to deploy.

## Passage and Temporal Experience

The moving spotlight gives a distinctive account of temporal experience. We experience the present as vivid because we are conscious beings whose experiences are occurring at the spotlit time. The pastness of a memory feels like pastness — not like mere earlier-than-now — because the time of the remembered event is no longer spotlit, and this is an objective metaphysical fact.

This is more substantive than the B-theory's explanation, which says: the present feels vivid because you are there, and "being there" is just a B-series relation. The moving spotlight provides a genuinely asymmetric account: there is a real metaphysical difference between being at the spotlit time and being at a non-spotlit time, and this difference grounds the phenomenological asymmetry. Whether that extra metaphysical machinery is worth the cost — particularly the meta-temporal regress it invites — is the central question for anyone evaluating the view.
