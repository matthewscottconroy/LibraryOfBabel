# Exercises and Thought Experiments: McTaggart and the Nature of Time

## Comprehension Questions

1. Explain the difference between the A-series and B-series. Give an example of an A-series claim and a B-series claim about the same event.

2. What is McTaggart's argument for the unreality of time? Identify the key premises and the role of the regress argument.

3. What is the difference between A-theory and B-theory? What does each view say about the "passage" of time?

## Discussion Questions

4. McTaggart argued that the A-series is more fundamental than the B-series because B-series relations (earlier/later) presuppose the A-series. Do you agree? Can you define "earlier" and "later" without reference to past, present, and future?

5. D.C. Williams argued that temporal passage is a "myth." Can the B-theorist explain our felt experience of time passing? Or is the experience irreducible?

6. If the A-theory is correct, what is the "present"? Can it be identified with an objective physical fact, or is it observer-relative?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Block Universe**: Imagine time as a four-dimensional block in which all events coexist. From a vantage point "outside" the block, past, present, and future are all equally visible. Does this picture make sense? Does it eliminate time or just redescribe it?

8. **The Moving Spotlight**: On this view, all times exist but the "spotlight of nowness" moves from earlier to later times. What determines the direction of the spotlight's movement? Could the spotlight move in the opposite direction?

9. **The Shrinking Past**: In presentism, the past no longer exists. But we have memories of the past. What are our memories memories of, if the past doesn't exist?
