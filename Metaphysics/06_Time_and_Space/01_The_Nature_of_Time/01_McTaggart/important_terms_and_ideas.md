# Important Terms and Ideas: McTaggart's Theory of Time

**A-Series**
McTaggart's term for the ordering of events as past, present, or future. A-series properties (pastness, presentness, futurity) change: what is now present will be past. McTaggart argued the A-series is essential to genuine time because change requires it.

**B-Series**
The ordering of events as earlier than, simultaneous with, or later than each other. B-series relations are permanent and do not change: if event A is earlier than event B, this is always the case. Critics and defenders of McTaggart debate whether the B-series alone can constitute real time.

**C-Series**
McTaggart's own positive ontological proposal: beneath apparent temporal order lies a non-temporal C-series of content-related positions. Time as ordinarily understood is a misleading appearance generated by the C-series plus the appearance of change; real reality is atemporal.

**McTaggart's Regress**
McTaggart's argument that A-series properties (past, present, future) are mutually inconsistent (no event can be all three) yet each event must have all three (it was future, is present, will be past). Resolving this by reference to times generates a vicious regress since those times also need A-series properties to be genuinely temporal.

**A-Theory vs. B-Theory**
The central contemporary debate McTaggart's paper initiated: A-theorists (presentists, growing block theorists, moving spotlight theorists) hold that A-series distinctions are fundamental; B-theorists (eternalists, four-dimensionalists) hold that the B-series is sufficient and that A-series talk reduces to B-series facts plus indexicality.

**The Unreality of Time**
McTaggart's conclusion: since the A-series is essential to time but generates a vicious regress that cannot be resolved, time does not genuinely exist. Reality is atemporal; the appearance of time is a misleading manifestation of a deeper, non-temporal structure.
