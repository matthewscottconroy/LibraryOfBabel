# References and Primary Sources: McTaggart's Theory of Time

## Foundational Texts

- McTaggart, J.M.E., "The Unreality of Time," *Mind* 17 (1908). The classic paper distinguishing A-series (past/present/future) from B-series (earlier/later), arguing that the A-series is essential to time but generates a vicious regress, leading to the paradoxical conclusion that time is unreal.

- McTaggart, J.M.E., *The Nature of Existence*, Volume II (1927). The book-length development of his argument, situating it within his idealist metaphysics and his positive account of a C-series underlying apparent temporal order.

- Dummett, Michael, "A Defence of McTaggart's Proof of the Unreality of Time," *The Philosophical Review* 69 (1960). A sympathetic reconstruction of McTaggart's argument, arguing that it is more compelling than typically acknowledged and that the regress argument is not easily dismissed.

## Contemporary Works

- Le Poidevin, Robin and MacBeath, Murray (eds.), *The Philosophy of Time* (1993). An anthology of classic and contemporary papers on the A/B-series distinction, McTaggart's argument, and the major positions in the metaphysics of time.

- Oaklander, L. Nathan and Smith, Quentin (eds.), *The New Theory of Time* (1994). Collects important contemporary exchanges between A-theorists and B-theorists responding to McTaggart's legacy.
