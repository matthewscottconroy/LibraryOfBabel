# The Growing Block Theory

Both presentism and eternalism face problems that seem to arise from their most characteristic commitments. Presentism says the past does not exist, then struggles to explain why truths about the past are true. Eternalism says all times exist equally, then struggles to explain why the present feels different from the past and future. Is there an intermediate position that avoids both difficulties?

The growing block theory says yes. It holds that the past and present are real — events, once they have occurred, continue to exist — but the future does not yet exist. As time passes, new events are "added" to the block, making it grow. The block universe is not static but expanding at its leading edge: the present.

C.D. Broad introduced this view in *Scientific Thought* (1923). His motivation was the intuition that the past is genuinely fixed and determinate while the future is open and indeterminate. Past events cannot be changed; the future is not yet settled. The growing block captures this asymmetry directly: past events are real (and thus determinate); future events are not real (and thus not yet determinate). The block grows as its edge advances.

## Formal Structure

The growing block occupies a precise position in the space of temporal ontologies:

- Presentism: only the present exists (t = now).
- Growing Block: the past and present exist (t ≤ now).
- Shrinking Block (rarely defended): the present and future exist.
- Eternalism: all times exist (all t).

The present is the boundary — the leading edge — where new events are occurring. As time passes, events at the "edge" become past events and new events occur at the new edge. This is what temporal passage amounts to on the growing block: genuine passage, in the sense that the block actually grows as new real events come into being.

## The Truthmaker Advantage

One of the chief advantages of the growing block over presentism is its handling of past-tense truthmakers. On presentism, the past does not exist, so there are no past events to serve as truthmakers for past-tense sentences. On the growing block, past events exist — they are permanently part of the block — and can straightforwardly serve as truthmakers.

"Caesar crossed the Rubicon" is true because the event of Caesar's crossing exists in the growing block, at the appropriate temporal location. The event is past (earlier than the present edge) but still exists. Its existence grounds the truth of the past-tense statement. This is arguably the simplest and most natural truthmaker story available for past-tense truths. No special "presentist truthmakers" or primitive tensed facts are needed.

## Michael Tooley's Development

Michael Tooley (*Time, Tense, and Causation*, 1997) develops a sophisticated version of the growing block he calls "the dynamic theory of time." Tooley argues that the dynamic theory is the only view that takes seriously both the reality of the past and the genuine openness of the future.

Tooley's key contribution is an analysis of what it means for an event to occur: an event's occurring is its coming to be real — its being "added" to the growing block. This is an irreducibly dynamic notion, not analyzable in purely static, B-series terms. The difference between an event that has occurred and one that has not is not merely a matter of their B-series positions but of their ontological status. Tooley also develops a careful account of "tensed facts" — facts about the past, present, and future that are not reducible to non-tensed facts.

## Causation and Physics

The growing block has natural connections to a productive account of causation. Causation is the process by which causes at earlier times produce effects at later times, with the growing block expanding as new effects come into being. The causal nexus is a real, productive relationship between events in the block, not merely a formal correlation. Each new event becomes permanently part of the growing structure once it occurs.

The growing block faces challenges from special relativity analogous to those facing presentism: the "present edge" of the block must be frame-independent for the view to be coherent, but the relativity of simultaneity denies any privileged definition of a global "now." Tooley responds that the growing block's edge is defined by the objective (absolute) present, which exists even if it is not operationally definable within special relativity. This is again a form of neo-Newtonian metaphysics — positing absolute temporal facts that go beyond what physics explicitly recognizes. We return to this challenge in the discussion of problems for the view.
