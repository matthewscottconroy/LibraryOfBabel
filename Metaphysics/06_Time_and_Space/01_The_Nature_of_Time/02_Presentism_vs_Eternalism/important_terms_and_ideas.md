# Important Terms and Ideas: Presentism vs. Eternalism

**Presentism**
The view that only presently existing things exist—past and future objects and events are not real. Presentists take the present moment to have privileged ontological status, making it the totality of what is. The view captures common intuitions about time but faces challenges from relativity.

**Eternalism**
The view that past, present, and future entities all equally exist—time is like space in that all temporal locations are ontologically on a par. On the eternalist "block universe" picture, the universe is a four-dimensional manifold in which all times are real. The appearance of temporal flow is an indexical feature of our perspective.

**Growing Block Theory**
A middle position: the past and present exist but the future does not yet exist. The block of reality grows as new events come into existence at the leading edge of the present. This view accommodates the reality of the past while preserving the intuition that the future is not yet settled.

**Moving Spotlight Theory**
An A-theorist eternalist position: all times exist (eternalism), but there is an objective moving spotlight of presentness that moves from earlier to later times, conferring objective nowness on each time in succession. This combination faces challenges about what grounds the motion of the spotlight.

**Temporal Parts**
On eternalist four-dimensionalism, objects persist by having distinct temporal parts at different times, analogously to having distinct spatial parts at different locations. A person is a four-dimensional worm composed of temporal stages. This view contrasts with endurantism, which holds that objects persist by being wholly present at each time.

**Relativity and Presentism**
Special relativity's relativity of simultaneity threatens presentism: if there is no absolute simultaneity, then what counts as "the present" is frame-relative, and presentism's claim that only the present exists yields different ontological verdicts from different frames. This is widely considered the most serious objection to presentism.
