# Mach's Principle and Inertia

Ernst Mach accepted Newton's empirical phenomena: the rotating water in the bucket becomes concave; rotating bodies feel centrifugal force. These are real. What Mach denied was Newton's explanation. Newton attributed these effects to acceleration relative to absolute space — an unobservable entity that makes no empirical difference to any local experiment. Mach's empiricist critique, developed in *The Science of Mechanics* (1883), is simple and penetrating: the concavity of the water is caused by its acceleration relative to the rest of the matter in the universe — primarily the distant "fixed stars" — not relative to a ghostly, empty framework.

"Mach's Principle" (a term coined by Einstein) is the claim that the local inertial properties of matter — including resistance to acceleration — are determined by the total distribution of mass in the universe. Remove all the matter in the universe except the bucket and the water, Mach implied, and there would be no inertial effects. The "flatness" or "concavity" of the water surface depends on the bucket's acceleration relative to the surrounding matter, not relative to empty space.

Mach writes: "No one is competent to predicate things about absolute space and absolute motion; they are pure things of thought, pure mental constructs, that cannot be produced in experience. All our principles of mechanics are... experimental knowledge concerning the relative positions and motions of bodies." The empiricist argument is: scientific theories should only posit entities whose existence makes an observable difference; absolute position and absolute velocity make no observable difference; therefore, absolute space should not be posited.

## Formulations of Mach's Principle

"Mach's Principle" is not a single claim but a family. At least three distinct versions can be distinguished:

**Weak Mach**: the local inertial frames are determined by the global distribution of matter. This is partially satisfied by general relativity through frame-dragging.

**Strong Mach**: the inertia of any body is entirely determined by its gravitational interaction with all other matter in the universe. In a universe with no other matter, a body would have no inertia. This is not satisfied by standard general relativity, which has vacuum solutions with non-trivial metric and hence non-trivial inertia structure.

**Relational Mach**: there are no absolute quantities (absolute position, velocity, rotation); all physical quantities are relational, defined with respect to the distribution of matter. This is the most radical form and is also not satisfied by standard general relativity, which retains some absolute geometric structure.

## Applied to the Bucket

On Mach's view, the concavity of the water results from the water's rotation relative to the entire distribution of matter in the universe. The fixed stars — the distant galaxies, clusters, and the cosmic background radiation — contribute to the inertial frame at any local point, through a form of gravitational interaction.

The striking prediction: if the universe contained only the bucket and water, with no other matter, there would be no inertial effects. The water surface would remain flat regardless of any rotation, because there is nothing to rotate relative to. Newton would say the surface becomes concave (if the water rotates absolutely); Mach would say it stays flat (if there is no matter relative to which rotation is defined). This prediction cannot be tested directly, but it represents a genuine empirical difference between Newton's and Mach's views.

## Mach's Principle in Modern Physics

Einstein's general theory of relativity was designed, in part, to satisfy Mach's principle. Einstein's field equations G_μν = 8πG/c⁴ T_μν couple the geometry of spacetime to the distribution of matter-energy. The most dramatic expression of Machian influence in GR is frame-dragging: a massive rotating body "drags" the spacetime around it, causing the inertial frames near the body to rotate slightly with respect to the distant stars. This has been confirmed experimentally (Gravity Probe B, 2011) and represents a genuine Machian effect.

However, GR does not fully satisfy Mach's principle. The theory allows for vacuum solutions — spacetime with no matter — that have non-trivial metric structure and well-defined inertial frames. A truly Machian theory would have no spacetime structure in the absence of matter. It also allows the Gödel rotating universe, in which matter rotates but the inertial frames are not dragged along with it; and the Minkowski vacuum solution, in which inertia is fully defined even with no matter present.

These solutions suggest that in GR, spacetime has its own degrees of freedom — gravitational waves, the vacuum metric structure — that are not determined by the matter distribution alone. Spacetime is not merely a relational structure derived from matter but a dynamical entity in its own right. Contemporary philosophers of physics debate Mach's principle in the context of quantum gravity, where the relationship between local inertia and global matter distribution remains an open question. Julian Barbour and Bruno Bertotti developed a fully Machian classical mechanics — the "Barbour-Bertotti" model — that reproduces Newtonian predictions without positing any absolute space or time. Whether a complete quantum theory of gravity will be fully Machian remains open.
