# Exercises and Thought Experiments: The Nature of Space

## Comprehension Questions

1. Explain Newton's bucket argument. What conclusion does Newton draw from the concavity of the water's surface?

2. State Leibniz's two arguments against absolute space (from the Principle of Sufficient Reason and from the Identity of Indiscernibles). How do they connect?

3. What is Mach's principle, and how does it differ from Newtonian and Leibnizian views?

## Discussion Questions

4. If relationism is true and space consists only of relations among objects, what would happen to space if all objects were annihilated? Would space disappear, or is this question meaningless for the relationist?

5. Newton's bucket argument is sometimes thought to support absolute space because the concavity of the water can only be explained by rotation relative to space, not relative to the bucket. But could the Machian relationist respond adequately? What would they say?

6. General relativity makes spacetime dynamic — it curves in response to matter. Does this support substantivalism (spacetime is a real entity that responds to matter) or relationalism (spacetime is constituted by material fields and their relations)?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Empty Universe**: Imagine a universe with two identical spheres and nothing else. Leibniz claims there is no fact about their absolute positions. Newton claims there is a fact about their absolute velocities. Who is right, and how would we know?

8. **Shifting the Universe**: Suppose the entire physical universe were shifted three feet to the north in absolute space. Would anything be different? If not, is absolute position real?

9. **Rotation Without Matter**: Could there be rotation in a universe with no matter? What would "rotation" mean in such a universe? How do absolutists and relationalists answer differently?
