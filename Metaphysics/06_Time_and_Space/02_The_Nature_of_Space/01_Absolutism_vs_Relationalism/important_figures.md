# Important Figures: Absolutism Versus Relationalism

**Isaac Newton (1643–1727)** defended absolute space and time in the *Principia Mathematica* (1687). His bucket argument is the central piece of evidence for absolute space.

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)** developed the relationalist view in the Leibniz-Clarke correspondence (1715–1716). His arguments from the Principle of Sufficient Reason and Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles remain the classic critique of absolutism.

**Ernst Mach (1838–1916)** developed empiricist relationalism in *The Science of Mechanics* (1883), arguing that inertial effects arise from rotation relative to the distant matter in the universe.

**Henri Poincaré (1854–1912)** argued for conventionalism about geometry: the choice between geometrical theories is conventional, not empirical.

**Albert Einstein (1879–1955)** developed special and general relativity, which transformed the debate by making spacetime dynamic (general relativity) and relative (special relativity). His correspondence on the hole argument illuminates his thinking about spacetime ontology.

**John Earman (b. 1942)** has written extensively on the philosophy of spacetime, including *World Enough and Space-Time* (1989), which is the definitive treatment of absolutism, relationalism, and the hole argument.

**Tim Maudlin (b. 1958)** defends a sophisticated substantivalism about spacetime in *Quantum Non-Locality and Relativity* and *The Metaphysics Within Physics* (2007).
