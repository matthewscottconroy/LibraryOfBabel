# Important Terms and Ideas: Absolutism vs. Relationalism about Space

**Absolute Space**
Newton's view that space is a real, immovable, non-relational container that exists independently of the objects within it and provides the framework for absolute motion and rest. Absolute space is not constituted by spatial relations between bodies but underlies them.

**Relationalism about Space**
The view, associated with Leibniz, that space consists entirely in spatial relations between objects—there is no space "between" objects over and above their standing in various distance and directional relations to each other. If there were no objects, there would be no space.

**Substantivalism**
The modern descendant of Newtonianism: spacetime is a genuine substance or entity in its own right, not merely a system of relations. Points of spacetime have independent existence and can be quantified over. Substantivalism contrasts with relationalism and faces the "hole argument" as a challenge.

**Newton's Bucket Argument**
Newton's argument for absolute space: a rotating bucket of water develops a concave surface, and this concavity persists even when the bucket is still relative to surrounding objects. The concavity tracks absolute rotation—rotation with respect to absolute space itself, not merely relative to other bodies.

**Leibniz's Shift Arguments**
Leibniz argues against absolute space using shift scenarios: if the entire universe were displaced or rotated in absolute space, no observable difference would result. By the Principle of Sufficient Reason and Principle of Identity of Indiscernibles, there can be no difference, so absolute positions do not exist.

**Mach's Principle**
The idea that inertial properties of matter (resistance to acceleration, inertial forces like centrifugal force) are determined by the distribution of all matter in the universe, not by relation to absolute space. Einstein drew on this principle in developing general relativity, though GR does not fully implement Mach's Principle.
