# References and Primary Sources: Absolutism vs. Relationalism about Space

## Foundational Texts

- Newton, Isaac, *Principia Mathematica*, Scholium to the Definitions (1687). Introduces absolute space as a real, immovable background structure independent of bodies and their relations; the rotating bucket argument is deployed to motivate absolute space.

- Leibniz, G.W. and Clarke, Samuel, *The Leibniz-Clarke Correspondence* (1715–16). The canonical debate between absolutism and relationalism; Leibniz argues that absolute space violates the Principle of Sufficient Reason and the Principle of Identity of Indiscernibles, while Clarke defends Newton.

- Mach, Ernst, *The Science of Mechanics* (1883). Critiques Newton's absolute space and anticipates relationalism by arguing that inertial effects are relative to the distribution of all matter in the universe (Mach's Principle), influencing Einstein's development of general relativity.

## Contemporary Works

- Earman, John, *World Enough and Space-Time* (1989). A rigorous treatment of the substantivalism/relationalism debate using modern mathematics and physics, distinguishing several versions of each position and assessing their merits.

- Huggett, Nick and Hoefer, Carl, "Absolute and Relational Theories of Space and Motion," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2018). A comprehensive survey of the debate from Newton to contemporary physics.
