# Minkowski Spacetime

In a lecture delivered in Cologne in 1908, Hermann Minkowski opened with an announcement: "Henceforth space by itself, and time by itself, are doomed to fade away into mere shadows, and only a kind of union of the two will preserve an independent reality." Einstein had in 1905 derived the special theory of relativity from two postulates — the equivalence of inertial frames and the constancy of the speed of light. Minkowski showed that these results are most naturally understood as expressing the geometry of a four-dimensional spacetime: the Lorentz transformations are simply rotations in this four-dimensional space, and the invariance of the speed of light is a consequence of the invariant spacetime interval s² = c²Δt² − Δx² − Δy² − Δz².

This reformulation was not merely a technical convenience. It was a conceptual transformation. Newton's mechanics had treated space and time as separate and absolute: three-dimensional Euclidean space provided the arena, and universal time provided the parameter. Minkowski spacetime is a single four-dimensional structure in which spatial and temporal separations are aspects of a unified interval. What one observer calls "spatial distance" and "temporal duration," another observer (moving relative to the first) describes differently — the two coordinates mix under Lorentz transformation. The physical content — the invariant interval — is the same for both.

This mixing has philosophical consequences that go well beyond the technical formalism. Simultaneity is relative: two events that are simultaneous in one inertial frame are not simultaneous in another. The "present" — the surface of simultaneous events — varies with the observer's state of motion. If existence is tied to the present (as presentism holds), then existence varies with the observer's velocity — a deeply counterintuitive consequence. The most natural interpretation of Minkowski spacetime is the block universe: all events at all times are equally real, and "now" is a perspectival, not an objective, category.

The files in this section examine Minkowski spacetime, the relativity of simultaneity and its implications for temporal ontology, and the broader philosophical upshots of special relativity — for the metaphysics of persistence, for the reality of the present, and for the relationship between causal structure and temporal order. The spacetime interval, the light cone structure, the twin paradox resolved geometrically, and the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for the block universe are the central topics.
