# Important Terms and Ideas: Special Relativity and Philosophy

**Relativity of Simultaneity**
Einstein's result that whether two spatially separated events are simultaneous is not absolute but depends on the inertial frame of reference. Events simultaneous in one frame are not simultaneous in another moving relative to it. This undermines the Newtonian conception of an absolute present moment.

**Minkowski Spacetime**
The four-dimensional geometric structure of special relativity, with three spatial dimensions and one temporal dimension combined into a single manifold. The geometry is pseudo-Riemannian with a Lorentzian signature, and the spacetime interval between events is the invariant quantity replacing absolute space and time separately.

**Spacetime Interval**
The invariant quantity ds² = c²dt² − dx² − dy² − dz² that all inertial observers agree on, despite disagreeing on spatial and temporal separations individually. The interval is timelike (for events connectable by slower-than-light travel), spacelike (for events connectable only by faster-than-light signals), or null (for events on the light cone).

**Lorentz Transformations**
The coordinate transformations relating measurements in different inertial frames in special relativity. They replace the Galilean transformations and entail time dilation (moving clocks run slow) and length contraction (moving objects are shorter in the direction of motion).

**Block Universe**
The philosophical interpretation of Minkowski spacetime as a static four-dimensional block in which all events—past, present, and future—equally exist. On this view, "now" is merely a perspectival indexical, and temporal becoming is not a fundamental feature of reality.

**Light Cone**
The structure in Minkowski spacetime defining the causal relations between events: the past light cone of an event contains all events that could have causally influenced it; the future light cone contains all events it could causally influence. Events outside both light cones are causally disconnected.
