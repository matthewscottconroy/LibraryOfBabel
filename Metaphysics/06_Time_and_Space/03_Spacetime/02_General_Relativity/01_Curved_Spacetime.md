# Curved Spacetime and General Relativity

Special relativity unified space and time into a single four-dimensional structure — Minkowski spacetime — and showed that the geometry of that structure is what explains the invariant structure of physical laws across inertial frames. But Minkowski spacetime is flat: its geometry is fixed, uniform, and indifferent to the matter it contains. Gravity, on this picture, remained unexplained — it was still a force, acting at a distance, added to the relativistic framework from outside rather than emerging from it.

Einstein's general relativity (1915) resolved this by a move of extraordinary conceptual boldness: it identified gravity with the curvature of spacetime. There is no gravitational force. What we call "gravitational attraction" is the motion of bodies along the straightest possible paths — geodesics — through a spacetime whose geometry is curved by the presence of matter and energy. The freely falling body is not accelerating; it is following its natural inertial path. What is accelerating is the observer standing on the ground, being pushed away from the geodesic they would naturally follow.

The Einstein field equations G_μν = (8πG/c⁴) T_μν express this concisely and exactly: the curvature of spacetime at each event is proportional to the matter-energy content at that event. Spacetime is no longer an inert background; it is a dynamical entity that interacts with matter and carries its own physical degrees of freedom.

The philosophical implications are substantial and run in several directions. The substantivalism-relationalism debate takes on new form: spacetime is no longer the fixed Newtonian container but a dynamical field that responds to matter — yet it has degrees of freedom of its own, as gravitational waves attest. The hole argument, which Einstein himself discovered during the construction of GR, shows that a naive reading of the manifold structure leads to radical indeterminism and must be resisted by any adequate interpretation. And the dynamic, curved geometry of GR puts pressure on every view of temporal ontology — presentism, eternalism, growing block — that was developed in the context of either Newtonian mechanics or flat Minkowski spacetime.

The files in this section examine spacetime curvature and the Einstein equations, the substantivalism-relationalism debate as it plays out in the context of GR, and the hole argument in detail.
