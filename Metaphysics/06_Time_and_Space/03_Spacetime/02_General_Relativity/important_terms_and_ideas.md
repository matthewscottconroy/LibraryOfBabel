# Important Terms and Ideas: General Relativity and Philosophy

**Spacetime Curvature**
In general relativity, gravity is not a force but the curvature of spacetime caused by the presence of mass-energy. Free-falling objects follow geodesics—the curved spacetime analogues of straight lines. The Einstein field equations relate the curvature of spacetime to the distribution of mass-energy.

**Metric Field**
The mathematical structure gμν that determines the geometry of spacetime—lengths, angles, and causal relations between events. In GR the metric field is a dynamical entity subject to physical laws (the Einstein equations), unlike in special relativity where the Minkowski metric is a fixed background.

**The Hole Argument**
Earman and Norton's argument against point-substantivalism: in GR, the field equations underdetermine which physical fields occupy which spacetime points (one can drag all physical fields smoothly within a region of spacetime, the "hole," to get a new but empirically equivalent solution). If spacetime points are individuals, this creates indeterminism; avoiding it requires relationalism or sophisticated substantivalism.

**Diffeomorphism Invariance**
GR is formulated so that the physical content is invariant under smooth coordinate transformations (diffeomorphisms). This invariance is thought to imply that only relational, not absolute, spatiotemporal facts have physical significance—though interpreting this implication is contested.

**Geodesic**
The path through curved spacetime that an object follows when no non-gravitational forces act on it—the generalization of a straight line to curved geometry. In GR, free-falling objects and light rays follow geodesics, with gravity understood as geodesic deviation caused by spacetime curvature.

**Background Independence**
The property of GR that distinguishes it from pre-relativistic theories: there is no fixed, non-dynamical background spacetime. The metric field is itself dynamical and interacts with matter. Background independence is a key desideratum for quantum gravity theories.
