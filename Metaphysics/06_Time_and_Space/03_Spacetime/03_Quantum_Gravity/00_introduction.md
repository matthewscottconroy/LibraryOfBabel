# Quantum Gravity and the Nature of Spacetime

The two great physical theories of the twentieth century — general relativity and quantum mechanics — are mutually inconsistent. General relativity describes gravity as the smooth curvature of spacetime; quantum mechanics describes other forces through discrete excitations of quantum fields. Attempts to quantize gravity generate infinities that cannot be renormalized by the techniques that work for other forces. The search for a theory of quantum gravity — a theory that unifies the smooth geometry of general relativity with the discrete structure of quantum mechanics — is the central project of theoretical physics.

The philosophical implications of quantum gravity for the metaphysics of spacetime are potentially revolutionary. If spacetime is itself a quantum system, then the smooth, continuous spacetime of general relativity is an approximation, valid at large scales but breaking down at the Planck scale. At the smallest scales, spacetime itself may be granular, discrete, or fundamentally quantum in a way that makes the classical description inapplicable. The question of what spacetime is at the fundamental level becomes as much a philosophical as a physical question.

Loop quantum gravity and string theory are the two leading research programs in quantum gravity, and they offer strikingly different pictures of the fundamental structure of spacetime. Loop quantum gravity suggests that spacetime is built out of discrete quanta of space — the "spin networks" and "spin foams" of the theory. String theory suggests that strings are the fundamental entities and spacetime is emergent from their dynamics, with the possibility of extra compact dimensions beyond the four we experience. Causal set theory suggests that the fundamental structure of reality is a discrete partial order of "causal atoms" from which spacetime geometry emerges at large scales.

For the metaphysician, the lesson of quantum gravity research is that our current concepts of space, time, and geometry are provisional at best. This section examines the philosophical implications of quantum gravity, both for the metaphysics of spacetime and for broader questions about emergence and the relationship between fundamental and derived structure.
