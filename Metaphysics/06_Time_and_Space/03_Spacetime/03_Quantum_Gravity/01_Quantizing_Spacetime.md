# Quantum Gravity and the Metaphysics of Spacetime

General relativity and quantum mechanics are the two most precisely confirmed theories in the history of physics. Each works extraordinarily well in its own domain. General relativity describes the large-scale structure of spacetime — the curvature generated by massive bodies, the expansion of the universe, the propagation of gravitational waves — with extraordinary precision. Quantum mechanics (and its offspring, quantum field theory) describes the behavior of matter at atomic and subatomic scales — with predictions confirmed to one part in ten billion in some cases.

The problem is that the two theories are fundamentally incompatible. General relativity describes gravity as the curvature of a smooth, continuous spacetime manifold, governed by deterministic field equations. Quantum mechanics describes physical systems in terms of superpositions, probabilistic measurement outcomes, and non-commuting observables defined on a fixed background spacetime. When one tries to construct a quantum theory of gravity — to apply quantum mechanics to the gravitational field itself — the two frameworks conflict at the level of their basic concepts. Quantum mechanics needs a fixed background time; GR has no fixed background. Quantum mechanics requires a Hilbert space defined on a fixed spacetime background; GR's spacetime is itself a dynamical variable that cannot serve as a fixed background.

This is not a problem that more experimental data will resolve in the near term: the scale at which quantum gravitational effects are expected to become important — the Planck scale, l_P ≈ 10⁻³⁵ m — is twenty orders of magnitude smaller than the proton diameter and far beyond any foreseeable experimental technology. The search for quantum gravity is a theoretical endeavor, guided by consistency requirements, mathematical beauty, and philosophical principle.

Two leading approaches — loop quantum gravity and string theory — make radically different proposals for what the fundamental ontology of spacetime is, and various programs in emergent spacetime suggest that spacetime may not be fundamental at all but may emerge from a pre-geometric quantum structure. The files in this section examine the quantum gravity problem, loop quantum gravity and its discrete spacetime geometry, and the idea of emergent spacetime — with attention throughout to what these programs imply for the metaphysics of space, time, and causation.
