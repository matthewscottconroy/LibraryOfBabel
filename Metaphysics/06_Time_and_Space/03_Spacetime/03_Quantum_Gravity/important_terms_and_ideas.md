# Important Terms and Ideas: Quantum Gravity and Philosophy

**The Problem of Time**
A deep conceptual problem in quantum gravity arising from the fact that general relativity treats time as dynamical and background-independent while quantum mechanics relies on a fixed external time parameter. Reconciling these frameworks requires rethinking the nature of time at the fundamental level.

**Loop Quantum Gravity**
A background-independent approach to quantum gravity that quantizes spacetime itself, suggesting that space has a discrete structure at the Planck scale. In LQG, the fabric of space is woven from discrete quanta—spin networks and spin foams—rather than a continuous manifold.

**String Theory**
The leading candidate for a unified theory of quantum gravity and particle physics, postulating that fundamental entities are one-dimensional strings rather than point particles. String theory requires extra spatial dimensions and has generated rich mathematical and philosophical discussions about the nature of spacetime.

**Background Independence**
The requirement that a theory of quantum gravity should not presuppose a fixed spacetime background, since spacetime itself is what the theory aims to explain. GR is background-independent; standard quantum field theory is not; achieving background independence in quantum gravity is a major conceptual challenge.

**Spacetime Emergence**
The hypothesis that classical spacetime—smooth, continuous, and approximately described by GR—emerges from some more fundamental non-spatiotemporal structure in the quantum gravitational regime. What this non-spatiotemporal substrate is and how spacetime emerges from it is a central open question.

**Planck Scale**
The scale (~10⁻³⁵ meters, ~10⁻⁴³ seconds) at which quantum gravitational effects are expected to become significant. At the Planck scale, the smooth spacetime of GR is expected to break down, giving way to quantum fluctuations of spacetime geometry. Physics at this scale is inaccessible to current experiment.
