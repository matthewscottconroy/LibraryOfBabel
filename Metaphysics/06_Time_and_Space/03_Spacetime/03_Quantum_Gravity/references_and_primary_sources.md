# References and Primary Sources: Quantum Gravity and Philosophy

## Foundational Texts

- Rovelli, Carlo, *Quantum Gravity* (2004). The leading technical and conceptual introduction to loop quantum gravity, with philosophical discussions of what quantizing spacetime implies about the nature of space, time, and geometry.

- Rovelli, Carlo, "Forget Time," *Foundations of Physics* 41 (2011). A philosophical paper arguing that time is not fundamental in the quantum gravitational regime and exploring what a timeless physics would mean for our understanding of physical reality.

- Barbour, Julian, *The End of Time: The Next Revolution in Physics* (1999). Argues for a timeless formulation of physics in which what we call time is an emergent feature of the quantum state of the universe, not a fundamental background structure.

## Contemporary Works

- Huggett, Nick and Wüthrich, Christian (eds.), *Beyond Spacetime: The Foundations of Quantum Gravity* (2018). Collects philosophical papers examining how space and time might emerge from or be replaced by something more fundamental in quantum gravity.

- Callender, Craig and Huggett, Nick (eds.), *Physics Meets Philosophy at the Planck Scale* (2001). An early interdisciplinary anthology on the philosophical significance of quantum gravity, including discussions of background independence, the problem of time, and the nature of quantum spacetime.
