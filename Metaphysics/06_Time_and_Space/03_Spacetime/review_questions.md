# Review Questions — Spacetime

1. Minkowski showed that special relativity is most naturally understood as a four-dimensional geometry in which the spacetime interval — not spatial distance or temporal duration separately — is the invariant quantity. What philosophical work does this geometric reframing do beyond the purely mathematical? In particular, does reinterpreting SR as a geometry of spacetime settle, deepen, or simply restate the metaphysical debate between A-theorists and B-theorists about the block universe?

2. The Rietdijk-Putnam argument concludes, from the relativity of simultaneity and the frame-equivalence principle, that all spacetime events exist equally — the block universe. Its key premise is that existence cannot be frame-relative. Neo-Newtonian presentists deny this by positing a privileged metaphysical frame; relativistic presentists try to redefine "the present" using light cones rather than simultaneity surfaces. What specifically goes wrong with the light-cone redefinition, and does the neo-Newtonian move introduce a worse problem than it solves?

3. General relativity abolishes the fixed background spacetime of special relativity: the metric becomes a dynamical field, coupling to matter through the Einstein field equations. Harvey Brown argues this means the geometric structure of spacetime is not primitive but is "read off" from the symmetry properties of matter laws. Maudlin replies that Brown conflates epistemology with ontology. Reconstruct this dispute as precisely as you can. Who has the better of it, and what additional evidence or argument would be needed to settle the matter?

4. The hole argument exploits diffeomorphism invariance to show that manifold substantivalism entails radical indeterminism inside a matter-free region. Sophisticated substantivalism dissolves this by denying that points have intrinsic identities ("haecceities"). The chapter notes a tension between this response and the growing block theory of time: sophisticated substantivalism denies that points have identities beyond their structural roles, yet growing block requires that past spacetime points persist with their identities intact. Spell out this tension and assess whether it constitutes a genuine problem for anyone who wants to hold both positions.

5. The Wheeler-DeWitt equation, which arises when one attempts to quantize GR in Hamiltonian form, is H|ψ⟩ = 0: the quantum state of the universe does not evolve. Barbour concludes that time is not fundamental — temporal succession is an appearance generated by correlations within a static quantum state. Rovelli's relational approach instead takes time to be relational between physical subsystems. Compare these two responses to the problem of time in quantum gravity. What does each view imply about the A/B theory debate and about the reality of temporal passage?

6. Loop quantum gravity implies that area and volume are quantized at the Planck scale — space is granular, not continuous. The theory is explicitly background independent: there is no spacetime prior to the spin network; the spin network is the quantum geometry. In what sense, if any, does LQG vindicate Leibnizian relationalism? Does the fact that nodes in a spin network are individuated by their relational properties (not intrinsic haecceities) mean that LQG is committed to a relational rather than substantivalist ontology of spacetime?

7. The AdS/CFT correspondence suggests that the geometry of a bulk spacetime is encoded in — and in some sense constituted by — the entanglement structure of a boundary quantum field theory. Mark Van Raamsdonk's result implies that removing entanglement between boundary regions disconnects the corresponding bulk regions: spacetime connectivity is a function of quantum entanglement. If this picture is correct, what are the implications for the debates about substantivalism and relationalism conducted at the level of classical GR? Do those debates concern fundamental structure, or merely an emergent description?

8. The chapter distinguishes weak emergence (in-principle derivability from a base theory) from strong emergence (not derivable even in principle). For spacetime emerging from quantum gravity, the answer appears to be weak emergence: bulk geometry is encoded in boundary data and can in principle be reconstructed from it. But the reconstruction procedures break down for generic quantum gravity states. Does this mean that spacetime concepts apply only in restricted near-classical regimes, and if so, what does this imply for the significance of the A/B theory debate and the presentism/eternalism debate as philosophical questions about the nature of time?
