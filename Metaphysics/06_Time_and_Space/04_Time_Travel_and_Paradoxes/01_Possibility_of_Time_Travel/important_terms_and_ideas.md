# Important Terms and Ideas: The Possibility of Time Travel

**Personal Time vs. External Time**
Lewis's distinction: personal time is measured along the traveler's worldline (how old the traveler gets), while external time is the coordinate time of the world. A time traveler can arrive at an external time earlier than they departed while having aged in the forward direction of their personal time. This distinction dissolves the apparent contradiction in time travel.

**Closed Timelike Curves (CTCs)**
Paths in spacetime that return to their starting point while always moving forward in local time. Gödel showed that GR permits spacetime solutions containing CTCs; a traveler following a CTC would return to their own past. Whether CTCs are physically realizable is a major question in physics and philosophy.

**Grandfather Paradox**
The apparent impossibility generated by imagining a time traveler who kills their own grandfather before the grandfather had children, thereby preventing the time traveler's existence and hence the murder. Lewis resolves this by distinguishing what the traveler "can" do simpliciter from what they can do given the full facts about the situation.

**Novikov Self-Consistency Principle**
The principle that only self-consistent histories are physically possible when CTCs are present: the past cannot be changed by a time traveler, because the traveler's actions are already part of the actual history. On this view, time travel is possible only if the traveler does things that were always going to happen.

**Chronology Protection Conjecture**
Hawking's conjecture that the laws of physics conspire to prevent the formation of CTCs—that quantum effects create divergences that destroy any incipient time machine before it can be completed. If correct, this would mean time travel to the past is physically impossible even if logically coherent.
