# References and Primary Sources: The Possibility of Time Travel

## Foundational Texts

- Lewis, David, "The Paradoxes of Time Travel," *American Philosophical Quarterly* 13 (1976). The canonical philosophical defense of the coherence of time travel; distinguishes personal from external time, resolves the grandfather paradox through the concept of ability, and argues that time travel is logically possible.

- Gödel, Kurt, "An Example of a New Type of Cosmological Solutions of Einstein's Field Equations of Gravitation," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 21 (1949). Presents the Gödel universe—a solution to Einstein's equations containing closed timelike curves—demonstrating that general relativity is consistent with backward time travel.

- Earman, John, Smeenk, Chris, and Wüthrich, Christian, "Do the Laws of Physics Forbid the Operation of Time Machines?" *Synthese* 169 (2009). A careful examination of whether physical laws rule out time machines and closed timelike curves.

## Contemporary Works

- Smeenk, Chris and Wüthrich, Christian, "Time Travel and Time Machines," in *The Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Time* (2011). A thorough survey of the physics and philosophy of time travel, covering closed timelike curves, chronology protection, and the major paradoxes.

- Nahin, Paul, *Time Machines: Time Travel in Physics, Metaphysics, and Science Fiction* (1999). An encyclopedic treatment of time travel covering both scientific and philosophical dimensions.
