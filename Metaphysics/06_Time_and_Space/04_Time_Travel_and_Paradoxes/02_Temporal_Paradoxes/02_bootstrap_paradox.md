# The Bootstrap Paradox

Here is a scenario that does not generate a logical contradiction but does generate a philosophical puzzle that is in some ways deeper than the grandfather paradox. A time traveler discovers, in their own time, a complete copy of Shakespeare's plays. They travel back to Elizabethan England and present the copy to Shakespeare. Shakespeare reads the plays, copies them out, publishes them under his name, and they become the famous works that centuries later our traveler finds and brings back to give to Shakespeare. The causal chain is closed:

I at t₁ → [transmitted to traveler] → I at t₂ → [transmitted back] → I at t₁

The information in the plays has no external origin. It was never created by any act of original authorship; it simply circulates in the loop. No contradiction arises — the history is internally consistent, every token of the plays is causally explained by the previous token in the loop. But something seems wrong. Where did the information come from? It exists, but it has no origin. This is the Bootstrap Paradox, and its challenge is not to logic but to our conception of explanation and causal origination.

## The Paradox Is Not the Grandfather Paradox

It is worth being clear about what the bootstrap paradox is not. The grandfather paradox poses a logical impossibility: an event whose occurrence implies its own non-occurrence. A self-consistent history cannot include Tim killing his grandfather before his father is born, because that would eliminate Tim, who is already in the history. The bootstrap paradox poses no such impossibility. A history in which the plays circulate in a causal loop is perfectly consistent: there is no event whose occurrence implies its own non-occurrence. Every event in the loop is the consequence of previous events in the loop, and the loop is closed.

The distinction matters. If the bootstrap paradox were a logical paradox, it would show that time travel is impossible. Since it is not — since the loop is consistent — it shows instead that our concepts of explanation, origination, and causation may need revision when applied to CTC spacetimes. The paradox is what we might call an ontological puzzle rather than a logical contradiction: not "this cannot be" but "this is puzzling in a way that demands philosophical analysis."

## The Principle of Sufficient Reason Under Pressure

The most direct way to articulate the puzzle is through the Principle of Sufficient Reason. In its strong form — the form Leibniz deployed against Newton — the PSR says that every fact has an explanation, and explanations that merely push the question back are not genuine explanations. The bootstrap loop seems to violate this principle at the level of the loop as a whole. Each token of the plays within the loop has a causal explanation — the plays at t₂ are explained by the plays at t₁ via the time traveler's copying, and the plays at t₁ are explained by the plays at t₂ via the time traveler's delivery. But the loop itself — the existence of the information in this causal structure at all, rather than not existing — has no explanation external to itself.

Lewis was comparatively sanguine about this. On his view, consistency is sufficient for metaphysical possibility, and the bootstrap loop is consistent. The demand for an explanation external to the loop is a demand that need not be met: the loop is a brute fact in the same way that the initial state of the universe is a brute fact on standard non-time-travel cosmology. We do not reject the Big Bang model on the grounds that the initial state of the universe lacks an external causal explanation; why should we reject bootstrap loops on the analogous grounds?

The response to Lewis is that there is a difference between the initial state of the universe being unexplained and a structured piece of information being uncreated. The initial state of the universe is not the kind of thing that admits of causal explanation by its very nature — it is the starting point. The plays, by contrast, are the kind of thing that is always produced by a creative process: syntax organized to carry semantic content, authored by a person who invented it. The bootstrap loop deprives the plays of exactly the kind of explanation they are the kind of thing to have. This is not a logical objection but a metaphysical one: the plays belong to a kind whose instances have explanations of a specific form, and the bootstrap loop fails to provide that form of explanation.

## Grounding and Circular Dependence

Contemporary metaphysics provides another way to press the puzzle. Grounding is the relation of metaphysical explanation: A grounds B when B holds in virtue of A, when A is what makes B the case. Standard accounts of grounding take it to be asymmetric (if A grounds B, then B does not ground A) and well-founded (there are no infinite descending chains of grounding, and no circular grounding structures). These two features together imply that the grounding structure of reality is hierarchical: everything is either fundamental (ungrounded) or explained by things closer to the fundamental level.

The bootstrap loop challenges both features. If the plays at t₁ are grounded (in part) in the plays at t₂, and the plays at t₂ are grounded (in part) in the plays at t₁, we have a circular grounding structure: each grounds the other. This is prohibited on the standard account. The question is whether the prohibition reflects a deep metaphysical truth or merely an assumption built into our default framework.

Jonathan Schaffer and others who defend strict hierarchical grounding conclude that bootstrap loops are metaphysically impossible, even if logically consistent. The impossibility is not a logical contradiction but a violation of the structural requirements on grounding. Critics of this position argue that grounding theory should not be allowed to rule out scenarios that are logically and causally coherent. Circular structures of dependence may be unusual, but unusual is not impossible.

Notice the connection to other philosophical debates. The question of whether circular grounding is possible arises in discussions of divine self-causation (whether God can be causa sui), in debates about whether the laws of nature are grounded in their instances or their instances in the laws, and in discussions of coherentist theories of justification (where beliefs can mutually support each other without any being foundational). The bootstrap paradox is a vivid instance of a general question about the permissibility of circular explanatory structures.

## The Information Question

A more technical formulation of the puzzle concerns information conservation. In standard quantum mechanics, evolution is unitary: information is neither created nor destroyed, only transformed. A bootstrap loop seems to involve information that has no source — it appears "for free" in the loop, with no point at which it was generated from a state that lacked it. Whether this constitutes a violation of information conservation depends on how "conservation" is understood in a CTC spacetime, and this is not a settled question.

Hawking argued that quantum effects near closed timelike curves might suppress or dissolve bootstrap loops by causing information to be degraded in the loop. This is connected to the broader question of whether the Chronology Protection Conjecture eliminates CTCs entirely. If CTCs cannot form, bootstrap loops cannot occur, and the paradox is physically moot. But as we noted earlier, the conjecture is unproven, and the philosophical analysis of what would be the case if CTCs existed remains valuable independently.

The most defensible conclusion is this: the bootstrap paradox is a genuine philosophical puzzle, not a logical impossibility. Consistent causal loops are compatible with the principle of non-contradiction; they challenge a strong form of the PSR and standard accounts of grounding; they place pressure on our intuitions about creative origination and the explanatory requirements on kinds of objects. Whether these pressures constitute a decisive objection to bootstrap causal loops depends on how seriously one takes the PSR and the hierarchical grounding thesis — both of which are contested metametaphysical positions. What the paradox does, regardless of its resolution, is illuminate the connections among causation, explanation, grounding, and the structure of time in a way that no purely temporal example could achieve.
