# Exercises and Thought Experiments: Temporal Paradoxes

## Comprehension Questions

1. What is the Grandfather Paradox and the Bootstrap Paradox? Distinguish them carefully.

2. Explain the thermodynamic arrow of time. Why does entropy tend to increase? What is the "Past Hypothesis" and why is it needed?

3. What is the causal arrow of time? How does Lewis derive the asymmetry of causation from the asymmetry of overdetermination?

## Discussion Questions

4. Huw Price argues that the causal arrow is perspectival: it reflects our position as embedded agents rather than an objective feature of the world. If true, what follows for our understanding of causation?

5. Boltzmann's statistical mechanics explains why entropy increases but predicts that entropy should also have been increasing in the very remote past — which contradicts the low entropy beginning of the universe. How should we respond to this tension?

6. Could there be a universe where time runs "backward" — where entropy decreases rather than increases? What would it be like to live in such a universe?

## Thought Experiments

7. **The Boltzmann Brain**: Statistical mechanics allows, with astronomically low probability, for entropy to spontaneously decrease — for an ordered brain to fluctuate into existence from chaos. Is your current experience consistent with being a Boltzmann Brain? What are the epistemological implications?

8. **The Maxwell's Demon**: Imagine a demon that sorts fast and slow molecules, creating temperature differences (decreasing entropy) without doing work. This violates the second law. What does this reveal about the law's status?

9. **The Time-Reversed Movie**: Watch a film played backward. Broken cups reassemble, smoke flows into cigarettes. Is a universe where these processes are normal — thermodynamically — conceivable? What would it be like?
