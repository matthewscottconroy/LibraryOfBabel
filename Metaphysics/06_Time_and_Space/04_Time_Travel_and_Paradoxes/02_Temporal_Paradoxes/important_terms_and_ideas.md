# Important Terms and Ideas: Temporal Paradoxes

**Bootstrap Paradox (Causal Loop)**
A scenario where an object or piece of information has no ultimate causal origin: it goes back in time and is its own cause. Example: a person receives a book from a future version of themselves, copies it, and sends the original back in time. The book's information exists in a causal loop with no originating source.

**The Grandfather Paradox**
A time traveler goes back and kills their grandfather before their parent is conceived, thereby preventing their own existence and hence the time travel that brought them back—a vicious causal loop. Lewis argues this is not impossible, only that the traveler is unable to kill their grandfather given the full facts.

**Lewis's Ability Response**
Lewis's resolution of the grandfather paradox: the time traveler "can" kill their grandfather in a weak sense (they have the relevant skills and opportunities) but "cannot" in a stronger sense given the full facts about how things actually turn out. The paradox conflates these two senses of ability.

**Causal Loop**
A sequence of events where each is caused by a later one, forming a closed loop with no external causal origin. Causal loops are permitted by self-consistent time-travel scenarios (Novikov consistency) but raise deep questions about the explanatory completeness of causal explanations.

**Temporal Bilocation**
The scenario where a time traveler is present at the same external time twice—once as a young person and once as an older version of themselves. This creates puzzles about identity (are they the same person?) and about what counts as a single moment in a world with CTCs.

**The Mutable Past**
The apparent paradox that time travel would allow the past to be changed, generating contradictions. Most philosophers who defend the coherence of time travel deny that the past can be changed: the traveler's actions are already part of the fixed past, and only self-consistent scenarios are possible.
