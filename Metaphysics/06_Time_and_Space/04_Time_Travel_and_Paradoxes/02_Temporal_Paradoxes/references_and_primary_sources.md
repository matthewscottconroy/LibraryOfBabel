# References and Primary Sources: Temporal Paradoxes

## Foundational Texts

- Lewis, David, "The Paradoxes of Time Travel," *American Philosophical Quarterly* 13 (1976). Addresses the main temporal paradoxes—the grandfather paradox and the bootstrapping paradox—arguing that they are puzzling but not logically impossible, using ability qualifications and self-consistent histories.

- Horwich, Paul, *Asymmetries in Time* (1987). Examines temporal paradoxes and the asymmetry of time, including discussions of backward causation and the relationship between temporal direction and the causal arrow.

- Smith, Nicholas J.J., "Time Travel," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2013). A comprehensive survey of the paradoxes of time travel and their proposed solutions, including Lewis's ability response, the multiple-streams view, and the bootstrap paradox.

## Contemporary Works

- Arntzenius, Frank and Maudlin, Tim, "Time Travel and Modern Physics," in *The Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Time* (2011). An up-to-date treatment of the physics and metaphysics of time travel paradoxes.

- Effingham, Nikk, *Time Travel: Probability and Impossibility* (2020). A recent monograph treating paradoxes of time travel with careful attention to probability and modal metaphysics.
