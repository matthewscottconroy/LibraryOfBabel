# Important Figures: The Direction of Time

## Ludwig Boltzmann (1844–1906)
Attempted to derive the thermodynamic arrow of time (entropy increase) from time-symmetric statistical mechanics via the H-theorem, prompting the reversibility and recurrence objections that have shaped all subsequent discussion. His probabilistic approach remains the foundation for statistical explanations of temporal asymmetry.

## Hans Reichenbach (1891–1953)
Offered a philosophical account of the direction of time in terms of the "mark method" and the asymmetry of causal processes, arguing that the arrow of time is grounded in the asymmetry of entropy increase rather than in any fundamental temporal asymmetry of physical laws.

## Paul Davies (born 1946)
Physicist and philosopher who has written accessibly about the arrows of time and the cosmological basis for temporal asymmetry, connecting thermodynamic, cosmological, and psychological arrows and exploring whether time has an objective direction.

## David Albert (born 1954)
Philosopher of physics who has developed the "Past Hypothesis" account of the arrow of time: all temporal asymmetry traces back to a cosmological boundary condition (the extremely low-entropy initial state of the universe) combined with statistical mechanics.
