# Important Terms and Ideas: The Direction of Time

**Arrow of Time**
The apparent one-directional flow of time—the difference between past and future—reflected in various physical, causal, and psychological asymmetries. The puzzle is that fundamental physical laws are (mostly) time-symmetric, yet the world exhibits a striking temporal asymmetry in entropy, causation, memory, and radiation.

**Thermodynamic Arrow**
The direction defined by entropy increase: systems spontaneously evolve from lower to higher entropy states. The second law of thermodynamics states that entropy never decreases in isolated systems, providing a macroscopic arrow of time. Statistical mechanics seeks to derive this from time-symmetric microscopic laws.

**The Past Hypothesis**
Albert's proposal that all temporal asymmetry traces to the cosmological initial condition—the extremely low entropy of the universe at or near the Big Bang. Given this boundary condition plus the statistical postulate, the increase of entropy toward the future is overwhelmingly probable.

**Reversibility Objection**
The objection to Boltzmann that if the microscopic laws are time-symmetric, for every evolution from low to high entropy there is an equally lawful reversed evolution. This objection shows that the thermodynamic arrow cannot be derived from symmetric laws alone without an asymmetric boundary condition.

**Causal Arrow**
The asymmetry between causes and effects: causes precede their effects. The causal arrow is often linked to the thermodynamic arrow—some philosophers argue that causation runs in the direction of entropy increase—while others take the causal arrow to be irreducible.

**Psychological Arrow**
The asymmetry between memory and anticipation: we remember the past but not the future; we can affect the future but not the past. Albert and Price argue this psychological asymmetry derives from the thermodynamic arrow via the nature of records and traces in low-entropy systems.
