# References and Primary Sources: The Direction of Time

## Foundational Texts

- Reichenbach, Hans, *The Direction of Time* (1956, posthumous). The classic philosophical treatment; analyzes the asymmetry of time in terms of entropy, the mark method, and the structure of causal processes, arguing that the direction of time is reducible to physical asymmetries.

- Price, Huw, *Time's Arrow and Archimedes' Point* (1996). Argues for a thoroughly time-symmetric physics and diagnoses hidden "time-bias" in physical and philosophical reasoning; challenges standard explanations of temporal asymmetry and defends a view from "Archimedes' Point."

- Albert, David, *Time and Chance* (2000). Develops the statistical mechanical account of temporal asymmetry, arguing that all arrows of time—thermodynamic, psychological, causal—derive from the Past Hypothesis (the low-entropy initial condition) combined with the statistical postulate.

## Contemporary Works

- Callender, Craig, "Thermodynamic Asymmetry in Time," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2021). A thorough survey of the arrows of time, statistical mechanics, and the main philosophical positions.

- Zeh, H. Dieter, *The Physical Basis of the Direction of Time* (5th ed., 2007). A comprehensive technical treatment of all known arrows of time and their relationships.
