# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Tripartite soul test**: Plato uses the conflict between reason and appetite to argue for the tripartite soul. Can you identify a case of internal conflict that might require positing a fourth part of the soul? Does Plato have resources to resist this expansion?

2. **Recollection argument**: Plato argues that mathematical knowledge (of perfect equality) could not come from experience and so must come from pre-natal acquaintance with Forms. Critique this argument: is it impossible that mathematical knowledge is derived from idealized experience rather than pre-natal acquaintance?

3. **Hylomorphic analysis**: Apply Aristotle's hylomorphic framework to a modern artifact (a computer). What is the matter? What is the form? Is the form separable from the matter? How does this analogy help or hinder understanding the soul-body relation?

4. **Epicurean symmetry**: Epicurus argues that pre-natal non-existence does not disturb us, so post-mortem non-existence should not either. Is this symmetry argument convincing? Are there relevant asymmetries between the two cases?

5. **Plotinus's ascent**: Plotinus describes a mystical union with the One as the goal of philosophical life. What would it mean for a contemporary philosopher to evaluate this claim? Is it a philosophical claim, a psychological claim, or a religious one?
