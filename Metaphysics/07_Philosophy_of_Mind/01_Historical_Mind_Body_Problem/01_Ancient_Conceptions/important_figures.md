# Important Figures

**Plato (428–348 BCE)**: Developed the tripartite soul and arguments for immortality in the *Phaedo*, *Republic*, and *Phaedrus*. His sharp soul-body dualism and theory of Forms defined the problem for subsequent tradition.

**Aristotle (384–322 BCE)**: Responded to Plato with hylomorphic psychology in *De Anima*, defining the soul as the form of the body. His hierarchical levels of soul (nutritive, sensitive, rational) provided a systematic biological framework for mental life.

**Epicurus (341–270 BCE)**: Developed atomist materialism, arguing that the soul consists of fine atoms that disperse at death. His argument that the fear of death is irrational has had enduring ethical and philosophical significance.

**Plotinus (204–270 CE)**: Neoplatonic philosopher who developed a hierarchical metaphysics of soul, Nous, and the One. His account of the soul's ascent toward union with the One influenced Augustine and medieval Christian psychology.

**Lucretius (c. 99–55 BCE)**: Roman Epicurean whose *De Rerum Natura* (On the Nature of Things) provides the most detailed and influential ancient materialist account of the soul.
