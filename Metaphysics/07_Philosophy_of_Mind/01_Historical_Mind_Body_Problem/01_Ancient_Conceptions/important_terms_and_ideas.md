# Important Terms and Ideas: Ancient Conceptions of Mind and Body

**Hylomorphism**
Aristotle's view that a living organism is a composite of matter (hyle) and form (morphe), where the soul is the form—the principle of organization and function—of the body. On this account, asking whether the soul and body are distinct is like asking whether wax and its shape are distinct.

**Tripartite Soul**
Plato's division of the soul into rational (logistikon), spirited (thymoeides), and appetitive (epithymetikon) parts, corresponding to the ruling, auxiliary, and productive classes in the ideal city. Psychic virtue is the rational part ruling the others in accordance with reason.

**Soul as Form**
Aristotle's definition of the soul as "the first actuality of a natural body that has the potential for life." The soul is not a separate substance added to the body but the body's functional organization—what it is to be a living thing of that kind.

**Platonic Dualism**
Plato's view that the soul is an immaterial substance that uses the body as an instrument. The soul is immortal, pre-existing and surviving bodily death; the body is the soul's prison or tomb. True knowledge requires the soul's liberation from bodily interference.

**Nous (Intellect)**
In both Plato and Aristotle, nous is the highest cognitive faculty, capable of grasping eternal truths. Aristotle distinguishes active from passive intellect; the active intellect (nous poietikos) is what makes things actually intelligible, and its nature and relationship to the body generated centuries of controversy.

**Pneuma**
In Stoic philosophy, the "breath" or vital spirit that pervades the body and serves as the material substrate of soul, perception, and rational activity. The Stoic soul is material but composed of an especially refined, active matter—a blend of fire and air.
