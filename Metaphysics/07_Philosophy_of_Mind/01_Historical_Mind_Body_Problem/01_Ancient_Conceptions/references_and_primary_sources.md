# References and Primary Sources: Ancient Conceptions of Mind and Body

## Foundational Texts

- Plato, *Phaedo* (c. 380 BCE). Develops the doctrine of the soul as immaterial and immortal, arguing that genuine knowledge requires the soul to separate from the body; presents the Forms as the proper objects of rational cognition.

- Plato, *Republic*, Book IV (c. 380 BCE). Distinguishes three parts of the soul—rational, spirited, and appetitive—and argues that psychic health consists in their proper ordering under reason's governance.

- Aristotle, *De Anima* (c. 350 BCE). The foundational ancient treatise on the nature of soul; introduces the hylomorphic account of soul as the form of a living body, critiquing Platonic dualism and anticipating functionalist themes.

- Epicurus, *Letter to Herodotus* (c. 300 BCE). Defends a materialist theory of the soul as composed of especially fine atoms, arguing that perception, thought, and sensation all have atomic explanations.

## Contemporary Works

- Caston, Victor, "Aristotle's Two Intellects: A Modest Proposal," *Phronesis* 44 (1999). A careful analytical reconstruction of Aristotle's notoriously difficult theory of the active and passive intellect.

- Shields, Christopher, *Aristotle* (2007). A comprehensive guide to Aristotle's philosophy including detailed treatment of *De Anima* and the hylomorphic theory of mind.
