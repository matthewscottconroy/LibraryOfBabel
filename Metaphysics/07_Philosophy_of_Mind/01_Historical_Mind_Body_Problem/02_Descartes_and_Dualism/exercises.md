# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Evil demon exercise**: Follow Descartes's Method of Doubt in your own first-person reflection. What is the last belief you can doubt? Is the cogito genuinely indubitable, or does Lichtenberg's objection ("there is thinking" rather than "I think") undermine it?

2. **Princess Elisabeth's challenge**: In a letter to Descartes, Elisabeth asked: if the soul is unextended, by what mechanism does it move the body? Write Descartes's best possible response to this challenge. Then write the strongest objection to that response.

3. **Conceivability and possibility**: Descartes argues that because he can clearly and distinctly conceive of himself existing without a body, he and his body are distinct. Arnauld countered with the triangle example. Construct your own counterexample to the principle that clear and distinct conceivability entails possibility.

4. **Malebranche's God**: Occasionalism holds that God causes every apparent mind-body interaction. Is this a coherent philosophical position or an ad hoc theological dodge? Compare to eliminativist positions that reject mental causation entirely.

5. **Zombie thought experiment**: Descartes's real distinction anticipates Chalmers's philosophical zombie argument. Is a zombie (a being physically identical to you but lacking consciousness) conceivable? Does its conceivability establish the possibility of consciousness without physical processes?
