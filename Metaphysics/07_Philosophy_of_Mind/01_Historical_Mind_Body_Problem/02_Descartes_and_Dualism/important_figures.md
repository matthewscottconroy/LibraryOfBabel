# Important Figures

**René Descartes (1596–1650)**: The defining figure of modern philosophy of mind. His *Meditations on First Philosophy* established substance dualism, the cogito as the foundation of knowledge, and the mind-body problem in its modern form.

**Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia (1618–1680)**: Descartes's most penetrating philosophical interlocutor, whose correspondence pressed him on the interaction problem with unusual force. Her critique anticipates subsequent challenges to dualism.

**Nicolas Malebranche (1638–1715)**: Cartesian who developed occasionalism as a solution to the interaction problem in *The Search after Truth*. His vision-in-God epistemology is a systematic alternative to Cartesian epistemology.

**G.W. Leibniz (1646–1716)**: Proposed pre-established harmony as an alternative to occasionalism. His monadological metaphysics eliminates genuine causal interaction between substances. The *New Essays on Human Understanding* and *Monadology* are key texts.

**Antoine Arnauld (1612–1694)**: Theologian and philosopher whose Fourth Objections to Descartes's *Meditations* raised the most acute criticism of the conceivability argument for the real distinction.
