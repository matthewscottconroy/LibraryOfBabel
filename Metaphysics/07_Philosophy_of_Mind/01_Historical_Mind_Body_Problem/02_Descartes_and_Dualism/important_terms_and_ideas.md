# Important Terms and Ideas: Descartes and Dualism

**Substance Dualism**
The view that mind and body are two ontologically distinct substances with incompatible essential properties: mind is essentially thinking and unextended (res cogitans); body is essentially extended and unthinking (res extensa). They are genuinely separate things, not properties of a single substance.

**Real Distinction**
Descartes's criterion: two things are really distinct if each can exist without the other. He argues that mind and body are really distinct because he can clearly and distinctly conceive each without the other. The real distinction grounds the possibility of disembodied minds and mindless bodies.

**The Interaction Problem**
The challenge to substance dualism posed most sharply by Princess Elisabeth: how can a non-spatial, unextended mind causally affect a physical body, and vice versa? Causal interaction seems to require contact or a common medium, which an unextended substance cannot have.

**Occasionalism**
Malebranche's response to the interaction problem: finite minds and bodies do not causally interact at all. God is the only genuine cause; mental "causes" are occasions on which God produces bodily effects, and bodily "causes" are occasions on which God produces mental effects.

**Res Cogitans / Res Extensa**
Descartes's two fundamental substances: thinking substance (res cogitans), whose essence is thought, and extended substance (res extensa), whose essence is spatial extension. The human person is an intimate union of both, but their natures are fundamentally different.

**The Pineal Gland**
Descartes's proposed site of mind-body interaction: the pineal gland, located at the center of the brain, where the soul most immediately controls the body by directing the flow of animal spirits. This anatomical speculation was abandoned quickly but illustrates Descartes's attempt to address the interaction problem empirically.
