# References and Primary Sources: Descartes and Dualism

## Foundational Texts

- Descartes, René, *Meditations on First Philosophy* (1641). The canonical statement of substance dualism: the Sixth Meditation argues for the real distinction between mind (res cogitans) and body (res extensa) and raises the problem of their interaction.

- Descartes, René, *The Passions of the Soul* (1649). Descartes's account of the soul-body union and its interaction via the pineal gland; attempts to explain how immaterial soul and extended body causally affect each other.

- Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia, correspondence with Descartes (1643). Elisabeth raises the interaction problem sharply: how can an unextended substance cause motion in a material body? Her challenge remains the most powerful objection to Cartesian dualism.

- Malebranche, Nicolas, *The Search After Truth* (1674–75). Develops occasionalism as a response to the interaction problem: mind and body do not causally interact directly; God is the sole genuine cause, occasioning mental and physical events in parallel.

## Contemporary Works

- Ryle, Gilbert, *The Concept of Mind* (1949). The influential critique of Cartesian dualism as a "category mistake"—treating the mind as a ghost in the machine. A classic source for understanding what dualists are committed to and what is wrong with it.

- Wilson, Margaret, *Descartes* (1978). A careful scholarly treatment of Descartes's philosophy of mind and his arguments for dualism, with attention to the interaction problem.
