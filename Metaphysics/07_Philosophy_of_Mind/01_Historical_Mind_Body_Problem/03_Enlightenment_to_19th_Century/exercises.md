# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Kant on the self**: Kant argues that the 'I' of apperception is a formal, not substantive, unity. Does this mean there is no self? What would a Kantian say to someone who claims to have discovered their real self through introspection?

2. **Stream of consciousness**: James argues that consciousness is a continuous flowing stream, not a chain of discrete states. Keep a real-time introspective log for five minutes, trying to capture the stream as it flows. Does this exercise support James's description?

3. **Ryle's category mistake**: Ryle claims Descartes committed a category mistake by treating 'the mind' as an inner object like other objects. Identify three everyday expressions about the mind that might be interpreted as category mistakes in Ryle's sense. Are they actually mistakes, or are they legitimate descriptions?

4. **Heidegger's ready-to-hand**: Find a piece of equipment that you use without thinking about it. Deliberately break it or impede its function. Describe the transition from absorbed use (ready-to-hand) to explicit attention (present-at-hand). How does this illuminate the difference between skilled action and deliberate cognition?

5. **Merleau-Ponty's body schema**: A blind person using a cane reports that after practice, they feel the texture of the ground through the tip of the cane, not at the hand. How does this phenomenon support Merleau-Ponty's claim that the body schema can extend beyond the biological body? What are the implications for prosthetics and technology?
