# Important Figures

**Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)**: The *Critique of Pure Reason* revolutionized the theory of mind by arguing that the mind's activity (synthesis under categories) constitutes objective experience. His Paralogisms critique rational psychology.

**G.W.F. Hegel (1770–1831)**: Developed a social and historical account of mind (*Geist*) in the *Phenomenology of Spirit*. Mind is not an individual substance but a self-realizing process culminating in Absolute Knowledge.

**Franz Brentano (1838–1917)**: Reintroduced intentionality as the mark of the mental in *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint*, founding scientific psychology in Germany and Austria and influencing Husserl directly.

**William James (1842–1910)**: Author of the *Principles of Psychology*, the foundational text of American psychology. Developed the stream of consciousness, pragmatist accounts of truth, and the concept of the specious present.

**Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)**: Founder of phenomenology, developing intentional analysis and the epoché in *Logical Investigations* and *Ideas I*. His analysis of time-consciousness and the ego is central to twentieth-century European philosophy of mind.

**Martin Heidegger (1889–1976)**: *Being and Time* replaced Cartesian subject-object dualism with Being-in-the-world. His analysis of tool-use and absorbed coping influenced Dreyfus's critique of classical AI.

**Maurice Merleau-Ponty (1908–1961)**: *Phenomenology of Perception* developed a phenomenology of the lived body, arguing that embodied skills — not mental representations — are the primary form of world-engagement.
