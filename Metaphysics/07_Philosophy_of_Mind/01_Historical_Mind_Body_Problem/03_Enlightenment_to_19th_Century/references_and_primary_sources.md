# References and Primary Sources: Enlightenment to 19th-Century Philosophy of Mind

## Foundational Texts

- Spinoza, Baruch, *Ethics* (1677), Part II. Defends dual-aspect monism: mind and body are not distinct substances but two attributes of a single infinite substance (God or Nature), known under the attributes of thought and extension.

- Leibniz, G.W., *Monadology* (1714). Defends a form of idealist panpsychism: the ultimate constituents of reality are mind-like monads; what appears as matter is a confused perception of these simple mental substances.

- La Mettrie, Julien Offray de, *Man a Machine* (1748). The most radical Enlightenment materialism: humans are entirely mechanical; the soul is a material function of the body; all mental phenomena are explicable by the mechanics of the nervous system.

- Kant, Immanuel, *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781), Paralogisms of Pure Reason. Critiques the rationalist arguments for the soul's simplicity and immortality; argues that we cannot know the ultimate nature of the thinking subject.

## Contemporary Works

- Vartanian, Aram, *La Mettrie's L'Homme Machine: A Study in the Origins of an Idea* (1960). Historical study of Enlightenment materialism and its sources.

- Brook, Andrew, *Kant and the Mind* (1994). A careful analytical reconstruction of Kant's views on the mind, self-consciousness, and the limits of psychological knowledge.
