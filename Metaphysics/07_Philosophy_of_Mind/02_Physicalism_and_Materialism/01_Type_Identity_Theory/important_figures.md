# Important Figures

**U.T. Place (1924–2000)**: "Is Consciousness a Brain Process?" (1956) launched the identity theory. Place argued for the empirical identification of consciousness with brain processes and introduced the distinction between "is" of definition and "is" of composition.

**J.J.C. Smart (1920–2012)**: "Sensations and Brain Processes" (1959) extended and defended the identity theory against objections, introducing topic-neutral analysis to handle the phenomenological fallacy objection.

**Herbert Feigl (1902–1988)**: "The 'Mental' and the 'Physical'" (1958) independently developed a sophisticated form of the identity theory, distinguishing phenomenal/nomological identity from conceptual identity.

**Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)**: Developed the multiple realizability objection to type identity theory in "Psychological Predicates" (1967), arguing for functionalism as an alternative. (Putnam later became critical of functionalism too.)

**Paul and Patricia Churchland**: Developed eliminative materialism as the most radical physicalist response, arguing that folk psychology (including type identity theory's categories) will be replaced by mature neuroscience.

**Saul Kripke (1940–present)**: *Naming and Necessity* (1980) developed a modal argument against the identity theory: mental-physical identity claims, if true, would be necessarily true — but they seem contingent. This challenges identity theory from a different direction than multiple realizability.
