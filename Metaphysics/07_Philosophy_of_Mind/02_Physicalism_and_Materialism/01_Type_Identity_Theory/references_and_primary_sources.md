# References and Primary Sources: Type Identity Theory

## Foundational Texts

- Smart, J.J.C., "Sensations and Brain Processes," *Philosophical Review* 68 (1959). The canonical statement of type identity theory: sensations are identical with brain processes; this is an empirical discovery like the identity of lightning with electrical discharge.

- Place, U.T., "Is Consciousness a Brain Process?" *British Journal of Psychology* 47 (1956). An earlier defense of the identity theory, arguing that phenomenal consciousness can be identified with neurophysiological processes.

- Feigl, Herbert, "The 'Mental' and the 'Physical'," *Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 2 (1958). Develops a "double language" identity theory, arguing that mental terms and physical terms refer to the same brain states via different modes of presentation.

## Contemporary Works

- Lewis, David, "An Argument for the Identity Theory," *Journal of Philosophy* 63 (1966). Argues for type identity on functionalist-causal grounds: mental states are defined by their causal roles, and causal roles are realized by brain states.

- Churchland, Paul, *Matter and Consciousness* (1984). A clear introduction to physicalism and the identity theory, defending eliminative materialism as the natural successor to the type identity view.
