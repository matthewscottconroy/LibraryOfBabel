# Non-Reductive Physicalism

Non-reductive physicalism attempts to honor two commitments simultaneously: the physicalist commitment that everything is physical (or at least physically realized), and the anti-reductionist commitment that mental properties are genuine, causally efficacious properties that are not reducible to physical properties. The key concept is supervenience: mental properties supervene on physical properties — there cannot be a difference in mental properties without a difference in physical properties — but mental properties are not reducible to physical properties, since they can be multiply realized by different physical configurations.

Davidson's anomalous monism is the canonical version of non-reductive physicalism. Davidson held that every mental event token is identical to some physical event token — there are no non-physical events. But mental properties (types) are not identical to physical properties (types), because the mental realm is governed by constitutive principles — principles of rationality and interpretive charity — that have no counterpart in the physical realm. Psychology is "anomalous" (not subject to strict deterministic laws) because the attribution of mental states is governed by normative constraints that physics does not share.

The problem of mental causation returns here with special force. If mental properties are distinct from physical properties, and if physical events have sufficient physical causes, how can mental properties make any causal difference? The mental token event causes a physical event — Davidson grants this, since token mental events are identical to token physical events. But does it cause the effect in virtue of its mental properties or only in virtue of its physical properties? Non-reductive physicalism seems committed to epiphenomenalism about mental properties, even if not about mental events.

This section examines non-reductive physicalism in its various forms, the problem of mental causation it faces, and the responses that have been proposed — including the claim that higher-level properties can be genuinely causally efficacious without being reducible to lower-level properties.
