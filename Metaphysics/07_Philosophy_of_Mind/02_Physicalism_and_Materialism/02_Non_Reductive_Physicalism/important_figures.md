# Important Figures

**Donald Davidson (1917–2003)**: Developed anomalous monism in "Mental Events" (1970), defending token identity while rejecting type identity. His account of mental anomalism via the principle of charity remains foundational.

**Jaegwon Kim (1934–2019)**: The most rigorous analyst of supervenience, reduction, and the exclusion problem. *Supervenience and Mind* (1993) and *Mind in a Physical World* (1998) are key texts. Kim ultimately concluded that non-reductive physicalism faces a dilemma between reduction and epiphenomenalism.

**David Chalmers (1966–present)**: *The Conscious Mind* (1996) deployed the conceivability argument and the hard problem to challenge physicalism. He defends property dualism (or more recently, a form of panpsychism or idealism).

**David Papineau (1947–present)**: *Philosophical Naturalism* (1993) and *Thinking about Consciousness* (2002) develop a posteriori physicalism and the causal argument for identifying phenomenal states with physical states.

**Brian Loar (1942–2003)**: Defended physicalism against the knowledge argument and conceivability arguments by developing the theory of phenomenal concepts — special concepts that refer to physical states in a distinctive way, explaining why the physical nature of experience is not a priori accessible.

**Ned Block (1942–present)**: Distinguished access consciousness from phenomenal consciousness. His work on the "explanatory gap" and the Chinese Nation/Chinese Brain thought experiments are central to the contemporary debate.
