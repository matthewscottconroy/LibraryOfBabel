# Important Terms and Ideas: Non-Reductive Physicalism

**Non-Reductive Physicalism**
The view that mental properties are physical in the sense of supervening on physical properties, but are not reducible to them—mental descriptions are not eliminable in favor of physical ones. Mental properties are real and causally efficacious even though they are not identical to physical properties.

**Supervenience**
A necessary covariation relation: mental properties supervene on physical properties if no two possible situations can be alike in all physical respects while differing mentally. Supervenience expresses the dependence of the mental on the physical without requiring reduction.

**Anomalous Monism**
Davidson's position: every mental event token is identical to some physical event token (monism), but mental event types cannot be subsumed under strict physical laws (anomalism). Psychology has its own irreducible vocabulary governed by norms of rationality rather than causal laws.

**Multiple Realizability**
The thesis that mental state types can be physically realized in many different ways. Pain in a human, an octopus, and a silicon robot might all be realized by very different physical states. This is the main motivation for non-reductive physicalism: if mental types are multiply realizable, they are not physical types.

**The Exclusion Problem**
Kim's challenge to non-reductive physicalism: if every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, and mental events are not identical to physical events, then mental causes are systematically excluded by physical causes. Non-reductive physicalism seems unable to make mental causation intelligible.

**Special Sciences**
Sciences like psychology, economics, and biology that have their own taxonomies and laws that crosscut the categories of physics. Fodor argues that the irreducibility of special sciences to physics is not a defect but a feature—their categories pick out genuinely real patterns that physics cannot capture.
