# References and Primary Sources: Non-Reductive Physicalism

## Foundational Texts

- Davidson, Donald, "Mental Events," in *Experience and Theory* edited by Foster and Swanson (1970). Introduces anomalous monism: every mental event is identical to some physical event (token physicalism), but there are no strict psychophysical laws—mental descriptions are anomalous relative to physical law.

- Putnam, Hilary, "Psychological Predicates," in *Art, Mind, and Religion* edited by Capitan and Merrill (1967). The original multiple realizability argument against type identity theory; argues that psychological states are functional kinds that can be realized by different physical substrates.

- Kim, Jaegwon, "The Myth of Nonreductive Materialism," *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Association* 63 (1989). The influential challenge to non-reductive physicalism: if mental properties supervene on but are not reducible to physical properties, it is unclear how they can have genuine causal power.

## Contemporary Works

- Fodor, Jerry, "Special Sciences," *Synthese* 28 (1974). Argues that special sciences (psychology, economics) are autonomous from physics because their categories crosscut physical categories; this is the theoretical basis for non-reductive physicalism.

- Yablo, Stephen, "Mental Causation," *Philosophical Review* 101 (1992). Proposes proportionality as a criterion for causal relevance, attempting to show how mental causes can be genuinely causally efficacious without being reducible to physical causes.
