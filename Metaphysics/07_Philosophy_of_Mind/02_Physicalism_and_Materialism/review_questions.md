# Review Questions — Physicalism and Materialism

1. The type identity theory models mental-neural identification on scientific reductions like water = H₂O. But Place and Smart insisted the identity is a posteriori and contingent. Why does the a posteriori character matter for the theory's philosophical credentials, and does it actually help the theory evade the Leibniz's Law objection?

2. Putnam's multiple realizability argument concludes that pain is not identical to C-fiber firing because octopi — with no C-fibers — can feel pain. The argument requires that pain is a single type across species. How strong is this premise, and how does its rejection (by Kim and others) affect both the argument against type identity and the positive case for functionalism?

3. Eliminative materialists claim folk psychology is a bad theory that will be superseded rather than reduced. The self-refutation objection says that to *believe* eliminativism is to employ the very categories eliminativism condemns. Assess the Churchlandian response to this objection. Does it succeed, and what would a successful eliminativism actually look like in practice?

4. Davidson's anomalous monism holds that mental events are identical to physical events (token identity) but that mental *types* resist reduction to physical types because mental description is normatively governed. Critics argue this makes mental properties causally inert — the content of a belief makes no physical difference. How serious is this objection, and what distinguishes anomalous monism from epiphenomenalism?

5. Supervenience captures the intuition that there can be no mental difference without a physical difference, but Kim showed that even Cartesian dualism is consistent with supervenience. What additional condition must physicalism satisfy beyond supervenience, and what is the significance of grounding as a candidate for this additional relation?

6. The causal closure principle — every physical event has a sufficient physical cause — is the engine of Kim's exclusion argument. Is this principle a metaphysical truth, an empirical finding, or a methodological assumption of physics? What would it mean for the mind-body debate if quantum indeterminacy genuinely underdetermined some physical events?

7. Kim's exclusion argument targets non-reductive physicalism, forcing a choice between full reduction and epiphenomenalism. The "constitution response" (Yablo, Bennett) argues that mental and physical causation do not compete because they describe the same causal contribution at different levels. Explain the response and identify its most serious vulnerability.

8. How does the debate between type identity theory, non-reductive physicalism, and eliminative materialism bear on what will become the hard problem of consciousness? Which position is best positioned to resist the later challenge from Chalmers, and why?
