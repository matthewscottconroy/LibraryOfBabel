# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **The interaction problem formalized**: If mind and body are genuinely distinct substances, any causal interaction must involve either (a) the mind exerting force on the body, (b) God intervening, or (c) pre-established coordination. For each option, identify the strongest philosophical objection. Which option is least problematic?

2. **Kripke's argument**: Kripke argues: if pain = C-fiber firing, then necessarily pain = C-fiber firing. But we can coherently imagine C-fibers firing without pain. Does this show that pain ≠ C-fiber firing? Or does it only show that we can imagine a situation in which we are deceived about whether pain is occurring?

3. **The mill argument**: Leibniz imagined walking through an enlarged brain as through a mill and finding only parts interacting with parts — no thought. Does this argument establish that thought is not physical? Could a physicalist make a similar argument about any property (like life) that seems to require more than parts?

4. **Personal identity for dualists**: If you are an immaterial substance, what makes you the same person today as yesterday? Propose criteria for the identity of immaterial substances and consider whether they work.

5. **Split brain cases**: Patients whose corpus callosum has been severed sometimes exhibit behavior suggesting two independent centers of consciousness. How should a substance dualist interpret this? Are there two minds? Is the single soul divided?
