# Important Figures

**René Descartes (1596–1650)**: The canonical substance dualist. The *Meditations*, the *Passions of the Soul*, and the correspondence with Princess Elisabeth define the position and its difficulties.

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)**: Responded to Descartes with the monadology and pre-established harmony. His "mill" argument against materialism is an early version of the conceivability argument.

**Saul Kripke (1940–present)**: *Naming and Necessity* (1980) developed a modal argument that pain cannot be identical to C-fiber firing, reviving dualist intuitions within analytic philosophy.

**Richard Swinburne (1934–present)**: *The Evolution of the Soul* (1986) defends substance dualism on grounds of the indivisibility and unity of the self, connecting the philosophical argument to religious views of the soul.

**John Foster (1941–2009)**: *The Immaterial Self* (1991) is a sustained analytic defense of Cartesian substance dualism, with careful attention to the interaction problem.
