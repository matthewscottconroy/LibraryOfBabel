# References and Primary Sources: Contemporary Substance Dualism

## Foundational Texts

- Swinburne, Richard, *The Evolution of the Soul* (1986). A contemporary defense of substance dualism on grounds of personal identity and the irreducibility of mental life; argues that the soul is a simple substance that survives bodily death.

- Foster, John, *The Immaterial Self* (1991). A detailed philosophical defense of Cartesian substance dualism, arguing that the self is an immaterial substance distinct from the body and that materialist accounts of mind are conceptually inadequate.

- Eccles, John and Popper, Karl, *The Self and Its Brain* (1977). An interactionist dualism defended by a neuroscientist and philosopher; argues that the conscious self is immaterial and interacts with the brain's supplementary motor area.

## Contemporary Works

- Chalmers, David, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 2 (1995). While not defending classical substance dualism, argues for a form of property dualism that motivates renewed interest in non-physicalist views of mind.

- Goetz, Stewart and Taliaferro, Charles, *A Brief History of the Soul* (2011). An accessible survey of dualist accounts of the soul from ancient to contemporary philosophy, defending the coherence and plausibility of substance dualism.
