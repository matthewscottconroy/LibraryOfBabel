# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Mary's Room**: Mary is a colorblind neuroscientist who knows all physical facts about color perception. She is released from a black-and-white room and sees red for the first time. Does she learn something new? If yes, does this show that not all facts are physical facts? If no, why does it seem as if she learns something?

2. **Epiphenomenalism test**: If phenomenal properties are causally inert (epiphenomenalism), then your decision to raise your hand is caused entirely by neural processes, not by your experience of deciding. Is this consistent with your first-person experience of deliberating and deciding? Is epiphenomenalism self-undermining (since beliefs about consciousness, being mental, would also be epiphenomenal)?

3. **The bat**: Nagel asks what it is like to be a bat, navigating by sonar. Can we know what this is like? Does the fact that we cannot imaginatively project into bat consciousness show that there are facts about consciousness not captured by third-person science?

4. **Emergence test**: Is the wetness of water strongly emergent from H₂O molecules? Is the transparency of glass strongly emergent from silicon dioxide molecules? If these are only weakly emergent, why should consciousness be strongly emergent? What makes consciousness special?

5. **The explanatory gap**: Levine distinguishes the epistemological gap (we can't explain how physical processes give rise to consciousness) from the metaphysical gap (consciousness is genuinely non-physical). Are these equivalent? Could there be an epistemic gap without a metaphysical gap?
