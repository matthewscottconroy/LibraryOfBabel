# Important Figures

**Frank Jackson (1943–present)**: The knowledge argument (Mary's Room) in "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982) is the canonical argument for property dualism about phenomenal properties. Jackson subsequently reversed his position, arguing for physicalism.

**Joseph Levine (1952–present)**: Coined the phrase "explanatory gap" in "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap" (1983), distinguishing the epistemic gap from the metaphysical one.

**Thomas Nagel (1937–present)**: "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974) introduced the concept of what it is like to be a subject — the subjective character of experience — as the central datum that materialism cannot accommodate.

**Colin McGinn (1950–present)**: Mysterianism: the view that the human mind is constitutionally incapable of solving the mind-body problem. *The Problem of Consciousness* (1991).

**Samuel Alexander (1859–1938)** and **C.D. Broad (1887–1971)**: Developed early emergentist accounts of mind, distinguishing strongly emergent from weakly emergent properties.
