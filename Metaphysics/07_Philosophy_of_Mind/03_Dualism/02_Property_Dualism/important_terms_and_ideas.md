# Important Terms and Ideas: Property Dualism

**Property Dualism**
The view that while there is only one kind of substance (physical), there are two fundamentally different kinds of properties: physical properties (describable in terms of structure, function, and disposition) and phenomenal properties (intrinsic, qualitative features of experience). Phenomenal properties are not reducible to physical ones.

**The Knowledge Argument**
Jackson's argument: Mary knows all physical facts about color vision but upon first seeing red learns something new—a phenomenal fact. Therefore, phenomenal facts are not physical facts, and physicalism is incomplete. Property dualists use this to motivate the irreducibility of qualia.

**The Explanatory Gap**
Levine's observation: even if we accept that pain is identical to C-fiber firing, we cannot explain why C-fiber firing feels the way it does. There is an explanatory gap between physical descriptions and phenomenal experience that seems to indicate something is missing from purely physical accounts.

**Phenomenal Properties (Qualia)**
The intrinsic, qualitative, subjective features of conscious experience: the redness of red, the painfulness of pain, the taste of coffee. Property dualists argue these are real properties that resist functional or physical reduction, constituting a second irreducible category of properties.

**Epiphenomenalism (about properties)**
The property dualist view that phenomenal properties are caused by physical properties but have no causal powers of their own—they are epiphenomena. This preserves the causal closure of the physical domain but at the cost of making qualia causally inert.

**Panprotopsychism**
A form of property dualism according to which proto-phenomenal properties (not yet phenomenal but giving rise to phenomenal properties under the right combination) are fundamental features of physical reality. This is Chalmers's preferred version of his naturalistic dualism.
