# References and Primary Sources: Property Dualism

## Foundational Texts

- Jackson, Frank, "Epiphenomenal Qualia," *Philosophical Quarterly* 32 (1982). The knowledge argument for property dualism: Mary the color scientist knows all physical facts about color vision but learns something new upon seeing red for the first time—phenomenal properties are not physical properties.

- Nagel, Thomas, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" *Philosophical Review* 83 (1974). Argues that subjective experience has an essentially perspectival character that physical descriptions cannot capture; consciousness resists objective, third-person reduction.

- Chalmers, David, *The Conscious Mind* (1996). The most systematic contemporary defense of property dualism (naturalistic dualism); argues that phenomenal properties are fundamentally distinct from functional/physical properties and require independent treatment.

## Contemporary Works

- Levine, Joseph, "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap," *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 64 (1983). Introduces the concept of the explanatory gap between physical processes and phenomenal experience—even if identity holds, we cannot understand why it does.

- Papineau, David, *Thinking about Consciousness* (2002). A physicalist response to property dualism, arguing that the intuitive pull toward dualism reflects our dual ways of thinking about mental states rather than any genuine ontological distinction.
