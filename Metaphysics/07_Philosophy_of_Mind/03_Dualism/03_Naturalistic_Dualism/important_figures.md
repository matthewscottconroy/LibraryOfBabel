# Important Figures

**David Chalmers (1966–present)**: *The Conscious Mind* (1996) is the defining text of contemporary property dualism. Chalmers introduced the "hard problem," the zombie argument, and naturalistic dualism. His more recent work explores panpsychism and idealism.

**Frank Jackson (1943–present)**: Jackson's knowledge argument and conceivability arguments complement Chalmers's. His later recantation (accepting physicalism via phenomenal concepts) defines one pole in the debate.

**Joseph Levine (1952–present)**: Developed the distinction between explanatory gap and metaphysical gap, arguing that the gap is epistemological rather than ontological.

**Philip Goff (1980–present)**: Defends panpsychism (not naturalistic dualism strictly), but the arguments (from conceivability, from the intrinsic nature of matter) overlap significantly with Chalmers's.

**Brian Loar (1942–2003)**: Developed the "phenomenal concepts strategy" as a physicalist response to Chalmers — the appearance of a gap is due to the special character of phenomenal concepts, not a genuine metaphysical gap.
