# Important Terms and Ideas: Naturalistic Dualism

**Naturalistic Dualism**
Chalmers's term for his position: phenomenal consciousness involves properties not reducible to functional or physical properties, but this is not supernatural dualism. Consciousness is a natural feature of the world that requires new fundamental laws—psychophysical laws—connecting physical processes to phenomenal experience.

**The Hard Problem**
The problem of explaining why there is subjective experience at all—why physical processes give rise to phenomenal consciousness, to "what it's like." Chalmers contrasts this with the "easy problems" (explaining cognitive functions, behavioral integration, access consciousness), which are in principle solvable by functional-physical explanation.

**Psychophysical Laws**
The laws that naturalistic dualism postulates to connect physical processes to phenomenal experience. Just as fundamental physics postulates laws relating mass to gravitational force, a completed science of mind would need laws relating physical complexity or information processing to specific phenomenal qualities.

**Structural Coherence**
Chalmers's principle that the structure of conscious experience matches the structure of the information it represents. This is one candidate psychophysical law: the fine-grained structure of phenomenal experience coherently encodes the structure of the physical information being processed.

**The Conceivability Argument**
Chalmers's argument: philosophical zombies are conceivable (beings physically identical to us but lacking phenomenal consciousness); conceivability entails metaphysical possibility; therefore phenomenal consciousness is not logically entailed by physical facts, which shows that physicalism is false.

**Combination Problem**
A challenge for panpsychist versions of naturalistic dualism: granted that proto-phenomenal properties are fundamental, how do they combine to produce the rich, unified phenomenal consciousness of a person? The combination problem may be as difficult as the original hard problem.
