# References and Primary Sources: Naturalistic Dualism

## Foundational Texts

- Chalmers, David, *The Conscious Mind* (1996). Coins and defends naturalistic dualism: consciousness is not supernatural but is a fundamental feature of nature, like mass or charge, that requires supplementing standard physical theory with psychophysical laws.

- Chalmers, David, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 2 (1995). The paper version of the argument; distinguishes the "easy problems" of consciousness (explaining cognitive functions) from the "hard problem" (explaining phenomenal experience).

- Strawson, Galen, "Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 13 (2006). Argues that taking physical reality seriously requires acknowledging that phenomenal properties are as real as any others, motivating a panpsychist rather than eliminativist response.

## Contemporary Works

- Frankish, Keith, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23 (2016). The illusionist response to naturalistic dualism: phenomenal consciousness as standardly conceived is an introspective illusion; what really exists are functional states that we systematically misdescribe as qualitative.

- Tononi, Giulio et al., "Integrated Information Theory of Consciousness," *PLOS Computational Biology* 12 (2016). A scientific theory attempting to identify consciousness with integrated information (Φ)—a naturalistic account that many find compatible with a broadly dualist interpretation.
