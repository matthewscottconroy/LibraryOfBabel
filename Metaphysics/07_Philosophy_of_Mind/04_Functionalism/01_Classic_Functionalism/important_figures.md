# Important Figures

**Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)**: Founder of functionalism. "Psychological Predicates" (1967) and "Minds and Machines" (1967) are foundational. Putnam later became a prominent critic of functionalism in *Representation and Reality* (1988).

**Alan Turing (1912–1954)**: The abstract theory of computation (Turing machines, the universal Turing machine) provided the mathematical framework for machine functionalism. The Turing Test, proposed in "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (1950), frames the question of machine mentality.

**Jerry Fodor (1935–2017)**: Developed the computational-representational theory of mind (CRTM) and the language of thought hypothesis. *The Language of Thought* (1975) and *Psychosemantics* (1987) are the central texts.

**David Armstrong (1926–2014)**: Developed causal role functionalism in *A Materialist Theory of the Mind* (1968). Armstrong connected mental state characterization to the causal roles posited by folk psychology.

**David Lewis (1941–2001)**: Formalized causal role functionalism using the Ramsey-Lewis method of defining theoretical terms by their role in a theory. "Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications" (1972) is the key paper.
