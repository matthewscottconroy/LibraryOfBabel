# Important Terms and Ideas: Classic Functionalism

**Functionalism**
The view that mental states are defined by their functional roles—their causal relations to sensory inputs, behavioral outputs, and other mental states—rather than by their physical constitution. What makes something a pain is not what it is made of but what it does: it is caused by tissue damage, causes avoidance behavior, produces distress, etc.

**Turing Machine Functionalism**
The early functionalist idea (Putnam) that mental states are computational states of a Turing machine: the mind is a program running on the hardware of the brain. Mental state types are defined by the machine's table of states and transitions, not by physical properties.

**Ramsey-Lewis Functionalism**
Lewis's approach: take the conjunction of all folk psychological truths, replace mental state terms with variables, and define mental states as whatever occupies the roles described by this Ramsey sentence. Mental states are the actual realizers of these roles—typically brain states.

**Causal Role**
The functional profile of a mental state: the pattern of causal relations it stands in to perceptual inputs, behavioral outputs, and other mental states. Pain's causal role includes being caused by tissue damage, causing distress and withdrawal behavior, and interacting with desires to motivate avoidance.

**Multiple Realizability (Functionalist)**
Functionalism accommodates multiple realizability naturally: since mental states are defined by causal roles, not physical properties, any physical system that realizes the right causal roles instantiates the same mental states, regardless of substrate.

**Absent Qualia**
Block's objection: it seems possible for a system to realize the correct functional organization (have all the right causal roles) while having no phenomenal experience whatsoever. A Chinese nation organized to simulate a brain's functional organization might compute the right outputs without any "inner light." This suggests functional organization is insufficient for consciousness.
