# References and Primary Sources: Classic Functionalism

## Foundational Texts

- Putnam, Hilary, "Minds and Machines," in *Dimensions of Mind* edited by Hook (1960). An early statement of functionalism: mental states are characterized by their functional/computational roles, not their physical constitution; minds are to brains as programs are to hardware.

- Lewis, David, "Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications," *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 50 (1972). Develops Ramsey-Lewis functionalism: mental state terms are implicitly defined by the totality of folk psychological theory; mental states are whatever plays the relevant causal roles.

- Armstrong, D.M., *A Materialist Theory of the Mind* (1968). A causal theory of mind: mental states are defined as states apt to be caused by certain inputs and apt to cause certain outputs and other mental states; this is a functionalist account within a physicalist framework.

## Contemporary Works

- Block, Ned, "Troubles with Functionalism," *Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 9 (1978). Influential objections to functionalism, including the absent qualia and inverted qualia problems, arguing that functional equivalence does not guarantee phenomenal equivalence.

- Tye, Michael, *Ten Problems of Consciousness* (1995). A PANIC (Poised, Abstract, Nonconceptual, Intentional Content) theory of consciousness that attempts to develop a functionalist account of qualia.
