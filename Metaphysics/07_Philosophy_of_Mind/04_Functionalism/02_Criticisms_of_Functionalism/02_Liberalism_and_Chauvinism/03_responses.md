# Responses to the Liberalism/Chauvinism Dilemma

The dilemma is genuine, and no single response commands consensus. But the responses that have been developed collectively illuminate what constraints a successful functionalist theory must satisfy and where the genuine philosophical pressure lies.

The most common functionalist response is to add structural constraints to the functional specification that rule out China Brain cases without ruling out alien minds. Spatial and temporal unity: the functional organization must be implemented in a spatially unified system with sufficiently fast causal connections. The China Brain fails because its components are too dispersed and its communication too slow. Real-time implementation: functional organization must operate on appropriate timescales for cognition — the China Brain fails because coordinating a billion people takes much longer than neural processing. Causal grain: the causal connections must be of the right grain, not just any causal relation but direct connections implementing the relevant dynamics; telephone calls are too coarse compared to synaptic transmission. These refinements preserve the core functionalist commitments while ruling out troublesome cases. The objection is that specifying any of these constraints precisely without circularity — without covertly appealing to biological substrate — is harder than it looks. "Appropriate timescales" and "right grain" seem to be doing important work without clear principled content.

Block's own response accepts that functionalism gives a correct account of access consciousness but not phenomenal consciousness. The China Brain may have access consciousness — functional information availability — without phenomenal consciousness. This reframes the dilemma: the liberalism problem applies specifically to phenomenal consciousness; the chauvinism problem applies to access consciousness, which functionalism handles correctly. This response is honest about what functionalism can and cannot explain. The cost is that it concedes the most philosophically significant domain — phenomenal experience — to fall outside functionalism's reach.

## Graded Consciousness, Panpsychism, and Abandonment

A more radical response embraces graded consciousness: functional organization determines a degree of consciousness, with thermostats having infinitesimal consciousness and humans having rich consciousness. The liberalism problem dissolves — we do not falsely deny consciousness to anyone, but simple systems have only very primitive consciousness. This is the approach associated with Integrated Information Theory: Tononi's phi measures the degree of integrated information, making consciousness a graded quantity that is precise rather than binary. The panpsychist-functionalist position accepts the consequence that consciousness is widespread but insists this is what the theory implies and that our intuitions against thermostat consciousness are anthropocentric rather than principled.

The phenomenal concepts strategy offers a different diagnosis: the dilemma assumes we can specify phenomenal consciousness in functional terms. If phenomenal concepts do not have functional primary intensions — if they refer to phenomenal properties by direct acquaintance rather than by functional description — then no specification of functional role is either too liberal or too chauvinistic about phenomenal consciousness. The dilemma is a pseudo-problem generated by the incorrect assumption that phenomenal terms are functionally definable.

Finally, some philosophers — Nagel, Chalmers, the later Jackson — take the dilemma as evidence that functionalism is fundamentally inadequate as a complete theory of mind. They do not agree on what replaces it, but they agree that no functional specification can correctly capture the extension of phenomenal consciousness terms. The dilemma functions as a diagnostic tool: it shows precisely where functionalism needs supplementation or replacement. That it has generated so much philosophical development — teleofunctionalism, two-dimensional approaches, realizer functionalism, phenomenal concepts strategy, IIT — is itself evidence that the diagnosis is pointing at something real and important in the structure of the mind-body problem.
