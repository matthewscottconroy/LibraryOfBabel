# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Chinese Room expanded**: Searle imagines a person in a room manipulating Chinese symbols according to rules without understanding Chinese. Does the system as a whole (rules + symbols + person) understand Chinese? If not, why not? If yes, does this save functionalism?

2. **China brain**: Suppose each of the 1.4 billion people of China each simulates one neuron of a human brain, with satellite signals coordinating their interactions. The system as a whole has all the causal organization of a human brain. Is there something it is like to be the China brain? Does your intuition here track a principled criterion or just familiarity?

3. **Liberalism problem**: A thermostat "believes" the temperature is above 70° (in the sense that it has a state caused by temperatures above 70° that causes it to turn on the AC). Is this a genuine belief? What minimal conditions must a state meet to be a belief? Do these conditions track a principled distinction or merely our intuitions about biological systems?

4. **The systematicity challenge**: Fodor argued that cognition is systematic and compositional: anyone who can think "John loves Mary" can also think "Mary loves John." Does functionalism explain this? Or does systematicity require something more — a language of thought with compositional syntax?

5. **Consciousness and access**: Block distinguishes access consciousness (information availability for reasoning and report) from phenomenal consciousness (what it is like). Can you think of cases where a system has one but not the other? Do these cases exist in neuroscience (blindsight patients, for example)?
