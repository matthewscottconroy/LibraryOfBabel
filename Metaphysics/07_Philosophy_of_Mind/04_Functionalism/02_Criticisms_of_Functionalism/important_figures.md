# Important Figures

**Ned Block (1942–present)**: "Troubles with Functionalism" (1978) introduced the liberalism/chauvinism dilemma and the Chinese Nation thought experiment. Block's distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness is central to current debates.

**John Searle (1932–present)**: The Chinese Room argument (in "Minds, Brains, and Programs," 1980) challenged computational functionalism. Searle argued that syntax (formal symbol manipulation) is not sufficient for semantics (meaning), so no Turing machine can genuinely think.

**Thomas Nagel (1937–present)**: "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974) articulated what functionalism cannot explain: the subjective character of experience — what it is like — is not captured by any functional description.

**David Chalmers (1966–present)**: The zombie argument and the hard problem press the absent qualia objection at the level of consciousness theory, arguing that functional organization is not sufficient for phenomenal experience.

**Joseph Levine (1952–present)**: Developed the explanatory gap argument — functional descriptions do not explain why physical processes feel the way they do — as an epistemological challenge (possibly without metaphysical implications).
