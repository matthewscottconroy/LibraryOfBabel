# References and Primary Sources: Criticisms of Functionalism

## Foundational Texts

- Searle, John, "Minds, Brains, and Programs," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 3 (1980). The Chinese Room argument: a system can manipulate symbols according to rules without understanding their meaning; therefore, syntax is not sufficient for semantics, and computation is not sufficient for mind.

- Block, Ned, "Troubles with Functionalism," *Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 9 (1978). Presents absent qualia (the Chinese nation) and inverted qualia as counterexamples to functionalism's claim that functional organization is sufficient for mentality.

- Nagel, Thomas, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" *Philosophical Review* 83 (1974). Argues that the subjective, perspectival character of experience cannot be captured by any objective, third-person account—including functional accounts.

## Contemporary Works

- Chalmers, David, "Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia," in *Conscious Experience* edited by Metzinger (1995). A nuanced examination of inverted and absent qualia thought experiments, arguing that functional duplicates must be phenomenally similar.

- Putnam, Hilary, *Representation and Reality* (1988). Putnam himself retreats from computational functionalism, arguing that functional organization is too broad (rats, bacteria could count as believers) and that meaning is not in the head.
