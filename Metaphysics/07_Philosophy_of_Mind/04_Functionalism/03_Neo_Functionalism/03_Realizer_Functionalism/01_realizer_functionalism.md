# Realizer Functionalism

Role functionalism — the standard form of functionalism — identifies mental states with functional roles: pain is the property of being in whatever state plays the pain-role. This makes pain a second-order property: the property of having some first-order physical property that plays the relevant causal role. The advantage is strict multiple realizability; the cost, as Jaegwon Kim pressed, is a problem about mental causation. If pain is a second-order property, then the causal work in any particular case is done by the first-order physical realizer (C-fiber firing), not by the second-order property (having something that plays the pain-role). Second-order properties do not add causal powers beyond those of their first-order realizers. Role functionalism risks epiphenomenalism about mental properties by the back door.

Realizer functionalism responds by identifying mental states not with the functional role but with the physical realizer of the functional role. Pain in humans is not the property of being in something-that-plays-the-pain-role; it is C-fiber firing, the particular physical state that plays the pain-role in humans. This is a first-order identity claim, not a second-order functional one. The causal powers of pain are exactly the causal powers of C-fiber firing — no problem of causal impotence arises. And since pain just is C-fiber firing, there is no gap between the physical realizer and the mental state in which qualia might "go absent": whatever properties C-fiber firing has are automatically properties of pain.

The cost is the loss of strict multiple realizability. If human pain = C-fiber firing and Martian pain = fluid-tube activation, then "pain" does not pick out a single natural kind across species — it picks out different properties that happen to play similar functional roles. Pain in humans and pain in Martians are different properties. This threatens the generality of psychological explanation: generalizations about pain cannot be stated at the level of pain as a type across species — they must be stated species-specifically. The explanatory unity that functionalism was supposed to provide is fragmented.

## Convergence with Type Identity Theory and the Question of Method

Realizer functionalism converges with type identity theory for any specific species or system. For humans, realizer functionalism entails that pain = C-fiber firing — the same claim as type identity theory. The difference is the method of identification: realizer functionalism identifies the referent of "pain" via functional criteria (C-fiber firing is pain because it plays the pain-role in humans), while type identity theory identifies it empirically (C-fiber firing is simply what we discover pain to be). For any given organism and mental state type, both theories may converge on the same physical identification.

This convergence suggests that the deep disagreement between functionalism and identity theory is about the method of mental state individuation rather than the result in any particular case. Realizer functionalism uses functional criteria as a guide to physical identification; identity theory uses empirical investigation directly. Both end up with mental types being physical types for any given organism. Whether this convergence shows that the debate was always more terminological than substantive, or whether it reveals that the two approaches have genuinely different commitments about the nature of mind, depends on how one views the role of functional criteria in fixing mental state reference. What realizer functionalism decisively shows is that the choice between multiple realizability and causal efficacy is a genuine trade-off, not a matter of one approach being simply superior to the other.
