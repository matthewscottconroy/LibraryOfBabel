# What Realizes Mental States?

When we say that pain is "realized" by C-fiber firing in humans, we mean that C-fiber firing is the physical state that implements the pain-functional-role — the physical basis in virtue of which the functional organization is present. The realization relation is intended to be distinct from identity: the realizer is not identical to the realized property, just as silicon is not identical to the program it runs. Realization is a relation of implementation or physical constitution, not identity. But the precise nature of this relation is deeply contested, and much of non-reductive physicalism's coherence depends on getting it right.

Block, Shoemaker, and others have distinguished several candidate notions. Subset realization: the realizer is a physical state whose causal powers include all the causal powers definitive of the realized property. C-fiber firing realizes pain if the causal powers definitive of pain — causing avoidance behavior, interacting with beliefs and desires, generating distress signals — are all among the causal powers of C-fiber firing. C-fiber firing may have additional causal powers not captured by the pain-role; these are what make the realizer "more than" the realized property. Causal realization: the realizer causes the realized property to obtain — C-fiber firing causes the pain-functional-role properties to be present. Constitutive realization: the realizer constitutes the realized property at the physical level, where pain just is C-fiber firing implementing the relevant functional role, which is closer to a type identity claim.

Polger and Shapiro have argued that most discussions of "realization" actually collapse into one of these — either a causal relation or a type identity claim in disguise. If realization is causation, it is too weak to ground the mental-physical dependence physicalists want. If it collapses into identity, the non-reductive character of functionalism is lost. Non-reductive physicalism depends on there being a third option: a grounding relation that is neither mere causation nor identity. Whether the realization relation can occupy this space is the central question.

## Grounding, Asymmetry, and the Kim Dilemma

Realization is asymmetric: C-fiber firing realizes pain, not pain realizes C-fiber firing. Shoemaker's subset account captures this asymmetry: the causal powers constitutive of pain are a subset of the causal powers of C-fiber firing, but not vice versa. The asymmetry grounds the direction of physicalist dependence — mental properties depend on physical properties in the sense that what it is for a mental property to be instantiated is for the right physical property to be instantiated.

Recent metaphysics has distinguished realization from the more general grounding relation: fact F is grounded in fact G if G explains or makes F true. Realization is a special case of grounding where the physical facts ground the mental facts through the physical instantiation of functional roles. Some philosophers argue that "realization" should be replaced by "grounding" in discussions of the mental-physical relation, since "realization" carries architectural assumptions that may not always be appropriate. Others maintain that "realization" captures something specific — the way functional properties are implemented by physical mechanisms — that the abstract grounding relation does not.

Kim's dilemma for non-reductive physicalism presses from the causal direction: either mental properties are identical to physical properties (reductive physicalism) or they are causally impotent (epiphenomenalism). The realization relation must be characterized in a way that avoids both horns. If realized mental properties have their own genuine causal powers, they seem to add to the causal structure of the world beyond what their physical realizers provide — which threatens causal overdetermination or violation of closure. If they have no causal powers beyond their physical realizers, they are causally inert. The defenders of non-reductive physicalism — Shoemaker, Block, Antony — have developed sophisticated responses, but the dilemma remains a live challenge. The coherence of the realization relation is not merely a technical matter; it determines whether non-reductive physicalism is a stable position or an unstable compromise between reduction and epiphenomenalism.
