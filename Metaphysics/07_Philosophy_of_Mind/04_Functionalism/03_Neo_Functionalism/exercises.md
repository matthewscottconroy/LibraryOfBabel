# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Biological function test**: According to teleofunctionalism, a state represents X if it was selected for its causal connection to X. Does this account apply to learned concepts (the concept "computer," acquired in your lifetime)? Does the concept of a "computer" have biological proper function?

2. **Misrepresentation**: A frog snaps at a passing BB pellet, mistaking it for a fly. The teleofunctionalist says the frog's visual state misrepresents the BB as a fly. How does teleofunctionalism explain this misrepresentation? Does this explanation work for human perceptual errors?

3. **Two-dimensionalism and Mary**: Two-dimensionalists argue that when Mary sees red for the first time, she learns a new primary intension for "red" — not new secondary facts about the world. Is this analysis convincing? Does it account for the felt novelty of Mary's new knowledge?

4. **Narrow vs. broad content**: Does the meaning of your thought "water is wet" depend on whether you are on Earth (where water = H₂O) or Twin Earth (where the "water" is XYZ)? If meaning depends on the environment (broad content), how can your psychology (which is internal) determine what you are thinking about?

5. **Innateness and content**: Nativists like Fodor argue that most lexical concepts are innate. If concepts are innate rather than learned, can teleofunctionalism account for their content? What is the "proper function" of the innate concept ELECTRON?
