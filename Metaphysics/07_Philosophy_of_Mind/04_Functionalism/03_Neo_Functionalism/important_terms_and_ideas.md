# Important Terms and Ideas: Neo-Functionalism

**Representationalism**
The view that phenomenal properties are identical with or constituted by representational properties—what a state represents, not just how it functions. Tye and Dretske argue that the phenomenal character of an experience is its (nonconceptual, poised) representational content, giving functional accounts of qualia a representational basis.

**PANIC Theory**
Tye's acronym: phenomenal states are Poised, Abstract, Nonconceptual, Intentional Content. Poised: they are positioned to influence belief and action. Abstract: they represent types, not tokens. Nonconceptual: no concepts are required to have them. Intentional: they represent properties of objects. Together these features account for qualia naturalistically.

**Higher-Order Theories**
Accounts of consciousness that define a mental state as phenomenally conscious when it is accompanied by a higher-order representation (a thought or perception) of that state. Rosenthal's higher-order thought theory and Lycan's inner sense theory are the main versions.

**Homuncular Functionalism**
Lycan's approach: instead of analyzing mental states in terms of their roles relative to the whole organism, we analyze them in terms of their roles within a hierarchy of sub-personal systems or "homunculi," each defined by their own functional roles. This decomposes the mind into increasingly simple functional units.

**Narrow vs. Wide Content**
The distinction between what a mental state represents in virtue of what is in the head (narrow content) and what it represents in virtue of relations to the external environment (wide content). Neo-functionalists disagree about whether to individuate mental states narrowly or widely for purposes of psychological explanation.

**Biological Naturalism**
Searle's alternative: mentality is a biological phenomenon—a higher-level causal feature produced by the specific causal powers of the brain. This is a form of naturalism that rejects both functionalism (which says substrate is irrelevant) and dualism (which says mind is non-physical).
