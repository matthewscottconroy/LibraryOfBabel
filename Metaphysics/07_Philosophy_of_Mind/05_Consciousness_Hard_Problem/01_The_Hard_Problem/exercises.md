# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **The explanation test**: We explain thunder by appealing to electrical discharge, rapid air expansion, and sound waves. Does this explanation leave anything out? Now consider explaining the redness of red experience in neural terms. Does the neural explanation leave anything out? If yes, what? If no, why does it seem to?

2. **Functional explanation**: Chalmers argues that functional explanations explain capacities but not experience. Can you construct a functional explanation that seems to explain why a process feels like something? What would it look like? Would Chalmers accept it as genuine?

3. **The hard problem for other phenomena**: Is there a "hard problem of heat" — an explanatory gap between molecular motion and the felt warmth of heat? If not, why is consciousness different? If yes, does this suggest the hard problem is a general feature of science, not specific to mind?

4. **Zombie world and behavior**: If philosophical zombies behave exactly like us — including reporting their "experiences," seeming to cry in pain, laughing when amused — is there any practical or moral difference between them and conscious beings? What does this tell us about the relationship between consciousness and behavior?

5. **The meta-problem**: Chalmers proposes a "meta-problem of consciousness" — why do we think there is a hard problem? If consciousness is physical, the hard problem is an illusion. What would an explanation of the meta-problem look like? Does solving the meta-problem solve the hard problem?
