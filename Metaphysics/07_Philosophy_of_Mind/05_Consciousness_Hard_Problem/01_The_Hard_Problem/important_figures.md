# Important Figures

**David Chalmers (1966–present)**: Introduced the hard problem and the easy/hard distinction in "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" (1995) and *The Conscious Mind* (1996). Defends property dualism, naturalistic dualism, and more recently panpsychism.

**Joseph Levine (1952–present)**: Introduced the "explanatory gap" in "Materialism and Qualia" (1983), distinguishing epistemological from metaphysical gaps. *Purple Haze* (2001) is his book-length treatment.

**Daniel Dennett (1942–present)**: The principal advocate of eliminativist or deflationary responses to the hard problem. *Consciousness Explained* (1991) and *From Bacteria to Bach and Back* (2017) deny that there is a hard problem in Chalmers's sense.

**Thomas Nagel (1937–present)**: "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974) articulated the subjective character of experience as irreducible to physical description. *Mind and Cosmos* (2012) argues that materialism is inadequate to explain consciousness, reason, and value.

**Colin McGinn (1950–present)**: Mysterianism: the human mind may be constitutionally incapable of solving the mind-body problem. *The Problem of Consciousness* (1991).
