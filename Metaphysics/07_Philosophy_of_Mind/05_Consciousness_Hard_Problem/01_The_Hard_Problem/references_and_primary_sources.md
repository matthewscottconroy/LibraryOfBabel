# References and Primary Sources: The Hard Problem of Consciousness

## Foundational Texts

- Chalmers, David, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 2 (1995). Introduces the distinction between the easy problems and the hard problem; argues that functional-physical explanation cannot account for phenomenal experience.

- Chalmers, David, *The Conscious Mind* (1996). Book-length development of the hard problem and its implications for the philosophy of mind, arguing for naturalistic dualism as the most promising response.

- Nagel, Thomas, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" *Philosophical Review* 83 (1974). Independently motivates the hard problem: any adequate theory of mind must explain subjective experience, which resists objective treatment.

## Contemporary Works

- Frankish, Keith, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23 (2016). Argues that phenomenal consciousness as Chalmers conceives it is an introspective illusion, deflating the hard problem rather than solving it.

- Levine, Joseph, *Purple Haze: The Puzzle of Consciousness* (2001). A careful examination of the explanatory gap and its relationship to the hard problem; argues the gap is genuine without committing to full-blown property dualism.
