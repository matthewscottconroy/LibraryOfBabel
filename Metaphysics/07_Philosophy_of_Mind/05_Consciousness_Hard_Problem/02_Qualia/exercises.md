# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Jackson's reversal**: Jackson says Mary only learns which physical state is the one she previously knew about as "the state that causes color perception." Is this adequate? When Mary sees red, does she merely learn an identity ("oh, that's the state I knew about"), or does she learn something genuinely new about the qualitative character of red?

2. **Dennett's challenge**: Dennett argues that the intuitions supporting qualia — that they are intrinsic, ineffable, private — are all artifacts of a mistaken self-model. Design a thought experiment to test whether phenomenal redness could turn out to be purely functional. Is there any empirical evidence that could settle this question?

3. **Inverted spectra revisited**: Suppose Martians have an inverted color spectrum relative to humans but behave identically (both call grass "green," both stop at red lights). Is there a fact of the matter about whether Martians see phenomenal red or phenomenal green when looking at grass? What would establish it?

4. **The grain argument**: Brain states are spatially extended and have grain (they are made of neurons with spatial properties). Phenomenal states seem not to be spatially extended in the same way. Does this "grain difference" establish that phenomenal states are not brain states?

5. **Access vs. phenomenal consciousness**: Ned Block argues a patient with blindsight has visual information that is access-conscious (they can use it for behavior) without being phenomenally conscious (there is nothing it is like to see it). Is this coherent? Could there be phenomenal experience that is not access-conscious?
