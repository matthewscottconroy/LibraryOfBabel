# Important Figures

**Frank Jackson (1943–present)**: The knowledge argument in "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982) is the most-discussed argument from qualia against physicalism. Jackson's subsequent reversal toward physicalism ("What Mary Didn't Know," then "Mind and Illusion") defines the dialectic.

**Daniel Dennett (1942–present)**: "Quining Qualia" (1988) argues that the traditional notion of qualia is incoherent. Qualia as traditionally described — intrinsic, ineffable, private, directly apprehensible — do not exist. What appear to be qualia are functional states misdescribed.

**Sydney Shoemaker (1931–present)**: Developed functional theories of qualia — qualia are intrinsic properties of mental states, but their intrinsic character is constituted by their functional role. "Functionalism and Qualia" (1975) and "Qualia and Consciousness" (1993).

**Ned Block (1942–present)**: Distinguishes phenomenal consciousness (qualia) from access consciousness (information availability). "On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness" (1995) is the canonical paper.

**Wilfrid Sellars (1912–1989)**: Distinguished "manifest image" (qualia as they appear) from "scientific image" (physical processes). His work on the "grain objection" to the identity theory is foundational.
