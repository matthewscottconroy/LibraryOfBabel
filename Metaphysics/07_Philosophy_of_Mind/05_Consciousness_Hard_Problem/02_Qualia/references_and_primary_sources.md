# References and Primary Sources: Qualia

## Foundational Texts

- Jackson, Frank, "Epiphenomenal Qualia," *Philosophical Quarterly* 32 (1982). The knowledge argument—Mary's Room—argues that qualia are not physical properties; Mary learns a new phenomenal fact upon seeing red for the first time.

- Nagel, Thomas, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" *Philosophical Review* 83 (1974). Argues that the subjective character of experience—what it is like—is a genuine feature of reality that cannot be captured in objective terms.

- Lewis, David, "What Experience Teaches," in *Mind and Cognition* edited by Lycan (1990). The "ability hypothesis" response to Jackson: Mary learns new abilities (to recognize, imagine, and remember red), not new propositional knowledge about an irreducible phenomenal property.

## Contemporary Works

- Dennett, Daniel, "Quining Qualia," in *Consciousness in Contemporary Science* edited by Marcel and Bisiach (1988). Argues that the concept of qualia, as standardly deployed in thought experiments, is incoherent; we should eliminate it rather than explain it.

- Shoemaker, Sydney, "The Inverted Spectrum," *Journal of Philosophy* 79 (1982). A careful examination of the inverted qualia hypothesis, arguing for functional individuation of qualia while acknowledging the intuitive force of spectrum inversion.
