# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **HOT theory test**: According to Rosenthal, a mental state is conscious iff one has a higher-order thought that one is in that state. Could you be in a higher-order mental state that misrepresents your first-order state? If yes, would you then be conscious of having an experience you're not actually having? Is this plausible?

2. **Global workspace and anesthesia**: Under general anesthesia, information processing in the brain continues but global broadcast is suppressed. Does this explain the loss of consciousness under anesthesia in a way that supports GWT? Does it also explain why anesthesia awareness (consciousness during surgery while apparently anesthetized) is so distressing?

3. **IIT's counterintuitive predictions**: According to IIT, a simple two-dimensional grid of logic gates could be highly conscious if appropriately connected. Is this prediction a reductio of IIT, or does it reveal that we have mistaken intuitions about consciousness? Could you accept that grids of logic gates are conscious?

4. **Neural correlates of consciousness**: NCC research identifies which neural processes are correlated with conscious experience. Chalmers argues that finding neural correlates does not solve the hard problem — it just identifies which physical processes consciousness is correlated with, not why these processes produce consciousness. Is this objection decisive?

5. **Self-representationalism**: Kriegel argues that conscious states represent themselves as well as their objects. Is self-representation sufficient for consciousness? Could a self-representing system lack consciousness? Can you think of a counterexample?
