# Important Terms and Ideas: Theories of Consciousness

**Global Workspace Theory**
Baars and Dehaene's theory: consciousness arises when information is broadcast from a global workspace to many specialized cognitive systems simultaneously. Conscious states are those that achieve widespread availability for reasoning, memory, action control, and verbal report.

**Higher-Order Thought (HOT) Theory**
Rosenthal's view: a mental state is phenomenally conscious when it is accompanied by a suitable higher-order thought to the effect that one is in that state. Consciousness is a matter of being represented at a higher meta-cognitive level; states not accompanied by such thoughts are unconscious.

**Integrated Information Theory (IIT)**
Tononi's theory: consciousness is identical to integrated information, measured as Φ (phi). A system is conscious to the degree that it generates information above and beyond its parts. The theory entails that any system with high Φ is conscious, even if not biological.

**Neural Correlates of Consciousness (NCCs)**
The minimal neural mechanisms jointly sufficient for a specific conscious experience. Identifying NCCs is a central empirical project in consciousness science; philosophical debate concerns what the correlation between neural states and phenomenal states implies about their relationship.

**Recurrent Processing Theory**
Victor Lamme's theory: consciousness arises from recurrent processing—feedback connections within cortical areas. Feed-forward processing generates unconscious representations; recurrent processing generates phenomenal consciousness. This is distinct from global workspace theories that emphasize broadcasting.

**Predictive Processing**
The view (Clark, Friston) that the brain is a prediction machine that constantly generates and updates models of the world. Some versions propose that consciousness arises from specific features of predictive processing—precision-weighted prediction errors—offering a unified account of perception, action, and consciousness.
