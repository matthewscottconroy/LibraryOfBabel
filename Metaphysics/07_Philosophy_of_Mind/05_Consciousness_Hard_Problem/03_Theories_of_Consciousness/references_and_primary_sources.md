# References and Primary Sources: Theories of Consciousness

## Foundational Texts

- Baars, Bernard, *A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness* (1988). Introduces Global Workspace Theory: consciousness arises when information is "broadcast" to a global workspace accessible to many cognitive systems; conscious states are those that achieve wide availability.

- Rosenthal, David, "Two Concepts of Consciousness," *Philosophical Studies* 49 (1986). The Higher-Order Thought (HOT) theory: a mental state is conscious when accompanied by a suitable higher-order thought about it; phenomenal consciousness requires meta-cognitive representation.

- Tononi, Giulio, "Consciousness as Integrated Information: A Provisional Manifesto," *Biological Bulletin* 215 (2008). Integrated Information Theory (IIT): consciousness is identical to integrated information (Φ); the degree of consciousness of a system equals its capacity to integrate information above and beyond its parts.

## Contemporary Works

- Dehaene, Stanislas, *Consciousness and the Brain* (2014). An empirical and theoretical development of Global Workspace Theory, examining the neural correlates of consciousness and the role of global ignition in conscious access.

- Chalmers, David, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 2 (1995). Reviews major theories and argues that none solve the hard problem, motivating the search for psychophysical laws.
