# Panpsychism

If consciousness cannot be derived from matter that is entirely non-conscious, perhaps the solution is to hold that matter is not entirely non-conscious. Panpsychism — the view that consciousness or proto-conscious properties are fundamental and ubiquitous features of reality — has a long history in philosophy (Leibniz, Whitehead, Russell) but has attracted renewed serious attention in contemporary philosophy of mind, driven by the perceived intractability of the hard problem.

The panpsychist's key argument is a modus tollens against the standard materialist picture. The standard picture holds that consciousness is a high-level property that emerges from entirely non-conscious matter; but no account of how this emergence works has been forthcoming — there is an explanatory gap between the physical description of the brain and the phenomenal character of experience. If consciousness cannot emerge from non-conscious matter, and if consciousness clearly exists, then perhaps consciousness (or something like it) is present at the fundamental level of nature.

Russell's panpsychism — Russellian monism — offers a specific variant. Russell noted that physics tells us only the structural and relational properties of the physical world (mass, charge, spacetime position), not its intrinsic, categorical properties. What fills in those intrinsic properties? Russell suggested that phenomenal properties might be the intrinsic nature of physical reality. Physical science gives us the structural facts; phenomenology gives us the intrinsic facts; and the two descriptions are complementary rather than competing.

The main challenge for panpsychism is the "combination problem": how do the micro-level proto-conscious properties of fundamental particles combine to produce the rich, unified conscious experience of the human mind? If electrons have micro-experiences, how do their micro-experiences combine into my experience of seeing the red apple? This combination problem is, arguably, as hard as the original hard problem.

This section examines panpsychism as a serious contemporary philosophical position, its motivations, its forms, and the challenges it faces.
