# Chalmers and Russellian Monism

David Chalmers is the most prominent contemporary defender of Russellian monism, and his case for the view is best understood against the backdrop of the options he finds inadequate. Type-B materialism — a posteriori physicalism — holds that consciousness is physical but that the identity is discoverable only empirically, not a priori; Chalmers argues this cannot explain why zombies are conceivable even after ideal reflection. Type-D dualism — interactionism — accepts the reality of phenomenal properties as non-physical but must explain how they causally influence physical events without violating causal closure. Type-E dualism — epiphenomenalism — avoids the causal closure problem by making phenomenal properties causally inert, but then cannot explain why we have beliefs about our experiences, since those beliefs would be caused by physical states, not by the experiences themselves. Eliminativism denies the reality of phenomenal properties but cannot explain away the data it claims not to need.

Russellian monism — type-F monism in Chalmers's taxonomy — occupies a position that he argues avoids the most serious problems of its rivals. The key distinction from standard physicalism: standard physicalism is committed to the claim that physical properties as characterized by physics are all the intrinsic properties there are. Russellian monism denies this. Physical properties have structural-causal profiles, but their intrinsic nature is not specified by physics. Physics is a purely structural science.

The argument from physics:

- P1: Physics describes only the structural-relational properties of matter — its causal profile.
- P2: If standard physicalism is true, the intrinsic nature of matter is exhausted by these structural-relational properties.
- P3: Phenomenal consciousness is not a structural-relational property.
- C: If standard physicalism is true, consciousness is either identical to a structural-relational property or unexplained.

Since consciousness cannot be identified with any structural-relational property without closing the explanatory gap a priori — which Chalmers argues is not possible — standard physicalism leaves consciousness unexplained. Russellian monism resolves this by rejecting P2: the intrinsic nature of physical properties is phenomenal or proto-phenomenal, filling the gap that physics leaves open.

## Causal Efficacy and the Space of Views

Chalmers emphasizes that Russellian monism makes consciousness causally efficacious in a direct way. Since consciousness (or its precursors) constitutes the intrinsic nature of physical properties, it is involved in all physical causation. There is no problem of mental causation because mentality is not a superadded extra; it is woven into the fabric of physical reality. This contrasts with property dualism, where phenomenal properties are non-physical additions to physical properties and must somehow causally influence physical processes without violating causal closure. On Russellian monism, all causation that happens via physical properties also involves their intrinsic natures — mental causation is not a problem because it is identical with physical causation described from the inside.

The Russellian framework is compatible with several distinct positions on the nature of the intrinsic properties. Panpsychist Russellian monism (Galen Strawson, Philip Goff) holds that the intrinsic natures are genuinely experiential: fundamental particles have primitive experiences. Panprotopsychist Russellian monism holds that the intrinsic natures are proto-experiential — the material from which experience is constructed, without being experience themselves. Idealist Russellian monism holds that the intrinsic natures are fully mental, and matter is a structural appearance of a mental reality. Neutral monist Russellian monism holds that the intrinsic natures are neither mental nor physical but a neutral underlying reality of which both mind and matter are aspects. What unifies these variants is the Russellian insight that physics describes structure without revealing intrinsic nature.

Chalmers treats Russellian monism as a research program rather than a completed theory. The combination problem — how proto-phenomenal properties give rise to full phenomenal consciousness — remains open. The positive characterization of intrinsic natures remains open. Whether the correct variant is panpsychist or panprotopsychist or idealist remains open. Chalmers's contribution is primarily to clarify the conceptual space: Russellian monism is a more attractive starting point than its rivals because it satisfies both the demands of physicalism (causal closure, the causal efficacy of consciousness) and the demands of consciousness studies (the reality of phenomenal properties, the explanatory gap) better than any alternative. Whether the combination problem can be solved within the framework is the central open question.
