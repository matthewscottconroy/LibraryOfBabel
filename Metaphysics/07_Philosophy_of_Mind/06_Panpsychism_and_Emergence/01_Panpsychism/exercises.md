# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Panpsychism vs. animism**: Panpsychists distinguish their view from animism (full consciousness everywhere) by holding that only proto-mental properties are fundamental. But what exactly is a "proto-mental" property? Is it just a disposition to produce consciousness under the right conditions? Does this reduce panpsychism to a form of functionalism?

2. **Combination problem as reductio**: The combination problem asks how micro-experiences combine into macro-experiences. Is this problem fatal to panpsychism? Compare it to the equivalent problem for physicalism (how do non-experiential micro-physical processes combine to produce consciousness). Which problem is harder?

3. **Galileo's error**: Goff argues that Galileo's exclusion of qualitative properties from science was a methodological error. But wasn't it a successful methodological error? Does panpsychism require revising scientific methodology, or just adding phenomenology as an additional fundamental level?

4. **Cosmopsychism**: If the universe as a whole is the fundamental conscious subject, then individual humans are parts of a larger consciousness. What are the ethical and metaphysical implications of cosmopsychism? Is it a form of idealism?

5. **Parsimony**: Physicalism and dualism each face explanatory challenges. Does panpsychism solve these challenges parsimoniously, or does it simply move them around? Is the combination problem harder or easier than the hard problem?
