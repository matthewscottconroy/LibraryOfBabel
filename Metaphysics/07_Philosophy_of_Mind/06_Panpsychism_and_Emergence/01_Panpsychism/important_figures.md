# Important Figures

**G.W. Leibniz (1646–1716)**: Monadology attributes some form of perception (petite perceptions) to all monads, including fundamental units of reality. Leibniz is the proto-modern panpsychist.

**Arthur Schopenhauer (1788–1860)**: *The World as Will and Representation* (1818) holds that the underlying reality of the world is Will — a blind, striving force that is the metaphysical ground of experience. Proto-panpsychist.

**William James (1842–1910)**: Advocated "psychical monism" — consciousness is everywhere, though most of it is below human awareness. The Radical Empiricist tradition.

**Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947)**: Process philosophy in *Process and Reality* (1929) attributes experience to all actual occasions. The most developed systematic panpsychist metaphysics.

**Galen Strawson (1952–present)**: "Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism" (2006) argues that a consistent physicalism must accept panpsychism, since consciousness is real and must have physical grounds.
