# Important Terms and Ideas: Classical Panpsychism

**Panpsychism**
The view that mentality (in some form: experience, sentience, or proto-mentality) is a fundamental and universal feature of reality, not an emergent property of complex biological systems. Every physical entity has some form of inner life or experiential property, however minimal.

**Panexperientialism**
A form of panpsychism associated with Whitehead and Griffin that holds all physical entities have some form of experience—some form of "taking account of" their environment. This is not full human-like consciousness but a primitive analogue present throughout nature.

**Hylozoism**
The ancient view that all matter is alive or has life-like properties. Closely related to panpsychism but focused on life rather than mind; the Milesian philosophers (Thales, Anaximenes) are often cited as hylozoists.

**Neutral Monism**
The view that the fundamental stuff of reality is neither mental nor physical but neutral—capable of being described in either mental or physical terms. William James and Bertrand Russell defended versions of this position, which is related to panpsychism but distinct.

**Psychophysical Identity (Panpsychist)**
On some panpsychist views, mental properties are identical with or constituted by physical properties—not by reducing the mental to the non-mental physical, but by seeing all physical properties as having an intrinsic mental or proto-mental nature.

**Cosmopsychism**
A version of panpsychism that takes the universe as a whole (rather than fundamental particles) as the primary bearer of consciousness, with individual conscious minds being derivative aspects of the cosmic mind. Defended by some contemporary panpsychists as avoiding the combination problem.
