# References and Primary Sources: Classical Panpsychism

## Foundational Texts

- Leibniz, G.W., *Monadology* (1714). The canonical early modern panpsychist text: all genuine substances (monads) are mind-like, with perception as a universal feature; what we call matter is a confused perception of a swarm of monads.

- Fechner, Gustav, *Zend-Avesta* (1851). A nineteenth-century scientific panpsychism: consciousness is a universal feature of matter, with different degrees of complexity corresponding to different degrees of mentality. Fechner's work influenced James and early scientific psychology.

- James, William, *The Principles of Psychology* (1890) and *A Pluralistic Universe* (1909). James was drawn toward a panpsychist view he called "pansychism" or "radical empiricism"—the idea that pure experience is the fundamental stuff of reality, both mental and physical.

## Contemporary Works

- Skrbina, David, *Panpsychism in the West* (2005). A comprehensive historical survey of panpsychist thought from the pre-Socratics to the present day.

- Seager, William, "Consciousness, Information, and Panpsychism," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 2 (1995). An influential contemporary defense of panpsychism as a response to the hard problem.
