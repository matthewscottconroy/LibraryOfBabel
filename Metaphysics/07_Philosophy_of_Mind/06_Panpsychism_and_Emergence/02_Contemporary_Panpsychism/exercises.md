# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Goff's argument**: Goff argues that because the hard problem is real and because interaction problems beset dualism, panpsychism is the most plausible option. Construct the strongest objection to Goff's argument. Is panpsychism really better positioned than its alternatives?

2. **Russellian monism and physics**: If consciousness is the intrinsic nature of physical properties, does this change our understanding of physics? What new empirical predictions would Russellian monism make, if any? Is it empirically distinguishable from physicalism or dualism?

3. **The grain objection revisited**: Panpsychism says particles have experiential properties. But the experiential quality of a photon (if it has any) seems impossibly unlike the rich experience of seeing red. Can proto-experiential properties at the micro-level explain the specific phenomenology of macro-level experience?

4. **Seager on emergence**: Seager considers whether emergence could solve the combination problem — macro-experience emerging from micro-experience. Is this strong emergence or weak emergence? Does it solve the combination problem or merely describe it?

5. **Functional panpsychism**: Some argue that functional organization at the micro-level is sufficient for proto-mentality. But if functional organization is sufficient for proto-mentality at the micro-level, does this not imply that functional organization is sufficient for full consciousness at the macro-level — collapsing panpsychism into functionalism?
