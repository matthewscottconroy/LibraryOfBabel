# Important Terms and Ideas: Contemporary Panpsychism

**Micropsychism**
The panpsychist view that fundamental physical entities (particles, fields) have micro-experiential properties—primitive, simple forms of experience. Human consciousness emerges from or is constituted by the combination of these micro-experiences in complex systems.

**The Combination Problem**
The central challenge for contemporary panpsychism: how do micro-experiences combine to form the rich, unified phenomenal consciousness of a person? If each particle has a simple micro-experience, it is unclear how their combination produces the unified, holistic consciousness we find in humans. This may be as hard as the original problem of emergence from non-experiential matter.

**Russellian Monism**
The view, inspired by Russell's neutral monism, that physics characterizes the structural and dispositional properties of matter but is silent about its intrinsic nature. Consciousness—or proto-consciousness—is the intrinsic nature of physical properties. This view accommodates both the completeness of physics and the irreducibility of consciousness.

**Constitutive vs. Emergent Panpsychism**
Constitutive panpsychism: macroscopic consciousness is constituted by or identical to combinations of micro-experiences. Emergent panpsychism: macro-consciousness emerges from but is not identical to micro-experiences. The combination problem is primarily a problem for the constitutive version.

**Subject Summing**
The combination problem made vivid: there are many micro-experiential subjects at the microphysical level; how does their combination produce a single unified experiential subject? Mere aggregation of subjects does not seem to yield a new subject; additional principles are needed.

**Integrated Information and Panpsychism**
Some defenders of Integrated Information Theory interpret it panpsychistically: since Φ can be non-zero for very simple systems, even basic physical systems have some degree of consciousness. This gives panpsychism a scientific guise, though critics argue it leads to implausible implications (thermostats are conscious).
