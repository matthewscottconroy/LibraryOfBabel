# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Is illusionism self-undermining?**: Illusionism holds that phenomenal consciousness is an illusion. But an illusion must appear to be something other than what it is. If there is an illusory appearance of phenomenal properties, is that appearance not itself a phenomenal property? Can Frankish escape this regress?

2. **Heterophenomenology**: Dennett proposes "heterophenomenology" — treating subjects' reports about their experience as data about their beliefs about their experience, rather than as direct reports of experience. Is this a viable methodology? Does it beg the question against the existence of qualia?

3. **Evolutionary argument for illusionism**: Frankish argues that we represent ourselves as having phenomenal properties because this representation is evolutionarily useful. Design a thought experiment that tests this claim. What would it mean for a representation to be "useful" in this context?

4. **The meta-problem without the hard problem**: Chalmers argues that solving the meta-problem does not solve the hard problem. Could the meta-problem be solved by showing that the appearance of phenomenal consciousness is a misrepresentation — that we represent ourselves as having qualia but don't? Would this dissolve the hard problem?

5. **Introspective reliability**: Schwitzgebel argues that introspection is systematically unreliable. If this is right, what can we conclude about the phenomenology of our experience? Does unreliable introspection support or undermine the hard problem?
