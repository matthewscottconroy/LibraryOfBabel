# Important Terms and Ideas: Illusionism

**Illusionism**
Frankish's term for the view that phenomenal consciousness—as ordinarily conceived, with intrinsic qualia—is an introspective illusion. We have strong intuitions that our experiences have ineffable, intrinsic qualitative properties, but these intuitions are misleading; what really exists are functional states with no such properties.

**Introspective Error**
The illusionist's explanation of why people believe in qualia: introspection systematically misrepresents functional states as having intrinsic qualitative properties they lack. The appearance of qualia is itself a cognitive/representational phenomenon with a functional-physical explanation.

**The Meta-Problem**
Chalmers's formulation: even if illusionism is correct and there are no qualia, we still need to explain why we believe we have qualia—why the hard problem seems so pressing. The meta-problem (explaining the appearance of qualia) may be tractable within functional-physical terms, providing a partial vindication of illusionism.

**Heterophenomenology**
Dennett's method: take subjects' first-person reports of their experience as data to be explained scientifically, without assuming that these reports accurately describe an inner phenomenal reality. This methodological stance brackets questions about the ultimate nature of qualia.

**Multiple Drafts Model**
Dennett's account of consciousness: there is no single point in the brain where information is integrated into a unified conscious experience (no Cartesian Theater). Instead, multiple processes generate multiple "drafts" of experience in parallel, with no privileged final version.

**Quasi-Realism about Qualia**
A position that accepts the functional reality of states that underlie quale-talk while denying that these states have the special intrinsic properties that constitute qualia in the philosophical sense. This is close to illusionism but frames itself as preserving rather than eliminating the phenomenon.
