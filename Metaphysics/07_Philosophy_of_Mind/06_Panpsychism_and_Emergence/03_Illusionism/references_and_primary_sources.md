# References and Primary Sources: Illusionism

## Foundational Texts

- Frankish, Keith, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23 (2016). The defining statement of contemporary illusionism: phenomenal consciousness as standardly conceived (with intrinsic, ineffable qualia) is an introspective illusion; what is real are functional states that we misdescribe as qualitative.

- Dennett, Daniel, *Consciousness Explained* (1991). Dennett's heterophenomenology and multiple drafts model; argues against Cartesian Theater models of consciousness and that qualia as intrinsic properties are a myth.

- Rey, Georges, "Towards a Computational Account of Akrasia and Self-Deception," in *The Multiple Self* edited by Elster (1986). An early eliminativist view arguing that phenomenal properties as typically conceived do not exist.

## Contemporary Works

- Frankish, Keith (ed.), *Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness* (2017). Anthology collecting contributions from defenders and critics of the illusionist position.

- Chalmers, David, "The Meta-Problem of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 25 (2018). Chalmers's response to illusionism: there is a genuine meta-problem of explaining why we believe we have qualia even if we don't, but this does not dissolve the hard problem of explaining consciousness itself.
