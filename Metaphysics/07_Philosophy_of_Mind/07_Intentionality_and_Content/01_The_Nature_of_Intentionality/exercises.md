# Exercises

1. **Intentionality without a subject**: Can a computer program have genuine intentionality — genuine aboutness — or only "derived" intentionality (intentionality attributed by users)? What would be the criteria for distinguishing original from derived intentionality?

2. **Non-intentional mental states**: Identify three mental states that might lack intentionality (moods, phenomenal pain, etc.). Does their existence refute Brentano's thesis that intentionality is the mark of the mental? Or can they be reanalyzed as intentional?

3. **Intensional contexts**: "Oedipus wanted to marry Jocasta" is true; "Oedipus wanted to marry his mother" is false (given his intentions). How does the failure of substitution here reveal the distinctive structure of intentional content? Does this failure pose a problem for causal theories of content?

4. **The aboutness of maps**: A map of Paris is "about" Paris in a sense. Is map-aboutness the same kind of intentionality as thought-aboutness? What are the similarities and differences? Does this suggest that intentionality is more widespread than thought?

5. **Natural signs vs. mental representations**: A tree ring is a "sign" of a year's growth. Smoke is a "sign" of fire. Are these cases of intentionality? What distinguishes natural signs from genuine intentional states?
