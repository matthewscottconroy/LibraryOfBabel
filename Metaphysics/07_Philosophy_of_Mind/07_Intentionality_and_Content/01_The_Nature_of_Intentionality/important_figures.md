# Important Figures

**Franz Brentano (1838–1917)**: Reintroduced intentionality as the mark of the mental in *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint* (1874). His direct influence on Husserl, Meinong, and the phenomenological tradition is incalculable.

**Alexius Meinong (1853–1920)**: Extended Brentano's theory of intentional objects to include non-existent objects in the "theory of objects" (*Gegenstandstheorie*). Bertrand Russell's theory of descriptions was partly a response to Meinong.

**Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)**: Developed intentional analysis into the method of phenomenology. His distinction between noesis and noema, and his analysis of intentional fulfillment and frustration, are central to the tradition.

**Jerry Fodor (1935–2017)**: The language of thought hypothesis holds that mental states have intentional content in virtue of their computational-representational structure. *The Language of Thought* (1975) and *Psychosemantics* (1987).

**Daniel Dennett (1942–present)**: The intentional stance — treating a system as if it has beliefs and desires — is a useful predictive strategy that does not require intentionality to be a fundamental feature of the world. *The Intentional Stance* (1987).
