# References and Primary Sources: The Nature of Intentionality

## Foundational Texts

- Brentano, Franz, *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint* (1874). Reintroduces intentionality as the mark of the mental: mental phenomena are distinguished from physical phenomena by their intentional inexistence—they are always directed toward or about an object.

- Husserl, Edmund, *Logical Investigations* (1900–01). Systematic phenomenological investigation of intentionality: the intentional act, its content (noema), and its object are carefully distinguished; this framework shapes all subsequent phenomenological treatments of mind.

- Chisholm, Roderick, *Perceiving: A Philosophical Study* (1957). Introduces the notion of intentionality into analytic philosophy through a linguistic criterion: sentences about intentional states have peculiar logical features (opacity, non-existential import) that distinguish them from physical descriptions.

## Contemporary Works

- Crane, Tim, *Elements of Mind* (2001). A clear and philosophically careful treatment of intentionality, its relationship to consciousness, and the main theories of mental content.

- Searle, John, *Intentionality: An Essay in the Philosophy of Mind* (1983). A biological naturalist account of intentionality: intentionality is a real biological phenomenon produced by the brain's causal powers; it is not reducible to computation or functional organization.
