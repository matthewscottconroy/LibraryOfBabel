# Narrow Content

Oscar and Twin Oscar behave identically. Both reach for liquids, both make the same utterances, both form the same inferences in similar situations. Yet their wide contents differ: Oscar's water-thought is about H₂O; Twin Oscar's is about XYZ. If behavior is determined by internal states, and wide content is not determined by internal states, then wide content is not what figures in the causal explanation of behavior. Something else must be the psychologically relevant notion. That something else is **narrow content** — the content of a mental state that supervenes solely on the subject's intrinsic physical or functional properties, independent of environmental differences.

Fodor makes the argument explicit. Cognitive psychology identifies mental states by their causal roles in explaining behavior. Since Oscar and Twin Oscar behave identically in relevantly similar situations, cognitive psychology must cite the same mental states to explain their behavior. But their wide contents differ. Therefore, cognitive psychology individuates states by something other than wide content. The psychologically real kind is narrow content, not wide content.

Several conceptions of narrow content have been developed. On **functional role content**, narrow content is determined by the inferential and causal role a mental state plays within the subject's cognitive economy — how it relates to perceptual inputs, other mental states, and behavioral outputs. The narrow content of WATER is characterized by its inferential connections (water is a liquid, water quenches thirst, water is necessary for life) and its role in generating behavior, independently of what actual substance these connections concern. Two subjects share narrow content if and only if their concepts play the same functional role.

The **two-dimensional conception**, developed by Chalmers following Kaplan and Stalnaker, characterizes narrow content as a function from possible environments (contexts) to extensions (wide contents). The narrow content of WATER is a function that maps any environment to the watery stuff in that environment — whatever plays the role of water there. Oscar and Twin Oscar share the same narrow content (the same function), but their applications of this function to their respective actual environments yield different wide contents (H₂O and XYZ). **Conceptual role semantics**, following Sellars and Brandom, characterizes narrow content as a concept's place in the "space of reasons" — the inferential practice — rather than its external reference.

## Three Challenges

The **holism problem** threatens functional role content. A concept's narrow content depends on all the inferential relations it has to other concepts. But if narrow content is holistic, any change in belief changes the narrow content of all related concepts, making content attribution across different belief systems highly unstable. Two speakers with slightly different background beliefs would have different narrow contents for their shared concepts, undermining the possibility of genuine disagreement or communication between different thinkers.

The **reality problem** asks whether narrow content is psychologically real — whether it corresponds to anything in the subject's actual psychology. Functional role content is often too abstract and too fine-grained to be identified with any neural or computational structure. If narrow content is merely a theoretical construct with no psychological correlate, it cannot play the explanatory role Fodor assigns to it. The demand for causal relevance, which motivates the appeal to narrow content in the first place, rebounds against it.

The **externalist challenge** presses harder. Burge and McDowell argue that narrow content is not a genuine psychological kind — it is an abstraction that plays no role in actual intentional explanation. Actual intentional explanation always explains how subjects are related to a specific environment. Abstracting away from the environment does not yield a richer notion of content; it yields an impoverished notion with no role in psychological or semantic explanation. When I explain why Oscar went to the kitchen, I explain his action as fetching water — and that description is essentially environment-relative. No narrow content description can replace it without changing the subject.

McDowell presses the philosophical point most directly. The very idea of narrow content reinstates a Cartesian picture that should be abandoned: an inner content that is then connected to the world by an external relation. Genuine thought is world-directed from the outset; there is no layer of purely internal content beneath this world-directedness. A thought that P just is the thought that P is the case in the world. The demand for narrow content as something prior to and independent of wide content reflects a philosophical illusion about the inner life.

A **two-factor compromise** holds that mental states have both a narrow cognitive component and a wide semantic component, each playing legitimate roles in explanation. The cognitive component explains the causal role of the state in the subject's psychology; the semantic component explains the intentional relationship between the subject and the world. The challenge is to specify the relationship between the two components clearly enough to show they are genuinely complementary. If narrow content is merely a function for generating wide content given context, it is not clear that it has any intrinsic intentional character of its own — which raises the question whether the two-factor view is a stable theoretical position or a restatement of the problem.
