# Exercises

1. **Twin Earth original**: Before 1750, Earthlings and Twin Earthlings were molecule-for-molecule identical. Did they have the same thoughts about water? If yes, does this show that pre-scientific meaning is narrow? If no, does this have surprising implications about the role of science in concept individuation?

2. **Burge's arthritis case**: Alf believes he has arthritis in his thigh (incorrectly). His doppelganger Alf* lives in a community that uses "arthritis" correctly to include thigh conditions. Do Alf and Alf* have the same beliefs? What determines the content of their beliefs about arthritis?

3. **Self-knowledge and externalism**: If content is externally determined, then I can be wrong about what I believe — I might think I believe that water is wet but actually believe that XYZ is wet (if I'm on Twin Earth without knowing it). Does externalism threaten the special status of first-person self-knowledge?

4. **Narrow content test**: Construct a thought experiment that tests whether narrow content (content that is the same for you and your Twin Earth counterpart) can play any explanatory role in cognitive science. Is there any cognitive phenomenon that requires narrow content?

5. **Social norms and content**: Burge argues that social practices determine the content of individual concepts. But individuals can deviate from and revise social practices. When does individual deviance change the content of your concept, and when does it merely make you deviant?
