# Important Figures

**Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)**: "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (1975) introduced Twin Earth and semantic externalism about natural kind terms. Putnam distinguished extension (what a term applies to) from stereotype (the associated description).

**Tyler Burge (1946–present)**: Extended externalism to ordinary concepts in "Individualism and the Mind" (1979) and "Individualism and Self-Knowledge" (1988). Burge's social externalism is the dominant form of content externalism.

**Jerry Fodor (1935–2017)**: Defended internalism and narrow content in *Psychosemantics* and *The Elm and the Expert*. Fodor argued that narrow content (content independent of environment) is required for psychological explanation.

**Michael Devitt (1938–present)**: Challenged Twin Earth thought experiments and defended a form of causal externalism that is more modest than Putnam's. *Coming to Our Senses* (1996).

**Frank Jackson (1943–present)**: Defended two-dimensional semantics as a framework for accommodating both internalist (primary intension) and externalist (secondary intension) intuitions.
