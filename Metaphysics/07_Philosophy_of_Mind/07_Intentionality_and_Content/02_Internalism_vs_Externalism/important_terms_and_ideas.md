# Important Terms and Ideas: Internalism vs. Externalism about Mental Content

**Semantic Externalism**
The view that the contents of mental states are partly constituted by facts about the external environment—the natural kinds one's thoughts are causally connected to, or the social-linguistic community one belongs to. What you mean by "water" depends on what water actually is, not just on what is in your head.

**Twin Earth**
Putnam's thought experiment: on Twin Earth, there is a liquid XYZ that looks and behaves like water but has a different chemical composition. A person on Earth and their Twin Earth counterpart use "water" to refer to H₂O and XYZ respectively, despite being molecule-for-molecule identical. This shows that content is not determined by intrinsic physical states.

**Narrow Content**
The component of mental content that is determined solely by the intrinsic properties of the thinker—what is "in the head." Narrow content is shared by me and my Twin Earth counterpart. Fodor and others defend narrow content as the relevant notion for psychological explanation.

**Wide Content**
Mental content that is partly constituted by the external environment or social community. What I mean by "water" in the wide sense is H₂O, even if I do not know the chemical composition. Wide content is not shared by intrinsic duplicates in different environments.

**Social Externalism (Burge)**
Burge's extension: thought contents are partly constituted by the semantic norms of one's linguistic community, not just causal-historical connections to natural kinds. If I use "arthritis" incorrectly, my thought still has the content determined by how the community uses the term.

**Privileged Access**
The claim that we have a special, authoritative, first-person knowledge of our own mental states. Externalism threatens privileged access: if content depends on the external world, how can we know our own contents without empirical investigation of the environment?
