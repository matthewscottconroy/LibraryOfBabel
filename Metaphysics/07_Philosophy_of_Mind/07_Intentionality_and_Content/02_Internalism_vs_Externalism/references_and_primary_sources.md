# References and Primary Sources: Internalism vs. Externalism about Mental Content

## Foundational Texts

- Putnam, Hilary, "The Meaning of 'Meaning'," in *Mind, Language and Reality* (1975). Twin Earth thought experiment: mental content is not "in the head"—meaning and extension are determined partly by the external environment. This is the canonical statement of semantic externalism.

- Burge, Tyler, "Individualism and the Mental," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 4 (1979). Extends externalism to mental content generally (not just natural-kind terms): thought contents are partly constituted by the social-linguistic community, not just by intrinsic properties of the thinker.

- Fodor, Jerry, *The Language of Thought* (1975). The classic defense of representational internalism: thought contents are determined by syntactic properties of internal representations; content is "narrow"—fixed by what is in the head.

## Contemporary Works

- McKinsey, Michael, "Anti-Individualism and Privileged Access," *Analysis* 51 (1991). Argues that externalism about content is incompatible with privileged access to one's own mental states, generating an a priori path from self-knowledge to facts about the external world.

- Farkas, Katalin, *The Subject's Point of View* (2008). Defends a form of internalism about phenomenal character and perceptual experience, arguing that how things seem is determined by the intrinsic properties of the subject.
