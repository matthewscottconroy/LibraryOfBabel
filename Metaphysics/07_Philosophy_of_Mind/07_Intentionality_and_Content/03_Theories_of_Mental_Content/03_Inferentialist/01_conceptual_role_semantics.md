# Conceptual Role Semantics

Consider the concept BACHELOR. It is partially defined by its inferential relations: a bachelor is an unmarried adult male, so BACHELOR is inferentially connected to UNMARRIED, ADULT, and MALE. Someone who lacked all these connections — who inferred nothing from being told that someone was a bachelor — would not seem to possess the concept. The inferential structure is partly constitutive of what the concept means. **Conceptual role semantics** (CRS) generalizes this observation: the meaning or content of a mental representation is determined by the inferential and computational role it plays within the cognitive system — its relations to other representations, to perceptual inputs, and to behavioral outputs. Rather than looking outward to causal relations with the world, CRS looks inward to the structural relations within the mind.

The concept RED is connected to perceptual inputs (red things cause RED-tokenings under normal conditions), to other color concepts (RED is more similar to ORANGE than to BLUE, incompatible with GREEN at the same location), and to practical commitments (avoiding red if one dislikes it, selecting red if one wants it). The inferential and perceptual connections of the concept seem to be what make it the concept RED rather than some other concept. The ELECTRON concept would have different content if one lacked the rest of one's physics beliefs — it is connected by inference to CHARGE, MASS, QUANTUM, and ORBITAL. This holism is a feature of CRS, not a bug: it explains why the theoretical terms of science have rich, interconnected content, and why concept acquisition involves learning a whole web of inferential relations rather than simply attaching a label to an object.

CRS comes in two versions. **Narrow CRS** holds that meaning is entirely determined by internal inferential role — the relations among representations within the cognitive system, independently of any connection to the external world. This gives a fully internalist account: two subjects with the same inferential roles have the same concept regardless of environmental differences. **Wide CRS** adds that meaning is also partly determined by the connections between inferential role and the external world — through perception (concepts applied in response to perceptual inputs) and through action (concepts figuring in plans). Wide CRS incorporates a causal-informational element: the inferential role includes "language entry" moves from world to concept and "language exit" moves from concept to action.

## Objections

The **holism problem** is the most pressing. If meaning is determined by inferential role, and role includes all inferential connections, then any two people who disagree about anything have different inferential roles for their concepts — and therefore mean different things. Thoroughgoing CRS seems to make translation between people with different beliefs impossible, and genuine disagreement incoherent (since disagreement requires sharing the concept about which one disagrees). CRS defenders respond that only some inferential connections are meaning-constituting; the others are merely empirical beliefs. But specifying which connections are constitutive without circularity — without presupposing a prior grasp of meaning — is a persistent challenge.

The **truth-conditional problem** raises a different concern. CRS explains meaning in terms of inferential role but struggles to explain truth conditions — what makes a thought true or false. Internal inferential connections do not by themselves determine which external facts satisfy the concepts. The semantic network seems to float free of reality, capturing the logical structure of thought without anchoring it to the world.

The **learning problem** challenges the connection between concept possession and inferential mastery. Children acquire concepts before mastering the full inferential apparatus associated with them. If concept possession requires full inferential mastery, novices have no concepts — which seems wrong. Wide CRS defenders respond that basic language entry and exit moves can anchor concepts early in development, with inferential mastery elaborated over time. Whether this preserves the core CRS thesis or dilutes it depends on how central the internal inferential structure is taken to be.
