# Exercises

1. **Disjunction problem**: Fodor argues that "Cow" represents cows (not horse-on-a-dark-night or faraway-fuzzy-thing) because cow-caused tokenings are primary; other-caused tokenings depend asymmetrically on them. Test this with a concept like "red." What is the primary cause of my RED concept, and what other things activate it asymmetrically?

2. **Teleosemantics and abstract concepts**: Teleosemantics grounds content in evolutionary history. Does this account work for the concept JUSTICE, INFINITY, or TURING MACHINE? What would it mean for these concepts to have been selected by evolution?

3. **Inferentialism and the world**: Brandom argues that language entry/exit moves connect inferential networks to reality. Is this connection sufficient to ground semantic content? Could two communities have the same inferential networks but refer to different things?

4. **Conceptual role and concept possession**: Peacocke holds that you possess a concept only if you accept certain inferences involving it. Does this make conceptual possession normative? What happens to someone who uses a concept while rejecting some of its "possession conditions"?

5. **Content holism**: Many theories of content imply holism — the content of each concept depends on its relations to all other concepts. But holism seems to make communication impossible (since no two people have exactly the same conceptual scheme). How can content theories avoid problematic holism?
