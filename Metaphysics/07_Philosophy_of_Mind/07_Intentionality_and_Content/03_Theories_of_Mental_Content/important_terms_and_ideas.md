# Important Terms and Ideas: Theories of Mental Content

**Informational Semantics**
The view that a representation's content is determined by what information it carries—what it is reliably correlated with in the world under normal conditions. A mental state has a certain content because it is caused (in a law-like way) by instances of a certain property.

**Teleosemantics**
The view that mental representation has content in virtue of biological function: what a representation represents is what it was the function of that type of representation to indicate, as determined by natural selection. Misrepresentation is explained by malfunction rather than by informational divergence.

**Misrepresentation Problem**
A challenge for informational theories: if content is determined by reliable causal covariance, it seems a representation could only be caused by what it represents, ruling out misrepresentation. But minds clearly misrepresent. Teleosemantics addresses this by distinguishing the actual cause of a token from the normal or proper cause of the type.

**Conceptual Role Semantics**
The view that the content of mental states is determined by their inferential and computational roles—their place in the network of mental states. What makes a belief a belief about dogs is its inferential connections with other beliefs, perceptual states, and behavioral dispositions.

**Indicator Semantics**
Dretske's version of informational semantics: a representation R represents F because R reliably indicates F—the occurrence of R carries the information that F is present. Under optimal (or learning period) conditions, R is caused by and only by F.

**Biological Function**
In teleosemantics, a trait's function is what it was selected for—the contribution it made to reproductive fitness of ancestors that explains why the trait is present now. Misrepresentation occurs when a representation fails to perform its proper function even when produced normally.
