# References and Primary Sources: Theories of Mental Content

## Foundational Texts

- Fodor, Jerry, *Psychosemantics* (1987). Defends an informational-teleological theory of narrow content; argues that mental representations have their content in virtue of nomic (law-like) relations to properties in the world.

- Millikan, Ruth, *Language, Thought, and Other Biological Categories* (1984). The teleosemantic account: mental representations have content in virtue of their evolutionary function—what they were "meant" to represent as determined by natural selection.

- Dretske, Fred, *Knowledge and the Flow of Information* (1981). An informational semantics: the content of a representation is determined by what information it carries—what it is nomically correlated with under optimal conditions.

## Contemporary Works

- Neander, Karen, *A Mark of the Mental: In Defense of Informational Teleosemantics* (2017). A sophisticated recent defense of informational teleosemantics as an account of mental content.

- Gauker, Christopher, *Words without Meaning* (2008). A skeptical treatment of mental content, arguing that the standard framework of propositional attitudes and their contents is theoretically problematic.
