# References and Primary Sources: The Exclusion Problem

## Foundational Texts

- Kim, Jaegwon, *Mind in a Physical World* (1998). The definitive statement of the exclusion problem and Kim's case against non-reductive physicalism; argues that mental causation requires reduction or elimination.

- Kim, Jaegwon, "Epiphenomenal and Supervenient Causation," *Midwest Studies in Philosophy* 9 (1984). An earlier paper developing the exclusion argument against anomalous monism and non-reductive physicalism.

- Davidson, Donald, "Mental Events," in *Experience and Theory* edited by Foster and Swanson (1970). Anomalous monism: every mental event is a physical event, and mental events cause physical events in virtue of their physical descriptions—the anomalous monist response to exclusion.

## Contemporary Works

- Yablo, Stephen, "Mental Causation," *Philosophical Review* 101 (1992). Defends mental causation using proportionality: mental causes are causally relevant because they are more proportionate (less specific) than neural causes, not despite their higher-level status.

- List, Christian and Menzies, Peter, "Non-Reductive Physicalism and the Limits of the Exclusion Principle," *Journal of Philosophy* 106 (2009). Defends non-reductive physicalism against Kim by arguing the exclusion principle is not valid in the presence of multiple levels of causal description.
