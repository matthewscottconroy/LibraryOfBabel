# Exercises

1. **Deviant causal chains**: Davidson says that reasons must cause actions "in the right way." Construct a scenario in which beliefs and desires cause an action through a deviant causal chain (the reasons cause the action, but not through normal deliberative processing). Does the action count as intentional? What does this tell us about intentional causation?

2. **Akrasia**: How does the belief-desire model explain weakness of will (acting against your better judgment)? Does Davidson's account commit him to saying akratic action is impossible, or can it accommodate it?

3. **Reasons in neuroscience**: Neuroscience can describe the neural processes that cause behavior. Does this description compete with or complement the reasons explanation? Can both be true simultaneously?

4. **Collective action**: A jury deliberates and reaches a verdict. The jury "decided" collectively. Is this a genuine intentional action? How does the belief-desire model apply to collective agents?

5. **Retroactive rationalization**: Psychological research (Nisbett and Wilson; Libet) suggests that we often confabulate reasons for actions that were actually caused by unconscious processes. Does this show that reasons are never causes, or that we can be wrong about which reasons caused our actions?
