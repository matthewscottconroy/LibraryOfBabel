# References and Primary Sources: Reasons and Causes

## Foundational Texts

- Davidson, Donald, "Actions, Reasons, and Causes," *Journal of Philosophy* 60 (1963). The canonical paper arguing against the Wittgensteinian view that reasons are not causes; reasons are causes—but they rationalize action in virtue of the beliefs and desires that constitute them.

- Anscombe, G.E.M., *Intention* (1957). A foundational text on intentional action; argues for a distinctive form of practical knowledge—knowledge "without observation"—that characterizes the agent's knowledge of their own intentional actions.

- von Wright, Georg Henrik, *Explanation and Understanding* (1971). Distinguishes causal explanation (covering-law model) from intentional explanation (practical syllogism) and argues that action explanation cannot be reduced to causal explanation.

## Contemporary Works

- Mele, Alfred, *Springs of Action* (1992). A comprehensive treatment of the motivational springs of intentional action, defending a causal account of action explanation while attending to the phenomena that motivate non-causal views.

- Dancy, Jonathan, *Practical Reality* (2000). Defends a non-Humean view of practical reasons: reasons are not motivating states but features of the world; the causal theory of action conflates the reasons for which one acts with the causes of one's action.
