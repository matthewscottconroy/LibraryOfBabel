# Emergence and Downward Causation

The concept of emergence has been invoked to explain how higher-level properties — mental properties, biological properties, chemical properties — can be genuinely real and causally efficacious despite arising from lower-level physical constituents. Emergence, in the most general sense, is the phenomenon of novel properties arising at higher levels of organization that are not simply addable from the properties of the lower-level parts. The color of water is emergent — it is not a simple sum of the optical properties of individual hydrogen and oxygen atoms.

Two distinct kinds of emergence are standardly distinguished. Weak emergence holds that higher-level properties are in principle derivable from lower-level properties given sufficient information about the lower-level system, but the derivation is so complex that we cannot practically perform it. Weak emergence is not ontologically significant: the higher-level properties are not genuinely "new" in any deep metaphysical sense; they are just descriptions of complex lower-level patterns. Strong emergence holds that higher-level properties are in principle irreducible to lower-level properties — there is no derivation, even in principle, of the higher-level from the lower-level. Consciousness, on the dualist picture, is a strongly emergent property.

Downward causation is the claim that higher-level properties can cause lower-level events — that the mental can cause the physical, or the biological can cause the chemical. This is controversial because it seems to conflict with physical causal closure: if every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, how can a higher-level property add any causal contribution? Defenders of downward causation argue that this conflict can be resolved if we understand causation holistically — as involving entire systems and their organizational properties — rather than merely as local, event-to-event relations.

This section examines emergence and downward causation, the conceptual distinctions between weak and strong emergence, and the implications for the metaphysics of mind and the unity of science.
