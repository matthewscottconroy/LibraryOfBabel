# Exercises

1. **Downward causation test**: Identify a candidate case of downward causation in biology (e.g., organism-level fitness influencing gene expression). Is this genuine downward causation or is it entirely explicable in terms of molecular-level processes? Does the answer depend on your position in the emergence debate?

2. **Emergence and novelty**: What would it mean for a property to be "genuinely novel" at the macro level — not just surprising, but genuinely not predictable in principle? Does consciousness meet this standard? Does liquidity?

3. **The two-way traffic problem**: If higher-level properties have downward causal influence, then higher-level changes can cause lower-level changes. But lower-level changes (neurons firing) also produce higher-level changes (mental states). Is there a problem of causal competition here, or can both directions of causation be accommodated?

4. **Sperry's hypothesis**: Sperry argued that consciousness is a causally efficacious emergent property that exerts top-down control over neural activity. Is this compatible with physicalism? How would you test it empirically?

5. **Reduction as defeat of emergence**: If a higher-level property is reduced to lower-level properties, is it thereby shown not to be strongly emergent? Does successful reduction always defeat claims to strong emergence, or can a reduced property be strongly emergent in some other sense?
