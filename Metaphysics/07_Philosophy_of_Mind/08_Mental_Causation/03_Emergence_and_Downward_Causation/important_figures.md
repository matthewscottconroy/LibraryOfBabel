# Important Figures

**C.D. Broad (1887–1971)**: Distinguished resultant (weakly emergent) from emergent (strongly emergent) properties in *The Mind and Its Place in Nature* (1925). Broad's account of emergence remains the standard reference point.

**Jaegwon Kim (1934–2019)**: Argued that strong emergence with genuine downward causation is incompatible with causal closure of the physical. *Mind in a Physical World* (1998).

**Timothy O'Connor (1965–present)**: Defends "diachronic emergence" — emergent properties arising from certain complex physical configurations, with genuine top-down causal powers. *Emergence: A Philosophical Account* (2010).

**Roger Sperry (1913–1994)**: Nobel laureate neuroscientist who advocated downward causation in the brain. Consciousness, he argued, is a causally efficacious emergent property that influences brain processes.

**Paul Davies (1946–present)** and **Philip Clayton (1956–present)**: Developed accounts of emergence in the context of physics and philosophy of science, discussing where and how higher-level properties can have downward causal influence.
