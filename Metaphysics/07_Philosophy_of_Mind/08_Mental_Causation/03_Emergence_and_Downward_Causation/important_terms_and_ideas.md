# Important Terms and Ideas: Emergence and Downward Causation

**Weak Emergence**
Emergence understood epistemologically: a property is weakly emergent when it arises from lower-level processes but could in principle (though perhaps not in practice) be derived from complete knowledge of those processes. Weak emergence is relatively uncontroversial and does not require new fundamental ontology.

**Strong Emergence**
A property is strongly emergent when it genuinely cannot be derived from, predicted from, or reduced to lower-level properties—even given complete knowledge. Strong emergence would require causal powers at the higher level that are not reducible to the causal powers of the constituents.

**Downward Causation**
Causation from higher organizational levels to lower ones: the whole causes changes in its parts. In the mental causation context, this means mental or psychological properties causally influencing neural events. Strong downward causation is controversial because it seems to conflict with the closure of physics.

**Synchronic vs. Diachronic Emergence**
Synchronic emergence: a property present at time t emerges from lower-level properties also present at t. Diachronic emergence: higher-level properties emerge through a temporal process from lower-level properties at earlier times. Most discussions of biological and mental emergence concern synchronic emergence.

**Causal Powers of Wholes**
The claim that organized wholes—cells, organisms, brains—have genuine causal powers that their parts lack. This is the positive claim behind emergence: when matter is organized into a complex system, new causal powers come into being that are exercised by the whole and explain phenomena not explicable at the level of parts.

**Kim's Supervenience Argument against Emergence**
Kim argues that if higher-level properties supervene on lower-level ones, any causal work done by higher-level properties is already done by lower-level properties. Strong emergence either violates supervenience (making the higher level causally independent) or collapses into weak emergence. Either way, strong emergence is problematic.
