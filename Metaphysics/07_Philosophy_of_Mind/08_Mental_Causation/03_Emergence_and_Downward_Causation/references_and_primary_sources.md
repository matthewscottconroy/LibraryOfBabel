# References and Primary Sources: Emergence and Downward Causation

## Foundational Texts

- Broad, C.D., *The Mind and Its Place in Nature* (1925). Distinguishes "mechanical" from "emergent" properties; emergent properties are those that genuinely arise from combinations of lower-level properties but cannot be predicted from complete knowledge of those properties.

- Sperry, Roger, "Mind, Brain, and Humanist Values," in *New Views of the Nature of Man* edited by Platt (1965). Neuroscientist and Nobel laureate who coined "emergent causation" and defended downward causation from mental to neural states as empirically real.

- Kim, Jaegwon, "Making Sense of Emergence," *Philosophical Studies* 95 (1999). Critical examination of strong emergence and downward causation, arguing that genuine downward causation is incoherent and that emergence must be understood as weak (epistemological) rather than strong (ontological).

## Contemporary Works

- Ellis, George, *How Can Physics Underlie the Mind?* (2016). Defends top-down causation in complex biological systems, arguing that higher-level causal powers are real and cannot be reduced to lower-level physics.

- Bedau, Mark and Humphreys, Paul (eds.), *Emergence: Contemporary Readings in Philosophy and Science* (2008). A comprehensive anthology collecting the main philosophical and scientific treatments of emergence.
