# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Argument from illusion**: The stick looks bent in water but is not. Are you aware of (a) the straight stick (which looks bent), or (b) a bent appearance (a sense datum)? What follows for naive realism if (b)?

2. **Hallucination**: If you hallucinate a pink elephant, what are you aware of? If you are aware of a mental object (sense datum), does this show that in all perception you are aware of mental objects?

3. **Disjunctivism**: McDowell argues that veridical perception and hallucination are fundamentally different kinds of experience that need not share a common core. Is this convincing? Can a disjunctivist explain why hallucination seems from the inside just like veridical perception?

4. **The transparency of experience**: When you introspect on your visual experience, do you find sense data (inner objects with qualitative properties) or do you find only properties of external objects? Does this phenomenological test settle the debate?

5. **Representationalism test**: If phenomenal character is identical to representational content, then two experiences with identical representational content must have identical phenomenal character. Can you construct a case where this fails?
