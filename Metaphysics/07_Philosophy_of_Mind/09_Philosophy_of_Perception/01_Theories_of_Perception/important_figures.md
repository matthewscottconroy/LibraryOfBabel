# Important Figures

**G.E. Moore (1873–1958)**: Early sense data theorist. *Some Main Problems of Philosophy* (1953) defends naive realism while using sense data vocabulary.

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)**: *The Problems of Philosophy* (1912) develops the classic sense data theory. Russell distinguished sense data (private, mental) from the physical objects that cause them.

**J.M. Hinton (1926–2007)** and **John McDowell (1942–present)**: Developed disjunctivism — the view that veridical perception and hallucination are fundamentally different kinds of mental event, not species of a common genus.

**John Campbell (1956–present)**: *Past, Space, and Self* (1994) and *Reference and Consciousness* (2002) defend naive realism — the view that objects are constituents of perceptual experience.

**Michael Tye (1950–present)**: Developed representationalism about perceptual experience. *Ten Problems of Consciousness* (1995) and *Consciousness, Color, and Content* (2000).
