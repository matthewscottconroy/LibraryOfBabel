# References and Primary Sources: Theories of Perception

## Foundational Texts

- Ayer, A.J., *The Foundations of Empirical Knowledge* (1940). A classic statement of sense-datum theory: perceptual experience is always and directly of sense-data—mental entities that represent external objects mediately.

- Austin, J.L., *Sense and Sensibilia* (1962). The influential critique of sense-datum theory: sense-datum talk is a philosophical construction that distorts rather than illuminates perceptual experience; ordinary perceptual claims are not about mental intermediaries.

- Dretske, Fred, *Seeing and Knowing* (1969). Develops a causal-informational account of perception; distinguishes epistemic from non-epistemic seeing, arguing that perception is fundamentally a matter of carrying information about the world.

## Contemporary Works

- Martin, M.G.F., "The Transparency of Experience," *Mind and Language* 17 (2002). Defends naïve realism through the transparency of experience: when we introspect our perceptual experience, we are presented with features of external objects, not mental intermediaries.

- Siegel, Susanna, *The Contents of Visual Experience* (2010). Argues that perceptual experience has rich representational content, including not just properties like color and shape but also natural kind and causal properties.
