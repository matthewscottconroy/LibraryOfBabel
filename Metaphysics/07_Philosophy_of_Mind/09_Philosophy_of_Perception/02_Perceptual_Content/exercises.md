# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Fine-grained color**: You can see a precise shade of chartreuse that you have no color name for and could not describe fully in words. Does this show that perceptual content is nonconceptual? Or can the concept be a non-linguistic demonstrative ('that shade')?

2. **McDowell's challenge**: If experience has nonconceptual content, how does it justify conceptual beliefs? Can a non-conceptual experience give you a reason to believe 'that apple is red'? McDowell says no. Is he right?

3. **Animal perception**: A frog perceives a fly and snaps at it. Does the frog's perceptual state have conceptual content? If not, does the frog perceive anything at all in the philosophically robust sense?

4. **Infant perception**: Infants perceive before they have concepts. Does this show that perception is nonconceptual, or does it show that infants perceive less (or differently) than concept-possessing adults?

5. **Cognitive penetration**: Prior beliefs can influence perception (expecting a banana can make you more likely to see a yellow object as banana-shaped). Does cognitive penetration show that perception is conceptual? Or does it merely show causal influence without constitutive conceptualization?
