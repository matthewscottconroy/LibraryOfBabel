# Important Terms and Ideas: Perceptual Content

**Conceptual Content**
Content that is structured by and immediately available for the exercise of concepts. McDowell argues that perceptual content is conceptual: what we perceive is already articulated in ways that can directly enter into inference and judgment. This view blurs the boundary between perception and cognition.

**Non-Conceptual Content**
Content that is carried by perceptual states independently of whether the perceiver possesses the relevant concepts. A creature without the concept of red can still see red things; the content "red here" is non-conceptual. This allows for perception to be richer than the perceiver's conceptual repertoire.

**The Fineness of Grain Problem**
An argument for non-conceptual content: we can perceive more specific shades of color than we have concepts for—we can discriminate between color shades for which we have no names or concepts. This fine-grained content outstrips our conceptual resources, suggesting perceptual content is non-conceptual.

**Scenario Content**
Peacocke's term: the non-conceptual content of a perceptual experience is its scenario—a specification of how things are positioned in space relative to the perceiver. Scenarios capture the perspectival, spatial structure of visual experience independently of the perceiver's concepts.

**Intentionalism**
The thesis that phenomenal character supervenes on representational content: experiences that represent the world the same way are phenomenally identical. Combined with externalism, this yields strong forms of content-based accounts of experience.

**Perceptual Richness**
Siegel's debate: do perceptual experiences represent only basic sensory properties (color, shape, motion) or rich properties like natural kinds (this is a dog), causal relations, and functional properties? She argues for perceptual richness, while others contend that such properties are cognitively imputed, not perceived.
