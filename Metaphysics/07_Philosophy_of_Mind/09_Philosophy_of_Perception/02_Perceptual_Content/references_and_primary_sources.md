# References and Primary Sources: Perceptual Content

## Foundational Texts

- Evans, Gareth, *The Varieties of Reference* (1982). Distinguishes conceptual from non-conceptual content and develops a theory of demonstrative thought grounded in perception; influential on subsequent debates about whether perceptual content is conceptual or non-conceptual.

- McDowell, John, *Mind and World* (1994). Defends the view that perceptual content is conceptual: the world presents itself in experience as already conceptually articulated; non-conceptual content is a myth born of a misguided epistemology.

- Peacocke, Christopher, *A Study of Concepts* (1992). Develops the notion of "scenarios" as the non-conceptual content of perceptual experience; argues that perceptual content outstrips conceptual content, providing the interface between perception and the world.

## Contemporary Works

- Siegel, Susanna, *The Contents of Visual Experience* (2010). Argues that visual experience represents rich properties—natural kinds, causal relations—beyond the sensory qualities that most accounts allow.

- Byrne, Alex, "Intentionalism Defended," *Philosophical Review* 110 (2001). Defends intentionalism—the thesis that phenomenal character supervenes on representational content—against standard objections involving inverted spectra and other cases.
