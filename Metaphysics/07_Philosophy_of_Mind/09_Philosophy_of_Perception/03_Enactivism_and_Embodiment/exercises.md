# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Change blindness**: Research shows we fail to notice large changes in visual scenes when attention is not directed to them. Noe uses this to argue against the snapshot model of vision. Does change blindness support enactivism, or does it only show that we do not maintain detailed internal representations of entire scenes?

2. **Sensorimotor contingencies**: Noe argues that perception involves knowledge of sensorimotor contingencies — how the look of an object changes as you move around it. Blind people who gain sight in adulthood initially cannot recognize three-dimensional objects visually. Does this support Noe's theory?

3. **Phantom pain**: Patients with amputated limbs often experience pain in the missing limb. Enactivism says pain is an enacted, embodied experience. How does it account for phantom pain?

4. **Dreaming**: In dreams, we have perceptual experiences without any sensorimotor engagement with an environment. Does dreaming refute enactivism? Or can enactivists account for dream phenomenology?

5. **Ecological psychology**: Gibson's affordance theory holds that we perceive the world in terms of opportunities for action. Is this compatible with representationalism? With enactivism? Compare Gibson's approach to Noe's.
