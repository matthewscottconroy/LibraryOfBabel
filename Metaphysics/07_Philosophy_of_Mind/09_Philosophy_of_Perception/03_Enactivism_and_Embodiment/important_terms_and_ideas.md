# Important Terms and Ideas: Enactivism and Embodiment

**Enactivism**
The view that cognition and perception are not computational processes over internal representations but are enacted through the sensorimotor engagement of an embodied organism with its environment. The mind is not in the head; it is constituted by the whole coupled system of organism and environment.

**Sensorimotor Contingencies**
Noë's concept: implicit knowledge of the lawful relationships between sensorimotor activity and sensory stimulation. When I move my eyes, I know how the visual array will change; this practical knowledge constitutes perceptual experience. Perception is mastery of sensorimotor contingencies, not passive reception.

**Embodied Cognition**
The thesis that the body plays a constitutive (not merely enabling) role in cognitive processes. Cognitive processes are not confined to the brain but are shaped by and distributed across the body and its interactions with the environment.

**The Extended Mind**
Clark and Chalmers's thesis: cognitive processes can extend beyond the skin and skull into the environment. When a cognitive process in the world functions in a way that would be cognitive if done in the head, it is part of the cognitive system. A notebook can be part of someone's extended mind.

**Radical Enactivism**
Hutto and Myin's version: basic cognition involves no representational content at all—organisms engage with their environments through sensorimotor coupling without representing them. Representation emerges only with the development of language and sophisticated cognition.

**Lived Body (Leib)**
Merleau-Ponty's distinction between the objective body (Körper)—the body as a physical object in the world—and the lived body (Leib)—the body as experienced from the first person, as the ground of all perceptual engagement. The lived body is not an object but the condition of possibility for encountering objects.
