# References and Primary Sources: Enactivism and Embodiment

## Foundational Texts

- Merleau-Ponty, Maurice, *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945). The foundational text of embodied phenomenology: perception is not a mental act performed on sensory data but an embodied, skilled engagement with the world; the body is not an object but the ground of all perception.

- Varela, Francisco, Thompson, Evan, and Rosch, Eleanor, *The Embodied Mind* (1991). Introduces enactivism: cognition is not computation over mental representations but enacted through the sensorimotor engagement of a living body with its environment.

- Noë, Alva, *Action in Perception* (2004). Defends sensorimotor enactivism: perceptual experience is constituted by implicit knowledge of how sensory stimulation varies with movement; perception is a mode of exploratory activity, not passive reception.

## Contemporary Works

- Clark, Andy and Chalmers, David, "The Extended Mind," *Analysis* 58 (1998). Argues that cognitive processes can extend beyond the brain into the body and environment; a notebook used in exactly the right way is part of the cognitive system.

- Thompson, Evan, *Mind in Life* (2007). Develops enactivism within a biological framework, arguing that life and mind are continuous and that consciousness is a form of self-organizing biological activity.
