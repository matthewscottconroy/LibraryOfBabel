# References and Primary Sources: Computational Theories of Mind

## Foundational Texts

- Turing, Alan, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," *Mind* 59 (1950). Introduces the Turing Test as an operational criterion for machine intelligence and provides the foundational argument for computational approaches to mind.

- Fodor, Jerry, *The Language of Thought* (1975). The classic defense of the Language of Thought Hypothesis: mental processes are computations over syntactically structured mental representations (mentalese), analogous to computations over sentences.

- Newell, Allen and Simon, Herbert, "Computer Science as Empirical Inquiry: Symbols and Search," *Communications of the ACM* 19 (1976). Proposes the Physical Symbol System Hypothesis: a physical symbol system has the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action.

## Contemporary Works

- Fodor, Jerry, *The Mind Doesn't Work That Way* (2000). A skeptical late Fodor: while input systems are modular and computational, the central processes of cognition (abduction, belief fixation) may be intractable for computational models.

- Marcus, Gary, *The Algebraic Mind* (2001). Defends rule-based symbol manipulation as essential for cognition, arguing against connectionist alternatives that model cognition purely through distributed representations.
