# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **The Chinese Room revisited**: Searle says the person in the room does not understand Chinese; the system as a whole does not understand Chinese either. Is the 'systems reply' (the whole system understands, even if the part does not) convincing? What would it take for a system to understand something?

2. **LLMs and the Chinese Room**: Large language models generate contextually appropriate responses without any sensorimotor grounding. Does Searle's Chinese Room argument apply to them? Is there something LLMs can do that the person in Searle's room cannot?

3. **Dreyfus's prediction**: Dreyfus predicted in the early 1970s that AI would be unable to achieve human-level performance in open-domain tasks because intelligence requires embodied situatedness. Contemporary LLMs perform impressively on many open-domain language tasks. Does this vindicate or refute Dreyfus?

4. **Moral status of AI**: If an AI system exhibits all the behavioral markers of consciousness (reports experiences, reacts appropriately to pain, has preferences), what moral status should we accord it? Does behavioral equivalence establish moral patiency?

5. **Artificial general intelligence**: Classical computationalism held that general intelligence requires implementing the right symbolic program. 4E approaches hold that general intelligence requires embodiment and environmental situatedness. LLMs suggest a third option: statistical pattern learning at scale. Which approach is most promising for achieving genuine general intelligence?
