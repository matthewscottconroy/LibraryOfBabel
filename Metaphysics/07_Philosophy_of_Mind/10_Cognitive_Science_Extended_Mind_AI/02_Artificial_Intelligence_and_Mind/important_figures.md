# Important Figures

**Alan Turing (1912–1954)**: Proposed the Turing Test and anticipated the development of artificial intelligence. His paper is the starting point for philosophical discussions of machine intelligence.

**John Searle (1932–present)**: The Chinese Room argument ('Minds, Brains, and Programs,' 1980) remains the most influential argument that computational symbol manipulation is not sufficient for genuine understanding.

**Hubert Dreyfus (1929–2017)**: *What Computers Can't Do* (1972) and *What Computers Still Can't Do* (1992) developed a phenomenological critique of classical AI, arguing that intelligence requires embodied, situated engagement with the world.

**Marvin Minsky (1927–2016)**: Pioneer of classical AI. *The Society of Mind* (1986) proposed a connectionist/modular account of intelligence.

**Daniel Dennett (1942–present)**: *Consciousness Explained* (1991) and *Darwin's Dangerous Idea* (1995) argue that consciousness and intelligence are computational and that AI can in principle replicate human cognition.
