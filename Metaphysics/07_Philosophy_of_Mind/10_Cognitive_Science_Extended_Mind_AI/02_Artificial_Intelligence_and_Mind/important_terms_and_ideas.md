# Important Terms and Ideas: Artificial Intelligence and Mind

**Strong AI**
The thesis that an appropriately programmed computer running the right program has genuine mental states—real understanding, beliefs, and consciousness—not mere simulation. Searle's Chinese Room is designed to refute Strong AI.

**Weak AI**
The thesis that AI systems can be useful tools for simulating and studying cognitive processes, without necessarily having genuine mental states. Even sophisticated AI systems merely simulate cognition; they do not instantiate it.

**The Turing Test**
An operational criterion for machine intelligence: a machine passes the Turing Test if a human judge cannot reliably distinguish its conversational outputs from those of a human. Turing proposed this as a replacement for the vague question "Can machines think?" Critics argue it tests linguistic behavior, not genuine understanding.

**Systems Reply**
The standard functionalist response to the Chinese Room: while the person in the room does not understand Chinese, the whole system (person + rules + symbols + room) does. Searle counters by "internalizing" the system, insisting understanding is still absent.

**Robot Reply**
A proposed extension of the Chinese Room: if the symbol-manipulating system were embodied in a robot with sensory and motor connections to the world, its symbols would gain genuine semantic content through causal connections to the environment. Searle finds this unconvincing.

**Deep Learning**
A paradigm in machine learning using multi-layer neural networks trained on large datasets. Deep learning has achieved remarkable performance in perception, language, and game-playing tasks, raising fresh questions about whether pattern-matching performance constitutes understanding and how close such systems are to human-level intelligence.
