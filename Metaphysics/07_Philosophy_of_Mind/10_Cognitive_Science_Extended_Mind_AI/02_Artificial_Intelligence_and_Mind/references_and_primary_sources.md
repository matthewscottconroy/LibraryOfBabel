# References and Primary Sources: Artificial Intelligence and Mind

## Foundational Texts

- Turing, Alan, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," *Mind* 59 (1950). The foundational paper: introduces the imitation game (Turing Test) as a criterion for machine intelligence and anticipates objections to machine thought.

- Searle, John, "Minds, Brains, and Programs," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 3 (1980). The Chinese Room argument against Strong AI: a computer manipulating symbols does not thereby understand anything; syntax is not sufficient for semantics or genuine intelligence.

- Dreyfus, Hubert, *What Computers Can't Do* (1972, revised 1979). A phenomenological critique of AI: human intelligence is essentially embodied and contextual; it cannot be captured by the manipulation of formal, context-independent rules.

## Contemporary Works

- Chalmers, David, "Does a Rock Implement Every Finite-State Automaton?" *Synthese* 108 (1996). Examines the implementation problem: when does a physical system implement a computation? This matters for understanding what it means for the brain to compute.

- Marcus, Gary and Davis, Ernest, *Rebooting AI* (2019). A critical assessment of current deep learning AI, arguing it lacks genuine understanding and reasoning, and proposing a path toward more robust artificial general intelligence.
