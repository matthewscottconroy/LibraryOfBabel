# Exercises and Thought Experiments

1. **Hume's bundle**: Hume says he never catches himself without a perception, never an ego separate from its contents. Try Hume's introspective exercise yourself. Do you find a self apart from your experiences? What would it mean to find one?

2. **Parfit's fission**: Imagine that your brain is divided and each hemisphere is transplanted into a different body, both of which survive and are psychologically continuous with you. Which survivor (if either) is you? What does your answer reveal about what personal identity requires?

3. **Wittgenstein on other minds**: Wittgenstein argues that the criteria for mental state attribution are behavioral and partly public. Does this mean that a zombie (physically and behaviorally identical to a conscious person) would have mental states? Or does Wittgenstein deny the coherence of the zombie thought experiment?

4. **Simulation theory**: You understand others' mental states by simulating them in your own mind — imagining yourself in their situation. Does this theory work for radically alien others (other species, people with very different experiences)? What are the limits of simulation?

5. **Metzinger's transparent self-model**: Metzinger argues that the self is a transparent self-model — a representation of the organism that we experience as ourselves, not as a representation. If this is right, is there any fact of the matter about whether I am conscious? Or is consciousness just a property of the self-model?
