# Important Terms and Ideas: Self and Other Minds

**Bundle Theory of Self**
Hume's view: there is no persisting self over and above the stream of perceptions. Introspection reveals only bundles of impressions and ideas—no impression of a self as the persisting subject. The self is a useful fiction or a bundle, not a genuine entity.

**The Problem of Other Minds**
The epistemological problem of justifying belief in the existence of other conscious minds. I have direct access to my own experience; I can only infer the existence of others' experiences from their behavior and physiology. The problem is whether such inference is justified and how.

**Argument from Analogy**
The traditional solution to the problem of other minds: I infer that others have minds by analogy with my own case—they behave as I do in circumstances where I have experiences, so they probably have experiences too. Critics argue this is a shaky inductive inference from a single case.

**Private Language Argument**
Wittgenstein's argument: a language whose expressions are given meaning solely by reference to private, inner states (accessible only to the speaker) is impossible. Meaning requires public criteria—ways of checking correctness that others can in principle access. Applied to the problem of other minds, it suggests that mental concepts are inherently public.

**Minimal Self**
Zahavi's phenomenological concept: beneath the narrative or reflective self lies a minimal, pre-reflective sense of mineness—the basic givenness of experience as one's own. This is not a Humean impression of a self but a structural feature of consciousness itself.

**Simulation Theory of Mind Reading**
The view that we understand other minds by simulating their mental processes in our own minds—imaginatively putting ourselves in their position. Contrasted with theory-theory (we use an implicit folk psychological theory to predict and explain others' behavior).
