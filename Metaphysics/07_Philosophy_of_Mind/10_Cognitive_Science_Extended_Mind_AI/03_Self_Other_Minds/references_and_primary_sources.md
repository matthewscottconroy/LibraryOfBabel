# References and Primary Sources: Self and Other Minds

## Foundational Texts

- Hume, David, *Treatise of Human Nature* (1739), Book I, Part IV, Section VI. The bundle theory of the self: introspection reveals only a bundle of perceptions, not any persisting self; there is no impression of a unified, continuous self.

- Kant, Immanuel, *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781), Analytic of Concepts, §16–17. The transcendental unity of apperception: the "I think" accompanies all representations as a formal, logical condition, but does not give knowledge of a substantial self.

- Wittgenstein, Ludwig, *Philosophical Investigations* (1953), §§243–315. The private language argument: a language whose meanings are determined by private, introspectively accessible states is impossible; the meaning of sensation terms requires public criteria.

## Contemporary Works

- Parfit, Derek, *Reasons and Persons* (1984), Part III. Uses fission, spectrum, and other cases to argue that personal identity does not matter in the ways we think; what matters is psychological continuity, not some further fact about the self.

- Zahavi, Dan, *Self and Subjectivity* (2005). A phenomenological defense of minimal selfhood: consciousness necessarily involves a pre-reflective sense of mineness—a weak but ineliminable self—that cannot be reduced to a bundle of representations.
