# Review Questions — Cognitive Science, the Extended Mind, and AI

1. Clark and Chalmers's parity principle holds that if a process occurring outside the skull would be recognized as cognitive were it inside the skull, it is cognitive wherever it occurs. Otto's notebook is their paradigm case. Critics argue this proves too much — the internet, or the entire social record of human knowledge, would count as cognitive on this principle. What constraints does the theory impose to limit cognitive extension, and are those constraints principled or ad hoc?

2. The extended mind thesis has implications for personal identity: if cognitive processes extend into tools, notebooks, and technological environments, then changes to those external resources affect who one is in a philosophically relevant sense. Does this make the external resources constitutive of the person in the same way biological continuity is, or is there an asymmetry? What would Parfit's account of personal identity imply about cases where external cognitive resources are destroyed?

3. Classical computationalism (CCTM) held that thought requires a language of thought — structured symbolic representations whose combinatorial syntax mirrors the compositional structure of meaningful content. Large language models achieve sophisticated language behavior through distributed statistical learning without any obviously compositional symbolic architecture. Does the success of LLMs refute CCTM, vindicate connectionism, or require a more nuanced analysis of what compositionality requires?

4. Searle distinguished weak AI (computers can simulate cognition) from strong AI (suitably programmed computers genuinely think). The Chinese Room argument aims to show that syntax is not sufficient for semantics — no formal symbol manipulation constitutes genuine understanding. The systems reply holds that understanding is a property of the whole system. Assess the relative strength of the Chinese Room, the systems reply, and the robot reply in the context of current large language models.

5. The problem of other minds holds that behavioral evidence cannot conclusively establish phenomenal consciousness in other beings, since zombie behavior is indistinguishable from conscious behavior. This problem applies with particular force to AI systems. If we cannot resolve the other minds problem for humans, how should we proceed practically with respect to the moral status of sophisticated AI? Does the problem of other minds collapse into the question of which theory of consciousness is correct?

6. The narrative self account holds that personal identity is constituted by the ongoing story a person constructs about their life — integrating past, present, and projected future into a coherent autobiography. What implications does this have for the moral status of beings (advanced AI systems, patients with severe amnesia, infants) who lack the narrative resources for this form of self-constitution? And does the narrative account succeed as a theory of personal identity, or does it presuppose rather than explain what makes it *the same self* doing the narrating?

7. LLMs are trained on vast human-generated text and acquire sophisticated language competencies without embodied interaction with the world. Embodied cognition theorists argue that genuine understanding of "red" or "heavy" requires sensorimotor grounding. LLMs' characteristic failure modes — confabulation about spatial and physical relations — might support this view. Does the evidence from LLM performance support or undermine the embodied cognition thesis, or is the relationship more complex?

8. The module of philosophy of mind ends here, but its questions cascade into ethics, metaphysics, and the philosophy of science. Identify two specific questions raised in this chapter — about AI, the self, or other minds — that depend for their resolution on positions developed in earlier chapters (for example, on functionalism, the hard problem, mental causation, or intentionality). For each, state the dependency clearly: what answer in an earlier chapter would settle or substantially shape the later question?
