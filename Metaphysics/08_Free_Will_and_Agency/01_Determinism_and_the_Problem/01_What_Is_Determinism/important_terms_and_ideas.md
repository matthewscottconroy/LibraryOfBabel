# Important Terms and Ideas: What Is Determinism?

**Determinism**
The thesis that every event is necessitated by prior events and the laws of nature: given the complete state of the world at any time, together with the laws, the future is uniquely fixed. Every event has a sufficient preceding cause; nothing could have happened differently given the same past and laws.

**Laplace's Demon**
Laplace's thought experiment: an intellect that knew the position and momentum of every particle in the universe, together with all the forces, could in principle calculate the future and past of every event. This "demon" represents the ideal of complete causal determinism.

**Physical Determinism**
The specific claim that the laws of physics (together with the complete physical state) entail every subsequent physical state. This is distinct from metaphysical determinism (every event has a sufficient cause) and logical fatalism (everything that happens must happen, regardless of causation).

**The Consequence Argument**
Van Inwagen's argument: (1) our actions are consequences of the laws and events before our birth; (2) we have no control over the laws or remote past; (3) we have no control over the consequences of things we have no control over; (4) therefore we have no control over our actions. This is the main argument for hard incompatibilism.

**Fatalism**
The view that the future is fixed regardless of what we do—not because of deterministic causation but because claims about the future already have determinate truth values. Fatalism is often confused with determinism but is a distinct position; one can be a determinist without being a fatalist.

**Indeterminism**
The thesis that not all events are necessitated by prior events and laws—some events are genuinely undetermined, occurring without sufficient prior cause. Quantum mechanics is widely interpreted as indeterministic (at the level of individual events). Indeterminism is a necessary but not sufficient condition for libertarian free will.
