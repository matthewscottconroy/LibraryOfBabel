# References and Primary Sources: What Is Determinism?

## Foundational Texts

- Laplace, Pierre-Simon, *A Philosophical Essay on Probabilities* (1814). The famous formulation of determinism via the Laplacean demon: a sufficiently intelligent being with complete physical knowledge could predict the entire future and reconstruct the entire past.

- d'Holbach, Baron, *The System of Nature* (1770). The most comprehensive Enlightenment defense of necessitarianism: all events, including human actions and thoughts, are determined by prior causes and the laws of nature.

- James, William, "The Dilemma of Determinism," in *The Will to Believe* (1897). A classic argument that determinism is incoherent with moral judgment: if all is determined, we cannot consistently regard any outcome as regrettable or avoidable.

## Contemporary Works

- Earman, John, *A Primer on Determinism* (1986). A rigorous philosophical and scientific examination of determinism, distinguishing its various forms and examining whether classical mechanics, general relativity, and quantum mechanics are deterministic.

- van Inwagen, Peter, *An Essay on Free Will* (1983). Introduces the Consequence Argument for incompatibilism: if determinism is true, our actions are consequences of laws and events before our birth; we cannot control laws or remote past; therefore we cannot control our actions.
