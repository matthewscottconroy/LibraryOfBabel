# Important Figures: Classical Compatibilism

## David Hume (1711–1776)
The canonical early modern compatibilist: argued in the *Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (1748) that the reconciliation of liberty and necessity is straightforward—liberty means acting according to one's motives without external constraint, and this is fully compatible with necessitation.

## John Stuart Mill (1806–1873)
Developed Hume's compatibilism in a more systematic direction; argued that the distinction between necessity and constraint is what matters morally, and that necessitated actions can be praiseworthy or blameworthy depending on whether they flow from the agent's character.

## G.E. Moore (1873–1958)
Contributed the "conditional analysis" of "can": "S could have done otherwise" means "S would have done otherwise if S had chosen to." This analysis attempts to make sense of the ability to do otherwise in a deterministic world.

## A.J. Ayer (1910–1989)
Defended a classical compatibilism in "Freedom and Necessity" (1954): freedom is the absence of external constraint; a free action is one caused by the agent's desires and character, not by external compulsion. Determinism is compatible with this sense of freedom.
