# Important Terms and Ideas: Classical Compatibilism

**Classical Compatibilism**
The view that free will and determinism are compatible—that we can act freely even in a deterministic world. The key is to define freedom correctly: freedom is acting according to one's own desires and motives without external compulsion, not the absence of causal determination.

**Hypothetical Analysis of "Can"**
Moore's analysis: "S could have done otherwise" means "S would have done otherwise if S had chosen (or tried, or desired) to." This translates "can" claims into counterfactuals that are true even in deterministic worlds where S in fact did not choose otherwise.

**Liberty as Absence of Constraint**
Hume's definition: liberty is freedom from external compulsion—acting as one wills, without being prevented or coerced by external forces. Determinism does not constrain in this sense; a necessitated action can still be free if it flows from the agent's own character and desires.

**Acting from Character**
Mill's emphasis: a free action is one that flows from the agent's own character—their settled dispositions, values, and desires. That character was formed by prior causes does not undermine freedom; what matters is that the action expresses who the person genuinely is.

**Hierarchical Account**
Frankfurt's development of compatibilism: a free will is one in which second-order volitions (desires about which desires to act on) align with first-order desires. A person acts freely when their first-order desire to do X is endorsed by their second-order volition to have that desire.

**Hard Determinism**
The incompatibilist position that classical compatibilists oppose: if determinism is true, no one is ever free, because genuine freedom requires the ability to do otherwise in the categorical sense (not just the conditional sense), and determinism rules this out.
