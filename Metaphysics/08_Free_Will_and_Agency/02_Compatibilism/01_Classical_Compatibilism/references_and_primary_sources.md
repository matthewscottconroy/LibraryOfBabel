# References and Primary Sources: Classical Compatibilism

## Foundational Texts

- Hume, David, *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (1748), Section VIII. The classic compatibilist reconciliation: liberty is "a power of acting or not acting, according to the determinations of the will"—this is fully compatible with universal causation.

- Mill, John Stuart, *A System of Logic* (1843), Book VI, Chapter II. Extends compatibilism and responds to the objection that determinism is "fatalistic"; argues that character determines action, and character is itself subject to deliberate cultivation.

- Moore, G.E., *Ethics* (1912), Chapter VI. Develops and defends the conditional analysis of "could have done otherwise": "could have" means "would have if certain conditions had obtained."

## Contemporary Works

- Ayer, A.J., "Freedom and Necessity," in *Philosophical Essays* (1954). A clear statement of classical compatibilism; distinguishes constraint from causation and argues only the former undermines freedom.

- Frankfurt, Harry, "Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person," *Journal of Philosophy* 68 (1971). Introduces the hierarchical account of free will: free will consists in having second-order volitions that align with first-order desires, moving compatibilism beyond the Humean account.
