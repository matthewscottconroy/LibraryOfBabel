# Important Figures: Frankfurt Cases

## Harry Frankfurt (1929–present)
Introduced the Frankfurt cases that revolutionized debate about free will and moral responsibility: thought experiments in which an agent acts freely (without alternative possibilities) because a counterfactual intervener would have ensured the same action had the agent tried otherwise. Frankfurt argued these cases show that moral responsibility does not require the ability to do otherwise.

## John Martin Fischer (born 1952)
Developed "semi-compatibilism" in response to Frankfurt cases: moral responsibility requires only "reasons-responsiveness"—the ability to recognize and respond to reasons—not the ability to do otherwise. Fischer distinguishes "guidance control" (which grounds responsibility) from "regulative control" (which requires alternatives).

## David Widerker (born 1949)
Developed the "flicker of freedom" and "dilemma defense" objections to Frankfurt cases; argues that Frankfurt cases either presuppose that the agent has some residual alternative possibilities (the flicker) or else they presuppose determinism (the dilemma), making them ineffective against the incompatibilist.

## Robert Kane (born 1938)
The leading contemporary libertarian; developed the "indeterminist moment" as a response to Frankfurt cases, arguing that genuine agency requires "ultimate responsibility" for one's character-forming choices, which requires indeterminism at the right level.
