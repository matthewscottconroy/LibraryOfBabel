# References and Primary Sources: Frankfurt Cases

## Foundational Texts

- Frankfurt, Harry, "Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility," *Journal of Philosophy* 66 (1969). Introduces Frankfurt cases: scenarios where an agent would have been ensured to perform an action anyway (by a counterfactual intervener), yet intuitively seems responsible. Challenges the Principle of Alternative Possibilities (PAP).

- Fischer, John Martin and Ravizza, Mark, *Responsibility and Control* (1998). Develops semi-compatibilism using Frankfurt cases; argues that guidance control (reasons-responsiveness) is sufficient for moral responsibility; alternative possibilities are not required.

- Kane, Robert, *The Significance of Free Will* (1996). The leading libertarian response to Frankfurt cases; argues that responsibility requires "ultimate responsibility" for one's own character, which cannot be provided by a deterministic or Frankfurt-style scenario.

## Contemporary Works

- Widerker, David, "Libertarianism and Frankfurt's Attack on the Principle of Alternative Possibilities," *Philosophical Review* 104 (1995). Presents the dilemma defense against Frankfurt cases: they either require a prior sign (preserving alternatives) or assume determinism.

- Pereboom, Derk, *Living Without Free Will* (2001). Uses Frankfurt-style cases and other arguments to defend hard incompatibilism, arguing that neither libertarianism nor compatibilism provides the kind of free will that matters.
