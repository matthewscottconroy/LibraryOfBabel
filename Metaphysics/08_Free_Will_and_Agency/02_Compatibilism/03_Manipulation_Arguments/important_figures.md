# Important Figures: Manipulation Arguments

## Derk Pereboom (born 1957)
Developed the four-case manipulation argument against compatibilism: by constructing a series of cases ranging from direct neuroscientific manipulation to ordinary deterministic causation, Pereboom argues that if agents in the manipulated cases lack responsibility, so do agents in ordinary deterministic cases.

## John Martin Fischer (born 1952)
The primary compatibilist respondent to manipulation arguments; argues that the relevant distinction is not between manipulation and ordinary causation but between reasons-responsive and reasons-unresponsive mechanisms. Manipulation typically involves bypassing reasons-responsiveness; ordinary deterministic causation does not.

## Alfred Mele (born 1951)
Has contributed extensively to debates about manipulation and free will, developing the "zygote argument" (if an agent's character was determined from conception by an manipulative process, do they have free will?) and examining what would make manipulation freedom-undermining.

## Gary Watson (born 1943)
Introduced the distinction between the "mesh" account of freedom (desires and values mesh) and the "reasons" account (acting for reasons); his work on manipulation cases explores how manipulation undermines the kind of self-governance that grounds responsibility.
