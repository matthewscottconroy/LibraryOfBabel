# Important Terms and Ideas: Manipulation Arguments

**Manipulation Arguments**
A family of arguments against compatibilism: if a manipulated agent (whose desires were installed by another) lacks free will despite satisfying compatibilist conditions, and if ordinary determined agents are similarly situated, then ordinary determined agents also lack free will. Compatibilist conditions are insufficient.

**Four-Case Argument**
Pereboom's version: Case 1 (direct neuroscientific manipulation), Case 2 (upbringing designed to produce specific desires), Case 3 (designer manipulation of evolutionary history), Case 4 (ordinary determinism). In each case the agent satisfies standard compatibilist conditions; if Case 1 lacks responsibility, so does Case 4.

**History-Sensitivity**
The compatibilist response that freedom depends not just on current reasons-responsiveness but on the history of how that responsiveness came about. Manipulation is freedom-undermining when it bypasses the agent's own reasons-responsive processes in its installation; ordinary causal history does not.

**The Zygote Argument**
Mele's variant: suppose a manipulator designs the zygote that will become you, fully intending that you grow up with specific character traits and commit specific actions. You satisfy compatibilist conditions but seem to lack genuine freedom. Does ordinary determinism differ relevantly from this case?

**Reasons-Responsive Mechanisms**
Fischer's key criterion: an agent acts freely when their action is produced by a mechanism that is appropriately reasons-responsive—the mechanism would have produced a different action if the agent had recognized sufficient reasons for it. Manipulation typically installs reasons-unresponsive mechanisms.

**Bypassing**
Watson's concept: manipulation undermines freedom when it bypasses the agent's own evaluative processes—installing desires or beliefs by circumventing rational agency. When an agent's character is formed by engaging (even causally) with reasons and values, this is not bypassing and does not undermine freedom.
