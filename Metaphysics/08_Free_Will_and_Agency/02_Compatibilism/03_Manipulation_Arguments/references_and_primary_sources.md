# References and Primary Sources: Manipulation Arguments

## Foundational Texts

- Pereboom, Derk, *Living Without Free Will* (2001). Presents the four-case manipulation argument: a sequence from direct brain manipulation to ordinary determinism, arguing that if the manipulated agent lacks responsibility, so does the ordinary determinist agent.

- Fischer, John Martin, "The Transfer of Non-Responsibility," in *Free Will* edited by Ekstrom (2001). Compatibilist response to manipulation arguments; argues that what matters is the nature of the mechanism (reasons-responsive vs. not), not whether the mechanism was installed by another agent.

- Mele, Alfred, *Free: Why Science Hasn't Disproved Free Will* (2014). Examines manipulation cases and their implications, arguing that compatibilism can distinguish freedom-undermining from freedom-preserving causal histories.

## Contemporary Works

- Watson, Gary, "Reasons and Responsibility," *Ethics* 104 (1996). Examines the relationship between acting for reasons and moral responsibility, relevant to manipulation cases that bypass rational agency.

- McKenna, Michael, *Conversation and Responsibility* (2012). Develops a conversational model of moral responsibility that addresses manipulation cases through the quality of will expressed in actions.
