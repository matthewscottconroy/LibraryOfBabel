# Review Questions — Compatibilism

1. Hume argues that causal determination by character is not merely compatible with moral responsibility but is actually *required* for it. Hobbes reaches a structurally similar conclusion from his materialist starting point. Where do their arguments converge, and where do they part ways? Which version of the classical compatibilist thesis is more vulnerable to the manipulation objection, and why?

2. The conditional analysis captures something genuine — the connection between freedom and the agent's will — but ultimately fails. What are the two most serious objections to it, and how does the shift to reasons-responsiveness improve on the conditional analysis rather than simply restating it?

3. Frankfurt's hierarchical mesh theory distinguishes the *unwilling addict* (unfree) from the *willing addict* (free by Frankfurt's lights). Is this distinction plausible? Does the willing addict's endorsement of her craving constitute genuine freedom, or does the case expose a limitation in the mesh account's purely synchronic focus?

4. Frankfurt cases are designed to show that the Principle of Alternate Possibilities is false. The chapter discusses both the flicker-of-freedom response and the dilemma objection. Which of these poses the stronger challenge to Frankfurt's conclusion, and can Fischer's "blockage" refinement answer it without begging the question against the incompatibilist?

5. Pereboom's four-case argument is constructed so that each case satisfies the same compatibilist conditions as the last. Fischer and Ravizza respond with a historical condition — mechanism ownership — while Wolf appeals to the bypassing/shaping distinction. Evaluate these two responses: do they identify a morally relevant difference between manipulation and normal causal formation, or do they simply redescribe the same causal history in evaluatively loaded terms?

6. Source compatibilism holds that what grounds responsibility is the actual sequence — whether the action springs from the agent's own values and reasons — rather than any counterfactual facts about alternatives. Is this a coherent stopping point, or does the move to sourcehood simply relocate the incompatibilist's challenge from alternate possibilities to the question of ultimate authorship?

7. The manipulation argument reveals that classical compatibilism focused on synchronic structure while genuine freedom may require an appropriate diachronic history. But the chapter also notes that every causal history traces back to unchosen factors — genes, early environment — before rational agency exists. Does the historical compatibilist have a principled response to this regress, or does it collapse into the hard incompatibilist challenge?

8. The chapter moves from Hume and Hobbes through Frankfurt cases and manipulation arguments to the conclusion that contemporary compatibilism must be both reasons-responsive and historically sensitive. Stepping back: does this progressive elaboration suggest that compatibilism is converging on the right account, or that each refinement is driven by a deeper version of the same incompatibilist intuition — that true agency requires something no causal account can supply?
