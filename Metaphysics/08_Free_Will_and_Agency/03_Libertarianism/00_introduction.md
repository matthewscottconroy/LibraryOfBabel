# Libertarianism About Free Will

Libertarianism in the metaphysical sense — the view that free will is real, incompatible with determinism, and that determinism is false of human agency — attempts to preserve what the compatibilist denies: that genuine freedom requires that the future be genuinely open, that things could have gone otherwise in a robust, non-relativized sense. The libertarian agrees with the hard incompatibilist that determinism would undermine freedom; but unlike the hard incompatibilist, the libertarian believes that human beings actually possess free will.

The challenge for libertarianism is explaining what kind of indeterminism would help. Quantum mechanical indeterminism might seem to provide the required openness, but random quantum fluctuations do not seem to be under the agent's control. An action that is random rather than determined seems, if anything, less free than a determined one. Freedom requires that the agent be the source of the action in a way that mere randomness does not provide.

Agent causation offers one libertarian response: alongside event causation (events cause events), there is a fundamentally different category of causation — agent causation — in which an agent, qua agent, causes her actions without being caused to cause them by prior events. This preserves the asymmetry of control: the agent is the source of the action in a sense that is irreducible to the causal history of her brain states. Critics object that agent causation is mysterious — it is hard to understand how a substance can cause an event without this being explained by further events — and that it does not fit naturally into a scientific worldview.

Event-causal libertarianism attempts to maintain libertarianism within an event-causal framework: the agent's reasons and deliberations play an indeterministic causal role in the production of actions, such that the action is responsive to the agent's reasons without being determined by them. This preserves agent control without invoking a special kind of causation. Non-causal libertarianism proposes that some actions are uncaused by prior events yet genuinely self-caused in a sense that grounds responsibility. This chapter develops these positions and evaluates whether any version of libertarianism successfully captures the kind of freedom that moral responsibility requires.
