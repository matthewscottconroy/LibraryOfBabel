# References and Primary Sources: Agent Causation

## Foundational Texts

- Reid, Thomas, *Essays on the Active Powers of Man* (1788). The foundational modern statement of agent causation: a free action is caused by the agent as substance, not by prior events. The agent's causal power is irreducible to event causation.

- Chisholm, Roderick, "Human Freedom and the Self," in *Free Will* edited by Watson (1982). The canonical contemporary statement of agent causation: free actions involve a special form of causation in which the agent, not an event involving the agent, is the cause.

- O'Connor, Timothy, *Persons and Causes: The Metaphysics of Free Will* (2000). The most sustained contemporary defense of agent causation; argues that agent causal power is an emergent property of persons and that it does not require violation of physical law.

## Contemporary Works

- Clarke, Randolph, *Libertarian Accounts of Free Will* (2003). Surveys agent causal and event-causal libertarianism, assessing their strengths and weaknesses with attention to the randomness objection.

- Pereboom, Derk, *Free Will, Agency, and Meaning in Life* (2014). Critical examination of agent causation and hard incompatibilist alternatives.
