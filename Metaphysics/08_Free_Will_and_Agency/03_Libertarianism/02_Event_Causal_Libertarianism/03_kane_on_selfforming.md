# Kane on Self-Forming Actions

Robert Kane's account of libertarian free will, developed in *The Significance of Free Will* (1996) and extended in subsequent work, is the most philosophically sophisticated defense of the position currently available. Kane's project is not merely to provide a metaphysical account of free will — a description of what it is — but to explain why free will *matters*: why the kind of freedom that is incompatible with determinism has genuine value for human life and self-understanding. His answer is that the significance of free will lies in its connection to ultimate responsibility — to the idea that we are in some genuine sense the "ultimate creators" of our own lives and characters. This framing shapes everything that follows.

## Self-Forming Actions: The Basic Idea

Kane holds that free will in the full libertarian sense is not required for every action. Most of our everyday actions are not candidates for the kind of freedom Kane has in mind. What matters are *self-forming actions* (SFAs) — actions in which the agent's will is not yet fully formed, situations of genuine deliberative conflict where competing values or reasons pull in different directions simultaneously.

Consider Kane's own examples. A businessperson is driving to an important meeting and witnesses a violent assault. She faces a genuine conflict: stop to help the victim (expressing her commitment to civic duty and compassion) or drive on to protect her business interests (expressing her practical commitments and self-interest). Both motivations are genuinely present and operative. Or consider a person deciding whether to resist an addictive temptation at a moment when both the pull of the addiction and the desire to reform are present with approximately equal force. These are SFAs: the agent is genuinely pulled in two directions simultaneously, and her decision partially constitutes what kind of person she will continue to be.

## The Neural Mechanism

Kane proposes that in SFAs, quantum indeterminacy plays a role in the neural processes implementing deliberation. The competing motivations are sustained by competing neural networks — each network maintaining one of the possible choices. These networks are in tension, and the outcome — which network "wins" — is indeterministic at the quantum level.

Crucially, the indeterminacy is not random in the pejorative sense. Both possible outcomes are motivated by the agent's actual reasons and values:

- If the businessperson decides for principle (stops to help), this expresses her commitment to doing right and her compassion.
- If she decides for self-interest (drives on), this expresses her concern for her business and her practical commitments.

Neither outcome is alien to her; both are genuinely hers. The indeterminacy occurs not between a motivated outcome and an unmotivated one, but between two motivated outcomes.

## The Formal Analysis of SFAs

Kane's analysis involves three key claims working together:

1. *Dual motivation*: In an SFA, the agent has genuine motivations for each of two (or more) possible outcomes. She is not merely weakly inclined toward both; she is genuinely pulled by reasons favoring each option.

2. *Indeterministic causation*: The neural process is indeterministic. The deliberation causes the action, but causation is probabilistic — both outcomes are caused (in the probabilistic sense) by the agent's reasons; neither is determined.

3. *Endorsement*: Whichever way the decision goes, the agent endorses it as her own. Having decided for principle, she recognizes this as expressing her values. Had she decided for self-interest, she would have recognized that as expressing her values. Both decisions would have been hers.

The combination of dual motivation, indeterministic causation, and endorsement is designed to defeat the luck objection. The decision is not mere luck because it is caused by the agent's reasons (not by quantum noise from outside the agent); both possible outcomes express the agent's values (neither is alien); and the agent endorses the outcome as her own (she takes responsibility for whichever way it goes).

## The Regress of Self-Formation

Kane's deeper argument is that SFAs matter not just at individual moments but across a lifetime. Free will is significant because it allows us to be the authors of our own characters. SFAs are the moments at which character is genuinely being formed — when we decide what kind of person we will be.

- P1: For a person to be the ultimate author of her life and character, there must be moments at which her character is being formed by her own choices, not merely expressed by a pre-formed character.
- P2: SFAs are precisely such moments: situations of genuine conflict in which the agent's character is not yet settled, and her decision partially constitutes what character she will have going forward.
- P3: For SFAs to ground genuine self-formation, they must be genuinely undetermined — otherwise the character formed is itself determined by prior factors and not genuinely the agent's creation.
- C: Genuine self-authorship requires the possibility of SFAs, which requires indeterminism.

This argument shows why the *significance* of free will depends on its being libertarian. Compatibilist guidance control does not provide ultimate self-authorship, because in a deterministic world, all of one's character — including the character expressed in apparent SFAs — traces back to factors entirely outside one's control.

## Objections and Kane's Responses

*The residual luck objection*: Even in Kane's SFAs, the specific outcome is determined by quantum chance, not by the agent. Kane's response: the luck objection assumes that control requires determination — that the agent controls the action only if her prior states determine it. But this assumption is exactly what libertarianism challenges. In an SFA, the agent exercises control through her dual motivations — both outcomes express her will. The quantum indeterminism does not undermine this because neither possible outcome is alien to her.

*The endorsement problem*: Critics argue that endorsing an outcome after the fact does not make it genuinely the agent's choice. Kane's response: the endorsement is not retrospective rationalization but prospective authorship. The agent goes into the SFA knowing she will endorse whichever outcome occurs because she has genuine motivations for both. This prospective endorsement is what distinguishes SFAs from mere chance events.

*The frequency problem*: SFAs are relatively rare. Most everyday actions flow from a formed character rather than involving genuine deliberative conflict. Does this mean most everyday actions are not free? Kane's response: everyday actions that flow from a well-formed character are free in a secondary sense — they flow from a character that was itself formed through SFAs. The freedom of everyday actions is inherited from the SFAs that formed the character they express.

*The neural plausibility problem*: Is there evidence that quantum indeterminacy plays the role Kane invokes at the neural level of deliberation? Kane acknowledges this is an empirical question currently unsettled. He argues that his account is at least empirically possible given what we know about quantum mechanics and neuroscience, and that the burden falls on critics to show it is empirically impossible rather than merely uncertain.

## Kane's Contribution

Kane's theory raises the dialectical level of the debate in several respects. It takes empirical constraints seriously, invoking quantum indeterminacy rather than mysterious substance causation. It directly addresses the luck objection through the concept of dual motivation. It connects the metaphysics of free will to its practical and existential significance, showing why the kind of freedom libertarianism requires matters for human self-understanding. And it restricts the indeterminism to specific kinds of deliberation — SFAs — avoiding the implausible claim that all actions are indeterministic.

Whether Kane's account succeeds against the luck objection remains contested. The deepest issue — whether indeterminism can provide control rather than merely chance — has not been resolved. But Kane's theory has become the touchstone for contemporary libertarian philosophy of action, and engaging with it is unavoidable for anyone who wants to assess whether libertarianism is a viable position.
