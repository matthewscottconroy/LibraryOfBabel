# Important Figures: Event Causal Libertarianism

## Robert Kane (born 1938)
The leading contemporary libertarian philosopher; developed the event causal account of libertarian free will, arguing that genuinely free choices involve quantum-level indeterminism at "self-forming actions" that build the agent's character.

## Carl Ginet (born 1932)
Defends a non-causal actional theory of intentional action and has contributed to libertarian accounts that do not require agent causation; his work focuses on the structure of intentional action and what makes it free.

## Laura Waddell Ekstrom (born 1964)
Defends a coherentist account of autonomy combined with event causal libertarianism: free actions arise from preferences that cohere with the agent's evaluative system and are caused in an indeterminate way by those preferences.

## Randolph Clarke (born 1957)
Examined both agent causal and event causal forms of libertarianism, developing a "complex" view that combines event causation with agent causation, and assessed the advantages and disadvantages of each approach to libertarian free will.
