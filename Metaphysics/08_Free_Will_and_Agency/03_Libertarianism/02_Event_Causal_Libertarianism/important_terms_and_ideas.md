# Important Terms and Ideas: Event Causal Libertarianism

**Event Causal Libertarianism**
The view that free actions are caused by the agent's reasons, motives, and character (as events or states), but this causation is indeterministic—the reasons make the action probable but do not necessitate it. This preserves causation by reasons without lapsing into randomness or agent causation.

**Self-Forming Actions (SFAs)**
Kane's term for the key category of free actions: moments of genuine self-formation where indeterministic quantum-level processes make the outcome uncertain. SFAs are actions through which agents build their own character; most subsequent actions flow deterministically from that character.

**Quantum Indeterminism**
The appeal to quantum mechanical indeterminism to locate genuine undetermination in neural processes. Kane argues that amplified quantum indeterminism in neural pathways during moments of intense deliberation provides the physical basis for genuinely free choices.

**The Luck Objection**
The objection that event causal libertarianism collapses freedom into luck: if the outcome of deliberation is indeterministic, then whether I choose A or B is a matter of chance. If it's chance, I am not the author of my choice. Event causalists respond that reasons-causation (even indeterministic) is not the same as luck.

**Reasons-Causation**
The relationship between the agent's reasons (beliefs, desires, values) and their actions: reasons cause actions in a libertarian framework, but without necessitating them. The indeterminism is in the causal relation between reasons and action, not in the reasons themselves being absent.

**Soft Incompatibilism**
Pereboom's term for views that reject compatibilism but remain agnostic about libertarianism; these views hold that if determinism is true, no one is free, but do not commit to indeterministic free will as the solution.
