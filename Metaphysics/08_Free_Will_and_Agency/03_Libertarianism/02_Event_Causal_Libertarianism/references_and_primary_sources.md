# References and Primary Sources: Event Causal Libertarianism

## Foundational Texts

- Kane, Robert, *The Significance of Free Will* (1996). The canonical defense of event causal libertarianism: free choices involve quantum-level indeterminism that allows for "self-forming actions" (SFAs) in which the agent's reasons causally influence (but do not determine) their choices.

- Ginet, Carl, *On Action* (1990). Defends a non-causal account of action combined with libertarian indeterminism; examines the structure of intentional action and what it means for an action to be undetermined.

- Ekstrom, Laura, *Free Will: A Philosophical Study* (2000). Defends an event causal libertarianism based on a coherentist account of preferences; free actions arise from preferences that cohere with the agent's values and are indeterministically caused by them.

## Contemporary Works

- Clarke, Randolph, *Libertarian Accounts of Free Will* (2003). A systematic comparative examination of event causal and agent causal libertarianism; evaluates the randomness objection and other challenges.

- Balaguer, Mark, *Free Will as an Open Scientific Question* (2010). Argues that the question of whether we have libertarian free will is genuinely open because it depends on unresolved empirical questions about quantum mechanics and neuroscience.
