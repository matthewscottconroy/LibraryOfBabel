# Important Figures: Non-Causal Libertarianism

## Carl Ginet (born 1932)
The most prominent defender of non-causal libertarianism: intentional actions have an intrinsic phenomenological feature (the "actish phenomenal quality") that makes them actions, without requiring that they be caused by mental states. His view combines this with indeterminism for libertarian freedom.

## Hugh McCann (1943–2016)
Defended a non-causal account of volition: acts of will are not caused by prior mental states but are basic, uncaused exercises of agency. This grounds freedom without appealing to indeterministic causation or agent causation as a distinct metaphysical category.

## Stewart Goetz (born 1952)
Defends a non-causal libertarianism within a broader substance dualist framework, arguing that agent causation is better understood as the soul directly producing actions without prior event causation; this view combines non-causal agency with a dualist metaphysics of mind.

## Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)
Though not strictly a non-causal libertarian in the contemporary sense, Kant's account of transcendental freedom—the noumenal self as standing outside the causal order—influenced non-causal accounts of agency by positing a self that initiates action without itself being determined by prior causes.
