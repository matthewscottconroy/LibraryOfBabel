# Important Terms and Ideas: Non-Causal Libertarianism

**Non-Causal Theory of Action**
The view that intentional actions are not constituted by their being caused by mental states (beliefs, desires, intentions) but by some intrinsic feature of the action itself—typically a phenomenological "actish" quality. What makes a movement an action is not its causal history but its intrinsic character.

**Actish Phenomenal Quality**
Ginet's term: the intrinsic phenomenological property that distinguishes actions from mere happenings—the felt quality of doing. When I raise my arm, there is something it is like to do so that is intrinsic to the action, not constituted by its causal antecedents.

**Volition**
An act of will: a basic mental act that initiates bodily movement. On non-causal accounts, volitions are not caused by prior mental states but are themselves primitive exercises of agency. This avoids the causal chain of mental events that both compatibilist and event-causal libertarian accounts require.

**Spontaneity**
The Kantian concept of the will's self-determination: genuine freedom involves the will determining itself through reason, not being determined by prior causes. The spontaneous will is not caused from without but issues from the agent's own rational nature.

**Randomness vs. Actishness**
Non-causal libertarians argue their view avoids the randomness objection: the actish quality of an action is not a random event but an exercise of agency. Even if undetermined, an action with the actish quality is a doing, not a mere happening—agency is built into the intrinsic nature of action.

**Basic Actions**
Actions that are performed without doing any other action first—primitive, foundational acts. Non-causal accounts typically hold that basic actions (moving a limb, uttering a word) are not caused by prior mental events but are direct exercises of agency.
