# References and Primary Sources: Non-Causal Libertarianism

## Foundational Texts

- Ginet, Carl, *On Action* (1990). The canonical defense of non-causal theory of action: simple acts have an intrinsic phenomenological quality (actishness) that makes them actions, not their being caused by mental states. Combined with indeterminism, this yields libertarian freedom.

- McCann, Hugh, *The Works of Agency: On Human Action, Will, and Freedom* (1998). Defends a non-causal account of volition: acts of will are basic, uncaused mental events that initiate action; the agent is genuinely the source of their actions without requiring prior mental causes.

- Kant, Immanuel, *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781), Third Antinomy. The transcendental idealist treatment of freedom: freedom from nature's causal determination is possible because the agent, as noumenal self, stands outside the empirical causal order.

## Contemporary Works

- Clarke, Randolph, *Libertarian Accounts of Free Will* (2003). Comparative assessment of causal and non-causal libertarian accounts; examines whether non-causal libertarianism can avoid the randomness objection.

- O'Connor, Timothy and Sandis, Constantine (eds.), *A Companion to the Philosophy of Action* (2010). Contains articles on non-causal and causal theories of action, providing context for non-causal libertarianism.
