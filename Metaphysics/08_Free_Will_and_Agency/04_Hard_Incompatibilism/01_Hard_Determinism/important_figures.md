# Important Figures: Hard Determinism

## Baron d'Holbach (1723–1789)
The most systematic Enlightenment hard determinist: all human actions are necessitated by prior causes (physiology, upbringing, circumstance); free will is an illusion; moral responsibility as ordinarily conceived is unjustified. Defended in *The System of Nature*.

## Clarence Darrow (1857–1938)
Famous trial lawyer who used hard determinist arguments in defense of clients, most notably in the Leopold and Loeb trial (1924), arguing that criminal behavior is the product of prior causes beyond the agent's control and that punishment should be replaced by treatment and prevention.

## Derk Pereboom (born 1957)
Contemporary hard incompatibilist (though he prefers this term to "hard determinist" since he holds free will is impossible whether or not determinism is true); argues in *Living Without Free Will* (2001) that we should accept the absence of free will and revise our moral practices accordingly.

## Galen Strawson (born 1952)
Developed the "basic argument" for the impossibility of ultimate moral responsibility: to be truly responsible for your actions, you must be responsible for the character that determines them, which requires being responsible for what made your character, generating an infinite regress.
