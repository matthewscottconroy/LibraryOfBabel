# Important Terms and Ideas: Hard Determinism

**Hard Determinism**
The conjunction of two claims: (1) determinism is true, and (2) determinism is incompatible with free will and moral responsibility. Hard determinists conclude that no one is ever genuinely free or deserves praise or blame for their actions.

**Causal Necessity**
The thesis that every event, including every human action, is causally necessitated by prior events and laws of nature. Given the complete causal history of the universe, every action that occurs could not have failed to occur—there is no genuine ability to do otherwise.

**Ultimate Moral Responsibility**
The kind of responsibility involved in genuine desert: being truly the source of one's actions in a way that makes one truly deserving of praise or punishment. Hard incompatibilists argue that this kind of responsibility requires being the ultimate cause of oneself, which is impossible.

**The Basic Argument**
Galen Strawson's argument: to be truly responsible for your action A, you must be responsible for your character C that caused A. To be responsible for C, you must be responsible for what caused C—your earlier character C'. This regress has no end; ultimate self-determination is impossible.

**Luck Egalitarianism Applied to Responsibility**
The view that if agents' circumstances, character, and opportunities are all matters of luck—factors beyond their ultimate control—then differential praise and punishment based on these factors is morally unjustifiable. Hard incompatibilists draw on luck considerations to undermine the desert basis for punishment.

**Revisionary Practices**
What hard incompatibilists propose in place of retributive punishment and desert-based praise: forward-looking practices justified by deterrence, rehabilitation, and public safety, without the backward-looking assumption that agents deserve to suffer for their actions.
