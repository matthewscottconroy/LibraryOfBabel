# References and Primary Sources: Hard Determinism

## Foundational Texts

- d'Holbach, Baron, *The System of Nature* (1770). The systematic Enlightenment defense of necessitarianism and the denial of free will: human beings are wholly natural, wholly determined, and wholly incapable of genuine self-determination.

- Pereboom, Derk, *Living Without Free Will* (2001). The most careful contemporary defense of hard incompatibilism; argues that neither compatibilism nor libertarianism provides the kind of free will required for basic desert moral responsibility.

- Strawson, Galen, "The Impossibility of Moral Responsibility," *Philosophical Studies* 75 (1994). Presents the basic argument: genuine moral responsibility requires ultimate self-origination, which is impossible for any being in any possible world.

## Contemporary Works

- Waller, Bruce, *Against Moral Responsibility* (2011). Argues that moral responsibility is an intellectually and ethically bankrupt concept that should be abandoned in favor of more humane and accurate understanding of human behavior.

- Levy, Neil, *Hard Luck: How Luck Undermines Free Will and Moral Responsibility* (2011). Argues that luck—both epistemic and circumstantial—pervades human action to a degree that undermines moral responsibility.
