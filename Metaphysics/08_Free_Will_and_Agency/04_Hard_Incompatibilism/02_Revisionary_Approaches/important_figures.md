# Important Figures: Revisionary Approaches to Free Will

## P.F. Strawson (1919–2006)
His essay "Freedom and Resentment" (1962) shifted the debate from metaphysics to moral psychology: what matters is whether agents are appropriate targets of "reactive attitudes" (resentment, gratitude, indignation) that constitute moral responsibility practices. Strawson was a compatibilist but his framework opened space for revisionary views.

## Derk Pereboom (born 1957)
The leading hard incompatibilist who argues for revising our moral practices: we should replace retributive punishment with forward-looking approaches, and replace reactive attitudes of resentment with forward-looking attitudes of concern. Hard incompatibilism can be "lived well."

## Manuel Vargas (born 1971)
Defends "revisionism": neither compatibilism nor incompatibilism as traditionally conceived is correct, but a revised notion of free will—tracking the practical purposes of responsibility practices—is both defensible and compatible with determinism.

## Susan Wolf (born 1952)
Defends a "Reason View" of free will: freedom consists in the ability to act in accordance with Reason—to see and respond to the True and the Good. This view leads to a revisionary account of responsibility that includes excusing conditions based on the agent's epistemic situation.
