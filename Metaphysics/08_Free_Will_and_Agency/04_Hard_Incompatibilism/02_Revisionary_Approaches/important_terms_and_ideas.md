# Important Terms and Ideas: Revisionary Approaches to Free Will

**Reactive Attitudes**
P.F. Strawson's term for the attitudes that constitute moral responsibility practices: resentment, gratitude, indignation, moral praise, and blame. These attitudes are responses to the quality of agents' wills—to whether they exhibit good will, ill will, or indifference. Strawson argues that the free will debate should focus on when these attitudes are appropriate.

**The Objective Stance**
Strawson's contrast to the reactive stance: treating an agent objectively—as an object to be managed, treated, or manipulated—rather than as a moral participant toward whom reactive attitudes are appropriate. We take the objective stance toward the insane or very young.

**Revisionism**
Vargas's position: traditional concepts of free will and moral responsibility are in various ways defective, but they can be revised to track what really matters—typically, the forward-looking purposes of responsibility practices. Revisionism holds that neither compatibilism nor incompatibilism wins as traditionally stated.

**Forward-Looking Responsibility**
Pereboom's proposal: abandoning retributive practices based on desert, we should adopt purely forward-looking practices justified by deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation, and public safety. This is consistent with hard incompatibilism and preserves much of the structure of moral life.

**The Reason View**
Susan Wolf's account: freedom requires the ability to act in accordance with Reason—not just any reasons but the True and the Good. This is a non-neutral account that grounds excusing conditions: those who cannot recognize the Good are not responsible.

**Quality of Will**
Gary Watson's and Strawson's idea: what makes agents appropriate targets of reactive attitudes is the quality of will expressed in their actions—whether they show good will, ill will, or indifference toward others. This quality-of-will criterion shapes revisionary accounts of responsibility.
