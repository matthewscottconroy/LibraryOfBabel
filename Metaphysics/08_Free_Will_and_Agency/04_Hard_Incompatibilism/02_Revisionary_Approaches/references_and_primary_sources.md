# References and Primary Sources: Revisionary Approaches to Free Will

## Foundational Texts

- Strawson, P.F., "Freedom and Resentment," *Proceedings of the British Academy* 48 (1962). The landmark paper repositioning the free will debate in terms of reactive attitudes: what grounds moral responsibility is our practices of holding responsible, which are partially constitutive of the moral life.

- Pereboom, Derk, *Living Without Free Will* (2001). Defends hard incompatibilism and argues that accepting the lack of free will need not be devastating; reactive attitudes can be replaced with forward-looking analogs, and life can be meaningful without desert-based responsibility.

- Vargas, Manuel, *Building Better Beings: A Theory of Moral Responsibility* (2013). Defends a revisionist account: the traditional conception of free will is flawed but can be revised to track the practical purposes of responsibility practices without requiring libertarian or classical compatibilist conditions.

## Contemporary Works

- Wolf, Susan, *Freedom Within Reason* (1990). The Reason View: freedom requires the ability to act in accordance with Reason (the True and the Good). This grounds a revisionary account with substantive requirements beyond mere reasons-responsiveness.

- Fischer, John Martin, *Deep Control: Essays on Free Will and Value* (2012). Examines the relationship between free will, moral responsibility, and what makes life meaningful, with attention to revisionary proposals.
