# Important Figures: Freedom and Neuroscience

## Benjamin Libet (1916–2007)
Neuroscientist whose experiments (1983) on the timing of conscious will relative to brain activity generated enormous philosophical debate; the "readiness potential" was detected ~550ms before movement, while subjects reported conscious intention only ~200ms before—suggesting unconscious brain processes precede conscious decisions.

## Daniel Wegner (1948–2013)
Psychologist who argued in *The Illusion of Conscious Will* (2002) that conscious will is not the cause of action but a post-hoc interpretation—we experience ourselves as willing actions that are already caused by unconscious processes. This would undermine the role of conscious agency.

## Patrick Haggard (born 1966)
Neuroscientist who extended and refined Libet-style experiments, contributing to precise measurement of the relationship between brain activity, conscious intention, and action, and who has engaged philosophically with the implications for agency.

## Alfred Mele (born 1951)
Philosopher who has most carefully examined the neuroscientific evidence about free will, arguing that the Libet experiments and related work do not establish that conscious will is an illusion and that the implications for free will have been overstated.
