# References and Primary Sources: Freedom and Neuroscience

## Foundational Texts

- Libet, Benjamin et al., "Time of Conscious Intention to Act in Relation to Onset of Cerebral Activity (Readiness-Potential)," *Brain* 106 (1983). The original Libet experiment; reports that readiness potentials precede subjects' reported awareness of intention by ~350ms, raising questions about the causal role of conscious will.

- Wegner, Daniel, *The Illusion of Conscious Will* (2002). Argues that conscious will is an epiphenomenal interpretation—a felt sense of willing that accompanies but does not cause action; unconscious processes are the real causes.

- Mele, Alfred, *Free: Why Science Hasn't Disproved Free Will* (2014). The most thorough philosophical assessment of the neuroscientific evidence; argues that Libet-style experiments are widely over-interpreted and do not establish that free will is an illusion.

## Contemporary Works

- Haynes, John-Dylan et al., "Reading Hidden Intentions in the Human Brain," *Current Biology* 17 (2007). Extension of Libet experiments using fMRI showing that brain activity predictive of a choice can be detected up to 10 seconds before the subject is consciously aware of choosing.

- Nahmias, Eddy, "Why We Have Free Will," *Scientific American* 312 (2015). An accessible compatibilist response to neuroscientific arguments, arguing they target a concept of free will that compatibilists do not hold.
