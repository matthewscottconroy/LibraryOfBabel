# Important Figures: The Nature of Metaphysics

## Aristotle (384–322 BCE)
The originator of the discipline: his *Metaphysics* (the works "after" the Physics) examines being qua being—what exists insofar as it exists, and the primary causes and principles of things. Aristotle established the central questions of ontology, substance theory, and the four causes.

## Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)
Transformed the discipline by asking how metaphysics is possible as a science: his Critique argues that traditional metaphysics overreaches pure reason. Kant distinguished phenomena (objects as they appear) from noumena (things in themselves) and confined legitimate metaphysics to the conditions of possible experience.

## Rudolf Carnap (1891–1970)
The most influential critic of traditional metaphysics in the twentieth century; argued in "Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology" (1950) and "The Elimination of Metaphysics" (1932) that metaphysical claims are cognitively meaningless—neither verifiable nor analytic.

## W.V.O. Quine (1908–2000)
Rehabilitated naturalistic ontology: the question of what exists is continuous with science, to be answered by quantifying over the values of variables in our best scientific theories. His "On What There Is" (1948) reshaped how analytic philosophers approach ontological questions.
