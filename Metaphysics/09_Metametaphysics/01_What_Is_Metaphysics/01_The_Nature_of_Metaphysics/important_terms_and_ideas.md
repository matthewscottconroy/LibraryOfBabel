# Important Terms and Ideas: The Nature of Metaphysics

**Metaphysics**
The branch of philosophy that investigates the most fundamental features of reality: what exists, what categories of things there are, what their ultimate nature is, and how they are related. Aristotle defined it as the science of being qua being—what it is to be anything at all.

**Being Qua Being**
Aristotle's characterization of the subject matter of first philosophy: not beings of a specific kind (physical, mathematical) but beings insofar as they are beings—the generic features that belong to everything that exists in virtue of existing.

**First Philosophy**
Aristotle's term for metaphysics: the most fundamental science, which investigates the principles and causes that apply to all things, unlike the special sciences that investigate specific domains. Contemporary metametaphysics asks whether first philosophy in this sense is possible.

**Metaphysical Anti-Realism**
The family of views that deny that metaphysical questions have mind-independent answers: either there are no metaphysical facts, or metaphysical facts are constituted by our conceptual schemes. Carnap's position is a strong anti-realism; Hirsch's quantifier variance is a weaker version.

**Quinean Ontology**
The methodological view that ontological questions are answered by asking what the variables of our best scientific theories must range over. The ontological commitments of a theory are the values of its bound variables. This approach makes ontology continuous with natural science.

**Metametaphysics**
The study of the nature and methodology of metaphysics itself: whether metaphysical questions are substantive, what makes metaphysical inquiry legitimate, how metaphysical claims relate to scientific claims, and whether traditional ontological disputes are genuine or merely verbal.
