# References and Primary Sources: The Nature of Metaphysics

## Foundational Texts

- Aristotle, *Metaphysics* (c. 350 BCE), Books Gamma and Epsilon. Defines the science of being qua being, distinguishes first philosophy from the special sciences, and examines what it means to study being as such rather than being under some special description.

- Kant, Immanuel, *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics* (1783). Accessible version of the first *Critique*'s argument about the limits and conditions of metaphysical knowledge; examines what metaphysics can legitimately aspire to.

- Carnap, Rudolf, "The Elimination of Metaphysics through Logical Analysis of Language," in *Logical Positivism* edited by Ayer (1959). The sharpest positivist critique: traditional metaphysical sentences are pseudo-statements, neither verifiable nor analytic.

## Contemporary Works

- Quine, W.V.O., "On What There Is," *Review of Metaphysics* 2 (1948). The founding document of Quinean ontology: what exists is what we must quantify over in our best theories; ontological questions are continuous with science.

- Chalmers, David, Manley, David, and Wasserman, Ryan (eds.), *Metametaphysics: New Essays on the Foundations of Ontology* (2009). The defining anthology of contemporary metametaphysics; contains essays by Sider, Hirsch, Fine, Thomasson, and others on the status and methodology of metaphysics.
