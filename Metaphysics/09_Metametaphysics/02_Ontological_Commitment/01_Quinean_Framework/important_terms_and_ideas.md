# Important Terms and Ideas: The Quinean Framework

**Ontological Commitment**
Quine's criterion: a theory is ontologically committed to whatever entities must exist for the theory to be true—the values that the theory's variables must range over. "To be is to be the value of a variable."

**Criterion of Ontological Commitment**
The methodological principle for determining what a theory says exists: paraphrase the theory into first-order logic and read off the ontological commitments from the range of quantification. Entities quantified over are what the theory says there are.

**The Quinean Ontological Method**
The procedure: (1) identify our best theories, (2) paraphrase them into canonical first-order logical form, (3) determine what the variables range over, (4) accept those ranges as our ontological commitments. This treats ontology as continuous with science.

**Internal vs. External Questions (Carnap)**
Carnap's distinction: internal questions ask, within a linguistic framework, whether certain entities exist (e.g., "Are there prime numbers?" within the language of mathematics). External questions ask whether to adopt a framework at all—a purely pragmatic, not factual, question. Quine rejected this distinction.

**Ontological Parsimony**
The methodological principle (Ockham's Razor) that we should not multiply entities beyond necessity—a theory that posits fewer entities is preferable, all else equal. Applied within Quinean ontology, parsimony favors theories with fewer ontological commitments.

**Paraphrase Strategy**
The approach of showing that apparent ontological commitments can be eliminated by paraphrasing sentences into equivalent forms that do not require the disputed entities. Quine himself used paraphrase to eliminate commitment to propositions and properties in favor of linguistic items.
