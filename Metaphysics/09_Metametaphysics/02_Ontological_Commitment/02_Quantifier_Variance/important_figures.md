# Important Figures: Quantifier Variance

## Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)
Introduced quantifier variance as a metaontological thesis: there are multiple equally good quantifier meanings, and ontological disputes often reflect different choices of quantifier rather than genuine disagreement about what exists. This was his response to Quine's ontological method.

## Eli Hirsch (born 1940)
The leading contemporary defender of quantifier variance: ontological disputes between, e.g., four-dimensionalists and three-dimensionalists, or universalists and nihilists about composition, are merely verbal—the parties use "exists" with different meanings, and both are correct given their own usage.

## Theodore Sider (born 1967)
The most influential critic of quantifier variance: argues in *Writing the Book of the World* (2011) that there is a single joint-carving use of the quantifier—the one that tracks the fundamental structure of reality. Ontological disputes are not merely verbal; some quantifiers are more natural than others.

## Amie Thomasson (born 1968)
Defends easy ontology: many ontological questions are easily answered by conceptual analysis of the conditions under which ordinary terms apply, without any need for deep metaphysical inquiry. This is distinct from quantifier variance but similarly deflationary.
