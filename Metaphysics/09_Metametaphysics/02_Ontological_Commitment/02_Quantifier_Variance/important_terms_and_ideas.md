# Important Terms and Ideas: Quantifier Variance

**Quantifier Variance**
The thesis that there are multiple possible meanings for the existential quantifier (∃), corresponding to different but equally legitimate conceptual schemes. When ontologists disagree about whether there are composite objects, they may be using "there are" with different meanings, and both are correct.

**Verbal Disputes**
Disputes that appear substantive but are merely about word choice or conceptual framework. Hirsch argues that many ontological disputes—especially about composition, persistence, and numbers—are verbal: the disputants are using key terms differently and talking past each other.

**Ontological Deflationism**
The view that ontological questions do not have deep, theory-independent answers—they are either unanswerable, merely verbal, or answerable by trivial conceptual analysis. Quantifier variance and easy ontology are forms of ontological deflationism.

**Joint-Carving Quantifier**
Sider's concept: among all the possible quantifier meanings, some more closely reflect the fundamental structure of reality—they "carve at the joints." On Sider's view, metaphysics aims to use the most natural, joint-carving quantifier, making ontological questions substantive.

**Ontological Pluralism**
The view associated with quantifier variance that there is no single correct answer to "What is there?" because existence is not univocal—different frameworks yield different, equally valid ontological verdicts. This contrasts with ontological monism, which holds there is one correct ontology.

**Easy Ontology**
Thomasson's view: for ordinary objects (tables, chairs, persons), existence questions are answered easily by applying the concepts that govern our ordinary use of terms—no deep metaphysical investigation is needed. If the application conditions for "table" are met, tables exist.
