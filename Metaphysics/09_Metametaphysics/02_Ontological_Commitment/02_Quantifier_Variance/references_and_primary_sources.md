# References and Primary Sources: Quantifier Variance

## Foundational Texts

- Putnam, Hilary, "Realism and Reason," in *Meaning and the Moral Sciences* (1978). Early formulation of the idea that different ontological schemes can be equally correct given different interpretations of the quantifier.

- Hirsch, Eli, "Quantifier Variance and Realism," *Philosophical Issues* 12 (2002). The definitive contemporary statement of quantifier variance; argues that major ontological disputes are verbal and that charity in interpretation reveals the disputants are talking past each other.

- Sider, Theodore, *Writing the Book of the World* (2011). The main contemporary critique of quantifier variance; argues for natural quantifiers that carve reality at its joints, making ontological disputes substantive.

## Contemporary Works

- Chalmers, David, "Ontological Anti-Realism," in *Metametaphysics* edited by Chalmers, Manley, and Wasserman (2009). A careful examination of metaontological anti-realism, including quantifier variance, and its implications for the status of ontological disputes.

- Thomasson, Amie, *Ordinary Objects* (2007). Defends easy ontology by arguing that existence questions for ordinary objects are answered by conceptual analysis, not by deep metaphysical investigation.
