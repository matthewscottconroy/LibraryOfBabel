# Important Figures: Neo-Fregeanism

## Gottlob Frege (1848–1925)
The original source: Frege's context principle ("only in the context of a sentence does a word have meaning") and his logicism (arithmetic is reducible to logic) inspired neo-Fregeans to argue that the existence of abstract objects can be established by logical and conceptual means.

## Crispin Wright (born 1942)
The founder of contemporary neo-Fregeanism; in *Frege's Conception of Numbers as Objects* (1983) argued that Hume's Principle (the number of Fs = the number of Gs iff there is a one-one correspondence between Fs and Gs) can serve as a logical foundation for arithmetic, vindicating a form of Frege's logicism.

## Bob Hale (1945–2017)
Wright's collaborator in developing neo-Fregeanism; co-authored *The Reason's Proper Study* (2001) defending abstractionism and responding to critics, including the bad company objection and the epistemic objection to abstract objects.

## Kit Fine (born 1946)
Examined the conditions under which abstraction principles like Hume's Principle are acceptable; his work on "the limits of abstraction" identifies principled constraints on neo-Fregean abstraction and engages with the neo-Fregean program from a sympathetic but critical perspective.
