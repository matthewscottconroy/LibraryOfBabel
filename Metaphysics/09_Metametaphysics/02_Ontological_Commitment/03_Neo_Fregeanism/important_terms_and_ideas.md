# Important Terms and Ideas: Neo-Fregeanism

**Hume's Principle**
The abstraction principle: the number of Fs = the number of Gs if and only if there is a one-one correspondence (bijection) between the Fs and the Gs. Neo-Fregeans argue this principle is a priori knowable and, together with second-order logic, suffices to derive arithmetic.

**Abstraction Principles**
Principles of the form: the abstraction of a = the abstraction of b iff a and b stand in equivalence relation R. Hume's Principle and Basic Law V are abstraction principles. Neo-Fregeans argue that acceptable abstraction principles provide a priori knowledge of abstract objects like numbers and directions.

**Context Principle**
Frege's methodological principle: "Only in the context of a sentence does a word have a meaning." Applied to abstract objects: we can secure reference to numbers by specifying the conditions under which number-terms have the same reference (Hume's Principle), without requiring ostensive introduction.

**Frege's Logicism**
The thesis that arithmetic reduces to logic: all arithmetical truths can be derived from purely logical axioms and definitions. Basic Law V (Frege's attempted foundation) is inconsistent (Russell's Paradox); neo-Fregeans replace it with Hume's Principle, which is consistent.

**Bad Company Objection**
An objection to neo-Fregeanism: Basic Law V is itself an abstraction principle of the same logical form as Hume's Principle, but it leads to contradiction. What principled criterion distinguishes acceptable from unacceptable abstraction principles? Neo-Fregeans must provide a principled answer.

**Julius Caesar Problem**
A challenge raised by Frege himself: Hume's Principle fixes the truth conditions of "the number of Fs = the number of Gs" but not of "Julius Caesar = the number of Fs." Without fixing the latter, we have not fully determined what numbers are—we cannot exclude their being identical with persons.
