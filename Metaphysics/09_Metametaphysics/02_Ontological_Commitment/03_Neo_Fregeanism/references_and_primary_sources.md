# References and Primary Sources: Neo-Fregeanism

## Foundational Texts

- Frege, Gottlob, *The Foundations of Arithmetic* (1884). The source of the context principle and the logicist program; introduces Hume's Principle (in §63) as a way of explaining reference to numbers.

- Wright, Crispin, *Frege's Conception of Numbers as Objects* (1983). The founding document of neo-Fregeanism; argues that Hume's Principle is a priori knowable and suffices to derive arithmetic, reviving a version of Frege's logicism.

- Hale, Bob and Wright, Crispin, *The Reason's Proper Study: Essays Towards a Neo-Fregean Philosophy of Mathematics* (2001). The main collection defending and developing neo-Fregeanism; responds to the Julius Caesar problem, bad company objection, and other challenges.

## Contemporary Works

- Fine, Kit, *The Limits of Abstraction* (2002). A careful technical and philosophical examination of neo-Fregean abstraction principles; identifies conditions for acceptable abstraction and explores the limits of the program.

- MacBride, Fraser, "On Finite Hume," *Philosophia Mathematica* 8 (2000). A critical examination of neo-Fregean attempts to establish the infinity of natural numbers from Hume's Principle.
