# Important Terms and Ideas: The Grounding Relation

**Grounding**
A relation of metaphysical dependence and determination: A grounds B if B holds in virtue of A—A is why B obtains. Grounding is typically characterized as non-modal (not reducible to necessity), asymmetric, irreflexive, and explanatory. Examples: mental facts are grounded in physical facts; mathematical facts are grounded in set-theoretic facts.

**Full vs. Partial Grounding**
Full grounding: A fully grounds B if A alone is sufficient to ground B. Partial grounding: A partially grounds B if A is one of several factors that together ground B. The distinction is important for analyzing complex grounding relations in metaphysics.

**Immediate vs. Mediate Grounding**
Immediate grounding: A immediately grounds B if there is no C such that A grounds C and C grounds B. Mediate grounding: A mediately grounds B via intermediate grounds. This distinction matters for understanding the structure of grounding chains and fundamentality.

**Grounding Transitivity**
The principle that if A grounds B and B grounds C, then A grounds C. This principle is widely accepted but has generated some debate; apparent counterexamples motivate refinements of the grounding relation.

**Grounding vs. Causation**
Grounding is distinct from causation: causal relations are between events in time; grounding relations hold between facts, propositions, or entities and are typically atemporal. Water's causing the wetness of the pavement is causal; the wetness's being grounded in the water's molecular structure is grounding.

**The Priority Structure of Reality**
The hierarchical ordering of facts by their grounding relations: fundamental facts at the bottom, derived facts at higher levels, all related by grounding. This structure is what Fine and Schaffer take to be the subject matter of metaphysics.
