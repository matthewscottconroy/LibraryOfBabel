# Important Terms and Ideas: Fundamentality

**Fundamental Facts / Entities**
Facts or entities that are ungrounded—they do not hold or exist in virtue of anything more basic. Fundamental facts are the foundation of the grounding hierarchy; everything else is grounded, ultimately, in fundamental facts.

**Priority Monism**
Schaffer's thesis: the whole universe is the one fundamental entity; all other things (particles, persons, events) are grounded in the whole. This is the monist alternative to priority pluralism (the view that fundamental entities are many small things).

**Priority Pluralism**
The more common view: fundamental entities are the smallest physical constituents of matter (particles, fields, or whatever physics ultimately posits). Composite things are grounded in, and less fundamental than, their physical constituents.

**Natural Properties (Lewis)**
Properties that are "perfectly natural"—those that ground objective resemblance and nomological connections between things. Mass and charge are perfectly natural; being grue or being more than 1 meter from the Eiffel Tower are not. Naturalness is a primitive metaphysical notion.

**Structure (Sider)**
Sider's generalization of Lewis's naturalness: not just properties but also relations, quantifiers, predicates, and logical constants can be more or less natural—more or less closely tracking the fundamental structure of reality. Metaphysics aims to describe reality using maximally structural vocabulary.

**Infinitely Descending Grounding**
The possibility that the grounding hierarchy has no fundamental level—every grounded fact is grounded in further grounded facts without end. This is "infinite descent." Whether physics and metaphysics require or rule out such descent is an open question.
