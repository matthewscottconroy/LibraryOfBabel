# References and Primary Sources: Fundamentality

## Foundational Texts

- Schaffer, Jonathan, "Is There a Fundamental Level?" *Noûs* 37 (2003). Examines whether the grounding hierarchy must have a fundamental level (bottom-out in ungrounded facts) or whether infinite regresses of grounding are possible.

- Sider, Theodore, *Writing the Book of the World* (2011). Develops the notion of "structure" as fundamental naturalness; argues that the best metaphysics uses vocabulary that tracks the world's fundamental joints, and that not all ways of describing the world are equal.

- Lewis, David, "New Work for a Theory of Universals," *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 61 (1983). Introduces "perfectly natural properties" as fundamental—the ones that ground all objective resemblance, nomological connections, and supervenience relations.

## Contemporary Works

- Bennett, Karen, *Making Things Up* (2017). Develops a unified account of building relations (grounding, composition, realization, etc.) and their implications for fundamentality and ontology.

- Wilson, Jessica, "No Work for a Theory of Grounding," *Inquiry* 57 (2014). A critical examination arguing that the appeal to a primitive grounding relation is unnecessary—the work can be done by specific metaphysical relations like realization, composition, and identity.
