# Important Terms and Ideas: Applications of Grounding

**Truthmaking**
The application of grounding to the correspondence of truth: a truth T is made true by some entity or fact that grounds T's obtaining. The truthmaking relation is a specific grounding relation between a proposition and its truthmaker.

**Supervenience and Grounding**
Grounding can explain why supervenience holds: if mental facts supervene on physical facts because they are grounded in physical facts, then the grounding relation explains the modal co-variation. Grounding is thus more metaphysically explanatory than supervenience alone.

**Normative Grounding**
The application of grounding to ethics and normativity: moral facts are grounded in natural facts—"in virtue of being painful, this action is wrong." Grounding here captures the non-reductive supervenience of the normative on the natural.

**Ontological Dependence**
A genus of grounding: entity A ontologically depends on entity B if A's existence or identity is grounded in B's existence or identity. Artifacts depend on their makers; holes depend on their hosts; events depend on their participants.

**Reduction via Grounding**
One kind of reduction: higher-level facts (biological, psychological) are reduced to lower-level facts (physical) by showing that the former are grounded in the latter. Grounding-based reduction preserves the reality of higher-level facts while explaining them in terms of lower-level ones.

**Grounding Physicalism**
The view that all facts are grounded, ultimately, in physical facts—the mental, the biological, the normative, and the mathematical are all grounded in the physical. This is a grounding-based form of physicalism that is distinct from reductive identity physicalism.
