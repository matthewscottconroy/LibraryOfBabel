# Important Terms and Ideas: Fine on Essence

**Fine's Essentialist Thesis**
Essence is prior to and more fundamental than modality: essential truths ground necessary truths, not vice versa. The correct order of explanation is: P is necessary because it follows from the essence of something—not: P is essential because the object has P necessarily.

**The Socrates-Singleton Counterexample**
Fine's main example against the modal account: Socrates is a member of the set {Socrates} in every world where both exist, so by the modal account, being a member of {Socrates} is essential to Socrates. But intuitively this is wrong—being a member of the singleton is not part of what Socrates is. The reverse (that Socrates is essential to {Socrates}) is correct.

**The Essentialist Operator**
Fine's formal device: "It is true in virtue of the nature of x that P" (often written ∈ₓP). This is the primitive essentialist operator; modal claims are derived from essentialist claims using this operator, not the other way around.

**Constitutive Essence**
The most basic level of Fine's hierarchy: properties that directly constitute what a thing is—core essentialist claims that do not derive from other essentialist claims about that thing. Being human is constitutive of Socrates; being non-self-membered is constitutive of sets.

**Real Definition**
An Aristotelian notion revived by Fine: a real definition specifies the essence of a thing—what it is for that thing to be the thing it is. Real definitions differ from nominal definitions (how we use words) and from analytic truths (consequences of concepts). Fine argues that metaphysics is fundamentally in the business of giving real definitions.

**Consequential Essence**
Properties that are part of a thing's essence derivatively—they follow from the thing's constitutive essence. Being possibly human is consequentially essential to Socrates (following from the constitutive essence that he is human). Fine distinguishes this from the constitutive core.
