# Important Terms and Ideas: Essence in Metaphysics

**Scientific Essentialism**
The view (Ellis, Bird) that natural kinds like electrons and water have real essences consisting in their fundamental causal powers and dispositional properties. These essences ground the necessity of natural laws: laws hold necessarily because they reflect the essences of natural kinds.

**Dispositional Essentialism**
A form of scientific essentialism: properties essentially have their causal or dispositional profiles. Charge essentially disposes charged objects to attract or repel other charged objects; this essence grounds the laws governing charge. Properties could not have different dispositional profiles.

**Individual Essence (Haecceity)**
The property of being a particular individual—the "thisness" or haecceity that makes this individual the very individual it is, distinct from all qualitative properties. Debates about individual essences concern whether there is anything more to being this individual than having certain qualitative properties.

**Kind Essence**
The essence of a natural kind—what it is to be a member of that kind. For water, the kind essence is being H₂O; for electrons, it is having a specific charge and mass. Kind essences ground the necessary properties of kind members and the laws governing the kind.

**The Grounding-Essence Connection**
Fine's thesis that grounding facts are themselves grounded in essence: A grounds B because it is part of the essence of something that A grounds B. Essence is thus the most fundamental explanatory resource in metaphysics, underlying both modal and grounding facts.

**Mereological Essentialism**
Chisholm's thesis that material objects have their parts essentially—an object could not have been composed of different parts and remained the same object. This is a strong essentialist thesis with implications for personal identity and the metaphysics of ordinary objects.
