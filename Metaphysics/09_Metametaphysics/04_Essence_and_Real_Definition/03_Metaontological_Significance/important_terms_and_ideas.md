# Important Terms and Ideas: Metaontological Significance of Essence

**Real Definition as Metaphysical Method**
Fine's methodological thesis: metaphysics proceeds by identifying real definitions—accounts of what it is for something to be what it is. Real definitions are not merely verbal; they capture the essences of things and ground other facts about them.

**Conceptual Analysis and Essence**
The relationship between traditional conceptual analysis (identifying the necessary and sufficient conditions for a concept's application) and essentialist analysis. Some (Jackson, Thomasson) hold that conceptual analysis tracks essential truths; others (Fine) argue essence outruns what concepts determine.

**Metaphysical Methodology**
The question of how metaphysics should proceed: by conceptual analysis, by inference to the best explanation, by essentialist analysis, or by naturalistic investigation? The metaontological significance of essence bears on which method is most appropriate for different metaphysical questions.

**The Priority of Essence**
Fine's claim that essence is explanatorily and methodologically prior: to understand why certain truths hold necessarily, or why certain facts ground others, we must understand the essences of the relevant entities. Essence is the explanatory bedrock of metaphysics.

**Normative Essence**
The application of essentialist concepts to normative domains: what is it to be a right action, a good person, a just institution? Normative essentialism holds that these concepts have real essences that determine what falls under them necessarily, not merely by convention.

**Analysis and Revisability**
A methodological question: should metaphysical analyses be revised when they yield counterintuitive results, or should they override intuitions? The answer depends partly on whether analyses aim at capturing essential truths (not easily revisable) or merely at regimenting ordinary usage (more revisable).
