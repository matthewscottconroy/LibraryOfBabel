## Map 3: Sider's Argument for Structure

```
SIDER'S REALISM ABOUT STRUCTURE
              │
OBSERVATION: Some descriptions of the world
are more natural than others:
   "All electrons have negative charge" (highly natural)
   "All grue things are grue" (highly unnatural)
              │
              ▼
SIDER'S THESIS:
Naturalness is a mind-independent feature;
it does not depend on our interests or purposes
              │
              ▼
The most fundamental description of reality
uses only perfectly natural predicates — those
that carve reality at its metaphysical joints
              │
              ▼
TASK OF FUNDAMENTAL METAPHYSICS:
Determine which descriptions carve at the joints;
determine the correct ontology by asking what
joint-carving descriptions quantify over
              │
DEFLATIONIST OBJECTION:
"More natural" means "more useful for our purposes";
naturalness is not mind-independent but is a
function of human conceptual practices
              │
SIDER'S RESPONSE:
If naturalness is just pragmatic utility,
we cannot distinguish genuine scientific
discovery from mere conventionally useful
redescription; the realist intuition that
there is a "right" description of reality
requires mind-independent naturalness
```
