# References and Primary Sources: The Scientific Realism Debate

## Foundational Texts

- van Fraassen, Bas, *The Scientific Image* (1980). The foundational statement of constructive empiricism: science aims at empirical adequacy; acceptance of a theory involves belief only that it is empirically adequate, not that its unobservable posits exist.

- Putnam, Hilary, *Mathematics, Matter and Method* (1979), Chapter 18. Presents the no-miracles argument: scientific realism is "the only philosophy that doesn't make the success of science a miracle."

- Laudan, Larry, "A Confutation of Convergent Realism," *Philosophy of Science* 48 (1981). The pessimistic meta-induction: history of science shows successful theories regularly abandoned along with their central unobservable posits.

## Contemporary Works

- Worrall, John, "Structural Realism: The Best of Both Worlds?" *Dialectica* 43 (1989). Defends structural realism: we should be realist about the mathematical structure of theories but agnostic about the nature of unobservables.

- Psillos, Stathis, *Scientific Realism: How Science Tracks Truth* (1999). The most systematic contemporary defense of scientific realism, responding to constructive empiricism and the pessimistic meta-induction.
