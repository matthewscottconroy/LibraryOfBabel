# Review Questions — Scientific Realism

1. Scientific realism distinguishes a metaphysical thesis, a semantic thesis, and an epistemic thesis. Why does it matter that these three theses can come apart? Identify a position that accepts the semantic thesis while rejecting the epistemic one, and explain what work that combination does.

2. The no-miracles argument appeals to novel predictive success — success in domains the theory was not built to cover — rather than merely successful prediction in general. Why is the "novel" condition philosophically important, and what would the anti-realist have to say to make the success look less miraculous without it?

3. Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction draws on cases like phlogiston, caloric, and the luminiferous ether. Evaluate the selective realist response: does the distinction between posits that drive novel predictions and those that are theoretically idle successfully defuse the historical challenge, or does it prove too much?

4. Van Fraassen accepts that theoretical sentences like "electrons exist" are truth-apt, while instrumentalism treats theoretical vocabulary as eliminable. What is at stake in this difference? Does constructive empiricism give a more defensible anti-realism than instrumentalism, and what challenges does its observable/unobservable distinction face?

5. The strong programme in sociology of scientific knowledge endorses the symmetry principle — the same kinds of causes should explain both successful and unsuccessful scientific beliefs. What does the symmetry principle commit its proponents to, and how does the self-undermining objection expose a tension at the heart of strong social constructivism?

6. Epistemic structural realism and ontic structural realism agree that mathematical structure is preserved across theory change, but they differ on whether entities with intrinsic natures underlie that structure. What is the "no relations without relata" objection, and how does OSR's appeal to "thin" objects attempt to answer it? Is the response adequate?

7. The Newman problem threatens to trivialize epistemic structural realism by showing that any world with the right number of objects can be given the required structural properties. What additional resources — causal efficacy, physical significance — do structural realists invoke to make their knowledge claims substantive, and do these resources come at a theoretical cost for the purely structural ambition?

8. Ontic structural realism and the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics are described as natural allies. What metaphysical commitments do they share, and does this alliance represent a genuine convergence between philosophy of physics and metaphysics, or a case of two revisionary views reinforcing each other's weaknesses?
