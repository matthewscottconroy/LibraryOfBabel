# Important Figures: What Are Natural Kinds?

## John Stuart Mill (1806–1873)
Distinguished "real kinds" (natural kinds like gold and tigers) from mere nominal groupings; argued that real kinds have infinitely many properties in common, grounding non-trivial inductive inference. This is a precursor to contemporary natural kinds theory.

## Saul Kripke (1940–2022)
Argued that natural kind terms are rigid designators referring to kinds with essential underlying natures; "water" refers to H₂O in every world. His work revived essentialism about natural kinds and grounded a causal-historical semantics for kind terms.

## Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)
Developed the causal theory of reference for natural kind terms and the associated natural kind essentialism; argued that meaning is not in the head—the extension of "water" is fixed by the actual nature of the kind, not by our descriptions of it.

## Richard Boyd (born 1942)
Developed the homeostatic property cluster (HPC) account of natural kinds: natural kinds are clusters of properties that tend to co-occur because of underlying causal mechanisms, without requiring an essence shared by all members. This accommodates biological and social kinds.
