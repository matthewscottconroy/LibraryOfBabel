# Important Terms and Ideas: What Are Natural Kinds?

**Natural Kinds**
Categories that "carve nature at its joints"—that correspond to genuine divisions in nature rather than arbitrary or merely conventional groupings. Water, gold, and electrons are paradigm natural kinds; bachelor and red-haired-left-hander are not. Natural kinds ground inductive inference and support counterfactuals.

**Essential Nature of a Kind**
The underlying property or structure that constitutes membership in a natural kind. For water, the essential nature is H₂O; for gold, atomic number 79. Kripke and Putnam argued that science discovers these essential natures a posteriori, and they hold necessarily.

**Homeostatic Property Cluster (HPC)**
Boyd's account: a natural kind is characterized by a cluster of properties that tend to co-occur because they are held together by a causal homeostatic mechanism. There need be no single essence; what unifies the kind is the mechanism that maintains the cluster. HPC accommodates biological variation.

**Rigidity of Kind Terms**
Kripke's thesis that natural kind terms like "water" and "gold" are rigid designators—they refer to the same kind in every possible world. This means that natural kind identities like "water = H₂O" are necessary truths, known a posteriori.

**Superficial vs. Underlying Properties**
Natural kind essentialism distinguishes the superficial properties by which we identify a kind (water is clear, odorless, liquid at room temperature) from the underlying properties that constitute its essence (water is H₂O). The underlying properties are what really make something water.

**Promiscuous Realism**
Philip Kitcher's view: there is no single privileged set of natural kinds—multiple systems of classification can all be equally real depending on the explanatory interests at stake. This is a pluralism about natural kinds that resists any single "correct" taxonomy.
