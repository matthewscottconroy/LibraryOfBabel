# References and Primary Sources: What Are Natural Kinds?

## Foundational Texts

- Kripke, Saul, *Naming and Necessity* (1980). Argues that natural kind terms are rigid designators; natural kinds have essential underlying natures discovered by science; water is necessarily H₂O.

- Putnam, Hilary, "The Meaning of 'Meaning'," in *Mind, Language and Reality* (1975). Twin Earth thought experiment; meaning and reference for natural kind terms are determined by the actual nature of the kind, not by mental representations.

- Boyd, Richard, "Homeostasis, Species, and Higher Taxa," in *Species: New Interdisciplinary Essays* edited by Wilson (1999). Develops the HPC account of natural kinds; natural kinds are homeostatic property clusters maintained by underlying causal mechanisms.

## Contemporary Works

- Khalidi, Muhammad Ali, *Natural Categories and Human Kinds* (2013). A comprehensive treatment of natural kinds across the sciences, defending a pluralistic account that accommodates different kinds of kinds in different domains.

- Bird, Alexander and Tobin, Emma, "Natural Kinds," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2022). A thorough survey of the main accounts of natural kinds and the philosophical debates surrounding them.
