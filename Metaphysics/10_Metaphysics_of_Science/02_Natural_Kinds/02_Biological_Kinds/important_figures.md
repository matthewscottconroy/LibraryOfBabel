# Important Figures: Biological Kinds

## Ernst Mayr (1904–2005)
Developed the Biological Species Concept: a species is a group of actually or potentially interbreeding populations reproductively isolated from other groups. Mayr's work dominated biology throughout the twentieth century and remains the standard starting point for philosophical discussions.

## Philip Kitcher (born 1947)
Defended promiscuous realism about biological taxa: there is no single correct species concept; multiple classificatory systems track real biological structure depending on explanatory interests. This challenges essentialist accounts of biological kinds.

## Michael Ghiselin (born 1939)
Proposed that biological species are individuals, not kinds or classes: a species is a historical lineage with boundaries in space and time, not a set of organisms sharing essential properties. This has profound implications for natural kinds theory applied to biology.

## David Hull (1935–2010)
Extended Ghiselin's view that species are individuals and applied it more broadly; also contributed to philosophy of biology more generally, examining natural selection, fitness, and the nature of biological explanation.
