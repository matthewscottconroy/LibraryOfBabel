# Important Figures: Psychiatric and Social Kinds

## Ian Hacking (1936–2023)
Developed "dynamic nominalism" and the concept of "looping effects": human kinds interact with classifications in ways that physical kinds do not—classifying people as having a certain disorder can change their behavior and self-understanding, which changes the kind itself.

## Peter Zachar (born 1958)
A leading philosopher of psychiatry who developed the "practical kinds" approach: psychiatric categories are best understood as pragmatic tools for clinical and research purposes rather than natural kinds with essences, while still being more than mere social constructs.

## Jerome Wakefield (born 1950)
Developed the harmful dysfunction analysis of mental disorder: a condition is a genuine disorder iff it involves dysfunction (failure of a designed function) and is harmful. This provides a naturalistic account that distinguishes genuine disorders from problems in living.

## Sally Haslanger (born 1955)
Applied social ontology to kinds, arguing that social categories like race and gender are real social kinds (not merely nominal) but that their reality is grounded in social practices and power relations rather than natural facts.
