# Important Terms and Ideas: Psychiatric and Social Kinds

**Interactive Kinds**
Hacking's term: human kinds that "interact" with their classifications—the way a kind is categorized can change the behavior and self-understanding of those categorized, which in turn can change the kind. This contrasts with indifferent kinds (physical kinds whose instances are unaffected by classification).

**Looping Effects**
The feedback mechanism in interactive kinds: people classified under a kind are changed by the classification (they respond to it, internalize it, resist it), which changes the characteristics of people falling under the kind, which may lead to revisions of the classification itself.

**Harmful Dysfunction Analysis**
Wakefield's account of mental disorder: a condition is a disorder iff (1) it involves the failure of a biological or psychological mechanism to perform its evolutionary function (dysfunction), and (2) this failure causes harm to the individual. This captures both naturalistic and normative elements.

**Social Kinds**
Kinds whose existence and nature are partly constituted by social facts—beliefs, practices, institutions, power relations. Race, gender, marriage, and money are social kinds. The question is whether social kinds are "real" (tracking genuine features of the world) or merely conventional.

**Structural Account of Social Categories**
Haslanger's approach: categories like gender and race are defined not by intrinsic properties of individuals but by their structural positions in systems of social relations—who has power over whom, who is exploited, who is privileged. These are real social structures, not mere cultural fictions.

**Practical Kinds**
Zachar's account: psychiatric categories are practical kinds—categories defined by their utility for clinical practice and research, not by essential natures. They are more than mere social constructs (they track real regularities) but less than natural kinds with underlying essences.
