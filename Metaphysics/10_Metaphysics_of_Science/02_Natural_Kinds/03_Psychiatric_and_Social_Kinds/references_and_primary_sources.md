# References and Primary Sources: Psychiatric and Social Kinds

## Foundational Texts

- Hacking, Ian, "The Looping Effects of Human Kinds," in *Causal Cognition* edited by Sperber, Premack, and Premack (1995). Introduces the looping effect: human kind classifications interact with those classified in ways that physical kind classifications do not.

- Wakefield, Jerome, "The Concept of Mental Disorder," *American Psychologist* 47 (1992). The harmful dysfunction analysis: a mental disorder is a harmful condition that involves the failure of a mental mechanism to perform its designed function.

- Haslanger, Sally, "Gender and Race: (What) Are They? (What) Do We Want Them To Be?" *Noûs* 34 (2000). Argues for a structural account of gender and race as social positions defined by systematic power relations.

## Contemporary Works

- Cooper, Rachel, *Classifying Madness: A Philosophical Examination of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders* (2005). A philosophical critique of DSM categories, examining what makes psychiatric kinds real or conventional.

- Khalidi, Muhammad Ali, *Natural Categories and Human Kinds* (2013). Defends a pluralistic account that treats psychiatric and social kinds as a distinctive type of natural kind—real but constituted differently from physical or biological kinds.
