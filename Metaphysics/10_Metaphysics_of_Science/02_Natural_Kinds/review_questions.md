# Review Questions — Natural Kinds

1. Natural kinds are said to support inductive projection, underwrite laws of nature, and ground explanation. Explain how these three roles are related. Could a grouping do one of these jobs without doing the others, and what would that show about the unity of the natural kinds concept?

2. Kripke-Putnam essentialism holds that "water is H₂O" is a necessary a posteriori truth. What exactly is a posteriori necessity supposed to mean, and how does the Twin Earth thought experiment establish that natural kind terms are rigid designators rather than descriptions? What challenge does the underdetermination of essence pose for identifying which structural features count as essential?

3. The homeostatic property cluster account was developed partly to handle biological kinds that resist essentialist treatment. What role does the homeostatic mechanism play — is it a substitute for an essence or a form of one? Consider Chakravartty's claim that HPC theory is best understood as moderate essentialism, and decide whether the difference between HPC and strict essentialism is one of kind or degree.

4. Dupré's promiscuous realism argues that there are too many equally legitimate classificatory schemes for nature to carve at a unique set of joints. How serious is this challenge for the realist about natural kinds? Does the promiscuity problem show that the concept of a natural kind is incoherent, or only that a specific metaphysical account of it is false?

5. Ghiselin and Hull argue that species are individuals rather than kinds, because they originate, evolve, and go extinct — things that kinds cannot do. What evidence from the pattern of biological speech do they use, and what is lost explanatorily if we accept that "tigers are carnivores" is a historical claim about an individual rather than a law about a kind? How does this bear on whether biology has genuine natural laws?

6. The gene concept appears to be multiple, with molecular, functional, informational, and Mendelian versions doing different theoretical work. Does the plurality of gene concepts show that "gene" is like "jade" — picking out heterogeneous things — rather than like "gold"? What implications does this have for the debate over the reduction of classical genetics to molecular biology?

7. Psychiatric kinds face both a validity challenge (do the diagnostic categories track real biological conditions?) and Hacking's "looping effect" (psychiatric categories reshape the very conditions they classify). How does the HPC account handle these features, and does the looping effect show that psychiatric kinds are fundamentally different from chemical or biological kinds?

8. Race and gender illustrate what the chapter calls the "descriptive-normative entanglement" of social kinds. What does this entanglement consist in, and what does it show about the relationship between the metaphysics of social kinds and questions about how those kinds should be reformed or abolished? Connect this to the earlier discussion of whether natural kinds require mind-independence.
