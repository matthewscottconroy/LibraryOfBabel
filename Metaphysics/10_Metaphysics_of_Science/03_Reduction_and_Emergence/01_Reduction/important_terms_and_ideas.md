# Important Terms and Ideas: Reduction

**Nagelian Reduction**
The classical model: a theory T₂ (e.g., thermodynamics) is reduced to T₁ (e.g., statistical mechanics) by deriving the laws of T₂ from the laws of T₁ together with bridge principles connecting the predicates of T₂ to those of T₁.

**Bridge Principles (Bridge Laws)**
Statements that connect the vocabulary of the reduced theory to the vocabulary of the reducing theory. For Nagelian reduction, bridge principles are required whenever the theories use different concepts (e.g., connecting "temperature" to "mean kinetic energy of molecules").

**Eliminative Reduction**
A reduction in which the higher-level categories are ultimately replaced rather than preserved. Phlogiston was not reduced to oxygen; it was eliminated. Eliminativists about folk psychology argue that beliefs and desires will be eliminated (not reduced) by mature neuroscience.

**Ontological vs. Epistemological Reduction**
Ontological reduction: higher-level entities just are lower-level entities (water is H₂O). Epistemological reduction: knowledge of higher-level facts is derivable from knowledge of lower-level facts. These can come apart: moral facts may be epistemologically irreducible even if ontologically grounded in natural facts.

**Unity of Science**
The program of unifying the sciences through reduction: physics is fundamental; chemistry reduces to physics; biology reduces to chemistry; psychology reduces to biology; etc. The unity of science thesis is associated with logical positivism and the Oppenheim-Putnam hierarchy.

**Multiple Realizability as Anti-Reductionist**
Putnam's argument: if mental state types (pain) are realizable by multiple physical types (C-fibers in humans, other structures in octopuses), then there is no single physical type that corresponds to pain—type reduction fails. This motivated non-reductive physicalism and functionalism.
