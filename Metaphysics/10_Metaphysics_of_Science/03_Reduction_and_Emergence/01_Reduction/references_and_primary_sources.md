# References and Primary Sources: Reduction

## Foundational Texts

- Nagel, Ernest, *The Structure of Science* (1961), Chapter 11. The classical treatment of reduction: a theory T₂ is reduced to T₁ when T₂'s laws can be derived from T₁'s laws plus bridge principles.

- Oppenheim, Paul and Putnam, Hilary, "Unity of Science as a Working Hypothesis," *Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 2 (1958). The unity of science program: a hierarchical reduction from physics through progressively more complex sciences.

- Kim, Jaegwon, *Mind in a Physical World* (1998). Argues that without reduction, mental properties cannot have genuine causal efficacy; non-reductive physicalism is unstable.

## Contemporary Works

- Schaffner, Kenneth, "Approaches to Reduction," *Philosophy of Science* 34 (1967). Refines the Nagelian model by allowing for "analog" (approximate) reductions and theory modification in the reducing theory.

- Bickle, John, *Philosophy and Neuroscience: A Ruthlessly Reductive Account* (2003). A defense of "ruthless reductionism" that takes seriously the prospects for reducing psychology directly to cellular and molecular neuroscience.
