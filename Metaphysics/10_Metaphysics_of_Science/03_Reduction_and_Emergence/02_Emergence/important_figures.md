# Important Figures: Emergence

## C.D. Broad (1887–1971)
Distinguished emergent from resultant properties in *The Mind and Its Place in Nature* (1925): emergent properties of a whole cannot be predicted from complete knowledge of the properties of its parts and the laws governing them. This is the canonical statement of strong emergence.

## Philip Anderson (1923–2020)
Nobel Prize-winning physicist who argued in "More Is Different" (1972) that emergent phenomena at each level of organization require their own explanatory principles—higher-level phenomena are not simply derivable from lower-level physics. A scientific perspective on strong emergence.

## David Chalmers (born 1966)
Applied emergence to consciousness: phenomenal consciousness may be an example of strong emergence—a property that arises from but is not deducible from or reducible to physical properties. His work connects metaphysical emergence to the hard problem of consciousness.

## Mark Bedau (born 1956)
Distinguished weak from strong emergence: weakly emergent properties can in principle be derived from lower-level properties (even if computationally intractable in practice); strongly emergent properties cannot be derived at all. Bedau defends weak emergence as scientifically respectable.
