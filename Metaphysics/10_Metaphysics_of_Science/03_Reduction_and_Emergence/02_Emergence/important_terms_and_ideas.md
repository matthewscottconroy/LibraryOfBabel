# Important Terms and Ideas: Emergence

**Weak Emergence**
A property is weakly emergent if it arises from lower-level properties and in principle could be derived from complete knowledge of those properties and their interactions, but in practice cannot be easily computed or predicted. Most cases of emergence in complex systems science are weak.

**Strong Emergence**
A property is strongly emergent if it genuinely cannot be derived from lower-level properties and laws, even given complete knowledge. Strong emergence would require new fundamental laws at the higher level—laws not deducible from lower-level physics. Consciousness is a candidate for strong emergence.

**Resultant vs. Emergent Properties**
Broad's distinction: resultant properties are deducible from the properties of parts (e.g., mass of a compound = sum of masses of parts); emergent properties are not (e.g., liquidity, consciousness). The distinction marks where lower-level explanation fails.

**Supervenience-Based Emergence**
Properties that are determined by lower-level properties (supervene on them) yet are not reducible to them. This is the standard "non-reductive physicalist" notion of emergence: higher-level properties are real, supervene on physical properties, but are explanatorily irreducible.

**Epistemic vs. Ontological Emergence**
Epistemic emergence: we cannot (even in principle) predict or derive higher-level properties from lower-level descriptions given our computational or conceptual limitations. Ontological emergence: higher-level properties are genuinely metaphysically irreducible to lower-level properties.

**Emergence and Causal Autonomy**
Whether emergent properties have genuine causal powers of their own (top-down causation) or whether all causation is ultimately bottom-up (downward causation is apparent, not real). This question connects emergence to the mental causation debate.
