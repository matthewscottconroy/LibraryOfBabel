# References and Primary Sources: Emergence

## Foundational Texts

- Broad, C.D., *The Mind and Its Place in Nature* (1925), Chapter 2. Distinguishes emergent from resultant properties; emergent properties require special emergent laws not derivable from the laws governing the parts alone.

- Anderson, Philip, "More Is Different," *Science* 177 (1972). The scientific case for emergence: as complexity increases, new laws and concepts become necessary; physics alone does not explain higher-level phenomena.

- Bedau, Mark, "Weak Emergence," *Philosophical Perspectives* 11 (1997). Distinguishes weak (epistemologically emergent) from strong (ontologically emergent) properties; argues that weak emergence is real and important.

## Contemporary Works

- Chalmers, David, "Strong and Weak Emergence," in *The Re-Emergence of Emergence* edited by Clayton and Davies (2006). Applies the weak/strong distinction to philosophy of mind; argues consciousness may be a genuine case of strong emergence.

- Humphreys, Paul, *Emergence: A Philosophical Account* (2016). A systematic philosophical treatment of emergence, distinguishing types and examining scientific case studies.
