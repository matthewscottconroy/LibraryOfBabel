# Important Figures: Levels of Nature

## Carl Hempel (1905–1997)
Contributed to the hierarchical view of sciences and their explanation, developing the deductive-nomological model of explanation that suggests science is organized into levels where higher-level explanations are derivable from lower-level ones.

## Herbert Simon (1916–2001)
Analyzed the hierarchical structure of complex systems in "The Architecture of Complexity" (1962); argued that nearly decomposable systems—with strong interactions within subsystems and weak interactions between subsystems—are ubiquitous and generate level structure.

## David Marr (1945–1980)
Proposed a three-level analysis of computational systems (computational, algorithmic, implementational) that has been influential in cognitive science and in discussions of levels of description in the sciences more broadly.

## William Wimsatt (born 1941)
The most systematic philosopher to examine levels of organization in biology and the special sciences; argued that levels are real but epistemically motivated rather than sharp divisions in nature; examined heuristics and their failures in reductive research.
