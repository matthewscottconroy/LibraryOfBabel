# Important Terms and Ideas: Levels of Nature

**Levels of Organization**
The hierarchical structure of the natural world: atoms, molecules, cells, organisms, ecosystems. Each level has its own characteristic entities, properties, and regularities. Levels are typically defined by containment relations and characteristic interaction strengths within vs. across levels.

**Mereological Hierarchy**
The nested structure of part-whole relations: quarks are parts of nucleons, nucleons of nuclei, nuclei of atoms, atoms of molecules, etc. Levels of organization correspond roughly to levels in the mereological hierarchy, though the correspondence is not perfect.

**Near Decomposability**
Simon's concept: a system is nearly decomposable if interactions within subsystems are much stronger than interactions between subsystems. Near decomposability supports level structure by allowing subsystems to be analyzed semi-independently.

**Interlevel Relations**
The relations between different levels: realization (lower-level entities realize higher-level properties), constitution (lower-level entities constitute higher-level ones), supervenience (higher-level properties supervene on lower-level ones), and grounding. These relations are central to understanding how levels are connected.

**The Reality of Levels**
A contested question: are levels genuine features of mind-independent reality, or are they artifacts of our ways of describing and investigating nature? Realists about levels hold they are objective; deflationists hold they are methodological conveniences.

**Level-Relative Explanation**
The idea that different levels of organization require different explanatory strategies and concepts. What counts as an adequate explanation at the cellular level (in terms of membrane potentials, ion channels) differs from what counts as an adequate explanation at the cognitive level (in terms of beliefs, desires, computations).
