# References and Primary Sources: Levels of Nature

## Foundational Texts

- Simon, Herbert, "The Architecture of Complexity," *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society* 106 (1962). Argues that complex systems are hierarchically organized; near decomposability at each level explains why level structure is ubiquitous in nature.

- Wimsatt, William, "Reductive Explanation: A Functional Account," *Boston Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 32 (1976). Examines how reductive explanations work in practice, with attention to levels of organization and their boundaries.

- Oppenheim, Paul and Putnam, Hilary, "Unity of Science as a Working Hypothesis," *Minnesota Studies in the Philosophy of Science* 2 (1958). The founding text of the hierarchical levels view in philosophy of science.

## Contemporary Works

- Eronen, Markus, "Levels of Organization: A Deflationary Account," *Biology and Philosophy* 30 (2015). Argues that levels are not fundamental features of reality but methodological tools; deflates metaphysical claims about levels.

- Craver, Carl and Bechtel, William, "Top-Down Causation Without Top-Down Causes," *Biology and Philosophy* 22 (2007). Examines what top-down causation amounts to at different levels of biological organization.
