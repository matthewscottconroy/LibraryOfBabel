# Important Figures: Quantum Mechanics and Philosophy

## Niels Bohr (1885–1962)
Developed the Copenhagen interpretation of quantum mechanics: quantum states describe measurement outcomes, not mind-independent reality; the observer and measuring apparatus play an ineliminable role. Bohr's anti-realist interpretation dominated physics for decades.

## Hugh Everett III (1930–1982)
Proposed the many-worlds (relative state) interpretation of quantum mechanics in 1957: the wave function never collapses; all outcomes occur in different branches of a universal wave function. This is the most discussed alternative to Copenhagen.

## John Bell (1928–1990)
Derived Bell's theorem (1964): any local hidden variable theory must satisfy certain inequalities; quantum mechanics violates them. Bell's theorem established that quantum mechanics is incompatible with local realism, transforming foundations of quantum mechanics.

## David Bohm (1917–1992)
Developed the pilot wave (Bohmian mechanics) interpretation: particles have definite positions guided by a pilot wave; quantum mechanics is a deterministic theory with hidden variables. Bohm's theory shows that quantum mechanics can be reinterpreted in a realist, deterministic framework.
