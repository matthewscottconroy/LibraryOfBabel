# Important Terms and Ideas: Quantum Mechanics and Philosophy

**Wave Function**
The mathematical object (a vector in Hilbert space) that represents the quantum state of a system. The wave function encodes the probabilities of different measurement outcomes and evolves according to the Schrödinger equation. The question of what the wave function represents (a real physical field, a probability distribution, an epistemic state) is central to foundations.

**Wave Function Collapse**
On the Copenhagen and related interpretations, when a quantum measurement is made, the wave function "collapses" from a superposition to a definite eigenstate corresponding to the observed outcome. The physical mechanism of collapse (if any) is the measurement problem.

**The Measurement Problem**
The problem of explaining how definite measurement outcomes arise from quantum systems that are in superpositions before measurement. The Schrödinger equation is linear and deterministic and does not produce collapses; something additional must explain why we observe definite outcomes.

**Entanglement**
A quantum correlation between particles such that measuring one particle instantaneously affects the state of the other, regardless of distance. Entangled particles are described by a non-separable wave function; this is the phenomenon behind Bell's theorem and EPR.

**Bell's Theorem**
The result that the predictions of quantum mechanics are incompatible with any local hidden variable theory: no theory that is both local (no influence faster than light) and realist (particles have definite properties before measurement) can reproduce quantum predictions. Experimental violations of Bell inequalities confirm quantum mechanics.

**Many-Worlds Interpretation**
Everett's interpretation: there is no wave function collapse; when a measurement is made, the universe branches into multiple worlds, one for each possible outcome. All outcomes occur; we find ourselves in one branch. This preserves unitarity and eliminates collapse but multiplies ontology enormously.
