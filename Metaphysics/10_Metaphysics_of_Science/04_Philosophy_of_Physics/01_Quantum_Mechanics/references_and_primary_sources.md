# References and Primary Sources: Quantum Mechanics and Philosophy

## Foundational Texts

- Bohr, Niels, "Can Quantum-Mechanical Description of Physical Reality Be Considered Complete?" *Physical Review* 48 (1935). Bohr's response to Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen, presenting the Copenhagen view that quantum mechanics gives a complete description of physical reality as it appears to observers.

- Bell, John, "On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen Paradox," *Physics* 1 (1964). Derives Bell inequalities; establishes that any local hidden variable theory disagrees with quantum mechanical predictions. Published in *Speakable and Unspeakable in Quantum Mechanics* (1987).

- Everett, Hugh, "'Relative State' Formulation of Quantum Mechanics," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 29 (1957). The original many-worlds interpretation: all measurement outcomes occur in different branches of the universal wave function.

## Contemporary Works

- Maudlin, Tim, *Quantum Non-Locality and Relativity* (1994). Examines the metaphysical implications of Bell's theorem for locality and relativity; argues that quantum mechanics requires some form of non-local connection.

- Wallace, David, *The Emergent Multiverse* (2012). The most systematic philosophical defense of the many-worlds interpretation, addressing the probability problem and the emergence of classical reality.
