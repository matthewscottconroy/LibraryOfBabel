# Important Terms and Ideas: Thermodynamics and Philosophy

**Entropy**
A measure of disorder or the number of microstates compatible with a given macrostate (Boltzmann: S = k log W). The second law states that entropy tends to increase in isolated systems. The philosophical challenge is explaining why entropy increases toward the future but not the past, given time-symmetric underlying physics.

**Second Law of Thermodynamics**
The principle that in an isolated system, entropy never decreases over time—it tends to increase or remain constant. This is the basis of the thermodynamic arrow of time. The law is statistical: violations are possible but overwhelmingly improbable.

**Maxwell's Demon**
A thought experiment: a tiny demon controls a gate between two gas chambers, allowing fast molecules through in one direction and slow molecules in the other, apparently decreasing entropy without doing work and violating the second law. Resolution: information erasure (Landauer's principle) involves entropy increase that compensates.

**Landauer's Principle**
The principle that erasing one bit of information requires dissipating a minimum of kT ln 2 of energy as heat, increasing entropy. This resolves Maxwell's demon: the demon's memory must be erased, increasing entropy and preserving the second law.

**Statistical Mechanics**
The theoretical framework that grounds thermodynamics in the mechanics of atoms and molecules; explains macroscopic thermodynamic behavior in terms of the statistical properties of microscopic states. The challenge is explaining temporal asymmetry given that the underlying mechanics is time-symmetric.

**Boltzmann's H-Theorem and Objections**
Boltzmann proved the H-theorem—that H (a statistical quantity related to entropy) must decrease over time—using time-symmetric mechanics. The reversibility objection (Loschmidt): for every entropy-increasing evolution there is a reversed, entropy-decreasing one. The recurrence objection (Zermelo): by the Poincaré recurrence theorem, any system will return close to its initial state, so entropy cannot increase indefinitely.
