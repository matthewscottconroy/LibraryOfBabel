# Review Questions — Philosophy of Physics

1. The measurement problem can be stated as an inconsistency among five assumptions: completeness, linearity, definiteness, the Born rule, and quantum universality. Each major interpretation resolves the inconsistency by rejecting one. Evaluate this structure: does the consistency proof show that quantum mechanics forces a choice among the interpretations, or only that some adjustment somewhere is required? Which assumption seems the most defensible to sacrifice, and why?

2. The many-worlds interpretation is ontologically austere at the fundamental level — only the wave function and the Schrödinger equation — while being ontologically extravagant in its consequences: an enormous proliferation of branches. Evaluate the probability problem. If all outcomes are real in some branch, what is the content of the Born rule? Is the decision-theoretic derivation of Born rule probabilities genuine, or does it presuppose what it purports to derive?

3. Bohmian mechanics is empirically equivalent to standard quantum mechanics, yet it offers a radically different ontological picture — deterministic, particle-based, with a nonlocal pilot wave. What does this empirical equivalence show about the limits of scientific realism? If two theories with mutually incompatible ontologies are observationally indistinguishable, how should a realist respond?

4. Bell's theorem establishes that local realism is incompatible with quantum mechanical predictions, and experiments have confirmed the violations. Different interpretations respond by sacrificing either locality (Bohmian mechanics) or definite pre-measurement properties (Copenhagen and many-worlds). What metaphysical picture of causation is forced by these results — nonlocal causation, retrocausation, or no causation at all for quantum correlations — and how does this bear on mereological principles like separability?

5. GRW and CSL are objective collapse theories that make empirically testable predictions differing from standard quantum mechanics. Copenhagen and QBism do not. Does this experimental testability give objective collapse theories a scientific advantage, or does it expose them to empirical refutation in a way that more instrumentally cautious interpretations avoid? What does this comparison reveal about the relationship between realism and testability in quantum foundations?

6. Boltzmann's statistical entropy is defined relative to a macrostate, which requires a coarse-graining of phase space. Does this make entropy observer-dependent, or is the relevant coarse-graining fixed by the physical capacities of macroscopic agents? Assess the Jaynes Bayesian interpretation: if the probabilities of statistical mechanics represent an agent's epistemic state, does thermodynamics become a subjective theory, and what are the costs of that conclusion?

7. The Past Hypothesis says the universe began in an extremely low-entropy macrostate, and this initial condition grounds the thermodynamic arrow of time, the epistemic arrow (memory), and the causal arrow. Evaluate this explanation: is the Past Hypothesis a genuine scientific explanation of temporal asymmetry, or does it merely relocate the puzzle from a local to a cosmological level? What would it mean to explain the Past Hypothesis itself?

8. Quantum entanglement violates mereological separability — the joint quantum state of two entangled particles is not determined by any combination of single-particle states. How does this support Schaffer's priority monism over the conventional part-prior picture of composition? Does the quantum mechanical case for holism connect to the discussion of emergence within physics (universality, renormalization), or do the two phenomena support different and potentially competing metaphysical conclusions?
