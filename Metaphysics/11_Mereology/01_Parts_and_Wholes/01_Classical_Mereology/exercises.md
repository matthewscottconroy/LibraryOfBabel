# Exercises: Classical Mereology

1. **Axiom check.** Classical mereology defines overlap as: *O(x, y)* iff ∃z(P(z, x) ∧ P(z, y)). Using only the three core axioms (reflexivity, antisymmetry, transitivity), prove that overlap is reflexive and symmetric but not transitive. Provide a concrete example showing that transitivity of overlap can fail.

2. **Unrestricted fusion.** Universalism says the fusion of your left hand and the planet Mars exists. Is this genuinely troubling? Write a short argument *for* its existence and a short argument *against*. Which do you find more persuasive, and why?

3. **Extensionality and coincidence.** A sculptor casts a statue from bronze. The statue and the lump of bronze seem to be composed of exactly the same atoms. The extensionality principle says they are therefore identical. But the lump was here before the casting. Does this temporal asymmetry count as a difference in parts? Evaluate whether a four-dimensionalist response adequately defends extensionality.

4. **Atomism vs. gunk.** Is gunk metaphysically possible? Construct the best argument you can that it is, and the best argument that it isn't. What would a gunky world imply for the notion of an intrinsic property?

5. **Leśniewski's nominalism.** Leśniewski wanted to avoid set theory by replacing sets with mereological sums. Can a mereological sum do the work of a set? Consider: {a, b} and {a, b, a∪b} are different sets, but their mereological sums seem to be the same object. Does this undermine the reduction? Explain.

6. **Reflection.** Is classical mereology a theory about how the world actually is, or merely a formal framework for modeling part-whole reasoning? What would it take for empirical evidence to be relevant to the choice of mereological axioms?
