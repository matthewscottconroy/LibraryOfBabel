# Further Reading: Classical Mereology

**Primary texts:**
- Leśniewski, S. (1916/1992). *Foundations of the General Theory of Sets*. In *Collected Works*, vol. 1. Kluwer.
- Leonard, H. S. & Goodman, N. (1940). "The Calculus of Individuals and Its Uses." *Journal of Symbolic Logic*, 5(2), 45–55.
- Lewis, D. K. (1991). *Parts of Classes*. Blackwell. [Appendix A is indispensable for the formal axioms.]

**Secondary and survey literature:**
- Simons, P. (1987). *Parts: A Study in Ontology*. Oxford University Press. [The standard reference.]
- Varzi, A. C. (2016). "Mereology." *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*. [Excellent comprehensive survey.]
- Hovda, P. (2009). "What is Classical Mereology?" *Journal of Philosophical Logic*, 38, 55–82. [Technical but thorough.]

**On unrestricted composition:**
- Lewis, D. K. (1986). *On the Plurality of Worlds*. Blackwell. [Section 4.3 defends universalism.]
- Sider, T. (2001). *Four-Dimensionalism*. Oxford University Press. [Chapter 4 gives the vagueness argument.]

**On extensionality and coincidence:**
- Wiggins, D. (1980). *Sameness and Substance*. Harvard University Press.
- Burke, M. B. (1994). "Preserving the Principle of One Object to a Place." *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research*, 54(3), 591–624.
