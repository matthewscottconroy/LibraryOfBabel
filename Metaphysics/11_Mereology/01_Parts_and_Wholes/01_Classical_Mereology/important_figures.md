# Important Figures in Classical Mereology

**Stanisław Leśniewski (1886–1939)** — Polish logician and founder of mereology as a formal discipline. Developed the system as a set-free foundation for mathematics, taking the concrete part-whole relation as more basic than abstract membership.

**Henry S. Leonard (1905–1967) and Nelson Goodman (1906–1998)** — Co-authored "The Calculus of Individuals" (1940), introducing mereology to anglophone philosophy and deploying it in Goodman's nominalist program to reconstruct knowledge using only individuals, not sets or universals.

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Major defender of mereological universalism. His *Parts of Classes* (1991) used mereological sums to give a reduction of set theory to mereology plus a singleton function, and his work throughout the 1980s–90s made mereology central to mainstream analytic metaphysics.

**Alfred Tarski (1901–1983)** — Provided the first rigorous axiomatization of mereology in the spirit of Leśniewski, clarifying its algebraic structure and connections to Boolean algebra minus a zero element.

**Peter Simons** — Author of *Parts: A Study in Ontology* (1987), the most comprehensive philosophical treatment of mereology in English, surveying classical systems and arguing for a more flexible account that permits coincident objects.

**Achille Varzi** — Contemporary authority on mereology and topology; has written extensively on boundary theory, vagueness in parthood, and the relationship between mereology and logic.
