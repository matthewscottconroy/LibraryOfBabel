# Important Terms and Ideas: Classical Mereology

**Part-Whole Relation**
The fundamental relation of mereology: x is a part of y. The part-whole relation is reflexive (everything is a part of itself), antisymmetric (if x is a part of y and y is a part of x, then x = y), and transitive (if x is a part of y and y is a part of z, then x is a part of z).

**Proper Part**
x is a proper part of y if x is a part of y and x ≠ y. Proper parthood is irreflexive and transitive. This excludes the trivial case where a thing is a "part" of itself.

**Overlap**
Two objects overlap if they share a common part. Disjointness is the absence of overlap: two objects are disjoint if they share no common part. These concepts are central to mereological sum and product operations.

**Mereological Sum (Fusion)**
The mereological sum of a collection of objects is the unique object that overlaps exactly those objects that overlap at least one member of the collection. Classical mereology (CEM) is unrestricted in permitting sums of arbitrary collections—even scattered, bizarre ones.

**Classical Extensional Mereology (CEM)**
The standard formal system of mereology: the part-whole relation satisfies reflexivity, antisymmetry, and transitivity; supplementation principles require that proper parts are always supplemented; unrestricted fusion allows arbitrary sums. CEM is the most common formal system for mereological investigation.

**Supplementation Principles**
Principles that require objects to have "supplementing" parts: Weak Supplementation states that if x is a proper part of y, there is some part of y that is disjoint from x. Strong Supplementation states that if x is not a part of y, then some part of x is disjoint from y. These prevent objects from having only one proper part.
