# References and Primary Sources: Classical Mereology

## Foundational Texts

- Leśniewski, Stanisław, "On the Foundations of Mathematics," *Przegląd Filozoficzny* (1927–31). The founding work of formal mereology: a rigorous axiomatization of the part-whole relation intended as an alternative to set theory, avoiding the paradoxes while grounding mathematics.

- Leonard, Henry and Goodman, Nelson, "The Calculus of Individuals and Its Uses," *Journal of Symbolic Logic* 5 (1940). Introduces the calculus of individuals to analytic philosophy; provides a mereological framework for nominalist ontology without sets.

- Lewis, David, *Parts of Classes* (1991). Applies mereology to set theory; argues that singletons are the "mereological atoms" of the set-theoretic universe and explores the relationship between mereology, plural quantification, and set theory.

## Contemporary Works

- Simons, Peter, *Parts: A Study in Ontology* (1987). The most systematic contemporary philosophical treatment of mereology; surveys the main axiom systems, defends supplementation principles, and applies mereology to events, processes, and abstract objects.

- Varzi, Achille, "Mereology," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2019). A comprehensive survey of classical and non-classical mereologies, their axioms, and their philosophical applications.
