# Exercises: Composition

1. **Van Inwagen's SCQ.** State the Special Composition Question precisely. Why does van Inwagen call it "special" rather than "general"? Explain the three main answers (universalism, nihilism, restricted) in your own words, giving one advantage and one disadvantage of each.

2. **Organicism.** Van Inwagen says tables don't exist but people do. Evaluate this claim. Is the difference between organisms and artifacts sharp enough to bear this metaphysical weight? What about coral reefs, crystals, or social insect colonies?

3. **Nihilism and persons.** If mereological nihilism is true, do persons exist? Assess the paraphrase strategy: can claims about persons be systematically replaced with claims about "simples arranged person-wise" without loss of content?

4. **Sider's vagueness argument.** Reconstruct Sider's argument from vagueness against restricted composition. Is the premise that "existence cannot be vague" defensible? Consider whether a supervaluationist approach to vagueness might undercut it.

5. **Hirsch's verbal dispute thesis.** Eli Hirsch argues that disagreements about composition are merely verbal. What would make a metaphysical dispute "merely verbal"? Is the dispute between van Inwagen and the universalist genuinely verbal? Give reasons.

6. **Contact theory.** Design the best possible contact theory of composition and then apply van Inwagen's objections to it. Do any of his objections fail to land? What revision, if any, could save a contact-based view?
