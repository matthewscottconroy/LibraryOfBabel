# Important Figures: Composition

**Peter van Inwagen (b. 1942)** — Posed the Special Composition Question in *Material Beings* (1990) and defended organicism—the view that only living organisms are genuine composite objects. Probably the single most influential figure in recent mereology debates.

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Defended mereological universalism as a byproduct of his modal realism; argued that fusions are "ontologically innocent."

**Theodore Sider (b. 1967)** — Extended the case for universalism via the argument from vagueness in *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001); also explored nihilism and the associated paraphrase strategies.

**Cian Dorr** — Developed a sophisticated form of mereological nihilism and argued for it on grounds of ontological parsimony.

**Peter Unger (b. 1942)** — Early articulator of the "problem of the many" and a precursor to nihilist themes in his paper "I Do Not Exist" (1979).

**Eli Hirsch** — Has argued that disputes about composition are merely verbal—a matter of different linguistic conventions rather than genuine metaphysical disagreement.

**Amie Thomasson** — Defends "easy ontology," the view that existence questions can often be settled by conceptual analysis; argues that ordinary composite objects straightforwardly exist.
