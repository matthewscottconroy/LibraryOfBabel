# References and Primary Sources: Composition

## Foundational Texts

- van Inwagen, Peter, *Material Beings* (1990). Poses the Special Composition Question (SCQ): under what conditions do objects compose a further object? Defends the answer that only living organisms compose; rejects universal composition and arbitrary sums.

- Lewis, David, *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986), Section 4.3. Defends unrestricted composition: for any objects, there exists their mereological sum. Lewis's argument appeals to the vagueness argument and the ontological innocence of mereological sums.

- Sider, Theodore, *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001), Chapter 5. Defends unrestricted composition using the vagueness argument: if composition is restricted, the boundary between composing and non-composing collections must be sharp or vague; sharp cutoffs are implausible; vagueness in composition facts is also implausible.

## Contemporary Works

- Korman, Daniel, *Objects: Nothing Out of the Ordinary* (2015). Defends ordinary objects (tables, chairs, persons) and their composition against revisionary ontologies that deny or radically restrict composition.

- Thomasson, Amie, *Ordinary Objects* (2007). Argues that ordinary objects exist without controversy; the composition question for them is answered by the conceptual conditions governing our ordinary terms.
