# Exercises: The Constitution Relation

1. **Constitution vs. identity.** Baker claims that the statue and the lump are constitutionally related but not identical. State the argument for their distinctness (using Leibniz's Law) as precisely as you can. What is the most promising reply from the mereological extensionalist?

2. **Temporal constitution.** A river flows constantly, with different water molecules making it up at different times. Is the river constituted by a different mass of water at each moment? What makes it the *same* river over time on a constitution view? Compare this with a four-dimensionalist account.

3. **The too-many-thinkers problem.** If a person and the organism that constitutes them are distinct colocated entities, then there appear to be two thinkers in your skull. How serious is this objection? Does Baker's constitution view have an adequate response?

4. **Kinds and properties.** Baker holds that kinds are metaphysically real and determine modal properties. Critically evaluate this claim. Is it plausible that belonging to the kind *artwork* could make the statue genuinely modally fragile in a way that the lump is not?

5. **Fine's qua-objects.** Kit Fine argues that we should individuate objects *qua* their kind (the statue qua artwork, the lump qua material). How does this approach handle the coincidence problem differently from Baker's? What are its advantages and disadvantages?

6. **The Ship of Theseus.** Reconstruct this puzzle in terms of constitution theory and in terms of classical mereology. Does constitution theory handle it better? Does the "rebuilt original ship" case pose a special problem?
