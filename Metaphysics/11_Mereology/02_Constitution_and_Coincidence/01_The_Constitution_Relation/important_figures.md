# Important Figures: The Constitution Relation

**Lynne Rudder Baker (1944–2017)** — The most systematic defender of constitution as a *sui generis* relation. Her book *Persons and Bodies* (2000) and *The Metaphysics of Everyday Life* (2007) work out a comprehensive "constitution view" applied to persons, artifacts, and organisms.

**Kit Fine (b. 1946)** — Developed the concept of *qua*-objects and argued for relative parthood and relativized identity conditions, offering an alternative framework for handling coincidence that does not require multiplying entities.

**Judith Jarvis Thomson (1929–2020)** — Worked extensively on personal identity and coincidence; her paper "The Statue and the Clay" (1998) gave one of the clearest statements of the coincidence puzzle.

**Michael Rea** — Has written on material constitution from a broadly conservative, metaphysically robust perspective, defending the view that constitution involves genuinely distinct entities.

**Mark Johnston** — Argued against "the many" and defended a form of the constitution view while being skeptical of four-dimensionalist solutions; his work emphasizes ordinary sortal concepts as metaphysically significant.
