# Important Terms and Ideas: The Constitution Relation

**Constitution**
The relation between two objects that share the same matter at a time without being identical. The clay constitutes the statue; the person constitutes (or is constituted by) the body. Constitution is distinct from identity: the constituted object and the constituting object differ in some properties and modal profiles.

**Material Constitution**
The specific form of constitution involving material objects: a material object M constitutes object O at time t when M and O share all their matter at t, but O and M belong to different kinds and have different persistence conditions.

**Constitution vs. Identity**
The debate about whether a statue and the clay that makes it up are numerically identical (one thing) or numerically distinct (two things constituting each other). Identity theorists appeal to parsimony; constitution theorists appeal to the different modal and sortal properties of statues and lumps of clay.

**Asymmetric Dependence**
A feature of constitution: the constituted object depends on the constituting matter, but not vice versa. The statue depends on the clay for its existence; the clay does not depend on the statue. This asymmetry distinguishes constitution from a symmetric coincidence relation.

**The Individuation Problem**
If a statue and its clay are distinct objects that share all their matter, there seem to be two objects occupying exactly the same space at the same time. This raises questions about how to individuate material objects and what principles of object-individuation are at work.

**Baker's Constitution View**
Lynne Rudder Baker's systematic account: x constitutes y at t iff x and y share all their matter at t, and x and y are of different primary kinds with different persistence conditions. Constitution is not identity; it is a real relation that generates a pluralism about material objects.
