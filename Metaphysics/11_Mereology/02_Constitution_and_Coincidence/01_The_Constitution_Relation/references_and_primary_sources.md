# References and Primary Sources: The Constitution Relation

## Foundational Texts

- Johnston, Mark, "Constitution Is Not Identity," *Mind* 101 (1992). Argues that the relationship between a statue and the clay that constitutes it is constitution, not identity; constitution is a real relation of asymmetric dependence between objects that share all their matter.

- Lynne Rudder Baker, *Persons and Bodies* (2000). Develops a systematic account of constitution as a relation between objects of different kinds that share matter at a time without being identical; applies this to persons and their bodies.

- Fine, Kit, "A Counterexample to Locke's Thesis," *The Monist* 83 (2000). Examines the relationship between material constitution and identity, developing a theory of "variable embodiment" that distinguishes the abstract artifact from its material realization.

## Contemporary Works

- Rea, Michael (ed.), *Material Constitution: A Reader* (1997). An anthology collecting classic papers on constitution, coincidence, and material objects.

- Koslicki, Kathrin, *The Structure of Objects* (2008). Develops a neo-Aristotelian account of material objects that distinguishes form from matter and provides a sophisticated treatment of constitution and coincidence.
