# Important Figures: Spatial Coincidence

**David Lewis (1941–2001)** — Argued against colocation via four-dimensionalism; his mereology of temporal parts dissolves spatial coincidence by multiplying temporal differences.

**Theodore Sider (b. 1967)** — *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001) is the most comprehensive defense of the four-dimensionalist solution to coincidence; chapter 5 addresses the statue-lump puzzle specifically.

**Judith Jarvis Thomson (1929–2020)** — Argued that coincidence is a genuine phenomenon that cannot be dissolved by four-dimensionalism, and that we must accept spatially coincident objects.

**Peter van Inwagen (b. 1942)** — Resists coincident objects while rejecting four-dimensionalism by holding that ordinary objects (except organisms) don't exist—eliminating one of the coincidents.

**Cody Gilmore** — Contemporary work on coincidence, especially the "grounding problem" for coincident objects (explaining why two coincident things have different properties without appeal to brute fact).

**Kathrin Koslicki** — Has argued for a hylomorphic approach to coincidence, drawing on Aristotle's form-matter distinction to explain how statue and lump differ.
