# References and Primary Sources: Spatial Coincidence

## Foundational Texts

- Wiggins, David, *Sameness and Substance Renewed* (2001). Defends the coincidence of distinct objects that share matter at a time; argues that identity is always relative to a sortal, and two things of different sorts can coincide spatially.

- Gibbard, Allan, "Contingent Identity," *Journal of Philosophical Logic* 4 (1975). Argues that the statue and the clay are contingently identical: they are the same object in the actual world but could have been distinct. This is an alternative to the full coincidence view.

- Sider, Theodore, *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001). Defends temporal parts as the solution to spatial coincidence: distinct four-dimensional objects can share three-dimensional temporal stages without problematic spatial coincidence.

## Contemporary Works

- Hawthorne, John, "Three-Dimensionalism," in *Oxford Studies in Metaphysics* vol. 1 edited by Zimmerman (2004). A careful examination of three-dimensionalist responses to coincidence puzzles, distinguishing several versions.

- Wasserman, Ryan, "Material Constitution," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2021). A comprehensive survey of the coincidence debate and the main responses, including relative identity, four-dimensionalism, and constitution views.
