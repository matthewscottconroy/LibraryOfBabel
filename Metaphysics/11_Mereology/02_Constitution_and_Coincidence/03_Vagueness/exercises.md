# Exercises: Vagueness and Mereology

1. **Sider's argument reconstructed.** Reconstruct Sider's argument from vagueness in premise-by-premise form. Identify which premise is most vulnerable and construct the best objection to it.

2. **Evans's argument.** Evans argued that "a = b" cannot be vague because doing so generates a contradiction via Leibniz's Law. Assess whether his argument succeeds. Does it show that *existence* cannot be vague, or only that *identity* cannot be?

3. **Epistemicism applied.** If epistemicism is true, there is a precise moment at which a developing fetus becomes a person with full moral status. Is this an acceptable consequence? What does it imply for policy debates about abortion?

4. **Supervaluationism.** Explain how a supervaluationist would handle the claim "the cloud exists" when there are many candidate collections of droplets. Does supervaluationism entail that "either the cloud exists or it does not" is true even when "the cloud exists" lacks a truth value?

5. **Borderline composition.** Is there a way to accept restricted composition without incurring Sider's argument? Consider whether there might be some natural property of objects that sharply demarcates composing-cases—one that isn't vague. How plausible is this?

6. **Vague parthood and personal identity.** A sorites series of cell-removal operations is performed on a body: at each step, one cell is removed. At which step does the resulting entity cease to be a person? Does vague parthood help or hurt in answering this question?
