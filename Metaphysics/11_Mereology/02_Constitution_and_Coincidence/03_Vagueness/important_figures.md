# Important Figures: Vagueness and Mereology

**Timothy Williamson (b. 1955)** — Leading defender of epistemic vagueness (epistemicism): all vague predicates have precise extensions, but we are ignorant of the cutoffs. Applied to mereology, this means composition has a precise but unknowable threshold.

**Theodore Sider (b. 1967)** — Argues from vagueness to universalism or nihilism; his *Four-Dimensionalism* chapter 4 is the key text.

**Gareth Evans (1946–1980)** — Argued that ontic vagueness is impossible; his brief paper "Can There Be Vague Objects?" (1978) launched a major debate.

**Roy Sorensen (b. 1951)** — Another defender of epistemicism, with particular attention to borderline cases in existence and identity.

**Rosanna Keefe** — Has developed a comprehensive supervaluationist theory of vagueness; her *Theories of Vagueness* (2000) is a major reference.

**Mark Heller** — Argued for "temporal counterpart theory" as a response to vagueness of composition; linked four-dimensionalism to solutions of mereological vagueness.
