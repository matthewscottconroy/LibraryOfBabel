# Important Terms and Ideas: Vagueness in Mereology

**Mereological Vagueness**
The question of whether the part-whole relation and composition can be vague. If there is a heap of sand, is each individual grain determinately a part of it? Is there a determinate fact about when we have one heap rather than two? Mereological vagueness concerns both part membership and composition.

**Sorites Paradox Applied to Objects**
The application of sorites reasoning to object identity and composition: take a ship; remove planks one by one; at what point does the ship cease to exist? Any sharp cutoff seems arbitrary; gradual removal seems to show there is no fact of the matter—but this threatens the existence of ordinary objects.

**Vague Objects**
The view that vagueness can be a feature of objects themselves—not just of our descriptions. A vague object has indeterminate boundaries: it is neither determinately the case that grain g is part of the heap, nor determinately the case that it is not. This is a form of ontic vagueness.

**Epistemicism about Vagueness**
The view that vague predicates have sharp extensions; it is our knowledge that is uncertain, not the world. Applied to mereology: there is a precise fact about whether grain g is part of the heap; we simply cannot know what it is. Williamson is the leading defender.

**Semantic Indeterminacy**
The view that vagueness is a matter of semantic indecision: our terms do not have precise enough meanings to settle borderline cases. Applied to composition: "the heap" does not have a precise enough referent to settle questions about precise membership, but this reflects our language, not the world.

**Ontic Vagueness**
Genuine metaphysical indeterminacy in the world, independent of language and knowledge. Barnes and Williams develop a theory: it can be determinately true that there is an object while remaining indeterminate exactly which object (which mereological sum) it is. This avoids both epistemicism and semantic indeterminacy views.
