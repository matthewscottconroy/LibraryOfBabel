# References and Primary Sources: Vagueness in Mereology

## Foundational Texts

- Unger, Peter, "There Are No Ordinary Things," *Synthese* 41 (1979). Argues that vagueness considerations show that ordinary objects do not exist; the sorites paradox applied to object boundaries shows that the concept of a physical object is incoherent.

- Sider, Theodore, "Against Vague Existence," *Philosophical Studies* 114 (2003). Argues that vagueness in existence claims is impossible; it cannot be vague whether a mereological sum exists, so either composition is always determinate (universalism) or never (nihilism).

- Tye, Michael, "Vague Objects," *Mind* 99 (1990). Defends the view that vagueness can be ontological—there genuinely are vague objects in the world, not merely vague descriptions of precise objects.

## Contemporary Works

- Williamson, Timothy, *Vagueness* (1994). Defends epistemicism: vague predicates have sharp extensions, but we do not know where the boundaries lie. Applied to mereology: there is a precise fact about when composition occurs, we just don't know it.

- Barnes, Elizabeth and Williams, J.R.G., "A Theory of Metaphysical Indeterminacy," *Oxford Studies in Metaphysics* 6 (2011). Develops a framework for genuine ontological indeterminacy, with applications to composition and identity.
