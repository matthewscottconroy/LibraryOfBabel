# Exercises: Temporal Mereology

1. **Temporal parts defined.** Give a precise definition of a temporal part. How does it differ from a spatial part? Give three concrete examples of temporal parts for a middle-distance runner completing a race.

2. **The argument from temporary intrinsics.** State Lewis's argument precisely. Evaluate each of the three endurantist responses (time-indexing, adverbialism, property-as-relation). Which, if any, adequately preserves the intrinsicness of temporary properties?

3. **Worm vs. stage.** Compare the worm view and the stage view. Which better handles ordinary claims like "I will be at the conference tomorrow"? Construct a case where the two views give different verdicts and explain which verdict is preferable.

4. **Science and spacetime.** Does the four-dimensional structure of spacetime in special relativity *require* temporal parts? Or merely permit them? Is there a way to be an endurantist and accept Minkowskian spacetime?

5. **Persistence and memory.** Perdurantists typically analyze personal identity over time in terms of overlapping worms. Is this compatible with the view that I am *responsible* for things my temporal parts did? Construct an endurantist objection and a perdurantist reply.

6. **Gunky time.** Could time be "gunky"—every interval having sub-intervals, with no atomic instants? If so, what happens to temporal parts defined as instantaneous time-slices? Can temporal mereology be adapted to accommodate gunky time?
