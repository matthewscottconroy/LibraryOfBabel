# Important Terms and Ideas: Temporal Mereology

**Temporal Parts**
On four-dimensionalism, objects have parts extended in time as well as space: temporal parts or stages are three-dimensional slices of a four-dimensional object at particular times. Just as a physical object has left and right spatial parts, it has earlier and later temporal parts.

**Perdurance**
The mode of persistence associated with four-dimensionalism: an object perdures if it persists by having distinct temporal parts at different times. The whole object is not wholly present at each time; different temporal stages are present at different times, connected by the right relations.

**Endurance**
The mode of persistence associated with three-dimensionalism: an object endures if it persists by being wholly present at each time it exists. The very same object—with all its properties—exists at each time, not a temporal stage of it. Endurance does not posit temporal parts.

**Stage Theory**
The version of four-dimensionalism (Sider, Hawley) where ordinary objects are momentary temporal stages rather than four-dimensional worms. Ordinary persistence talk is about what stages are counterparts of each other; there are no worms composed of stages—only the stages themselves.

**Worm Theory**
The version of four-dimensionalism where ordinary objects are four-dimensional worms—fusions of all their temporal stages across time. The whole worm is the person, the table, etc.; stages are proper parts of worms. This contrasts with stage theory and endurance.

**Temporal Mereology**
The application of mereological principles to temporal parts: temporal parts are related to temporal wholes as spatial parts are related to spatial wholes. The fusion of all temporal stages of an object just is the four-dimensional object; mereological principles apply across temporal as well as spatial dimensions.
