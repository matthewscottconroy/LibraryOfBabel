# Important Figures: Correspondence Theory

**Aristotle (384–322 BCE)** — First clear formulation of the correspondence intuition: "to say of what is that it is, and of what is not that it is not, is true."

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — Canonical scholastic formulation: *veritas est adaequatio rei et intellectus* (truth is the adequation of mind and thing).

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — Developed logical atomism; proposed that atomic propositions correspond to atomic facts composed of particulars and universals.

**Ludwig Wittgenstein (1889–1951)** — Early "picture theory" in the *Tractatus*: propositions are logical pictures of possible facts; truth is the picture's matching its fact.

**J. L. Austin (1911–1960)** — "Truth" (1950) gave a carefully worked-out correspondence account in terms of statements being correlated with types of situations.

**David Armstrong (1926–2014)** — Defended states of affairs as the truth-makers for correspondence; *A World of States of Affairs* (1997) is the major systematic account.

**Kit Fine (b. 1946)** — Recent work connecting truthmaking and essence to correspondence-theoretic concerns.
