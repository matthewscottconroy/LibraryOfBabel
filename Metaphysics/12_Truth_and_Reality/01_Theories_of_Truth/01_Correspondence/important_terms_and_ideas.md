# Important Terms and Ideas: Correspondence Theory of Truth

**Correspondence Theory of Truth**
The view that a proposition (or sentence, or belief) is true if and only if it corresponds to a fact or how things are in the world. Truth is a relation between a bearer of truth (proposition) and reality; the relation is correspondence.

**Facts**
In correspondence theories, facts are what true propositions correspond to. Facts are typically conceived as complex entities in the world: objective states of affairs constituted by objects and properties. Negative facts (what is not the case) and general facts present challenges.

**Truthmakers**
Entities whose existence makes propositions true. Armstrong's version of correspondence: true propositions are made true by states of affairs. The truthmaker for "the cat is on the mat" is the state of affairs of the cat's being on the mat. Truthmaking is a real relation of necessitation.

**The Correspondence Relation**
The relation between a true proposition and the fact it corresponds to. Articulating this relation precisely is a challenge: it cannot be pure similarity (propositions and facts are too different in nature), nor can it be mere co-existence (false propositions co-exist with many facts). Isomorphism is one proposal.

**Structural Isomorphism**
An attempt to specify correspondence: a proposition corresponds to a fact when there is a structural isomorphism between them—the structure of the proposition mirrors the structure of the fact. Components of the proposition represent components of the fact in a one-to-one way.

**Objections to Correspondence**
The main challenges: (1) What are facts? If they are mind-independent complexes, how do we access them? (2) Negative facts: what makes "Socrates is not flying" true? (3) The identity theory of truth: propositions just are the facts they express. (4) Deflationary challenge: "p is true" says no more than p.
