# References and Primary Sources: Correspondence Theory of Truth

## Foundational Texts

- Aristotle, *Metaphysics* Γ, 1011b25. The classic statement: "To say of what is that it is, and of what is not that it is not, is true." Truth is a matter of what one says corresponding to how things are.

- Russell, Bertrand, "On the Nature of Truth and Falsehood," in *Philosophical Essays* (1910). An early analytic defense of correspondence: propositions are true when they correspond to facts; facts are complexes in the world to which propositions stand in a relation of correspondence.

- Austin, J.L., "Truth," *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society*, Supplementary Volume 24 (1950). Develops a sophisticated correspondence theory: a statement is true when the situation it describes is correlated with the type of situation the statement was made to describe.

## Contemporary Works

- Armstrong, David, *Truth and Truthmakers* (2004). Defends a correspondence theory in terms of truthmakers: true propositions are made true by states of affairs in the world; correspondence is explained by the truthmaking relation.

- Künne, Wolfgang, *Conceptions of Truth* (2003). A comprehensive survey of all major theories of truth, with particular attention to correspondence and its competitors.
