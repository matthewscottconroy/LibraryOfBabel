# Coherence Theory of Truth

The coherence theory of truth holds that truth is not a matter of correspondence to an external world but of coherence within a system of beliefs, propositions, or judgments. A proposition is true if it coheres with — fits, is consistent with, is mutually supportive of — the other propositions in the relevant system. Truth is a property of propositions within a system, not a relation between propositions and a mind-independent world.

The coherence theory has its natural home in idealist metaphysics. If the external world is not independent of mind — if what there is is constituted by thought — then the natural picture of truth as correspondence to a mind-independent world is unavailable. The British idealists (Bradley, Bosanquet) developed coherence as the appropriate criterion of truth for an idealist world: what is true is what belongs to the maximally coherent system of judgments that constitutes reality.

The main difficulties for the coherence theory are two. First, there can be many mutually coherent systems of propositions: the fictional universe of a novel is internally coherent; so is a paranoid delusional system; so is a complete alternative history of the world. Which system is the true one? The coherence theorist must say that only one system — or only certain kinds of systems — count; but specifying which requires criteria beyond mere coherence.

Second, coherence seems to be a matter of degree: systems can be more or less coherent. But truth is not a matter of degree — a proposition is true or false, not more-true or less-true. If truth requires perfect coherence, then no proposition is true; if it requires merely sufficient coherence, then too many propositions count as true.

Contemporary defenses of coherence-like views tend to be epistemological rather than metaphysical: coherence is a criterion of justification, not of truth. This "coherentist" epistemology is compatible with a correspondence view of truth. This section examines the coherence theory of truth, its idealist home, and the difficulties it faces.
