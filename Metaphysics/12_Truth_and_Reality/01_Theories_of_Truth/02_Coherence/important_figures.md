# Important Figures: Coherence Theory

**F. H. Bradley (1846–1924)** — *Appearance and Reality* (1893); the most developed idealist coherentism. Held that truth is a matter of participation in the coherent Absolute.

**Bernard Bosanquet (1848–1923)** — British Idealist who developed coherentism in *Logic* (1888) and *The Principle of Individuality and Value* (1912).

**Harold Joachim (1868–1938)** — Author of *The Nature of Truth* (1906), the most systematic early defense of the coherence theory.

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)** — Despite being the leading critic of British Idealism, Russell's engagement with coherentism sharpened the debate; his "The Monistic Theory of Truth" (1907) identifies key objections.

**Keith Lehrer (1936–2019)** — Developed coherentism primarily as a theory of epistemic justification; his *Theory of Knowledge* (1990) is a key contemporary text.

**Laurence BonJour (b. 1943)** — *The Structure of Empirical Knowledge* (1985) offers a major contemporary defense of epistemic coherentism.
