# Important Terms and Ideas: Coherence Theory of Truth

**Coherence Theory of Truth**
The view that truth consists in coherence with a system of beliefs or judgments. A proposition is true if and only if it fits, supports, and is supported by a comprehensive, mutually consistent system of propositions.

**Coherence as Mutual Support**
On the coherence theory, coherence is not merely logical consistency (absence of contradiction) but something stronger: mutual support, implication, or explanatory integration. A coherent system is one whose members strengthen and entail each other.

**The Isolation Objection**
The main challenge to coherence theories: a false but internally consistent story could achieve high coherence without being true. The coherence of propositions within a system does not ensure their correspondence to reality; there could be multiple, incompatible but internally coherent systems.

**The Input Problem**
How do new beliefs enter the coherence system? If truth is entirely determined by coherence within a system, there seems no way for external reality to constrain what is true. Coherence theories must explain how sensory experience or empirical input constrains the system.

**Degrees of Truth**
Bradley and Blanshard hold that truth admits of degrees: individual judgments are more or less true depending on how fully they cohere with the absolute. Only the complete, coherent whole is fully true; individual propositions are partial truths. This is connected to absolute idealism.

**Epistemological vs. Metaphysical Coherentism**
Epistemological coherentism: beliefs are justified by their coherence with other beliefs (a theory of justification). Metaphysical coherentism: truth consists in coherence (a theory of truth). These are distinct views; one can hold epistemological coherentism without the metaphysical version.
