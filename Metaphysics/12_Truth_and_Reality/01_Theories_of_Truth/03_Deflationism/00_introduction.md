# Deflationism About Truth

Deflationism about truth holds that truth is not a substantive property of propositions or sentences — it is not the correspondence to facts, coherence with a system, or any other metaphysically robust relation. Truth is a "thin" concept whose entire content is captured by its logical role. The deflationist's core claim: to say that "Snow is white" is true is to say no more than that snow is white. The truth predicate is a device of generalization and endorsement, not a description of a metaphysically loaded property.

The redundancy theory (Ramsey) held that "It is true that p" is equivalent to "p": adding "is true" to a proposition adds no information. The disquotational theory held that truth is characterized by the T-schema: "p" is true if and only if p — where inserting the sentence on the left gives the proposition on the right. These theories are "deflationary" because they deflate the ambitions of a substantive theory of truth: there is nothing more to truth than the T-schema.

Minimalism (Horwich) is the most carefully developed deflationary view. The axioms of the theory of truth are just the instances of the T-schema — the collection of all sentences of the form "'p' is true iff p" — and nothing more is needed to explain everything we want to say about truth. Truth is not a property over and above the truth-values of particular claims; it is a logical device for generalizing over claims.

Deflationism has significant advantages: it avoids the puzzles about the correspondence relation and the nature of facts, it explains the logical behavior of the truth predicate simply and elegantly, and it is compatible with any first-order theory of the world. The challenges include: explaining how truth can do its normative work (being the goal of inquiry, the standard for assertion) if it has no substantive content; handling liar-paradox sentences; and explaining the systematic connection between truth and justification.

This section examines deflationism in its various forms, the motivations for it, and the objections it faces.
