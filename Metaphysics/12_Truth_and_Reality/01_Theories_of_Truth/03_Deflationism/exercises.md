# Exercises: Deflationism

1. **The T-schema.** State the T-schema precisely. Is it a definition of truth, a partial definition, or merely a constraint? Evaluate Tarski's approach: does it give us a *theory* of truth or just a formal device?

2. **Redundancy vs. minimalism.** Ramsey's redundancy theory says "It is true that P" just means P. Horwich's minimalism says the T-schema is the whole theory of truth but "is true" is still a predicate. What is the difference? Does the distinction matter?

3. **The normativity objection.** We aim at truth. If truth is just a disquotational device, why should we aim at it rather than at coherence or utility? Evaluate the deflationist response.

4. **The Liar paradox.** Construct the Liar sentence: "This sentence is false." Apply the T-schema. What goes wrong? Does the deflationist have an adequate response? Compare the hierarchical approach with a dialethic (true contradiction) response.

5. **Explaining success.** A deflationist claims "true" doesn't name a substantive property. But we seem to explain the success of science by saying that scientific theories are (approximately) true. Can the deflationist give this explanation? If not, is that a decisive objection?

6. **Wright's challenge.** Crispin Wright argues that even if truth is minimal, it may still have "platitudes" (truth is distinct from warranted assertibility, truth is the norm of assertion, etc.) that give it more structure than pure deflationism admits. Evaluate this challenge.
