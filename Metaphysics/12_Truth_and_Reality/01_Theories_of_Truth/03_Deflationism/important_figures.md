# Important Figures: Deflationism

**Frank Ramsey (1903–1930)** — Early formulation of the redundancy theory: "It is true that Caesar was murdered" = "Caesar was murdered"; truth adds nothing.

**W. V. O. Quine (1908–2000)** — Developed disquotationalism; saw truth as a device for semantic ascent in *Philosophy of Logic* (1970).

**Alfred Tarski (1901–1983)** — Formal T-schema and definition of truth for formal languages; read by deflationists as vindicating the schema-centered approach.

**Paul Horwich (b. 1947)** — *Truth* (1990) is the most systematic minimalist deflationism; the T-schema for propositions is all there is to truth.

**Dorothy Grover, Joseph Camp, Nuel Belnap** — Prosentential theory: "true" is a prosentence-forming operator, not a predicate.

**Hartry Field (b. 1946)** — Has moved between deflationism and correspondence; his discussions of truth and ontology are central to the contemporary debate.

**Scott Soames (b. 1946)** — *Understanding Truth* (1999) provides a critical engagement with deflationism and Tarski.
