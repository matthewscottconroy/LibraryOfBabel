# Important Terms and Ideas: Deflationary Theories of Truth

**Deflationary Theory of Truth**
The family of views holding that truth is not a substantive property—adding "is true" to a statement adds no new information about the world. The truth predicate is a logical or linguistic device, not a real property that requires metaphysical analysis.

**Redundancy Theory**
Ramsey's view: "It is true that p" is simply equivalent to "p"; "is true" is a redundant predicate that can always be eliminated without loss. Truth does not name a real property.

**Disquotation**
Quine's account: the function of "is true" is to disquote—to move from mentioning a sentence to using it. "'Snow is white' is true" is equivalent to "Snow is white"; the quotation marks and the truth predicate cancel out. Truth is a device for semantic ascent and descent.

**Minimalism (Horwich)**
The concept of truth is exhausted by all instances of the equivalence schema: ⟨p⟩ is true iff p. No substantive analysis of truth is needed; the property of being true is as minimal as possible—constituted entirely by its conformity to the equivalence schema.

**Prosentential Theory**
Grover, Camp, and Belnap's view: "that's true" functions as a prosentence (like a pronoun but for sentences) that picks up its content from context. Truth talk is anaphoric reference to previously expressed propositions, not predication of a property.

**Inflationary Challenge to Deflationism**
The objection that deflationary truth cannot explain the role of truth in explanations like "All of Einstein's beliefs are true, therefore each one is" (quantificational uses), or its role in epistemology and logic. Deflationists argue these uses are handled by the disquotational schema without requiring a substantive property.
