# References and Primary Sources: Deflationary Theories of Truth

## Foundational Texts

- Ramsey, Frank P., "Facts and Propositions," *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society*, Supplementary Volume 7 (1927). The first clear statement of redundancy theory: "It is true that Caesar was murdered" says nothing more than "Caesar was murdered"; "true" adds nothing.

- Quine, W.V.O., "Disquotation and Truth," in *Pursuit of Truth* (1990). Disquotational theory: "Snow is white" is true iff snow is white; the predicate "is true" is merely a device for disquotation, with no descriptive content.

- Horwich, Paul, *Truth* (1990, 2nd ed. 1998). Develops minimalism: the concept of truth is fully captured by all instances of the equivalence schema (⟨p⟩ is true iff p); nothing deeper needs to be said about what truth is.

## Contemporary Works

- Field, Hartry, "Tarski's Theory of Truth," *Journal of Philosophy* 69 (1972). Argues that Tarski's theory is a deflationary account of truth and examines its relationship to physicalism about semantics.

- Stoljar, Daniel and Damnjanovic, Nic, "The Deflationary Theory of Truth," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2010). A comprehensive survey of deflationary theories, their motivations, and the main objections.
