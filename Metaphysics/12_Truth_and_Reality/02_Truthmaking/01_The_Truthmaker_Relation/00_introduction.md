# The Truthmaker Relation

When a proposition is true, something in the world makes it true — the world is responsible for the truth of the proposition in some way that goes beyond the proposition's own intrinsic character. This is the intuition behind truthmaker theory: every truth has a truthmaker, an entity in the world whose existence necessitates the truth of the proposition. The truthmaker is the worldly ground of the proposition's truth.

The truthmaker principle — every truth has a truthmaker — sounds anodyne, but its implications are substantial. If it holds, then every truth is "grounded" in something real: there are no "free-floating" truths that are true without any corresponding worldly basis. This connects truthmaker theory to the broader metaphysics of grounding: what makes a proposition true is the existence of a certain entity, and the truth is grounded in that entity.

What are truthmakers? Armstrong's answer: states of affairs — entities composed of individuals and universals in a specific combination. The proposition "The apple is red" is made true by the state of affairs of the apple being red. This is a structured entity — not just the apple, not just redness, but the apple and redness combined in a particular way. States of affairs have a "thick" existence — they are more than the sum of their constituents — and it is their existence that necessitates the truth of the corresponding proposition.

The necessitation condition — the truthmaker must necessitate the truth of the proposition — is the core of truthmaker theory. If the truthmaker exists, the proposition is true; there is no possible world where the truthmaker exists but the proposition is false. This rules out merely contingent grounds: the truth is not merely correlated with the truthmaker; it could not fail to be true if the truthmaker exists.
