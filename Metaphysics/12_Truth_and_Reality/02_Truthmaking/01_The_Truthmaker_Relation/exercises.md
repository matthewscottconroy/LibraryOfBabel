# Exercises: The Truthmaker Relation

1. **Define truthmaking.** A truthmaker T for proposition P is an entity such that necessarily, if T exists, P is true. Evaluate this definition. Is necessity the right relation? Could T make P true without necessitating it?

2. **Maximalism.** State the truthmaker maximalism thesis. Give three propositions for which finding a truthmaker seems easy, and three for which it seems very difficult. Does the difficulty of the hard cases show that maximalism is false?

3. **Negative truths.** Armstrong invokes a "totality state of affairs" to make "there are no dragons" true. Is this a satisfying truthmaker? What alternatives are there? Evaluate the claim that negative truths simply don't need truthmakers.

4. **Sparse truthmaking.** Cameron and others argue that only a restricted class of truths need truthmakers. What principled criterion could demarcate which truths need them? Is there an attractive sparse view?

5. **Lewis's difference-making.** Lewis argued that truthmakers should make a *difference*—that the truth of P should depend counterfactually on the truthmaker. How does this differ from the necessitation account? Give an example where they come apart.

6. **Truthmaking and ontology.** Truthmaker theory is supposed to constrain our ontology—we should posit the entities needed to serve as truthmakers for our truths. Is this a good methodological principle? What would it imply for the existence of abstract objects?
