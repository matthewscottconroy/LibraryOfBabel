# Important Terms and Ideas: The Truthmaker Relation

**Truthmaker**
An entity in the world that makes a proposition true. A truthmaker for "Socrates is sitting" is typically taken to be the state of affairs of Socrates's sitting. The truthmaking relation connects a proposition to the worldly entity responsible for its truth.

**Truthmaker Principle**
The principle that every (contingent) truth has a truthmaker—some entity whose existence necessitates the truth. Armstrong defends this as a fundamental metaphysical principle; critics argue it cannot be satisfied for negative truths and general truths.

**Truthmaking Necessitation**
The condition that a truthmaker T for proposition p must be such that T's existence necessitates p's truth: it is metaphysically impossible for T to exist and p to be false. This ensures truthmakers genuinely explain truth rather than merely correlate with it.

**States of Affairs**
Armstrong's preferred truthmakers: complex entities consisting of an object instantiating a property (or standing in a relation). "Socrates is sitting" is made true by the state of affairs [Socrates's sitting]. States of affairs are distinct from facts in some uses but often treated as equivalent.

**Negative Truths Problem**
The challenge for truthmaker theory: "Socrates is not standing" is true. What makes it true? A negative fact? The absence of the state of affairs [Socrates's standing]? Absences seem to be non-entities; positing negative facts is theoretically costly. This is a major challenge for the truthmaker principle.

**Truthmaker Maximalism vs. Truthmaker Monism**
Maximalism: every truth has a truthmaker. Monism: there is one truthmaker for all truths (typically the world as a whole). Most truthmaker theorists defend maximalism; monism avoids the negative truths problem but at the cost of making all truths equally basic.
