# Essence and Truthmaking

States of affairs and tropes serve as truthmakers for contingent truths: the rose's-redness exists contingently, and its existence necessitates "the rose is red." But necessary truths — "water is H₂O," "triangles have three sides," "necessarily, Hesperus is Phosphorus" — pose a different challenge. If a truth is necessary, it holds across all possible worlds; but a contingent state of affairs or trope cannot hold across all possible worlds. A newer development in truthmaker theory meets this challenge by connecting truth-grounding to *essence* — the notion of what an object is in itself, independent of its merely accidental properties.

Kit Fine's neo-Aristotelian account of essence has been influential here. For Fine (*Essence and Modality*, 1994), the essence of an object *x* is constituted by the real definitions that pertain to *x* as such — what it is to be *x*. Essence grounds necessary truths: "Socrates is a person" is necessarily true because being a person is part of Socrates's essence.

**Fine on essence**

Fine begins with the observation that the standard modal account of essence is inadequate. On the modal account, an entity *x* has essentially the property *F* iff necessarily *x* has *F* (□Fx). But this misclassifies: necessarily, Socrates is a member of the singleton {Socrates}, but it does not belong to Socrates's essence to be a member of that set — it belongs to the essence of the set to contain Socrates. Fine concludes that essence is not reducible to necessity; essence is a prior, metaphysically foundational notion.

The correction: *x* has essentially the property *F* iff it is *true in virtue of the nature of x* that *x* has *F*. Essential truths are grounded in natures, not in possible-worlds semantics. This gives a fine-grained account that distinguishes the essence of Socrates (being human, being rational, being a particular person) from accidental necessities (belonging to singleton-{Socrates}).

**The connection to truthmaking**

The connection runs as follows:

- P1: Essential truths are those true in virtue of the nature of an entity.
- P2: "Water is H₂O" is true in virtue of the nature (essence) of water.
- P3: The essence of water is its truthmaker for "water is H₂O."
- C: Essence can serve as a truthmaker, grounding necessary truths without contingent states of affairs.

This extends truthmaker theory to necessary truths in a principled way. On a Lewisian possible-worlds analysis, necessary truths are true at all possible worlds, and the truthmakers for necessary truths are the totality of possible worlds — an enormous ontological commitment. On Fine's essence-based account, the truthmaker for "water is H₂O" is just the essence of water, which is an actual entity (the nature of water) rather than a transworld structure. Fine has argued similarly that many modal truths — truths about what is necessarily or essentially so — are grounded in essence rather than in possible worlds or Kripkean necessity. "Necessarily, if Hesperus exists, then Hesperus is identical to Phosphorus" is true because it is part of the essence of Hesperus (= Phosphorus) to be self-identical and to be that particular planet.

Fine's framework uses an essence operator □_x (*true in virtue of the nature of x*):

□_x φ iff φ is true in virtue of the nature of *x* (the essence of *x* entails φ)

Essential necessities: □_x φ → □ φ   (essential truths are necessary, but not vice versa)
Not vice versa: □ φ does not entail □_x φ for any *x*   (necessary truths may not be essential to anything in particular)

The essence-based truthmaker: φ is made true by the essence of *x* iff □_x φ. The truthmaker is not a state of affairs but the essential nature of *x* — what *x* is.

**Grounding and essence**

The essence-based approach also connects to debates about *grounding*. Grounding is a non-causal explanatory relation: *p* is grounded in *q* means *q* is the metaphysical explanation of *p*. Essence-based truthmaking is one species of grounding: a proposition is true in virtue of (grounded in) the essential features of the entities it concerns. The grounding relation: p < q (p is grounded in q) iff q is the metaphysical explanation of p — not merely an entailment of p, but the reason why p is true. Essence grounds many metaphysical necessities: "triangles have interior angles summing to 180°" is grounded in the essence of triangularity. "Sets are individuated by their members" is grounded in the essence of set-hood.

A key advantage of the essence-grounding approach: it explains the asymmetry of grounding. If *p* is grounded in *q*, then *q* is not thereby grounded in *p*. The truth "Socrates is a member of {Socrates}" is grounded in the essence of {Socrates}, not vice versa. Possible-worlds semantics does not capture this asymmetry — if *p* and *q* are both necessary truths, they are both true at all possible worlds, with no evident direction of explanation. Essence gives the direction.

**Lowe's four-category ontology**

Jonathan Lowe's four-category ontology — distinguishing objects (individual substances), kinds (universals over substances), attributes (universals over properties), and modes (individual property-instances, or tropes) — provides another framework in which essence plays a central truthmaking role. For Lowe, the relationship between kinds and their attributes grounds the truth of laws and essential generalizations, while the relationship between objects and their modes grounds singular truths. Specifically: "All gold is malleable" is made true by the relationship between the kind GOLD and the attribute MALLEABILITY — a formal ontological relation between universals that is not a contingent state of affairs but a necessary structural feature of the ontology. Lowe calls this "formal ontological dependence." The truthmaker for a law is not a second-order state of affairs (as in Armstrong) but a formal relation among the ontological categories.

For Lowe's four-category ontology, the truthmaker for "All gold is malleable" is the formal ontological relation between the kind GOLD and the attribute MALLEABILITY. Formally: Characterizes(MALLEABILITY, GOLD). This formal ontological relation — a constitutive connection between universals — is the truthmaker, not a second-order state of affairs.

**Objections**

The main objection to essence-based truthmaking is the intelligibility of "essence." If essence is explained in modal terms (□Fx), it is inadequate (as Fine argued). If it is primitive, we need some account of what essence is and why we should believe in it. Fine treats essence as a primitive notion, justified by its theoretical utility and by the inadequacy of modal alternatives. Critics (including Lewis) suspect that "essence" and "real definition" are obscure notions that generate the appearance of explanation without providing it.

A second objection: essence cannot serve as a truthmaker for all truths, only for essential truths. For contingent truths ("the cat is on the mat"), essence is the wrong kind of ground. Essence-based truthmaking is therefore a supplement to, not a replacement for, state-of-affairs or trope-based truthmaking.

Consider "Water is necessarily H₂O" under different truthmaking frameworks. Under states of affairs: the truthmaker is the nomic state of affairs N(WATER, H₂O-CONSTITUTION) — the universal WATER standing in the nomic relation to the constitutive property H₂O-CONSTITUTION. This is Armstrong's approach. Under essence: the truthmaker is the essence of water — what water is. It is part of the nature of water to be constituted by H₂O molecules. The truth is grounded in the essential nature of the natural kind, not in a contingent state of affairs or a second-order nomic relation. Under tropes: the truthmaker is the molecular constitution trope of each water sample — the particular H₂O-constitution of each sample. But tropes are contingent; no trope makes a necessary truth true. The trope account handles contingent truths well but struggles with necessities, which is precisely where essence-based accounts excel.

Essence-based truthmaking connects to the Aristotelian tradition of real definitions. A real definition states the essence of a kind — what it is to be a member of that kind. "Water is H₂O" is a real definition of water: it states the essence of the kind, not merely a contingent fact about it. Real definitions are not merely verbal (linguistic conventions) but metaphysical: they reveal the natures of kinds. The truthmakers for essential truths are thus the "real definitions" or natures of entities — a return to Aristotelian essentialism within a contemporary truthmaker framework.
