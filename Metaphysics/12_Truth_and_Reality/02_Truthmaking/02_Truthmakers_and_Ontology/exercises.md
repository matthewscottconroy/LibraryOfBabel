# Exercises: Truthmakers and Ontology

1. **States of affairs.** Why can't the particular object alone, or the universal alone, serve as a truthmaker for singular predications? How does Armstrong's state of affairs do better? What is the "non-mereological" unity of a state of affairs?

2. **Tropes vs. universals.** Compare trope-based and universal-based accounts of truthmaking. What does each view commit to ontologically? Which is more parsimonious? What are the main objections to each?

3. **Bundles.** If ordinary objects are bundles of tropes, what holds the tropes together? Compare the bundle theory response to this question with the bare-particular theory response.

4. **Essence and necessity.** Fine argues that essential truths are grounded in essence rather than in possible worlds. Reconstruct this argument. What would it mean for the truthmaker for "water is necessarily H₂O" to be the essence of water rather than a modal fact?

5. **Lowe's four categories.** Describe Lowe's four-category ontology (objects, kinds, attributes, tropes). How does each category contribute to truthmaking? Is this ontological scheme more or less plausible than Armstrong's two-category scheme (particulars and universals)?

6. **Abstract truthmakers.** Mathematical Platonism says abstract objects (numbers) are truthmakers for mathematical truths. Evaluate this claim. Is being a truthmaker compatible with being causally inert and non-spatiotemporal?
