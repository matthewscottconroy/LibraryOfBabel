# Important Terms and Ideas: Truthmakers and Ontology

**Ontological Economy via Truthmakers**
The use of truthmakers to motivate or constrain ontological theories: the question "What truthmakers do we need?" serves as a way of determining what entities to posit. The requirement to provide truthmakers for all truths constrains acceptable ontologies.

**The Ontological Argument from Truthmakers**
The methodological claim that questions about what exists can be approached via the truthmaker principle: to determine whether properties, numbers, or relations exist, ask whether they are needed as truthmakers. This connects ontology to the theory of truth.

**Minimal Truthmakers**
The most economical entities sufficient to make a proposition true—nothing in them is "extra" from the perspective of truthmaking. Armstrong argues that minimal truthmakers for contingent truths are states of affairs; the existence of the state of affairs is sufficient but nothing less is.

**Supervenience and Truthmaking**
The relationship between the standard supervenience conception of grounding and truthmaking: if truth supervenes on being, then truths are determined by what exists (by the truthmakers). This connects the truthmaker principle to broader physicalist and grounding-based accounts of reality.

**Necessitating vs. Sufficient Conditions**
Some theorists (Restall, Bigelow) argue that truthmakers need only be sufficient for truth (their existence makes it true) without strict necessitation. This relaxes the standard necessitation requirement and allows a wider range of entities to serve as truthmakers.

**Logical Truthmakers**
The challenge of providing truthmakers for logical truths (tautologies, necessary truths). If every truth has a truthmaker, what makes "It is not the case that P and not-P" true? Possible responses include: logical truths need no truthmakers (they are made true by nothing—they hold of necessity), or every entity makes every logical truth true.
