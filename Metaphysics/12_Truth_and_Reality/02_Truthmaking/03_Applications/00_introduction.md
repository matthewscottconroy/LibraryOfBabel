# Applications of Truthmaker Theory

Truthmaker theory earns its philosophical weight by illuminating specific debates — by helping to settle, or at least clarify, disputes about what exists and how various kinds of truths are made true. The method is to ask: what must exist for this truth to have a truthmaker? The answer constrains the ontology.

Modal truths are a central application. It is true that it is possible that there be a golden mountain. What makes this true? Lewis's answer: there is a concrete possible world containing a golden mountain; the possible world is the truthmaker. The actualist's answer: there is an abstract possible state of affairs — or a maximal proposition — that could obtain but does not; this abstract entity is the truthmaker. Or: the actual world has certain structural features (certain dispositions, certain fields of possibility) that ground the modal fact. The choice of truthmaker commits us to a position on the ontology of modality.

Moral truths present a different challenge. If moral sentences like "Torturing innocents for fun is wrong" are genuinely true, they need truthmakers. The moral realist holds that there are moral facts — moral states of affairs involving evaluative properties — that serve as truthmakers. The constructivist holds that moral truths are made true by facts about what rational agents would agree to. The non-cognitivist denies that moral sentences are truth-apt and hence denies that they need truthmakers.

Negative existentials — "There are no unicorns," "The round square does not exist" — are notoriously difficult to give truthmakers for. The most natural candidaie — the empty space where the unicorn should be — is not an entity in the right sense. Armstrong's totality states of affairs — the state of affairs of all actual things being exhausted by the actual things — serve as truthmakers for negative existentials by entailing the non-existence of anything further.

This section examines the applications of truthmaker theory to modal, moral, and negative truths, showing how the theory connects to broader ontological debates.
