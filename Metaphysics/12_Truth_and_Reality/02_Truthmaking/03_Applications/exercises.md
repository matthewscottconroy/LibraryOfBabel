# Exercises: Applications of Truthmaking

1. **Modal realism.** Lewis holds that possible worlds are concrete entities as real as the actual world. Evaluate his claim that they serve as truthmakers for modal propositions. What are the main objections to modal realism as a truthmaking strategy?

2. **Combinatorialism.** Armstrong argues that all possibilities are recombinations of actual properties and individuals. What is the truthmaker for "it is possible that there be an alien color" (a color nothing actual has)? Does combinatorialism have a satisfying answer?

3. **Mathematical fictionalism.** Field holds that mathematics is false but useful. Does the success of mathematics in science provide evidence that it is true? Evaluate the indispensability argument against Field's fictionalism.

4. **Moral truthmakers.** If moral realism is true, what kind of entity could serve as a truthmaker for "gratuitous cruelty is wrong"? Compare naturalistic and non-naturalistic candidates. Which faces fewer difficulties?

5. **Mackie's queerness.** Mackie argues that if moral facts existed, they would be "queer"—unlike anything else in our ontology. Is this a good argument? Could the same worry be raised about mathematical or modal facts?

6. **Quasi-realism.** Blackburn argues that expressivism can "earn" the right to speak of moral truths. Is the quasi-realist's "true" the same concept as the realist's "true"? Does quasi-realism give us enough to satisfy ordinary moral discourse?
