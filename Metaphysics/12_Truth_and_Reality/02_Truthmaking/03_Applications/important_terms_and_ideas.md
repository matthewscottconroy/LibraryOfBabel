# Important Terms and Ideas: Applications of Truthmaking

**Truthmakers for Negative Truths**
One of the hardest application problems: "There are no unicorns" is true; what makes it true? Proposed truthmakers include: the totality of what exists (everything that is) plus the fact that this is everything (totality facts); negative states of affairs; or the obtaining of the actual world as a totality.

**Totality Facts**
Armstrong's proposal for truthmakers of general and negative truths: alongside positive states of affairs, there are "totality facts" that state that the positive facts are all the facts. "All swans are white" is made true by all the individual swan-whiteness facts plus the totality fact that there are no other swans.

**Modal Truthmakers**
What makes "Possibly P" or "Necessarily P" true? On modal realism, possible truths are made true by what is true at some possible world. On actualism, modal truths must be grounded in actual entities—essences, powers, or abstract structures—that necessitate the modal facts.

**Mathematical Truthmakers**
The problem of providing truthmakers for mathematical truths without positing abstract objects like numbers. Bigelow's strategy: mathematical truths are made true by structural features of the physical world—the instantiation of universals in the right patterns. This is a nominalist-friendly approach to mathematical ontology.

**Negative Existentials**
"Unicorns do not exist" is true. If truth requires a truthmaker, what is the truthmaker for a negative existential? This is a special case of the negative truths problem; proposed solutions include the world as a whole, totality facts, or denying that negative existentials need individual truthmakers.

**Truthmaking and Reductionism**
Cameron's application: we can reduce ontological commitments by showing that apparent commitments to complex objects are actually grounded in simpler entities serving as truthmakers. If the truth about composite objects is made true by simpler entities, we need not posit the composites themselves.
