# Review Questions — Truthmaking

1. The truthmaker relation is defined by necessitation: T makes p true iff, necessarily, if T exists then p is true. This is a weaker requirement than structural isomorphism between proposition and worldly entity. What does this weakening gain, and what — if anything — does it give up compared to the correspondence theory's stronger demand for structural mirroring?

2. Armstrong argues that states of affairs, not bare particulars or bare universals, are the canonical truthmakers for atomic predicative truths, because only states of affairs satisfy the necessity condition. Reconstruct his elimination argument carefully. Does it succeed, or can a trope theorist or nominalist block one of its premises?

3. Truthmaker maximalism holds that every truth has a truthmaker. The cases of negative truths, universal generalizations, and modal truths are supposed to be its hardest test cases. Explain why Armstrong invokes the totality state of affairs for negative truths. Then assess whether the totality state of affairs is itself the kind of entity maximalism can comfortably endorse, or whether it creates an explanatory regress.

4. Cameron's sparse truthmaking view holds that only ungrounded truths require truthmakers; grounded truths inherit their truth from more fundamental truths. This relieves maximalism of its most problematic commitments. But does moving from truthmaking to grounding actually solve the problems, or does it merely redescribe them? What would a committed maximalist say in response?

5. The chapter presents three competing ontologies for truthmakers — Armstrong's states of affairs (universals + instantiation), tropes, and essence-based grounds — and each has characteristic strengths and vulnerabilities. Map the fit between each ontology and one of these three problem domains: contingent predicative truths, necessary truths, and causal truths. Does any single ontology handle all three domains well?

6. Fine argues that the modal account of essence — what is essential to x is what x necessarily has — is inadequate because it misclassifies cases like Socrates's membership in {Socrates}. Explain why the modal account gives the wrong result here and how Fine's essence operator □_x corrects it. What does this correction imply for the truthmakers of necessary truths more broadly?

7. The applications chapter considers three domains — modal truths, ethical truths, and mathematical truths — where standard truthmaker theory faces distinctive difficulties. Compare the difficulties. Are they the same kind of problem in each domain (a difficulty about the ontological status of the truthmakers), or do they differ in kind? Which domain poses the sharpest challenge?

8. The indispensability argument concludes that if mathematics is indispensable to our best scientific theories, and those theories are true, then mathematical entities exist and serve as truthmakers. Field's fictionalism denies this by arguing that mathematics is conservative but not true. Assess this debate in terms of what each side owes us: what the indispensabilist owes us about epistemic access to abstract objects, and what the fictionalist owes us about the scope of "indispensable."
