# Exercises: The Realism Debate

1. **Two commitments.** Realism has an ontological thesis and a semantic thesis. Give an example of a position that accepts one but not the other. Is either commitment more fundamental?

2. **Dummett's argument.** Reconstruct Dummett's argument that realism requires recognition-transcendent truth and that such truth is incoherent. Identify the key premise about meaning. Is the manifestation requirement defensible?

3. **Putnam's internal realism.** What is Putnam's argument that "which objects exist" is scheme-relative? Does this collapse into relativism? Evaluate Putnam's claim that internal realism is compatible with a modest objectivity.

4. **Wright's pluralism.** Wright argues that different discourses (science, ethics, aesthetics) may require different accounts of truth. Is truth a unified concept or a cluster of related concepts? What would a pluralism about truth imply for the realism debate?

5. **Constructivism.** The social constructivist says that scientific facts are socially constructed—they are the outcomes of social processes rather than discoveries of mind-independent nature. Is this compatible with saying that water is genuinely H₂O? Where does construction end and discovery begin?

6. **The metaphysics/semantics divide.** Dummett says the realism debate is semantic, not ontological. Many contemporary metaphysicians disagree and treat it as ontological. Which framing is more productive? Can the debate be conducted at both levels simultaneously?
