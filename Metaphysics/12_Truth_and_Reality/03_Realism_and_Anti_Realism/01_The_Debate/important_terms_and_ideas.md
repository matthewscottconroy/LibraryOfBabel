# Important Terms and Ideas: The Realism/Anti-Realism Debate

**Metaphysical Realism**
The view that there is a mind-independent world with a determinate structure; our theories and concepts aim at truth about this world; truth is correspondence to mind-independent reality. Putnam's "metaphysical realism" adds the claim that there is a unique correct description of the world.

**Anti-Realism**
The denial of some version of realism: either there is no mind-independent world, or truth is not correspondence to a mind-independent reality, or the world has no determinate structure independent of conceptual schemes. Anti-realism takes many forms: idealism, constructivism, verificationism.

**Dummett's Semantic Anti-Realism**
Dummett's version: to understand a statement is to know its assertibility conditions—the conditions under which one would be justified in asserting it. Since we cannot verify truth-conditions that transcend verification, we should reject bivalence for statements without determinate verification conditions.

**Internal Realism**
Putnam's intermediate position: objects and properties exist, but only relative to a conceptual scheme. There is no scheme-independent way of describing the world; truth is what is rationally acceptable in ideal conditions. This rejects "metaphysical realism" while preserving a form of objectivity.

**Bivalence**
The principle that every proposition is either true or false. Realists about a domain typically defend bivalence for that domain; anti-realists (Dummettian) reject it for propositions whose truth conditions transcend our verification capacities.

**Cognitive Command**
Wright's criterion for distinguishing robust from minimal realism: a discourse exhibits cognitive command if it is a priori that any dispute is due to a cognitive shortcoming (error, ignorance) in one party. Domains with cognitive command are plausible candidates for realism; those without it are not.
