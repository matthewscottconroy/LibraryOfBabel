# References and Primary Sources: The Realism/Anti-Realism Debate

## Foundational Texts

- Dummett, Michael, "Realism," *Synthese* 52 (1982). The most influential anti-realist manifesto: realism about a domain is the claim that propositions in that domain have determinate truth values independent of our epistemic access; anti-realism rejects this, tying truth to conditions of verification.

- Putnam, Hilary, *Reason, Truth and History* (1981). Defends internal realism (later called pragmatic realism): truth is ideal rational acceptability within a conceptual scheme; mind-independent "metaphysical realism" is incoherent.

- Wright, Crispin, *Truth and Objectivity* (1992). Develops the framework for assessing different domains for realist or anti-realist credentials; distinguishes minimal truth from robust, "cognitive command" truth.

## Contemporary Works

- Devitt, Michael, *Realism and Truth* (2nd ed. 1997). A naturalistic defense of realism: the world exists mind-independently and our best theories are approximately true of it. Realism is a metaphysical thesis about the world, not a semantic thesis about truth.

- Miller, Alexander, "Realism," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2019). A comprehensive survey of the many varieties of realism and anti-realism in the analytic tradition.
