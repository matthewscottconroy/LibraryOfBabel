# Exercises: Idealism

1. **Berkeley's master argument.** Berkeley argued that we cannot conceive of an object existing unperceived, because the very act of conceiving it is a form of perceiving it. Reconstruct this argument. Does it succeed? Is there a way to conceive of something unperceived?

2. **Kant's Copernican revolution.** Kant said he was making a "Copernican revolution" in philosophy: instead of knowledge conforming to objects, objects conform to knowledge (to the forms of intuition and categories). Explain what this means. Does it imply that science gives us knowledge of a mind-constituted world?

3. **The thing-in-itself.** Kant says the thing-in-itself is unknowable. But how do we know it exists? Reconstruct the tension in Kant's position. How did post-Kantian idealists (Fichte, Hegel) respond to this tension?

4. **Bradley's Absolute.** Bradley argues that all relations are "infected with contradiction" and that only the Absolute—a relationless whole—is fully real. Evaluate this argument. Is the claim that all relations are self-contradictory defensible?

5. **Putnam's internal realism vs. Berkeley.** Both Putnam (in his internal realist phase) and Berkeley deny mind-independent reality. How do they differ? Is Putnam's internal realism a form of idealism?

6. **Why idealism fell.** Russell and Moore rejected British Idealism. Reconstruct one of their main objections. Is it conclusive? Is there a contemporary form of idealism that survives it?
