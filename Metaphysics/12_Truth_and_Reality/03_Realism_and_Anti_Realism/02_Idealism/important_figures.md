# Important Figures: Idealism

**George Berkeley (1685–1753)** — *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge* (1710): "esse est percipi"; the original idealist claim that physical objects are collections of ideas.

**Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)** — *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781/1787): transcendental idealism; the world as experienced is structured by the mind; the thing-in-itself is unknowable.

**G. W. F. Hegel (1770–1831)** — Absolute Idealism; history and reality as the self-development of Geist (spirit); *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807) and *Science of Logic* (1812–1816).

**F. H. Bradley (1846–1924)** — *Appearance and Reality* (1893): the most rigorous British Idealist account; the Absolute as the only fully real entity.

**T. H. Green (1836–1882)** — Neo-Kantian idealism with political and ethical applications; *Prolegomena to Ethics* (1883).

**Timothy Sprigge (1932–2007)** — Late twentieth-century defender of Absolute Idealism; *The Vindication of Absolute Idealism* (1983).

**Bernardo Kastrup (b. 1975)** — Contemporary analytic idealist; *Decoding Schopenhauer's Metaphysics* (2020) and related works.
