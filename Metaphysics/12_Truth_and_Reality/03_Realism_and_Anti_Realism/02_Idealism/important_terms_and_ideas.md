# Important Terms and Ideas: Idealism

**Idealism**
The metaphysical view that reality is fundamentally mental or mind-dependent. There are many forms: subjective idealism (Berkeley: only minds and their ideas exist), transcendental idealism (Kant: the structure of experience is mind-imposed), absolute idealism (Hegel: reality is the Absolute Idea's self-realization).

**Immaterialism (Berkeley)**
Berkeley's specific form of idealism: there is no mind-independent material substance; what we call material objects are collections or regularities of ideas in minds (primarily in the mind of God). "To be is to be perceived."

**Transcendental Idealism**
Kant's position: space, time, and the categories of the understanding (causality, substance, etc.) are a priori forms that the mind imposes on experience. We know objects as they appear to us (phenomena), not as they are in themselves (noumena). Kant denies empirical idealism while defending transcendental idealism.

**Absolute Idealism**
The post-Kantian German idealist position (Fichte, Schelling, Hegel): reality is the self-development of an absolute subject or spirit. Finite minds and material nature are aspects or moments in the self-expression of the Absolute. The distinction between mind and world is ultimately overcome.

**Phenomenalism**
The view that material objects are logical constructions from actual and possible sense-data or appearances. "There is a table in the room" is analyzed in terms of conditionals about what experiences would be had by observers in appropriate conditions. A form of idealism without commitment to minds other than one's own.

**Idealism and the External World Skepticism**
Berkeley argued that idealism avoids skepticism about the external world: since objects just are collections of ideas, there is no gap between experience and reality. But critics argue that idealism faces its own skeptical challenges—about the existence of other minds and of God's sustaining perceptions.
