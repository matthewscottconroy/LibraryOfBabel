# References and Primary Sources: Idealism

## Foundational Texts

- Berkeley, George, *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge* (1710). The canonical immaterialist idealism: esse est percipi—to be is to be perceived. Material objects are collections of ideas in minds; there is no mind-independent material substance.

- Kant, Immanuel, *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781), Transcendental Aesthetic and Analytic. Transcendental idealism: space, time, and the categories of understanding are forms imposed by the mind on experience; we know phenomena (appearances) but not things-in-themselves.

- Hegel, G.W.F., *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807). Absolute idealism: reality is the self-development of Absolute Spirit; nature and finite minds are moments in the self-realization of the Absolute. The rational is the real and the real is rational.

## Contemporary Works

- Foster, John, *The Case for Idealism* (1982). A detailed contemporary defense of phenomenal idealism; argues that material objects are logical constructions from actual and possible sensory experiences.

- Guyer, Paul, "Idealism," in *Oxford Companion to Philosophy* edited by Honderich (2005). A survey of the major forms of idealism in the history of philosophy.
