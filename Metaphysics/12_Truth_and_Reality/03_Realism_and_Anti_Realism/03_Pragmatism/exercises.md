# Exercises: Pragmatism

1. **Peirce vs. James.** Compare Peirce's account of truth (ideal limit of inquiry) with James's (what works). Which is more defensible? Which faces fewer objections?

2. **The utility objection.** James says truth is what it is "useful to believe." Russell objected that the useful and the true can come apart—a false belief might be more useful than a true one. How should a pragmatist respond?

3. **Dewey's warranted assertibility.** Dewey proposes replacing "true" with "warranted assertible." Is warranted assertibility a better concept for epistemology? What does the replacement lose, if anything?

4. **Rorty's eliminativism.** Rorty says we should stop asking "what is truth?" and just try to make our practices better. Is this a satisfying response to the philosophical puzzles about truth, or is it a change of subject?

5. **Brandom's inferentialism.** For Brandom, meaning is constituted by inferential role. "Water" means what it means because of its inferential connections to "liquid," "H₂O," "transparent," etc. Can inferentialism handle proper names and indexicals? Does it preserve an adequate notion of truth?

6. **Pragmatism and realism.** Putnam calls his later view "pragmatic realism." Is pragmatism compatible with realism, or does pragmatism inevitably slide toward anti-realism? What would a pragmatic realist say about "the number of stars is even or odd"?
