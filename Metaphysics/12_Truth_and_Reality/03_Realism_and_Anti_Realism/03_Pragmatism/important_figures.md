# Important Figures: Pragmatism

**Charles Sanders Peirce (1839–1914)** — Founder of pragmatism; "fixation of belief" and "how to make our ideas clear"; truth as the ideal limit of inquiry.

**William James (1842–1910)** — *Pragmatism* (1907): truth as what "works"; the most influential and most criticized version of pragmatist truth.

**John Dewey (1859–1952)** — Instrumentalism; *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (1938); knowledge as problem-solving; rejection of the spectator theory.

**Richard Rorty (1931–2007)** — *Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature* (1979); neo-pragmatism as therapy; rejection of the quest for foundations; *Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity* (1989).

**Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)** — Pragmatic realism; engaged with classical pragmatism throughout his career; *Pragmatism: An Open Question* (1995).

**Robert Brandom (b. 1950)** — Inferentialism and neo-pragmatism; *Making It Explicit* (1994); the normative dimension of assertoric practice.
