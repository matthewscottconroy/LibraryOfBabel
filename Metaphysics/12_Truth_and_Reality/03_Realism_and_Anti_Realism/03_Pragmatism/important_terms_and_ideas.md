# Important Terms and Ideas: Pragmatism and Truth

**Pragmatist Theory of Truth**
The view that truth is not correspondence to a mind-independent reality but is defined by practical utility or ideal inquiry. Peirce: truth is what inquiry converges on. James: truth is what works. Dewey: truth is warranted assertibility—the outcome of successful inquiry.

**The Method of Pragmatism**
Peirce's method: to determine the meaning of a concept, ask what practical difference it would make. To determine what "truth" means, ask what practical consequences distinguish true beliefs from false ones. True beliefs guide action successfully.

**Warranted Assertibility**
Dewey's substitute for truth: a belief is warrantedly assertible when it has been produced by a process of genuine inquiry—tested against evidence and found to solve the problem that prompted inquiry. This is a naturalistic, process-based substitute for the concept of truth.

**Neo-Pragmatism (Rorty)**
Rorty's radical pragmatism: the concept of truth as accurate representation should be abandoned. Inquiry is not directed toward mirroring reality but toward solving problems; "truth" is solidarity—what our community would agree to after free and open discussion.

**Convergence Theory**
Peirce's version: truth is what the community of inquirers would converge on in the ideal long run. This avoids relativism (it is not what any actual community believes) while remaining pragmatist (truth is defined in terms of inquiry, not correspondence).

**Pragmatism and Anti-Realism**
The question of whether pragmatism is a form of anti-realism. Rorty is clearly anti-realist; Peirce is harder to classify—his convergence theory might accommodate realism about the objects of inquiry. Most pragmatists resist the claim that reality is mind-dependent while rejecting the metaphysical realist's correspondence picture.
