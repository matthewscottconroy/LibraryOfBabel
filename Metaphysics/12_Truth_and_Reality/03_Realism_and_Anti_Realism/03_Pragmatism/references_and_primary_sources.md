# References and Primary Sources: Pragmatism and Truth

## Foundational Texts

- Peirce, Charles Sanders, "How to Make Our Ideas Clear," *Popular Science Monthly* 12 (1878). The founding pragmatist paper: the meaning of an idea consists in its conceivable practical consequences; truth is what inquiry converges on in the ideal long run.

- James, William, *Pragmatism* (1907). Develops the pragmatist theory of truth: truth is what works; true ideas are those that we can assimilate, validate, and verify; truth is an attribute of the expedient in the way of believing.

- Dewey, John, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (1938). Develops warranted assertibility as the proper aim of inquiry; truth is not a property of propositions but of judgments that have passed through the process of inquiry.

## Contemporary Works

- Rorty, Richard, *Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature* (1979). Neo-pragmatism: truth is not a matter of accurate representation of mind-independent reality; it is solidarity—what the community would agree to given ideal conditions of discussion. Abandons traditional epistemology.

- Misak, Cheryl, *Truth and the End of Inquiry* (1991). Defends a Peircean pragmatism: truth is what would be agreed to by an ideal scientific community at the end of inquiry—a rigorous, non-relativistic form of pragmatism.
