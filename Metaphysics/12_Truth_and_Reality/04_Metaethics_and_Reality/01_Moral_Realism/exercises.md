# Exercises: Moral Realism

1. **The open question argument.** State Moore's open question argument precisely. Does it show that "good" is indefinable, or only that it is not synonymous with any particular natural predicate? Evaluate whether Cornell realism (empirical reduction without synonym) escapes the argument.

2. **Moral explanation.** Sturgeon argues that moral facts (like the fact that Hitler was morally depraved) are needed to explain non-moral facts (like the Holocaust). Evaluate this argument. Does moral explanation require moral realism?

3. **Supervenience.** Moral properties supervene on natural properties: no moral difference without a natural difference. Is supervenience compatible with non-naturalism? What explains the supervenience relation if moral properties are not natural?

4. **Parfit's convergence.** Parfit argues that consequentialism, Kantianism, and contractualism converge on the same normative conclusions, which he takes as evidence for moral objectivity. Evaluate this argument. Does convergence among theories imply that there are moral facts they are all tracking?

5. **Non-natural properties.** Moore says "good" is a non-natural, indefinable property. What does it mean for a property to be "non-natural"? How would we come to know facts about non-natural properties?

6. **Moral progress.** We treat the abolition of slavery as moral progress—a change toward the morally correct view. How does moral realism explain moral progress? Can anti-realism give an equally good account?
