# Important Figures: Moral Realism

**G. E. Moore (1873–1958)** — *Principia Ethica* (1903): "good" is a simple non-natural property; open question argument against naturalism; intuitionism.

**W. D. Ross (1877–1971)** — *The Right and the Good* (1930): prima facie duties as self-evident moral truths; pluralistic non-naturalism.

**Peter Railton (b. 1950)** — Sophisticated consequentialist naturalism; "Moral Realism" (1986) in *Philosophical Review* is a key paper.

**Nicholas Sturgeon (b. 1942)** — Cornell realism; defended the explanatory role of moral facts in explaining moral beliefs.

**Russ Shafer-Landau (b. 1963)** — *Moral Realism: A Defence* (2003); non-naturalist realism.

**Derek Parfit (1942–2017)** — *On What Matters* (2011); argued for irreducibly normative truths; defended realism against anti-realist alternatives.

**T. M. Scanlon (b. 1940)** — Contractualism as a form of moral realism grounded in what we owe to each other; *What We Owe to Each Other* (1998).
