# References and Primary Sources: Moral Realism

## Foundational Texts

- Moore, G.E., *Principia Ethica* (1903). The classic non-naturalist moral realism: goodness is a simple, indefinable, non-natural property; the naturalistic fallacy is committed by any attempt to define good in natural terms.

- Parfit, Derek, *On What Matters*, Volume III (2011). Defends non-naturalist moral realism: there are objective normative truths that are true independently of what anyone believes, desires, or has approved.

- Shafer-Landau, Russ, *Moral Realism: A Defence* (2003). A systematic contemporary defense of non-naturalist moral realism; argues that moral properties are sui generis—neither reducible to natural properties nor causally inert.

## Contemporary Works

- Enoch, David, *Taking Morality Seriously: A Defense of Robust Realism* (2011). Argues for "robust realism": there are objective moral facts that are fundamental and irreducible to natural facts; their existence explains the phenomenon of deliberation.

- Railton, Peter, "Moral Realism," *Philosophical Review* 95 (1986). Defends naturalist moral realism: moral properties are natural properties (roughly, what promotes objective welfare); moral facts are naturalistically accessible.
