# Anti-Realism in Ethics

Moral anti-realism is the denial that there are objective, mind-independent moral facts. The anti-realist holds that moral claims do not describe features of the world that would exist whether or not anyone had moral attitudes; rather, moral claims express attitudes, or are true only relative to practices or standards that are themselves not objectively determined.

Error theory (associated with Mackie) is the most radical form of anti-realism: moral claims purport to describe objective moral facts, but there are no such facts, so all moral claims are false. When we say "Cruelty is wrong," we are genuinely claiming that cruelty has an objective property (wrongness) that is part of the fabric of reality; but there is no such property, and the claim is false. Error theory preserves the logical structure of moral discourse (moral claims genuinely express propositions, have truth-conditions) while denying that any of them are true.

Expressivism (emotivism, prescriptivism, and its more sophisticated contemporary descendants) holds that moral claims do not express propositions at all. "Cruelty is wrong" does not describe a state of affairs; it expresses a negative attitude toward cruelty, or prescribes avoiding cruelty, or commends not performing it. Moral language has a distinctive use — not describing but expressing and guiding — and it is a mistake to take it as making fact-stating claims.

Contemporary expressivism (Blackburn's "quasi-realism," Gibbard's norm-expressivism) is more sophisticated: it attempts to vindicate the surface grammar of moral claims — their apparent truth-aptness, their logical behavior in arguments, their role in conditional claims — without committing to the existence of moral facts. This "quasi-realism" shows how an expressivist can earn the right to use truth-apt moral language without accepting the metaphysical commitments of moral realism.

This section examines moral anti-realism — error theory, expressivism, and quasi-realism — with attention to the motivations for each position and the challenges they face.
