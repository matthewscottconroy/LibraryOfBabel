# Exercises: Anti-Realism in Ethics

1. **The Frege-Geach problem.** State the problem precisely. Why is "if lying is wrong, then getting your brother to lie is wrong" a problem for simple emotivism? Evaluate Blackburn's quasi-realist response.

2. **Mackie's arguments.** State the argument from queerness and the argument from relativity as precisely as you can. Which is stronger? Does the parallel with mathematical objects undermine the argument from queerness?

3. **Error theory vs. expressivism.** Both error theory and expressivism are anti-realist. What is the key difference? Which is more revisionary of ordinary moral practice? Is the ordinary moral practitioner an implicit error theorist or implicit expressivist?

4. **Quasi-realism's bootstrapping problem.** Critics say quasi-realism just reinvents moral realism from expressive materials—by the end of the project, we have moral truths. Is this a problem or a success for quasi-realism? What is Blackburn's response?

5. **Moral motivation.** Expressivism connects moral judgment to motivation: if "wrong" expresses disapproval, then judging something wrong is already motivating. Does moral realism have a comparable account of moral motivation? Evaluate the internalism/externalism debate.

6. **Fictionalism.** A moral fictionalist says moral discourse is useful fiction—we know that moral claims are false, but we maintain the practice because it serves important social functions. Is this a stable position? Is there a difference between moral fictionalism and error theory?
