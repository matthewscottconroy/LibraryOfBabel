# Important Figures: Anti-Realism in Ethics

**David Hume (1711–1776)** — Precursor; the is-ought gap; "reason is the slave of the passions"; sentimentalism.

**A. J. Ayer (1910–1989)** — Emotivism in *Language, Truth and Logic* (1936): moral sentences are "ejaculations."

**Charles Stevenson (1908–1979)** — Refined emotivism in *Ethics and Language* (1944): emotive and descriptive meaning.

**J. L. Mackie (1917–1981)** — Error theory in *Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong* (1977): moral claims are uniformly false.

**Simon Blackburn (b. 1944)** — Quasi-realism; *Spreading the Word* (1984), *Essays in Quasi-Realism* (1993).

**Allan Gibbard (b. 1942)** — Norm-expressivism; *Wise Choices, Apt Feelings* (1990), *Thinking How to Live* (2003).

**Richard Joyce (b. 1966)** — Contemporary error theory; *The Myth of Morality* (2001).
