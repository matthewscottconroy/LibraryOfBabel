# Exercises: Aesthetics and Reality

1. **Hume's standard of taste.** Hume tries to maintain both that taste is subjective and that there is a standard by which some judgments are better than others. Is this coherent? Who counts as the "ideal judge"?

2. **Kant on aesthetic judgment.** Kant says aesthetic judgments claim universal validity but are not based on concepts. What does this mean? How does it differ from both realism (beauty is an objective property) and subjectivism (beauty is purely a matter of taste)?

3. **The argument from disagreement.** There is widespread disagreement about what is beautiful. Does this count against aesthetic realism? Compare with moral realism, where a similar argument is used. Is the analogy apt?

4. **Response-dependence.** "X is beautiful" means "x would produce aesthetic pleasure in a suitably idealized observer." What is the ideal observer? Can this be specified without circularity?

5. **Sibley's aesthetic concepts.** Frank Sibley argued that aesthetic concepts (graceful, garish, unified, powerful) are not condition-governed—no list of non-aesthetic features is sufficient to apply them. What does this imply for aesthetic realism? For aesthetic anti-realism?

6. **Art and beauty.** Much contemporary art is deliberately ugly, banal, or transgressive. Does this pose a problem for aesthetic realism? Is beauty the central aesthetic property, or is it one among many?
