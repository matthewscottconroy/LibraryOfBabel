# Important Terms and Ideas: Aesthetics and Reality

**Aesthetic Realism**
The view that there are objective aesthetic facts—that a painting really is beautiful or sublime, that a symphony really is profound, independently of what any observer thinks. Aesthetic properties are real properties of objects, not projections of subjective responses.

**Aesthetic Anti-Realism / Subjectivism**
The view that aesthetic properties are not objective features of objects but are constituted by the responses of perceivers. "Beautiful" expresses a favorable aesthetic response; there is no beauty in the object independently of its being found beautiful.

**Aesthetic Response-Dependence**
The view that aesthetic properties are real but constituted by the responses of suitably qualified observers: something is beautiful if and only if suitably sensitive, attentive, and informed observers respond to it with the appropriate positive aesthetic response. This is a middle path between full objectivism and subjectivism.

**The Antinomy of Taste (Kant)**
Kant's puzzle: aesthetic judgments claim universal agreement ("This is beautiful" implies everyone should find it so), yet they are grounded in subjective feeling (there is no concept that compels the judgment). This antinomy is resolved by positing the "free play" of imagination and understanding.

**Aesthetic Concepts (Sibley)**
Terms like "graceful," "elegant," "garish," "unified" that apply to artworks and natural objects based on their aesthetic character. Sibley argued these concepts require taste for their application: no set of non-aesthetic conditions is sufficient to guarantee the application of aesthetic terms.

**The Supervenience of Aesthetic Properties**
Aesthetic properties supervene on non-aesthetic (physical, structural) properties: two objects that are exactly alike in all non-aesthetic respects must be exactly alike in all aesthetic respects. This supervenience claim is consistent with both realism and anti-realism but poses constraints on how aesthetic properties can be understood.
