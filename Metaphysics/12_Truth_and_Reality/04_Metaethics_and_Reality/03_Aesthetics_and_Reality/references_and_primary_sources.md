# References and Primary Sources: Aesthetics and Reality

## Foundational Texts

- Hume, David, "Of the Standard of Taste," in *Essays, Moral, Political and Literary* (1757). A foundational text on aesthetic judgment: while taste varies, there is a standard derivable from the responses of ideal critics with the right sensibility and conditions.

- Kant, Immanuel, *Critique of Judgment* (1790). Kant's aesthetics: judgments of beauty make a claim to universal agreement while being grounded in subjective feeling; this "subjective universality" is the distinctive feature of aesthetic judgment.

- Sibley, Frank, "Aesthetic Concepts," *Philosophical Review* 68 (1959). Introduces aesthetic concepts—terms like "graceful," "balanced," "garish"—and examines how they apply: they require taste for their application, unlike non-aesthetic terms.

## Contemporary Works

- Railton, Peter, "Aesthetic Value, Moral Value, and the Ambitions of Naturalism," in *Aesthetics and Ethics* edited by Levinson (1998). Examines the parallel between aesthetic and moral realism; argues for a naturalistic account of aesthetic value.

- Stecker, Robert, *Aesthetics and the Philosophy of Art* (2010). A comprehensive introduction to the main positions in aesthetics, including realism and anti-realism about aesthetic properties.
