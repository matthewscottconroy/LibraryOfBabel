# Exercises: Cosmological Arguments

1. **Kalam vs. Leibnizian.** What is the key structural difference between the Kalam and the Leibnizian cosmological argument? Which requires a temporal beginning of the universe, and which does not? Which is more vulnerable to the findings of modern physics?

2. **The PSR.** The Principle of Sufficient Reason says everything has an explanation. Is this principle true? Can you think of cases where it seems to fail? Is a weakened version—every contingent fact has an explanation—sufficient to run the Leibnizian argument?

3. **The Fallacy of Composition.** Hume argues that even if each contingent thing has a cause, it doesn't follow that the collection of all contingent things has a cause. Evaluate this objection. Is "why is there something rather than nothing" a legitimate question?

4. **Necessary being.** What does it mean for a being to be "necessary"? Compare logical necessity (non-existence is self-contradictory) with metaphysical necessity (non-existence is impossible in all possible worlds). Which kind of necessity does the cosmological argument require?

5. **The Taxicab problem.** If everything requires a cause, why doesn't God? If God is exempt, why can't the universe be exempt? What principled distinction allows the theist to stop the regress at God?

6. **Scientific cosmology.** The Big Bang and the Borde-Guth-Vilenkin theorem suggest the universe had a beginning. Does this support the Kalam's second premise? What would a physicist say about "before the Big Bang"?
