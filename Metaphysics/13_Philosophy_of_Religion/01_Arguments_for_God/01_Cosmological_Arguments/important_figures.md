# Important Figures: Cosmological Arguments

**al-Kindi (c. 801–873)** — Early Islamic philosopher who developed a cosmological argument: the universe is finite in time and therefore has a cause.

**al-Ghazali (1058–1111)** — *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*: defended a Kalam cosmological argument; denied the eternity of the world.

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — Five Ways in the *Summa Theologiae*: the argument from motion, from causation, from contingency (Third Way), from degrees of perfection, and from governance.

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)** — "Why is there something rather than nothing?"; the Principle of Sufficient Reason as the basis for the cosmological argument.

**Samuel Clarke (1675–1729)** — *A Demonstration of the Being and Attributes of God* (1705): systematic Leibnizian cosmological argument.

**David Hume (1711–1776)** — *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* (1779): powerful critique of all cosmological arguments including the Fallacy of Composition.

**William Lane Craig (b. 1949)** — Contemporary champion of the Kalam; *The Kalam Cosmological Argument* (1979).

**Alexander Pruss (b. 1973) and Robert Koons (b. 1957)** — Contemporary defenders of Leibnizian arguments with weakened PSR.
