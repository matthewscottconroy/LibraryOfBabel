# Important Terms and Ideas: Cosmological Arguments

**Cosmological Argument**
A family of arguments for God's existence that proceed from the existence or nature of the cosmos. The common structure: the universe (or motion, causation, or contingency within it) requires an explanation; the ultimate explanation must be something that is self-explanatory or necessary; this is God.

**First Cause**
Aquinas's argument from efficient causation: there is no infinite regress of causes; there must be a first cause that is itself uncaused—the unmoved mover or uncaused cause. This first cause is what everyone calls God.

**Argument from Contingency**
The third of Aquinas's ways: contingent beings depend on other beings for their existence; a chain of contingent beings cannot be infinite; there must be a necessary being—one that cannot not exist—on which everything else depends.

**The Principle of Sufficient Reason (PSR)**
The principle that every fact or being has a sufficient reason for its existence or character. Leibniz applies this cosmologically: the universe and every fact within it must have a sufficient reason, which cannot be found within the contingent universe itself but only in a necessary being outside it.

**The Kalām Argument**
The argument: (1) Everything that begins to exist has a cause; (2) The universe began to exist; (3) Therefore, the universe has a cause. Craig argues that this cause must be uncaused, changeless, timeless, and immensely powerful—and since the cause of time must be personal (able to act without a prior temporal cause), it is God.

**Necessary Being**
A being whose non-existence is impossible—one that could not fail to exist. The cosmological argument requires that God is a necessary being: God's existence explains the contingent universe, but God's own existence requires no further explanation because God exists necessarily.
