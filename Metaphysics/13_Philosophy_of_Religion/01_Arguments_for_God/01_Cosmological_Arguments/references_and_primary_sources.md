# References and Primary Sources: Cosmological Arguments

## Foundational Texts

- Aquinas, Thomas, *Summa Theologica* (1265–74), Part I, Question 2, Article 3. The Five Ways; the first three are versions of cosmological arguments from motion, causation, and contingency. The third way (argument from contingency) is the most metaphysically sophisticated.

- Leibniz, G.W., "On the Ultimate Origination of Things" (1697). The Principle of Sufficient Reason applied cosmologically: why is there something rather than nothing? The world's existence requires a sufficient reason external to it—God as the necessary being.

- Craig, William Lane, *The Kalām Cosmological Argument* (1979). Revives the medieval kalām argument: everything that begins to exist has a cause; the universe began to exist; therefore the universe has a cause—which must be uncaused, timeless, and personal.

## Contemporary Works

- Pruss, Alexander, *The Principle of Sufficient Reason: A Reassessment* (2006). A rigorous defense of the Principle of Sufficient Reason and its application to cosmological arguments.

- Sobel, Jordan Howard, *Logic and Theism* (2004). A critical examination of the major arguments for and against God's existence, including detailed analysis of cosmological arguments.
