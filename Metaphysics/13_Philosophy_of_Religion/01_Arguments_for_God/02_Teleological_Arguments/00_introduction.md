# Teleological Arguments for God's Existence

The world appears to exhibit design. Living organisms are extraordinarily complex, and their complexity serves their survival and reproduction in ways that suggest planning. The eye is an almost miraculously efficient optical instrument; the immune system is a sophisticated defensive system; the genetic code is an information storage and retrieval system of unparalleled elegance. These features of the natural world have led many to infer that the world was designed by an intelligent creator — God.

Paley's watchmaker argument is the classical formulation. If you found a watch on a heath, you would infer that it had a designer — the complex, functional organization of its parts, each contributing to the watch's function, could not plausibly have arisen by chance. Living organisms exhibit the same kind of complex functional organization; therefore, by analogy, they too were designed. The analogy grounds the inference to an intelligent designer.

Hume's Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion contains the most sustained pre-Darwinian critique of the argument. Hume raised: the analogy between organisms and watches is weak; the world is as much like a vegetable growing as like a watch; if we argue from design, we should infer a merely competent designer, not an omnipotent and omniscient one; and the argument at best establishes a designer, not the God of monotheism with all the traditional attributes.

Darwin's theory of natural selection provided a non-design explanation of biological complexity that Hume could only gesture at. Natural selection can produce the appearance of design without any designer: heritable variation plus differential reproduction plus time produces organisms of remarkable complexity and apparent fit to their environments. The neo-Darwinian synthesis makes the argument from biological complexity to design much harder to sustain.

The fine-tuning argument is the contemporary successor to the design argument: the fundamental constants of physics appear to be "finely tuned" for the existence of complex structures and life. If any constant were slightly different, no stars, no chemistry, no life would be possible. Is this fine-tuning best explained by design or by anthropic selection (we can only observe a universe compatible with our existence)?

This section examines teleological arguments, the Darwinian challenge, and the fine-tuning argument.
