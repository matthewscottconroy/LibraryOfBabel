# Exercises: Teleological Arguments

1. **Paley's analogy.** Reconstruct Paley's watchmaker argument as an argument by analogy. Identify the two things being compared, the shared property, and the inferred property. How strong is the analogy?

2. **Darwin's response.** How does natural selection undermine Paley's argument? Does it show that design is false, or only that design is unnecessary? What would Paley have said?

3. **Hume's objections.** Summarize Hume's three main objections to the design argument (weak analogy, imperfect design, regress). Which is most powerful? Does fine-tuning escape any of them?

4. **The multiverse response.** The multiverse hypothesis says that if enough universes exist with varying constants, we should expect to find ourselves in a life-permitting one. Evaluate this as a response to fine-tuning. Is a multiverse hypothesis less explanatory than a designer?

5. **Prediction.** The fine-tuning argument claims that theism better "predicts" fine-tuned constants than naturalism does. Evaluate this claim using Bayes' Theorem. What prior probabilities are relevant?

6. **Intelligent Design.** Proponents of Intelligent Design argue that certain biological systems (e.g., the bacterial flagellum) are "irreducibly complex"—they could not have evolved by natural selection. Evaluate this claim. Is irreducible complexity a scientific argument or a philosophical one?
