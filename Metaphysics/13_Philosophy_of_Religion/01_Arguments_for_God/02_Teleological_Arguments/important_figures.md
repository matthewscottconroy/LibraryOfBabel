# Important Figures: Teleological Arguments

**Plato (428–348 BCE)** — *Timaeus*: the Demiurge as a craftsman who imposes rational order on primordial chaos; early teleological thinking.

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — Fifth Way: things without understanding act for an end (like an arrow guided by an archer); governance argument.

**William Paley (1743–1805)** — *Natural Theology* (1802): the watchmaker analogy; detailed enumeration of biological design features.

**David Hume (1711–1776)** — *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* (1779): classic critique of the analogical design argument.

**Charles Darwin (1809–1882)** — *On the Origin of Species* (1859): natural selection as an alternative explanation for biological complexity; undermined Paley's argument.

**Robin Collins** — Contemporary defender of the fine-tuning argument; "The Teleological Argument" in *The Blackwell Companion to Natural Theology*.

**Richard Swinburne (b. 1934)** — Probabilistic version of the design argument in *The Existence of God* (1979).

**Richard Dawkins (b. 1941)** — *The Blind Watchmaker* (1986): evolution as the explanation of apparent biological design without a designer.
