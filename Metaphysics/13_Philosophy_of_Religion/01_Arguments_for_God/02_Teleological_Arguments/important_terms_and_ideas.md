# Important Terms and Ideas: Teleological Arguments

**Teleological Argument (Argument from Design)**
An argument for God's existence from the appearance of purpose, order, or design in the universe or in living organisms. The structure: the universe exhibits features (complexity, fine-tuning, biological adaptation) that are best explained by intentional design; the designer is God.

**The Watchmaker Analogy**
Paley's analogy: just as finding a watch on a heath implies a designer (because of its complex, purposive structure), finding complex biological organisms implies a divine designer. The functional complexity of eyes, wings, and hearts cannot arise by chance.

**Natural Selection (Darwinian Response)**
Darwin's challenge to the teleological argument: biological complexity and adaptation can be explained by natural selection without invoking a designer. Apparent purposiveness in nature is the result of differential survival and reproduction over vast time spans—no intelligence is needed.

**The Fine-Tuning Argument**
A modern teleological argument: the fundamental constants of physics (the strength of gravity, the mass of the electron, etc.) are fine-tuned for the existence of life; any slight variation would have made life impossible. The fine-tuning is best explained by design rather than chance.

**The Anthropic Principle**
The observation that the universe must have the conditions necessary for our existence (since we are here to observe it). Atheists use this to explain fine-tuning without design: given the many possible universes (multiverse), we necessarily find ourselves in one compatible with life.

**Hume's Objections**
Hume's critique: (1) The analogy between the universe and an artifact is weak—the universe is not enough like a machine to infer a designer by analogy; (2) The design hypothesis does not explain everything—who designed the designer?; (3) Other hypotheses (generation, necessity) are equally supported by the evidence.
