# References and Primary Sources: Teleological Arguments

## Foundational Texts

- Aquinas, Thomas, *Summa Theologica*, Part I, Question 2, Article 3, Fifth Way. The argument from governance: things lacking intelligence act for ends; this requires an intelligent director—God as the ultimate explanation of natural teleology.

- Paley, William, *Natural Theology* (1802). The watchmaker analogy: just as finding a watch implies a designer, the complexity and adaptation of organisms imply a divine designer. Paley's argument targets biological complexity.

- Hume, David, *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* (1779). The classic critique of the teleological argument through the character Philo; argues the analogy between nature and a machine is weak, design explains nothing without explaining the designer, and random processes could produce apparent order.

## Contemporary Works

- Swinburne, Richard, *The Existence of God* (2nd ed. 2004). Defends a probabilistic teleological argument: the fine-tuning of the universe's constants for life is best explained by theism, which confers higher probability on this evidence than does atheism.

- Dembski, William, *The Design Inference* (1998). Develops a formal criterion for detecting design: small probability combined with an independently specifiable pattern indicates design. A version of intelligent design applied to biological systems.
