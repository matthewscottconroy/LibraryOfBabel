# Important Figures: Ontological Arguments

**Anselm of Canterbury (1033–1109)** — *Proslogion* (c. 1078): "that than which nothing greater can be conceived"; the original ontological argument.

**Gaunilo of Marmoutiers (c. 994–c. 1083)** — "On Behalf of the Fool": the "perfect island" parody; immediate contemporary critique.

**René Descartes (1596–1650)** — *Meditations* (1641): Fifth Meditation version; existence belongs to the essence of a perfect being as three angles belong to a triangle's essence.

**Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)** — *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781): "existence is not a predicate"; classic objection.

**Charles Hartshorne (1897–2000)** — Modal version: necessary existence as a perfection; *Man's Vision of God* (1941).

**Norman Malcolm (1911–1990)** — "Anselm's Ontological Arguments" (1960): distinguished two Anselmian arguments; defended the modal version.

**Alvin Plantinga (b. 1932)** — *The Nature of Necessity* (1974): Plantinga's modal version in S5; showed the argument is formally valid.
