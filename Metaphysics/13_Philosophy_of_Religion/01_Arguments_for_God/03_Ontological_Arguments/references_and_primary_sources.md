# References and Primary Sources: Ontological Arguments

## Foundational Texts

- Anselm of Canterbury, *Proslogion* (1077–78). The original ontological argument: God is "that than which nothing greater can be conceived"; such a being must exist in reality as well as in the understanding, since existence in reality is greater than existence in the understanding alone.

- Descartes, René, *Meditations on First Philosophy*, Fifth Meditation (1641). A Cartesian ontological argument: existence is a perfection; God is supremely perfect; therefore God necessarily exists. Existence is part of God's essence.

- Plantinga, Alvin, *The Nature of Necessity* (1974), Chapter X. The modal ontological argument: a maximally great being is possibly actual; a maximally great being is necessarily actual if actual; therefore a maximally great being is actual.

## Contemporary Works

- Oppy, Graham, *Ontological Arguments and Belief in God* (1995). The most comprehensive critical survey of all versions of the ontological argument; argues that no version succeeds.

- Sobel, Jordan Howard, *Logic and Theism* (2004). A rigorous logical analysis of classical and modal ontological arguments; presents formal objections to each version.
