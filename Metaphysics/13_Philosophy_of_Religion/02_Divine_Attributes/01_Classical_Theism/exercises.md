# Exercises: Classical Divine Attributes

1. **Paradox of omnipotence.** State the paradox of the stone precisely. Evaluate the response that "create a stone too heavy for God to lift" is not a coherent task. Is this response satisfying?

2. **Foreknowledge and freedom.** Reconstruct the incompatibilist argument that divine foreknowledge is incompatible with human freedom. Which response (compatibilism, Molinism, open theism, timelessness) do you find most compelling?

3. **The Euthyphro dilemma.** Is the dilemma genuine? Evaluate the "third option"—that God's nature is goodness—as a way out. Does this make morality arbitrary, external to God, or neither?

4. **Omniscience and future contingents.** If there is no truth now about what I will freely do tomorrow, does God's omniscience require knowing future free actions? Or is not knowing undetermined truths compatible with omniscience?

5. **Moral perfection.** Can God sin? The traditional answer is no—but if God *cannot* sin, is God free? Does moral perfection require the freedom to do wrong?

6. **Maximal greatness.** Anselm defines God as the greatest conceivable being. Is "greatest" a coherent concept? Could there be a being greater than any given great being?
