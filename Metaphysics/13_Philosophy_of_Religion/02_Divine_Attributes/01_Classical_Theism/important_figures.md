# Important Figures: Classical Theism and Divine Attributes

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — The *Summa Theologiae* gives the most systematic medieval account of omnipotence, omniscience, and omnibenevolence.

**Anselm of Canterbury (1033–1109)** — "That than which nothing greater can be conceived" grounds the analysis of divine greatness-making properties.

**Maimonides (1138–1204)** — *Guide for the Perplexed*: apophatic theology—God's attributes must be understood negatively to preserve divine simplicity.

**J. L. Mackie (1917–1981)** — *The Miracle of Theism* (1982): systematic critique of arguments for and attributes of God.

**Alvin Plantinga (b. 1932)** — *Does God Have a Nature?* (1980): critique of divine simplicity; *God, Freedom, and Evil* (1974): on omnipotence and the problem of evil.

**Richard Swinburne (b. 1934)** — *The Coherence of Theism* (1977): rigorous analytic account of divine attributes.

**Peter Geach (1916–2013)** — Distinguished "God is omnipotent" from "God can do anything"; articulated the standard response to the paradox of the stone.
