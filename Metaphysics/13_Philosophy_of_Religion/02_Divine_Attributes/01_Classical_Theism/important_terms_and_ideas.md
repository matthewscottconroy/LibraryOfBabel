# Important Terms and Ideas: Classical Theism

**Classical Theism**
The view that God is omnipotent (all-powerful), omniscient (all-knowing), perfectly good, eternal, and the creator of everything from nothing. God is also typically held to be simple, immutable, impassible, and necessarily existent. This is the theism of Anselm, Aquinas, and the mainstream of Western philosophical theology.

**Omnipotence**
God's attribute of being all-powerful: God can do anything that is logically possible. Classic puzzles: can God create a stone so heavy God cannot lift it? Can God do evil? Responses typically restrict omnipotence to logically possible actions and argue that the ability to do evil would reflect a deficiency, not a power.

**Omniscience**
God's attribute of knowing all truths. Problems: does omniscience include foreknowledge of free choices? Knowing what it is like to sin? Knowing indexical truths ("It is now 3pm")? Middle knowledge (God's knowledge of counterfactuals of freedom—what any free creature would do in any circumstances)?

**Perfect Goodness**
God's being morally perfect—the source and standard of all goodness. The Euthyphro dilemma: Is something good because God commands it (divine command theory), or does God command it because it is good? Either horn seems problematic for classical theism.

**Aseity**
God's property of being self-existent—existing in virtue of God's own nature, not in virtue of anything external to God. God is not caused by or dependent on anything else; all other things depend on God, but God depends on nothing.

**Impassibility**
The divine attribute of being unaffected by external things—not subject to suffering, emotion, or change caused by creatures. Classical theism holds God is impassible; open theism and process theology deny this, arguing God is genuinely responsive to creation.
