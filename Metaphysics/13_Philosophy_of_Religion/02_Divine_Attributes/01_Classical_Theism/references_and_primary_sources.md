# References and Primary Sources: Classical Theism

## Foundational Texts

- Aquinas, Thomas, *Summa Theologica*, Part I, Questions 3–13. The via negativa and the positive attributes of God; argues for divine simplicity, immutability, eternity, omnipotence, omniscience, and perfect goodness.

- Anselm of Canterbury, *Monologion* (1076). An extended argument for God's existence and attributes by reflection on what is absolutely greatest; establishes the perfection attributes through reason alone.

- Swinburne, Richard, *The Coherence of Theism* (1977). A systematic analytical examination of the major divine attributes; argues that the classical theist conception of God is coherent, defending omnipotence, omniscience, perfect goodness, and necessary existence.

## Contemporary Works

- Davies, Brian, *An Introduction to the Philosophy of Religion* (4th ed. 2011). A clear introduction to the philosophy of religion including coverage of classical theism and its attributes.

- Leftow, Brian, *God and Necessity* (2012). A rigorous treatment of divine necessity and the modal attributes of God from a classical theist perspective.
