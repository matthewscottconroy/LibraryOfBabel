# Exercises: Divine Eternity

1. **Boethius's eternity.** Boethius says God possesses "simultaneously and completely" what temporal beings possess only partially and successively. Explain what this means. Does it make sense to say that God is "co-present" with all of history at once?

2. **Timelessness vs. everlastingness.** What are the main arguments for God being timeless rather than everlasting? What are the main arguments for everlastingness? Which do you find more persuasive, and why?

3. **Foreknowledge and the eternal present.** Boethius argues that God's timeless knowledge of your action is not *prior* to your action, so it doesn't predetermine it. Evaluate this response. Does the temporal/atemporal distinction do the required work?

4. **Divine action.** If God is timeless and immutable, how can God act in history—answer prayers, work miracles, become incarnate? Is a timeless God capable of genuine historical involvement?

5. **Open theism.** Clark Pinnock and others argue that God voluntarily limits foreknowledge of future free actions to preserve human freedom. Evaluate this move. Is a God who lacks foreknowledge of some future events still omniscient?

6. **Relativity.** Special relativity holds that simultaneity is relative to reference frame—there is no absolute "present." Does this pose a problem for the doctrine of timelessness? Or for everlastingness?
