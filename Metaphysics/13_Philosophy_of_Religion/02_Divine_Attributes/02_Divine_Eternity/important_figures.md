# Important Figures: Divine Eternity

**Boethius (c. 477–524)** — *The Consolation of Philosophy*, Book V: classic formulation of divine timelessness as "simultaneous and perfect possession of unlimited life."

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — *Summa Theologiae* Ia, q. 10: developed Boethius's account of eternity and distinguished it from everlastingness (*sempiternity*).

**Augustine of Hippo (354–430)** — *Confessions* Book XI: time is a creature; God exists in an eternal present; anticipates later timelessness doctrines.

**Brian Leftow (b. 1956)** — *Time and Eternity* (1991): the most rigorous contemporary defense of divine timelessness.

**Paul Helm (b. 1940)** — *Eternal God* (1988): defends timelessness and explores its implications for prayer, providence, and incarnation.

**Nicholas Wolterstorff (b. 1932)** — *"God Everlasting"* (1975): influential defense of divine temporality (everlastingness) over timelessness.

**William Hasker (b. 1935)** — *God, Time, and Knowledge* (1989): defends open theism; argues timelessness is incoherent.
