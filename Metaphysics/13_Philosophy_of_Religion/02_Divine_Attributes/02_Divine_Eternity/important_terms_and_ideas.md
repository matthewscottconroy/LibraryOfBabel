# Important Terms and Ideas: Divine Eternity

**Divine Eternity**
God's relationship to time. Two main views: (1) Timelessness (Boethius, Aquinas): God is outside time altogether, existing in an eternal present. (2) Everlastingness (Swinburne): God exists in time but without beginning or end. The debate concerns which view best coheres with divine attributes.

**Timelessness**
The classical view: God does not exist in time, has no past, present, or future, and is not subject to temporal succession. God's eternal "now" encompasses all of time at once. This preserves divine immutability and is associated with divine simplicity.

**Everlastingness**
The alternative: God exists through all of time, with no beginning and no end, but is genuinely temporal—God is present at each moment, acts in history, and knows what time it now is. This view is more compatible with personal interaction between God and creatures.

**The Nunc Stans**
Boethius's term for God's "eternal now"—the atemporal present in which God apprehends all of time simultaneously. Just as a viewer from a height can see a road ahead and behind simultaneously, God sees all of time from an eternal perspective.

**Foreknowledge and Free Will**
The problem: if God timelessly knows that I will do X tomorrow, does this make my doing X necessary and thus not free? Timelessness defends foreknowledge by denying it is "fore-knowledge"—God does not know the future before it occurs but sees it timelessly. Critics argue this does not solve the problem.

**Open Theism**
The view that God is genuinely temporal, responsive to creaturely choices, and does not have exhaustive foreknowledge of free future decisions. Open theism values divine responsiveness and genuine creaturely freedom at the cost of classical omniscience. Defended by Boyd, Hasker, and Pinnock.
