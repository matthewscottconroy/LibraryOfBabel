# References and Primary Sources: Divine Eternity

## Foundational Texts

- Boethius, *The Consolation of Philosophy* (524 CE), Book V. The classic account of divine eternity as timelessness: God exists in an eternal present that encompasses all of time at once ("nunc stans"), allowing God to foreknow free acts without determining them.

- Anselm of Canterbury, *Monologion* (1076). Defends divine timelessness: God does not exist within time but is the cause of time; God's eternity is not infinite temporal duration but the absence of temporal succession.

- Swinburne, Richard, "God and Time," in *Reasoned Faith* edited by Stump (1993). Defends divine temporality: God exists in time, knows what time it now is, and acts in history; timelessness is incoherent and incompatible with God's being a personal agent.

## Contemporary Works

- Leftow, Brian, *Time and Eternity* (1991). The most thorough philosophical defense of divine timelessness; argues that an atemporal God can know temporal truths and act in the world.

- Padgett, Alan, *God, Eternity and the Nature of Time* (1992). Defends "relative timelessness": God exists in an unlimited time that is not constrained by our measured time; this is a form of eternity that allows divine knowledge and action in the world.
