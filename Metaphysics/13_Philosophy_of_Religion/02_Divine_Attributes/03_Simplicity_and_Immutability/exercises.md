# Exercises: Simplicity and Immutability

1. **Divine simplicity explained.** What does it mean for God to be "simple"? Why does Aquinas think composition implies dependence? Evaluate the inference from divine perfection to divine simplicity.

2. **Plantinga's objection.** Plantinga argues that divine simplicity is incoherent: if God's wisdom = God's power = God, then wisdom = power, which is clearly false. Is this a good objection? How do defenders of simplicity respond?

3. **Immutability and relationship.** If God is immutable, can God genuinely respond to prayer? Evaluate the distinction between God's essential nature (immutable) and God's relational properties (can vary). Does this distinction save immutability?

4. **Open theism.** Open theists say God limits foreknowledge to preserve creaturely freedom and enable genuine divine-human relationship. What does this imply for divine sovereignty and providence? Is a God who "takes risks" still worthy of worship?

5. **Process theology.** Whitehead and Hartshorne argue that God is genuinely affected by the world—God has a "consequent nature" shaped by creaturely experience. Is this compatible with divine perfection? What does it gain and lose compared to classical theism?

6. **Simplicity and the Trinity.** Classical theism holds that God is simple; Christian theology holds that God is triune (Father, Son, Holy Spirit). Are these compatible? How have theologians attempted to reconcile them?
