# Important Figures: Simplicity and Immutability

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)** — *Summa Theologiae* Ia, qq. 3–9: the most systematic defense of divine simplicity and immutability in the Western tradition.

**Maimonides (1138–1204)** — *Guide for the Perplexed*: negative theology; divine simplicity requires that all positive attributes be denied or reinterpreted.

**Alvin Plantinga (b. 1932)** — *Does God Have a Nature?* (1980): influential critique of divine simplicity.

**Brian Davies (b. 1951)** — Contemporary Thomist defender of divine simplicity; *An Introduction to the Philosophy of Religion* and *The Thought of Thomas Aquinas*.

**Clark Pinnock (1937–2010)** — Chief architect of open theism; *The Openness of God* (1994, with others).

**John Sanders (b. 1956)** — *The God Who Risks* (1998): full statement of open theist theology.

**Nicholas Wolterstorff (b. 1932)** — Argued for divine changeability in "God Everlasting" (1975).

**Paul Helm (b. 1940)** — Defends classical immutability against open theism.
