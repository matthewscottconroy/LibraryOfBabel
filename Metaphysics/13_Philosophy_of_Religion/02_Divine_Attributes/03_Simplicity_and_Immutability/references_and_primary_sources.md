# References and Primary Sources: Divine Simplicity and Immutability

## Foundational Texts

- Aquinas, Thomas, *Summa Theologica*, Part I, Questions 3 and 9. The doctrine of divine simplicity: God has no parts, no composition of essence and existence, no real distinction between attributes. God's essence is God's existence. Divine immutability follows from simplicity.

- Kretzmann, Norman and Stump, Eleonore, "Absolute Simplicity," *Faith and Philosophy* 2 (1985). A defense of the classical doctrine of divine simplicity; argues that the doctrine is coherent and essential to a theism that identifies God with the ultimate ground of being.

- Plantinga, Alvin, *Does God Have a Nature?* (1980). A critique of divine simplicity: the doctrine implies that God and God's properties are identical, and that all of God's properties are necessary—but this seems to eliminate contingent divine properties and raises problems about God's freedom.

## Contemporary Works

- Dolezal, James, *God Without Parts: Divine Simplicity and the Metaphysics of God's Absoluteness* (2011). A systematic defense of divine simplicity from a classical theist perspective.

- Brower, Jeffrey, "Simplicity and Aseity," in *The Oxford Handbook of Philosophical Theology* edited by Flint and Rea (2009). A careful defense of a version of divine simplicity that responds to Plantinga's objections.
