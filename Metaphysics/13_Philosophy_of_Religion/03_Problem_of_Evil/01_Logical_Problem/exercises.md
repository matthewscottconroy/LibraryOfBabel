# Exercises: The Logical Problem of Evil

1. **Mackie's argument.** State Mackie's logical argument from evil in valid form. Is the argument formally valid? Which premise is most plausible, and which is most questionable?

2. **The free will defense.** Explain Plantinga's transworld depravity concept. How does it defuse the logical problem? Does it show that the conjunction (God exists, evil exists) is *possible*, or that it is *likely*?

3. **Moral evil vs. natural evil.** Does the free will defense apply to natural evil? Evaluate Plantinga's suggestion that natural evil might result from free angelic beings. Is this a plausible theodicy?

4. **Counterfactuals of freedom.** Plantinga's defense depends on counterfactuals like "if God had created free being X, X would have sinned." Are such counterfactuals true or false? Does their truth require determinism?

5. **Greater goods.** Some argue that a world with both virtue and vice is better than a world with virtue alone, because virtue has more value when exercised in the face of temptation and suffering. Evaluate this "greater good" response to moral evil.

6. **Plantinga vs. Hick.** Compare Plantinga's free will defense with Hick's soul-making theodicy. Which better handles: (a) moral evil, (b) natural evil, (c) the suffering of innocent children?
