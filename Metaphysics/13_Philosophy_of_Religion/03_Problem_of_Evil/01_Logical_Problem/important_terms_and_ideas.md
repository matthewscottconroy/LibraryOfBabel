# Important Terms and Ideas: The Logical Problem of Evil

**The Logical Problem of Evil**
The argument that the existence of an omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good God is logically incompatible with the existence of evil. If God exists with these attributes, God would prevent all evil; but evil exists; therefore such a God does not exist.

**Mackie's Inconsistent Triad**
The three propositions held to be jointly inconsistent: (1) God is omnipotent; (2) God is wholly good; (3) Evil exists. Mackie argues that given (1) and (2), God would eliminate all evil, so (3) follows only if God does not exist. Theists must show that (1), (2), and (3) can all be true.

**The Free Will Defense**
Plantinga's response: it is possibly the case that God cannot create free beings who always freely choose correctly. If this is possible, then it is possible for God to exist, create free beings, and have those beings produce evil—without any inconsistency in the original triad.

**Transworld Depravity**
Plantinga's concept: a being suffers from transworld depravity if, in every possible world where God creates that being with free will, the being performs at least one morally wrong action. If all possible free persons suffer from transworld depravity, God cannot create any free person who always chooses correctly.

**Moral vs. Natural Evil**
Moral evil is caused by free creatures (murder, theft, cruelty). Natural evil is caused by natural processes (disease, earthquakes, animal suffering). The free will defense addresses moral evil but not natural evil; theodicy of natural evil requires different resources (soul-making, greater goods, etc.).

**The G.E. Moore Shift**
Plantinga's reversal: instead of arguing that God exists and evil is compatible, show that the argument is not sound by providing a possible world where God and evil coexist. The burden is not to explain how God and evil coexist but only to show that their coexistence is logically possible.
