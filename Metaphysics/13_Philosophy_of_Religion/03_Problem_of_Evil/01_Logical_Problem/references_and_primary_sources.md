# References and Primary Sources: The Logical Problem of Evil

## Foundational Texts

- Mackie, J.L., "Evil and Omnipotence," *Mind* 64 (1955). The canonical statement of the logical problem: the propositions "God is omnipotent," "God is perfectly good," and "Evil exists" are logically inconsistent; theism is self-contradictory.

- Plantinga, Alvin, *God, Freedom, and Evil* (1974). The definitive response: develops the Free Will Defense—possibly, God cannot create free beings who always choose correctly; therefore, it is not within God's power to create a world with free creatures and no evil. The logical problem of evil is solved.

- Flew, Antony, "Divine Omnipotence and Human Freedom," in *New Essays in Philosophical Theology* edited by Flew and MacIntyre (1955). Presents the logical problem of evil and challenges the free will defense.

## Contemporary Works

- van Inwagen, Peter, *The Problem of Evil* (2006). A comprehensive treatment of both logical and evidential problems of evil; defends modified skeptical theism.

- Howard-Snyder, Daniel (ed.), *The Evidential Argument from Evil* (1996). An anthology covering the transition from logical to evidential problem of evil.
