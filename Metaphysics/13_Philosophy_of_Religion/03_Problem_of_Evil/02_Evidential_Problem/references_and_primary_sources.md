# References and Primary Sources: The Evidential Problem of Evil

## Foundational Texts

- Rowe, William, "The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism," *American Philosophical Quarterly* 16 (1979). The canonical statement: there exist instances of intense suffering that an omnipotent, omniscient being could have prevented without sacrificing any greater good; a perfectly good God would prevent such suffering; therefore no such God exists.

- Wykstra, Stephen, "The Humean Obstacle to Evidential Arguments from Suffering," *International Journal for Philosophy of Religion* 16 (1984). Defends CORNEA (Condition of Reasoned Epistemic Access): we should not expect to see God's reasons for permitting evil, so the absence of apparent reasons is weak evidence against theism.

- Swinburne, Richard, *Providence and the Problem of Evil* (1998). Defends theodicy: human freedom, soul-making, and the development of virtue provide sufficient reasons for God to permit extensive suffering.

## Contemporary Works

- Draper, Paul, "Pain and Pleasure: An Evidential Problem for Theists," *Noûs* 23 (1989). Argues that the distribution of pain and pleasure in the world is much better predicted by the hypothesis of indifference (no God) than by theism.

- van Inwagen, Peter, *The Problem of Evil* (2006). Skeptical theism response: our cognitive limitations make it likely we cannot discern God's reasons for permitting evil; the evidential problem is weaker than it appears.
