# Exercises: Suffering, Hiddenness, and Meaning

1. **Schellenberg's argument.** Reconstruct the hiddenness argument. What does it mean for God to be "perfectly loving"? Is it plausible that a perfectly loving God would ensure that all non-resistant persons have access to relationship with God?

2. **Responses to hiddenness.** Evaluate the response that divine hiddenness preserves human autonomy. Does the "epistemic distance" between God and creatures serve a positive spiritual purpose? Is there an autonomous religious response that an overwhelming divine presence would undermine?

3. **Ivan Karamazov's rebellion.** Ivan accepts that God may exist but refuses to worship a God who permits the suffering of innocents. Is this a philosophical argument or an existential stance? Can philosophy engage with it? How should a theist respond?

4. **Frankl's logotherapy.** Frankl argues that meaning can be found in unavoidable suffering. Does this show that evil is compatible with a good life? Does it provide evidence for or against theism?

5. **Adams's horrendous evils.** Adams argues that evils are "horrendous" when they defeat the good of the sufferer's life—even the most transcendent goods cannot outweigh them. How does she think God can respond to horrendous evils? Is participation in suffering (through incarnation and passion) an adequate response?

6. **Secular meaning.** Can life have deep meaning in the face of radical evil without the resources of theism? Compare Camus's absurdism, Wolf's secular meaning theory, and Frankl's logotherapy as responses to suffering without God.
