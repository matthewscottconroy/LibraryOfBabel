# Important Figures: Suffering, Hiddenness, and Meaning

**J. L. Schellenberg (b. 1959)** — *Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason* (1993): definitive statement of the hiddenness argument; also *The Hiddenness Argument* (2015).

**Victor Frankl (1905–1997)** — *Man's Search for Meaning* (1946): logotherapy; meaning found in suffering without God.

**Marilyn McCord Adams (1943–2017)** — *Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God* (1999): theistic response to the worst evil; divine participation.

**Fyodor Dostoevsky (1821–1881)** — *The Brothers Karamazov* (1879–1880): Ivan Karamazov's rebellion; existential encounter with evil.

**Albert Camus (1913–1960)** — *The Myth of Sisyphus* (1942): absurdism; meaning without transcendence; confronting evil without theism.

**Paul Moser (b. 1957)** — *The Elusive God* (2008): response to hiddenness; God requires "filial knowledge" through transformation, not merely propositional evidence.

**Paul Draper (b. 1957)** — Evidential arguments; "The Skeptical Theist" in debates with Bergmann.
