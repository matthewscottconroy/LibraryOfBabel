# References and Primary Sources: Suffering and Meaning

## Foundational Texts

- Hick, John, *Evil and the God of Love* (1966). The Irenaean theodicy: God creates persons as immature, with potential for growth; suffering and evil are necessary conditions for the development of virtue and the achievement of a relationship with God ("soul-making").

- Swinburne, Richard, *Providence and the Problem of Evil* (1998). Provides a "greater goods" theodicy emphasizing the value of free choice, virtue development, and knowledge of the world as goods that justify God's permission of suffering.

- Kushner, Harold, *When Bad Things Happen to Good People* (1981). A pastoral theological response: Rabbi Kushner, in the wake of his son's death, argues that God is not omnipotent—God is good but not the cause of or able to prevent all suffering.

## Contemporary Works

- Adams, Marilyn McCord, *Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God* (1999). Argues that theodicy must address "horrendous evils"—evils so severe they seem to defeat the meaning of an individual's life. God must be able to make meaning of each individual's life despite horrendous evil.

- Stump, Eleonore, *Wandering in Darkness: Narrative and the Problem of Suffering* (2010). Uses narrative as a vehicle for theodicy; argues that suffering can be integrated into a meaningful life through God's loving engagement with each individual.
