# Exercises: Faith and Reason

1. **Clifford vs. James.** Clifford says it is always wrong to believe anything without sufficient evidence. James says in genuine options with insufficient evidence, we may believe. Who is right? Is religious belief a "genuine option" in James's sense?

2. **Natural theology.** Assess the prospects for natural theology after Hume and Kant. Can the cosmological, teleological, and ontological arguments jointly make theism probable, even if no single argument is conclusive?

3. **Properly basic belief.** Plantinga argues belief in God can be "properly basic"—rational without evidential support. Is this plausible? Is there a principled distinction between belief in God as properly basic and belief in astrology as properly basic?

4. **The sensus divinitatis.** Plantinga invokes Calvin's "sense of the divine" as a faculty that produces theistic belief. If theism is false, this faculty doesn't exist or malfunctions. How does this affect the rationality of theistic belief for someone uncertain about theism's truth?

5. **Barth's fideism.** Karl Barth denied that natural theology provides any knowledge of God—only divine revelation does. Evaluate this position. Is there a coherent epistemology that justifies faith entirely independently of reason?

6. **Pragmatic faith.** Can faith be justified on purely pragmatic grounds—because believing is good for you or for society? Evaluate Pascal's Wager as a pragmatic argument for religious belief.
