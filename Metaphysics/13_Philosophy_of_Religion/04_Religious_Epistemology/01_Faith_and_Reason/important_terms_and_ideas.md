# Important Terms and Ideas: Faith and Reason

**The Relationship between Faith and Reason**
The central question of religious epistemology: Is religious faith rational? Is it in conflict with, independent of, or supported by reason and evidence? Positions range from fideism (faith transcends reason) to natural theology (reason supports faith) to Reformed epistemology (faith is properly basic).

**Natural Theology**
The project of establishing theological truths through reason and natural evidence, without appeal to revelation. The cosmological, teleological, and ontological arguments are exercises in natural theology. Aquinas distinguished natural theology from revealed theology.

**Fideism**
The view that religious faith is not subject to rational justification or critique; faith is an autonomous mode of commitment that transcends reason. Kierkegaard is the paradigmatic fideist: the leap of faith is a passionate embrace of what reason cannot vindicate.

**Reformed Epistemology**
Plantinga's view: belief in God can be "properly basic"—rationally held without being based on arguments or evidence, because it is produced by a reliable cognitive faculty (the sensus divinitatis or sense of the divine). Faith is epistemologically like perceptual belief.

**The Sensus Divinitatis**
Calvin's term, revived by Plantinga: a natural human faculty that, under appropriate conditions, produces belief in God. If this faculty is reliable and functioning properly, the beliefs it produces have warrant—the property that distinguishes knowledge from merely lucky true belief.

**Evidentialism**
The epistemological principle that belief is rational only when it is based on adequate evidence. Applied to religion: it is irrational to believe in God without sufficient evidence. Classical evidentialists (Clifford) demand strict evidence; Reformed epistemologists reject evidentialism as itself an unsupported evidential requirement.
