# References and Primary Sources: Faith and Reason

## Foundational Texts

- Aquinas, Thomas, *Summa Contra Gentiles* (1259–65), Book I, Chapters 1–9. Distinguishes what can be known by reason (natural theology) from what can be known only by faith (revealed theology); both reason and faith are paths to truth, with reason serving as a preamble to faith.

- Kierkegaard, Søren, *Philosophical Fragments* (1844) and *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* (1846). The fideist tradition: Christian faith requires a "leap" beyond what reason can justify; the paradox of the Incarnation cannot be resolved by reason but must be embraced in passion.

- Plantinga, Alvin, *Warranted Christian Belief* (2000). Reformed epistemology: belief in God can be properly basic—not requiring argument or evidence—because it can be produced by a reliable cognitive faculty (sensus divinitatis). Faith and reason are not in conflict.

## Contemporary Works

- Swinburne, Richard, *Faith and Reason* (1981). A systematic examination of the relationship between faith and reason; defends the rationality of religious belief grounded in probabilistic inference.

- Wolterstorff, Nicholas, *Reason within the Bounds of Religion* (1976). Argues that Christian commitment can legitimately shape the theoretical framework within which one evaluates evidence and arguments.
