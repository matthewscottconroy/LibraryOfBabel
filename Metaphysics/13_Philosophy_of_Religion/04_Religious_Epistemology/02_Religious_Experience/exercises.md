# Exercises: Religious Experience

1. **James's typology.** Reconstruct James's four marks of mystical experience (ineffability, noetic quality, transience, passivity). Are these features common to all religious experience, or only to intense mystical states? Is "noetic quality" compatible with ineffability?

2. **Alston's analogy.** Alston argues that perceiving God is epistemically similar to perceiving the external world. Evaluate the analogy. What features of sensory perception does religious experience share? What does it lack?

3. **The diversity objection.** Different traditions report different mystical contents—a personal God, impersonal unity, no-self. Does this diversity undermine the evidential force of religious experience? How might Alston or Stace respond?

4. **Constructivism.** Katz argues that all experience is interpretively structured—there is no "pure" pre-interpretive mystical experience. Is this right? Does it imply that mystical experience cannot provide evidence for any particular conception of ultimate reality?

5. **Naturalistic explanation.** Psychedelic substances can produce experiences phenomenologically similar to religious experiences. Does this undermine the claim that religious experience is veridical? Or can God use pharmacological mechanisms?

6. **The principle of credulity.** Swinburne proposes that if something seems to be the case, we should believe it unless we have specific reasons to doubt it. Does this principle justify belief based on religious experience?
