# References and Primary Sources: Religious Experience

## Foundational Texts

- James, William, *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902). The foundational psychological and philosophical study of religious experience; argues that religious experiences have a noetic quality, are transient, passive, and ineffable, and can provide evidence for religious belief.

- Alston, William, *Perceiving God: The Epistemology of Religious Experience* (1991). Defends the doxastic practice of "mystical perception": forming beliefs about God on the basis of apparent experience of God is as epistemically legitimate as forming beliefs about the physical world on the basis of sense perception.

- Swinburne, Richard, *The Existence of God* (2nd ed. 2004), Chapter 13. Defends the Principle of Credulity: we have prima facie reason to believe that things are as they seem—including seemings to experience God.

## Contemporary Works

- Gellman, Jerome, *Experience of God and the Rationality of Theistic Belief* (1997). Examines the epistemological significance of mystical experiences and their role in justifying theistic belief.

- Taves, Ann, *Religious Experience Reconsidered* (2009). A cognitive science approach to religious experience; examines how experiences come to be designated as religious and what this implies for their epistemic status.
