# Exercises: Pluralism, Exclusivism, Inclusivism

1. **Hick's pluralism.** Explain Hick's distinction between the Real and our concepts of it. How does this Kantian move help him account for religious diversity? What does it mean to say that no positive description of the Real is correct?

2. **The incoherence objection.** If the Real transcends all conceptual categories, can we say anything true about it—even that it is "real" or "ultimate"? Does Hick's pluralism collapse into agnosticism or atheism?

3. **Plantinga's exclusivism.** Plantinga argues that merely knowing others disagree gives me no specific evidence against my own belief. Is this right? Evaluate the analogy with ordinary perceptual disagreement. Does religious disagreement give stronger reasons for revision than ordinary perceptual disagreement?

4. **Anonymous Christians.** Rahner's concept of the anonymous Christian claims that non-Christians can be saved through Christ without knowing it. Is this respectful of non-Christian traditions? Does it undermine the importance of explicit religious commitment?

5. **Comparing traditions.** Hick proposes that the moral criterion—capacity to transform selfishness into Reality-centeredness—distinguishes more from less authentic religious traditions. Evaluate this criterion. Is it a genuine cross-traditional standard, or does it smuggle in liberal Western values?

6. **Religious diversity and religious belief.** Given that billions of people hold different, mutually incompatible religious beliefs, and that no single tradition has obvious epistemic superiority, should a reflective person hold any confident religious belief at all? What does intellectual humility require?
