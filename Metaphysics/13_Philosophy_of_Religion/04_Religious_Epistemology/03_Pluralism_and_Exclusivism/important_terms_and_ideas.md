# Important Terms and Ideas: Pluralism and Exclusivism

**Religious Pluralism**
The view that multiple religious traditions are equally valid paths to ultimate reality or salvation—none has a monopoly on religious truth. Hick's version: the Ultimate Reality is beyond all human conception; the major traditions are all partial, culturally conditioned responses to it.

**Religious Exclusivism**
The view that one's own religion has uniquely correct beliefs about ultimate reality and that salvation or liberation is available only through this tradition. Most traditional forms of Christianity, Islam, and some other traditions have been exclusivist.

**Religious Inclusivism**
The view that while one's own religion has the fullest truth, elements of truth and paths to salvation are found in other religions. Rahner's "anonymous Christians" is a Catholic form: all who respond to divine grace are implicitly Christian even without explicit faith.

**The Problem of Religious Diversity**
The epistemological challenge: given that intelligent, sincere, well-informed people hold radically different and incompatible religious beliefs, what follows for the rationality of any particular religious belief? The diversity of religious belief seems to undermine confidence in any one tradition's exclusive truth claims.

**Epistemic Peer Disagreement**
The issue: if an epistemic peer (someone as intelligent, informed, and fair-minded as oneself) holds incompatible religious beliefs, this is evidence that one's own beliefs may be wrong. Religious diversity creates widespread peer disagreement, which seems to demand either revision or explanation.

**The Soteriological Problem**
The theological version of the diversity problem: if salvation requires specific beliefs or practices, what becomes of those who through no fault of their own are outside the relevant tradition? Exclusivism faces the challenge that vast numbers of sincere people are condemned for circumstantial lack of access to saving truth.
