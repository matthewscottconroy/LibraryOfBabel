# References and Primary Sources: Pluralism and Exclusivism

## Foundational Texts

- Hick, John, *An Interpretation of Religion* (1989). The canonical statement of religious pluralism: the great world religions are different culturally conditioned responses to the same ultimate transcendent reality. None has exclusive access to the truth; all are equally valid paths to salvation.

- Plantinga, Alvin, "Pluralism: A Defense of Religious Exclusivism," in *The Rationality of Belief and the Plurality of Faith* edited by Senor (1995). Defends exclusivism against charges of irrationality and arrogance: it can be rational and non-culpably wrong to hold exclusive beliefs about one's own religion.

- Rahner, Karl, *Theological Investigations*, Volume V (1966). Defends "anonymous Christians": those outside explicit Christianity who respond to divine grace are implicitly Christian and can achieve salvation. A form of Christian inclusivism.

## Contemporary Works

- Quinn, Philip and Meeker, Kevin (eds.), *The Philosophical Challenge of Religious Diversity* (2000). A comprehensive anthology on religious diversity and its epistemological and soteriological implications.

- Basinger, David, *Religious Diversity: A Philosophical Assessment* (2002). A systematic treatment of the main positions on religious diversity, with attention to their implications for apologetics and interfaith dialogue.
