# Index: Metaphysics

Alphabetical index of key terms, concepts, arguments, and philosophers across all 13 domains. Format: **Term** — brief definition — *see: domain/file*

---

## A

**A-series** — McTaggart's ordering of time by the properties of being past, present, and future; essential to A-theory of time — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**A-theory of time** — the view that tense (past, present, future) is metaphysically fundamental and not reducible to tenseless B-relations — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**A posteriori necessity** — a truth that is necessary (could not be otherwise) yet discoverable only empirically; Kripke's paradigm case: "water is H₂O" — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Absent qualia** — the possibility that a system could instantiate all functional role conditions for mental states without any phenomenal experience; objection to functionalism — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Absolute space** — Newton's view that space is a real container existing independently of material objects; actual position and velocity are defined absolutely — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Abstract objects** — entities that are non-spatial, non-temporal, and causally inert (numbers, sets, propositions, universals on the Platonist view) — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Actualism** — the view that everything that exists is actual; possible worlds, if they exist, are abstract entities (sets, states of affairs) rather than concrete realities — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Adams, Robert M.** — actualist philosopher of modality; developed propositional ersatzism; philosopher of religion (the problem of evil, divine foreknowledge) — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Agent causation** — a kind of causation exercised by agents as substances, irreducible to event-causation; posited by libertarian accounts of free will — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Alston, William** — religious epistemologist; developed the doxastic practice approach to religious belief — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Anselm of Canterbury** — medieval philosopher who formulated the original ontological argument (*Proslogion*, c.1078) — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Anomalous monism** — Davidson's view that mental events are identical to physical events, but there are no strict laws connecting mental and physical types — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Aquinas, Thomas** — medieval philosopher; developed the Five Ways (cosmological arguments); articulated classical theism; defends divine simplicity and timeless eternity — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Aristotelian immanent realism** — universals exist only when instantiated; they are wholly present in each of their instances; defended by Armstrong — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

**Armstrong, D. M.** — Australian metaphysician; defended Aristotelian realism about universals, states of affairs as truthmakers, the DTA account of laws, and the combinatorial theory of possibility — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md; 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md; 12_Truth_and_Reality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Atomism (mereological)** — the view that every object has parts, and the parts-of-parts process terminates in simples with no proper parts — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Aseity** — the property of being self-existent, dependent on nothing outside oneself for existence; a traditional divine attribute — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

## B

**B-series** — McTaggart's ordering of time by the tenseless relation *earlier than*; the ordering preserved in B-theory — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**B-theory of time** — all temporal facts are reducible to tenseless B-relations; A-properties are perspectival — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Baker, Lynne Rudder** — philosopher who developed the Constitution View; argued that persons and bodies are constitutionally but not numerically identical — *see: 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md*

**Bare particular** — the substratum or "thin" individual that individuates a substance and supports its properties, itself lacking qualitative character — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md; 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

**Benacerraf, Paul** — philosopher who formulated the epistemological challenge to Platonism: if mathematical objects are causally inert abstract entities, how can we know anything about them? — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Best Systems Account (BSA) / MRL** — Lewis's view that laws are axioms and theorems of the system that best combines simplicity and strength in describing all particular facts — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

**Bigelow, John** — metaphysician; developed truthmaker theory for mathematics and the theory of structural universals (with Pargetter) — *see: 12_Truth_and_Reality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Bird, Alexander** — philosopher of science; defends dispositional essentialism: all fundamental properties are purely dispositional and laws are metaphysically necessary — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

**Black, Max** — philosopher who introduced the "two perfect spheres" thought experiment challenging the bundle theory of particulars — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

**Block, Ned** — philosopher of mind; introduced the access/phenomenal consciousness distinction; developed objections to functionalism (the China brain) — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Block universe** — see: *Eternalism* — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Boethius** — medieval philosopher; proposed that God is timeless and knows all events in an "eternal present," resolving the foreknowledge-freedom problem — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Bradley, F. H.** — British idealist; formulated Bradley's regress (the regress of relations); defended the coherence theory of truth — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md; 12_Truth_and_Reality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Bradley's regress** — the regress generated by the apparent need for a further relation to connect a property to its bearer, and so on indefinitely — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

**Bundle theory (of particulars)** — a particular object is a bundle of universals (or tropes) standing in the co-presence relation; there is no underlying substratum — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md; 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

## C

**Campbell, Keith** — philosopher who developed trope theory systematically (*Abstract Particulars*, 1990) — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

**Carnap, Rudolf** — logical empiricist; distinguished internal from external questions; deflationist about ontology; developed the framework-internal / framework-external distinction — *see: 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

**Causal closure of the physical** — every physical event that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Causal exclusion argument** — Kim's argument that non-reductive physicalism is unstable: if mental properties are not physical, they are causally excluded by sufficient physical causes — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Causal theory of reference** — names and natural kind terms refer to objects/kinds by causal-historical chains, not by descriptive content — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Chalmers, David** — philosopher of mind; formulated the "hard problem of consciousness"; developed the conceivability argument against physicalism; introduced the zombie thought experiment — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**China brain** — Block's thought experiment: the population of China, organized to simulate a human brain, would satisfy functional conditions for mental states without thereby having mental states — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Chinese Room argument** — Searle's argument that syntax (symbol manipulation) is insufficient for semantics (meaning): a person following rules for manipulating Chinese symbols does not understand Chinese — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Chisholm, Roderick** — philosopher who defended agent causation and mereological essentialism — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md; 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Class nominalism** — the property *redness* is identified with the class of all red things — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

**Classical mereology** — the formal system of part-whole relations developed by Leśniewski and formalized by Simons; includes extensionality (same parts → same object) and unrestricted fusion — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Coincident objects** — two distinct objects occupying the same spatial region at the same time; the statue and the clay as a paradigm case — *see: 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md; 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Compatibilism** — the thesis that free will (in the sense relevant to moral responsibility) is compatible with causal determinism — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Composition** — the mereological relation by which some objects together make up a further object — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Conceivability-possibility principle** — the thesis that what is ideally conceivable is metaphysically possible; applied by Chalmers in the zombie argument — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Consequence Argument** — van Inwagen's argument that if determinism is true, our actions are consequences of laws and events outside our control, hence we have no free will — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Constitution relation** — the non-identity relation holding when one object is made of another without being identical to it; statue is constituted by clay — *see: 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md*

**Constructive empiricism** — van Fraassen's view that science aims at empirical adequacy, not truth; belief in unobservables goes beyond what evidence warrants — *see: 10_Metaphysics_of_Science/00-domain-survey.md*

**Cosmological argument** — argument for God's existence from the existence of contingent beings; includes Leibniz's PSR version and the Kalam version — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Counterfactual analysis of causation** — Lewis's view that *c* causes *e* if *e* counterfactually depends on *c* (or is connected to *c* by a chain of counterfactual dependencies) — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

**Counterfactual conditionals** — conditionals of the form "if A were the case, B would be the case"; analyzed by Lewis via similarity between possible worlds — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Craig, William Lane** — philosopher of religion; defended the Kalam cosmological argument; neo-Lorentzian presentism — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md; 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

## D

**Davidson, Donald** — philosopher; developed anomalous monism (token identity theory); attacked the correspondence theory of truth via the slingshot; developed a semantic theory of meaning via truth conditions — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md; 12_Truth_and_Reality/00-domain-survey.md*

**De re / de dicto** — *de re*: modality predicated of a thing itself; *de dicto*: modality predicated of a proposition. "Necessarily, the largest planet is a planet" (de dicto); "Jupiter is necessarily a planet" (de re) — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Deflationism about ontology** — see: *Carnap; Hirsch; Thomasson* — *see: 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

**Deflationism about truth** — the truth predicate has only a disquotational/logical role; there is no substantive property of truth — *see: 12_Truth_and_Reality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Dependence (ontological)** — an entity *x* ontologically depends on entity *y* if *x* could not exist without *y*; a key tool in neo-Aristotelian ontology — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md; 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

**Determinism** — the thesis that the laws of nature plus the state of the universe at any time uniquely determine the state of the universe at every later time — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Direction of time** — the asymmetry between past and future, grounded by thermodynamics and the Past Hypothesis; studied by Price, Callender, and Albert — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Dispositional essentialism** — Bird's view that all fundamental properties are purely dispositional; laws are metaphysically necessary because they flow from the essential natures of properties — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

**Dispositions** — property-like entities essentially directed toward their manifestations; fire has the disposition to burn, glass the disposition to shatter — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

**DTA account** — Dretske-Tooley-Armstrong account of laws as relations of necessitation *N(F, G)* between universals — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

**Dummett, Michael** — philosopher of language; developed anti-realism about meaning and truth based on warranted assertibility; argued for intuitionist logic in disputed domains — *see: 12_Truth_and_Reality/00-domain-survey.md*

## E

**Eliminative materialism** — Churchland's view that folk psychology is a radically false theory; mental states as ordinarily conceived do not exist and will be replaced by neuroscience — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Ellis, Brian** — philosopher; defends scientific essentialism and dispositional essentialism; natural kinds have essential natures that ground necessity — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

**Emergence (weak/strong)** — *weak*: higher-level properties derivable in principle from lower-level facts but by very complex paths; *strong*: higher-level properties not derivable even in principle — *see: 10_Metaphysics_of_Science/00-domain-survey.md*

**Endurantism** — the view that objects are wholly present at each time they exist; there are no temporal parts — *see: 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md; 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Epistemology of modality** — the study of how we come to know modal truths; involves conceivability, imagination, and rational intuition — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Ersatzism** — the view that possible worlds are abstract entities (sets of propositions, states of affairs, linguistic descriptions) rather than concrete spatiotemporal realities — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Essence** — the properties constitutive of what a thing is, as opposed to its merely accidental properties; Fine argues essence is prior to modality — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Essentialism** — the thesis that objects have essential properties they could not lack while remaining what they are; revived by Kripke — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Eternalism** — past, present, and future entities exist equally; the world is a four-dimensional "block" — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Event-causal libertarianism** — free actions involve causation by reasons-states, but the causation is indeterministic; defended by Kane — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Evolutionary debunking arguments** — arguments that evolutionary explanations of our moral or religious beliefs undermine their epistemic justification — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Existential quantifier** — the logical symbol "∃", interpreted as "there exists at least one"; Quine's criterion ties ontological commitment to what must fall within its domain — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Explanatory gap** — Levine's term for the conceptual gap between physical/functional descriptions and phenomenal descriptions; even complete physical knowledge would not explain *why* C-fiber firing feels painful — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

## F

**Fine, Kit** — analytic philosopher; distinguished essence from modality; developed the grounding framework; argued that ontological questions are about what is "real" not merely existent — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md; 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md; 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

**Fine-tuning argument** — the physical constants of the universe are extraordinarily fine-tuned for the existence of complex life; best explained by an intelligent designer — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Fischer, John Martin** — compatibilist philosopher; developed reasons-responsiveness as the criterion of moral responsibility (with Ravizza) — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Fission** — the thought experiment in which a person's brain is divided and each hemisphere transplanted into a different body; poses a challenge to psychological continuity theories of personal identity — *see: 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md*

**Fodor, Jerry** — philosopher of mind; multiple realizability argument against type identity theory; defended autonomy of special sciences — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md; 10_Metaphysics_of_Science/00-domain-survey.md*

**Foundationalism (about grounding)** — there are fundamental, ungrounded facts; derivative facts are grounded ultimately in these — *see: 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

**Four-category ontology** — Lowe's view that there are four irreducible ontological categories: substances, kinds, attributes, and modes — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Four-dimensionalism** — see: *Perdurantism* — *see: 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md*

**Frankfurt cases** — Frankfurt's thought experiments involving counterfactual interveners; argued to show that moral responsibility does not require alternative possibilities — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Frankfurt, Harry** — philosopher; "Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility" (1969); hierarchical analysis of free will via first-order and second-order desires — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Free will defense** — Plantinga's response to the logical problem of evil: God could not have created free creatures who always do right; so the permission of evil is compatible with omnipotence and perfect goodness — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Functionalism** — mental states are defined by their causal/functional roles, not by their physical constitution; allows multiple realizability — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Fundamental / derivative** — the distinction between entities that are not grounded in anything further (fundamental) and those that exist in virtue of others (derivative) — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md; 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

## G

**Gaunilo of Marmoutiers** — Benedictine monk who offered the "perfect island" objection to Anselm's ontological argument — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Gibbard, Allan** — philosopher who introduced Lumpl and Goliath to argue for contingent identity of statue and clay; concept of "contingent identity" — *see: 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md*

**God** — in philosophical discussion, a being that is omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good, eternal, and the creator of all contingent reality — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Grounding** — the non-causal, explanatory relation holding between facts when one obtains *in virtue of* another; the core notion of neo-Aristotelian ontology — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md; 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

**Growing block view** — the past and present exist, but the future does not yet; reality grows by accretion — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Gunk** — a term from Lewis for matter in which every part has a further proper part; an atomless gunky material is possible in classical mereology — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

## H

**Haecceities** — non-qualitative primitive thisness; the property of being *this particular individual*; posited for cross-world individuation — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md; 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Hard incompatibilism** — free will (in any sense robust enough for moral responsibility) is incompatible with determinism and with mere indeterminism — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Hard problem of consciousness** — Chalmers's term for the difficulty of explaining why physical processes give rise to subjective experience (qualia, what-it-is-like); not addressed by functional explanations — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Hick, John** — philosopher of religion; developed the soul-making theodicy (*Evil and the God of Love*, 1966) — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Hirsch, Eli** — philosopher; developed quantifier variantism as a defense of deflationism about ontology — *see: 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

**Hole argument** — Earman and Norton's argument that general relativistic substantivalism generates radical indeterminism via diffeomorphism invariance — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Homeostatic property cluster (HPC) theory** — Boyd's theory that natural kinds are clusters of co-occurring properties sustained by homeostatic mechanisms; accommodates biological species — *see: 10_Metaphysics_of_Science/00-domain-survey.md*

**Horwich, Paul** — philosopher; developed minimalism about truth; *Truth* (1990) — *see: 12_Truth_and_Reality/00-domain-survey.md*

**Hume, David** — empiricist philosopher; argued that causation consists in constant conjunction; denied necessary connection in nature; undermined the cosmological and design arguments — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

## I

**Identity of Indiscernibles** — the principle that qualitatively identical things are numerically identical; challenged by Black's two spheres — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

**Immanent realism** — see: *Aristotelian immanent realism* — *see: 02_Universals_and_Particulars/00-domain-survey.md*

**Incompatibilism** — the thesis that free will (in a robust sense) is incompatible with determinism — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Indispensability argument** — Quine-Putnam: we should be ontologically committed to mathematical entities because they are indispensable to our best scientific theories — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Intentionality** — the "aboutness" or directedness of mental states; the property of being about or representing something — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Interventionist theory of causation** — Woodward's view that *X* causes *Y* iff an ideal intervention on *X* would change *Y* — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

**Inverted qualia** — the hypothesis that two subjects could have functionally identical mental lives while having phenomenally inverted experiences (your green is phenomenally my red) — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**INUS condition** — Mackie's refinement of regularity theory: a cause is an Insufficient but Necessary part of an Unnecessary but Sufficient condition — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md*

## J

**Jackson, Frank** — philosopher; formulated the knowledge argument (Mary's Room) against physicalism — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Joint-carving** — Sider's concept: a predicate or description "carves reality at its joints" if it picks out a perfectly natural property or structure; fundamental descriptions maximize joint-carving — *see: 09_Metametaphysics/00-domain-survey.md*

## K

**Kalam cosmological argument** — everything that begins to exist has a cause; the universe began to exist; therefore the universe has a cause — *see: 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Kane, Robert** — philosopher; defends event-causal libertarianism using quantum indeterminacy in neural processes; self-forming actions — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Kim, Jaegwon** — philosopher of mind; developed the causal exclusion argument against non-reductive physicalism; defended reductionism — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md; 10_Metaphysics_of_Science/00-domain-survey.md*

**Knowledge argument (Mary's Room)** — Jackson's argument: Mary knows all physical facts about color vision but learns something new when she first sees red; hence physical facts do not exhaust the facts — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Kripke, Saul** — philosopher; developed possible worlds semantics, the theory of rigid designators, a posteriori necessity, and origin essentialism (*Naming and Necessity*, 1980) — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md*

## L

**Ladyman, James** — philosopher of science; developed ontic structural realism; argued (with Ross) that only structurally realist metaphysics is scientifically respectable — *see: 10_Metaphysics_of_Science/00-domain-survey.md*

**Leibniz, Gottfried Wilhelm** — rationalist philosopher; formulated the PSR-based cosmological argument; the principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles; relational theory of space — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md; 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**Leibniz's Law** — if *x = y*, then *x* and *y* have all the same properties; often used to argue for non-identity of statue and clay — *see: 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md*

**Leonard-Goodman calculus of individuals** — early formal mereological system developed by Leonard and Goodman (1940); equivalent to classical mereology — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Leśniewski, Stanisław** — Polish logician who developed mereology as an alternative to set theory — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Levine, Joseph** — philosopher who coined the term "explanatory gap" (1983) — *see: 07_Philosophy_of_Mind/00-domain-survey.md*

**Lewis, David** — analytic philosopher who shaped virtually every area of metaphysics: modal realism, counterfactual semantics, best systems account of laws, temporal parts, plurality of worlds — *see: 05_Modality/00-domain-survey.md; 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md; 03_Identity_and_Persistence/00-domain-survey.md*

**Libertarianism (metaphysical)** — the view that free will exists and requires the falsity of determinism; agents have genuine alternative possibilities — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Lowe, E. J.** — philosopher; developed the four-category ontology; defended neo-Aristotelian ontology — *see: 01_Ontology/00-domain-survey.md*

## M

**Mackie, J. L.** — philosopher who developed the INUS condition for causation; formulated the logical problem of evil against theism — *see: 04_Causation_and_Laws/00-domain-survey.md; 13_Philosophy_of_Religion/00-domain-survey.md*

**McTaggart, J. M. E.** — British idealist; developed the A-series/B-series distinction and argued for the unreality of time — *see: 06_Time_and_Space/00-domain-survey.md*

**Manipulation argument** — Pereboom's argument that a person whose actions result from deterministic natural processes is no more responsible than one directly manipulated by a neuroscientist — *see: 08_Free_Will_and_Agency/00-domain-survey.md*

**Mechanism** — a sequence of entities and activities that is productive of a regular change; the unit of explanation in biology and neuroscience (Machamer, Darden, Craver) — *see: 10_Metaphysics_of_Science/00-domain-survey.md*

**Mereological essentialism** — Chisholm's view that objects have their parts essentially; material change destroys and replaces the object — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Mereological nihilism** — no plurality of objects ever composes a further object; only simples exist — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Mereological universalism** — for any plurality of objects, there is a mereological sum that is their composed whole — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*

**Mereology** — the formal theory of parts and wholes — *see: 11_Mereology/00-domain-survey.md*
