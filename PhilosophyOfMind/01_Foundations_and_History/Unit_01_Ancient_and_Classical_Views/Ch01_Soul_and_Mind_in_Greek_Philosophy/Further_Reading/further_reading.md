# Further Reading: Soul and Mind in Greek Philosophy

**Primary Sources**

- **Plato, *Phaedo*** — The central dialogue for Plato's argument for the immortality of the soul and the thesis that the soul is distinct from and superior to the body. Essential primary reading.
- **Plato, *Republic*, Book IV** — Contains Plato's tripartite division of the soul into reason, spirit, and appetite, linked to his political theory.
- **Aristotle, *De Anima* (On the Soul)** — The most systematic ancient treatment of mind and soul. Aristotle's hylomorphic analysis of soul as form of a living body is the essential counterpoint to Platonic dualism.
- **Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Book I** — Connects the theory of soul to ethics and the good life; introduces the notion of function (*ergon*) central to Aristotle's conception of human flourishing.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Julia Annas, *An Introduction to Plato's Republic*** (1981) — Clear, philosophically sophisticated guide to Plato's political and psychological theory, including the soul's tripartition.
- **W.D. Ross, *Aristotle*** (1923) — Still a reliable overview of Aristotle's main doctrines, including his psychology.
- **Martha Nussbaum and Amélie Rorty (eds.), *Essays on Aristotle's De Anima*** (1992) — A collection of essays by leading scholars on the major interpretive controversies in Aristotle's psychology.
- **Myles Burnyeat, "Is an Aristotelian Philosophy of Mind Still Credible?"** in Nussbaum and Rorty (1992) — A key essay on whether Aristotle's hylomorphism offers a genuine alternative to contemporary physicalism.
- **F.M. Cornford, *Plato's Theory of Knowledge*** (1935) — Classic treatment of Plato's epistemology and its connection to the theory of soul.
- **Christopher Shields, *Aristotle*** (2007, Routledge) — A comprehensive contemporary introduction to Aristotle's philosophy including his psychology.
