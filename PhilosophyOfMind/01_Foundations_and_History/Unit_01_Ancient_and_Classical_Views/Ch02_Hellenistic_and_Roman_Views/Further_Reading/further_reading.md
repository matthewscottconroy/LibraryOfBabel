# Further Reading: Hellenistic and Roman Views

**Primary Sources**

- **Epicurus, *Letter to Menoeceus* and *Letter to Herodotus*** — The accessible primary texts for Epicurean psychology: the soul as a physical compound dissolved at death, and the therapeutic consequences for the fear of death.
- **Lucretius, *De Rerum Natura* (On the Nature of Things)** — A Latin poem presenting Epicurean atomism in full; Books III and IV cover the soul, perception, and dreams with remarkable detail and philosophical depth.
- **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*** — First-person Stoic reflections on the rational soul, its governance of the passions, and its relationship to the universal logos.
- **Epictetus, *Discourses* and *Enchiridion*** — The clearest Stoic treatment of the distinction between what is "up to us" (our judgments and intentions) and what is not, grounding Stoic psychology.

**Secondary Literature**

- **A.A. Long and D.N. Sedley, *The Hellenistic Philosophers*, 2 vols.** (1987) — The definitive sourcebook, with extensive translations of fragments and lucid commentary on Stoic, Epicurean, and Skeptic philosophy of mind and ethics.
- **A.A. Long, *Hellenistic Philosophy*** (2nd ed., 1986) — Accessible and authoritative overview of the three main schools.
- **Brad Inwood and L.P. Gerson (eds.), *The Epicurus Reader*** (1994) — Translations of the key Epicurean texts with helpful introduction.
- **Christopher Gill, *The Structured Self in Hellenistic and Roman Thought*** (2006) — A sophisticated analysis of how Stoics and Epicureans conceived the self and its relationship to reason and emotion.
- **Julia Annas, *Hellenistic Philosophy of Mind*** (1992) — The best focused treatment of psychology across the Hellenistic schools.
