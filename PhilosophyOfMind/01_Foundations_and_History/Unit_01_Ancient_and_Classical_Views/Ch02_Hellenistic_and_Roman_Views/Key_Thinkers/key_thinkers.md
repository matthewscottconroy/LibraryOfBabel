# Key Thinkers: Hellenistic and Roman Views

---

**Epicurus (341–270 BCE)**
Founder of the Epicurean school. Epicurus developed a thoroughly materialist psychology: the soul is composed of fine atoms and disperses at death, rendering the afterlife impossible and the fear of death irrational. The goal of philosophy is *ataraxia* (tranquility), achieved by removing false beliefs about death and the gods.
*Key works:* *Letter to Menoeceus*, *Letter to Herodotus*, *Principal Doctrines* (fragments)

---

**Lucretius (c. 99–55 BCE)**
Roman poet and Epicurean philosopher. His *De Rerum Natura* is the most complete surviving exposition of Epicurean atomism, including a detailed psychology of sensation, dreaming, and the argument that the mortality of the soul is a liberation rather than a loss.
*Key works:* *De Rerum Natura* (On the Nature of Things)

---

**Chrysippus (c. 279–206 BCE)**
The third head of the Stoic school and its most systematic philosopher. Chrysippus developed the Stoic doctrine of the *hegemonikon* (the rational, governing part of the soul centered in the heart), the theory of *lekta* (sayables, the propositional content of mental states), and a detailed analysis of the passions as false value judgments.
*Key works:* Works survive only in fragments; best accessed through Long and Sedley (1987)

---

**Marcus Aurelius (121–180 CE)**
Roman emperor and Stoic philosopher. The *Meditations* are a private record of his efforts to practice Stoic psychology: governing the soul through reason, distinguishing impressions from assent, and maintaining equanimity in the face of fortune.
*Key works:* *Meditations*

---

**Pyrrho of Elis (c. 360–270 BCE)**
Founder of ancient Pyrrhonism, the most thoroughgoing form of ancient skepticism. His followers argued that the absence of certain knowledge justifies suspending all judgment (*epoché*), which leads to *ataraxia* through a different route than the Epicureans.
*Key works:* Accessible through Sextus Empiricus, *Outlines of Pyrrhonism*
