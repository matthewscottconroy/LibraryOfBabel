# Further Reading: Medieval Philosophy of Mind

**Primary Sources**

- **Augustine, *Confessions*, Books X–XI** — Profound first-person phenomenology of memory, time-consciousness, and the soul's turning toward God; philosophically rich engagement with inner experience.
- **Thomas Aquinas, *Summa Theologica*, Part I, Questions 75–89** — Aquinas's systematic treatment of the soul, intellect, and their relationship to the body, integrating Aristotelian psychology with Christian theology.
- **Ibn Sina (Avicenna), *Book of the Soul* (Kitāb al-Nafs)** — Avicenna's sophisticated neo-Aristotelian account of the soul, including the famous "Flying Man" thought experiment arguing for the soul's immateriality.
- **John of the Cross, *The Dark Night of the Soul*** — Mystical phenomenology of interior life; philosophically interesting as first-person description of extreme altered states.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Anthony Kenny, *Aquinas on Mind*** (1993) — Clear and philosophically rigorous treatment of Aquinas's psychology for readers with analytic backgrounds.
- **Deborah Black, "Imagination and Estimation: Arabic Paradigms and Western Transformations"** (1993) — On how Islamic philosophy transformed Aristotelian faculty psychology.
- **Peter Adamson, *Philosophy in the Islamic World*** (2016) — Accessible survey of Arabic and Islamic philosophy including major contributions to philosophy of mind.
- **Norman Kretzmann and Eleonore Stump (eds.), *The Cambridge Companion to Aquinas*** (1993) — Excellent essays on Aquinas's philosophy of mind within the broader metaphysical context.
- **Étienne Gilson, *The Spirit of Medieval Philosophy*** (1936) — A classic treatment of how medieval thinkers integrated Greek philosophy with Christian thought on the soul and person.
