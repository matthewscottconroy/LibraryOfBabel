# Key Thinkers: Medieval Philosophy of Mind

---

**Augustine of Hippo (354–430 CE)**
North African bishop and theologian. Augustine's *Confessions* contains some of the most searching phenomenological analyses of memory, time, and the inner life in Western philosophy. He influenced later philosophy of mind through his analysis of the soul's self-knowledge (*intus*, the inner word) and his account of the will as a distinct faculty.
*Key works:* *Confessions*, *On the Trinity*, *The City of God*

---

**Ibn Sina / Avicenna (980–1037 CE)**
Persian philosopher and physician. Avicenna developed a sophisticated neo-Aristotelian psychology that distinguished internal senses (common sense, imagination, estimation, memory) from external senses, and argued via the Flying Man thought experiment that the soul can know itself without reference to the body.
*Key works:* *Book of the Soul* (part of the *Shifa*/Healing), *Remarks and Admonitions*

---

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)**
Dominican friar and the most influential scholastic philosopher. Aquinas synthesized Aristotelian hylomorphism with Christian theology, arguing that the soul is the form of the body while also capable of subsisting separately (as a form without matter, though not a complete substance). His analysis of the intellect — distinguishing agent and patient intellect — became the standard framework for medieval cognitive psychology.
*Key works:* *Summa Theologica*, *Summa Contra Gentiles*, *On Being and Essence*

---

**William of Ockham (c. 1287–1347)**
Franciscan friar and nominalist. Ockham rejected universal forms and argued that mental concepts are natural signs (mental terms) that stand for things without requiring Platonic or Aristotelian entities. His nominalist psychology anticipates later empiricist and computational approaches.
*Key works:* *Summa of Logic*, *Quodlibetal Questions*
