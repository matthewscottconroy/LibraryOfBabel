# Further Reading: Early Modern Turning Points

**Primary Sources**

- **Descartes, *Meditations on First Philosophy*** — The foundational text for early modern philosophy of mind; the *cogito*, the distinction between res cogitans and res extensa, and the problem of mind-body interaction.
- **Descartes, *The Passions of the Soul*** — Descartes's attempt to reconcile his dualism with an account of how the passions connect soul and body; includes famous discussion of the pineal gland.
- **Locke, *Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, Books I–II** — Empiricist theory of mind: the mind as a "white paper" furnished entirely by experience; influential accounts of personal identity, substance, and primary/secondary qualities.
- **Spinoza, *Ethics*, Parts II–III** — Spinoza's monist alternative to Cartesian dualism: mind and body are two attributes of a single substance, and the order of ideas parallels the order of things.
- **Leibniz, *New Essays on Human Understanding* and *Monadology*** — Leibniz's rationalist psychology including the doctrine of petites perceptions (unconscious perceptions) and pre-established harmony.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Anthony Kenny, *Descartes: A Study of His Philosophy*** (1968) — Philosophically rigorous analysis of Descartes's main positions.
- **Margaret Wilson, *Descartes*** (1978) — Excellent on the mind-body problem and Cartesian dualism.
- **Michael Ayers, *Locke*, 2 vols.** (1991) — The definitive philosophical commentary on Locke's *Essay*.
- **Jonathan Bennett, *A Study of Spinoza's Ethics*** (1984) — Analytically rigorous treatment of Spinoza's metaphysics and psychology.
- **Roger Woolhouse, *Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz: The Concept of Substance in Seventeenth-Century Metaphysics*** (1993) — Comparative treatment of the three major rationalist systems.
