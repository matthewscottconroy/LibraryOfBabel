# Key Thinkers: Early Modern Turning Points

---

**René Descartes (1596–1650)**
French mathematician and philosopher. Descartes's substance dualism — the strict separation of thinking substance (*res cogitans*) from extended substance (*res extensa*) — defined the mind-body problem for subsequent centuries. His *cogito ergo sum* established first-person certainty as the starting point of modern epistemology, and his mechanistic physiology of the body (with the soul interacting via the pineal gland) set the agenda for debates about mental causation.
*Key works:* *Meditations on First Philosophy*, *Discourse on Method*, *Passions of the Soul*, *Rules for the Direction of the Mind*

---

**Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677)**
Dutch philosopher of Portuguese-Jewish origin. Spinoza rejected Cartesian dualism with a thoroughgoing monism: there is only one substance (God or Nature), of which mind and body are two attributes. This "dual-aspect" theory eliminates the interaction problem and is a significant ancestor of modern property dualism and neutral monism.
*Key works:* *Ethics*, *Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect*

---

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)**
German philosopher and mathematician. Leibniz proposed pre-established harmony as a solution to the interaction problem: mind and body do not causally interact but run in perfect synchrony by divine design. His doctrine of petites perceptions — unconscious, infinitesimally small perceptions that aggregate into conscious ones — anticipates later accounts of unconscious mental processing.
*Key works:* *Monadology*, *New Essays on Human Understanding*, *Discourse on Metaphysics*

---

**John Locke (1632–1704)**
English empiricist philosopher. Locke proposed that the mind begins as a "white paper" furnished by experience alone (against innate ideas). His account of personal identity as consisting in continuity of consciousness (memory) was the first systematic philosophical treatment of the topic and remains foundational for the debate.
*Key works:* *Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, *Two Treatises of Government*

---

**Nicolas Malebranche (1638–1715)**
French philosopher and Oratorian priest. Malebranche developed occasionalism — the view that God is the only true cause, and apparent mind-body causation is God's intervention on each "occasion." His system is a philosophically coherent response to the interaction problem that influenced subsequent debates.
*Key works:* *The Search After Truth*, *Dialogues on Metaphysics and on Religion*
