# Further Reading: Kant and the Transcendental

**Primary Sources**

- **Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, "Transcendental Analytic" and "Paralogisms of Pure Reason"** — The Paralogisms directly address the soul and self-knowledge; the Analytic develops the transcendental unity of apperception as the formal condition of experience.
- **Kant, *Critique of Practical Reason* and *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*** — Kant's practical philosophy and its connection to his account of rational agency and the will.
- **Kant, *Anthropology from a Pragmatic Point of View*** — Kant's empirical psychology and account of the mind's faculties as they appear in ordinary human life; a useful complement to the first Critique.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Paul Guyer, *Kant and the Claims of Knowledge*** (1987) — A comprehensive and rigorous treatment of the first Critique, including the theory of mind.
- **Henry Allison, *Kant's Transcendental Idealism*** (2nd ed., 2004) — Influential defense of Kant's idealism with strong attention to the role of the transcendental subject.
- **Patricia Kitcher, *Kant's Transcendental Psychology*** (1990) — Argues for reading Kant's first Critique as offering a cognitive-psychological theory; valuable for connecting Kant to philosophy of mind.
- **Robert Pippin, *Kant's Theory of Form*** (1982) — Sophisticated analysis of Kant's formal conditions of experience.
- **Sebastian Gardner, *Kant and the Critique of Pure Reason*** (1999) — A clear and philosophically sensitive introduction accessible to non-specialists.
