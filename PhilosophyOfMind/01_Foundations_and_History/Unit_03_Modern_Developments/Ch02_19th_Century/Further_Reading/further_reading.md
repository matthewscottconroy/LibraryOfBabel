# Further Reading: 19th Century Developments

**Primary Sources**

- **William James, *The Principles of Psychology*** (1890) — The founding text of scientific psychology; still rewarding for its philosophical sophistication on topics including the stream of consciousness, habit, the self, and emotion.
- **William James, *Essays in Radical Empiricism*** — James's mature metaphysics; the doctrine of "pure experience" as the fundamental category anticipates neutral monism.
- **G.W.F. Hegel, *Phenomenology of Spirit*, "Consciousness" and "Self-Consciousness" sections** — Hegel's dialectical account of how consciousness develops through stages toward self-knowledge; influential on existentialism, phenomenology, and critical theory.
- **Hermann von Helmholtz, *Handbook of Physiological Optics***, Vol. III — Helmholtz's theory of unconscious inference in perception; a key scientific text in the history of cognitive science.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Gerald Myers, *William James: His Life and Thought*** (1986) — The most comprehensive intellectual biography of James.
- **Charles Taylor, *Hegel*** (1975) — The best English-language introduction to Hegel's system, including his philosophy of mind.
- **Robert J. Richards, *Darwin and the Emergence of Evolutionary Theories of Mind and Behavior*** (1987) — On Darwin's influence on 19th-century psychology and philosophy of mind.
- **Christopher Green and Philip Groff, *Young Wittgenstein's Philosophical Logic*** — On the transition from 19th-century psychology to early analytic philosophy.
- **Edward Reed, *From Soul to Mind: The Emergence of Psychology from Erasmus Darwin to William James*** (1997) — Historical account of the development of scientific psychology.
