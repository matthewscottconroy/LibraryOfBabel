# Key Thinkers: 19th Century Developments

---

**William James (1842–1910)**
American philosopher and psychologist, founder of pragmatism and functional psychology. James's *Principles of Psychology* introduced the "stream of consciousness" as a fundamental description of mental life — continuous, personal, always changing, selective — against the associationist atomism of the British empiricists. His emotion theory (what is now called the James-Lange theory) and his account of habit as the basis of character remain influential.
*Key works:* *Principles of Psychology*, *The Varieties of Religious Experience*, *Essays in Radical Empiricism*, *Pragmatism*

---

**G.W.F. Hegel (1770–1831)**
German idealist philosopher. Hegel's dialectical analysis of consciousness — moving from sense-certainty through perception to self-consciousness and beyond — describes the development of mind through its engagement with the world and with other self-consciousnesses. His concept of Geist (spirit/mind) as social and historical transformed philosophy of mind from a study of the individual psyche to a study of collective mentality.
*Key works:* *Phenomenology of Spirit*, *Encyclopedia of the Philosophical Sciences* (especially the Philosophy of Mind)

---

**Charles Darwin (1809–1882)**
English naturalist. Darwin's theory of natural selection transformed philosophy of mind by grounding human psychology in evolutionary continuity with other species, motivating comparative psychology, and raising questions about the adaptive function of consciousness and emotion. His *Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals* is a philosophical milestone.
*Key works:* *On the Origin of Species*, *The Descent of Man*, *The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals*

---

**Wilhelm Wundt (1832–1920)**
German psychologist who established the first experimental psychology laboratory in Leipzig (1879). Wundt's program of experimental introspection attempted to analyze consciousness into its elementary components — sensations, feelings, volitions — using rigorous experimental methods. His methods and assumptions were contested by later psychologists but set the agenda for the new science.
*Key works:* *Principles of Physiological Psychology*, *Elements of Folk Psychology*
