# Further Reading: The Rise of Analytic Philosophy

**Primary Sources**

- **Bertrand Russell, *The Problems of Philosophy*** (1912) — Russell's accessible introduction to empiricist epistemology; good entry point to early analytic philosophy's concerns.
- **Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations*** (1953) — The pivotal text for the "linguistic turn" in philosophy of mind; the private language argument and the critique of inner ostension are essential reading.
- **Gilbert Ryle, *The Concept of Mind*** (1949) — The most influential behaviorist analysis of mental concepts; introduces the "category mistake" and the critique of the "ghost in the machine."
- **G.E. Moore, "The Refutation of Idealism"** (1903) — The paper that launched analytic philosophy; argues for direct realism about perception against idealist philosophy.
- **Gottlob Frege, *The Foundations of Arithmetic* and "On Sense and Reference"** — Foundational for analytic philosophy of language; the sense/reference distinction is directly relevant to debates about mental content.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Michael Dummett, *Origins of Analytic Philosophy*** (1993) — Argues that analytic philosophy shares its starting point with Husserl's phenomenology, despite their surface divergence.
- **P.M.S. Hacker, *Wittgenstein: Meaning and Mind*** (1990) — Part of Hacker's monumental commentary on the *Philosophical Investigations*; particularly valuable for philosophy of mind.
- **Scott Soames, *Philosophical Analysis in the Twentieth Century***, 2 vols. (2003) — A comprehensive history of analytic philosophy's development.
- **Anthony Quinton, "The Character of Mind"** in *Philosophy of Mind* (ed. Guttenplan, 1994) — Useful overview of the rise of the analytic tradition in philosophy of mind.
