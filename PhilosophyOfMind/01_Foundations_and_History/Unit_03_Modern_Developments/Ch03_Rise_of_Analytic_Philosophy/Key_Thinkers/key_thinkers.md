# Key Thinkers: The Rise of Analytic Philosophy

---

**Gottlob Frege (1848–1925)**
German mathematician and logician. Frege's distinction between *Sinn* (sense — the mode of presentation) and *Bedeutung* (reference — the object picked out) is directly relevant to philosophy of mind: it provided the framework for distinguishing between how mental states represent their objects (sense/content) and what they represent (reference). His analysis of thought as something objective — distinct from both the subjective mental act and the physical world — influenced Husserl as well as analytic philosophy.
*Key works:* *Foundations of Arithmetic*, "On Sense and Reference," "The Thought"

---

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)**
British philosopher and mathematician. Russell's logical atomism proposed that the structure of language mirrors the structure of reality; his theory of descriptions and his analysis of belief contexts were foundational for analytic philosophy of mind and language. His later "neutral monism" — that mental and physical events are different arrangements of the same neutral stuff — is an important position in the ontology of mind.
*Key works:* *The Principles of Mathematics*, *Problems of Philosophy*, *The Analysis of Mind*, *Human Knowledge*

---

**Ludwig Wittgenstein (1889–1951)**
Austrian-British philosopher whose work falls into two distinct phases. The early *Tractatus* proposed a "picture theory" of meaning; the later *Philosophical Investigations* dismantled this and developed a use-based theory of meaning with revolutionary implications for philosophy of mind: the private language argument against purely private mental contents, the account of "seeing-as," and the critique of Cartesian inner theater.
*Key works:* *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*, *Philosophical Investigations*, *Zettel*, *On Certainty*

---

**Gilbert Ryle (1900–1976)**
British philosopher. Ryle's *The Concept of Mind* (1949) systematically argued that Cartesian dualism rests on a "category mistake" — treating the mind as a thing of the same kind as the body but made of different stuff, rather than as a set of dispositions and abilities. His "logical behaviorism" dissolved mental terms into behavioral dispositions. Despite behaviorism's demise, his critique of the Cartesian "ghost in the machine" remains influential.
*Key works:* *The Concept of Mind*, *Dilemmas*
