# Further Reading: The Cognitive Revolution

**Primary Sources and Landmark Papers**

- **George Miller, "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two"** (1956) — The landmark paper demonstrating limits of short-term memory; helped establish cognitive science's methodology.
- **Noam Chomsky, *Syntactic Structures*** (1957) and **review of Skinner's *Verbal Behavior*** (1959) — The critique of behaviorism and the argument for innate linguistic structure that helped launch the cognitive revolution.
- **Alan Newell and Herbert Simon, *Human Problem Solving*** (1972) — The landmark work establishing that human cognition can be modeled as symbol processing.
- **Jerry Fodor, *The Language of Thought*** (1975) — Argues for a language-like format for mental representation; central text of computationalist philosophy of mind.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Howard Gardner, *The Mind's New Science: A History of the Cognitive Revolution*** (1985) — The best narrative history of the cognitive revolution, covering all the contributing disciplines.
- **Jerome Bruner, *In Search of Mind*** (1983) — Memoir by one of the founders of cognitive science; illuminates the personal and institutional history.
- **Francisco Varela, Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch, *The Embodied Mind*** (1991) — Critique of the classical cognitive revolution from embodied and phenomenological perspectives; important corrective.
- **Ned Block, "Troubles with Functionalism"** (1978) — Key philosophical critique of the computational theory of mind; introduces the absent qualia argument.
- **Philip Johnson-Laird, *The Computer and the Mind*** (1988) — Clear exposition of the computationalist research program by a leading cognitive scientist.
