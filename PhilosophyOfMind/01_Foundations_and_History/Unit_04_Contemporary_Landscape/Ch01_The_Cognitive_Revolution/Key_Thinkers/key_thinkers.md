# Key Thinkers: The Cognitive Revolution

---

**Noam Chomsky (1928–)**
American linguist, philosopher, and political thinker. Chomsky's argument that human language is too complex and too rapidly acquired to be explained by behaviorist conditioning — and his proposal that humans have an innate universal grammar — was a decisive blow to behaviorism and a founding move of the cognitive revolution. His generative linguistics established the computational paradigm for studying mental structure.
*Key works:* *Syntactic Structures*, review of Skinner's *Verbal Behavior*, *Language and Mind*, *Rules and Representations*, *The Minimalist Program*

---

**Alan Turing (1912–1954)**
British mathematician and computing pioneer. Turing's conceptual invention of the universal Turing machine and his proposal of the "Imitation Game" (Turing Test) as an operational criterion for machine intelligence set the conceptual agenda for computational approaches to mind. His work made precise the idea that intelligence could be defined functionally — by input-output behavior rather than substrate.
*Key works:* "On Computable Numbers, With an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem" (1936), "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (1950)

---

**Jerry Fodor (1935–2017)**
American philosopher and cognitive scientist. Fodor was the most influential philosophical champion of the cognitive revolution. His Language of Thought hypothesis argued that thought is in a language-like format (Mentalese); his modularity thesis proposed that peripheral cognitive systems are encapsulated domain-specific modules; his functionalism grounded mental states in causal-functional roles rather than biological substrate.
*Key works:* *The Language of Thought*, *Representations*, *The Modularity of Mind*, *Psychosemantics*, *The Mind Doesn't Work That Way*

---

**Herbert Simon (1916–2001)**
American cognitive scientist and Nobel laureate in economics. Simon, with Newell, developed the first computational models of human problem-solving and introduced the concept of "satisficing" (bounded rationality). His work established AI simulation as a method for testing psychological theories.
*Key works:* *Human Problem Solving* (with Newell), *The Sciences of the Artificial*, *Models of Man*
