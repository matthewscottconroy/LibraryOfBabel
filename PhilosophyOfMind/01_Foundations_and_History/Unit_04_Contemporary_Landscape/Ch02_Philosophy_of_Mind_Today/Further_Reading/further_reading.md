# Further Reading: Philosophy of Mind Today

**Introductions and Overviews**

- **Jaegwon Kim, *Philosophy of Mind*** (3rd ed., 2011) — The standard textbook; clear, comprehensive, analytically rigorous.
- **Tim Crane, *Elements of Mind*** (2001) — An accessible but philosophically sophisticated overview of the core topics.
- **John Heil, *Philosophy of Mind: A Contemporary Introduction*** (3rd ed., 2013) — Clear and balanced treatment of the main debates.
- **David Chalmers (ed.), *Philosophy of Mind: Classical and Contemporary Readings*** (2002) — The best anthology; covers all major topics with key primary texts.

**Landmark Contemporary Works**

- **David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind*** (1996) — The book that reinvigorated the hard problem debate; argues against physicalist reduction of consciousness.
- **Daniel Dennett, *Consciousness Explained*** (1991) — The most influential physicalist/functionalist account of consciousness; the "multiple drafts" model.
- **Ned Block, "On a Confusion About a Function of Consciousness"** (1995) — Introduces the access/phenomenal consciousness distinction; one of the most cited papers in philosophy of mind.
- **Andy Clark and David Chalmers, "The Extended Mind"** (1998) — The foundational paper of the extended mind movement.
- **Patricia Churchland, *Neurophilosophy*** (1986) — Argues for deep integration of philosophy and neuroscience; foundational for the field.

**For the Current Frontiers**

- **Anil Seth, *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*** (2021) — Accessible account of predictive processing approaches to consciousness from a leading neuroscientist.
- **Stanislas Dehaene, *Consciousness and the Brain*** (2014) — Global workspace theory explained and defended with rich empirical detail.
