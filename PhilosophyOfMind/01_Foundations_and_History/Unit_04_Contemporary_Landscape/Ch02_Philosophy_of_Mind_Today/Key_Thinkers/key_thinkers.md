# Key Thinkers: Philosophy of Mind Today

---

**David Chalmers (1966–)**
Australian philosopher. Chalmers revitalized the hard problem of consciousness with his distinction between "easy" problems (explaining cognitive functions) and the "hard" problem (explaining why there is phenomenal experience). He has defended property dualism, the conceivability argument for the possibility of zombies, and the extended mind thesis. Among the most influential living philosophers of mind.
*Key works:* *The Conscious Mind*, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness," "The Extended Mind" (with Clark), *The Character of Consciousness*

---

**Daniel Dennett (1942–2024)**
American philosopher and cognitive scientist. Dennett's "heterophenomenology" proposes treating first-person reports as data without taking them at face value; his "multiple drafts" model of consciousness denies a single Cartesian theater of consciousness. He is the leading proponent of a deflationary, functionalist approach to consciousness and a persistent critic of qualia-based arguments.
*Key works:* *Brainstorms*, *Consciousness Explained*, *Kinds of Minds*, *Freedom Evolves*, *From Bacteria to Bach and Back*

---

**Ned Block (1942–)**
American philosopher. Block's distinction between phenomenal consciousness (P-consciousness) and access consciousness (A-consciousness) is one of the most important conceptual clarifications in contemporary philosophy of mind. His absent qualia and inverted qualia arguments against functionalism are essential reference points.
*Key works:* "Troubles with Functionalism," "On a Confusion About a Function of Consciousness," "The Harder Problem of Consciousness"

---

**Patricia Churchland (1943–)**
Canadian-American philosopher. Churchland is the leading proponent of neurophilosophy — the integration of neuroscience and philosophy — and of eliminative materialism about folk psychology. Her work advocates that empirical neuroscience should reshape philosophical questions about mind.
*Key works:* *Neurophilosophy*, *The Computational Brain* (with Sejnowski), *Touching a Nerve*

---

**Andy Clark (1957–)**
British philosopher. Clark developed the extended mind thesis (with Chalmers), embodied and enactive approaches to cognition, and predictive processing as a framework for understanding mind. His work bridges philosophy, cognitive science, and AI.
*Key works:* *Being There*, *Supersizing the Mind*, *Surfing Uncertainty*
