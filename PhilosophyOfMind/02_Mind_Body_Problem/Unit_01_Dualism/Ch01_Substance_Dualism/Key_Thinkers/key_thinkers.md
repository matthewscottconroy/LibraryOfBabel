# Key Thinkers: Substance Dualism

---

**René Descartes (1596–1650)**
The canonical substance dualist. Descartes argued for two irreducibly distinct substances: *res cogitans* (thinking substance, defined by thought) and *res extensa* (extended substance, defined by spatial extension). Their interaction via the pineal gland was philosophically problematic, as Princess Elisabeth immediately noted. See also Chapter 2 (Early Modern Turning Points).
*Key works:* *Meditations on First Philosophy*, *Passions of the Soul*, *Principles of Philosophy*

---

**Richard Swinburne (1934–)**
British philosopher of religion and philosopher of mind. Swinburne defends a form of substance dualism grounded in the conceivability of the soul's existence without the body, arguing that this conceivability establishes genuine possibility. He connects his dualism to arguments for theism.
*Key works:* *The Evolution of the Soul*, *Mind, Brain, and Free Will*

---

**John Foster (1941–2009)**
British philosopher. Foster argued for a "mentalist" ontology in which mental items are ontologically primary; his defense of the immaterial self attempted to show that the best explanation of unified consciousness requires positing a non-physical subject.
*Key works:* *The Immaterial Self*, *The Nature of Perception*

---

**C.D. Broad (1887–1971)**
British philosopher who systematically surveyed positions on the mind-body problem. Broad's careful taxonomy of the logical space of possible theories — distinguishing forms of dualism, materialism, and emergence — remains useful for orientation in the debate.
*Key works:* *The Mind and Its Place in Nature*
