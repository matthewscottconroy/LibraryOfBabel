# Further Reading: Property Dualism

**Primary Sources and Key Papers**

- **David Chalmers, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness"** (1995, *Journal of Consciousness Studies*) — The most widely read statement of property dualism grounded in the hard problem.
- **Frank Jackson, "Epiphenomenal Qualia"** (1982) — Introduces the knowledge argument (Mary's Room) as an argument for non-physical qualia.
- **Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"** (1974) — The subjective character of experience as a property not capturable by physical description; essential statement of the property dualist intuition.
- **David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind***, Part II — Full defense of property dualism with discussion of epiphenomenalism and alternatives.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Brian Loar, "Phenomenal States"** (1990) — Influential physicalist response to the knowledge argument; argues phenomenal concepts are a distinct mode of presentation of physical states.
- **Robert Kirk, *Zombies and Consciousness*** (2005) — Careful analysis of the zombie argument as a route to property dualism.
- **Jaegwon Kim, "The Myth of Nonreductive Physicalism"** (1989) — Argues property dualism collapses into epiphenomenalism via the causal exclusion problem.
- **Joseph Levine, "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap"** (1983) — Introduces the explanatory gap concept; property dualism-adjacent but argues for epistemic rather than ontological gap.
- **David Papineau, *Thinking About Consciousness*** (2002) — A physicalist response to property dualism; defends the phenomenal concept strategy.
