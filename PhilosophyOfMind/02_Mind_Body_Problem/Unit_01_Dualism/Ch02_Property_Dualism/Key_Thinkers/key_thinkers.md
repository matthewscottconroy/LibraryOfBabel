# Key Thinkers: Property Dualism

---

**Thomas Nagel (1937–)**
American philosopher. Nagel's "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" argued that subjective experience has an irreducibly perspectival character that no objective physical account can capture. He is a property dualist in the broad sense, though he has also explored "neutral monism" and argued that the existence of consciousness is evidence that our current physical concepts are fundamentally incomplete.
*Key works:* *Mortal Questions*, *The View from Nowhere*, *Mind and Cosmos*

---

**Frank Jackson (1943–)**
Australian philosopher. Jackson's knowledge argument (the Mary case) purports to show that phenomenal properties are non-physical: Mary knows all physical facts but learns something new upon seeing red, so phenomenal properties are not physical. Jackson has since recanted this argument, but the argument itself remains the sharpest statement of the intuition behind property dualism.
*Key works:* "Epiphenomenal Qualia," *From Metaphysics to Ethics*

---

**David Chalmers (1966–)**
See entry in Chapter: Philosophy of Mind Today. Chalmers's property dualism holds that phenomenal properties are fundamental features of the world not reducible to physical properties, while he remains agnostic about whether they require a separate substance.
*Key works:* *The Conscious Mind*, *The Character of Consciousness*

---

**Joseph Levine (1952–)**
American philosopher. Levine introduced the concept of the "explanatory gap" — the fact that even if we know that pain is C-fiber firing, we cannot explain *why* C-fiber firing produces pain rather than no experience at all. He argues this gap is epistemic but generates a distinctive constraint on physicalist theories.
*Key works:* "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap," *Purple Haze*
