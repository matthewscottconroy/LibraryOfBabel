# Further Reading: Type Identity Theory

**Primary Papers**

- **U.T. Place, "Is Consciousness a Brain Process?"** (1956) — The founding paper of type identity theory; Place's careful argument for identifying consciousness with brain processes.
- **J.J.C. Smart, "Sensations and Brain Processes"** (1959) — Develops and defends the identity theory against objections; introduces the topic-neutral analysis of mental terms.
- **Herbert Feigl, "The 'Mental' and the 'Physical'"** (1958) — Independent and philosophically deep defense of the identity thesis.
- **Hilary Putnam, "Psychological Predicates"** (1967) — The multiple realizability argument against type identity theory; the paper that shifted debate toward functionalism.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Jaegwon Kim, *Physicalism, Or Something Near Enough*** (2005) — Argues that functionalism is unstable and type identity theory is the most coherent physicalist option for mental causation.
- **Sydney Shoemaker, *Identity, Cause, and Mind*** (1984) — Collected essays developing a sophisticated functionalism but with important engagement with identity theory.
- **C.V. Borst (ed.), *The Mind/Brain Identity Theory*** (1970) — Classic anthology collecting the founding papers.
- **Brian McLaughlin, "Type Dualism, Token Dualism, and the Causal Efficacy of the Mental"** — On the relationship between type identity, token identity, and mental causation.
