# Nonreductive Physicalism and the Supervenience Thesis

Nonreductive physicalism is the view that mental properties are genuinely distinct from physical properties — not reducible to them — but that all mental facts are nevertheless determined by physical facts. It tries to stake out a position between full reductionism (mental = physical, type identity) and dualism (mental ≠ physical, two distinct kinds of reality).

**The Core Commitments**

A nonreductive physicalist holds:

1. **Physicalism**: All entities are physical, or are constituted by physical entities. There is no non-physical substance.

2. **Token identity**: Each particular mental event or state is identical to a particular physical event or state.

3. **Type non-identity**: Mental types (pain, belief, desire) are not identical to any physical types. There are no psychophysical type-identity claims.

4. **Supervenience**: Mental properties supervene on physical properties. No mental difference without physical difference.

5. **Autonomy**: Psychological explanation is autonomous from physical/neural explanation. The regularities described in psychological terms are real and not eliminable without loss.

**The Tension**

The tension within nonreductive physicalism is the threat of epiphenomenalism. If mental properties are not identical to physical properties, and if physical causal closure holds, then what causal work do mental properties do? Kim's exclusion argument threatens to show that only the physical properties do the causal work, and the mental properties are "along for the ride."

Nonreductive physicalists have developed several responses to this threat:

**Causal Pluralism**: Different levels of description provide genuinely different causal explanations. The mental-level explanation and the physical-level explanation are both real; they describe the same events from different perspectives without competing.

**Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Properties**: Mental properties are not just structural-relational properties of physical events (as the eliminativist would say) but genuinely intrinsic features that make a difference. This requires a non-Humean account of causal powers.

**Levels of Reality**: The world has genuinely different levels of organization, each with its own causal properties. A higher-level property can have causal powers not possessed by the lower-level properties that realize it.

**The Program of Non-Reductive Physicalism**

Philosophers like Brian Loar, Robert Van Gulick, and Claude Peacocke have developed detailed versions of nonreductive physicalism. The common thread is maintaining the reality and explanatory significance of mental properties while acknowledging that they supervene on physical properties.

A key move is the distinction between *explaining* mental properties in physical terms and *eliminating* them. Nonreductive physicalists typically hold that even if a complete physical explanation of behavior is available, mental-level explanations are not thereby dispensable. They pick out different explanatory patterns — psychological laws describe regularities that are invisible at the physical level.

**The Current State**

Nonreductive physicalism is the dominant position in contemporary philosophy of mind, at least in the analytic tradition. Most philosophers are neither identity theorists nor dualists; they hold that mental properties are real, distinct from physical properties (in some sense), but physically grounded.

The main challenge — making this precise while avoiding the exclusion problem — has not been fully met. Different versions of nonreductive physicalism face different versions of the challenge. The field continues to develop accounts of causal relevance, emergent properties, and the levels of reality that try to meet the challenge.

Whether these efforts will ultimately succeed — whether nonreductive physicalism is a stable position or an unstable middle ground that collapses into either reductionism or dualism — is one of the open questions in philosophy of mind.
