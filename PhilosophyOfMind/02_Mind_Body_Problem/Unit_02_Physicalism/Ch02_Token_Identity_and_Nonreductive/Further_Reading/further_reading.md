# Further Reading: Token Identity and Non-Reductive Physicalism

**Primary Papers**

- **Donald Davidson, "Mental Events"** (1970) — Introduces anomalous monism: mental events are token-identical with physical events, but there are no strict psychophysical laws.
- **Hilary Putnam, "The Nature of Mental States"** (1967, also titled "Psychological Predicates") — Multiple realizability as the argument for functionalism over type identity.
- **Jerry Fodor, "Special Sciences"** (1974) — Defends the autonomy of higher-level sciences (including psychology) from reduction to physics.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Jaegwon Kim, "The Myth of Nonreductive Physicalism"** (1989) — The canonical challenge: non-reductive physicalism cannot secure genuine mental causation.
- **Tyler Burge, "Mind-Body Causation and Explanatory Practice"** — Defense of non-reductive physicalism against Kim's exclusion argument.
- **Brian McLaughlin and Karen Bennett, "Supervenience"** (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy) — Clear overview of the supervenience relations that define non-reductive physicalism.
- **Sydney Shoemaker, *Physical Realization*** (2007) — Defends a form of non-reductive physicalism by arguing that mental properties are realized by, but not identical to, physical properties.
- **Louise Antony and Joseph Levine, "The Nomic and the Robust"** — On the relationship between anomalous monism and mental causation.
