# Key Thinkers: Token Identity and Non-Reductive Physicalism

---

**Donald Davidson (1917–2003)**
American philosopher. Davidson's anomalous monism holds that mental events are token-identical with physical events (there are no non-physical events), but there are no strict laws connecting mental and physical descriptions. Mental descriptions are governed by the norms of rationality (consistency, charity), which cannot be reduced to causal laws. This position grounds mental causation while denying type reduction, but has been challenged as collapsing into epiphenomenalism.
*Key works:* *Essays on Actions and Events*, *Inquiries into Truth and Interpretation*

---

**Hilary Putnam (1926–2016)**
American philosopher. Putnam's multiple realizability argument was decisive for non-reductive physicalism: because the same mental state can be realized in different physical substrates (human brains, octopus nervous systems, silicon computers), mental types cannot be type-identical with physical types. Though Putnam later moved away from functionalism, his argument remains foundational.
*Key works:* "The Nature of Mental States," "Minds and Machines," *Reason, Truth, and History*, *Representation and Reality*

---

**Jerry Fodor (1935–2017)**
Fodor's "Special Sciences" paper argued that the special sciences — psychology, economics, biology — are autonomous from physics because their generalizations hold across physical realizations. This supports non-reductive physicalism: psychological laws are genuine but not reducible to physical laws.
*Key works:* *The Language of Thought*, "Special Sciences," *Psychological Explanation*

---

**Tyler Burge (1946–)**
American philosopher. Burge's arguments for anti-individualism (the content of mental states depends partly on the social and physical environment) are relevant to non-reductive physicalism. He has also defended the autonomy of psychological explanation against Kim-style reductionism.
*Key works:* "Individualism and the Mental," "Mind-Body Causation and Explanatory Practice"
