# Further Reading: Eliminative Materialism

**Primary Papers and Books**

- **Paul Churchland, "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes"** (1981) — The canonical statement of eliminativism; argues folk psychology is a false theory to be replaced by mature neuroscience.
- **Patricia Churchland, *Neurophilosophy*** (1986) — Sustained argument for bringing philosophy of mind into alignment with neuroscience.
- **Paul Churchland, *Matter and Consciousness*** (3rd ed., 2013) — Accessible textbook statement of eliminativism; good entry point.

**Secondary Literature — Defenses**

- **Paul Churchland, "Reduction, Qualia, and the Direct Introspection of Brain States"** (1985) — Argues that even phenomenal consciousness can be scientifically explained.
- **William Ramsey, Stephen Stich, and Joseph Garon, "Connectionism, Eliminativism, and the Future of Folk Psychology"** (1990) — Argues connectionist AI gives empirical support to eliminativism.

**Secondary Literature — Critiques**

- **Lynne Rudder Baker, *Saving Belief*** (1987) — Sustained defense of folk psychology against eliminativism.
- **Jerry Fodor, *Psychosemantics***, Chapter 1 — Argues the predictive success of folk psychology vindicates it.
- **Michael Bishop and Stephen Stich, "The Flight to Reference"** (1998) — On the relationship between folk psychology and scientific psychology.
- **Terence Horgan and James Woodward, "Folk Psychology Is Here to Stay"** (1985) — Defense of folk psychological generalizations.
