# Key Thinkers: Eliminative Materialism

---

**Paul Churchland (1942–)**
Canadian philosopher. Churchland's "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes" is the definitive statement of the view that folk psychology — beliefs, desires, intentions — is a false theory that will be replaced by mature neuroscience, as caloric was replaced by thermodynamics. He has also developed positive accounts of how connectionist networks might implement cognition without propositional attitudes.
*Key works:* "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes," *Matter and Consciousness*, *The Engine of Reason, the Seat of the Soul*, *Scientific Realism and the Plasticity of Mind*

---

**Patricia Churchland (1943–)**
Canadian-American philosopher; wife and philosophical collaborator of Paul Churchland. Her *Neurophilosophy* founded the subdiscipline and argued that the traditional questions of philosophy of mind will be progressively answered by neuroscience. More cautious than Paul about wholesale elimination, she focuses on the co-evolution of folk and scientific psychology.
*Key works:* *Neurophilosophy*, *The Computational Brain* (with Sejnowski), *Touching a Nerve*, *Conscience*

---

**W.V.O. Quine (1908–2000)**
American philosopher. Quine's naturalism — the view that philosophy is continuous with natural science — provides the methodological foundation for eliminativism. His concept of "ontological relativity" and his rejection of the analytic/synthetic distinction supported the view that our ordinary mental vocabulary might simply be replaced as science advances.
*Key works:* *Word and Object*, "Two Dogmas of Empiricism," *Ontological Relativity and Other Essays*

---

**Stephen Stich (1943–)**
American philosopher and cognitive scientist. Stich's *From Folk Psychology to Cognitive Science* (1983) argued that folk psychological categories would not survive scientific scrutiny. Though he later moved away from strong eliminativism, his detailed analysis of the relationship between folk and scientific psychology remains important.
*Key works:* *From Folk Psychology to Cognitive Science*, *The Fragmentation of Reason*
