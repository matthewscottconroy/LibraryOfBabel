# Further Reading: Reductive Physicalism

**Key Papers**

- **David Lewis, "An Argument for the Identity Theory"** (1966) — Develops a functionalist version of reductive physicalism via Ramsey-sentence analysis.
- **Frank Jackson, "What Mary Didn't Know"** (1986) — The knowledge argument that has dominated debate about reductive physicalism; Mary knows all physical facts but learns phenomenal facts upon seeing red.
- **Brian Loar, "Phenomenal States"** (1990/1997) — The influential "phenomenal concepts strategy" response: phenomenal concepts pick out physical states through a distinctive mode of presentation.
- **David Papineau, "The Antipathetic Fallacy and the Boundaries of Consciousness"** (1993) — Argues that phenomenal concepts cause us to resist identifying phenomenal properties with physical ones.

**Secondary Literature**

- **Jaegwon Kim, *Physicalism, Or Something Near Enough*** (2005) — Argues that full reduction is required for genuine mental causation; the best case for reductive physicalism.
- **Ned Block and Robert Stalnaker, "Conceptual Analysis, Dualism, and the Explanatory Gap"** (1999) — Physicalist response to the explanatory gap; denies that a priori reduction is required for ontological reduction.
- **Frank Jackson, *From Metaphysics to Ethics*** (1998) — Jackson's mature physicalism including the Canberra Plan approach to mental content.
- **Daniel Stoljar, *Ignorance and Imagination*** (2006) — The "phenomenal concept strategy" examined in detail; defends a version of reductive physicalism.
- **Yujin Nagasawa and Daniel Stoljar (eds.), *There's Something About Mary*** (2004) — Anthology on the knowledge argument with responses.
