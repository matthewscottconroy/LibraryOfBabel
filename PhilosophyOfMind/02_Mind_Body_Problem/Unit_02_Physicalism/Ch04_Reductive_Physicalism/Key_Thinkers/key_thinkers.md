# Key Thinkers: Reductive Physicalism

---

**David Lewis (1941–2001)**
American philosopher and logician. Lewis developed a functionalist reductive physicalism in which mental state types are defined by their causal roles (as specified in folk psychology), and these functional roles are then identified with whatever physical states play them. His modal realism and possible worlds semantics underpinned his analysis of counterfactuals, causation, and supervenience — all key for physicalist philosophy of mind.
*Key works:* "An Argument for the Identity Theory," "Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications," *On the Plurality of Worlds*, *Papers in Metaphysics and Epistemology*

---

**David Papineau (1947–)**
British philosopher and leading contemporary reductive physicalist. Papineau defends physicalism via the "causal closure" argument: since the physical world is causally closed, mental causes must be physical causes. He has also developed the phenomenal concepts strategy to explain why we resist identifying phenomenal properties with physical ones.
*Key works:* *Philosophical Naturalism*, *Thinking About Consciousness*, *Philosophical Devices*

---

**Frank Jackson (1943–)**
Australian philosopher. Though Jackson's original knowledge argument was used against reductive physicalism, he has since become a defender of a version of it through the Canberra Plan: phenomenal properties are identified with the physical properties that play the relevant functional roles in folk psychology. His recantation of the anti-physicalist reading of Mary's case is philosophically significant.
*Key works:* "Epiphenomenal Qualia," "What Mary Didn't Know," *From Metaphysics to Ethics*

---

**Sydney Shoemaker (1931–)**
American philosopher. Shoemaker's functionalism identifies mental properties with functional properties, understood as second-order physical properties. His account of "physical realization" is an important attempt to understand how mental properties are implemented in, but distinct from, the physical without abandoning reduction.
*Key works:* *Self-Knowledge and Self-Identity*, *Identity, Cause, and Mind*, *Physical Realization*
