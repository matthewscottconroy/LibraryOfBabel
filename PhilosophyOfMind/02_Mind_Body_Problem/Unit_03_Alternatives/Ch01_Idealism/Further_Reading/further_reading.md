# Further Reading: Idealism

**Primary Sources**

- **George Berkeley, *Three Dialogues Between Hylas and Philonous*** and ***A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge*** — Berkeley's immaterialism: *esse est percipi* (to be is to be perceived); the most accessible primary text for idealism.
- **Hegel, *Encyclopedia of the Philosophical Sciences*, Part III: Philosophy of Mind** — Hegel's idealist philosophy of mind as absolute spirit coming to know itself.
- **F.H. Bradley, *Appearance and Reality*** (1893) — British absolute idealism; argues that the material world is appearance, with a unified Absolute as the underlying reality.

**Secondary Literature — Historical Idealism**

- **Colin Turbayne, *The Myth of Metaphor*** (1962) — On Berkeley's argument against matter.
- **Kenneth Westphal, *Hegel's Epistemological Realism*** (1989) — Challenges the standard idealist reading of Hegel.

**Contemporary Idealism**

- **Bernardo Kastrup, *Why Materialism Is Baloney*** (2014) and ***The Idea of the World*** (2019) — Popular defenses of analytic idealism — the view that mind is fundamental and matter is an appearance within it.
- **Philip Goff, *Galileo's Error*** (2019) — Argues that panpsychism (which shades into idealism) is better supported than physicalism.
- **John Foster, *The Case for Idealism*** (1982) — Rigorous contemporary defense of British idealism.
- **Robert Adams, "Idealism Vindicated"** — On the coherence of idealist metaphysics.
