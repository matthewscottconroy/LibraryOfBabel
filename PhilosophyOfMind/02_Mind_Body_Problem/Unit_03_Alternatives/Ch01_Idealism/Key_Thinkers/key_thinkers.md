# Key Thinkers: Idealism

---

**George Berkeley (1685–1753)**
Irish bishop and philosopher. Berkeley's immaterialism argued that the very concept of matter as something existing independently of perception is incoherent; all that exists are minds and their ideas. His *esse est percipi* principle and his argument from the relativity of perception are the classic defense of idealism. God's perception guarantees the continued existence of "unperceived" objects.
*Key works:* *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge*, *Three Dialogues Between Hylas and Philonous*

---

**G.W.F. Hegel (1770–1831)**
See also entry in 19th Century Developments. Hegel's absolute idealism argues that reality is the self-development of Spirit (Geist) through dialectical stages, culminating in philosophy's self-knowledge. His idealism is not the subjective idealism of Berkeley but an objective idealism in which thought and being share a rational structure.
*Key works:* *Phenomenology of Spirit*, *Science of Logic*, *Encyclopedia of the Philosophical Sciences*

---

**F.H. Bradley (1846–1924)**
British absolute idealist. Bradley argued that the categories we use to describe the material world — space, time, causation, relation — are internally contradictory and must be appearances of a more fundamental Absolute. His critique of common sense metaphysics influenced Bertrand Russell and G.E. Moore (in opposition), prompting the analytic movement.
*Key works:* *Appearance and Reality*, *Principles of Logic*

---

**Bernardo Kastrup (1974–)**
Contemporary analytic idealist. Kastrup argues that consciousness is the fundamental category of reality and that the physical world is a representation within universal consciousness. He connects his idealism to empirical arguments about the hard problem and dissociative identity disorder as a model for how multiple conscious perspectives arise.
*Key works:* *The Idea of the World*, *Why Materialism Is Baloney*, *Decoding Schopenhauer's Metaphysics*
