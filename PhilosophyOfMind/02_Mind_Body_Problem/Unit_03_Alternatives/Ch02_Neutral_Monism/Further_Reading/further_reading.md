# Further Reading: Neutral Monism

**Primary Sources**

- **William James, *Essays in Radical Empiricism*** (1912) — James's late metaphysics: "pure experience" as the neutral stuff of which both mind and matter are configurations.
- **Bertrand Russell, *The Analysis of Mind*** (1921) and ***The Analysis of Matter*** (1927) — Russell's most sustained engagement with neutral monism; constructs the self from series of mental events without a substantial subject.
- **Ernst Mach, *The Analysis of Sensations*** (1886) — Mach's phenomenalist account of both physical and psychological as elements in experience; influenced James and Russell.

**Secondary Literature**

- **David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind***, Ch. 8 — Discusses neutral monism as a response to the hard problem alongside panpsychism.
- **Leopold Stubenberg, "Neutral Monism"** (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy) — Clear, comprehensive survey of the position and its variants.
- **Michael Lockwood, *Mind, Brain, and the Quantum*** (1989) — Develops a form of neutral monism connecting consciousness research to physics.
- **Grover Maxwell, "Rigid Designators and Mind-Brain Identity"** — On the relationship between neutral monism and the identity theory.
- **Brian McLaughlin, "Emergence and Supervenience"** — On how neutral monism relates to emergence doctrines.
