# Key Thinkers: Neutral Monism

---

**William James (1842–1910)**
In his late "radical empiricism," James proposed that "pure experience" is the fundamental stuff of reality — neutral between mind and matter. Mental and physical are not two substances or two fundamentally different properties; they are different functional groupings of the same experiential elements. This "neutral monism" influenced Russell and anticipates some features of contemporary panpsychism.
*Key works:* *Essays in Radical Empiricism*, "Does 'Consciousness' Exist?"

---

**Bertrand Russell (1872–1970)**
Russell adopted neutral monism in *The Analysis of Mind* (1921), arguing that both mental events and physical events are constructions from neutral elements (sensations/percepts). The self is not a substance but a series of mental events. His "structural realism" about physics — that physical theory captures structural rather than intrinsic properties of matter — connects his neutral monism to his philosophy of physics.
*Key works:* *The Analysis of Mind*, *The Analysis of Matter*, *My Philosophical Development*

---

**Ernst Mach (1838–1916)**
Austrian physicist and philosopher. Mach's "phenomenalism" held that the elements of sensation are the building blocks of both physical and psychological description. His rejection of absolute space and time influenced Einstein; his neutral "elements" influenced James and Russell.
*Key works:* *The Analysis of Sensations*, *The Science of Mechanics*

---

**Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677)**
Often read as a proto-neutral monist: mind and body are two attributes of a single substance (God or Nature). This "dual-aspect" view is the closest historical precursor to neutral monism, though Spinoza's metaphysics is richer and more systematic than the neutral monist programs of James and Russell.
*Key works:* *Ethics*
