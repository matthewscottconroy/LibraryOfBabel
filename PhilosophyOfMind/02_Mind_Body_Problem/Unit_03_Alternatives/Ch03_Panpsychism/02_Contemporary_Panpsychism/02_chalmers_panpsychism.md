# Chalmers and Panpsychism

David Chalmers, the philosopher most associated with formulating the "hard problem of consciousness," has moved toward panpsychism as what he takes to be the most defensible solution to that problem. His version of panpsychism is distinctive in its connection to Russellian monism and in its focus on the structure-intrinsic nature distinction.

**From the Hard Problem to Panpsychism**

Chalmers's famous argument is that consciousness cannot be explained in terms of physical processes as physics characterizes them, because there is always an explanatory gap between structural-functional descriptions and phenomenal facts. No functional analysis of perception, attention, or information processing logically entails any phenomenal facts.

This argument establishes that consciousness is not logically supervenient on the physical as physics conceives it. But it does not establish dualism. Chalmers's alternative: consciousness might be logically supervenient on something deeper — the intrinsic nature of the physical — even if it is not derivable from the structural-relational properties that physics describes.

**Russellian Monism**

Chalmers defends what he calls "Russellian monism" — a position inspired by Russell's observation that physics describes only structural-relational features of the world. Physics tells us that matter has charge, mass, and spin, and how these properties relate to each other. But it does not tell us what these properties consist in intrinsically — what it is, "from the inside," to have charge.

Russell suggested that the intrinsic nature of matter might be phenomenal or proto-phenomenal. Russellian monism holds that what realizes the structural-relational properties of matter is intrinsically experiential. The intrinsic nature of what physics describes as "charge" might be a form of proto-experience.

**Three Versions**

Chalmers distinguishes three versions of Russellian monism:

1. **Panqualityism**: Fundamental physical entities have intrinsic properties that are phenomenal qualities — the same kinds of qualities as our experiences of color, sound, etc. These qualities realize the structural roles that physics attributes to particles.

2. **Panprotopsychism**: Fundamental physical entities have proto-experiential properties — properties that are neither straightforwardly physical (not structural-relational) nor straightforwardly experiential (not phenomenal in the full sense), but that can combine to yield phenomenal properties.

3. **Full panpsychism**: Fundamental physical entities are themselves conscious subjects — they have both experiential content and some form of subjectivity.

Chalmers finds panprotopsychism most defensible, since it is less committal than full panpsychism while still addressing the hard problem.

**The Advantages of Russellian Monism**

Russellian monism has several advantages over standard physicalism and property dualism:

- It does not deny the existence of phenomenal consciousness.
- It does not create an interaction problem (consciousness is constituted by the intrinsic nature of matter, not a separate entity that needs to causally interact with matter).
- It is compatible with physics — it accepts the structural-relational picture of physics while going beyond it to posit intrinsic properties.
- It avoids the combination problem in a specific way: if macro-consciousness is constituted by the intrinsic properties of micro-level matter (not additional properties on top of them), the combination is a matter of organization of existing properties.

**Remaining Difficulties**

Despite these advantages, Chalmers acknowledges several unresolved issues:

**The Revelation Problem**: We have no clear idea what proto-experiential properties are. The concept is defined negatively (not structural, not fully phenomenal) rather than positively. Without a positive characterization, panprotopsychism is more of a label than an explanation.

**The Combination Problem**: How proto-experiential properties at the micro level constitute phenomenal experience at the macro level remains mysterious. Even if we accept that electrons have proto-experiential properties, it is unclear how those properties combine to produce the rich phenomenal experience of seeing red.

**The Unity Problem**: Macro-consciousness is unified in ways that micro-level proto-experiential properties are not. What produces unity at the macro level, and why does the specific organization of matter in a human brain produce consciousness while other organizations do not?

**The Current Status of Panpsychism**

Chalmers's careful philosophical work has helped establish panpsychism as a serious philosophical option — not a New Age fantasy but a rigorous attempt to address a genuine problem. It remains a minority view, but it is taken seriously in way it was not twenty years ago, and the combination problem it faces is now recognized as a specific, tractable philosophical problem rather than a vague objection.
