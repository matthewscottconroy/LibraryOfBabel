# Russellian Monism

Russellian monism is not quite panpsychism and not quite standard physicalism; it is a position that tries to occupy a distinctive space by exploiting Russell's observation about the limits of physical description. It is one of the most philosophically sophisticated positions on the mind-body problem, and its implications have been extensively explored in recent years.

**Russell's Insight**

In *The Analysis of Matter* (1927), Bertrand Russell argued that physics is a purely structural science. It tells us the mathematical structure of the world — how various quantities relate to each other and how they evolve over time — but it is silent about the intrinsic nature of the things that have these structural properties.

Consider an electron. Physics tells us that it has a property "negative charge" that stands in various relations to "positive charge," that it interacts with electromagnetic fields in characteristic ways, that it has a property "mass" that relates to "force" and "acceleration" in Newton's law. But what it is for an electron to have charge — what charge consists in intrinsically, from the "inside" — physics does not say. Physics describes structure; it does not reveal substance.

**The Space for Intrinsic Properties**

If physics is purely structural, it leaves an open question: what intrinsic properties realize the structural relations that physics describes? Something has to be the concrete basis for the structural features of matter. What is it?

Russell's suggestion: perhaps the intrinsic nature of matter is experiential or proto-experiential. This would explain why we find experiential properties in the world (we are part of the physical world, and we have experiences) without positing any additional, non-physical entities.

**Russellian Monism as a Family of Positions**

Russellian monism is not a single position but a family, distinguished by what kind of intrinsic properties are posited:

- **Consciousness as the intrinsic nature of physical properties**: The most panpsychist version. What realizes the structural-relational properties of matter is phenomenal consciousness. Macro-consciousness is constituted by the micro-level consciousness of fundamental particles.

- **Proto-consciousness as the intrinsic nature**: Less committal. Fundamental particles have intrinsic properties that are neither straightforwardly experiential nor straightforwardly non-experiential, but that can constitute experience when appropriately organized.

- **Quiddities**: Some Russellian monists posit "quiddities" — bare intrinsic properties with no specific character — as the intrinsic nature of physical properties. This version does not commit to any experiential character for fundamental matter.

**Why Russellian Monism Is Attractive**

Russellian monism is attractive because it has a shot at solving both the combination problem and the hard problem simultaneously:

**Against the hard problem**: If the intrinsic nature of physical matter is proto-experiential, then consciousness is not something mysteriously added to the physical world; it is the intrinsic nature of the physical world becoming organized into complex patterns.

**Against causal exclusion**: Consciousness is not a separate entity causally competing with physical processes; it is the intrinsic nature of those same physical processes. There is no overdetermination because there is only one causal process, described structurally by physics and intrinsically as conscious.

**Against the knowledge argument**: Mary learns something new when she sees red for the first time because she gains direct acquaintance with an intrinsic property (the experiential property of redness-processing). Before her release, she knew the structural-relational properties of color processing; after, she knows the intrinsic nature.

**The Remaining Challenges**

**The combination problem**: Even granting proto-experiential intrinsic properties at the micro level, explaining how they combine to form macro-level consciousness remains difficult. What is the relationship between the intrinsic properties of electrons and the experience of seeing red?

**The evolution of consciousness**: If all matter has intrinsic proto-experiential properties, why do only some physical systems (brains) generate unified, rich consciousness? What is special about biological neural networks?

**Empirical traction**: Russellian monism is hard to test. It accepts all the structural findings of physics while claiming there are also intrinsic properties that physics cannot detect. Is this a coherent scientific hypothesis or an untestable metaphysical addendum?

**The Future of Russellian Monism**

Russellian monism has attracted increasing philosophical attention in the last two decades, with serious work by Chalmers, Goff, Montero, Strawson, and others. Whether it can be developed into a fully satisfying account of the place of consciousness in nature — overcoming the combination problem and maintaining scientific credibility — is an open question that represents one of the frontiers of philosophy of mind.
