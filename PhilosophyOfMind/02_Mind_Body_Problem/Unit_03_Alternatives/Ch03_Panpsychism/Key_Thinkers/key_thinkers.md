# Key Thinkers: Panpsychism

---

**Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716)**
A historical precursor to panpsychism: Leibniz's monads are the ultimate constituents of reality and are all, in their way, minds — perceiving and appetitive, though at varying levels of clarity and distinctness. His monadology is a form of panpsychism avant la lettre, though it is also idealism (there is no matter at the fundamental level, only minds).
*Key works:* *Monadology*, *Discourse on Metaphysics*

---

**Galen Strawson (1952–)**
British philosopher. Strawson's "realistic monism" argues that physicalism itself — if taken seriously as a claim that everything is physical, and if consciousness is real — entails panpsychism: there must be experiential properties at the micro-level that give rise to macro-level experience without discontinuity. His argument from the impossibility of radical emergence has reinvigorated the debate.
*Key works:* "Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism," *Selves*

---

**Philip Goff (1978–)**
British philosopher and the leading contemporary advocate of panpsychism. Goff argues that Galilean science excluded qualitative properties (colors, sounds as experienced) from the material world by definition, creating the hard problem. Restoring qualitative properties to the fundamental level — panpsychism — dissolves the problem.
*Key works:* *Consciousness and Fundamental Reality*, *Galileo's Error*, *Why? The Purpose of the Universe*

---

**Giulio Tononi (1960–)**
Italian-American neuroscientist. Tononi's Integrated Information Theory (IIT) entails panpsychism: consciousness is identified with integrated information (phi), which is present in any system with causal integration above zero. IIT gives panpsychism a precise, measurable form and connects it directly to neuroscience.
*Key works:* "Consciousness as Integrated Information: A Provisional Manifesto," "Consciousness: Here, There and Everywhere?" (with Koch)
