# Further Reading: The Hard Problem of Consciousness

## Primary Sources

**David Chalmers, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" (1995)** — The paper that introduced the phrase "the hard problem." Chalmers distinguishes functional, behavioral, and cognitive phenomena (easy problems) from the question of why physical processes give rise to subjective experience at all. Essential first reading.

**Joseph Levine, "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap" (1983)** — Levine introduced the concept of the "explanatory gap" — the sense that even if identity theorists are correct that pain = C-fibers firing, this identity is unexplained in a way that straightforward scientific identities are not. Precursor and companion to Chalmers' hard problem.

**Frank Jackson, "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982)** — Jackson's knowledge argument (the Mary story) presents a thought experiment designed to show that physicalism is false: Mary knows all physical facts about color vision but learns something new when she first sees red. A pivotal paper for the conceivability-based anti-physicalist tradition.

**Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974)** — Nagel argues that the subjective character of experience — what it is like — resists objective scientific reduction, presenting one of the earliest and most influential versions of the explanatory gap problem.

## Secondary Literature

**Daniel Dennett, "Quining Qualia" (1988)** — Dennett argues that the concept of qualia is incoherent and that intuitions about absent or inverted qualia do not withstand scrutiny. A rigorous physicalist response that has generated substantial debate.

**Brian Loar, "Phenomenal States" (1990)** — Loar defends a type-identity version of physicalism against the knowledge argument by arguing that phenomenal concepts refer directly to physical properties while differing in mode of presentation. Influential in the phenomenal concepts strategy.

**Katalin Balog, "Phenomenal Concepts" (2012)** — Surveys and critically evaluates the phenomenal concepts strategy — the family of responses to Chalmers and Jackson that appeal to special features of our concepts of experience rather than the nature of the experiences themselves.

**Philip Goff, *Galileo's Error* (2019)** — Goff argues that Galileo's mathematical-physical framework for science deliberately excluded qualities from the physical domain, generating the hard problem. He defends panpsychism as the most coherent solution.

**Guven Guzeldere, "The Many Faces of Consciousness: A Field Guide" (1997)** — A useful survey of the landscape of consciousness debates, distinguishing multiple uses of "consciousness" and mapping the logical space of solutions to the hard problem.
