# Key Thinkers: Higher-Order Theories of Consciousness

---

**David Rosenthal** (b. 1939)

Rosenthal is the principal architect of higher-order thought (HOT) theory. His central thesis is that a mental state is conscious just in case it is accompanied by a suitable higher-order thought — a thought, not itself conscious, that one is in that very mental state. States that are not targeted by any HOT are, on this view, unconscious even if they influence behavior. Rosenthal argues the theory explains the contrast between conscious and unconscious mental states in a way that does not appeal to intrinsic qualitative properties, making it compatible with a broadly functionalist, naturalist worldview.

*Key works:* "Two Concepts of Consciousness" (1986); *Consciousness and Mind* (2005); "Explaining the Subjective Character of Consciousness" (2004)

---

**William Lycan** (b. 1945)

Lycan defends higher-order perception (HOP) or inner sense theory as an alternative to Rosenthal's HOT approach. On Lycan's view, a mental state is conscious when an inner scanning mechanism (analogous to perception) takes it as its object. Lycan emphasizes that inner monitoring is a functional process embedded in cognitive architecture, not a distinct conscious episode. He has been a persistent critic of qualia-based objections to functionalism.

*Key works:* *Consciousness* (1987); *Consciousness and Experience* (1996); "Perspectival Representation and the Knowledge Argument" (1996)

---

**Peter Carruthers** (b. 1952)

Carruthers defends a "dispositional" or "consumer" version of HOT theory, arguing that a state is phenomenally conscious when it is available to, rather than actually the target of, higher-order representations. He integrates this with his broader thesis that humans lack direct introspective access to most mental processes, citing experimental evidence that self-knowledge is largely confabulated. Carruthers situates consciousness within a framework that emphasizes the modularity of mindreading and the evolutionary function of inner monitoring.

*Key works:* *Language, Thought and Consciousness* (1996); *The Opacity of Mind* (2011); *Human and Animal Minds* (2019)

---

**Uriah Kriegel** (b. 1974)

Kriegel develops a "same-order" or self-representational theory of consciousness as a sophisticated alternative to standard HOT accounts. On his view, a state is conscious not because it is targeted by a numerically distinct higher-order state, but because it represents itself — a conscious state has an intrinsic self-representational structure. This avoids the "targetlessness" objection to HOT theory (that a HOT could exist without the lower-order state it purports to be about).

*Key works:* *Subjective Consciousness: A Self-Representational Theory* (2009); *The Sources of Intentionality* (2011); *Brentano's Philosophical System* (2018)
