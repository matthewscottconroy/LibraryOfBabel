# Key Thinkers: Global Workspace Theory

---

**Bernard Baars** (b. 1946)

Baars is the originator of global workspace theory (GWT), proposing in 1988 that consciousness can be understood as a "global workspace" — a limited-capacity cognitive system that integrates information from specialized, unconscious processors and broadcasts it widely across the brain. Unconscious processes run in parallel and independently; conscious contents are those that "win" access to the workspace and are thereby made available to many other cognitive systems simultaneously. GWT is primarily a functional and psychological theory, though Baars has connected it to neuroscience.

*Key works:* *A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness* (1988); *In the Theater of Consciousness* (1997); *The Cognitive Revolution in Psychology* (1986)

---

**Stanislas Dehaene** (b. 1965)

Dehaene developed the "global neuronal workspace" (GNW) model, grounding Baars' psychological framework in specific neuroscientific architecture. He identifies conscious access with the "ignition" of a widespread network of frontal and parietal neurons connected by long-range axons, which amplifies and broadcasts information that would otherwise remain localized. Dehaene has used experimental paradigms such as masking, inattentional blindness, and studies of patients under anesthesia to identify neural signatures of conscious and unconscious processing.

*Key works:* *Consciousness and the Brain* (2014); "Toward a Cognitive Neuroscience of Consciousness" (2001, with Changeux); *How We Learn* (2020)

---

**Jean-Pierre Changeux** (b. 1936)

Changeux, a neuroscientist, collaborated with Dehaene to develop the neural implementation of GWT. His earlier work on allosteric proteins and neural Darwinism provided a background for thinking about how large-scale neural dynamics give rise to cognition. Together with Dehaene, he elaborated a formal neural network model in which conscious states correspond to self-sustained, reverberating activity in a global network.

*Key works:* *Neuronal Man* (1985); "A Neuronal Model of a Global Workspace in Effortful Cognitive Tasks" (1998, with Dehaene); *The Physiology of Truth* (2004)

---

**Ned Block** (b. 1942)

While not a defender of GWT, Block's work is central to debates about it. He argues that GWT conflates access consciousness (information available for reasoning and report) with phenomenal consciousness (qualitative experience), and that experiments supporting GWT show only that global broadcasting correlates with access, leaving phenomenal consciousness under-explained. His challenge has driven ongoing refinements within GWT research.

*Key works:* "On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness" (1995); "Perceptual Consciousness Overflows Cognitive Access" (2011); "The Anna Karenina Theory of the Unconscious" (2023)
