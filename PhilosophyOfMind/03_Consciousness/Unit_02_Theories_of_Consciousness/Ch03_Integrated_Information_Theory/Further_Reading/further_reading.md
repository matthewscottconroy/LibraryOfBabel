# Further Reading: Integrated Information Theory

## Primary Sources

**Giulio Tononi, "Consciousness as Integrated Information: A Provisional Manifesto" (2008)** — Tononi presents the core thesis of IIT: consciousness is identical to integrated information (phi, Φ) — the amount of information generated by a system above and beyond its parts. This accessible paper is the best starting point for understanding the theory's motivation and structure.

**Giulio Tononi, Melanie Boly, Marcello Massimini, and Christof Koch, "Integrated Information Theory: From Consciousness to Its Physical Substrate" (2016)** — An authoritative overview of IIT 3.0, detailing the five axioms (existence, composition, information, integration, exclusion) and corresponding postulates, and connecting the theory to empirical methods.

**Giulio Tononi, "An Information Integration Theory of Consciousness" (2004)** — The original formal statement of IIT, arguing that the theory can explain cases like cerebellar vs. cerebral cortex differences in consciousness, and account for the distinction between conscious and unconscious processing.

## Secondary Literature

**Christof Koch, *The Feeling of Life Itself* (2019)** — Koch's accessible defense of IIT, arguing that it is the leading scientific theory of consciousness. He examines its implications for animal consciousness, disorders of consciousness, and the possibility of machine consciousness.

**Scott Aaronson, "Why I Am Not An Integrated Information Theorist" (2014)** — A sharp critique from a computer scientist, arguing that IIT entails counterintuitive consequences: simple logical systems (like expander graphs) would have very high phi while the human cortex might have surprisingly low phi by some measures. Widely discussed.

**Daniel Dennett, "Facing Up to the Hard Question of Consciousness" (2019)** — Dennett critiques IIT as a theory that smuggles in dualist assumptions under the guise of information theory, and argues that phi is not a plausible measure of anything philosophically significant.

**Ned Block, "The Deficiency of IIT" (2023)** — Block argues that IIT conflates phenomenal and access consciousness, and that its axioms do not support its panpsychist implications. A systematic philosophical critique.

**Max Tegmark, "Consciousness as a State of Matter" (2015)** — Explores the claim that consciousness could be a distinct state of matter characterized by integrated information, developing a "perceptronium" hypothesis. Extends IIT's implications into physics.
