# Key Thinkers: Integrated Information Theory

---

**Giulio Tononi** (b. 1960)

Tononi is the principal developer of Integrated Information Theory (IIT), first proposed in 2004. IIT begins from phenomenological axioms — consciousness exists, is structured, is informative, is integrated, and is definite — and derives physical "postulates" about what must be true of the substrate of consciousness. The theory's central measure, phi (Φ), quantifies the amount of information generated by a system as a whole beyond its parts. Tononi argues that IIT uniquely explains the richness of phenomenal experience, the difference between cerebral cortex and cerebellum in generating consciousness, and the effects of sleep and anesthesia.

*Key works:* "An Information Integration Theory of Consciousness" (2004); "Consciousness as Integrated Information" (2008); "Integrated Information Theory: From Consciousness to Its Physical Substrate" (2016)

---

**Christof Koch** (b. 1956)

Koch is a neuroscientist who collaborated with Francis Crick on the neural correlates of consciousness (NCC) program and later became the leading scientific advocate for IIT. He argues that IIT is the best available scientific theory because it makes principled, testable predictions and provides a coherent framework for thinking about consciousness across species and artificial systems. Koch has championed the empirical study of NCC using neuroimaging and lesion methods and is a central figure in connecting IIT to clinical research on disorders of consciousness.

*Key works:* *The Quest for Consciousness* (2004); *The Feeling of Life Itself* (2019); "Neural Correlates of Consciousness: Progress and Problems" (2016, with Tsuchiya)

---

**Marcello Massimini** (b. 1970)

Massimini is a neuroscientist whose work on transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) combined with EEG has provided the most direct empirical tests of IIT predictions. His "perturbational complexity index" (PCI) — a measure of the brain's response complexity to TMS — has been used to assess the level of consciousness in clinical populations, including patients with disorders of consciousness, and tracks closely with predictions based on integrated information. His work bridges IIT's theoretical framework and practical clinical application.

*Key works:* "Breakdown of Cortical Effective Connectivity During Sleep" (2005); "Probing the Conscious Mind with TMS" (2012); "Perturbational Complexity Index" (2013, with Casali et al.)

---

**David Chalmers** (b. 1966)

Chalmers is not a developer of IIT but has engaged closely with it, often noting its structural similarities to his own property dualism. He has argued that IIT's core insight — that consciousness is constituted by integrated information — could be interpreted as a version of panpsychism. His public "adversarial collaboration" with Koch on the relative merits of IIT vs. Global Workspace Theory has become one of the most visible debates in consciousness science.

*Key works:* *The Conscious Mind* (1996); "The Meta-Problem of Consciousness" (2018); Adversarial Collaboration on IIT vs. GWT with Koch (published 2023 in *Nature Human Behaviour*)
