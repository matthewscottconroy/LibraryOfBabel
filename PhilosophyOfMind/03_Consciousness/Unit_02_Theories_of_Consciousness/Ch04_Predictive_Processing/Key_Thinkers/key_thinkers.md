# Key Thinkers: Predictive Processing and Consciousness

---

**Karl Friston** (b. 1959)

Friston is the neuroscientist responsible for the free-energy principle, the mathematical framework underlying predictive processing. He proposes that biological agents minimize variational free energy — a measure of the difference between an agent's model and its sensory data — through either perceptual inference (updating models) or active inference (acting to change sensory data). Friston connects the framework to the Bayesian brain hypothesis, homeostasis, and evolutionary theory, providing the most ambitious unifying account in contemporary theoretical neuroscience.

*Key works:* "A Theory of Cortical Responses" (2005); "The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?" (2010); "Active Inference and Epistemic Value" (2015)

---

**Andy Clark** (b. 1957)

Clark is the philosopher most responsible for developing predictive processing into a broad theory of mind. He argues that the brain maintains a hierarchical generative model of the world, constantly generating predictions and updating them based on prediction errors. Clark sees PP as consistent with and indeed supporting an embodied, extended view of cognition, and has used it to revisit classic debates about perception, action, and the self. His work bridges theoretical neuroscience and analytic philosophy of mind.

*Key works:* *Being There* (1997); *Surfing Uncertainty* (2016); "Whatever Next?" (2013)

---

**Jakob Hohwy** (b. 1971)

Hohwy has developed the philosophical implications of predictive processing most systematically, particularly regarding perception and consciousness. He argues that PP supports a form of "inferentialism" about perception — we never directly perceive the world but only infer its hidden causes from sensory signals. He applies this to the self, mental illness, and the boundaries of the mind, and engages critically with enactivist alternatives that resist the PP framework's internalist tendencies.

*Key works:* *The Predictive Mind* (2013); "The Variational Neuroethology of Dread" (2017); "Predictive Processing and the Representation Wars" (2020)

---

**Anil Seth** (b. 1972)

Seth, a neuroscientist at the University of Sussex, has developed the "controlled hallucination" view of perception, arguing that all conscious experience — including veridical perception — is the brain's actively generated best guess about the causes of sensory signals. Seth extends this view to selfhood, arguing that the sense of being a self is itself a form of predictive inference about the body's internal states. His public lectures and book have made PP-based approaches to consciousness prominent beyond specialist audiences.

*Key works:* *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness* (2021); "Theories of Consciousness" (2021, with Bayne); "The Cybernetic Bayesian Brain" (2015)
