# Key Thinkers: Biological Naturalism

---

**John Searle** (b. 1932)

Searle defends "biological naturalism" — the view that consciousness and intentionality are genuine biological phenomena caused by and realized in the brain, but irreducible to computational or functional descriptions. Consciousness is, for Searle, an emergent feature of the brain in the way liquidity is an emergent feature of water molecules — a real higher-level property, but one fully caused by lower-level physical facts. He resists both eliminativism (consciousness is not real) and strong functionalism (consciousness can be multiply realized in any system that implements the right functional organization). His Chinese Room argument is his central contribution to debates about artificial intelligence and functionalism.

*Key works:* "Minds, Brains, and Programs" (1980); *Intentionality* (1983); *The Rediscovery of the Mind* (1992); *Mind: A Brief Introduction* (2004)

---

**Antonio Damasio** (b. 1944)

Damasio is a neuroscientist whose work supports a biological emphasis on the body's role in consciousness. He argues that consciousness depends on the brain's ongoing representation of the body's internal states (the "proto-self" and "core self"), and that emotion and feeling are not separate from cognition but constitutive of it. His patient studies (e.g., Phineas Gage, patients with ventromedial prefrontal damage) show that consciousness and rational agency are disrupted when neural-bodily integration breaks down.

*Key works:* *Descartes' Error* (1994); *The Feeling of What Happens* (1999); *Self Comes to Mind* (2010)

---

**Gerald Edelman** (1929–2014)

Edelman developed a biological theory of consciousness centered on "neural Darwinism" or the theory of neuronal group selection (TNGS). He proposed that consciousness arises from re-entrant signaling between the thalamocortical system (generating primary consciousness) and areas involved in language and memory (generating higher-order consciousness). Edelman opposed computational accounts of mind, arguing that the brain's selectional processes are fundamentally different from algorithmic computation.

*Key works:* *Neural Darwinism* (1987); *The Remembered Present* (1989); *Bright Air, Brilliant Fire* (1992); *A Universe of Consciousness* (2000, with Tononi)

---

**Patricia Churchland** (b. 1943)

While not a defender of Searle's specific biological naturalism, Churchland's neurophilosophy represents a closely related commitment to grounding theories of mind in neuroscience rather than in functional or computational abstractions. She argues that folk psychology is likely to be replaced by mature neuroscience, and that the categories of consciousness, belief, and desire may not survive scientific reduction. Her work challenges both Searle's anti-reductionism and computationalist functionalism.

*Key works:* *Neurophilosophy* (1986); *The Engine of Reason, the Seat of the Soul* (1995, with Sejnowski); *Touching a Nerve* (2013)
