# Key Thinkers: Sleep, Dreams, and Hypnosis

---

**J. Allan Hobson** (1933–2021)

Hobson was a sleep scientist who transformed the scientific study of dreams with his activation-synthesis hypothesis (with Robert McCarley, 1977). He proposed that REM sleep is characterized by random activation of the brainstem, which the cortex synthesizes into dream narratives. This challenged Freudian interpretation and grounded dream theory in neuroscience. Later, Hobson developed a "proto-consciousness" hypothesis, suggesting that REM dreaming is a form of consciousness ancestral to waking consciousness, serving to develop and maintain the brain's capacity for simulation.

*Key works:* "The Brain as a Dream State Generator" (1977, with McCarley); *The Dreaming Brain* (1988); *Dreaming as Delirium* (1999); *Dream Life: An Experimental Memoir* (2011)

---

**Antti Revonsuo** (b. 1963)

Revonsuo is a Finnish cognitive scientist whose "threat simulation theory" provides an evolutionary account of dreaming. He argues that the biological function of dreaming is to simulate threatening social and physical scenarios, training the dreamer's responses to danger. His broader work on consciousness has also explored the "zombie problem" and the relationship between phenomenal consciousness and neural processes.

*Key works:* "The Reinterpretation of Dreams" (2000); *Inner Presence: Consciousness as a Biological Phenomenon* (2006); *Consciousness: The Science of Subjectivity* (2010)

---

**Ernest Hilgard** (1904–2001)

Hilgard was a Stanford psychologist whose "neodissociation theory" became influential in hypnosis research. He proposed that hypnosis involves a division of the "executive control system" such that one part of the subject's mind follows hypnotic suggestion while another part — the "hidden observer" — retains awareness of the true situation. His experimental work on hypnotic analgesia provided evidence for the hidden observer phenomenon, though the interpretation remains contested.

*Key works:* *Hypnotic Susceptibility* (1965); *Divided Consciousness* (1977); "The Problem of Divided Consciousness" (1994)

---

**Mark Solms** (b. 1961)

Solms is a neuropsychologist who has revived interest in Freudian approaches to dreams through neuropsychological evidence. His research on patients with brain damage suggests that the loss of dreaming is associated with damage to forebrain regions involved in motivation and desire (SEEKING system), rather than the brainstem REM-generating system as Hobson suggested. He argues this supports a psychoanalytic interpretation: dreaming is driven by motivated desire, not random activation.

*Key works:* *The Neuropsychology of Dreams* (1997); *The Brain and the Inner World* (2002, with Turnbull); *The Hidden Spring* (2021)
