# Key Thinkers: Disorders of Consciousness

---

**Adrian Owen** (b. 1966)

Owen is a neuroscientist at Western University whose landmark 2006 Science paper demonstrated that a patient diagnosed as in a vegetative state could respond to verbal instructions by activating specific brain regions in fMRI scans. His subsequent research developed paradigms allowing some patients to "communicate" through mental imagery, challenging received diagnoses and raising profound questions about the relationship between behavioral signs and inner awareness. His work has transformed both clinical practice and philosophical debates about consciousness.

*Key works:* "Detecting Awareness in the Vegetative State" (2006); "Using Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging to Detect Covert Awareness in the Vegetative State" (2007); *Into the Gray Zone* (2017)

---

**Steven Laureys** (b. 1968)

Laureys, a Belgian neurologist, is a pioneer in the clinical and scientific study of coma, vegetative state, and minimally conscious state. His neuroimaging research has mapped the neural correlates of different levels of consciousness impairment and helped establish diagnostic criteria distinguishing vegetative from minimally conscious states. He has also investigated "locked-in syndrome" — in which patients are fully conscious but cannot move — as the clearest dissociation between behavioral responses and inner awareness.

*Key works:* "The Neural Correlate of (Un)awareness" (2005); "Death, Unconsciousness and the Brain" (2005); *The Boundaries of Consciousness* (ed., 2005)

---

**Nicholas Schiff** (b. 1961)

Schiff is a neurologist and consciousness researcher at Weill Cornell whose work focuses on the neural circuits underlying recovery of consciousness after severe brain injury. His mesocircuit hypothesis proposes that many disorders of consciousness result from disruption of thalamocortical communication and that targeted deep brain stimulation may restore function. He has led clinical trials using DBS to promote recovery in minimally conscious patients.

*Key works:* "Recovery of Consciousness After Brain Injury" (2010); "Thalamocortical Contributions to Disorders of Consciousness" (2005); DBS trial in minimally conscious state (2007)

---

**Joseph Fins** (b. 1959)

Fins is a bioethicist whose work focuses on the rights and moral status of patients with disorders of consciousness. He argues that patients in minimally conscious states retain moral standing that is inadequately recognized in current medical, legal, and ethical frameworks. His book *Rights Come to Mind* documents cases in which proper diagnosis and treatment were denied to patients who had residual awareness, and calls for systematic reforms.

*Key works:* *Rights Come to Mind* (2015); "Disorders of Consciousness and Disordered Care" (2013); "A Proposed Ethical Framework for Interventional Cognitive Neuroscience" (2007)
