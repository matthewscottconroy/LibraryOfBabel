# Further Reading: Aboutness and Intentionality

## Primary Sources

**Franz Brentano, *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint* (1874, trans. 1973)** — Brentano reintroduced the medieval scholastic concept of "intentional inexistence" to distinguish mental phenomena from physical ones: mental states are characterized by being "directed upon" an object, whether or not that object exists. This is the founding text of the modern philosophy of intentionality.

**Edmund Husserl, *Logical Investigations*, Vol. 2 (1901, trans. 2001)** — Husserl elaborates Brentano's intentionality thesis, distinguishing the act (noesis) from its content (noema) and arguing that intentionality is the fundamental structure of consciousness. Investigation V provides the most detailed analysis of intentional acts.

**John Searle, *Intentionality: An Essay in the Philosophy of Mind* (1983)** — Searle argues that intentionality is a biological property of mental states with "conditions of satisfaction" — what must be the case for the state to be fulfilled. He distinguishes derived intentionality (as in language) from original intentionality (as in minds).

## Secondary Literature

**Tim Crane, *Elements of Mind* (2001)** — A clear introductory treatment of intentionality and its relationship to consciousness. Crane defends the irreducibility of intentional notions and examines non-existent objects and the problem of "intentional inexistence."

**Alex Byrne, "Intentionalism" (2001)** — Argues that phenomenal properties of experience are representational properties. A useful entry point for the debate between pure representationalism and accounts that treat qualia as non-representational.

**Colin McGinn, *Mental Content* (1989)** — A comprehensive treatment of the nature of mental content, examining causal, conceptual role, and teleological approaches. Engages with the debate between internalism and externalism.

**Terrence Horgan and John Tienson, "The Intentionality of Phenomenology and the Phenomenology of Intentionality" (2002)** — Argues for an intimate connection between intentionality and phenomenal consciousness, against views that cleanly separate the two.

**Graham Priest, *Towards Non-Being* (2005)** — Priest uses dialetheist logic to analyze intentionality toward non-existent objects, providing a rigorous formal treatment of the "intentional inexistence" problem.
