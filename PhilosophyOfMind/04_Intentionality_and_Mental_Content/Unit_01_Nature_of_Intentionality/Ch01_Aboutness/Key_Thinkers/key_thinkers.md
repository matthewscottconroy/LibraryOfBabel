# Key Thinkers: Aboutness and Intentionality

---

**Franz Brentano** (1838–1917)

Brentano is the source of the modern philosophical tradition of intentionality. In *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint* (1874), he argued that what distinguishes mental phenomena from physical phenomena is "intentional inexistence" — the property of being directed at or about an object that may be "inexistent" (present only in the mind, as in imagination or hallucination). Brentano made intentionality the defining mark of the mental, a claim that has structured the field ever since. He also argued for the primacy of first-person, introspective access to mental states.

*Key works:* *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint* (1874); *On the Origin of Our Knowledge of Right and Wrong* (1889); *Descriptive Psychology* (1982, posthumous)

---

**Edmund Husserl** (1859–1938)

Husserl developed Brentano's notion of intentionality into the systematic method of phenomenology. He distinguished the intentional act (noesis) from its content (noema) and from the intended object itself, allowing analysis of cases in which the object does not exist or is not veridically represented. Husserl's analyses of perceptual, imaginative, and signifying intentionality across the *Logical Investigations* and *Ideas* remain the most detailed phenomenological accounts available.

*Key works:* *Logical Investigations* (1900–01); *Ideas I* (1913); *Cartesian Meditations* (1931)

---

**John Searle** (b. 1932)

Searle argues that intentionality is a biological property of the mind — not a functional or relational property — with "conditions of satisfaction." For Searle, beliefs, desires, perceptions, and intentions are intrinsically directed at states of affairs, and this directedness is not reducible to causal or computational relations. He contrasts "original" intentionality (possessed by genuine mental states) with "derived" intentionality (possessed by symbols and artifacts only by virtue of the original intentionality of minds that use them).

*Key works:* *Intentionality* (1983); *The Rediscovery of the Mind* (1992); *Mind: A Brief Introduction* (2004)

---

**Tim Crane** (b. 1962)

Crane defends a broadly Brentanian view of intentionality, arguing that it is irreducible and that intentional relations differ fundamentally from ordinary causal relations. He is particularly concerned with the problem of "intentional inexistence" — how we can think of or be directed at objects that do not exist — and argues that standard approaches (Fregean senses, modes of presentation, fictional objects) do not fully resolve this problem.

*Key works:* *Elements of Mind* (2001); *The Mechanical Mind* (2003); *The Objects of Thought* (2013)
