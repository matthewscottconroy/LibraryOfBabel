# Further Reading: Propositional Attitudes

## Primary Sources

**Bertrand Russell, "On Propositions: What They Are and How They Mean" (1919)** — Russell develops the concept of propositional attitudes — mental states like belief, desire, and hope that take propositions as their objects. A foundational text in the logical analysis of mental states.

**Gottlob Frege, "On Sense and Reference" (1892, trans. 1952)** — Frege's distinction between sense (Sinn) and reference (Bedeutung) is essential for understanding propositional attitudes: beliefs about Hesperus and Phosphorus can differ even though both are Venus, because the senses differ. Central to debates about narrow vs. wide content.

**Roderick Chisholm, *Perceiving: A Philosophical Study* (1957)** — Chisholm develops a careful analysis of intentional contexts and the "intentional inexistence" of mental content, arguing that ordinary language reports of propositional attitudes resist reduction to non-intentional vocabulary.

**Jerry Fodor, "Propositional Attitudes" (1978)** — Fodor argues that propositional attitudes are real and scientifically tractable, and that the computational theory of mind provides their best account: beliefs and desires are computational relations to mental representations with propositional content.

## Secondary Literature

**Stephen Stich, *From Folk Psychology to Cognitive Science* (1983)** — Stich develops a "syntactic theory of mind," arguing that propositional attitude psychology will not survive the transition to cognitive science, since neural computation is indifferent to content.

**Jerry Fodor, *Psychosemantics* (1987)** — Defends propositional attitude psychology against eliminativist challenge, arguing that beliefs and desires are causally efficacious and that their content can be naturalized via causal-informational semantics.

**Donald Davidson, "Mental Events" (1970)** — Davidson argues for "anomalous monism": mental events are identical to physical events but cannot be subsumed under strict physical laws; the propositional attitudes are characterized by their role in a web of rationality.

**Mark Schroeder, "Propositional Attitudes" (2012)** — A clear survey of the literature on propositional attitudes, covering debates about their nature, their content, and their role in explanation.
