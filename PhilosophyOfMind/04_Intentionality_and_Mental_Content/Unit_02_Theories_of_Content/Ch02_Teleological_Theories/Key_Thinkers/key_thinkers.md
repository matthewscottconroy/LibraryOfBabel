# Key Thinkers: Teleological Theories of Mental Content

---

**Ruth Millikan** (b. 1933)

Millikan is the principal architect of teleosemantics. In *Language, Thought, and Other Biological Categories* (1984), she argued that mental representations have content in virtue of their biological function — what they were "supposed" to do in the evolutionary history of the organism. A representation's content is the condition under which it historically succeeded in fulfilling its biological purpose. Her account handles misrepresentation naturally: a state misrepresents when it fails to fulfill its proper function. She extended this framework to language and communication.

*Key works:* *Language, Thought, and Other Biological Categories* (1984); "Biosemantics" (1989); *White Queen Psychology and Other Essays* (1993); *Beyond Concepts* (2017)

---

**David Papineau** (b. 1947)

Papineau defends a teleological account that grounds content in the biological functions of desire and belief-like states. Desires have content — represent their satisfaction conditions — because those conditions are what the desires were selected to produce; beliefs have content — represent truth conditions — because those truth conditions are what they were selected to reliably correlate with. Papineau has used this framework to address the mind-body problem and to argue for a thoroughgoing physicalism about mental content.

*Key works:* *Reality and Representation* (1987); *Philosophical Naturalism* (1993); *Thinking about Consciousness* (2002)

---

**Karen Neander** (b. 1953)

Neander has developed the most sophisticated contemporary version of teleosemantics, combining selected functions with informational relations. She argues that a representation represents X if the mechanism that produces it has the function (by natural selection) of detecting X — where "detecting" involves a reliable, non-accidental causal relation. This informational teleosemantics handles difficult cases including swampman, novel content, and misrepresentation more elegantly than pure selected-effects theories.

*Key works:* "Functions as Selected Effects" (1991); *A Mark of the Mental* (2017); "Teleological Theories of Mental Content" (2004)

---

**Fred Dretske** (1932–2013)

Although Dretske's primary account is causal-informational rather than teleological, his work in *Explaining Behavior* (1988) introduces a temporal dimension — the learning period — that gives content a quasi-teleological status: the function of the representation is established by the history of its reliable co-occurrence with its content. This hybrid approach has been influential in connecting causal and teleological strands of content theory.

*Key works:* *Explaining Behavior* (1988); *Naturalizing the Mind* (1995); *Perception, Knowledge and Belief* (2000)
