# Further Reading: Inferential Role Semantics

## Primary Sources

**Wilfrid Sellars, "Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind" (1956)** — Sellars attacks the "Myth of the Given" and argues that the meaning of mental and linguistic representations is constituted by their inferential and epistemic roles within a "space of reasons." This is the foundational text for inferential/conceptual role approaches to content.

**Ned Block, "Advertisement for a Semantics for Psychology" (1986)** — Block distinguishes narrow content (determined by inferential role "in the head") from wide content (determined by relations to the environment), and argues for a two-factor theory that combines both. A key text for understanding inferential role semantics (IRS) in philosophy of mind.

**Christopher Peacocke, *A Study of Concepts* (1992)** — Peacocke proposes that concepts have "possession conditions" specifying what inferential transitions a thinker must be disposed to make to possess them. A rigorous development of conceptual role as constitutive of mental content.

## Secondary Literature

**Paul Boghossian, "The Rule-Following Considerations" (1989)** — Examines Wittgenstein's rule-following paradox and its implications for theories of mental content, including inferential role semantics. Argues that IRS faces a version of the paradox.

**Jerry Fodor and Ernest Lepore, *Holism: A Shopper's Guide* (1992)** — A sustained critique of inferential role semantics and related holistic theories of content, arguing that holism about meaning makes content indeterminate and undermines the systematicity of language and thought.

**Robert Brandom, *Making It Explicit* (1994)** — Brandom develops the most systematic inferentialist semantics in contemporary philosophy, arguing that the contents of concepts are entirely determined by their inferential roles within a practice of giving and asking for reasons. A demanding but central work.

**Gilbert Harman, "Wide Functionalism" (1987)** — Defends a version of functional role semantics that is holistic: a mental state's content is constituted by its functional role in the entire network of the agent's beliefs and dispositions. Useful for understanding holism's attractions and problems.
