# Further Reading: Content Externalism

## Primary Sources

**Hilary Putnam, "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (1975)** — Putnam's twin earth thought experiment establishes that the meaning of natural kind terms is not "in the head" — two internally identical beings on Earth and Twin Earth mean different things by "water" because they are embedded in different environments. Foundational for semantic externalism.

**Tyler Burge, "Individualism and the Mental" (1979)** — Burge's arthritis thought experiment extends externalism beyond natural kinds to ordinary concepts: a person's belief content depends on their social community's usage, not just on their internal states. This "social externalism" is arguably broader and more radical than Putnam's.

**Tyler Burge, "Other Bodies" (1982)** — Extends externalist arguments to demonstrative and indexical content, arguing that the content of thoughts involving demonstratives depends on the actual environment.

## Secondary Literature

**Tyler Burge, *Origins of Objectivity* (2010)** — A comprehensive, systematic defense of externalism in perception and thought, arguing that representation is inherently objective in that it sets conditions the world must meet.

**Donald Davidson, "Knowing One's Own Mind" (1987)** — Davidson argues that first-person authority and self-knowledge are consistent with externalism: the authority of first-person reports does not require privileged access to internal states, because what one's thoughts are about is not determined by internal states alone.

**Ned Block and Robert Stalnaker, "Conceptual Analysis, Dualism, and the Explanatory Gap" (1999)** — Examines the relationship between semantic externalism and the mind-body problem, arguing against the use of two-dimensional semantics to dissolve philosophical puzzles.

**Frances Segal, *A Slim Book about Narrow Content* (2000)** — Examines the case for narrow content (content determined by internal states alone) as a response to externalism, and evaluates whether any coherent notion of narrow content is available.

**Brian Loewer and Georges Rey (eds.), *Meaning in Mind: Fodor and His Critics* (1991)** — Includes debates about the relationship between Fodor's causal-informational semantics and the externalist arguments of Putnam and Burge.
