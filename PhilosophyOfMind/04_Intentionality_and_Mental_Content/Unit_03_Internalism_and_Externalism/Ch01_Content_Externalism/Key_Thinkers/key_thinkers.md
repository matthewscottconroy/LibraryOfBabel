# Key Thinkers: Content Externalism

---

**Hilary Putnam** (1926–2016)

Putnam's twin earth argument is the classic text establishing semantic externalism. He imagines a world (Twin Earth) exactly like Earth except that the substance in oceans and rivers is XYZ rather than H2O. An Earthling and their twin have identical internal states, but mean different things by "water." Putnam concluded that "meanings just ain't in the head" — the content of natural kind concepts is determined partly by the actual constitution of the kinds in the environment. He developed this into a broader argument that meaning is socially and environmentally anchored.

*Key works:* "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (1975); *Reason, Truth and History* (1981); *Representation and Reality* (1988)

---

**Tyler Burge** (b. 1946)

Burge is the philosopher who most systematically developed and defended content externalism. His arthritis thought experiment showed that the content of beliefs about social-institutional kinds (diseases, artifacts, social roles) depends on communal usage rather than individual knowledge. Burge extended this to show that mental content generally — including perceptual content — is constitutively dependent on environmental and social conditions. He has defended a form of anti-individualism against all forms of content internalism.

*Key works:* "Individualism and the Mental" (1979); "Other Bodies" (1982); *Origins of Objectivity* (2010)

---

**Donald Davidson** (1917–2003)

Davidson's "radical interpretation" framework and his principle of charity lead to externalist conclusions: the content of a belief is determined by what would make the belief true in the standard conditions of the interpreter's world. Davidson connects this to his "social externalism" — the content of our thoughts is determined partly by the social practices and environmental context in which they are embedded. He argues that the externalist conclusion is compatible with ordinary first-person authority.

*Key works:* "Radical Interpretation" (1973); "Knowing One's Own Mind" (1987); *Inquiries into Truth and Interpretation* (1984)

---

**Gareth Evans** (1946–1980)

Evans argued that reference and content in thought are determined by causal-historical relations to the objects thought about — "information links" connecting thinker to world. His account of "Russell's Principle" (one must know which object one is thinking about to have a singular thought) and his treatment of demonstrative and recognitional concepts contributed to externalism about singular thought. Evans' early death cut short a philosophical career of exceptional promise.

*Key works:* *The Varieties of Reference* (1982, posthumous); "The Causal Theory of Names" (1973); "Understanding Demonstratives" (1981)
