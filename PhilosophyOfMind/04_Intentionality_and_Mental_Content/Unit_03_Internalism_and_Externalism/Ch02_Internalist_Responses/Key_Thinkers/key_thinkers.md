# Key Thinkers: Internalist Responses to Externalism

---

**Jerry Fodor** (1935–2017)

Fodor was externalism's most persistent and sophisticated critic. His "methodological solipsism" paper (1980) argues that cognitive science must posit narrow content — content that supervenes on internal physical states — because cognitive processes operate over internal syntactic structures without regard for their semantic relations to the external world. Fodor insisted that the computational theory of mind requires narrow content, and he developed the asymmetric dependence account as an attempt to naturalize content in a way that recovers the causal efficacy of narrow content states.

*Key works:* "Methodological Solipsism" (1980); *The Language of Thought* (1975); *Psychosemantics* (1987); *In Critical Condition* (1998)

---

**Gabriel Segal** (b. 1953)

Segal is among the most sustained contemporary defenders of narrow content and individualism about mental content. He argues that there is a coherent notion of narrow content — content shared by an Earthling and their Twin Earth counterpart — that is scientifically explanatory because it is what determines the cognitive and behavioral differences between individuals. He also argues that Burge's social externalism can be resisted because the constitutive role of social practice in determining content is far less radical than Burge claims.

*Key works:* *A Slim Book about Narrow Content* (2000); "Seeing What Is Not There" (1989); "The World, the Flesh and the Subject" (1994)

---

**Frank Jackson** (b. 1943)

Jackson developed the two-dimensional semantic framework as a way of distinguishing two kinds of content: "A-intension" (how a term applies across contexts of utterance, roughly narrow content) and "C-intension" (how a term applies across contexts of evaluation, roughly wide content). This allows him to acknowledge the insights of externalism while preserving a role for internal conceptual content in epistemology and the philosophy of mind. Jackson argues that descriptive content is what matters for cognitive significance.

*Key works:* *From Metaphysics to Ethics* (1998); "What Mary Didn't Know" (1986); "Reference and Description Revisited" (1998)

---

**Colin McGinn** (b. 1950)

McGinn, in *Mental Content* (1989), argued for a two-factor theory of content, combining a narrow component (captured by conceptual role) with a wide component (determined by causal-historical relations to the environment). He acknowledged the force of externalist arguments while insisting that narrow content cannot be eliminated, because it is what grounds the rational and inferential relations among mental states. His work represents a sophisticated attempt to honor both internalist and externalist intuitions.

*Key works:* *Mental Content* (1989); *The Problem of Consciousness* (1991); *Character of Mind* (1997)
