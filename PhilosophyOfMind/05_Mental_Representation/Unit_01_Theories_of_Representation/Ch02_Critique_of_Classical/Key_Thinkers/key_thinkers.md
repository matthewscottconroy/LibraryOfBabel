# Key Thinkers: Critique of Classical Representationalism

---

**Hubert Dreyfus** (1929–2017)

Dreyfus is the most prominent philosophical critic of classical AI and symbolic representationalism. Drawing on Merleau-Ponty and Heidegger, he argued that human cognition is grounded in practical, embodied skills that cannot be captured by rule-governed manipulation of discrete symbols. Chess-playing and language understanding, he contended, rely on holistic pattern recognition and situational awareness that escape formalization. Dreyfus's critique anticipated by decades the later shift toward connectionism and embodied cognition.

*Key works:* *What Computers Can't Do* (1972/1992); *Being-in-the-World* (1991); "Why Heideggerian AI Failed" (2007)

---

**Paul Churchland** (b. 1942)

Churchland's eliminative materialism holds that folk psychology — and with it the propositional attitude psychology formalized in classical representationalism — is a false theory that will be superseded by neuroscience. He argues that the categories of belief, desire, and sentence-like mental representations do not carve nature at its joints, and that mature brain science will work with different categories (such as activation patterns in neural networks). His positive proposal appeals to state-space semantics: cognitive states are vectors in high-dimensional spaces.

*Key works:* "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes" (1981); *A Neurocomputational Perspective* (1989); *The Engine of Reason, the Seat of the Soul* (1995)

---

**Tim van Gelder** (b. 1962)

Van Gelder is the central defender of the dynamical systems approach to cognition as an alternative to computational representationalism. His influential 1995 paper argues that the Watt governor — which achieves remarkable control without discrete symbolic computation — shows that intelligent behavior does not require representational processing. He proposes "dynamical cognition" as a replacement framework in which cognitive states are characterized by their mathematical properties (attractors, trajectories in state space) rather than their representational content.

*Key works:* "What Might Cognition Be, If Not Computation?" (1995); *Mind as Motion* (ed. with Port, 1995); "The Dynamical Hypothesis in Cognitive Science" (1998)

---

**Rodney Brooks** (b. 1954)

Brooks, a roboticist, argued in "Intelligence Without Representation" (1991) that robust, flexible behavior in the world can be achieved through layered systems of direct sensorimotor coupling rather than central planning and explicit representation. His subsumption architecture demonstrated that autonomous robots could navigate and interact with complex environments without maintaining internal world-models. This work influenced embodied cognition and challenged the necessity of classical symbolic representational architecture.

*Key works:* "Intelligence Without Representation" (1991); "A Robust Layered Control System for a Mobile Robot" (1986); *Flesh and Machines* (2002)
