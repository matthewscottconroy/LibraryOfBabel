# Key Thinkers: Maps, Models, and Simulations

---

**Kenneth Craik** (1914–1945)

Craik proposed in *The Nature of Explanation* (1943) that the brain functions by constructing internal "working models" of reality — structural analogs that can be used to simulate and anticipate events in the world. This model-based picture of cognition anticipated both computational approaches to mind and the later mental models tradition. Craik died young in a bicycle accident, but his brief contributions were enormously influential.

*Key works:* *The Nature of Explanation* (1943)

---

**Edward Tolman** (1886–1959)

Tolman's experiments on latent learning in rats provided the first systematic evidence for internal cognitive representations that were not reducible to stimulus-response associations. His 1948 concept of the "cognitive map" — an internal representation of spatial layout that could be used flexibly in novel conditions — challenged behaviorism and established spatial representation as a central topic in cognitive psychology. His work was confirmed at the neural level by O'Keefe and Nadel's discovery of hippocampal place cells.

*Key works:* "Cognitive Maps in Rats and Men" (1948); *Purposive Behavior in Animals and Men* (1932)

---

**Philip Johnson-Laird** (b. 1936)

Johnson-Laird developed the "mental models" theory of reasoning, arguing that people reason by constructing, inspecting, and manipulating analog representations of possible situations rather than by applying formal inference rules. His research showed that the difficulty of reasoning tasks depends on the number of models required to assess a conclusion, not on formal logical complexity. The mental models framework has been applied to language comprehension, spatial cognition, and decision-making.

*Key works:* *Mental Models* (1983); *How We Reason* (2006); *Mental Models and Deductive Reasoning* (with Byrne, 1991)

---

**John O'Keefe** (b. 1939)

O'Keefe discovered "place cells" in the rat hippocampus — neurons that fire selectively when the animal is at a specific location in its environment — providing the neural basis for Tolman's cognitive map concept. He later co-discovered "grid cells" with May-Britt and Edvard Moser. His work established the hippocampus as the key neural substrate for spatial mapping and navigation, and opened the question of whether the hippocampal system supports other forms of relational, model-like representation.

*Key works:* *The Hippocampus as a Cognitive Map* (1978, with Nadel); "Place Units in the Hippocampus of the Freely Moving Rat" (1971); Nobel Lecture (2014)

---

**Alvin Goldman** (b. 1938)

Goldman has developed simulation theory — the view that we understand others' minds by simulating them: running our own cognitive and affective processes "off-line" to predict and explain the behavior of others. This simulation-based account of mindreading is relevant for the role of mental simulation broadly, and connects the mental models tradition to debates about theory of mind, mirror neurons, and the basis of empathy.

*Key works:* *Simulating Minds* (2006); "Interpretation Psychologized" (1989); *Knowledge in a Social World* (1999)
