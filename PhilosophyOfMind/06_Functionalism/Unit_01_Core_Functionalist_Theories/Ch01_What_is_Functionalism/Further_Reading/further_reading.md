# Further Reading: What is Functionalism?

## Primary Sources

**Hilary Putnam, "The Nature of Mental States" (1967)** — The canonical functionalist paper, arguing that mental states are defined by their functional roles (causal relations to inputs, outputs, and other mental states) rather than by their physical constitution. Introduces the multiple realizability argument against type identity theory.

**David Lewis, "An Argument for the Identity Theory" (1966) and "Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications" (1972)** — Lewis develops "analytic functionalism" (or Ramsey sentence functionalism), arguing that mental state terms are implicitly defined by the folk-psychological theory in which they figure. Mental states are whatever states play the relevant causal roles.

**Sydney Shoemaker, "Functionalism and Qualia" (1975)** — Shoemaker applies the functionalist framework to the problem of qualia, arguing that qualia are functional states and that inverted qualia scenarios do not pose a decisive objection.

## Secondary Literature

**Ned Block and Jerry Fodor, "What Psychological States Are Not" (1972)** — A critical examination of functionalist claims, distinguishing machine-table functionalism from other versions and raising early objections about qualia that anticipate the absent qualia problem.

**Jaegwon Kim, *Philosophy of Mind* (1996/2010)** — A comprehensive textbook that gives a clear and balanced account of functionalism, its motivation, varieties, and challenges. An excellent overview for students.

**David Chalmers, *The Conscious Mind* (1996), Chapter 2** — Chalmers accepts functionalism about cognitive states but argues it fails for phenomenal consciousness, providing a clear and influential statement of the limits of the functionalist program.

**Bechtel and Mundale, "Multiple Realizability Revisited" (1999)** — Critiques the multiple realizability argument that underlies functionalism, arguing that proper individuations of mental and physical types make multiple realizability much rarer than typically supposed.
