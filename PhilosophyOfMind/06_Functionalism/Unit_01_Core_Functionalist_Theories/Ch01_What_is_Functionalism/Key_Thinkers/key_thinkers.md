# Key Thinkers: What is Functionalism?

---

**Hilary Putnam** (1926–2016)

Putnam introduced functionalism as a theory of mental states in the 1960s, arguing against type identity theory on the grounds that mental states can be multiply realized — implemented in different physical substrates while retaining the same functional organization. On his view, pain is whatever state is caused by tissue damage and causes avoidance behavior, regardless of its physical composition. Putnam later became a prominent critic of computationalist functionalism, arguing that computation is observer-relative and cannot fix genuine mental content.

*Key works:* "The Nature of Mental States" (1967); "Minds and Machines" (1960); *Mind, Language and Reality* (1975); *Representation and Reality* (1988)

---

**David Lewis** (1941–2001)

Lewis developed analytic functionalism — the view that mental state terms are implicitly defined by the role they play in commonsense (folk) psychological theory, reformulated as a Ramsey sentence. Mental states are whatever physical states play those folk-theoretically specified roles. Lewis combined this with a type identity theory: once we have identified what plays the pain role in a given creature, we can identify pain with that physical state type. Lewis's functionalism preserves reductionism while accommodating multiple realizability.

*Key works:* "An Argument for the Identity Theory" (1966); "Psychophysical and Theoretical Identifications" (1972); *Philosophical Papers*, Vols. I–II (1983, 1986)

---

**Sydney Shoemaker** (b. 1931)

Shoemaker developed a distinctive "causal theory of properties" on which mental properties are constituted by their causal powers — a version of functionalism grounded in a metaphysical account of properties rather than folk-psychological theory. He argued that this "property functionalism" can accommodate qualia because phenomenal properties just are functional properties, defined by their causal roles. He also developed influential arguments connecting functionalism to self-knowledge and personal identity.

*Key works:* *Self-Knowledge and Self-Identity* (1963); "Functionalism and Qualia" (1975); *Identity, Cause, and Mind* (1984); *Physical Realization* (2007)

---

**Jerry Fodor** (1935–2017)

Fodor's "psychofunctionalism" grounds mental state individuation in the states and processes posited by a mature cognitive science rather than in folk psychology. He argued that the computational theory of mind provides the right functional organization: mental states are individuated by their computational roles, and these computational roles map onto the structure of mental representations in the language of thought. Fodor's functionalism was anti-holistic, insisting on local computational roles rather than the entire web of beliefs.

*Key works:* *The Language of Thought* (1975); *Psychological Explanation* (1968); "Special Sciences" (1974)
