# Key Arguments, Concepts, and Thought Experiments: Varieties of Functionalism

## Key Arguments

**Lewis's Causal-Functional Definition of Mental Terms**
David Lewis argued that mental state terms can be given causal-functional definitions by the following procedure: collect all the platitudes of folk psychology (pain is caused by injury, causes distress, causes avoidance behavior, interacts with desires and beliefs...); form the Ramsey sentence by replacing all mental terms with variables; define a mental term as referring to whatever physical state uniquely satisfies the relevant variable in the Ramsey sentence. On this account, mental terms are uniquely functional definable: they refer to whatever physical states play the relevant roles.

**The Distinction Between Analytic and Psychofunctionalism**
Analytic functionalism (Lewis, Armstrong) holds that functional definitions are given a priori by conceptual analysis of folk psychology: we analyze "pain" by reflecting on what folk psychology says about pain. Psychofunctionalism (Fodor, Putnam in later work) holds that the correct functional definitions are determined empirically by psychology and cognitive science, not by armchair conceptual analysis. The distinction matters for whether functional definitions are knowable a priori and for the relationship between folk and scientific psychology.

**The Role-Realizer Distinction (Lewis)**
Lewis distinguished between the mental state itself (the role) and its physical realizer (the state that plays the role). The mental state is a second-order state: it is the property of being in some physical state that plays the relevant functional role. The physical state that happens to play the role is the realizer of that mental state. This distinction is important for multiple realizability: different physical states can realize the same mental state (same role). It also allows functional properties to be distinct from physical properties while being physically implemented.

**The Ramsey Sentence Strategy**
Frank Ramsey proposed replacing the names in a theory with variables and quantifying over them: instead of "pain is caused by injury, interacts with beliefs and desires, and causes avoidance," we say "there exists a state S such that S is caused by injury, S interacts with beliefs and desires, and S causes avoidance, and pain = S." Lewis applied this to mental terms: mental states are defined as the unique occupants of the roles specified in the Ramsey sentence of folk psychology. The strategy simultaneously defines all mental terms and gives them a functional analysis.

**The Liberalism-Chauvinism Dilemma**
Block argued that functionalism faces a dilemma: if the functional criteria for having mental states are too liberal, the theory attributes mental states to systems we intuitively reject (the China Brain, simple thermostats); if they are too restrictive, they exclude systems that clearly have mental states (creatures with different functional organizations than humans). Any functionalist theory must navigate between unacceptable liberalism (attributing too much mentality) and unacceptable chauvinism (attributing too little). Block argued that no functionalist theory resolves this dilemma satisfactorily.

## Core Concepts

**Analytic Functionalism**
Analytic functionalism holds that the functional definitions of mental terms are analytic truths—knowable a priori by conceptual analysis of ordinary mental concepts. Lewis and Armstrong are the primary defenders. On this view, it is a conceptual truth that pain is caused by tissue damage and causes avoidance behavior; to know what pain is is to know what functional role it plays. Analytic functionalism is part of the broader Canberra Plan methodology, which holds that conceptual analysis of commonsense frameworks provides the input for scientific identification of realizers.

**Psychofunctionalism**
Psychofunctionalism holds that the correct functional characterizations of mental states are not given by folk psychology but by empirical cognitive science. The scientific study of cognition (computationally characterized models of perception, memory, reasoning) provides the functional definitions, not armchair analysis of ordinary concepts. Psychofunctionalism allows cognitive science to revise or reject folk psychological categories while remaining functionalist about the resulting scientific categories. It is more tolerant of revisionary theories and is the form of functionalism most compatible with scientific practice.

**Ramsey Sentence**
A Ramsey sentence is generated by taking a theory, replacing all the theoretical terms (such as mental state terms) with existentially bound variables, and asserting the resulting sentence. The Ramsey sentence asserts that there exist states (the values of the variables) that satisfy all the relational claims the theory makes. Applied to folk psychology, the Ramsey sentence asserts that there exist physical states playing all the roles folk psychology attributes to mental states. Lewis argued that mental terms refer to the unique physical states that satisfy the Ramsey sentence—providing a functional analysis of mental terms.

**Role vs. Realizer**
Lewis's role-realizer distinction separates the functional role (the second-order property of being in some state that plays role R) from the physical realizer (the particular first-order physical state that actually plays R). The mental state, strictly speaking, is the role—the second-order property. The physical state is the realizer. This explains how mental terms can refer to different physical states in different species while still picking out the same mental property: the same role may be realized differently. The distinction is also used to explain the difference between the mental property (role) and its physical basis (realizer).

**Token Functionalism**
Token functionalism holds that each particular (token) mental event is a token of some physical type, while mental types are defined by functional roles that can be variably realized. Unlike type identity theory (each mental type = one physical type), token functionalism allows that different tokens of the same mental type may be physically heterogeneous—as long as each individual token is physically realized. Token functionalism is closely related to Davidson's anomalous monism: each mental event token is a physical event, but mental descriptions are irreducible to physical descriptions.

## Thought Experiments

**Block's China Brain**
Ned Block imagines the entire population of China organized to realize the functional organization of a single human brain—each person plays the role of a neuron, communicating via radio links. Does the China nation-brain have mental states? Functionalism seems committed to saying yes—the functional organization is right. But this seems deeply counterintuitive: surely 1.4 billion people, each following simple rules, do not collectively have a single pain experience. The China Brain thought experiment is the definitive case for the "liberalism" objection to functionalism.

**Lewis's Population of China Avoidance (Role-Realizer)**
Lewis responds to Block-style objections by noting that the relevant functional roles must be specified at the right level of description—the individual-person level, not the aggregate level. The "China Brain" functional organization does not satisfy the right Ramsey sentence for an individual mind because it fails at the relevant level of description. The thought experiment tests how carefully we specify which functional organization is the relevant one and invites the question: what determines which level of description is the right one for mental state attribution?

**The Absent-Minded Functionalist**
Suppose a philosopher accepts functionalism and then meets a perfect silicon robot that satisfies all the functional criteria for having beliefs and desires. Should they engage with it as a person? The functionalist answer is yes—the robot has genuine mental states—but this outcome seems uncomfortable: functional equivalence may not be sufficient for moral standing or genuine experience. The thought experiment tests the practical implications of functionalism and the tension between functional criteria for mental state attribution and our intuitive grounds for attributing experience and moral status.
