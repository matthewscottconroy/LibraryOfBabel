# Key Thinkers: Varieties of Functionalism

---

**Ned Block** (b. 1942)

Block has been both a developer of and a careful critic of functionalism. He distinguishes between "machine functionalism" (mental states as computational states of a Turing machine), "psychofunctionalism" (states defined by empirical cognitive science), and "analytic functionalism" (states defined by folk-psychological theory). He argues that while psychofunctionalism is the most defensible version, all versions face serious difficulties when applied to phenomenal consciousness. His "absent qualia" and "China Brain" objections are the most influential challenges to functionalist accounts of experience.

*Key works:* "Troubles with Functionalism" (1978); "Psychologism and Behaviorism" (1981); "On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness" (1995)

---

**William Lycan** (b. 1945)

Lycan defends "homuncular functionalism" — a teleological version of functionalism in which mental states are defined by their functional roles within a hierarchical system of sub-systems, each playing a role that contributes to the system's overall purposes. This teleological dimension, Lycan argues, avoids the mere-realization problem: it is not enough that any physical system implements the functional organization; it must do so for the right reasons. Lycan applies this framework to consciousness, perception, and intentionality.

*Key works:* *Consciousness* (1987); *Consciousness and Experience* (1996); *Mind and Cognition* (ed., 1999)

---

**Daniel Dennett** (b. 1942)

Dennett defends a form of "heterophenomenological functionalism" in which mental states are fully characterized by their functional roles, and in which apparent recalcitrant phenomena (qualia, the hard problem) are revealed, on analysis, not to require anything beyond functional explanation. His intentional stance theory holds that mental states are attributed to systems insofar as this attribution strategy predicts behavior; there is no further fact about whether a system "really" has beliefs or experiences.

*Key works:* *Content and Consciousness* (1969); *Brainstorms* (1978); *Consciousness Explained* (1991)

---

**Functionalism and Alan Turing** (1912–1954)

Turing's foundational work on computation and the "Turing Test" provided the conceptual framework for machine functionalism. His 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" proposes that if a machine can converse indistinguishably from a human, we should attribute intelligence and (by extension) mental states to it. This "operational" criterion for mentality is the ancestor of functionalist criteria for mental state attribution. While Turing did not develop a philosophical theory of functionalism, his work made it possible.

*Key works:* "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (1950); "On Computable Numbers" (1936)
