# Key Thinkers: Qualia and Functionalism

---

**Ned Block** (b. 1942)

Block's twin objections to functionalism — absent qualia and inverted qualia — have driven debate for four decades. The absent qualia objection uses thought experiments (the "China Brain" — the entire population of China instantiating the functional organization of a mind) to argue that functional organization is insufficient for phenomenal experience. The inverted qualia objection uses cases of systematic qualitative inversion (red and green experiences swapped) that preserve functional organization, arguing that phenomenal properties are not functional properties.

*Key works:* "Troubles with Functionalism" (1978); "Qualia" (1994, in *A Companion to the Philosophy of Mind*); *Consciousness, Function, and Representation* (2007)

---

**Frank Jackson** (b. 1943)

Jackson's knowledge argument poses a direct challenge to functionalism: since Mary knows all functional facts about color vision but learns something new (what it is like to see red) upon leaving her black-and-white room, phenomenal facts are not functional facts. Jackson originally endorsed dualism about qualia, though he later retreated to the view that what Mary learns is a new ability or new acquaintance rather than a new fact.

*Key works:* "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982); "What Mary Didn't Know" (1986); *From Metaphysics to Ethics* (1998)

---

**David Chalmers** (b. 1966)

Chalmers argues that the conceivability of philosophical zombies — beings physically and functionally identical to us but with no phenomenal consciousness — shows that phenomenal consciousness is not constituted by functional organization. This is his central objection to functionalism about experience, though he accepts functionalism about cognitive states. His positive view holds that phenomenal properties are fundamental and not reducible to functional ones.

*Key works:* *The Conscious Mind* (1996); "Absent Qualia, Faded Qualia, Dancing Qualia" (1996); "The Meta-Problem of Consciousness" (2018)

---

**Sydney Shoemaker** (b. 1931)

Shoemaker provides the most sustained functionalist response to qualia objections. He argues that absent qualia are genuinely impossible because phenomenal properties are constituted by their causal/functional roles: a state with the qualitative character of red-experience has this character in virtue of the causal powers it confers. This "causal theory of properties" extends to qualia, making them a species of functional properties rather than something "over and above" them.

*Key works:* "Functionalism and Qualia" (1975); "Absent Qualia Are Impossible" (1981); *Physical Realization* (2007)
