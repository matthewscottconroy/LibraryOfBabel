# Key Thinkers: Classical Theories of Perception

---

**G.E. Moore** (1873–1958)

Moore helped establish the sense-datum theory by arguing that in any perceptual experience there is a mental entity — a sense-datum — with the sensible qualities the experience presents. When I see a blue patch, I am directly aware of a blue sense-datum. Moore's "Status of Sense-Data" lectures distinguished sensing (direct acquaintance with sense-data) from perceiving (awareness related to physical objects through sense-data). His work set the terms for debates about the nature of perceptual experience in analytic philosophy.

*Key works:* "The Refutation of Idealism" (1903); "The Status of Sense-Data" (1913); *Some Main Problems of Philosophy* (1953)

---

**H.H. Price** (1899–1984)

Price's *Perception* (1932) is the most systematic philosophical defense of sense-datum theory. He carefully distinguished the act of sensing from the sense-datum sensed, argued that sense-data are the primary objects of perceptual acquaintance, and developed the theory's implications for the analysis of illusion, hallucination, and the physical object. His meticulous treatment made the sense-datum theory a rigorous philosophical position rather than a casual assumption.

*Key works:* *Perception* (1932); *Hume's Theory of the External World* (1940); *Thinking and Experience* (1953)

---

**J.L. Austin** (1911–1960)

Austin's ordinary language critique of the sense-datum theory in *Sense and Sensibilia* remains one of the most effective philosophical attacks on a widely held theory. He argued that the "argument from illusion" — that perceptual error proves we are always directly aware of sense-data — rests on confused and inflated uses of ordinary perceptual language. Austin did not offer a developed positive theory of perception but demonstrated that the case for sense-data had not been made rigorously.

*Key works:* *Sense and Sensibilia* (1962, reconstructed from notes); *How to Do Things with Words* (1962); "Other Minds" (1946)

---

**Roderick Chisholm** (1916–1999)

Chisholm's *Perceiving* (1957) provides a careful analysis of perceptual language and argument for sense-data alongside an epistemological account of perceptual knowledge. He introduced the notion of "appeared to" as a way of capturing perceptual content without committing to the existence of sense-data, making his position closer to adverbialism in later work. His analysis of the conditions for justified perceptual belief became foundational for reliabilist epistemology.

*Key works:* *Perceiving: A Philosophical Study* (1957); *Theory of Knowledge* (1966); *Person and Object* (1976)
