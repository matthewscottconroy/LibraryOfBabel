# Further Reading: Multisensory Perception

## Primary Sources

**E.T. Rolls and A. Treves, *Neural Networks and Brain Function* (1998)** — Provides a computational framework for understanding how sensory information from different modalities is integrated in the brain, foundational for the neuroscience of multisensory processing.

**Barry Stein and M. Alex Meredith, *The Merging of the Senses* (1993)** — The defining text on multisensory integration, reporting experiments on superior colliculus neurons that respond to multiple sensory modalities with "superadditive" responses when inputs from different senses are temporally and spatially congruent.

**Ernst and Banks, "Humans Integrate Visual and Haptic Information in a Statistically Optimal Fashion" (2002)** — Landmark paper demonstrating that multisensory integration follows maximum likelihood estimation, providing the empirical foundation for Bayesian accounts of cross-modal binding.

## Secondary Literature

**Charles Spence and Jon Driver (eds.), *Crossmodal Space and Crossmodal Attention* (2004)** — A major collection of research on attention and spatial representation across sensory modalities, connecting experimental work to philosophical theories of perception.

**Kevin O'Regan and Alva Noë, "A Sensorimotor Account of Vision and Visual Consciousness" (2001)** — Argues that visual experience is constituted by knowledge of sensorimotor contingencies, providing a theoretical framework that naturally extends to multisensory perception as involving multiple overlapping sensorimotor skills.

**Paul Bach-y-Rita and Stephen Kercel, "Sensory Substitution and the Human-Machine Interface" (2003)** — Reviews sensory substitution devices (converting visual information into tactile patterns) and their implications for the nature of perceptual modality and consciousness.

**Ophelia Deroy and Charles Spence, "Why We Are Not All Synesthetes" (2013)** — Examines the philosophical implications of cross-modal correspondences and synesthesia for theories of perceptual unity and the boundaries of the senses.
