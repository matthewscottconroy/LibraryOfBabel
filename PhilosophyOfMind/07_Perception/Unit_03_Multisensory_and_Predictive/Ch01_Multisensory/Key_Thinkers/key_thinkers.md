# Key Thinkers: Multisensory Perception

---

**Barry Stein** (b. 1942)

Stein, a neuroscientist at Wake Forest, is the leading researcher on multisensory integration at the level of individual neurons. His work with Meredith on the superior colliculus demonstrated that multisensory neurons exhibit "superadditive" responses when inputs from different senses are spatially and temporally aligned — they fire much more strongly to a combined audio-visual stimulus than the sum of their responses to each modality separately. This "inverse effectiveness" principle has become a cornerstone of the neuroscience of multisensory perception.

*Key works:* *The Merging of the Senses* (1993, with Meredith); "Merging of the Senses: The Origins of Multisensory Integration" (2004)

---

**Charles Spence** (b. 1969)

Spence is a psychologist at Oxford who has conducted extensive research on cross-modal correspondences — systematic associations between features of different sensory modalities (e.g., high pitch and small size, or brightness and sourness). His work illuminates how sensory modalities interact, interfere, and enhance one another in everyday perception, and has applied multisensory research to design, gastronomy, and consumer experience. He has also studied sensory substitution and the rubber hand illusion.

*Key works:* *Crossmodal Space and Crossmodal Attention* (ed. with Driver, 2004); *Gastrophysics* (2017); numerous articles on cross-modal correspondences and the rubber hand illusion

---

**Paul Bach-y-Rita** (1934–2006)

Bach-y-Rita pioneered sensory substitution research, developing devices that convert visual information into tactile patterns that blind users can learn to use for navigation and object recognition. His work challenged the assumption that perception is modality-specific, suggesting instead that perceptual experience can arise from any sensory input that conveys the relevant information in a usable form. His research has implications for theories of perceptual plasticity and the neural basis of perception.

*Key works:* *Brain Mechanisms in Sensory Substitution* (1972); "Tactile-Visual Substitution: Some Neurological Considerations" (1983); "Sensory Substitution and the Human-Machine Interface" (2003)

---

**Alva Noë** (b. 1964)

Noë's sensorimotor account of perception provides a theoretical framework that naturally accommodates multisensory perception. On his view, each sensory modality is characterized by the distinctive sensorimotor contingencies (patterns of stimulus change with movement) it involves; cross-modal perception arises when the agent integrates multiple sensorimotor skills. Noë also contributed to debates about sensory substitution, arguing that the tactile-vision substitution achieved by Bach-y-Rita's devices supports his enactivist account of vision.

*Key works:* *Action in Perception* (2004); "A Sensorimotor Account of Vision" (2001, with O'Regan); *Out of Our Heads* (2009)
