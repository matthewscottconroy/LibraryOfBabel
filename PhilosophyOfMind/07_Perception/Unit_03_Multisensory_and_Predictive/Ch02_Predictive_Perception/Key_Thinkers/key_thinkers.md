# Key Thinkers: Predictive Perception

---

**Hermann von Helmholtz** (1821–1894)

Helmholtz is the originator of the "unconscious inference" model of perception, which anticipates modern predictive processing. He argued that the visual system automatically and non-consciously draws inferences from ambiguous retinal stimulations to arrive at stable perceptions of objects in the world. These inferences are based on learned regularities about how objects typically cause stimulation. Helmholtz's empiricist, inference-based account of perception has been revived and formalized in Bayesian and predictive coding frameworks.

*Key works:* *Treatise on Physiological Optics* (1867); "The Recent Progress of the Theory of Vision" (1868); *On the Sensations of Tone* (1863)

---

**Karl Friston** (b. 1959)

Friston's hierarchical predictive coding model provides the most mathematically rigorous account of predictive perception. He proposes that every level of the cortical hierarchy generates predictions of activity at the level below, and that only prediction errors (mismatches between prediction and input) are passed upward. Perception consists in finding the best model of sensory causes — minimizing prediction error throughout the hierarchy. Action is reframed as changing sensory input to match predictions rather than updating the model.

*Key works:* "A Theory of Cortical Responses" (2005); "The Free-Energy Principle" (2010); "Predictive Coding Under the Free-Energy Principle" (2009, with Kiebel)

---

**Andy Clark** (b. 1957)

Clark has done the most to philosophically develop and publicize predictive processing as a theory of perception. He argues that the predictive brain is simultaneously a "perception machine" (generating top-down predictions) and an "action machine" (using active inference to fulfill proprioceptive predictions). He has engaged with objections from enactivism and direct realism, arguing that predictive processing is more compatible with embodied approaches than critics suppose.

*Key works:* *Surfing Uncertainty* (2016); "Whatever Next?" (2013); "Busting Out" (2017)

---

**Anil Seth** (b. 1972)

Seth's "controlled hallucination" framework extends predictive perception to conscious experience: all experience — including veridical perception — is the brain's actively generated, probabilistically constrained model of the causes of sensory input. Seth argues that this framework unifies the science of perception and consciousness and has important implications for understanding illusions, hallucinations, and the nature of the self as a predictive inference about the body.

*Key works:* *Being You* (2021); "The Cybernetic Bayesian Brain" (2015); "Theories of Consciousness" (2021, with Bayne)
