# Key Thinkers: What Makes an Action?

---

**G.E.M. Anscombe** (1919–2001)

Anscombe's *Intention* (1957) is the founding text of analytical philosophy of action. She argued that the concept of intentional action cannot be understood in terms of any antecedent mental state that causes behavior; rather, actions are intentional relative to a description, and practical reasoning (the "practical syllogism") is the form of rational explanation of intentional action. Her analysis of the "direction of fit" between desire and world, and belief and world, has become standard. She also developed the concept of "basic actions" and analyzed the structure of deliberate action.

*Key works:* *Intention* (1957); "Modern Moral Philosophy" (1958); *Collected Philosophical Papers* (1981)

---

**Donald Davidson** (1917–2003)

Davidson's "Actions, Reasons, and Causes" (1963) restored causality to the philosophy of action. Against Wittgensteinian views that reasons merely rationalize rather than cause actions, Davidson argued that a reason (a belief-desire pair) is the cause of an action when it causes it in the right way. His "anomalous monism" holds that mental events (including reasons) are identical to physical events, explaining their causal efficacy, while resisting the reduction of mental descriptions to physical ones.

*Key works:* "Actions, Reasons, and Causes" (1963); "Agency" (1971); *Essays on Actions and Events* (1980)

---

**Harry Frankfurt** (b. 1929)

Frankfurt argued that what distinguishes action from mere bodily movement is the agent's active guidance of behavior — the will's involvement rather than any particular antecedent mental state. His "hierarchical" theory of freedom holds that free action requires that the agent identify with (endorses via second-order volitions) the motivating desire. This theory of free will is intertwined with his account of what makes something an action rather than a mere happening.

*Key works:* "Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility" (1969); "Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person" (1971); "The Problem of Action" (1978); *The Importance of What We Care About* (1988)

---

**Jennifer Hornsby** (b. 1951)

Hornsby defends the view that actions are inner events — specifically "tryings" — that cause bodily movements. This account explains the possibility of unsuccessful action (one tries to raise one's arm but cannot) and provides a distinct mental event that can serve as the proximal cause of behavior. She has also contributed to the debate about the "irreducibility" of intentional action explanation and the problem of deviant causal chains.

*Key works:* *Actions* (1980); "Agency and Actions" (2004); "Trying to Act" (1980)
