# Further Reading: Intention and Planning

## Primary Sources

**Michael Bratman, *Intention, Plans, and Practical Reason* (1987)** — Bratman argues that intentions are not reducible to belief-desire pairs but are a distinct mental state with characteristic functional roles: they settle deliberation, guide ongoing action, and constrain future deliberation by creating rational commitments. His planning theory of intention has become the standard account.

**Donald Davidson, "Intending" (1978)** — Davidson's analysis of intention as either a belief-desire pair (when the action is in the future) or the evaluative judgment that culminates in action. Develops his causal theory of action into an account of prospective intention.

**G.E.M. Anscombe, *Intention* (1957)** — Includes Anscombe's analysis of "future-directed intention" — the relationship between deciding to do something and the subsequent execution of that decision — through the lens of practical reasoning.

## Secondary Literature

**John Searle, *Intentionality* (1983), Chapter 3** — Searle distinguishes "prior intentions" (formed before action) from "intentions-in-action" (present in the action itself), arguing both are intentional states with conditions of satisfaction that must be causally self-referential.

**Alfred Mele, *Springs of Action: Understanding Intentional Behavior* (1992)** — A careful treatment of the relationship between intention, motivation, akrasia (weakness of will), and self-control. Mele provides empirically informed philosophical analysis.

**Bratman, Faces of Intention: Selected Essays on Intention and Agency (1999)** — A collection of Bratman's essays developing and defending planning theory, addressing collective intention, self-governance, and the relationship between intention and agency.

**John Bishop, *Natural Agency* (1989)** — Examines the relationship between reasons-based explanation and causal explanation in action theory, arguing for a naturalist account that preserves the rational character of action.
