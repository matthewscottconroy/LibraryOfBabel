# Key Thinkers: Downward Causation

---

**Donald Campbell** (1916–1996)

Campbell introduced the term "downward causation" in 1974 to describe how higher-level organizational features of biological systems constrain the behavior of lower-level components. His paradigm case is natural selection: the fitness landscape (a higher-level, relational property) selects which genetic variants survive, thereby causing lower-level genetic change. Campbell argued that genuine downward causation does not violate the laws of physics but rather involves higher-level constraints that shape the range of physical possibilities.

*Key works:* "Downward Causation in Hierarchically Organised Biological Systems" (1974); *Methodology and Epistemology for Social Science* (1988)

---

**Roger Sperry** (1913–1994)

Sperry, the Nobel laureate known for split-brain research, argued that mental properties exercise genuine downward causal influence on neural processes. His "emergent interaction" view held that conscious states are emergent properties of brain organization that in turn constrain the firing of individual neurons — the whole shapes its parts. Sperry positioned this as a middle path between reductive materialism and dualism.

*Key works:* "Mind, Brain, and Humanist Values" (1965); "A Modified Concept of Consciousness" (1969); "Changing Priorities" (1981)

---

**Jaegwon Kim** (1934–2019)

Kim's critique of downward causation is the most influential challenge to the concept. He argues that for higher-level properties to causally influence lower-level properties, there must be laws connecting them — but then either this is a case of reduction (the higher-level reduces to the lower), or the higher-level property is causally inert relative to its base. Kim uses this to argue that non-reductive physicalism is committed to epiphenomenalism about mental causation.

*Key works:* "The Nonreductivist's Troubles with Mental Causation" (1993); *Mind in a Physical World* (1998); *Physicalism, Or Something Near Enough* (2005)

---

**George Ellis** (b. 1943)

Ellis, a cosmologist and philosopher of science, has developed the most rigorous contemporary account of top-down causation across multiple levels of physical, biological, and psychological organization. He distinguishes several types of top-down causation (adaptive selection, constraints, information closure) and argues that they are genuine causal relations irreducible to bottom-up summation. His work provides technical resources for defending the autonomy of mental causation in a physically complete world.

*Key works:* *On the Moral Nature of the Universe* (1996, with Murphy); "Top-Down Causation and Emergence" (2012); "How Downward Causation Occurs in the Brain" (2016)
