# Further Reading: Deep Learning and the Mind

## Primary Sources

**LeCun, Bengio, and Hinton, "Deep Learning" (2015)** — A comprehensive review in *Nature* by three of the central figures in deep learning, explaining the architecture of deep neural networks, how they learn through backpropagation, and their achievements across perception, language, and game-playing.

**Geoffrey Hinton, Simon Osindero, and Yee-Whye Teh, "A Fast Learning Algorithm for Deep Belief Nets" (2006)** — The paper that revived interest in deep networks by showing that deep belief networks can be trained efficiently using greedy layer-wise pretraining.

**Yann LeCun, Yoshua Bengio, and Patrick Haffner, "Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition" (1998)** — Introduces convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for visual processing, demonstrating their superiority over classical methods for digit recognition. A foundational paper for modern computer vision.

## Secondary Literature

**Gary Marcus and Ernest Davis, *Rebooting AI: Building Artificial Intelligence We Can Trust* (2019)** — A sober assessment of deep learning's limitations for general intelligence, arguing that current systems lack systematic reasoning, common sense, and causal understanding. Raises philosophical challenges.

**Yoshua Bengio, "The Consciousness Prior" (2017)** — A speculative paper arguing that consciousness serves to extract sparse, high-level representations from continuous experience — a framework connecting deep learning to theories of consciousness.

**Thomas Griffiths, Tobias Gerstenberg, and Noah Goodman, "Doing More with Less: Meta-Reasoning and Meta-Learning in Humans and Machines" (2020)** — Examines whether deep learning models of cognition can be extended to capture flexible, systematic human learning, using Bayesian and probabilistic program induction frameworks.

**Nick Bostrom, *Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies* (2014)** — Philosophical and strategic analysis of the long-term implications of artificial general intelligence, relevant for discussions about deep learning's relationship to minds capable of genuine understanding.
