# Key Thinkers: Deep Learning and the Mind

---

**Geoffrey Hinton** (b. 1947)

Hinton is the most influential figure in the development of deep learning. His work on backpropagation (1986), Boltzmann machines, and restricted Boltzmann machines laid the foundations for modern deep networks, and his 2006 paper on deep belief nets revived interest in deep architectures after the "AI winter." Hinton has increasingly reflected on the philosophical implications of deep learning, arguing that distributed representations in neural networks challenge classical symbolic AI and that the resemblance to biological neural networks is more than superficial.

*Key works:* "Learning Representations by Back-Propagating Errors" (1986); "A Fast Learning Algorithm for Deep Belief Nets" (2006); "Reducing the Dimensionality of Data with Neural Networks" (2006)

---

**Yann LeCun** (b. 1960)

LeCun developed convolutional neural networks (CNNs), architectures with weight sharing and local connectivity inspired by the structure of visual cortex, that proved dramatically more efficient for image recognition. His work at Bell Labs and later Facebook AI Research has driven the application of deep learning to computer vision, speech recognition, and natural language processing. LeCun has argued that current deep learning lacks the kind of world-model-based reasoning necessary for human-level intelligence.

*Key works:* "Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition" (1998); "Deep Learning" (2015, with Bengio and Hinton); various talks on "Self-Supervised Learning" and world models

---

**Yoshua Bengio** (b. 1964)

Bengio is a pioneer of deep learning known for work on language models, recurrent neural networks, and attention mechanisms. He has engaged philosophically with questions about the relationship between deep learning and consciousness, proposing in "The Consciousness Prior" (2017) that conscious experience involves sparse, high-level representations that deep networks might approximate. He has been a prominent voice in AI safety and ethics.

*Key works:* "The Consciousness Prior" (2017); "Deep Learning" (2015, with LeCun and Hinton); "Attention and Memory in Deep Learning and NLP" (2015)

---

**Gary Marcus** (b. 1970)

Marcus is a cognitive scientist and persistent critic of deep learning as a model of human cognition. He argues that deep learning systems lack systematic compositional reasoning, cannot generalize from small amounts of data the way humans do, and lack the kind of causal and structural understanding that underlies human intelligence. He advocates for hybrid architectures combining neural networks with symbolic reasoning.

*Key works:* *The Algebraic Mind* (2001); *Kluge: The Haphazard Construction of the Human Mind* (2008); *Rebooting AI* (2019, with Davis); "Deep Learning: A Critical Appraisal" (2018)
