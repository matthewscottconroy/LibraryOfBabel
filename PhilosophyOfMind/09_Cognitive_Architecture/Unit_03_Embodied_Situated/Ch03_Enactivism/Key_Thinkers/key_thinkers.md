# Key Thinkers: Enactivism

---

**Francisco Varela** (1946–2001)

Varela, a neuroscientist and Buddhist scholar, developed the concept of autopoiesis (from Maturana) into a framework for enactivism. He argued that living and cognitive systems are "autopoietic" — self-producing, self-maintaining networks — and that cognition is the autonomous sense-making of such systems in interaction with their environment. His integration of systems biology, phenomenology, and contemplative practice was philosophically ambitious and scientifically unusual. He introduced "neurophenomenology" as a research program combining first-person phenomenological investigation with third-person neuroscience.

*Key works:* *The Embodied Mind* (1991, with Thompson and Rosch); *Principles of Biological Autonomy* (1979); *Ethical Know-How* (1999)

---

**Alva Noë** (b. 1964)

Noë is the most prominent proponent of sensorimotor enactivism in analytic philosophy. His central claim is that visual experience is constituted by the exercise of sensorimotor knowledge — knowledge of how visual stimulation would change as one moves. Perceiving is not a process that produces an internal representation; it is a form of active engagement with the environment. Noë draws on this to argue that experience cannot be produced in a vat, making enactivism a form of direct realism.

*Key works:* *Action in Perception* (2004); *Out of Our Heads* (2009); *Strange Tools* (2015)

---

**Evan Thompson** (b. 1962)

Thompson is the philosopher who has most thoroughly developed the theoretical foundations of enactivism and defended it against critics. In *Mind in Life* he argues for the "life-mind continuity thesis" — that minimal forms of mind (sense-making, value-laden behavior) are continuous with life itself — and extends this to phenomenal consciousness. He engages critically with predictive processing and representationalist alternatives, arguing that enactivism offers a more adequate account of the first-person perspective.

*Key works:* *The Embodied Mind* (1991, with Varela and Rosch); *Mind in Life* (2007); *Waking, Dreaming, Being* (2015)

---

**Ezequiel Di Paolo** (b. 1968)

Di Paolo has extended enactivism into a comprehensive theoretical framework that includes adaptivity, agency, and social cognition. His "participatory sense-making" account (with De Jaegher) argues that social cognition arises from the dynamic coupling of two autonomous systems engaged in joint interaction, not from individual mindreading. Di Paolo has also articulated the most systematic account of "agency" within an enactivist framework, distinguishing between mere reactivity and genuine sense-making.

*Key works:* "Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, Teleology, Agency" (2005); "Participatory Sense-Making" (2007, with De Jaegher); *Enactive Psychiatry* (2018, with Massimi and De Jaegher)
