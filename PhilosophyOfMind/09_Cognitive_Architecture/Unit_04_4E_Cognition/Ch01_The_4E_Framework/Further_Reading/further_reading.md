# Further Reading: The 4E Framework

## Primary Sources

**Shaun Gallagher (ed.), *The Oxford Handbook of 4E Cognition* (2018)** — A comprehensive reference volume covering all four "E"s: embodied, embedded, enacted, and extended cognition. Includes contributions from the leading figures across all traditions.

**Andy Clark, *Being There* (1997)** — Clark's early systematic treatment arguing that brains, bodies, and environments form an integrated cognitive system, covering the key themes that would be organized under the 4E label.

**Francisco Varela, Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch, *The Embodied Mind* (1991)** — Introduces the enactivist strand of 4E cognition, the most radical of the four E's, challenging not only the boundaries of the cognitive system but the very framework of cognition as representation.

## Secondary Literature

**Mark Rowlands, *The New Science of the Mind* (2010)** — Rowlands distinguishes the four E's and their relationships to each other, argues for the fundamental importance of the embodied and enacted dimensions, and engages with critics.

**Richard Menary (ed.), *The Extended Mind* (2010)** — A collection of papers on extended cognition including the original Clark-Chalmers paper, critical responses, and replies, providing a thorough overview of the extended strand of 4E cognition.

**Lawrence Shapiro, *Embodied Cognition* (2011)** — A balanced, critical survey of the philosophical and empirical literature on embodied cognition, distinguishing three main claims (conceptualization, replacement, and constitution) and evaluating the evidence for each.

**Lambros Malafouris, *How Things Shape the Mind: A Theory of Material Engagement* (2013)** — Extends 4E cognition to archaeology and material culture, arguing that material things are constitutive partners in cognitive processes, not merely tools.
