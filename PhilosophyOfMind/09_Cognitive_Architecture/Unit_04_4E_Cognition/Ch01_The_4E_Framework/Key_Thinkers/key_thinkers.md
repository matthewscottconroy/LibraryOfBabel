# Key Thinkers: The 4E Framework

---

**Shaun Gallagher** (b. 1948)

Gallagher is one of the primary synthesizers of the 4E (embodied, embedded, enacted, extended) cognition framework. He has articulated the phenomenological basis of embodied cognition (body schema, body image, proprioception), connected enactivism to empirical developmental and clinical research, and applied the 4E framework to social cognition, psychopathology, and education. His "ecological" and "interactivist" extensions of the 4E approach have pushed the field toward more relational and dynamical models.

*Key works:* *How the Body Shapes the Mind* (2005); *The Phenomenological Mind* (2012, with Zahavi); *Enactivist Interventions* (2017)

---

**Andy Clark** (b. 1957)

Clark is the central figure connecting the extended, embodied, and embedded strands of 4E cognition within analytic philosophy. He has argued consistently that the "skin/skull" boundary is not the boundary of the cognitive system, and that cognitive science must be reformulated to include bodily and environmental processes. His later work on predictive processing represents an attempt to synthesize 4E insights with a representationalist computational framework.

*Key works:* *Being There* (1997); *Natural-Born Cyborgs* (2003); *Supersizing the Mind* (2008); *Surfing Uncertainty* (2016)

---

**Mark Rowlands** (b. 1963)

Rowlands has worked to systematize and philosophically ground the 4E framework, distinguishing the different senses in which cognition might be extended, embedded, embodied, or enacted, and arguing for a principled basis for the "new science of the mind." He has also engaged in debates about animal cognition and moral status from an enactivist perspective.

*Key works:* *The Body in Mind* (1999); *The New Science of the Mind* (2010); *Animal Rights: Moral Theory and Practice* (2009)

---

**Alva Noë** (b. 1964)

Noë's enactivism is one of the most philosophically distinctive strands of 4E cognition. He argues that consciousness and experience are not produced inside the brain but are constituted by the skilled, embodied engagement of organisms with their environments. Noë has applied this framework to aesthetics, arguing in *Strange Tools* that art is a form of reorganizing our organized activities rather than producing representations.

*Key works:* *Action in Perception* (2004); *Out of Our Heads* (2009); *Strange Tools* (2015)
