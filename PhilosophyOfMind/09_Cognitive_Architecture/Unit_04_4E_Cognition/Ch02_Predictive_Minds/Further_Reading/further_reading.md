# Further Reading: Predictive Minds

## Primary Sources

**Andy Clark, *Surfing Uncertainty: Prediction, Action, and the Embodied Mind* (2016)** — The definitive philosophical treatment of predictive processing as a theory of mind. Clark argues that the brain is fundamentally a prediction machine and explores its implications for perception, action, cognition, and consciousness.

**Karl Friston, "The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?" (2010)** — Friston's comprehensive statement of the free energy principle, arguing that all adaptive behavior can be understood as minimizing variational free energy — a measure of the difference between the agent's model and its sensory data.

**Jakob Hohwy, *The Predictive Mind* (2013)** — Hohwy's philosophical development of predictive processing, arguing for an "inferentialist" account of perception and consciousness and examining the theory's implications for mental illness and the self.

## Secondary Literature

**Jakob Hohwy and Anil Seth (eds.), *Philosophy and Predictive Processing* (2020)** — A collection of essays applying predictive processing to philosophical problems including consciousness, the self, emotions, social cognition, and mental illness.

**Jakob Hohwy, "The Self-Evidencing Brain" (2016)** — A focused philosophical examination of how the predictive brain "self-evidences" — provides its own evidence for its own hypotheses — and the implications for autonomy, agency, and mental disorder.

**Lisa Feldman Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made* (2017)** — Extends predictive processing to emotion, arguing that emotional experiences are active constructions of the brain that predict and regulate the body's internal states.

**Thomas Parr and Karl Friston, "The Anatomy of Inference: Generative Models and Brain Structure" (2018)** — A technical paper examining how the hierarchical structure of predictive processing maps onto the anatomy of the cortex, providing neuroscientific grounding for the framework.

**Michael Kirchhoff et al., "The Markov Blankets of Life: Autonomy, Active Inference and the Free Energy Principle" (2018)** — Examines the relationship between Friston's free energy principle and the boundaries of cognitive systems, relevant for debates about extended mind and 4E cognition.
