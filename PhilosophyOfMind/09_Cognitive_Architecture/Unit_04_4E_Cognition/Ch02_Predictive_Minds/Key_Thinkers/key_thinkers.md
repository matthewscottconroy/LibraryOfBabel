# Key Thinkers: Predictive Minds

---

**Karl Friston** (b. 1959)

Friston's free energy principle is the most ambitious unifying framework in theoretical neuroscience and cognitive science. He argues that all brain function — perception, action, learning, attention — can be understood as minimizing variational free energy, which is approximately equivalent to minimizing prediction error. On this view, the brain maintains a generative model of the world, and both perception (updating the model) and action (changing the world to match model predictions) serve to reduce the discrepancy between model and reality.

*Key works:* "The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?" (2010); "Active Inference and Epistemic Value" (2015); "A Theory of Cortical Responses" (2005)

---

**Andy Clark** (b. 1957)

Clark's *Surfing Uncertainty* (2016) made predictive processing accessible and philosophically rich. He argues that the predictive processing framework vindicates a sophisticated form of embodied cognition: the brain's predictions are calibrated by and partially constituted through bodily and environmental engagement. Clark has also examined how predictive minds illuminate consciousness, the self, and psychopathology, and has explored its relationship to the 4E cognition tradition.

*Key works:* *Surfing Uncertainty* (2016); "Whatever Next?" (2013); "Busting Out" (2017)

---

**Jakob Hohwy** (b. 1971)

Hohwy's philosophical development of predictive processing is the most systematic in the literature. He argues that the predictive brain is separated from the world by a "veil of inference" — it only ever accesses its own predictions and prediction errors, never the world directly. This "inferentialism" about perception has implications for consciousness (all experience is model-dependent) and mental illness (disorders can be understood as failures of inference). Hohwy has engaged extensively with enactivist and direct realist alternatives.

*Key works:* *The Predictive Mind* (2013); "The Variational Neuroethology of Dread" (2017); *Philosophy and Predictive Processing* (ed. with Seth, 2020)

---

**Anil Seth** (b. 1972)

Seth synthesizes predictive processing with consciousness research, arguing that conscious experience is a "controlled hallucination" — the brain's best probabilistic guess about the causes of its sensory signals, constrained by but not reducible to that input. He extends this to selfhood, arguing that the phenomenal self is a predictive model of the body in its environment. Seth's work bridges computational neuroscience, philosophy of mind, and clinical applications.

*Key works:* *Being You* (2021); "The Cybernetic Bayesian Brain" (2015); "Theories of Consciousness" (2021, with Bayne)
