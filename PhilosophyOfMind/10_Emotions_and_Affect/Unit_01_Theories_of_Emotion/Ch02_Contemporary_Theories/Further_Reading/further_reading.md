# Further Reading: Contemporary Theories of Emotion

## Primary Sources

**Lisa Feldman Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain* (2017)** — Barrett argues against basic emotion theories, presenting the "theory of constructed emotion": emotions are not discovered but built, moment by moment, from core affect (valence and arousal), conceptual knowledge, and predictive brain processes.

**Antonio Damasio, *Descartes' Error: Emotion, Reason, and the Human Brain* (1994)** — Damasio's "somatic marker hypothesis" argues that emotions provide bodily signals that mark options as good or bad, guiding decision-making. Patients with ventromedial prefrontal damage lose both appropriate emotional responses and the ability to make rational decisions.

**Jesse Prinz, *Gut Reactions: A Perceptual Theory of Emotion* (2004)** — Prinz defends a neo-Jamesian, embodied theory: emotions are perceptions of bodily states that represent core relational themes (relevance of a situation to one's concerns). A careful contemporary defense of a somatic view.

## Secondary Literature

**Nico Frijda, *The Emotions* (1986)** — Frijda's comprehensive appraisal theory distinguishes "action readiness" as the core of emotion: emotions are states of readiness to act in ways relevant to the appraised situation. An important bridge between cognitive appraisal and motivational accounts.

**Klaus Scherer, "Appraisal Theory" (1999)** — A survey and development of appraisal theory, distinguishing multiple appraisal dimensions (novelty, pleasantness, goal relevance, coping potential, normative significance) that together determine the specific emotion.

**Joseph LeDoux, *The Emotional Brain* (1996)** — LeDoux's neuroscientific account of fear, focusing on the amygdala as the locus of rapid, pre-cognitive emotional processing. Explores the two pathways theory (fast subcortical vs. slow cortical) for emotional responses.

**James Gross, "The Emerging Field of Emotion Regulation" (1998)** — Gross's process model of emotion regulation, describing how emotions unfold through a sequence of stages (situation selection, attention deployment, cognitive change, response modulation) each of which is a site for regulatory intervention.
