# Key Thinkers: Contemporary Theories of Emotion

---

**Antonio Damasio** (b. 1944)

Damasio's "somatic marker hypothesis" provides one of the most influential contemporary theories of emotion's role in cognition. He argues that emotions produce bodily markers — dispositions toward or away from options — that guide decision-making by narrowing the space of choices to be considered. Evidence comes from patients with ventromedial prefrontal cortex damage who show disrupted emotional responses and corresponding deficits in real-world decision-making despite normal performance on standard IQ tests. Damasio's work helped overthrow the assumption that reason and emotion are opposed.

*Key works:* *Descartes' Error* (1994); *The Feeling of What Happens* (1999); *Self Comes to Mind* (2010)

---

**Lisa Feldman Barrett** (b. 1966)

Barrett is the leading proponent of the "theory of constructed emotion" — the view that emotions are not natural kinds with fixed neural signatures but are constructed from more basic ingredients: core affect (valence and arousal), interoceptive signals, concepts, and context. She challenges Ekman's basic emotion theory and argues that cross-cultural studies have been systematically misinterpreted. Her work draws on predictive processing, conceptual act theory, and developmental psychology.

*Key works:* *How Emotions Are Made* (2017); "Are Emotions Natural Kinds?" (2006); *The Atlas of the Heart* (2021)

---

**Jesse Prinz** (b. 1971)

Prinz defends a neo-Jamesian "embodied appraisal theory": emotions are perceptions of bodily changes that represent "core relational themes" — the relevance of a situation to one's concerns (e.g., fear represents something as dangerous). He argues that emotions require bodily involvement — a person without a body cannot have genuine emotions — and that this is compatible with the representational, intentional character of emotions. Prinz engages extensively with the philosophical debate between cognitive and somatic theories.

*Key works:* *Gut Reactions* (2004); *The Emotional Construction of Morals* (2007); *Beyond Human Nature* (2012)

---

**Joseph LeDoux** (b. 1949)

LeDoux is a neuroscientist whose work on the amygdala and fear conditioning has shaped the neuroscience of emotion. His "low road" theory posited a fast, subcortical pathway from sensory input to the amygdala that bypasses cortical processing, enabling rapid fear responses before conscious awareness. His later work has revised this: he distinguishes the neural circuits of "defensive survival behaviors" from the subjective feeling of fear, arguing that emotional feelings are distinct from emotional behaviors.

*Key works:* *The Emotional Brain* (1996); *The Synaptic Self* (2002); *Anxious* (2015)
