# Further Reading: Empathy and Sympathy

## Primary Sources

**Adam Smith, *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759)** — Smith grounds moral judgment in "sympathy" — not sharing another's feeling, but imagining how an "impartial spectator" would react to a situation. This distinction between direct sympathetic sharing and imaginative projection prefigures later debates about empathy.

**Edmund Husserl, *Cartesian Meditations*, Fifth Meditation (1931)** — Husserl's analysis of "intersubjectivity" and the "empathy" (Einfühlung) through which we experience other minds. He argues that we experience other subjects as "analogical apperceptions" of our own bodily self — constituting their minds through a form of empathic projection.

**Edith Stein, *On the Problem of Empathy* (1917, trans. 1964)** — Stein's doctoral dissertation provides the most rigorous phenomenological account of empathy, distinguishing it from emotional contagion, projection, and sympathy. Empathy is, for Stein, a distinctive act of "co-experience" that is neither the other's experience nor one's own.

## Secondary Literature

**Alvin Goldman, *Simulating Minds: The Philosophy, Psychology, and Neuroscience of Mindreading* (2006)** — Goldman argues that we understand others' minds by simulating them — running our own cognitive-affective processes "off-line." Empathy is a form of simulation in which we re-enact the other's emotional state.

**Jesse Prinz, "Against Empathy" (2011)** — Prinz argues that empathy is not a reliable or appropriate basis for moral motivation: it is biased toward the vivid and proximate, promoting partiality rather than impartial concern. A widely discussed critique.

**Frans de Waal, *The Age of Empathy: Nature's Lessons for a Kinder Society* (2009)** — De Waal surveys primate research showing sophisticated forms of empathy and pro-social behavior in non-human animals, arguing that empathy has deep evolutionary roots and is fundamental to moral life.

**Suzanne Keen, "A Theory of Narrative Empathy" (2006)** — Examines the role of literary narrative in cultivating empathy, connecting philosophical accounts of empathy to the psychology of reading and the ethics of fiction.
