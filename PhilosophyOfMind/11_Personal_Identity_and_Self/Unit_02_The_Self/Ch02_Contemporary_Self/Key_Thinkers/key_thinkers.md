# Key Thinkers: Contemporary Theories of Self

---

**Thomas Metzinger (1958–)**
German philosopher of mind at the University of Mainz, prominent in the science of consciousness. Metzinger's phenomenal self-model (PSM) theory argues that what we call "the self" is a representational construct—a model the brain builds of itself as a whole system. Because this model is transparent (we cannot see it as a model), we mistake it for a real self. His theory draws on neuroscientific and clinical evidence including out-of-body experiences, rubber-hand illusions, and depersonalization disorder, and has radical implications: phenomenal selfhood is real, but no self exists as an object in the world.
*Key works:* *Being No One* (2003), *The Ego Tunnel* (2009), "The Minimal Self" (2014)

---

**Antonio Damasio (1944–)**
Portuguese-American neuroscientist and philosopher at the University of Southern California. Damasio proposed a layered theory of self and consciousness grounded in the brain's representation of bodily states. His hierarchy of proto-self (body maps), core self (moment-to-moment subjective awareness arising from the interaction of organism and object), and autobiographical self (narrative memory-based identity) offers a neuroscience-informed account of how selfhood is constructed rather than given. His clinical work with patients suffering frontal lobe damage and his somatic marker hypothesis have been widely influential across neuroscience and philosophy.
*Key works:* *Descartes' Error* (1994), *The Feeling of What Happens* (1999), *Self Comes to Mind* (2010)

---

**Dan Zahavi (1967–)**
Danish phenomenologist at the University of Copenhagen and Oxford, leading authority on Husserl. Zahavi defends a phenomenological minimal self—the pre-reflective first-person character of experience, its "mineness"—as a genuine and philosophically indispensable feature of consciousness. He argues against Metzinger's no-self view that even the claim that experience is illusory presupposes a subject for whom the illusion occurs. His work carefully distinguishes the minimal self from the narrative or social self, showing that these are complementary rather than competing accounts of different dimensions of selfhood.
*Key works:* *Subjectivity and Selfhood* (2005), *Self and Otherness* (2014), *Self and Other: Exploring Subjectivity, Empathy, and Shame* (2014)

---

**Shaun Gallagher (1948–)**
American philosopher at the University of Memphis, known for his work on embodied cognition and phenomenology. Gallagher's distinction between the body schema (implicit, action-oriented representation) and body image (explicit, conscious awareness of the body) has become standard in discussions of embodied selfhood. His account of the minimal bodily self emphasizes action and ownership rather than cognition, drawing on both Merleau-Ponty and empirical neuroscience to argue that the most fundamental form of self-experience is motor rather than narrative.
*Key works:* *How the Body Shapes the Mind* (2005), *Phenomenology* (2012), *The Phenomenological Mind* (with Zahavi, 2008)
