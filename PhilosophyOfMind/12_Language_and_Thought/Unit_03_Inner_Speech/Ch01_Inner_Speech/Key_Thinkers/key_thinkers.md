# Key Thinkers: Inner Speech

---

**Lev Vygotsky (1896–1934)**
Soviet developmental psychologist and founder of the sociocultural theory of mind. Vygotsky's account of inner speech as the internalization of social dialogue remains the foundational framework for the field. He argued that cognitive development proceeds from the social to the individual: language begins as a tool for communication, becomes "egocentric speech" (private but still vocalized) during childhood, and is finally internalized as inner speech that serves both regulatory and cognitive functions. For Vygotsky, inner speech is structurally distinct from external speech—highly abbreviated and predicated—making it a genuine object of philosophical and psychological investigation.
*Key works:* *Thought and Language* (1934), *Mind in Society* (1978)

---

**Charles Fernyhough (1968–)**
British psychologist at Durham University and leading contemporary researcher on inner speech. Fernyhough has developed a neo-Vygotskyan account of inner speech emphasizing its dialogic character—inner speech involves an internalized intersubjective structure with multiple "voices"—and has connected this account to research on auditory verbal hallucinations in psychosis. His descriptive work on the varieties of inner speech (condensed, expanded, deliberate, spontaneous) has helped create a more nuanced phenomenological taxonomy that can be connected to neuroscientific research.
*Key works:* *The Voices Within* (2016), "Inner Speech: Development, Cognitive Functions, Phenomenology, and Neuroscience" (2015, with Alderson-Day)

---

**Ben Alderson-Day (1984–)**
British cognitive neuroscientist at Durham University, collaborator with Fernyhough. Alderson-Day's research focuses on the neural underpinnings of inner speech and its relationship to the experience of hearing voices. His work has been important in showing that inner speech activates speech-production mechanisms in the brain, connecting phenomenological and neuroscientific approaches. His research on the "predictability" of inner speech and on individual differences in inner speech phenomenology has advanced the empirical study of the phenomenon.
*Key works:* "Inner Speech: Development, Cognitive Functions, Phenomenology, and Neuroscience" (2015, with Fernyhough), *Uncharted: How Uncertainty Shapes the Mind* (2021)

---

**Rupert Hurlburt (1942–)**
American psychologist at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas, developer of Descriptive Experience Sampling (DES). Hurlburt's methodology uses randomly triggered beepers to interrupt subjects and prompt immediate reports of their ongoing inner experience, including inner speech. His careful work has shown enormous individual variability in the frequency and character of inner speech—some people frequently think in words, others rarely do—challenging assumptions that inner speech is universal or predominant. His methodology has been controversial but has generated important empirical data.
*Key works:* *Sampling Normal and Schizophrenic Inner Experience* (1990), *Describing Inner Experience? Proponent Meets Skeptic* (with Schwitzgebel, 2007)
