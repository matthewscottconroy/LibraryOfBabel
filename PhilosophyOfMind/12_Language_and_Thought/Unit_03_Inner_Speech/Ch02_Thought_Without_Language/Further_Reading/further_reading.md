# Further Reading: Thought Without Language

## Primary Sources

**José Luis Bermúdez, *Thinking Without Words* (2003)** — A systematic philosophical account of non-linguistic thought in both humans and non-human animals. Bermúdez argues that creatures without language can have genuine beliefs and desires characterized by proto-logical inferential relations, challenging the Davidsonian view that thought requires language. Essential reading for anyone interested in the relationship between language and cognition.

**Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations* (1953)** — Wittgenstein's private language argument challenges the idea that there could be a language intelligible only to a single individual, and his later philosophy is deeply suspicious of the notion that thought could proceed independently of public linguistic practice. While not directly about non-linguistic thought, his views set the terms for understanding how language and thought relate and have been interpreted both as supporting and challenging the possibility of thought without language.

**Donald Davidson, "Thought and Talk" (1975), in *Inquiries into Truth and Interpretation*** — Davidson argues that having genuine propositional attitudes requires having a language, since the normative standards for interpreting thought are provided by linguistic usage. This influential position makes thought without language impossible by definition, and has been challenged by theorists of non-linguistic thought in animals and preverbal infants.

**Irene Pepperberg, *The Alex Studies: Cognitive and Communicative Abilities of Grey Parrots* (1999)** — Pepperberg's decades-long research on Alex the African grey parrot revealed sophisticated cognitive abilities—object classification, counting, simple arithmetic, and apparent understanding of concepts like "same" and "different"—suggesting complex thought in a creature whose language capacities are learned rather than natural. Raises important questions about the relationship between language, thought, and cognitive complexity.

**Susan Goldin-Meadow, *The Resilience of Language: What Gesture Creation in Deaf Children Can Tell Us About How All Children Learn Language* (2003)** — Studies of deaf children who have not been exposed to sign language show that they spontaneously develop gesture systems with systematic grammatical properties, suggesting that the drive toward language and structured communication is robust and not dependent on linguistic input.

## Secondary Literature

**Temple Grandin, *Thinking in Pictures and Other Reports from My Life with Autism* (1995)** — Grandin, an autistic animal scientist, reports that she thinks in images rather than words and that her visual thinking style is both different from typical cognition and cognitively powerful for understanding animal behavior. Her first-person account raises important questions about the diversity of human cognitive styles and the relationship between language and thought.

**Marc Hauser, *Wild Minds: What Animals Really Think* (2000)** — A readable survey of comparative cognitive research on the mental capacities of animals, covering numerical reasoning, spatial memory, causal understanding, and theory of mind. Provides accessible coverage of the evidence for non-linguistic cognition and its scope.

**David McNeill, *Hand and Mind: What Gestures Reveal About Thought* (1992)** — McNeill argues that gesture and speech form a single computational system and that gesture reveals aspects of thought that are not expressible in the sequential, segmented structure of speech. His account suggests that thought has a spatial-synthetic character that is only partially captured by linguistic structure.

**Stanislas Dehaene, *The Number Sense: How the Mind Creates Mathematics* (1997)** — Dehaene's research on the "number sense" proposes that humans (and many animals) have an innate approximate number system that operates independently of language. Mathematical cognition, at least in its basic forms, appears to be non-linguistically grounded, with language augmenting rather than constituting numerical thought.

**Emmanuel Dupoux (ed.), *Language, Brain, and Cognitive Development: Essays in Honor of Jacques Mehler* (2001)** — Contains important papers on the relationship between language and cognition in development and across species, covering the extent to which non-linguistic cognitive capacities precede and inform linguistic development.
