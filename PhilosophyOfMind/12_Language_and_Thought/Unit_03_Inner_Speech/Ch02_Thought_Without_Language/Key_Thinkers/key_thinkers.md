# Key Thinkers: Thought Without Language

---

**Donald Davidson (1917–2003)**
American analytic philosopher at UC Berkeley, known for his philosophy of action, mind, and language. Davidson's argument that genuine propositional thought requires language is among the most influential positions on the relationship between language and thought. He held that attributing beliefs and desires requires interpreting a creature within the "space of reasons," and that this interpretation is only possible for creatures with language. This view entails that non-human animals and preverbal infants cannot have genuine beliefs, a conclusion that has generated significant opposition from comparative cognitivists and philosophers of animal mind.
*Key works:* "Thought and Talk" (1975), "Rational Animals" (1982), *Inquiries into Truth and Interpretation* (1984)

---

**José Luis Bermúdez (1964–)**
Spanish-American philosopher at Texas A&M University, working on the philosophy of psychology and self-consciousness. Bermúdez's *Thinking Without Words* (2003) develops a systematic account of non-linguistic thought, arguing that creatures without language can possess genuine propositional attitudes characterized by proto-logical inferential norms. His account distinguishes thoughts from language while preserving their normativity, providing a philosophical framework for interpreting cognitive science research on animal minds and preverbal infants.
*Key works:* *Thinking Without Words* (2003), *The Paradox of Self-Consciousness* (1998)

---

**Ludwig Wittgenstein (1889–1951)**
Austrian-British philosopher, one of the most influential philosophers of the twentieth century. Wittgenstein's private language argument in the *Philosophical Investigations* (1953) challenges the idea that language could be genuinely meaningful while being intelligible only to a single subject. More broadly, his later philosophy suggests that what we call "thinking" is deeply entangled with public linguistic practices and forms of life, making the concept of purely private or non-linguistic thought philosophically problematic. His views have been read both as supporting and undermining the possibility of thought without language.
*Key works:* *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (1921), *Philosophical Investigations* (1953), *On Certainty* (1969)

---

**Temple Grandin (1947–)**
American animal scientist at Colorado State University and prominent autistic advocate. Grandin's first-person accounts of her visual thinking style—reporting that she thinks in pictures rather than words, with rich spatial detail—have been important evidence in debates about the diversity of human cognitive styles and the non-necessity of verbal inner speech for complex reasoning. Her expertise in animal behavior and her claim that her visual thinking gives her distinctive insight into animal experience connects the question of non-linguistic thought to debates about animal minds.
*Key works:* *Thinking in Pictures* (1995), *Animals in Translation* (2005), *The Autistic Brain* (2013)

---

**Susan Goldin-Meadow (1946–)**
American cognitive psychologist at the University of Chicago, expert on gesture and language development. Goldin-Meadow's research on deaf children developing home sign systems, on gesture in language acquisition, and on the relationship between gesture and thought has shown that structured communicative expression is more fundamental than any particular language. Her work suggests that the cognitive underpinnings of language—referential, predicative, and sequential structure—can develop without exposure to a conventional linguistic system, illuminating the relationship between pre-linguistic thought and language.
*Key works:* *The Resilience of Language* (2003), *Hearing Gesture: How Our Hands Help Us Think* (2003)
