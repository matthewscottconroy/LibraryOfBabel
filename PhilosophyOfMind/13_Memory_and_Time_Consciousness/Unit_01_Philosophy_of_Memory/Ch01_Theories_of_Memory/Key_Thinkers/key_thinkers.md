# Key Thinkers: Theories of Memory

---

**Henri Bergson (1859–1941)**
French philosopher, Nobel laureate in Literature (1927). Bergson's *Matter and Memory* (1896) distinguishes two fundamentally different forms of memory: habit memory, which is the body's accumulated motor dispositions (knowing how to ride a bicycle), and pure memory, the completely preserved past experience that exists in its original singularity. He argues that genuine recollection is not retrieval from a neural storage site but an active process of contraction—the past leaps into the present. His anti-localizationist arguments challenged the prevailing neuroscience of his time and anticipate contemporary debates about memory as simulation.
*Key works:* *Matter and Memory* (1896), *Time and Free Will* (1889), *Creative Evolution* (1907)

---

**Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)**
German philosopher, founder of phenomenology. Husserl's meticulous analysis of time-consciousness distinguishes the living present (which includes retention of the just-passed and protention of the about-to-come) from secondary memory (recollection, in which the past is re-presented). This distinction is crucial for understanding how episodic memory differs from both perception and imagination: recollection involves re-presenting a past experience as past, with a characteristic temporal indexicality. Husserl's treatment has influenced all subsequent phenomenological approaches to memory.
*Key works:* *On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time* (lectures 1905–1910, published 1928), *Logical Investigations* (1900–1901)

---

**Sven Bernecker (1965–)**
German-American philosopher at UC Irvine and University of Cologne, specializing in epistemology and philosophy of mind. Bernecker has developed the most systematic analytic account of the metaphysics and epistemology of memory, defending a causal-simulationist theory on which remembering an event requires an appropriate causal connection to the original experience together with a phenomenologically appropriate simulation. His work engages rigorously with both philosophy and cognitive science, and he has addressed the relationship between memory, knowledge, and justified belief.
*Key works:* *The Metaphysics of Memory* (2008), *Memory: A Philosophical Study* (2010)

---

**Kourken Michaelian (1978–)**
Anglo-American philosopher at the University of Otago, New Zealand. Michaelian has developed a simulationist account of episodic memory that treats remembering as a constructive, imaginative process rather than mere retrieval—memory is more like mental time travel than like reading from a book. His work integrates philosophical analysis with cognitive science research, arguing that the constructive character of memory does not undermine its epistemic value. He has also contributed to the philosophy of collective and extended memory.
*Key works:* *Mental Time Travel* (2016), "Against Trace Theory" (2011)

---

**John Sutton (1968–)**
Australian philosopher at Macquarie University. Sutton has worked on the history of memory theories, the philosophy of memory traces, and the relationship between memory and the body. His historical research traces how changing conceptions of the brain's material substrate have shaped theories of how memories are stored and retrieved. His more recent work on distributed and embodied memory examines how memory extends beyond the individual brain into bodily skills, external media, and social practices.
*Key works:* *Philosophy and Memory Traces* (1998), "Memory" (2003, *Macmillan Encyclopedia of Cognitive Science*)
