# Further Reading: The Specious Present

## Primary Sources

**William James, *The Principles of Psychology*, Ch. 15: "The Perception of Time" (1890)** — James introduced the concept of the "specious present"—the felt duration of the present moment, typically a few seconds—distinguishing it from the mathematical instant and from the remembered past. His vivid descriptions of temporal experience and his empirical approach to consciousness make this chapter essential reading and one of the great passages in philosophical psychology.

**Edmund Husserl, *On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time* (1928/1964, trans. Churchill)** — Husserl's detailed analysis of the structure of time-consciousness distinguishes the primal impression (the "now"), retention (the just-past held in living consciousness), and protention (the just-anticipated). His account of how these three phases combine into a single intentional act provides the most rigorous phenomenological account of how we experience temporal flow.

**Barry Dainton, *Stream of Consciousness: Unity and Continuity in Conscious Experience* (2000)** — Dainton develops the "extensionalist" account of temporal experience, arguing that experience itself is temporally extended rather than being a series of momentary states. He compares this view with the Husserlian retention-protention model and with Strict Interval accounts, providing a rigorous analysis of their differences and respective strengths.

**Craig Callender, *What Makes Time Special?* (2017)** — A physicist-philosopher's treatment of the relationship between physical time and experienced time, arguing that the "moving spotlight" of experienced nowness has no counterpart in fundamental physics. Addresses the specious present from the perspective of philosophy of physics and metaphysics of time.

**Ian Phillips, "Perceiving Temporal Properties" (2010), *European Journal of Philosophy*** — Phillips defends an extensionalist account of temporal experience against the retentionalist alternative, arguing that the phenomenology of temporal passage requires that experience itself be temporally extended rather than built from instantaneous states. A careful contemporary treatment in analytic philosophy.

## Secondary Literature

**Robin Le Poidevin, *The Images of Time: An Essay on Temporal Representation* (2007)** — An accessible philosophical examination of how time is represented in experience, covering the specious present, temporal passage, and the relationship between experienced and objective time. Suitable for students approaching the topic for the first time.

**Jenann Ismael, *The Situated Self* (2007)** — Develops an account of the perspectival, temporally situated character of experience, arguing that the felt "nowness" of the present is a feature of the self's embeddedness in time rather than a feature of time itself. Bridges philosophy of time and philosophy of mind.

**E.R. Clay, "The 'Specious Present': A New Inquiry into a Recent Doctrine" (1882)** — The original coinage of the term "specious present," predating James but less well-known. Clay argued that what we call "the present" is always a small span of time rather than an instantaneous point.

**Shaun Gallagher, "Phenomenological Contributions to a Theory of Social Cognition" (2001)** — Though focused on social cognition, this paper illuminates how temporal structure of experience (including the specious present) relates to the embodied and intersubjective dimensions of consciousness.

**Thomas Metzinger (ed.), *Conscious Experience* (1995)** — Contains papers on temporal experience and the phenomenology of the specious present from both phenomenological and neuroscientific perspectives. Good anthology for interdisciplinary approaches.
