# Key Thinkers: The Specious Present

---

**William James (1842–1910)**
American philosopher and psychologist, founder of functional psychology and pragmatism. James introduced the "specious present" concept in *The Principles of Psychology* (1890) to describe the felt duration—typically a few seconds—within which temporal experience is unified. He argued that consciousness is a stream rather than a series of discrete states, and that within this stream a "saddle-back" of the specious present allows us to experience both the just-past and the about-to-come as part of a single unified experiential moment. His vivid phenomenological observations set the agenda for all subsequent work on temporal experience.
*Key works:* *The Principles of Psychology* (1890), *Essays in Radical Empiricism* (1912), *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902)

---

**Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)**
German philosopher, founder of phenomenology. Husserl's detailed analysis of time-consciousness, developed in lectures from 1905–1910 and published in 1928, distinguished the primal impression (the hyle of the now-point), retention (the just-passed phase still held in living consciousness as modified), and protention (anticipation of the about-to-come). His key insight—that retention is not a secondary memory or re-presentation but an integral part of the living present—distinguishes phenomenological retentionalism from simple snapshot accounts of experience. The structure of retention-impression-protention became the fundamental framework for phenomenological time-consciousness.
*Key works:* *On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time* (1928), *Ideas I* (1913)

---

**Barry Dainton (1958–)**
British philosopher of mind and metaphysics at the University of Liverpool. Dainton developed "extensional" accounts of temporal experience as an alternative to Husserlian retentionalism, arguing that experience itself occupies temporal intervals rather than being built from momentary primal impressions. In his view, temporal passage is directly perceived because experiences are genuinely extended in time—we do not reconstruct duration from a series of instants. His detailed comparison of extensionalism, retentionalism, and discrete moment theories provides the most rigorous contemporary treatment of the analytic options.
*Key works:* *Stream of Consciousness* (2000), *Time and Space* (2001), "The Phenomenal Continuum" (2010)

---

**Ian Phillips (1981–)**
British philosopher at Johns Hopkins University, working on philosophy of mind and the science of perception. Phillips has been a leading voice in defending extensionalist accounts of temporal experience and in arguing that the phenomenology of time-consciousness poses genuine challenges for physicalist accounts of mind. His careful analysis of whether temporal passage can be a feature of perceptual experience, and his engagement with empirical evidence on time perception, have advanced the philosophical understanding of what the phenomenology of temporal flow requires.
*Key works:* "Perceiving Temporal Properties" (2010), "Consciousness and Criterion: On Block's Case for Unconscious Seeing" (2016)

---

**Robin Le Poidevin (1962–)**
British philosopher at the University of Leeds, specializing in metaphysics and philosophy of time. Le Poidevin's work on temporal representation bridges philosophy of time and philosophy of mind, examining how temporal properties—duration, order, passage—are represented in experience and whether experienced time corresponds to objective time. His accessible treatments of the specious present and the metaphysics of time make his work valuable as both a scholarly and pedagogical resource.
*Key works:* *The Images of Time* (2007), *Travels in Four Dimensions* (2003), "The Experience and Perception of Time" (2019, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*)
