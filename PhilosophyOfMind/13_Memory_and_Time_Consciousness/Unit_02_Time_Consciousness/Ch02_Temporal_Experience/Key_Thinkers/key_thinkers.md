# Key Thinkers: Temporal Experience

---

**Martin Heidegger (1889–1976)**
German philosopher, one of the most influential and controversial figures in twentieth-century thought. Heidegger's analysis of temporality in *Being and Time* (1927) argues that time is not primarily an objective container in which events occur but the fundamental structure of human existence (Dasein). Primordial temporality has three "ecstases"—having-been (past as thrown facticity), coming-towards (future as ownmost possibility), and making-present (present as engagement with the world)—which are unified in the structure of care. Heidegger's account of death as Dasein's ownmost possibility provides temporality with its individualizing force.
*Key works:* *Being and Time* (1927), *The Concept of Time* (1924), *Time and Being* (1962)

---

**Paul Ricoeur (1913–2005)**
French philosopher working in the hermeneutic and phenomenological traditions. Ricoeur's three-volume *Time and Narrative* (1984–1988) develops a philosophy of historical and fictional narrative as the primary mode through which humans make time humanly meaningful. Drawing on Augustine's *Confessions* and Aristotle's *Poetics*, Ricoeur argues that there is an "aporia of time" that can only be addressed through narrative—the "emplotment" that transforms a sequence of events into a story with beginning, middle, and end. His account directly connects temporal experience to personal identity and the meaning of a human life.
*Key works:* *Time and Narrative* (3 vols., 1984–1988), *Oneself as Another* (1992)

---

**Matthew Ratcliffe (1973–)**
British philosopher at the University of York, specializing in phenomenological psychopathology. Ratcliffe's phenomenological research on depression includes important insights into how emotional disorders alter temporal experience: in depression, the future loses its openness and the past its distance, leaving the sufferer in a distorted temporal world where possibilities appear foreclosed. His concept of "existential feelings"—background orientations toward the world that structure all more specific emotions—has proven valuable for understanding how mood disorders affect the temporal structure of lived experience.
*Key works:* *Feelings of Being* (2008), *Experiences of Depression* (2015)

---

**Thomas Fuchs (1958–)**
German psychiatrist and philosopher at Heidelberg University, leading figure in phenomenological psychiatry. Fuchs's work examines how temporal experience is embodied and lived in bodily habits, rhythms, and intercorporeal engagements. His research on temporal disruptions in depression (retardation, temporal stagnation), mania (temporal acceleration), and schizophrenia (fragmentation of temporal flow) provides important phenomenological-clinical evidence for how time-consciousness is grounded in the lived body and is vulnerable to disruption by illness.
*Key works:* *Ecology of the Brain* (2018), "Implicit and Explicit Temporality" (2007), *Leib und Lebenswelt* (2008)

---

**Augustine of Hippo (354–430 CE)**
North African bishop and philosopher, one of the most important Christian thinkers. Augustine's analysis of time in Book XI of the *Confessions* remains philosophically remarkable: he argues that the present has no duration and that past, present, and future are all aspects of the soul's present—the present of past things (memory), the present of present things (attention), and the present of future things (expectation). This psychological analysis of time anticipates both Husserlian phenomenology and contemporary debates about the specious present, while embedding temporal experience within a theology of eternity.
*Key works:* *Confessions* (397–400 CE), *The City of God* (413–426 CE)
