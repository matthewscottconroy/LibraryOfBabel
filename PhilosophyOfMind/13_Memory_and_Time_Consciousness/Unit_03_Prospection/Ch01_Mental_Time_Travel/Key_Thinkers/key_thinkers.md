# Key Thinkers: Mental Time Travel

---

**Endel Tulving (1927–)**
Estonian-Canadian cognitive neuroscientist at the Rotman Research Institute, Toronto. Tulving's distinction between episodic and semantic memory (1972) and his concept of autonoetic consciousness—the sense of subjective time that characterizes episodic recollection—established the foundational framework for mental time travel research. He coined the term "chronesthesia" for the mental faculty enabling subjective time travel and argued that this capacity may be uniquely or highly developed in humans. His theoretical framework has been hugely productive in guiding neuroscientific research on memory systems.
*Key works:* "Episodic and Semantic Memory" (1972), "Memory and Consciousness" (1985), "Episodic Memory: From Mind to Brain" (2002)

---

**Daniel Schacter (1952–)**
American cognitive neuroscientist at Harvard University. Schacter's "constructive episodic simulation" hypothesis holds that the same brain system that enables episodic memory also supports future imagination, planning, and counterfactual thinking—a "prospective brain." His research program using neuroimaging, patient studies, and experimental psychology has been central to establishing that memory and prospection share neural architecture (primarily the default mode network), with profound implications for understanding why memory is constructive rather than reproductive.
*Key works:* *The Seven Sins of Memory* (2001), "Remembering the Past to Imagine the Future" (2007, with Addis and Buckner), *The Future of Memory* (2012)

---

**Kourken Michaelian (1978–)**
Anglo-American philosopher at the University of Otago. Michaelian has provided the most philosophically developed account of mental time travel, arguing within a simulationist framework that episodic memory is a form of imaginative simulation constrained by causal traces of experience. His work addresses the epistemological challenge: if memory is constructive rather than reproductive, can it still yield genuine knowledge of the past? He argues it can, provided appropriate reliability conditions are met—a position that has significant implications for how we understand the epistemic standing of first-person reports.
*Key works:* *Mental Time Travel* (2016), "Against Trace Theory" (2011)

---

**Thomas Suddendorf (1966–)**
German-Australian developmental and comparative psychologist at the University of Queensland. Suddendorf has conducted comparative research examining whether non-human animals possess the capacity for genuine mental time travel or show only more limited forms of behavioral flexibility. His work argues that while many animals can engage in prospective behaviors (caching food, for example), genuine mental time travel—involving autonoetic self-projection—may require cognitive capacities (including a concept of time and a capacity for open-ended future simulation) that are uniquely developed in humans.
*Key works:* *The Gap* (2013), "The Evolution of Foresight" (2007, with Corballis)

---

**Donna Rose Addis (1976–)**
New Zealand-Canadian cognitive neuroscientist at Harvard University and the Rotman Research Institute. Addis has conducted leading neuroimaging and clinical research on the neural substrates of episodic memory and future imagination, contributing to the development and empirical testing of the constructive episodic simulation hypothesis. Her studies of amnesic patients—who show parallel deficits in past recollection and future imagination—provide some of the strongest evidence that these capacities share common neural mechanisms centered on the hippocampus and default mode network.
*Key works:* "Remembering the Past to Imagine the Future" (2007, with Schacter and Buckner), "The Hippocampus and Imagining the Future" (2012)
