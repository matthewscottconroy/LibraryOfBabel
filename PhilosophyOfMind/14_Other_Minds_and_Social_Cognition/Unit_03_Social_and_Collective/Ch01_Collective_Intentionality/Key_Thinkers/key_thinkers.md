# Key Thinkers: Collective Intentionality

---

**John Searle (1932–)**
American philosopher of mind and language at UC Berkeley. Searle introduced the concept of collective intentionality as a philosophically primitive feature of social life that cannot be reduced to individual I-intentions plus mutual beliefs. On his view, we-intentions (as in "we are attacking together") exist in individual minds but have an irreducibly collective form that distinguishes them from I-intentions to cooperate. His analysis connects collective intentionality to his broader philosophy of social reality, arguing that social institutions are constituted by collective intentional assignment of functions and status through "X counts as Y in context C" declarations.
*Key works:* "Collective Intentions and Actions" (1990), *The Construction of Social Reality* (1995), *Making the Social World* (2010)

---

**Michael Bratman (1945–)**
American philosopher at Stanford University, known for his planning theory of agency. Bratman's reductionist account of shared intention analyzes it in terms of individual intentions with appropriate content, meshing sub-plans, and mutual responsiveness to each other's intentions. He argues that shared intention creates distinctive normative relations—obligations of mutual support, responsiveness to the other's intentions, and re-evaluation—without requiring irreducibly plural subjects or primitive we-intentions. His account is influential for being both philosophically rigorous and tractable for practical philosophy.
*Key works:* *Intention, Plans, and Practical Reason* (1987), "Shared Cooperative Activity" (1992), *Faces of Intention* (1999)

---

**Margaret Gilbert (1942–)**
British-American philosopher at UC Irvine. Gilbert's "plural subject" theory holds that social groups and collective phenomena are constituted by joint commitments—special normative bonds created when individuals jointly commit together to espouse a view or act in a certain way. These joint commitments create obligations and entitlements between members that are not reducible to individual obligations: I am obligated to you because of our joint commitment, not because of anything I individually committed to. Her anti-reductionism extends to collective belief, social convention, and political authority.
*Key works:* *On Social Facts* (1989), *Living Together: Rationality, Sociality, and Obligation* (1996), *Joint Commitment: How We Make the Social World* (2014)

---

**Raimo Tuomela (1940–2011)**
Finnish philosopher at the University of Helsinki. Tuomela developed a comprehensive philosophy of sociality distinguishing between I-mode and we-mode attitudes. We-mode intentions are taken from the perspective of a group as a group—they involve operating as a group member—rather than merely as an individual whose intentions relate to group activity. He argued that genuine collective action, social norms, and group beliefs require we-mode attitudes that cannot be fully reduced to I-mode attitudes, even very complex ones. His systematic treatment of the structure of social agency remains a major resource.
*Key works:* *Social Action* (1984), *The Importance of Us* (1995), *The Philosophy of Sociality* (2007)

---

**Matthew Tomasello (1950–)**
American developmental psychologist and primatologist at Duke University. Tomasello's research on shared intentionality and joint attention has provided the empirical foundation for understanding how collective intentionality develops in humans and why it is so much more elaborate in humans than in other primates. His account of how "shared intentionality"—the ability to engage in genuinely joint attention and cooperative activities—underlies the uniquely human forms of culture, language, and cumulative learning has been highly influential in connecting philosophical and empirical approaches to collective cognition.
*Key works:* *The Cultural Origins of Human Cognition* (1999), *A Natural History of Human Morality* (2016), *Becoming Human* (2019)
