# Key Thinkers: Distributed Cognition

---

**Edwin Hutchins (1948–)**
American cognitive anthropologist and cognitive scientist at UC San Diego. Hutchins' *Cognition in the Wild* (1995) established distributed cognition as a research paradigm by showing that navigation on a US Navy ship is best understood as a cognitive system distributed across crew members, instruments, and representations rather than as the sum of individual cognitive acts. His approach argues that cognitive science must study naturally occurring cognitive systems in their ecological context rather than isolating individual cognitive processes in laboratories. His later work on embodied cognition and gesture has extended these insights.
*Key works:* *Cognition in the Wild* (1995), "Distributed Cognition" (2000), *How Brains Make Up Their Minds* (2019)

---

**Andy Clark (1957–)**
Scottish philosopher and cognitive scientist at the University of Edinburgh. Clark is the most prolific and influential defender of the extended mind thesis, arguing that mind is constitutively coupled with body and world rather than being bounded by skull and skin. His parity principle—that if a non-biological process plays the same functional role as a biological cognitive process, it should count as cognitive—grounds his argument that external tools, representations, and media can literally be parts of cognitive systems. His work on predictive processing extends this framework to the brain's construction of reality from internally generated predictions.
*Key works:* *Being There* (1997), *Supersizing the Mind* (2008), *Surfing Uncertainty* (2016)

---

**David Chalmers (1966–)**
Australian philosopher at NYU, known for his work on consciousness and philosophy of mind. Chalmers co-authored "The Extended Mind" (1998) with Clark, arguing using the Parity Principle that beliefs stored in external media (like Otto's notebook) are genuine beliefs when they meet the same functional criteria as internal beliefs. While better known for his work on consciousness and the hard problem, his contribution to extended mind theory has been enormously influential and has generated a major debate about the bounds of the mental.
*Key works:* "The Extended Mind" (1998, with Clark), *The Conscious Mind* (1996), *Constructing the World* (2012)

---

**Merlin Donald (1939–)**
Canadian cognitive neuroscientist and psychologist at Queen's University. Donald's evolutionary account of human cognition proposes that modern minds are hybrid systems combining biological memory with "exograms"—external memory traces in cultural and material media. He argues that this cognitive integration of biological and cultural memory is what enables the cumulative cultural learning that distinguishes human from animal cognition. His theory bridges cognitive science, evolutionary biology, and the history of culture.
*Key works:* *Origins of the Modern Mind* (1991), *A Mind So Rare: The Evolution of Human Consciousness* (2001)

---

**Lambros Malafouris (1972–)**
Greek cognitive scientist and archaeologist at Oxford University. Malafouris' "Material Engagement Theory" argues that material things—artifacts, tools, signs—are not merely external supports for cognition but constitutively shape mental processes. His approach bridges cognitive science, archaeological theory, and philosophy of mind to argue that human cognitive evolution cannot be understood apart from the co-evolution of minds and material culture. His work extends distributed cognition claims deep into the archaeological record.
*Key works:* *How Things Shape the Mind* (2013), "At the Potter's Wheel: An Argument for Material Agency" (2008)
