# Key Thinkers: Husserlian Method

---

**Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)**
German philosopher, founder of phenomenology. Husserl's method of phenomenological investigation begins with the epoché—the "bracketing" of the natural attitude's commitment to the independent existence of the world—in order to disclose the structures of pure consciousness. The eidetic reduction then identifies essential, invariant features of intentional acts. The noesis-noema distinction describes each intentional act as having both a real, psychological side (noesis: the act of intending) and an ideal, correlative content (noema: the sense through which the object is intended). Husserl's transcendental idealism holds that the world as we know it is constituted by transcendental subjectivity, though the precise sense of "constitution" remains debated.
*Key works:* *Logical Investigations* (1900–1901), *Ideas I* (1913), *Cartesian Meditations* (1931), *The Crisis of European Sciences* (1936/1970)

---

**Franz Brentano (1838–1917)**
German philosopher and psychologist, Husserl's teacher. Brentano revived the scholastic concept of intentionality—the "aboutness" or "directedness" of mental states—arguing that what distinguishes mental from physical phenomena is that mental acts are always directed toward an object (which may or may not exist). His "descriptive psychology" aimed to classify and analyze the basic types of mental act. While Husserl transformed Brentano's approach in crucial ways, Brentano's identification of intentionality as the mark of the mental is the direct ancestor of Husserl's phenomenological project.
*Key works:* *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint* (1874), *The Origin of the Knowledge of Right and Wrong* (1889)

---

**Robert Sokolowski (1934–)**
American phenomenologist at the Catholic University of America. Sokolowski is known for developing "categorial intuition"—following Husserl's account in the Sixth Logical Investigation—and for providing some of the most rigorous and accessible expositions of Husserlian method for English-speaking audiences. His *Introduction to Phenomenology* (2000) is widely used as a teaching text and represents one of the clearest guides to the epoché, the noesis-noema distinction, and the phenomenological reduction available in English.
*Key works:* *Husserlian Meditations* (1974), *Presence and Absence* (1978), *Introduction to Phenomenology* (2000)

---

**Dan Zahavi (1967–)**
Danish phenomenologist at the University of Copenhagen and Oxford. Zahavi has done more than any other contemporary philosopher to make Husserl's phenomenological method available to analytic philosophy of mind and to defend its continuing relevance. His scholarly work covers Husserl's development from the *Logical Investigations* through genetic phenomenology, his account of self-consciousness and subjectivity, and the application of phenomenological method to consciousness science. He argues that phenomenology and analytic philosophy are complementary rather than competing approaches.
*Key works:* *Husserl's Phenomenology* (2003), *Subjectivity and Selfhood* (2005), *Self and Other* (2014)

---

**Hubert Dreyfus (1929–2017)**
American philosopher at UC Berkeley, known for bringing phenomenology into dialogue with cognitive science and AI. Dreyfus's critique of classical AI drew heavily on Husserlian and Heideggerian phenomenology, arguing that cognitivist approaches to mind fail to account for the background of practical skills and coping that grounds all explicit cognition. His work showed that Husserlian intentionality—especially in its concrete, embodied forms—provides resources for understanding cognition that computer models of mind cannot capture.
*Key works:* *What Computers Can't Do* (1972), *Being-in-the-World* (1991), "Husserl, Intentionality, and Cognitive Science" (1982)
