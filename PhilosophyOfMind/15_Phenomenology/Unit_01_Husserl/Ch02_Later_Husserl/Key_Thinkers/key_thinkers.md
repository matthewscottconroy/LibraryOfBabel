# Key Thinkers: Later Husserl

---

**Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)**
German philosopher and founder of phenomenology. Husserl's later work takes a decisive genetic and historical turn: instead of examining static intentional structures, genetic phenomenology investigates how these structures come to be constituted through passive synthesis, temporal flow, and embodied habit. The concept of the Lebenswelt (lifeworld)—the pre-scientific, pre-theoretical world of everyday practical engagement—emerges as both the forgotten ground of science and the proper starting point for phenomenology. Husserl's diagnosis of a "crisis" in European science as a loss of the connection between scientific theory and lived meaning remains culturally resonant.
*Key works:* *The Crisis of European Sciences* (1936/1970), *Analyses Concerning Passive and Active Synthesis* (1966/2001), *Experience and Judgment* (1948)

---

**Alfred Schutz (1899–1959)**
Austrian-American sociologist and philosopher at the New School for Social Research. Schutz applied Husserlian phenomenology to the social world, developing a systematic account of how the structures of ordinary social life—typification, relevance, the natural attitude, the we-relationship—are constituted from the first-person perspective of social actors. His work provided the philosophical foundations for phenomenological sociology and ethnomethodology, showing how the life-world concept can be made fruitful for empirical social science.
*Key works:* *The Phenomenology of the Social World* (1932/1967), *Collected Papers* (3 vols., 1962–1966)

---

**Thomas Luckmann (1927–2016) and Peter Berger (1929–2017)**
Austrian-German sociologist (Luckmann) and American sociologist (Berger). Together they extended Schutz's phenomenological sociology into the sociology of knowledge, arguing that social reality is a human construction maintained by shared interpretive frameworks. Their account of the "social construction of reality" drew on Husserl's life-world concept to show how everyday knowledge is institutionalized and how institutions are legitimated through symbolic universes.
*Key works:* *The Social Construction of Reality* (1966, Berger and Luckmann)

---

**Klaus Held (1936–)**
German phenomenologist at the University of Wuppertal. Held is known for his scholarship on the late Husserl, particularly his expositions of genetic phenomenology and the lifeworld concept. His work has been important in making the complexities of Husserl's later manuscripts accessible to students and scholars, and in showing the systematic connections between early and late Husserl.
*Key works:* *Lebendige Gegenwart* (1966), "Husserl's Phenomenological Method" (2003)

---

**Maurice Merleau-Ponty (1908–1961)**
French phenomenologist. While Merleau-Ponty's primary contribution is to body phenomenology, his engagement with the late Husserl—particularly *Ideas II* and the lifeworld concept—was formative. He developed Husserl's notion of the lived body (*Leib*) into a full-scale phenomenology of embodied perception and argued that the life-world concept requires radical rethinking of the relationship between subject and world. His critique of Husserl's residual idealism shaped much subsequent phenomenology.
*Key works:* *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945), "Husserl at the Limits of Phenomenology" (2002)
