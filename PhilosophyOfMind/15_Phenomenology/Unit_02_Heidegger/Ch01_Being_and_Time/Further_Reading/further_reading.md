# Further Reading: Being and Time

## Primary Sources

**Martin Heidegger, *Being and Time* (1927, trans. Stambaugh 1996 or Macquarrie and Robinson 1962)** — The central work of twentieth-century continental philosophy, arguing that the fundamental question of philosophy is the question of Being, and that this question must be approached through an analysis of the being who asks it—Dasein (human being). The analysis of Dasein's structure—being-in-the-world, care, thrownness, das Man, anxiety, being-toward-death, and temporality—provides both an ontological foundation and a phenomenological description of human existence.

**Hubert Dreyfus, *Being-in-the-World: A Commentary on Heidegger's "Being and Time," Division I* (1991)** — The most influential English-language commentary on Division I of *Being and Time*, providing clear analytic reconstructions of Heidegger's arguments and situating them in relation to cognitive science, AI, and the philosophy of action. Dreyfus's interpretation emphasizes the practical, coping character of Dasein's everyday engagement.

**Frederick Olafson, *Heidegger and the Philosophy of Mind* (1987)** — Examines the implications of Heidegger's *Being and Time* for the philosophy of mind, arguing that Heidegger's account of intentionality, agency, and the first-person perspective provides resources that analytic philosophy of mind has overlooked.

**Charles Guignon (ed.), *The Cambridge Companion to Heidegger* (1993)** — A comprehensive collection covering all phases of Heidegger's work, with particularly valuable essays on *Being and Time*, the analysis of Dasein, and the connections to analytic philosophy and cognitive science.

**William Blattner, *Heidegger's Being and Time: A Reader's Guide* (2006)** — A clear and philosophically careful guide to *Being and Time*, suitable for students encountering Heidegger for the first time. Blattner explains the key concepts—Dasein, being-in-the-world, care, temporality—with analytical precision.

## Secondary Literature

**Taylor Carman, *Heidegger's Analytic: Interpretation, Discourse, and Authenticity in "Being and Time"* (2003)** — A philosophically sophisticated interpretation of *Being and Time* that focuses on Heidegger's analysis of interpretation, discourse (Rede), and authenticity, engaging with analytic philosophy of language throughout.

**Cristina Lafont, *Heidegger, Language, and World-Disclosure* (2000)** — Examines Heidegger's account of how language discloses the world, connecting this to questions about the relationship between conceptual schemes, interpretation, and reality. Essential for understanding Heidegger's contribution to philosophy of language.

**Hans-Georg Gadamer, *Truth and Method* (1960)** — Gadamer develops Heideggerian hermeneutics into a full-scale philosophical hermeneutics, arguing that understanding is always historically situated and that the hermeneutic circle is not a problem to be overcome but the very structure of human understanding.

**Mark Wrathall (ed.), *Heidegger and Unconcealment: Truth, Language, and History* (2011)** — Examines Heidegger's concept of truth as alētheia (unconcealment) and its development from *Being and Time* through the later work. Important for understanding the continuity and change in Heidegger's thought.

**Richard Polt, *Heidegger: An Introduction* (1999)** — A clear and accessible introduction to Heidegger's entire career, from *Being and Time* through the turn and the later works on technology and language. Suitable for students and non-specialists.
