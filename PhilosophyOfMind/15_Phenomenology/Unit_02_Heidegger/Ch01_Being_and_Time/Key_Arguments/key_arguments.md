# Key Arguments, Concepts, and Thought Experiments: Being and Time

## Key Arguments

**The Ontological Difference**
Heidegger argues that the entire history of Western philosophy has confused beings (entities: the things that exist) with Being (das Sein: the sheer being or "being-ness" of beings). Metaphysics has always asked "what are beings?" and answered by specifying a supreme being (God, substance, subject) but has never raised the question of Being itself — what it means for anything to be at all. This oblivion of Being is not accidental but is built into the structure of Dasein's everyday absorption in beings. Being and Time's project is the "destruction" of the tradition and the recovery of the question of Being.

**The Being-in-the-World Argument Against Cartesianism**
Heidegger argues that the Cartesian picture of a subject facing an external world is ontologically derivative: Dasein's fundamental mode of being is being-in-the-world — a unitary structure in which being, world, and self are co-constitutive, not externally related. The subject is never first "in here" and then facing "out there"; Dasein is always already involved with things, others, and situations. This argument dissolves the Cartesian problem of the external world by showing it arises only within an artificially decontextualized theoretical attitude.

**The Equipment Analysis (Ready-to-Hand)**
Heidegger argues that the fundamental way in which things show up for Dasein is not as objects of theoretical contemplation (present-at-hand) but as equipment (Zeug) — as ready-to-hand: organized in a web of in-order-to and for-the-sake-of relations that constitute their mode of being. When the hammer is working properly, it "withdraws" from attention; it is when equipment fails (breaks, becomes unavailable, gets in the way) that it first becomes conspicuous as a thing with properties. This analysis challenges the primacy of the theoretical attitude in epistemology.

**The Existential Analytic and Authenticity**
Heidegger argues that the fundamental ontological structures of Dasein — thrownness, projection, falling, care, being-toward-death — can be covered over by inauthenticity: the absorption in the anonymous "they-self" (das Man) that dispels anxiety and the call of conscience by distributing responsibility across no one in particular. Authenticity is not a higher faculty but a "modification" of inauthenticity in which Dasein owns its ownmost possibilities, including its death, in anticipatory resoluteness. The argument connects ontology to ethics without reducing either to the other.

**The Structure of Care**
Heidegger argues that the being of Dasein as a whole is care (Sorge): a unitary structure of thrownness (always already in a world, factically), projection (always already ahead of itself in possibilities), and falling (always already amid beings in the world). Care is not psychological concern but the ontological structure of Dasein's existence — the way its being is always a "to-be" — and it unifies the apparently disparate existential structures of Being and Time into a single ontological whole.

## Core Concepts

**Dasein**
Dasein (literally "being-there") is Heidegger's term for the being that is characterized by an understanding of Being — paradigmatically, the human being, but defined by its ontological capacity rather than its species membership. Dasein is not a subject with properties but a being whose being is always an issue for it — a being for whom its own being matters. Dasein exists through its possibilities and its factical thrownness, not as a substance or thing.

**Being-in-the-World**
Being-in-the-world is Heidegger's term for the unitary structure of Dasein's fundamental mode of existence: the "in" is not spatial containment but the engaged dwelling of Dasein in a world it is already involved with and that is always already structured by projects, others, and significance. The concept replaces the Cartesian subject-object structure with an original unity in which Dasein, world, and others are co-constitutive.

**Ready-to-Hand (Zuhandenheit) and Present-at-Hand (Vorhandenheit)**
Equipment that is ready-to-hand is encountered in its functional role within a web of instrumental relations — used without being thematized. When equipment breaks or is missing, it becomes present-at-hand: it stands out as an object with properties, available for theoretical scrutiny. For Heidegger, the ready-to-hand is ontologically primary; the present-at-hand is a derivative mode that arises from disruption of practical engagement. This distinction challenges the traditional epistemological starting point of a subject contemplating objects.

**Thrownness (Geworfenheit)**
Thrownness is the ontological structure by which Dasein always already finds itself in a situation it did not choose: its factical circumstances (language, culture, body, historical epoch) are not chosen but found, constituting the "from which" of Dasein's projection. Thrownness is the past dimension of Dasein's temporality — the always-already of existence. It is not a contingent limitation but constitutive of what Dasein is: a being that is "thrown" into a world and must take up its thrown situation in its projects.

**Being-Toward-Death**
Being-toward-death is the existential structure by which Dasein relates to its own death as its ownmost, non-relational, certain, and indefinitely impending possibility. Death is not a future event but a structural feature of Dasein's existence: it is always possible and always individualizes Dasein. Being-toward-death in authenticity — facing rather than fleeing one's mortality — is the condition for genuine resoluteness and ownership of one's existence.

**Das Man (The They-Self)**
Das Man is Heidegger's term for the anonymous, impersonal structure of everyday social existence: the "they" that sets standards, makes decisions, and distributes responsibility so that no one in particular is responsible. One drives "the way they drive," speaks "the way one speaks," fears "what one fears." Das Man is not a specific social group but the ontological structure of public averageness that disperses Dasein and covers over its ownmost possibilities. Authenticity involves a modification of das Man, not its elimination.

## Thought Experiments

**The Broken Hammer**
Heidegger's phenomenological observation: when the hammer is working smoothly, it "withdraws" — you are not attending to it, only to the nail. When the hammer breaks, it suddenly becomes conspicuous as a thing with properties: too heavy, the wrong shape. This passage from ready-to-hand to present-at-hand illustrates that the theoretical attitude (attending to things as objects with properties) is not primary but derived from practical breakdown. The thought experiment challenges the assumption that perception of objects is the fundamental mode of world-contact.

**The Call of Conscience**
Heidegger describes an existential experience: the call of conscience (Gewissensruf) that summons Dasein from its absorption in das Man to its ownmost possibilities. The call is uncanny — it comes from nowhere and from Dasein itself simultaneously — and its content is silence: it calls Dasein to itself without words. As a phenomenological exercise, attending to moments of anxious self-awareness when one recognizes that one has been "going along" with the they-self and senses a demand to take up one's own existence is meant to reveal the call-structure of conscience.

**Anxiety (Angst) and Uncanniness**
Heidegger distinguishes anxiety (Angst) from fear: fear has a specific object (I am afraid of the dog), while anxiety is free-floating — its "object" is world-as-such, nothing in particular. In anxiety, the familiar world becomes strange (unheimlich: not-at-home), and Dasein is confronted with its groundless thrownness. This phenomenological observation reveals that the everydayness of Dasein is a flight from this uncanniness, and that authenticity requires facing it. The thought experiment is the invitation to notice and stay with a genuine moment of anxiety rather than immediately dispersing it.
