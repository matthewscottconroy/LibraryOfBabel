# Key Thinkers: Being and Time

---

**Martin Heidegger (1889–1976)**
German philosopher, student of Husserl and one of the most influential—and controversial—figures in twentieth-century thought. *Being and Time* (1927) offers a fundamental ontology in which the question of Being is approached through analysis of Dasein's structure. Key concepts include: being-in-the-world (the original unity of subject and environment against Cartesian dualism), ready-to-hand versus present-at-hand (the priority of practical engagement over theoretical contemplation), thrownness (Dasein's factical givenness to itself), das Man (the anonymous public "they" through which Dasein initially understands itself), care (the unified structure of existence), and being-toward-death (the individuating possibility). Heidegger's analysis transformed phenomenology from a philosophy of consciousness into an ontology of existence.
*Key works:* *Being and Time* (1927), *The Basic Problems of Phenomenology* (1975/1982), *Introduction to Metaphysics* (1935/1959)

---

**Hubert Dreyfus (1929–2017)**
American philosopher at UC Berkeley. Dreyfus's *Being-in-the-World* (1991) is the most influential analytic interpretation of Heidegger's *Being and Time* in English, providing a detailed reconstruction of Division I that emphasizes the priority of practical, non-representational coping over explicit theory. His interpretation of Heidegger as providing a fundamental critique of representationalism has been enormously productive in philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and AI, connecting Heidegger to debates about embodied, embedded, and enactive cognition.
*Key works:* *Being-in-the-World* (1991), *What Computers Can't Do* (1972), *Mind Over Machine* (1986)

---

**Taylor Carman (1963–)**
American philosopher at Barnard College. Carman's work on Heidegger combines close textual scholarship with analytic philosophical precision, arguing for readings of *Being and Time* that make its claims tractable within contemporary philosophy of language and mind. His emphasis on Heidegger's account of interpretation, assertoric discourse, and authenticity has been influential in showing the connections between Heidegger's phenomenology and questions in philosophy of language.
*Key works:* *Heidegger's Analytic* (2003), *Merleau-Ponty* (2008)

---

**Charles Guignon (1944–)**
American philosopher at the University of South Florida. Guignon's work on Heidegger has focused on authenticity, the narrative structure of Dasein's existence, and the connections between Heidegger's ontology and practical philosophy. His edited collections and monographs have helped make Heidegger accessible to students and have shown the relevance of Heidegger's analysis to questions about character, identity, and the good life.
*Key works:* *Heidegger and the Problem of Knowledge* (1983), *On Being Authentic* (2004)

---

**William Blattner (1960–)**
American philosopher at Georgetown University. Blattner's work on Heidegger's temporality and on his relationship to Kant and idealism provides some of the most philosophically careful engagements with the deepest arguments of *Being and Time*. His reader's guide to *Being and Time* has become a standard teaching resource, and his account of Heidegger's temporality as a revision rather than rejection of Kantian views on time has been widely discussed.
*Key works:* *Heidegger's Temporal Idealism* (1999), *Heidegger's Being and Time: A Reader's Guide* (2006)
