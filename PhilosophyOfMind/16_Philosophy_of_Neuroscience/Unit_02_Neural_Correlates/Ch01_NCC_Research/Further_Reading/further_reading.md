# Further Reading: Neural Correlates of Consciousness

## Primary Sources

**Francis Crick and Christof Koch, "Towards a Neurobiological Theory of Consciousness" (1990), *Seminars in the Neurosciences*** — The paper that launched the NCC (Neural Correlates of Consciousness) research program as a scientific enterprise, arguing that consciousness science should focus on finding the minimal neural mechanisms sufficient for any conscious experience. Crick and Koch proposed focusing on visual consciousness and binocular rivalry as tractable entry points.

**Christof Koch, *The Quest for Consciousness: A Neurobiological Approach* (2004)** — Koch's comprehensive account of the NCC research program, covering binocular rivalry, blindsight, neuroimaging, and the proposal that NCCs for visual consciousness involve certain patterns of neural firing in posterior cortex. Engages with philosophical questions about the relationship between NCCs and consciousness theories.

**Nikos Logothetis, "Vision: A Window on Consciousness" (1999), *Scientific American*** — Logothetis's research on binocular rivalry in monkeys, which allows the identification of neurons whose firing tracks the animal's reported perception rather than the physical stimulus, represents one of the cleanest methods for identifying NCCs. This accessible review explains the approach and its significance.

**Victor Lamme, "Why Visual Attention and Awareness Are Different" (2003), *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*** — Argues that visual awareness and attentional access are dissociable, implying that the NCC for consciousness is distinct from the NCC for attention or global broadcast. This paper is important for debates about the relationship between global workspace theories and local recurrent processing accounts of NCCs.

**Ned Block, "On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness" (1995), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*** — Block's distinction between access consciousness (information that is globally available for report and reasoning) and phenomenal consciousness (what it is like) has been central to debates about NCC methodology: NCCs for access consciousness may not be NCCs for phenomenal consciousness.

## Secondary Literature

**Stan Dehaene, *Consciousness and the Brain: Deciphering How the Brain Codes Our Thoughts* (2014)** — A readable synthesis of global workspace theory and NCC research by the leading proponent of the global neuronal workspace, arguing that consciousness corresponds to ignition—the global broadcast of information across frontoparietal cortex.

**Christof Koch, Marcello Massimini, Melanie Boly, and Giulio Tononi, "Neural Correlates of Consciousness: Progress and Problems" (2016), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*** — A comprehensive update on the state of NCC research, covering methodological advances, the relationship to theories of consciousness, and remaining challenges. An excellent current overview.

**Eric Schwitzgebel, *Perplexities of Consciousness* (2011)** — Examines the methodological difficulties of introspection-based reports in consciousness research, arguing that we systematically misrepresent our own conscious experience and that this creates deep problems for NCC methodology.

**Thomas Metzinger (ed.), *The Blackwell Companion to Consciousness* (2007)** — A comprehensive handbook covering all aspects of consciousness science and philosophy, including NCC research, theories of consciousness, and philosophical interpretations of neuroscientific findings.

**Henry Markram, "The Human Brain Project" (2012), *Scientific American*** — Reviews the large-scale neuroscientific program aimed at comprehensive brain simulation, raising philosophical questions about what a complete computational model of the brain would and would not tell us about consciousness.
