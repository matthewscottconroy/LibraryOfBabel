# Key Thinkers: Neural Correlates of Consciousness

---

**Francis Crick (1916–2004)**
British molecular biologist and neuroscientist, co-discoverer of DNA's double helix. In his later career, Crick turned to the study of consciousness, arguing that consciousness is a scientific rather than philosophical problem and that progress requires identifying the specific neural mechanisms correlated with experience. His collaboration with Christof Koch launched the NCC program as a research enterprise, and his proposal that 40Hz oscillations in visual cortex are the neural correlate of visual awareness—though later largely abandoned—helped establish the scientific agenda. His book *The Astonishing Hypothesis* (1994) presented the neuroscientific approach to consciousness for general readers.
*Key works:* *The Astonishing Hypothesis* (1994), "Towards a Neurobiological Theory of Consciousness" (1990, with Koch)

---

**Christof Koch (1956–)**
German-American neuroscientist, longtime collaborator of Crick and currently Chief Scientist at the Allen Institute for Brain Science. Koch has been the most sustained advocate of the NCC research program, proposing specific neural systems as candidates for the physical correlates of consciousness and engaging seriously with philosophical theories. He has proposed that the "posterior hot zone"—comprising visual, auditory, and other sensory cortices—rather than prefrontal cortex is the locus of the NCC, a claim that contrasts with global workspace theories. He has also engaged extensively with Integrated Information Theory.
*Key works:* *The Quest for Consciousness* (2004), *Consciousness: Confessions of a Romantic Reductionist* (2012)

---

**Giulio Tononi (1960–)**
Italian-American neuroscientist and psychiatrist at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. Tononi developed Integrated Information Theory (IIT), which proposes a mathematical measure (phi) of consciousness defined as the amount of integrated information a system generates above and beyond its parts. IIT provides a principled account of what the NCC research program should be looking for—not just any neural correlate but the substrate of maximum integrated information. His work has been influential in connecting NCC research to a theory that generates testable predictions about consciousness in unusual systems.
*Key works:* "Consciousness as Integrated Information" (2004), "Consciousness as Phi" (2008)

---

**Nikos Logothetis (1950–)**
Greek neuroscientist at the Max Planck Institute. Logothetis's binocular rivalry research in macaques—identifying neurons in inferior temporal cortex whose firing tracks the animal's reported percept rather than the physical stimulus—represents one of the most methodologically clean approaches to identifying NCCs. His work demonstrated that cells in higher visual areas show rivalry-driven responses, while cells in early visual areas do not, suggesting that NCCs for visual awareness are located at relatively high levels of visual processing.
*Key works:* "Vision: A Window on Consciousness" (1999), "Single Units and Conscious Vision" (1998)

---

**Victor Lamme (1961–)**
Dutch neuroscientist at the University of Amsterdam. Lamme argues that consciousness corresponds to local recurrent processing in the visual cortex—specifically, the re-entrant feedback signals that modulate feedforward visual responses—rather than to global broadcast as in global workspace theories. His proposal that rich visual phenomenology can occur without attentional access or frontal involvement has been influential in debates about the scope of unconscious visual processing and the relationship between attention and consciousness.
*Key works:* "Why Visual Attention and Awareness Are Different" (2003), "Visual Functions Generate Separate Streams toward Front and Back of the Brain" (2006)
