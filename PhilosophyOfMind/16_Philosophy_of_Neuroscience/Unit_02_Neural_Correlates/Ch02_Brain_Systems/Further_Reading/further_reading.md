# Further Reading: Brain Systems

## Primary Sources

**Andy Clark, *Surfing Uncertainty: Prediction, Action, and the Embodied Mind* (2016)** — Clark's synthesis of the predictive processing framework, arguing that the brain is fundamentally a prediction machine that generates top-down predictions of sensory input and updates models when predictions are violated. This framework, which builds on Friston's free energy principle, provides a unified account of perception, action, and cognition that has been widely influential in both neuroscience and philosophy of mind.

**Karl Friston, "The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?" (2010), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*** — Friston's proposal that the brain minimizes "free energy"—a measure of the difference between predicted and actual sensory inputs—as a unified principle governing all neural computation. This principle grounds predictive processing and active inference as the brain's fundamental strategy.

**Marcus Raichle et al., "A Default Mode of Brain Function" (2001), *PNAS*** — The paper that identified the default mode network (DMN)—a set of brain regions that are more active during rest and mind-wandering than during focused task performance. The DMN has subsequently been linked to autobiographical memory, prospection, social cognition, and self-referential processing.

**Elizabeth Bates and Brian MacWhinney, "Functionalism and the Competition Model" (1989)** — An influential account of language processing that emphasizes the role of competition between cue-based strategies across languages, connecting language to broader brain organization.

**Alan Baddeley, *Working Memory, Thought, and Action* (2007)** — A comprehensive treatment of working memory—the system that temporarily holds and manipulates information—and its neural substrates, including the role of the prefrontal cortex in executive control of working memory.

## Secondary Literature

**Michael Graziano, *Rethinking Consciousness: A Scientific Theory of Subjective Experience* (2019)** — Graziano's "attention schema theory" proposes that consciousness is the brain's model of its own attention process—a simplified internal representation of attentional state. His theory connects NCC research to executive brain systems in a novel way.

**Donald Stuss and Robert Knight (eds.), *Principles of Frontal Lobe Function* (2002)** — A comprehensive treatment of the role of prefrontal cortex in working memory, executive function, decision-making, and planning, covering both clinical and experimental evidence.

**Angela Friederici, *Language in Our Brain: The Origins of a Uniquely Human Capacity* (2017)** — A detailed account of the neural basis of language, covering both structural and functional neuroimaging evidence and the development of language networks. Addresses the evolution of language from a comparative neuroscience perspective.

**Roger Buckner, "The Brain's Default Network: Origins and Implications for the Study of Psychosis" (2013), *Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience*** — Reviews the neuroscience of the default mode network and its relationship to consciousness, autobiographical memory, and clinical disorders including depression and schizophrenia.

**Jakob Hohwy, *The Predictive Mind* (2013)** — A philosophical development of the predictive processing framework, examining its implications for perception, agency, attention, and self-models. Complements Clark's treatment with more careful philosophical analysis.
