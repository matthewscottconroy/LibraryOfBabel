# Key Thinkers: AI Consciousness

---

**David Chalmers (1966–)**
Australian philosopher at NYU, known for articulating the hard problem of consciousness. Chalmers has been one of the most philosophically serious thinkers about AI consciousness, engaging with the question of whether AI systems could have genuine phenomenal consciousness rather than dismissing it. His functionalist sympathies lead him to take seriously the possibility that sufficiently well-organized information-processing systems could be conscious, but he argues that current LLMs almost certainly fall short of the required conditions. His engagement with IIT, global workspace theory, and higher-order theories in the context of AI consciousness makes his work the key reference point.
*Key works:* *The Conscious Mind* (1996), "Could a Large Language Model be Conscious?" (2023), *Reality+* (2022)

---

**Giulio Tononi (1960–)**
Italian-American neuroscientist at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. Tononi's Integrated Information Theory (IIT) has significant implications for AI consciousness: the theory holds that consciousness is identical to integrated information (phi), and that conventional computers—with their modular, feed-forward architectures—may have much lower phi than biological brains, even if they are functionally more powerful. IIT thus implies that powerful AI systems could be functionally intelligent while having little or no consciousness, inverting the common assumption that intelligence and consciousness go together.
*Key works:* "Consciousness as Integrated Information" (2004), "Consciousness: Here, There and Everywhere?" (2015, with Koch)

---

**Susan Schneider (1966–)**
American philosopher of mind at the University of Florida. Schneider has engaged extensively with questions about AI consciousness, personal identity in the context of digital uploading, and the nature of mind in computational systems. Her *Artificial You* (2019) examines whether a digital copy of a person would be the same person and whether AI systems could have genuine consciousness, arguing that without a deeper understanding of what makes a system conscious we cannot assume that functional equivalence preserves consciousness.
*Key works:* *The Language of Thought: A New Philosophical Direction* (2011), *Artificial You* (2019)

---

**Eric Schwitzgebel (1968–)**
American philosopher at UC Riverside, known for his work on introspection and the reliability of self-knowledge. Schwitzgebel has argued for genuine uncertainty about AI consciousness, examining what reasons we might have to attribute or deny consciousness to AI systems and finding that our epistemic situation is more uncertain than either confident attributers or confident deniers typically acknowledge. His work on the "moral status of future AI" examines what obligations we might have if we are genuinely uncertain about whether AI systems can suffer.
*Key works:* *Perplexities of Consciousness* (2011), "A Defense of the Rights of Artificial Intelligences" (2015, with Garza)

---

**Murray Shanahan (1960–)**
British AI researcher and philosopher at Imperial College London. Shanahan has developed careful philosophical analyses of machine consciousness and the technological singularity that avoid both the hype of singularity enthusiasts and the dismissiveness of skeptics. His work on how to think about consciousness in AI systems draws on philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and AI research, and his engagement with the question of what AI systems could be—as opposed to what they currently are—has been important for maintaining philosophical rigor in a fast-moving field.
*Key works:* *The Technological Singularity* (2015), *Embodiment and the Inner Life* (2010)
