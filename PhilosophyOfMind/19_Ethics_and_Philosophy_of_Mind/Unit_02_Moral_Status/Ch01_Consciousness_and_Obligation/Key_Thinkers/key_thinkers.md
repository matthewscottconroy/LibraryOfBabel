# Key Thinkers: Consciousness and Moral Obligation

---

**Peter Singer (1946–)**
Australian utilitarian philosopher at Princeton University. Singer has done more than any other contemporary philosopher to connect questions of consciousness to moral obligation. His preference utilitarianism holds that the capacity to have preferences—to be able to be frustrated or satisfied—grounds moral consideration, and that this capacity is connected to sentience and phenomenal consciousness. His arguments against speciesism—treating the interests of one's own species as automatically more important than those of other species—are grounded in the claim that suffering is suffering regardless of species. His work has shaped both philosophical discussion and practical animal ethics.
*Key works:* *Animal Liberation* (1975), *Practical Ethics* (1979/2011), *The Expanding Circle* (1981)

---

**Jeff McMahan (1954–)**
British moral philosopher at Oxford University. McMahan's account of moral status ties it to the capacity for a biographical life—a unified experiential trajectory with past and future—rather than to mere sentience. This means that moral status is graduated: creatures with richer psychological lives have more at stake in surviving and in being treated well. His framework has been applied to questions about fetal development, cognitive disability, animal ethics, and AI, generating a nuanced account of moral status that tracks psychological complexity.
*Key works:* *The Ethics of Killing* (2002), *Killing in War* (2009)

---

**Mary Anne Warren (1946–2010)**
American philosopher at San Francisco State University. Warren's account of personhood identified a cluster of features—consciousness, self-awareness, rationality, self-motivated activity, communicative capacity, and self-concept—whose possession to a significant degree constitutes full personhood. Her gradated account allows for moral consideration of entities that possess some but not all of these features, providing a framework for thinking about fetal development, cognitive disability, and animal status.
*Key works:* "On the Moral and Legal Status of Abortion" (1973), *Moral Status: Obligations to Persons and Other Living Things* (1997)

---

**Christine Korsgaard (1952–)**
American Kantian philosopher at Harvard University. Korsgaard has argued that consciousness grounds moral status because beings who are conscious have a perspective on their own good—they value their lives and experiences from the inside—and that this perspective generates obligations for others. Her account grounds animal moral status in phenomenal consciousness without requiring full rational autonomy, extending Kantian ethics to animals in a way that Kant himself did not.
*Key works:* *Creating the Kingdom of Ends* (1996), *Fellow Creatures* (2018)

---

**Nick Bostrom (1973–)**
Swedish philosopher at Oxford University. Bostrom has examined the moral status of potential post-human and artificial minds, arguing that consciousness—not biological substrate—is the relevant variable. His work raises the question of whether we might be creating morally considerable entities in AI systems and what obligations this would generate. His broader work on existential risk situates questions about consciousness and moral status within a long-term perspective on the future of mind.
*Key works:* *Superintelligence* (2014), "The Ethics of Artificial Intelligence" (2011, with Yudkowsky)
