# Key Thinkers: Brain Interventions

---

**Julian Savulescu (1963–)**
Australian philosopher and bioethicist at Oxford University. Savulescu is one of the most prominent defenders of cognitive enhancement, arguing that we are not only permitted but may be obligated to use available means to improve human cognitive and moral capacities. His "procreative beneficence" principle extends to the enhancement of existing persons, and his engagement with the ethics of memory modification, cognitive enhancement, and moral enhancement has been influential in setting the terms of the debate.
*Key works:* *Human Enhancement* (2009, ed. with Bostrom), "Procreative Beneficence" (2001), "Genetic Enhancement" (various)

---

**Michael Sandel (1953–)**
American political philosopher at Harvard University. Sandel's critique of human enhancement in *The Case Against Perfection* (2007) argues that the drive to perfect and master human traits—including cognitive and emotional capacities—reflects a "Promethean" attitude that fails to acknowledge the giftedness of talent and the importance of accepting what we have not chosen. His concerns about enhancement are not primarily about harm but about virtue and the kind of society we become when we treat all human characteristics as available for optimization.
*Key works:* *The Case Against Perfection* (2007), *Justice: What's the Right Thing to Do?* (2009)

---

**Walter Glannon (1957–)**
Canadian philosopher at the University of Calgary, specializing in neuroethics. Glannon has provided rigorous philosophical analyses of brain interventions including deep brain stimulation, pharmacological mood modification, and memory dampening. His work addresses the authenticity concern—whether brain interventions compromise the expression of one's authentic self—while also examining questions of responsibility, identity, and the relationship between neural states and personal agency. His *Brain, Body, and Mind* (2011) is one of the most comprehensive philosophical treatments of neuroethics.
*Key works:* *Brain, Body, and Mind* (2011), *Bioethics and the Brain* (2006)

---

**Adam Kolber (1970–)**
American legal scholar at Brooklyn Law School. Kolber has examined the legal and ethical implications of memory modification technologies, particularly propranolol-based dampening of traumatic memories. He argues that concerns about authenticity and legal evidence are addressable and do not justify prohibiting voluntary memory modification, and that individuals should generally have a right to modify their own mental states. His work bridges philosophy, law, and empirical neuroscience.
*Key works:* "Therapeutic Forgetting" (2006), "The Therapeutic Ordeals" (2014)

---

**Nick Bostrom (1973–)**
Swedish philosopher at Oxford University. Bostrom's transhumanist position holds that humans should actively develop and use technologies to enhance their cognitive, emotional, and physical capacities beyond what is achievable by nature. His work on enhancement connects to his broader concerns about existential risk and the long-term future of human and post-human intelligence. He has addressed specific questions about the ethics of cognitive enhancement, memory modification, and other brain interventions while defending the general principle that enhancement is permissible and often beneficial.
*Key works:* *Human Enhancement* (2009, ed. with Savulescu), "The Transhumanist FAQ" (various), *Superintelligence* (2014)
