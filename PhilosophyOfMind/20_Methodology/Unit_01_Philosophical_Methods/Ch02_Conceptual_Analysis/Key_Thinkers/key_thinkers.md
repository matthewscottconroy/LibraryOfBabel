# Key Thinkers: Conceptual Analysis

---

**Edmund Gettier (1927–2021)**
American philosopher at the University of Massachusetts Amherst. Gettier's three-page 1963 paper offered decisive counterexamples to the traditional analysis of knowledge as justified true belief, transforming epistemology. His cases—in which an agent has a true, justified belief that is still not knowledge because the justification and the truth are accidentally related—showed that even long-accepted conceptual analyses can be overturned by carefully constructed cases. The paper exemplifies both the power of conceptual analysis as a philosophical method and its vulnerability to refutation.
*Key works:* "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?" (1963)

---

**Frank Jackson (1943–)**
Australian philosopher at ANU and Princeton. Jackson's *From Metaphysics to Ethics* (1998) is the most ambitious contemporary defense of conceptual analysis as the core philosophical method. He argues that philosophy's task is to analyze folk concepts and then determine what satisfies them, using two-dimensional semantics to preserve a rigorous notion of a priori conceptual knowledge without commitment to the analytic-synthetic distinction as Quine criticized it. His account connects conceptual analysis to reference and meaning in a way that responds to Quinean challenges.
*Key works:* "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982), *From Metaphysics to Ethics* (1998)

---

**W.V.O. Quine (1908–2000)**
American analytic philosopher at Harvard University. Quine's critique of the analytic-synthetic distinction challenges the foundations of traditional conceptual analysis by arguing that there is no principled distinction between truths that hold by virtue of meaning and truths that hold by virtue of fact. His holistic, naturalistic epistemology—according to which philosophy and science form a continuum—denies the a priori status of conceptual analysis and supports a naturalized approach to philosophy of mind.
*Key works:* "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" (1951), *Word and Object* (1960), "Epistemology Naturalized" (1969)

---

**Jonathan Weinberg (1972–)**
American experimental philosopher at the University of Arizona. Weinberg's early experimental philosophy research (with Nichols and Stich) showed that epistemic intuitions about Gettier cases vary systematically across cultures, challenging the universality assumptions of traditional conceptual analysis. His work helped establish experimental philosophy as a method for empirically evaluating the reliability of philosophical intuitions, though he has also been careful about what this actually establishes about philosophical methodology.
*Key works:* "Normativity and Epistemic Intuitions" (2001), "Intuitions: Still Not Evidence" (2007)

---

**Herman Cappelen (1968–)**
Norwegian philosopher at the University of Hong Kong and previously Edinburgh. Cappelen's *Philosophy Without Intuitions* (2012) challenged the widespread assumption that philosophy essentially relies on pre-theoretical intuitions, arguing that this claim has been exaggerated. He argues that most philosophical arguments appeal to considered judgments rather than raw intuitions, and that the experimental philosophy challenge to intuitions does not undermine philosophical methodology as radically as its proponents claim. His work represents a careful response to the experimental philosophy critique from within analytic philosophy.
*Key works:* *Philosophy Without Intuitions* (2012), *Fixing Language: An Essay on Conceptual Engineering* (2018)
