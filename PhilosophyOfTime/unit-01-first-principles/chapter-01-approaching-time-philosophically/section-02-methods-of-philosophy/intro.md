# Section 1.2: The Methods of Philosophy Applied to Time

---

## Section Introduction

Philosophy is sometimes described as the discipline that lacks a method. Scientists run experiments, mathematicians prove theorems, historians consult archives. What do philosophers do? The question is not entirely unfair, but the honest answer is that philosophers use a cluster of methods — some shared with other disciplines, some distinctive — and the choice of method matters enormously to the results you get.

In the philosophy of time, four methods are particularly important: *conceptual analysis* (examining what our temporal concepts mean and what they presuppose), *intuition pumps and thought experiments* (using carefully crafted hypothetical scenarios to test and develop theories), *reflective equilibrium* (the iterative process of bringing intuitions and principles into coherent alignment), and *naturalistic and revisionary approaches* (letting science constrain or even revise philosophical conclusions). This section introduces each of these and, crucially, begins to assess their limitations — because in the philosophy of time, the limitations of methods are at least as instructive as their successes.
