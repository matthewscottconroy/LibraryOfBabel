# Section 1.4: A Map of the Terrain

---

## Section Introduction

Every inquiry benefits from a map — not a final chart of fully explored territory, but a rough schematic that shows where the main regions are, how they connect, and which paths lead between them. The philosophy of time is large enough that it is easy to lose one's sense of the whole while working through a particular part. This section provides the orientation.

Four major regions of inquiry constitute the field: the *metaphysics of time* (ontological and structural questions about what time is and how it is structured); the *philosophy of physics of time* (questions about what physical theories imply about the nature and structure of time); the *phenomenology and psychology of time* (questions about how time is experienced and represented); and *time in ethics, identity, and existence* (questions about how temporal structure bears on practical and existential concerns). These regions are not sealed compartments; their questions interact, and some of the most interesting problems sit precisely at their boundaries.
