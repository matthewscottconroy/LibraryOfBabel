# Chapter 2: Important Concepts

---

**Folk Conception of Time**
The implicit, pre-theoretical picture of time that structures ordinary temporal cognition and practice. The folk conception is not a theory anyone explicitly adopts; it is the background framework presupposed by everyday temporal talk, planning, memory, and expectation. Articulating it carefully is a genuine philosophical task.

**Knowledge-How vs. Knowledge-That**
A distinction introduced by Gilbert Ryle. Knowledge-how is practical competence — the ability to do something skillfully. Knowledge-that is propositional knowledge — the ability to state that something is the case. Most ordinary temporal competence is knowledge-how that outstrips the corresponding knowledge-that; this gap is what produces the Augustinian puzzle about time.

**Container View (Absolute Time)**
The view that time is a medium or vessel in which events are located, existing independently of those events. Even if no events occurred, time would still exist and "flow." Isaac Newton's absolute time is the paradigm case: it "flows equably without relation to anything external."

**Relational View**
The view that time consists entirely of the temporal relations among events — simultaneity, succession, duration — and has no existence independent of the events that stand in those relations. Leibniz is the paradigm defender; time, on this view, is a feature of how events relate to each other, not a container over and above them.

**A-Series**
J. M. E. McTaggart's term for the ordering of events by the dynamic, one-place properties of being past, present, or future. A-series properties are not permanent: what is now future will become present, and what is now present will become past. The A-series is associated with temporal flow and the objective present.

**B-Series**
McTaggart's term for the ordering of events by the fixed, permanent, two-place relations of "earlier than," "later than," and "simultaneous with." B-series relations do not change: if the Battle of Thermopylae is earlier than the Battle of Actium, it is always earlier. The B-series is associated with the static, four-dimensional "block universe" picture.

**Grammatical Tense**
The systematic, obligatory encoding of temporal location into the morphological structure of verbs. Tense differs from aspect: tense locates an event in time relative to a reference point (usually the time of utterance), while aspect describes the internal temporal structure of the event (completed, ongoing, habitual, etc.).

**Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis (Linguistic Relativity)**
The hypothesis, associated with Edward Sapir and Benjamin Lee Whorf, that the language one speaks shapes or determines the thoughts one can have. The strong version (language determines thought) has been largely discredited. The weak version (language can influence or facilitate certain ways of thinking) retains some empirical support and remains philosophically relevant.

**Tensed vs. Tenseless Discourse**
The distinction between temporal statements whose truth value changes over time (tensed: "the meeting is happening") and statements whose truth value is permanent (tenseless: "the meeting is earlier than the lecture"). Tensed discourse seems to presuppose an objective present; tenseless discourse does not. This grammatical distinction maps onto the A-theory/B-theory debate.

**Token-Reflexive Analysis**
A semantic analysis of tensed statements, associated with Reichenbach and Mellor, on which "the event is happening now" means approximately "the event is happening at the same time as this utterance token." The analysis eliminates the need for objective A-series presentness; what appeared to be a claim about objective nowness turns out to be a claim about a B-series simultaneity relation.

**Tense Logic (Prior)**
A formal logical system, developed by Arthur Prior, that treats temporal operators — "it was the case that" (P) and "it will be the case that" (F) — analogously to modal operators. Prior's tense logic provides a rigorous semantic framework for tensed discourse and embodies a commitment to the reality of the A-series.

**Indexical Expression**
An expression whose reference shifts systematically with features of the context of utterance. Temporal indexicals include "now," "then," "yesterday," "soon," and "recently." Following David Kaplan, we distinguish an indexical's character (the rule by which it picks out a referent relative to a context) from its content (what it contributes to the proposition expressed in a specific context).

**Cyclical Time**
The conception of time as repeating in great cycles rather than running from a unique beginning to a unique end. Found in Hindu cosmology (kalpas, yugas), Stoic eternal recurrence, and Mesoamerican calendar systems. Raises philosophical challenges about personal identity across recurrence, the coherence of historical uniqueness, and the problem of temporal origins.

**Creatio Ex Nihilo**
The theological doctrine that God created the universe out of nothing, implying that time itself has a beginning. Central to the Abrahamic religious traditions, this doctrine raises philosophical questions about what "before" the creation means, about the structure of eternity, and about divine foreknowledge versus temporal openness.

**Dreamtime (Altyerre)**
Among Aboriginal Australian peoples, the founding creative time of ancestral beings — not simply past but continuously present and active in the land, ritual, and community. Philosophically, the Dreaming represents a temporal ontology in which founding time underlies rather than precedes the present, challenging Western linear models.

**Homogeneous Time**
The conception of time as a uniform, quantitative medium in which all units are intrinsically equal and in which no moment has qualitative significance in itself. This conception was made practically salient by the mechanical clock. Contrasted with qualitative or lived time, in which temporal units have intrinsic differences in significance, texture, and meaning.

**Sidereal vs. Solar Time**
Two natural measures of time: sidereal time is measured relative to the fixed stars (Earth's rotation with respect to inertial space); solar time is measured relative to the sun (Earth's rotation with respect to the sun). They differ by approximately four minutes per day due to Earth's orbital motion. The existence of multiple natural measures of time raises philosophical questions about which (if any) reflects "real" time.

**Distentio Animi (Augustine)**
Augustine's term for the "distension of the mind" — the stretched, extended character of conscious temporal experience. Past, present, and future are not external realities but modes of attention: memory, attention, and anticipation, respectively. Augustine's account is an early and sophisticated phenomenological analysis of time consciousness.
