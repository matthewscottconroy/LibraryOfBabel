# Chapter 4: Important Concepts

---

**Timaeus**
Plato's cosmological dialogue (ca. 360 BCE), in which the character Timaeus delivers an account of the creation of the universe. The *Timaeus* contains Plato's most systematic treatment of time, the Demiurge, the Forms, and the Receptacle. It was one of the most widely read and influential philosophical texts of antiquity and the early medieval period.

**Demiurge**
The divine craftsman of the *Timaeus* (*dêmiourgos* — artisan). He creates the cosmos by imposing mathematical order on pre-existing material (the Receptacle), using the eternal Living Being as his model. He is motivated by goodness and lack of envy: he wants everything to be as good as possible. He is not an omnipotent creator ex nihilo but a craftsman working within constraints.

**Living Being (Intelligible Model)**
The eternal Form that serves as the Demiurge's model for creating the cosmos. The Living Being is the most comprehensive of the Forms — an eternal, intelligible totality encompassing all the formal principles of living things. The cosmos is made as a temporal likeness of this eternal model.

**Time as a Moving Image of Eternity**
Plato's definition at *Timaeus* 37d: "a moving image of eternity, proceeding according to number." Time is an *image* (*eikôn*) of eternity — it genuinely represents eternity but imperfectly, through cyclical motion that approximates what eternity holds all at once. The motions of the celestial bodies are the specific moving images that constitute time.

**Aion (Eternity)**
The Greek term Plato uses for the eternal model's mode of existence — not endless temporal duration but timeless being, to which the predicates "was" and "will be" do not apply. Only "is" correctly applies to eternal being. Eternity is *all-at-once* (*tota simul*), not spread across temporal succession.

**Chronos (Time)**
The Greek term for ordinary, measurable time — the time of the clock and calendar, in which events have dates, durations, and positions relative to each other. In the *Timaeus*, *chronos* is the created moving image of *aion*, produced when the Demiurge orders the cosmos by the celestial motions.

**Being vs. Becoming**
Plato's fundamental ontological distinction. *Being* (*ousia*) is the eternal, unchanging realm of the Forms: stable, necessary, fully real, the object of genuine knowledge. *Becoming* (*genesis*) is the temporal, changing realm of perceptible things: unstable, contingent, imperfectly real, the object of belief or opinion. Time is the medium of becoming; eternity is the mode of being.

**Forms (Ideas)**
The eternal, unchanging, intelligible objects of genuine Platonic knowledge. Forms are not located in time or space; they are not sensible objects; they are not particular things. The Form of Beauty is beautiful, necessarily, unconditionally, unchangingly. Particular temporal things are beautiful insofar as they participate in the Form of Beauty, but they are beautiful only imperfectly, contingently, and temporarily.

**Receptacle (Chora / Hypodoche)**
The "third kind" (*triton genos*) in the *Timaeus* — alongside Being and Becoming. The Receptacle is the characterless, all-receiving medium that underlies all temporal change. It has no positive properties of its own but receives the impressions of the Forms that appear in the temporal world. It is known "by a kind of bastard reasoning" rather than by reason or perception.

**Participation**
The relation between temporal things and the Forms: a temporal thing *participates in* (or *partakes of*) a Form insofar as it instantiates or reflects the Form's character. A beautiful face participates in the Form of Beauty; a just action participates in the Form of Justice. The participation relation is philosophically puzzling (it generates the Third Man regress) but central to Plato's metaphysics.

**Divided Line**
Plato's image in the *Republic* (509d–511e) of an epistemological and ontological hierarchy: images, perceptible objects, mathematical objects, and Forms, corresponding to four cognitive states: conjecture (*eikasia*), perception-based belief (*pistis*), hypothetical reasoning (*dianoia*), and intellectual insight (*noêsis*). Temporal things occupy the middle levels, beneath the eternal Forms.

**Episteme vs. Doxa**
Knowledge (*episteme*) versus opinion or belief (*doxa*). For Plato, genuine knowledge is of the unchanging eternal Forms; it is necessarily true, certain, and rationally justified. Opinion is of the changing temporal world; it can be accurate and justified but is fallible because its objects change. Natural science, including cosmology, produces *doxa*, not *episteme*, in the strict sense.

**Anamnesis (Recollection)**
The doctrine, developed in the *Meno* and *Phaedo*, that learning is the recovery of knowledge the soul already possesses from its pre-embodied existence with the Forms. Temporal experience — encountering beautiful, equal, or just things — triggers the soul's recollection of the corresponding Forms. Recollection is not ordinary temporal memory of past events but the recovery of atemporal knowledge.

**Likely Story (Eikos Logos)**
Plato's term for the cosmological account in the *Timaeus* (*eikôs logos* or *eikôs mythos*): a probable, plausible account rather than a strict demonstration. Because the subject matter (the temporal, material cosmos) is in the domain of becoming rather than being, the best we can achieve is a probable account. This epistemic humility is part of the philosophical content.

**Third Man Argument**
An objection to Platonic participation, raised by Plato himself in the *Parmenides* (132a–b) and by Aristotle (*Metaphysics* 990b17–991a8). If a beautiful thing is beautiful because it participates in the Form of Beauty, what makes the Form of Beauty itself beautiful? Another Form? Then a third Form is needed to explain what the first Form and the original beautiful thing have in common — and so on, in an infinite regress.

**Tota Simul (Boethius)**
Boethius's Latin formulation of divine eternity in the *Consolation of Philosophy* V.6: *interminabilis vitae tota simul et perfecta possessio* — "the whole, simultaneous, and perfect possession of an unlimited life." This formulation captures the Platonic idea that eternity is not endless temporal duration but an all-at-once possession of complete being, without before or after. It became the standard definition of eternity in medieval Christian theology.

**World Soul**
The intelligent animating principle of the cosmos in the *Timaeus* (34b–37c), constructed by the Demiurge from mathematical ratios. The World Soul animates and governs the cosmos; it produces the celestial motions that constitute time; and it serves as the cosmic counterpart to the individual soul that inhabits each living body.
