# Section 3: The Receptacle and Becoming

---

## Section Introduction

The cosmogony of the *Timaeus* involves not two but three fundamental principles: Being, Becoming, and a third that is philosophically the most puzzling of all — the *Receptacle* (*chora*, sometimes *hupodochê*, "that which receives"). Plato introduces the Receptacle in *Timaeus* 48e–52d, in a second creation account that supplements the first by introducing the material dimension of the cosmos.

The introduction is striking. Plato says that when the first account was given (of the Demiurge, the model, and the cosmos), he was "omitting" the Receptacle — a "third kind" (*triton genos*) alongside Being and Becoming. Now he must address it, even though it is difficult to talk about and resistant to ordinary forms of predication.

The Receptacle is the "nurse of becoming" (*Timaeus* 49a): it is the medium in which the images of the Forms appear, the material substrate that receives impressions from the eternal model. It is not itself a Form; it is not any particular kind of thing; it is the precondition of all temporal, material existence.

Understanding the Receptacle is important for the philosophy of time because it is the *material* dimension of temporal existence — the side of the temporal world that is not explained by its participation in Forms but that provides the *where* and the *when* of temporal things: their location in space and time.

This section has a single subsection examining the Receptacle in detail.
