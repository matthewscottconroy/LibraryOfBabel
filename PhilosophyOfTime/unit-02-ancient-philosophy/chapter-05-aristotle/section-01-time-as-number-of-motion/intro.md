# Section 1: Time as the Number of Motion

---

## Section Introduction

Aristotle's *Physics* is his systematic account of natural change: what motion is, what causes it, how space and time structure it. The investigation of time occurs within this broader investigation, and it cannot be understood in isolation from Aristotle's general framework.

This section sets up and explains the definition of time that is the center of Aristotle's account. The first subsection provides the context: what Aristotle is doing in *Physics* IV.10–14, and why he approaches time through the study of motion. The second subsection unpacks the definition itself, examining what "number," "motion," and "before and after" mean in Aristotle's framework. The third subsection addresses an important negative claim: Aristotle's argument that time cannot simply be identified with motion.

Three terms need preliminary clarification. *Physis* (the Greek word the *Physics* takes its title from) means "nature" — the principle of motion and rest that living things and natural objects possess in themselves. *Kinesis* means "motion" or "change" — Aristotle uses it broadly for any kind of change: locomotion (change of place), qualitative change (change in quality), quantitative change (change in quantity), and substantial change (coming to be or passing away). *Hupokeimenon* means "substrate" or "underlying subject" — the thing that undergoes change and persists through it.

These three terms are central to Aristotle's account of time, and keeping them in view will make the account easier to follow.
