# Section 2: The Now

---

## Section Introduction

The concept of the Now (*to nun*) plays a central and somewhat puzzling role in Aristotle's account of time. On the one hand, the Now is indispensable: it is what makes time a unified whole rather than a disconnected heap of intervals. On the other hand, the Now raises some of the deepest puzzles in the philosophy of time: Is the Now always the same one, or always a different one? Is it a part of time, or a boundary? Does it make sense to say that the Now "moves"?

These questions are not merely technical. They bear on the relationship between time and temporal consciousness, between the mathematical structure of the time line and the phenomenological experience of a present moment. Aristotle addresses them in *Physics* 219b9–220a24, and his analysis — while dense — contains genuine philosophical insights.

This section has two subsections. The first examines the Now as a boundary: its role in unifying time and marking the distinction between past and future. The second examines the puzzle of whether the Now is always the same or always different, and assesses Aristotle's solution.
