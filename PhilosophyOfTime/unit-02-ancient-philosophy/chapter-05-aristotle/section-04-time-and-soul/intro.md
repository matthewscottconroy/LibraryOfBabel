# Section 4: Time and the Soul

---

## Section Introduction

Among the most discussed and most puzzling passages in Aristotle's account of time is a brief section in *Physics* IV.14 (223a16–29) where Aristotle raises the question: would there be time if there were no soul?

The question might sound like a contemporary debate about mind-dependence — does time exist independently of minds? But in Aristotle's context, the question is more specific and more philosophically precise. Aristotle has defined time as the number of motion. Numbers, for Aristotle, require a numbering subject — an intellect or soul that counts. If there were no soul to count, would there be any numbers, and therefore any time?

This question connects Aristotle's philosophy of time to his philosophy of mind, to the Platonic tradition's interest in the relationship between soul and time, and to the modern debate about whether time is mind-independent. The passage is brief, somewhat hedged, and admits of multiple interpretations — which is part of what makes it philosophically fertile.
