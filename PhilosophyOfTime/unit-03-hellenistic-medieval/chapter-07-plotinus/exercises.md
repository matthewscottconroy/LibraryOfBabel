# Chapter 7: Exercises

## Comprehension and Analysis

**Exercise 7.1**
Explain the three Plotinian hypostases: the One, Intellect, and Soul. What is the relationship between them? How does each hypostasis relate to time and eternity?

**Exercise 7.2**
Plotinus defines eternity (*aion*) as the "complete, simultaneous, and full life" of Intellect. Explain what each part of this definition means. How does this differ from the concept of sempiternity (endless time)?

**Exercise 7.3**
Plotinus says that "time is the life of the Soul in a movement of passage from one way of life to another" (*Enneads* III.7.11). What does this mean? In what sense is time the "life" of Soul rather than a container in which Soul lives?

**Exercise 7.4**
Explain Plotinus's critique of Aristotle's definition of time as "the number of motion with respect to before and after." What is the regress problem that Plotinus identifies? Is this criticism successful?

**Exercise 7.5**
Compare Plotinus's account of time with the Stoic account (from Chapter 6). Identify at least three significant differences. Which account do you find more philosophically defensible, and why?

**Exercise 7.6**
Does Plotinus's account make time "merely subjective"? Is the cosmic Soul a sufficiently objective foundation for time's reality? Write a 400-word response defending or challenging the view that Plotinus's account avoids problematic subjectivism.

**Exercise 7.7**
How does Plato's account of time in the *Timaeus* (time as the "moving image of eternity") relate to Plotinus's account? In what ways does Plotinus transform the Platonic material rather than simply repeating it?

**Exercise 7.8**
Plotinus connects his account of time to a critique of motion-based accounts: motion cannot number itself; something outside motion must do the measuring. Formulate a response that an Aristotelian might give to this critique. Then evaluate whether Plotinus's position can accommodate this response.

## Observational and Experimental Exercises

**Lab Exercise 7.A — The Simultaneous vs. Sequential Grasp**

Choose a piece of music you know well — something you can sing or recall mentally. Try two different ways of "having" the piece in mind. First, recall it sequentially: sing or hum through it from beginning to end, experiencing each section as it comes. Then try to hold the entire piece in mind *simultaneously* — not as a sequence but as a single unified whole. (You might try imagining seeing the musical score all at once, or thinking of the piece "as a whole" rather than "as a sequence.") Which is easier? What does the difference between these two modes of attention tell you about Plotinus's contrast between the sequential life of Soul and the simultaneous grasp of Intellect? Write a brief reflection.

**Lab Exercise 7.B — Absorption and Time-Loss**

Identify an activity that regularly causes you to "lose track of time" — deep reading, creative work, exercise, conversation. After your next such experience, note: how long did the absorbed state last? How different was your felt sense of duration from the clock time? Plotinus says that when the soul is most engaged with Intellect, it approaches atemporal existence. Does your experience of absorption provide any phenomenological support for the idea of degrees of temporal engagement? What are the limits of using such experiences as evidence for Plotinus's account?

**Lab Exercise 7.C — Drawing the Hierarchy**

Create a visual diagram of Plotinus's three hypostases, showing: (1) the relationship between them (procession from above, return from below); (2) the temporal characteristics of each level (beyond time / atemporal eternity / temporal succession); (3) where individual human experience fits in the diagram. Write a brief explanatory caption for each element of the diagram. Then discuss: does the diagram help or distort understanding of Plotinus's account?

## Essay Questions

**Exercise 7.9 (Essay)**
Plotinus says that time is generated by Soul; Aristotle says that time is measured by soul. Are these two different positions or are they closer than they appear? Write an essay of 800–1000 words comparing the two accounts and assessing which is more defensible.

**Exercise 7.10 (Essay)**
Plotinus anticipates what later became the central question in the phenomenology of time: is time a feature of the world that consciousness measures, or a feature of consciousness that is projected onto the world? Write an essay of 1000 words tracing this question from Plotinus through Augustine (previewing Chapter 8 if you have read ahead) and assessing whether phenomenological or realist accounts of time are more plausible.

**Exercise 7.11 (Essay)**
Is the concept of a "timeless" entity coherent? Plotinus holds that the One and Intellect are outside time entirely. But contemporary philosophers often argue that the concept of a timeless entity faces serious problems (how can a timeless entity act in time? know temporal truths?). Write an essay of 800–1000 words assessing whether Plotinus's atemporal hypostases are philosophically defensible.
