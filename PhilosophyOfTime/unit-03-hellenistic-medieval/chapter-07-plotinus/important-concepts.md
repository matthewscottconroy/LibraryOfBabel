# Chapter 7: Important Concepts

---

**Hypostasis** (*hypostasis*)
In Plotinus's system, a "hypostasis" is a fundamental level of reality — an ontological stratum that has its own mode of being and from which other levels proceed by emanation. Plotinus recognizes three hypostases: the One, Intellect (*Nous*), and Soul (*Psychê*). The three hypostases form a strict hierarchy of ontological dignity and dependence.

**The One** (*to Hen*)
The ultimate principle in Plotinus's ontology: absolutely simple, undifferentiated, beyond being and thought. The One is not a being among beings; it is the source of all being, from which Intellect proceeds by emanation. The One is beyond time in the most radical sense: it is not even the right kind of thing to be in or outside time; temporal categories do not apply to it at all.

**Intellect** (*Nous*)
The first emanation from the One; the level at which being and thought coincide. Intellect is pure self-thinking thought — it knows the Platonic Forms and knows itself in knowing them. Intellect's mode of being is eternal (*aion*): its life is complete, simultaneous, and without succession. It grasps all things at once rather than moving through them sequentially.

**Soul** (*Psychê*)
The second emanation from Intellect; the principle that animates and orders the physical world. Soul's mode of being is temporal: it cannot hold all its knowledge and activity in a single act but must move through them discursively, one after another. This sequential, restless character of Soul's activity is the source of time.

**Eternity** (*Aion*)
Plotinus's technical term for the mode of being of Intellect: the complete, simultaneous, and full life that lacks all temporal succession or division. Eternity is not endless time but a radically different mode of being: one in which everything is present at once, without any "not yet" or "no longer." This concept differs from Platonic eternity (which emphasizes unchangingness) by adding the emphasis on simultaneous completeness.

**Sempiternity**
Endless temporal duration — existing at all times, without beginning or end. Sempiternity is a property of things that are *in* time but that persist through all of it. It should not be confused with Plotinian eternity (*aion*), which is not a temporal mode of being at all. A sempiternal being endures; an eternal being does not endure because it is not in time.

**Procession** (*Prohodos*)
The Plotinian term for the emanation of a lower hypostasis from a higher one. Procession is not deliberate creation; it is the natural "overflow" of a higher principle's being into a lower mode. The One overflows into Intellect; Intellect overflows into Soul; Soul overflows into the physical world. Procession is accompanied by "return" (*epistrophê*): each lower level aspires to return to the level above it.

**Emanation**
The process by which each lower level of Plotinus's ontology proceeds from the one above it. Unlike creation in Christian theology (where God creates by a free act of will), Plotinian emanation is a necessary, natural overflow analogous to the way light radiates from a luminous source. The higher level does not diminish in giving rise to the lower; it remains what it is while the lower level comes into being.

**Time as Life of Soul**
Plotinus's central definition from *Enneads* III.7.11: time is the life of Soul in its movement of passage from one way of life to another. Time is not a container in which Soul lives; it is the form of Soul's activity — the sequential, discursive mode in which Soul actualizes what Intellect grasps all at once. This makes time real (it is a genuine feature of cosmic Soul) but not independent of mind (it requires Soul as its source).

**Discursive Activity** (*Dianoia*)
The mode of thought that proceeds step by step, from one consideration to another — the kind of reasoning that moves through premises to conclusions rather than grasping everything at once. Soul is discursive in this sense: it cannot hold all its knowledge and activity in a single act. The discursive character of Soul is what makes it temporal, as opposed to Intellect's intuitive, simultaneous grasp.

**Moving Image of Eternity**
Plato's phrase from the *Timaeus* (37d) for time: time is a "moving image of eternity" created by the Demiurge to make the cosmos as perfect as possible. Plotinus appropriates and transforms this phrase: for him, time is an image of eternity not because it was made by a craftsman but because it is the temporal expression of an eternal rational order. The "moving" is Soul's discursive movement, not physical motion.

**The *Nunc Stans* (Standing Now)**
A concept anticipated in Plotinus and developed in Augustine and medieval theology: the divine or eternal "now" that does not flow or pass but is simultaneously complete. In Plotinus, Intellect's life is analogous to a *nunc stans*; in Augustine, it becomes the model for divine eternity as contrasted with creaturely temporal experience.

**Regress Problem in Temporal Measurement**
Plotinus's argument against Aristotle: if time is the measure of motion, what does the measuring? Another motion? And what measures that? The regress cannot be stopped within a motion-based account of time; it requires a non-motion standard, which Plotinus identifies as Soul. This argument shows that any definition of time in terms of motion must appeal to something that is not itself motion — namely, a mind capable of establishing temporal standards.

**Temporal Dependence on Mind**
The thesis, held in different forms by Plotinus and Augustine, that time is not an independent feature of a mindless physical world but requires something like mind (Soul, for Plotinus; individual consciousness, for Augustine) as its source or ground. This is distinct from the claim that time is merely illusory; it is the claim that time's reality is grounded in mental or quasi-mental activity.

**Return** (*Epistrophê*)
The complementary movement to procession: each hypostasis in Plotinus's system not only proceeds from the one above it but aspires to return to that higher level. Soul aspires to return to Intellect; Intellect aspires to return to the One. This aspiration is what motivates the discursive activity of Soul — it is trying to recapture Intellect's simultaneous completeness — and it thus provides the dynamic that generates time.
