# Chapter 7: Important Researchers

---

**Plotinus** (204–270 CE)
Neoplatonist philosopher, born in Egypt, educated in Alexandria, and eventually teaching in Rome. Plotinus is the central figure of what later came to be called Neoplatonism — the tradition that developed a systematic metaphysics from Platonic sources. His *Enneads*, compiled and edited by his student Porphyry, cover metaphysics, cosmology, ethics, and aesthetics. Treatise III.7, "On Eternity and Time," is his primary contribution to the philosophy of time and one of the greatest treatments of the subject in the ancient world.

**Porphyry** (c. 234–305 CE)
Philosopher, biographer, and editor of Plotinus's *Enneads*. Porphyry studied with Plotinus in Rome and after Plotinus's death edited his fifty-four treatises into six groups of nine (hence "Enneads," from the Greek for "nine"). His editorial decisions — including the order and arrangement of the treatises — have significantly shaped how Plotinus is read. Porphyry also wrote an important *Life of Plotinus*, which is our best biographical source.

**A. H. Armstrong** (1909–1997)
British philosopher and classicist, whose seven-volume Loeb Classical Library translation of the *Enneads* (1966–1988) remains the standard English translation and is the one cited throughout this chapter. Armstrong's introductions to individual treatises are scholarly and philosophically helpful. His book *The Architecture of the Intelligible Universe in the Philosophy of Plotinus* (1940) is an essential secondary source.

**Richard Sorabji** (b. 1934)
British philosopher and historian of ancient philosophy. His *Time, Creation and the Continuum* (1983) devotes extensive attention to Plotinus's account of time, situating it within the broader ancient and medieval discussions. Sorabji is unusually good at identifying the precise philosophical problems that Plotinus is addressing and at tracing the reception of his arguments in later thinkers.

**Eyjólfur K. Emilsson** (b. 1954)
Icelandic philosopher at the University of Oslo, one of the leading contemporary interpreters of Plotinus. His book *Plotinus* (2017) in the Routledge Philosophers series is the most accessible and philosophically rigorous introduction to Plotinus's thought currently available. His earlier work on Plotinus's epistemology (*Plotinus on Sense-Perception*, 1988) is also relevant for understanding the relationship between Soul and temporal awareness.

**Lloyd P. Gerson** (b. 1944)
Canadian philosopher and historian of philosophy. His *Plotinus* (1994) in the Routledge Arguments of the Philosophers series provides a systematic exposition of Plotinus's metaphysics and its relationship to earlier Greek philosophy. Gerson has also edited *The Cambridge Companion to Plotinus* (1996), which contains valuable essays on specific aspects of Plotinus's thought.

**Cristina D'Ancona** (b. 1956)
Italian philosopher and classicist, Professore Ordinaria at the University of Pisa. D'Ancona has done important work on the transmission of Plotinus and Neoplatonism into the Arabic philosophical tradition, which is directly relevant to understanding how Plotinian themes about time and eternity reached Islamic and Jewish medieval philosophy. Her edited volume *The Libraries of the Neoplatonists* (2007) is a key resource.

**Kevin Corrigan**
Canadian philosopher and classicist. His book *Reading Plotinus: A Practical Introduction to Neoplatonism* (2004) is among the most student-friendly introductions to Plotinus available. Corrigan combines careful textual scholarship with genuine philosophical engagement, making complex Plotinian arguments accessible to readers without a background in ancient philosophy.
