# Section 7.1: The Three Hypostases

---

## Section Introduction

Every account of time is embedded in a broader metaphysical vision of what reality is and how it is structured. For Plotinus, the relevant metaphysical vision is a tiered ontology of three fundamental levels — the One, Intellect, and Soul — each of which "proceeds" from the level above it and each of which has a distinctive relationship to time.

The word "hypostasis" (*hypostasis*) means something like "underlying reality" or "what stands beneath" — a substrate of being. Plotinus uses it for each of the three fundamental levels of his ontology. What is important is that the three hypostases are not equal in reality or in dignity: they form a strict hierarchy, with the One at the apex as the source of all being, Intellect as its first emanation, and Soul as the emanation from Intellect. Each level proceeds from the one above it by a kind of overflow or emanation, not through deliberate creation but through a metaphysical necessity analogous to the way light proceeds from a luminous source.

This hierarchical structure is the key to understanding time: time belongs to the lowest of the three hypostases, Soul, and not to the two higher ones. Understanding why the One and Intellect are outside time — and precisely what "outside time" means in each case — requires understanding what they are. That is the work of Section 7.1.
