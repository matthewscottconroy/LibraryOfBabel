# Section 7.2: Time as the Life of Soul

---

## Section Introduction

We have seen that for Plotinus, eternity is the mode of being of Intellect: complete, simultaneous, without succession. Time, by contrast, is the mode of being of Soul. But this is a strange claim. We are accustomed to thinking of time as a feature of the physical world — something measured by clocks, something that flows regardless of whether any mind is attending to it. Plotinus is saying that time is, in some sense, a product of Soul's activity — that Soul generates time rather than merely being in it.

This claim requires very careful handling. It does not mean that time is merely subjective — a hallucination or a convenient fiction. It means that time has the kind of reality that belongs to Soul's activity: genuine, structured, ordered, but dependent on Soul in the way that a performance depends on a performer. The physical world is temporal because Soul animates it, and Soul's temporal character derives from its own nature as a discursive, sequential mode of intellection.

Section 7.2.1 examines the central passage in *Enneads* III.7 where Plotinus articulates this view. Section 7.2.2 explores its implications, including the comparison with Augustine's later *distentio animi* and the question of whether Plotinus's account makes time subjective in a troubling sense.
