# Section 7.3: Plotinus's Critique of Earlier Accounts

---

## Section Introduction

Plotinus's positive account of time as the life of Soul emerges from a sustained critique of his predecessors. *Enneads* III.7 begins not with Plotinus's own position but with a survey and criticism of earlier views: those who identified time with motion, with the measure of motion, with the sphere of heaven, and — most importantly — those in the Aristotelian tradition who defined time as the number of motion. Plotinus's critique of Aristotle is philosophically important in its own right and illuminates his own positive view.

The central critique is that defining time through motion puts the explanatory relationship backwards. Motion is temporal; but that means motion's temporal character is what needs explaining, not what does the explaining. Any definition of time in terms of motion takes for granted what it should be explaining: namely, what makes motion temporal in the first place. For Plotinus, the answer is that motion is temporal because it is an expression of Soul's activity, and Soul's activity is temporal because of Soul's nature. You cannot explain time by appealing to motion; you must explain time's presence in motion by appealing to what underlies motion — and that is Soul.
