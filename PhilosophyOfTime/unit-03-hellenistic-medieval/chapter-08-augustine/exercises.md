# Chapter 8: Exercises

## Comprehension and Analysis

**Exercise 8.1**
Augustine argues that the question "what was God doing before He created the world?" contains a false presupposition. What is the presupposition, and why is it false? Is Augustine's response a philosophical argument or merely a theological dodge? Defend your answer.

**Exercise 8.2**
Explain the concept of the *nunc stans* — the standing now — as Augustine uses it to characterize God's eternity. How does this differ from the concept of sempiternity (endless time)? Can the concept of a genuinely timeless existence be made coherent?

**Exercise 8.3**
Augustine identifies three modes of the present: *memoria* (memory), *contuitus* (attention), and *expectatio* (expectation). Explain what he means by each. Why does he say there are not three times but three aspects of the present? Do you find this analysis convincing?

**Exercise 8.4**
Augustine argues that what we measure when we measure time is the "impression" (*affectio*) left in the mind by passing events. Explain this claim carefully. Does it make temporal measurement merely subjective? What would Augustine say to someone who insists that we measure objective durations, not mental impressions?

**Exercise 8.5**
Explain the concept of *distentio animi*. What is the soul "stretched" between? How does this concept solve the problem of how we can experience duration when the strict present is durationless?

**Exercise 8.6**
Compare Augustine's solution to the problem of the present (via *distentio animi*) with Chrysippus's solution (via the strict present as a durationless limit). What does each thinker preserve and what does each sacrifice? Which do you find more philosophically adequate?

**Exercise 8.7**
Husserl acknowledged Augustine as a predecessor of his own analysis of internal time-consciousness. Identify two specific points of structural similarity between Augustine's account and what you know of Husserl's "retention" and "protention." Does Husserl's more technical treatment advance Augustine's analysis, or does it merely restate it in different vocabulary?

**Exercise 8.8**
Augustine says that to ask what time is is, in some sense, to ask about the soul. Is this a form of idealism about time? What distinguishes Augustine's view from the view that time is merely a product of individual human minds?

## Observational and Experimental Exercises

**Lab Exercise 8.A — Reciting and Analyzing**

Choose a short poem or song that you know by heart (at least 8 lines). Before beginning, notice: can you hold the whole thing "in mind" at once? Now recite it slowly, attending to your experience as you do. At each moment, notice: what is in expectation (you know what is coming), what is in attention (what you are currently saying), and what is in memory (what you have just said). After finishing, write a brief account of how memory, attention, and expectation operated during the recitation. Does your experience support or complicate Augustine's account?

**Lab Exercise 8.B — Time Estimation Without a Clock**

Choose three activities of different lengths (for example: making a cup of tea, reading five pages, writing a paragraph) and try to estimate how long each took *without looking at a clock*. Then check your estimates. What was the source of your time estimate? Was it something external (the motion of the sun, ambient noise) or internal (a sense of how much you had done, felt pace of activity)? Reflect: does your experience support Augustine's claim that temporal measurement is ultimately measurement of inner impressions rather than outer events?

**Lab Exercise 8.C — Varying the Felt Duration**

Deliberately engage in two activities of equal clock duration (say, 15 minutes each): one that is highly engaging and absorbing, one that is tedious and boring. After each, estimate how long the activity lasted. Compare your estimates. What do these findings suggest about the relationship between objective time and experienced duration? Does the variability of felt duration support or challenge Augustine's claim that we measure the soul's impressions rather than objective temporal intervals?

## Essay Questions

**Exercise 8.9 (Essay)**
Augustine argues that "there was no 'before' creation" because time itself was created. Compare this position with what contemporary physicists say about time and the Big Bang. Do they support, refute, or simply fail to address Augustine's philosophical claim? Write an essay of 800–1000 words.

**Exercise 8.10 (Essay)**
Is Augustine's account of time a form of idealism? Compare his position with Kant's (which you will study in Chapter 12) on whether time is a form of inner sense rather than an objective feature of the mind-independent world. Write an essay of 900–1100 words examining the similarities and differences.

**Exercise 8.11 (Essay)**
Ricoeur argues that the aporia of time that Augustine identifies is "resolved" not by philosophy but by narrative — by the human activity of telling stories about our experience. Write an essay of 1000–1200 words explaining Ricoeur's argument and assessing whether narrative is a satisfying response to the philosophical puzzles Augustine raises, or whether it sidesteps rather than resolves them.

**Exercise 8.12 (Essay)**
Augustine says: "The mind stretches out and takes note and says: 'How long was that sound? It lasted as long as this'" (*Confessions* XI.27.36). Write a response, from the perspective of a physicist, explaining what temporal measurement *actually* involves — what is really being measured when we time an event. Then write Augustine's reply to the physicist. Finally, offer your own assessment of where the truth lies.
