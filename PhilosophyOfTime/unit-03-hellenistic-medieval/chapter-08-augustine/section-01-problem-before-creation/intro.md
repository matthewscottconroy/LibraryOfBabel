# Section 8.1: The Problem Before Creation

---

## Section Introduction

The question that launches Augustine's inquiry into time is deceptively simple: "What was God doing before He made heaven and earth?" (*Confessions* XI.10.12, trans. Chadwick 1991: 228). Most of us have asked some version of this question, or heard it asked — usually with a comic edge: "He was preparing Hell for people who ask questions like that." Augustine knows the joke. He quotes it and dismisses it: not because the answer is offensive but because it is philosophically shallow. The real answer to the question is not a comic evasion but a metaphysical insight: there was no "before," because time itself was created.

This is Augustine's philosophical thesis, and it is a genuine contribution to metaphysics, not merely a theological convenience. Section 8.1 examines: the argument that time was created along with the world; what it means for God's eternity to be a *nunc stans* (a standing, non-flowing now); and how this contrast between God's eternal present and human temporal experience sets the stage for the rest of Book XI.
