# Section 8.3: *Distentio Animi* — The Stretching of the Soul

---

## Section Introduction

Augustine's positive account of time reaches its climax in the concept of *distentio animi* — the distension or stretching of the soul. This is not a casual metaphor; it is a precisely chosen image for what Augustine takes to be the fundamental structure of temporal existence. The soul is "stretched" between memory and expectation, with attention to the present as the active, connecting element in the middle. This stretching is not a problem to be solved; it is what temporal existence is.

Understanding *distentio animi* requires holding together two things that Augustine insists on simultaneously: first, that temporal experience is genuinely tripartite — involving past, present, and future as real dimensions of experience; second, that these three dimensions are all given in and through the present moment, through memory, attention, and expectation. The soul is not in three places at once; it is stretched in three directions at once, and this stretching constitutes the temporal character of its life.

Section 8.3.1 examines the concept of *distentio animi* in detail. Section 8.3.2 compares Augustine's account with Aristotle's and asks which more adequately captures the nature of time.
