# Section 8.4: The Problem of the Present

---

## Section Introduction

One of the most striking features of Augustine's inquiry in *Confessions* XI is that he does not stop pressing the argument when it becomes uncomfortable. Having established the threefold present and the *distentio animi*, he continues to push on the concept of the present itself — and what he finds is deeply puzzling. Even the present, which he has been treating as the one genuine temporal dimension, dissolves under close analysis.

This section follows Augustine's argument in XI.15.20 and XI.20.26 as he applies the same divisive analysis that Chrysippus applied in antiquity: if the present is any positive duration, it contains parts that are already past and parts that are not yet present. The smallest possible present — a year, a month, a day, an hour, a moment — can always be further divided. What is the "present" moment, strictly speaking? It has no parts, no duration — and yet it is the only one of the three times that "exists." The puzzle is not resolved; it is acknowledged as a genuine depth of the problem.
