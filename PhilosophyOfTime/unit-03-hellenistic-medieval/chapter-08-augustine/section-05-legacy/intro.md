# Section 8.5: Augustine's Legacy

---

## Section Introduction

Few philosophical texts have had the influence on subsequent thought that Book XI of the *Confessions* has had on the philosophy of time. Augustine's analysis of temporal experience was not immediately absorbed into the mainstream of medieval philosophy — the scholastic tradition was more interested in Aristotelian metaphysics — but it remained available as a resource, and it entered the modern philosophical discussion at exactly the moment when phenomenology was being invented.

Section 8.5.1 traces Augustine's influence: the immediate medieval reception, the Husserlian transformation, and the broader significance of his analysis for contemporary debates about temporal experience, narrative, and the philosophy of mind.
