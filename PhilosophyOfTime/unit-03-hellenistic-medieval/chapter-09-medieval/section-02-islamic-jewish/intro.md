# Section 9.2: Islamic and Jewish Philosophy of Time

---

## Section Introduction

The philosophical tradition that became scholasticism was not a purely Latin Christian achievement. It was built from the encounter of Latin Christian thought with Greek philosophy — and that encounter was mediated in large part through Arabic. The translation of Aristotle into Arabic, the Arabic commentaries on Aristotle, and the original philosophical systems developed by Islamic and Jewish thinkers all played crucial roles in shaping the questions and concepts that Christian scholastics inherited.

For the philosophy of time, the Islamic and Jewish traditions contributed two major elements: first, a sophisticated elaboration and critique of Aristotle's account of time (particularly in Avicenna and Averroes); second, a rigorous engagement with the question of whether the world is eternal (in Avicenna, Averroes, and Maimonides) that set the terms for the later Christian debate. Section 9.2 surveys these contributions, focusing on Avicenna, Averroes, and Maimonides.
