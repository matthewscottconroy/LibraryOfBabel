# Section 9.3: Aquinas

---

## Section Introduction

Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274 CE) is the greatest systematic philosopher of the medieval period and one of the greatest in the entire Western tradition. His synthesis of Aristotelian philosophy and Christian theology — achieved in thousands of pages of extraordinarily disciplined argumentative prose — shaped Catholic philosophy for centuries and remains a living intellectual tradition. His treatment of time in the *Summa Theologiae* is a model of philosophical precision: he distinguishes multiple questions, addresses each systematically, considers objections, and offers carefully reasoned replies.

The most important structural innovation in Aquinas's philosophy of time is his three-way distinction among *tempus* (ordinary time), *aevum* (angelic or quasi-temporal duration), and *aeternitas* (divine eternity). This three-tiered account solves several problems simultaneously: it allows for gradations of temporality, it provides a place for the existence of angels (a theological requirement), and it articulates the difference between divine eternity and creaturely time with more precision than a simple binary allows.
