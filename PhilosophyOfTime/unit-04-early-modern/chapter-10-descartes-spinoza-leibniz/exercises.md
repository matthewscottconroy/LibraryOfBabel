## Comprehension and Analysis

**Exercise 10.1**
Explain Descartes's distinction between "duration" and "time." In what sense does Descartes treat time as an "abstraction"? How does this distinction relate to the contemporary debate between absolute and relational accounts of time?

**Exercise 10.2**
Explain Descartes's doctrine of continuous creation. What is the key premise about the ontological independence of temporal moments? What implications does continuous creation have for temporal continuity and causation?

**Exercise 10.3**
Spinoza distinguishes between duration (which is real) and time (which is a product of imagination). Explain this distinction carefully. What exactly is "imaginative" about ordinary temporal thinking? What would adequate knowledge of a temporal event look like?

**Exercise 10.4**
Explain what it means to know something *sub specie aeternitatis*. Is this a coherent concept? What would it mean, practically, to know a historical event "under the aspect of eternity"? Can human beings actually achieve this kind of knowledge?

**Exercise 10.5**
Reconstruct Leibniz's PSR argument against absolute time. What is the false presupposition that absolute time introduces? Is the PSR itself a principle that can be defended independently of its application to the time debate?

**Exercise 10.6**
Explain how the Identity of Indiscernibles generates an argument against absolute time. What would Clarke say in response? Who do you think has the better of the argument?

**Exercise 10.7**
What was the central issue in the Leibniz-Clarke correspondence that Leibniz could not fully resolve? How did Mach's Principle attempt to address this issue? Did Mach succeed?

**Exercise 10.8**
Compare Leibniz's relational theory of time with Epicurus's account of time as an "accident of accidents." What do the two accounts have in common? What are the significant differences?

## Observational and Experimental Exercises

**Lab Exercise 10.A — Introspecting Duration and Time**

Sit quietly for two minutes without looking at a clock. Attend to your sense of time passing. Now ask yourself: what are you aware of when you are aware of time passing? Is it something external (a sound, a change in light), something internal (a sense of having thought so many thoughts, breathed so many breaths), or something else? After two minutes, estimate how long you sat. Compare your estimate to actual elapsed time. Then reflect: does this experiment support Descartes's claim that "time" (as a measure) is an abstraction from more concrete durations, or does it support Spinoza's claim that "time" (as a flow) is a product of imagination?

**Lab Exercise 10.B — The Relational/Absolute Thought Experiment**

Imagine two universes that are intrinsically qualitatively identical in all their events, processes, and relations — but one occurred in the years 1–2026 CE and the other in the years 1001–3026 CE (one thousand years later). Is there any detectable difference between these universes from the inside? Write a short account of what Leibniz would say about this thought experiment, what Newton/Clarke would say, and what you think the right conclusion is. Does the undetectability of the difference count as strong evidence that absolute time is not real?

**Lab Exercise 10.C — Watching Rotation**

Find an object you can spin (a top, a coin, a lazy Susan). Spin it and observe the behavior. Now ask: what determines whether the object is "really" rotating, as opposed to the rest of the universe rotating around it? Can you tell the difference from inside the spinning system? (Hint: if you were a tiny creature standing on the spinning object, would you feel the rotation?) Reflect on what this suggests about Leibniz's relational account of rotation and Newton's absolute space response.

## Essay Questions

**Exercise 10.9 (Essay)**
Descartes holds that the parts of time are "not mutually dependent." Is this true? If temporal moments are genuinely independent of each other, what holds them together into a continuous temporal sequence? Write an essay of 800–1000 words exploring this question, drawing on Descartes's continuous creation doctrine and considering how a Leibnizian or Newtonian might respond.

**Exercise 10.10 (Essay)**
Spinoza holds that time, as ordinarily conceived, is a product of imagination rather than adequate knowledge. Is this position compatible with the manifest reality of change, events, and temporal sequence that we observe in the world? Write an essay of 900–1100 words assessing whether Spinoza's account can accommodate the reality of temporal change.

**Exercise 10.11 (Essay)**
Does Leibniz's relational theory of time face a fatal objection from inertia and rotation — from the mechanics argument that Clarke presses? Write an essay of 1000–1200 words reconstructing the mechanics argument, explaining Mach's response, and assessing whether the relational theory can handle the challenge posed by Newtonian mechanics.

**Exercise 10.12 (Essay)**
Compare Leibniz's view that time is the order of non-simultaneous things with contemporary B-theory ("eternalism") about time. Are they the same view? What does Leibniz add that B-theory lacks, or vice versa? Write an essay of 900–1100 words.
