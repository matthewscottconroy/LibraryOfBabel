# Section 10.1: Descartes on Duration and Continuous Creation

---

## Section Introduction

Descartes's contribution to the philosophy of time is less celebrated than his contributions to epistemology and metaphysics, but it is philosophically significant — and in one respect, astonishing. His account of duration as a mode of substance, developed in the *Principles of Philosophy*, provides a clear ontological account of what temporal existence means within a substance-dualist framework. And his doctrine of continuous creation — the claim that God must re-create the world at every moment — generates a startling implication for temporal continuity that has fascinated philosophers ever since.

Section 10.1.1 examines duration as a mode of substance. Section 10.1.2 examines continuous creation and its implications.
