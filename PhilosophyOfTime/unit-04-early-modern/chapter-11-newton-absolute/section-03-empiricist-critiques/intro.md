# Section 11.3: Empiricist Critiques of Absolute Time

---

## Section Introduction

The rationalist critique of absolute time (Leibniz's PSR arguments) is one line of attack. The empiricist critique is another, and it operates from different principles. Where Leibniz argues that absolute time is metaphysically incoherent (because it violates the PSR), the empiricists argue that absolute time is epistemically empty — we have no experience of it, and therefore no genuine concept of it. Whatever we call "absolute time" is either a fiction generated by misusing language or an idea that reduces, on analysis, to nothing more than our experience of temporal succession.

This section surveys the empiricist critiques of Locke and Berkeley (Section 11.3.1) and Hume (Section 11.3.2). These critiques anticipate Kant's more systematic treatment in the next chapter: Kant will agree with the empiricists that absolute time is not something we experience from the outside, but he will draw a very different conclusion about what this shows.
