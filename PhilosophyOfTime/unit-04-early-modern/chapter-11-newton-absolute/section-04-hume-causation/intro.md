# Section 11.4: Hume on Causation and Temporal Priority

---

## Section Introduction

Hume's analysis of time connects directly to one of his most famous and influential contributions: his analysis of causation. Hume holds that causes always temporally precede their effects — or, more carefully, that our concept of causation includes temporal priority as a necessary component. This claim raises several philosophical questions: Is temporal priority conceptually required for causation? Could causes and effects be simultaneous? Could effects precede their causes (backward causation)? And what grounds the temporal asymmetry of causation — why do causes precede rather than follow effects?

These questions connect the philosophy of time to fundamental questions in the philosophy of science and metaphysics. They anticipate contemporary debates about the arrow of time, backward causation, and the relationship between the causal and temporal orders.
