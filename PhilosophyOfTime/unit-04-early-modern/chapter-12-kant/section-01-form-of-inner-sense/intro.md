# Section 12.1: Time as the Form of Inner Sense

---

## Section Introduction

The "Transcendental Aesthetic" is the first major division of Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason*. "Aesthetic" here does not mean the study of beauty; it comes from the Greek *aisthesis* (sensation) and means the study of sensible intuition — the study of how the mind receives experience. The word "transcendental" in Kant's vocabulary means "concerned with the a priori conditions of the possibility of experience." The Transcendental Aesthetic, therefore, is the study of the a priori conditions of sensible experience.

Kant's central claim in this section is that space and time are not features of things as they are in themselves but forms of our sensible intuition — the structural conditions imposed by the mind on whatever is given in experience. Space is the form of outer intuition (the form through which we represent objects outside us); time is the form of inner sense (the form through which we represent our own inner states and, derivatively, all outer objects).

Section 12.1.1 examines the Transcendental Aesthetic and the concept of a form of intuition. Section 12.1.2 examines why time has priority over space as the form of inner sense, and what "inner sense" means.
