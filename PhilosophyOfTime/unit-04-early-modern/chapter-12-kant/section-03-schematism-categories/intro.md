# Section 12.3: The Schematism and the Analogies of Experience

---

## Section Introduction

One of the most original and technically demanding sections of the *Critique of Pure Reason* is the "Schematism of the Pure Concepts of the Understanding." The problem Kant is addressing is this: the pure categories of the understanding (causality, substance, community) are purely conceptual — they have no sensory content. Experience involves intuitions — sensory presentations in space and time. How can pure categories apply to sensory intuitions? There must be some mediating element that shares features with both.

Time is that mediating element. The "temporal schema" of each pure category is the time-determination that corresponds to the category and allows it to be applied to experience. The temporal schema of causality, for example, is necessary temporal succession: when we apply the concept of causality to experience, we are applying it through its temporal schema — the schema of "what follows according to a rule."

The Analogies of Experience — three principles concerning the temporal relations of phenomena — follow from the schematized categories and provide the framework for Kant's account of objective temporal experience.
