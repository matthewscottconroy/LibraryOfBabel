# Section 12.5: Post-Kantian Idealism and Time

---

## Section Introduction

Kant's account of time as the form of inner sense was enormously influential on his immediate successors in German Idealism. Fichte, Schelling, and Hegel each took up Kantian themes about the relationship between mind and time, and each transformed them in ways that moved further and further from the Kantian framework. This section provides a brief treatment of Fichte's temporal ego and Hegel's dialectical account of time — enough to map the trajectory of post-Kantian idealism and to see what philosophical questions drove it.

The broad theme is what might be called the "radicalization" of Kantian idealism: where Kant had held that time is the form of inner sense — a contribution of the transcendental subject — the post-Kantians moved toward the view that time is constitutive of the very structure of spirit or rationality itself. This is a significant philosophical move, and it sets the stage for the phenomenological tradition of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
