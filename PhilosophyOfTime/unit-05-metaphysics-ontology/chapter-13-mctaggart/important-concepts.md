# Chapter 13 Important Concepts

---

**A-Series**
The ordering of temporal positions by the properties of being past, present, or future. A-series properties change as time passes: what is now future will become present and then past. McTaggart regarded the A-series as essential to time but ultimately incoherent.

**B-Series**
The ordering of temporal positions by the permanent, tenseless relations of "earlier than," "later than," and "simultaneous with." B-series facts do not change over time; the relation "earlier than" between two events holds timelessly. B-theorists argue the B-series is all the temporal structure that exists.

**C-Series**
McTaggart's term for a bare, directionless ordering of positions — a pure sequence without the temporal direction of the B-series or the dynamic properties of the A-series. McTaggart regarded the C-series as the underlying atemporal reality from which the appearance of time is generated.

**Tenseless Facts**
Facts stated without grammatical tense, such as "E₁ is earlier than E₂." Tenseless facts have the same truth value at all times and constitute the ontological basis of the B-series. Distinguishing tenseless from tensed facts is crucial to the A/B debate.

**Temporal Passage**
The intuitive phenomenon of time "moving" or "flowing" — events transitioning from future to present to past. A-theorists hold that passage is a genuine feature of reality requiring A-series properties; B-theorists typically hold that passage is either illusory or reducible to B-series variation.

**Vicious Regress**
An infinite regress in which each level of explanation presupposes the very thing it was invoked to explain, so that the regress cannot be completed and the explanation fails. McTaggart argued that attempting to resolve A-series contradictions by relativizing properties to times generates a vicious regress of this kind.

**A-Properties**
The properties of being past, being present, and being future. On the A-theorist's view, these are genuine, objective properties of temporal positions. On the B-theorist's view, they are either indexical perspectival facts or simply non-existent.

**Indexical Analysis of Tense**
The view, associated with Prior and others, that tensed statements do not attribute genuine A-properties but instead express an observer's perspectival location in time — analogous to the way "here" expresses a spatial location rather than attributing a genuine property of "hereness."

**Cambridge Change**
Change that consists merely in something's coming to stand in a new relation to something else, without any intrinsic change in the thing itself. For example, Socrates "becomes shorter" than Plato when Plato grows, without Socrates changing at all. The worry is that B-series "change" is merely Cambridge change.

**Token-Reflexive Analysis**
An analysis of tensed statements in which their meaning is given in terms of the relation between the time of utterance (or the token statement itself) and other events. "E is present" means "E is simultaneous with this utterance." This is a B-theoretic strategy for handling tensed language.

**Temporal Becoming**
The coming-into-being and passing-away of events — the idea that events genuinely occur, emerge from the future, and recede into the past. A-theorists typically hold that becoming is real and fundamental; B-theorists typically hold that it is either reducible or illusory.

**The Unreality of Time**
McTaggart's conclusion that time does not genuinely exist because neither the A-series nor the B-series can provide a coherent account of it. The underlying reality is the atemporal C-series; the appearance of time is a systematic misrepresentation of that reality.

**Permanent Relations**
Relations that hold timelessly and cannot change, such as B-series ordering relations. The fact that 1066 CE is earlier than 1969 CE is a permanent relation: it holds now, it held before either event, and it will hold after both.

**Objective Presentness**
The view, characteristic of A-theory, that there is an objective, mind-independent fact about which moment is present. This is contrasted with the B-theorist's view that "present" is merely perspectival or indexical.

**Misperception Account**
McTaggart's positive claim that our experience of temporal flow is a systematic misperception of the C-series — that we apprehend the atemporal underlying order but represent it to ourselves as temporal. An analogy: we "see" two lines as unequal in length, but the underlying reality (the lines) are equal.
