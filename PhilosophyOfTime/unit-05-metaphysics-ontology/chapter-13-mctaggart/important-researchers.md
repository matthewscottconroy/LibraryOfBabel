# Chapter 13 Important Researchers

---

**J. M. E. McTaggart (1866–1925)**
John McTaggart Ellis McTaggart was a British idealist philosopher at Trinity College, Cambridge, best known for his 1908 paper "The Unreality of Time" and his two-volume *The Nature of Existence* (1921, 1927). His A/B distinction is the single most influential conceptual contribution to analytic philosophy of time. McTaggart's broader metaphysics was an unusual form of personal idealism in which reality consists of an infinite community of spiritual substances — a view he arrived at through meticulous logical argument rather than religious conviction. Despite the apparent oddness of his conclusion about time, his argument remains philosophically rigorous and continues to generate serious scholarly attention.

**Arthur N. Prior (1914–1969)**
Arthur Prior was a New Zealand–born philosopher who founded tense logic — a formal system for reasoning about time that uses tense operators (P for "it was the case that," F for "it will be the case that") as primitive logical connectives rather than quantifiers over times. His response to McTaggart's regress — that A-properties are not genuine properties but indexical expressions — is one of the most important moves in the philosophy of time. Prior also wrote illuminatingly on the emotional and existential significance of temporal location, as in his famous paper "Thank Goodness That's Over" (1959). His work *Past, Present and Future* (1967) remains foundational.

**D. H. Mellor (1938–2020)**
Hugh Mellor was a British philosopher at Cambridge who developed one of the most sophisticated defenses of B-theory in the twentieth century. His *Real Time* (1981) and *Real Time II* (1998) defend the view that all genuine temporal facts are B-series facts and that tensed language is fully analyzable in terms of token-reflexive relations. Mellor's careful work on the relationship between tensed language and tenseless ontology — distinguishing the *meaning* of tensed statements from their *truth conditions* — is indispensable for understanding the contemporary B-theory position.

**Michael Dummett (1925–2011)**
Michael Dummett was one of the most influential British philosophers of the twentieth century, known primarily for his work on logic, philosophy of language, and the philosophy of mathematics. His 1960 paper "A Defence of McTaggart's Proof of the Unreality of Time" offered a reconstruction of McTaggart's argument that emphasizes the irreducibility of tensed discourse rather than the contradiction of A-properties. Dummett's broader project of anti-realist semantics — grounding meaning in verification conditions rather than truth conditions — has implications for the philosophy of time that continue to be explored.

**E. J. Lowe (1950–2014)**
Jonathan Lowe was a British metaphysician who defended a robust form of A-theory against B-theoretic reductions throughout his career. His *The Possibility of Metaphysics* (1998) contains a careful defense of A-theory against McTaggart's regress and against Mellor's B-theoretic analysis. Lowe's work is notable for combining rigorous analytical argument with a commitment to taking the deliverances of ordinary experience seriously as data for metaphysics. He argued that the A-series is irreducibly real and that its apparent contradictions can be resolved without generating a vicious regress.

**C. D. Broad (1887–1971)**
Charlie Broad was a British philosopher who provided important early analyses of temporal ontology, including the "growing block" view of time (see Chapter 15). His discussion of McTaggart in *An Examination of McTaggart's Philosophy* (1938) is still one of the most thorough and technically precise engagements with McTaggart's arguments. Broad had a sharp eye for distinguishing what McTaggart's argument actually requires from what commentators mistakenly attribute to it.

**F. H. Bradley (1846–1924)**
Francis Herbert Bradley was the leading British Idealist philosopher of his generation and McTaggart's most important intellectual context. Bradley's *Appearance and Reality* (1893) developed the general framework of distinguishing between how reality appears to us and what it is in itself — a framework McTaggart deployed in his argument for the unreality of time. Understanding Bradley's method of internal critique (showing that appearances generate contradictions that point to a deeper reality) is helpful for understanding why McTaggart argues as he does.

**Robin Le Poidevin (born 1962)**
Robin Le Poidevin is a British philosopher of time whose work spans the metaphysics of time, the philosophy of religion, and the phenomenology of temporal experience. His *Change, Cause and Contradiction* (1991) examines McTaggart's argument with great care and develops a sophisticated version of the B-theory that attempts to accommodate what is genuinely correct in the A-theorist's intuitions. Le Poidevin is also an excellent guide to the history of the debate, and his anthologies on philosophy of time remain standard reference works.
