# Section 13.1: The A-Series and the B-Series

---

## Section Introduction

Every philosophical project needs its conceptual tools laid down before the heavy work can begin. McTaggart's first contribution — the one that has outlasted every controversy about his conclusion — was a pair of carefully articulated concepts for thinking about time: the A-series and the B-series. These are not just labels for positions in a debate. They represent two genuinely different ways of thinking about what temporal order consists in, and getting the distinction properly in view is prerequisite to everything that follows in this unit and much of what follows in the rest of this book.

This section introduces both series — and the third, more neglected notion of the C-series — through McTaggart's original formulations, supplemented by examples chosen to bring the difference to life. We will examine what kind of ordering each series provides, what kind of facts constitute each series, and why the difference between them is philosophically significant rather than merely terminological.

By the end of this section, you should have the distinction as a reliable working tool. Do not underestimate the importance of that: philosophers who proceed with a blurry A/B distinction produce blurry philosophy.
