# Section 13.3: The Regress Argument

---

## Section Introduction

Having established (to his satisfaction) that the A-series is fundamental to time, McTaggart then delivers the coup de grâce: the A-series is not merely fundamental but incoherent. The argument is known as the McTaggart regress, and it is the most discussed, most contested, and most philosophically productive part of his 1908 paper.

The argument has the structure of a dilemma. If the A-series is required for time, and the A-series generates a contradiction, then time is contradictory — hence unreal. The contradiction McTaggart identifies is this: A-properties are mutually incompatible, yet every event must possess all three of them. Every event is (or was, or will be) past, present, and future. Those three properties cannot be co-instantiated. But they must be, if time is real.

The "obvious" response — that an event has these properties at *different times* — launches an infinite regress, because the "different times" reintroduce A-series properties, and the same problem recurs.

This section presents the regress argument in its original form, then turns in 13.3.2 to the most important responses that have been offered. The argument is genuinely difficult, and the question of whether any response is fully adequate remains open.
