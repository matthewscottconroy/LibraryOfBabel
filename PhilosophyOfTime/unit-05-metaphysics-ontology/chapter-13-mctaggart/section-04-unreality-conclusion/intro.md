# Section 13.4: The Unreality of Time — McTaggart's Conclusion

---

## Section Introduction

Having argued that the A-series is indispensable to time and that the A-series is incoherent, McTaggart draws his famous conclusion: time is unreal. But this is not the conclusion of a purely negative argument. McTaggart is a systematic metaphysician, and his claim about the unreality of time is embedded within a positive account of what *is* real: the C-series, the atemporal underlying order that generates the *appearance* of temporal flow.

This section examines that conclusion carefully. What exactly does McTaggart mean by the unreality of time? How does the C-series give rise to the temporal appearance? And is his positive picture coherent?

The section also takes a step back to ask a broader question: even if McTaggart's argument is flawed, what has it shown? The argument's legacy is not primarily its conclusion but its methodology — its insistence on precision about what temporal concepts require, and its demonstration that naive assumptions about time are philosophically fragile.
