# Chapter 14 Exercises: B-Theory and Eternalism

---

## Comprehension and Analysis

**1.** Explain the block universe thesis in your own words to someone with no philosophical background. What are the three most likely points of confusion or resistance you would encounter? How would you respond to each?

**2.** The Rietdijk-Putnam argument concludes from special relativity that all events are real. Identify each premise, and evaluate whether each is true and whether the inference is valid. Which premise is most vulnerable, and what is the best objection to it?

**3.** Reconstruct the truthmaker argument for eternalism. Now give the best available presentist response. Does the presentist response fully discharge the burden of providing truthmakers for past-directed truths?

**4.** Explain the difference between the worm theory and the stage theory of persistence. Give an example — a person aging over twenty years — and show how each theory would describe what happens. Which account do you find more intuitively compelling, and why?

**5.** B-theorists say "now" is an indexical like "here." Construct a careful argument that "now" and "here" are *not* analogous in the relevant sense. Then construct the best B-theoretic reply.

---

## Synthetic and Argumentative

**6.** The passage problem holds that the block universe is "static" and cannot account for temporal flow. Is this a decisive objection? Identify three possible B-theoretic responses and rank them in order of plausibility, explaining your ranking.

**7.** Consider the phenomenological objection: the vivid, universal experience of the present as uniquely actual seems to track something real. Write a dialogue (one to two pages) between a committed B-theorist and a committed phenomenologist, in which each makes their strongest points. Who wins?

**8.** "The block universe is compatible with genuine free will." Evaluate this claim, distinguishing between: (a) the block universe and determinism as separate theses, (b) compatibilist vs. libertarian accounts of free will, and (c) whether your own preferred account of free will is threatened by the block universe.

## Lab and Observational Exercises

**9. Lab — The Block Universe Meditation.** Find a quiet space and spend ten minutes imagining the block universe view as vividly as you can. Imagine your entire life laid out in four dimensions — your birth, childhood, adolescent stages, adult stages, old age, all equally real, all co-existing in the manifold. Your current temporal stage is just one position among them. What does it feel like to try to inhabit this picture? Does the experience of inhabiting it change how you feel about the present moment? Write a half-page reflection on what you noticed.

**10. Lab — Temporal Indexicals in Daily Life.** Choose a news article or a page from a historical text. Identify every temporal indexical (expressions whose temporal reference shifts with context: "now," "today," "yesterday," "soon," "recently," "currently," etc.). Rewrite the passage replacing all indexicals with B-series formulations (dates, "earlier than," "simultaneous with," etc.). What is gained and lost in the translation? Does anything essential resist translation?

**11. Lab — Personal Identity Through Time.** Collect photographs of yourself from as many different ages as you can (childhood, adolescence, early adulthood, etc.). Look at them in order. On the worm theory, all these are temporal stages of a single four-dimensional worm — you. On the stage theory, each is a distinct entity related to the others by a temporal counterpart relation. Which picture feels more accurate to your experience of yourself? Write a brief reflection (150–200 words) on whether your intuitions here provide any evidence for or against either theory.

## Reflection

**12.** The B-theory requires saying that your great-grandchildren — if you will have them — already exist in the four-dimensional manifold. Is this thought disturbing, reassuring, or neutral? Does your emotional response to it provide any evidence about which metaphysical view is correct, or is emotional response simply irrelevant to metaphysics? Reflect in half a page.
