## Annotated Entries

**Mellor, D. H. 1998. *Real Time II*. London: Routledge.**
The definitive statement of a sophisticated, technically rigorous B-theory. Mellor distinguishes the meaning of tensed claims from their truth conditions, gives a careful token-reflexive analysis, and engages thoroughly with the best A-theoretic objections. Begin with Chapter 1 for the overview; work through Chapters 4–6 for the detailed analysis of tensed language.

**Sider, Theodore. 2001. *Four-Dimensionalism: An Ontology of Persistence and Time*. Oxford: Clarendon Press.**
The standard contemporary reference on worm theory and B-theoretic metaphysics. Chapters 3–5 develop worm theory and stage theory in detail. The book is technically demanding but written with exceptional clarity; the introductory and concluding chapters are accessible to readers without background in formal metaphysics.

**Smart, J. J. C. 1949. "The River of Time." *Mind* 58 (232): 483–494.**
The foundational statement of the mid-twentieth-century B-theory, accessible and engagingly written. Smart's argument against temporal passage has been discussed continuously since its publication. Essential reading for understanding the historical development of the debate.

**Williams, Donald C. 1951. "The Myth of Passage." *Journal of Philosophy* 48 (15): 457–472.**
A classic defense of the block universe that develops the "temporal-as-spatial" analogy with great rhetorical force. Williams argues that the idea of temporal passage is a confusion — a category error — and that the block universe is the only coherent picture of temporal reality. Essential alongside Smart as a statement of the classical B-theory position.

**Putnam, Hilary. 1967. "Time and Physical Geometry." *Journal of Philosophy* 64 (8): 240–247.**
The philosophical statement of what is now called the Rietdijk-Putnam argument. Short, clearly written, and philosophically important. Putnam's version of the argument is more focused on the metaphysics of existence than Rietdijk's, and his engagement with the A-theorist's possible responses is instructive.

**Maudlin, Tim. 2012. *Philosophy of Physics: Space and Time*. Princeton: Princeton University Press.**
An excellent introduction to the philosophy of spacetime physics, written by one of the most important philosophers of physics working today. Particularly valuable for connecting the block universe debate to the actual content of special and general relativity. Chapters 3 and 4 are directly relevant to this chapter's material.

**Lewis, David. 1986. *On the Plurality of Worlds*. Oxford: Blackwell. Chapter 4.**
Lewis's worm theory of temporal persistence is embedded within his broader modal realism, but Chapter 4's discussion of the distinction between spatial and temporal extent is accessible on its own. Lewis's prose is among the most enjoyable in analytic philosophy, and his argument is a model of clarity.

**Hawley, Katherine. 2001. *How Things Persist*. Oxford: Clarendon Press.**
A balanced and careful examination of the endurantism/perdurantism debate, with careful attention to stage theory as a distinct alternative. Hawley's approach is to take the ordinary cases seriously and ask which theory gives the best account of them. A good companion to Sider's more theoretical treatment.

**Craig, William Lane. 2000. *The Tensed Theory of Time: A Critical Examination*. Dordrecht: Kluwer.**
An extensive and passionate critique of B-theory from the perspective of a committed A-theorist. Craig's objections are forceful, though his own positive view is sometimes underargued. Reading this alongside Mellor gives an excellent sense of what is at stake in the debate.

**Dainton, Barry. 2000. *Stream of Consciousness: Unity and Continuity in Conscious Experience*. London: Routledge.**
A rich and carefully argued account of temporal consciousness that engages directly with the phenomenological objection to B-theory. Dainton defends a version of extensionalism about temporal experience — the view that the specious present is genuinely temporally extended — and his book is the best place to start on the empirical and philosophical psychology of temporal experience.
