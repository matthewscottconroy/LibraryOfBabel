# Chapter 14 Important Concepts

---

**Block Universe**
The metaphysical thesis that all past, present, and future events exist with equal ontological status in a four-dimensional manifold. On this view, there is no "front edge" of reality advancing from past to future; the temporal dimension is fully extended and complete.

**Eternalism**
The view that objects and events at all times exist; past and future entities are as real as present ones. Eternalism is the ontological corollary of the block universe thesis. It contrasts with presentism (only present things exist) and the growing block view (only past and present things exist).

**Tenseless Statement**
A statement whose truth value does not vary with the time of its utterance — for example, "Event A is earlier than Event B." Tenseless statements express B-series facts and are permanent: they are true, or false, at every time.

**Indexical Expression**
An expression whose reference depends on the context of utterance. "I" refers to whoever is speaking; "here" refers to where the speaker is; "now" — on the B-theory — refers to when the speaker is. Indexicals do not pick out metaphysically special objects; they pick out contextually specified objects.

**Token-Reflexive Analysis**
A B-theoretic strategy for giving truth conditions of tensed statements in terms of relations between events and the token utterance (or the time of the utterance). "It is raining now" (uttered at t) = "It is raining at t." The analysis eliminates reference to objective A-properties.

**Perdurantism (Worm Theory)**
The view that objects persist through time by having temporal parts at each moment of their existence, just as they have spatial parts at each region of their spatial extent. A person is a four-dimensional temporal worm; the person "now" is a temporal stage of that worm.

**Temporal Stage**
An instantaneous or brief time-slice of a four-dimensional temporal worm. On perdurantism, temporal stages are the fundamental bearers of temporal properties; on stage theory, they are what we refer to when we use ordinary object terms.

**Stage Theory**
A variant of four-dimensionalism holding that ordinary objects are instantaneous temporal stages, and persistence is constituted by temporal counterpart relations among stages rather than by a four-dimensional worm of which stages are parts. Associated with Sider and Hawley.

**Temporal Counterpart Relation**
On stage theory, the relation that holds between temporal stages in virtue of which they are "the same object" at different times. Modeled on Lewis's modal counterpart relation; grounded in physical continuity, psychological continuity, and causal ancestry.

**Problem of Temporary Intrinsics**
The problem that one and the same object seems to instantiate incompatible properties at different times (a leaf is both green and yellow). Worm theory resolves it by attributing the incompatible properties to different temporal stages rather than to the numerically identical object simpliciter.

**Rietdijk-Putnam Argument**
An argument from the relativity of simultaneity in special relativity to the conclusion that all events — past, present, and future — are equally real. If there is no privileged present, then events cannot "become" real at a particular time; hence all events exist timelessly.

**Relativity of Simultaneity**
In special relativity, the fact that events simultaneous in one inertial reference frame are not simultaneous in all inertial reference frames. This follows from the constancy of the speed of light and is central to the argument against objective presentness.

**Truthmaker Principle**
The principle that for every true proposition, there exists something in reality that makes it true. The truthmaker argument for eternalism holds that truths about past events require truthmakers, and the most natural candidates are the past events themselves — which must therefore still exist.

**Passage Problem**
The objection to B-theory that it cannot account for the passage of time, because the block universe is a static four-dimensional structure and nothing "moves" through it. The B-theorist's response is either to deny that genuine passage exists or to explain it in terms of the B-series structure.

**Four-Dimensionalism**
The metaphysical view that objects have both spatial and temporal parts, and that the correct way to understand persistence is in terms of temporal extension rather than strict identity across time. Four-dimensionalism is the natural ontological complement to the block universe view.
