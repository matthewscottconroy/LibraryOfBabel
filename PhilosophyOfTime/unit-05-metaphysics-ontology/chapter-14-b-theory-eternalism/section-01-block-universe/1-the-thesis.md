# 14.1.1 — The Block Universe Thesis

---

The block universe view is best approached by taking its central claim seriously: the universe is a four-dimensional entity, and all of its temporal parts — past, present, and future — are equally real. There is no ontological hierarchy among temporal positions. The year 1000 BCE is just as real as 2026 CE; 2500 CE is just as real as today. What varies is not the reality of temporal positions but their relations to observers: some temporal positions are earlier than this one, some later, and one — the one you occupy — is the present.

This view has been defended with different emphases by different philosophers. J.J.C. Smart argued early on that temporal becoming — the sense in which events genuinely emerge from the future and recede into the past — is an illusion created by our temporal perspective, and that the "moving now" adds nothing to the B-series facts (Smart 1949: 485–487). D.H. Mellor developed the most technically rigorous version of the B-theory, arguing that all genuine temporal facts are tenseless B-series facts and that the truth conditions of tensed statements can be given in terms of token-reflexive relations without positing A-properties (Mellor 1998: Ch. 1). Ted Sider extended the picture into a comprehensive four-dimensionalist metaphysics in which objects, like the universe itself, are extended through the temporal dimension as well as the spatial dimensions (Sider 2001: Introduction).

## The Four-Dimensional Manifold

Think of the block universe this way. Ordinary three-dimensional space contains objects arranged at different spatial locations. You are here; a friend is there; a mountain is somewhere else. All of them exist simultaneously — they are all real at the same time. Now extend this picture into the temporal dimension. The block universe holds that events at different temporal locations are related to one another as events at different spatial locations are related: they all exist, and they are arranged relative to one another, but they are not simultaneous with each other. Just as you and your friend both exist despite being at different places, you and your great-grandparents both exist (in the tenseless sense) despite being at different times.

The universe, on this picture, is a four-dimensional manifold of events — each event a point in spacetime, related to other events by B-series relations. The manifold extends through space in three dimensions and through time in one dimension, and all of it is equally real. There is no "front edge" of reality advancing from past to future; the manifold is complete.

A useful spatial analogy: imagine a novel printed on pages. Each page exists; the events described on page 1 are just as real as the events on page 200. "The beginning of the story" and "the end of the story" are not ontological distinctions — the events at the beginning are not less real than those in the middle. Similarly, for the block universe theorist, "the past" and "the future" are not ontological distinctions: they are positions within a fully existing manifold.

## What the Block Universe Does Not Say

The block universe view is frequently misunderstood in two directions, and it is worth being explicit about what it does not claim.

**It does not claim that the future is fixed or determined.** Whether the future is determined depends on the laws of nature and initial conditions — on the dynamical structure of the manifold. The block universe is compatible with both determinism and indeterminism. A genuinely indeterministic universe would have a block structure whose future portion is fixed (since all of it equally exists) while remaining genuinely stochastic from the perspective of any temporal stage within it. This is a subtle but important point that Chapter 14.5 examines in detail.

**It does not claim that our experience of temporal flow is false.** The block universe view is a metaphysical claim about what there is; it is not, by itself, a phenomenological claim about how experience seems. Block universe theorists owe us an account of why time *seems* to flow — and they have offered various accounts — but they are not committed to dismissing temporal experience as straightforwardly deceptive.

## Smart and the Origins of Block Universe Thinking

J.J.C. Smart's 1949 paper "The River of Time" is one of the most accessible early defenses of the block universe view. Smart's central move is to challenge the notion of temporal "flow" or "passage." We speak as if time is moving — as if the present is advancing through the temporal manifold. But what would it mean for time to move? Movement, ordinarily, is change of position over time. So temporal movement would be change of temporal position over time — but that requires a second-order time with respect to which the first-order time is moving. And what moves through that second-order time? A third-order time? The regress mirrors McTaggart's, and Smart's conclusion is the same: the idea of time "flowing" is incoherent, and what we are left with is the static, four-dimensional block (Smart 1949: 487).
