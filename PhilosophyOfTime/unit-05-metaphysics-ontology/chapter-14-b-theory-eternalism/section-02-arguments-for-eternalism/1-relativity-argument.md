# 14.2.1 — The Argument from Relativity: Rietdijk-Putnam

---

The most powerful argument for the block universe comes not from armchair metaphysics but from physics. Special relativity, as standardly interpreted, seems to imply that there is no observer-independent fact about what is present, and from this it follows — by a short but serious argument — that all events are real. This is the Rietdijk-Putnam argument.

## The Physical Background

In Newtonian physics, simultaneity is absolute. If event A and event B are simultaneous for one observer, they are simultaneous for all observers. There is a global "now" — a cosmic moment that divides the universe into the past and the future for everyone, everywhere. This supports a natural A-theoretic picture: what is present is what is objectively happening throughout the universe at this moment.

Einstein's special relativity (1905) overturned this assumption. According to special relativity, simultaneity is frame-relative: events that are simultaneous in one inertial reference frame are not simultaneous in another. An observer moving relative to you will assign different simultaneity relations to the same events than you do. If you are stationary relative to two events A and B and judge them simultaneous, an observer moving at a significant fraction of the speed of light relative to you may judge A to be earlier than B.

This is not an epistemic limitation — it is not that both observers have the correct facts and are describing them in different coordinate systems. On the standard interpretation (and on any interpretation that takes Lorentz invariance seriously), there is no privileged rest frame, and therefore no privileged simultaneity relation. The relativity of simultaneity is a feature of the physical world, not merely of our descriptions of it.

## The Rietdijk-Putnam Argument

C.W.A. Rietdijk and Hilary Putnam, independently, developed the philosophical implications of special relativity for the ontology of time (Rietdijk 1966; Putnam 1967). Their argument has roughly the following structure:

1. According to special relativity, simultaneity is relative to inertial reference frame.
2. There is no privileged reference frame.
3. Therefore, there is no observer-independent fact about what events are "simultaneous with now" — i.e., no objective global present.
4. But if there is no objective present, there is no privileged time at which events "become real."
5. If events do not "become real" at a particular time, then they must already be real — they exist timelessly.
6. Therefore, all events — past, present, and future — are equally real.
7. Therefore, eternalism is true.

Putnam's version of the argument is especially vivid. Suppose you are on Earth, and there is a Martian walking on Mars. You and the Martian are in relative motion. On your simultaneity slice, your "now" includes a particular event on Mars — call it M. For the Martian walking in a different direction, their "now" may include a Martian event M* that is future for you — i.e., an event that has not yet occurred on your timeline. But the Martian is just as much in the present as you are. If we agree that what is present for anyone is real, then M* — which is future for you but present for the Martian — is real. And by an iterative version of this argument, every event in the spacetime manifold is real (Putnam 1967: 240–241).

## Assessment

The Rietdijk-Putnam argument is philosophically important, but it has been challenged in several ways.

**The neo-Lorentzian response** holds that special relativity is compatible with a privileged reference frame — the "absolute rest frame" — even if we cannot empirically detect it. The Lorentz ether interpretation of special relativity is empirically equivalent to Einstein's, but it posits an absolute simultaneity relation associated with the privileged frame. Presentists like Dean Zimmerman and the neo-Lorentzians argue that this is a coherent response: metaphysics can outrun our epistemic access (Zimmerman 2008: 212–215).

**The "no fact of the matter" response** accepts that there is no privileged present, but denies that this implies eternalism. Perhaps what we should say is that there is no fact about which events are real — that reality itself is frame-relative. This view has its own problems (it seems to lead to relativism about existence), but it has been seriously developed.

**The Minkowski spacetime response** holds that Rietdijk-Putnam presents a false dichotomy. In special relativity, neither presentism nor the naive block universe is the correct ontology; the correct ontology involves spacetime regions, light cones, and causal structure — not the three-dimensional "slices" of either view. Tim Maudlin (2012: 184–188) argues that the correct way to think about the ontology of special relativity is in terms of the four-dimensional spacetime manifold itself, which supports the block universe picture but requires reconceptualizing both space and time.

Despite these responses, the Rietdijk-Putnam argument remains a formidable challenge. Any view that posits an objective global present must explain how it is compatible with the physics of special relativity — and that is a difficult task.
