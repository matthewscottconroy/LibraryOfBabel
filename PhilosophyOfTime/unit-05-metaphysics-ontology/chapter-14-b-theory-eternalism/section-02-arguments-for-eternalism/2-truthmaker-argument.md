# 14.2.2 — The Truthmaker Argument for Eternalism

---

The second major argument for the block universe comes from the philosophy of language and concerns the conditions under which statements about the past can be true. It is called the **truthmaker argument**, and it runs as follows.

## The Principle of Truthmaking

The truthmaker principle holds that for every true proposition, there must be something in reality that *makes* it true — some fact, entity, or state of affairs whose existence is responsible for the proposition's truth. This principle is plausible and widely endorsed. "There is a cat on the mat" is true because of the existence of a particular cat and mat in a particular spatial relation. "Gold has atomic number 79" is true because of facts about the nature of gold atoms. There is something in reality that grounds every truth.

The truthmaker argument for eternalism asks: what makes truths about the past true?

## The Argument

Consider the proposition: "Julius Caesar crossed the Rubicon in 49 BCE."

This proposition is true. It is true now, and it was true yesterday, and it will be true tomorrow. But what makes it true? What is the truthmaker?

If the presentist is correct — if only the present moment exists — then there is nothing in current reality that *is* Caesar's crossing. The crossing has ceased to exist. It is, quite literally, nothing — not a past thing, not an absent thing, but a non-existent thing. But then what makes the proposition true? What in present reality constitutes its truthmaker?

One might say: the *past* makes it true. But the whole point is that the past does not exist for the presentist; it has been replaced by the present. To appeal to the past as a truthmaker is to acknowledge that the past, in some sense, still exists — which is what the eternalist claims.

Another attempt: perhaps the proposition is made true by *traces* of the past — by present physical records, geological strata, written histories, causal consequences of the crossing. This is sometimes called the "immanent causation" response. But this response faces a difficulty: the truthmaker for "Caesar crossed the Rubicon" should not be merely the present physical traces, because those traces could in principle exist even if Caesar had never actually crossed the river (they could have been planted, or could have arisen by chance). The truthmaker should be the crossing itself, not its downstream effects.

The eternalist has a clean answer: the truthmaker for "Caesar crossed the Rubicon in 49 BCE" is Caesar's crossing, which exists (tenselessly) at the temporal position 49 BCE. The crossing has not ceased to exist; it exists at its temporal location as securely as a rock exists at its spatial location. The crossing is there, in the four-dimensional manifold, making the proposition true.

## The Force of the Argument

The truthmaker argument is not decisive — presentists have developed sophisticated responses (see Chapter 15, Section 15.1.2) — but it identifies a genuine pressure point for any view that denies the existence of past events. The pressure is this: if the past is literally nothing, then past-directed truths seem to float free of any ontological ground.

The eternalist's picture is, in this respect, maximally generous: since all events exist (at their temporal locations), there is always a truthmaker available for propositions about any time. The past is not gone; it is merely elsewhere in the temporal manifold.

## An Important Asymmetry

Notice that the truthmaker argument applies asymmetrically across temporal views. For the block universe theorist, it is not only past events that have truthmakers — future events do too. The proposition "the Sun will continue to exist for approximately five billion more years" is made true by facts about the Sun's future temporal stages, which exist as genuinely as its current temporal stage. This is both a strength (every temporal claim has a truthmaker) and a point of controversy (it seems to imply that the future is "already there," which raises free will worries addressed in Section 14.5).
