# 14.5.1 — The Block Universe and Determinism: Are They the Same?

---

The question arises naturally: if the future already exists — if your future actions are already laid out in the four-dimensional manifold — how can they be free? The block universe seems to entail that what you will do tomorrow is already settled. It is already there in the block.

Let us be precise about what is being claimed and whether it is correct.

## Two Distinct Theses

The block universe thesis and the determinism thesis are distinct, and it is important not to conflate them.

**The block universe thesis (eternalism):** All times and events — past, present, and future — exist with equal ontological status. The four-dimensional manifold is complete; there is no ontological distinction between existing now and existing at another time.

**The determinism thesis:** Every event is necessitated by prior events together with the laws of nature. Given the state of the universe at any time t, the laws of nature determine a unique state at every subsequent time.

These are logically independent. Consider:

- A universe could be **deterministic and an eternalist block**: every future event exists in the manifold and is necessitated by prior events.
- A universe could be **indeterministic and still an eternalist block**: future events exist in the manifold, but which future events occur is not determined by prior events — it is a matter of genuinely stochastic physical processes. The outcome of a quantum measurement is not determined by any prior state, but it is still a real event in the four-dimensional manifold.
- A universe could be **deterministic and not a block**: perhaps the laws of nature determine a unique future from any present state, but future states do not *yet exist* — they will come to exist as time advances (this is roughly the growing block view, see Chapter 15).

The key point: the block universe thesis is an ontological claim about what exists; the determinism thesis is a nomological claim about what necessitates what. Existence and necessitation are different. You can have the former without the latter.

## What "Fixed" Means in the Block Universe

When we say that the future is "already there" in the block universe, this needs careful interpretation. What we mean is: there exist, in the four-dimensional manifold, events at temporal positions later than the present position. Those events are real. But saying they are *real* is not the same as saying they are *necessary* or *determined*.

Here is an analogy. Suppose you are reading a finished novel. The events in chapter 20 are "already there" — they are in the book, they exist on the pages. Does this mean the hero's choice in chapter 20 was *determined* by his character in chapter 1? Not at all. Those are separate questions. The ontological fact that chapter 20 events exist in the book is independent of the causal/nomological question of whether the hero's choice was determined. Similarly, in the block universe, the ontological fact that future events exist is independent of the nomological question of whether they are determined by present conditions.

## Free Will in the Block Universe

Does the block universe threaten free will? This depends on which theory of free will you accept.

**Compatibilism** — the view that free will is compatible with determinism — holds that free will requires only that your actions flow from your desires, reasoning, and character in the right way, without interference or compulsion. On compatibilism, even if your future actions are determined (not just that they exist in the block, but that they are necessitated by prior states), they can still be free in the relevant sense. The block universe is fully compatible with compatibilism.

**Libertarianism** (about free will, not political philosophy) — the view that genuine free will requires the ability to do otherwise, in a strong metaphysical sense, at the moment of choice — faces a challenge not from the block universe per se but from *determinism*. If future events are necessitated by prior events, then at any moment of choice you could not have done otherwise (given the prior state of the universe and the laws). This threatens libertarian free will — but again, this is the threat from *determinism*, not from the *block universe* per se.

Crucially, if the laws of nature are indeterministic — and there is good evidence from quantum mechanics that they are, at the fundamental level — then the block universe is compatible with there being a genuine range of possible futures. The future events that exist in the manifold are not necessitated by the present state; they are the actual outcomes of indeterministic processes. In a quantum block universe, the future is "already there" in the sense that events at future temporal positions are real, but those events are not determined — they are the results of genuinely stochastic processes.

## The Deeper Question

There is a deeper question lurking here that the block universe does raise, even for compatibilists. If your future actions already exist in the four-dimensional manifold, then in a sense you have already made them — they are simply part of the manifold, awaiting your temporal stage's arrival at the relevant position. Does this change how you should think about deliberation?

Some philosophers — including Storrs McCall (1994) and others attracted to the "open future" views discussed in Chapter 15 — argue that genuine deliberation requires an open future: a future that is not yet settled, not yet real, not yet part of any completed manifold. On this view, the very activity of deliberating — of genuinely weighing alternatives as genuinely open — presupposes that the future does not yet exist.

The block universe theorist can respond: deliberation does not require that the future be *unreal*; it requires only that your deliberation be causally efficacious — that your reasoning process be among the causes of your action. Whether the future exists in a four-dimensional manifold is orthogonal to whether your deliberation matters to what happens next.

This debate is not resolved, but it is important to note that the block universe does not straightforwardly eliminate free will. It changes the *picture* within which free will must be understood, but whether it eliminates free will depends on additional premises about what free will requires.
