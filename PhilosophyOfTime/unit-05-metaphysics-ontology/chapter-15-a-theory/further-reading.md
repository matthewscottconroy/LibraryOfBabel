## Annotated Entries

**Broad, C. D. 1923. *Scientific Thought*. London: Kegan Paul. Chapter 2.**
The original formulation of the growing block view, written with Broad's characteristic care and precision. Chapter 2, "Ostensible Temporality," introduces the view and discusses its implications. Broad is always worth reading for his honesty about what remains problematic in his own positions.

**Markosian, Ned. 2004. "A Defense of Presentism." *Oxford Studies in Metaphysics*, vol. 1, 47–82. Oxford: Oxford University Press.**
The clearest and most accessible statement of the case for presentism in contemporary philosophy. Markosian presents the arguments honestly, acknowledges the difficulties, and proposes constructive responses. An ideal starting point for anyone new to the presentism debate.

**Bourne, Craig. 2006. *A Future for Presentism*. Oxford: Clarendon Press.**
A comprehensive examination of presentism and its competitors. Despite the title, Bourne is genuinely even-handed: he examines all temporal ontologies with equal rigor. His formulation of the epistemological problem for the growing block (Chapters 2–3) is essential and has been widely influential.

**Skow, Bradford. 2015. *Objective Becoming*. Oxford: Oxford University Press.**
The definitive contemporary defense of the moving spotlight theory. Skow takes the objections seriously and responds with technical precision. Even those unconvinced by the view will benefit from Skow's careful analysis of what objective becoming requires. Chapters 6–8 deal directly with the main objections.

**Zimmerman, Dean. 2008. "The Privileged Present: Defending an 'A-theory' of Time." In *Contemporary Debates in Metaphysics*, 211–225. Oxford: Blackwell.**
The best short defense of presentism and the neo-Lorentzian response to the relativity challenge. Zimmerman is one of the most technically capable defenders of A-theory, and this essay is an excellent introduction to his approach. Read alongside the B-theorist's responses in the same volume.

**Prior, Arthur N. 1967. *Past, Present and Future*. Oxford: Clarendon Press. Chapters 6–7.**
Prior's treatment of future contingents and the logic of the open future in formal terms. Chapters 6–7 develop the tense logic framework for handling future contingent propositions. Technically demanding but philosophically essential for anyone interested in the formal dimensions of the open future debate.

**Putnam, Hilary. 1967. "Time and Physical Geometry." *Journal of Philosophy* 64 (8): 240–247.**
The philosophical statement of the relativity challenge to presentism. Short, clear, and forceful. Required reading for understanding why the debate about presentism and physics matters. Read alongside Zimmerman's response for a complete picture of the exchange.

**Bigelow, John. 1996. "Presentism and Properties." *Philosophical Perspectives* 10: 35–52.**
An important early paper in the modern debate on the truthmaker problem for presentism. Bigelow formulates the problem clearly and surveys the main presentist strategies. His own proposal — that properties of present objects can serve as surrogates for past entities — has been widely discussed and criticized.

**Aristotle. *De Interpretatione*. Chapter 9. Translated by J. L. Ackrill. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1963.**
The original sea battle passage (18a28–18b25) is short, dense, and worth reading several times. Ackrill's translation is clear and his notes (in the Clarendon Aristotle series) are helpful. The argument is remarkably compressed; one benefit of reading primary texts is discovering how much subsequent commentary expands a few lines of original.

**Fine, Kit. 1975. "Vagueness, Truth and Logic." *Synthese* 30 (3–4): 265–300.**
The classic development of supervaluationism, primarily motivated by vagueness but with direct applications to future contingents. The paper is technically demanding — section 5 on "super-truth" is the key section — but Fine's writing is precise and the argument is worth following carefully.
