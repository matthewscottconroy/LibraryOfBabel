# Chapter 15 Important Concepts

---

**Presentism**
The metaphysical thesis that only present things exist. Past events have ceased to exist; future events do not yet exist. Reality is exhausted by what is currently the case. Presentism captures a strong commonsense intuition but faces the truthmaker problem and the challenge from special relativity.

**Growing Block View**
The metaphysical thesis that the past and present exist, but the future does not. Reality grows as new events occur, with the present constituting the leading edge of an ever-accumulating block. Associated with C.D. Broad; solves the truthmaker problem but generates an epistemological problem about knowing one's position in the block.

**Moving Spotlight Theory**
The metaphysical thesis that all times exist (like the block universe), but there is an objectively moving present — a "spotlight" that advances through the four-dimensional manifold from past to future. The present is a primitive, non-relational, mind-independent property of one time at a time.

**Truthmaker Problem**
The difficulty for presentism (and to some extent for the growing block view) of grounding past-directed truths without appealing to past entities. If the past no longer exists, what makes true propositions about the past true?

**Lucretianism about the Past**
A presentist strategy for handling the truthmaker problem: current trace-properties and causal consequences of past events serve as truthmakers for past-directed propositions. Named loosely after Lucretius's discussion in *De Rerum Natura*, but developed in modern form by philosophers like Bigelow.

**Ersatzism about the Past**
A strategy for grounding past-directed truths by appealing to abstract representations — "ersatz" past events — that exist in the present. The abstract facts encode past states of affairs without those states of affairs themselves needing to exist. A form of platonism about temporal facts.

**Neo-Lorentzian Strategy**
A presentist response to the challenge from special relativity that posits a privileged reference frame defining absolute simultaneity, even though the frame is empirically undetectable. On this view, metaphysics can outrun empirical access: there is an objective present, but we cannot identify which frame it corresponds to.

**Open Future**
The metaphysical thesis that the future is genuinely undetermined and ontologically "not yet real" — that there is no complete fact about what will happen. Associated with presentism and the growing block; challenged by the block universe view, which treats all times as equally real.

**Fatalism**
The thesis that the future is logically fixed — that every future event is necessary in the sense that it is already settled by present truths. Distinguished from determinism (which is a causal/nomological thesis): even in an indeterministic universe, fatalism could hold if bivalence applies to future contingent propositions.

**Bivalence**
The logical principle that every proposition is either true or false, with no third value. Classical logic endorses bivalence. Aristotle's sea battle argument suggests that accepting bivalence for future contingent propositions entails fatalism.

**Future Contingent**
A proposition about a future event that could go either way — the event is neither necessary nor impossible, but genuinely open. "There will be a sea battle tomorrow" is a paradigm case. Future contingents are the focus of the debate about bivalence and fatalism.

**Supervaluationism**
A logical approach to indeterminacy in which a proposition is "supertrue" if it is true in all admissible precisifications, "superfalse" if false in all, and indeterminate if true in some and false in others. Applied to future contingents, it preserves excluded middle while blocking individual truth-value assignments to future contingent propositions.

**Epistemological Problem (Growing Block)**
The challenge, raised sharply by Craig Bourne, of explaining how an agent embedded in a growing block can know they are at the leading edge rather than in the interior of an already-completed block. Since the experience of being at the edge is indistinguishable from being embedded in the middle, the growing block cannot guarantee that agents have access to their position.

**Rate Problem (Moving Spotlight)**
The problem for the moving spotlight theory of explaining at what rate the present advances. "One second per second" seems trivially true; anything more informative seems to require a second-order time parameter against which the first-order present is measured, threatening a vicious regress.

**Endurantism**
The view that ordinary objects persist through time by being wholly present at each moment of their existence — contrasted with perdurantism (worm theory), which holds that objects have temporal parts. Endurantism is natural for A-theorists, particularly presentists, who hold that objects exist entirely in the present.
