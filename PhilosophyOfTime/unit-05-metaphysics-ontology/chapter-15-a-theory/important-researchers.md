# Chapter 15 Important Researchers

---

**C. D. Broad (1887–1971)**
Charlie Broad was a British philosopher whose *Scientific Thought* (1923) introduced the growing block view of time, one of the first systematic defenses of an A-theoretic position in analytic philosophy. Broad was notable for the scrupulous care and intellectual honesty of his work; he identified both the attractions and the difficulties of his position with equal rigor. His multi-volume *An Examination of McTaggart's Philosophy* (1933–1938) remains the most thorough engagement with McTaggart available. Broad's philosophical style — slow, systematic, alert to complexity — is a model for philosophers working in the metaphysics of time.

**Dean Zimmerman (born 1963)**
Dean Zimmerman is an American philosopher at Rutgers University who is one of the most prominent contemporary defenders of A-theory, particularly of presentism. His work combines detailed engagement with the physics of special relativity (developing the neo-Lorentzian strategy) with rigorous analytical metaphysics. His essay "The Privileged Present" (2008) is the clearest short defense of presentism in the contemporary literature, and his work on the persistence of objects through time is equally important.

**Bradford Skow (born 1975)**
Bradford Skow is an American philosopher of physics at MIT whose *Objective Becoming* (2015) provides the most rigorous and systematic defense of the moving spotlight theory available. Skow takes the objections to the view seriously and responds to them with technical precision. His engagement with the physics of time — particularly quantum mechanics and the nature of temporal passage — makes the book valuable for readers interested in the intersection of philosophy and physics.

**Ned Markosian (born 1963)**
Ned Markosian is an American philosopher who has written extensively on presentism and on related issues in the metaphysics of time. His "A Defense of Presentism" (2004) is a careful and honest presentation of the view's strengths and weaknesses. Markosian is notable for his willingness to acknowledge the genuine difficulties facing presentism rather than dismissing them, and for proposing constructive responses that engage seriously with the truthmaker problem.

**Craig Bourne (born 1976)**
Craig Bourne is a British philosopher whose *A Future for Presentism* (2006) is a sustained and rigorous examination of the presentism debate. Despite its title, the book is not exclusively a defense of presentism; it examines the difficulties facing all temporal ontologies with unusual evenhandedness. Bourne's formulation of the epistemological problem for the growing block is particularly important and has been widely discussed.

**Arthur N. Prior (1914–1969)**
Prior's relevance to this chapter extends beyond his response to McTaggart's regress. His tense logic framework is the natural formal setting for A-theoretic views; it treats A-series distinctions as logically primitive rather than reducible. Prior was also deeply concerned with the metaphysics of the open future, arguing in *Past, Present and Future* (1967) that future contingent propositions are genuinely indeterminate and that Aristotle's response to the sea battle argument is defensible within a rigorous logical framework.

**Aristotle (384–322 BCE)**
Aristotle's discussion of time in *Physics* IV and his treatment of future contingents in *De Interpretatione* 9 remain essential sources. His argument about the sea battle and its implications for bivalence, fatalism, and the openness of the future has been continuously discussed for over two millennia. Aristotle's response — denying bivalence for future contingents — remains one of the live options in the contemporary debate, and his argument is notable for its logical precision, formulated centuries before formal logic existed as a discipline.

**Kit Fine (born 1946)**
Kit Fine is a British philosopher who has made major contributions to formal metaphysics, philosophy of language, and formal semantics. His development of supervaluationism — one of the main strategies for handling the logic of future contingents — is technically rigorous and philosophically important. Fine's broader work on modality, essence, and grounding has deep connections to the philosophy of time, and his engagement with questions about the structure of reality makes his work essential reading for anyone interested in the metaphysics of time.
