# Chapter 15: A-Theory — Presentism, Growing Block, and the Moving Spotlight

---

## Chapter Introduction

If you find the block universe unsatisfying — if the thought that all times are equally real, that the present is merely a perspectival indexical, and that temporal flow is either illusory or redundant strikes you as missing something essential — you are in good philosophical company. A-theory is the name for a family of views that insist on the reality and metaphysical importance of the distinction between past, present, and future. For A-theorists, the present is not just one more position in a static four-dimensional manifold; it is the site of what is *actually happening*, the locus of genuine reality.

But A-theory is not a single view. It is a family of positions that differ in precisely what they say about the ontological status of the past and future, and in how they handle the difficulties that come with the territory. This chapter examines three major members of that family.

**Presentism** is the most radical: only present things exist. The past has ceased to exist; the future does not yet exist. Reality is constituted entirely by what is presently the case. This view has an immediate appeal — it matches the common intuition that the past is gone and the future has not arrived — but it faces serious difficulties, especially regarding the truthmakers for past-directed claims and the challenge from special relativity.

**The growing block view**, developed by C.D. Broad, holds a middle position: the past and present exist, but the future does not. The universe is literally growing as new events occur, with the present as the leading edge of an ever-accumulating reality. This view captures something of the presentist's intuition about the openness of the future while preserving the existence of the past.

**The moving spotlight theory** accepts the full four-dimensional block of events (like the B-theory) but adds to it an objectively moving present — a "spotlight" that singles out one time as genuinely present, a spotlight that advances through the manifold. This view preserves the objective reality of A-series properties while acknowledging the block structure that physics seems to support.

Each view faces distinctive difficulties, and this chapter examines those difficulties honestly. The goal is not to tell you which A-theory is correct — that remains contested — but to give you the tools to evaluate them yourself.
