# 15.1.3 — The Challenge from Special Relativity

---

Beyond the truthmaker problem, presentism faces what many philosophers regard as the most serious challenge from contemporary physics: the challenge from special relativity. The challenge is not merely that special relativity makes things complicated; it is that, on a standard reading, special relativity is *inconsistent* with presentism — at least in its naive form.

## The Problem: No Global Present

Presentism requires a global, objective present: a three-dimensional "slice" of the universe that constitutes the present moment, with the past below it and the future above it. This is the ordinary picture of presentism.

But special relativity, as we saw in Chapter 14.2.1, denies that there is an observer-independent, privileged simultaneity relation. In Minkowski spacetime — the spacetime of special relativity — there is no global, frame-independent "now." For any pair of spacelike-separated events (events that could not be connected by a light signal), there exist reference frames in which the events are simultaneous, frames in which one is earlier, and frames in which the other is earlier. None of these frames is privileged.

If presentism requires that there be a determinate global present — a fact about what is simultaneous with what that holds independently of any reference frame — and if special relativity says there is no such fact, then presentism is incompatible with special relativity. That is a significant problem.

Hilary Putnam made this point forcefully: "I shall argue that the problem of the reality of the future and the problem of the status of 'future' have been in principle solved by the Special Theory of Relativity" (Putnam 1967: 240). His conclusion was that special relativity establishes eternalism.

## The Neo-Lorentzian Response

The most influential presentist response is the **neo-Lorentzian strategy**, associated with philosophers like Dean Zimmerman and physicists like John Bell. The strategy is to note that special relativity is *empirically* compatible with the existence of a privileged rest frame — a frame that corresponds to "absolute rest" — even though we cannot empirically detect which frame it is. The Lorentz ether theory is empirically equivalent to special relativity: it makes all the same experimental predictions, but it posits an underlying preferred frame that defines absolute simultaneity.

If the correct metaphysics is neo-Lorentzian, then there is an objective fact about what is simultaneous, even though our experiments cannot tell us which simultaneity relation is the correct one. The presentist's global present would correspond to the simultaneity relation of the privileged frame. The fact that we cannot identify which frame is privileged is an epistemological limitation, not an argument against the metaphysics.

Zimmerman argues that this is a coherent and defensible strategy: "The presentist should simply accept that the physics we have doesn't tell us which foliation of spacetime corresponds to objective simultaneity, while insisting that one of them does" (Zimmerman 2008: 213).

The objection to this strategy is that it seems ad hoc: we are positing a feature of reality (the privileged frame) specifically to save presentism, with no independent evidence for it. The neo-Lorentzian replies that this is the standard structure of underdetermination in science: empirical equivalence does not establish that the alternative is false, only that we cannot confirm it empirically. If independent metaphysical reasons support presentism — including the arguments of Section 15.1.1 — then the neo-Lorentzian strategy is not ad hoc but a principled reconciliation of metaphysics and physics.

## The Relativized Presentism Response

A second response, more revisionary, is **relativized presentism**: the claim that what is present is relative to a reference frame, and there is no frame-independent present. This is a deflationary move: rather than defending an objective global present, the presentist accepts that "what is present" is always relative to some frame, just as B-theorists say "what is present" is relative to an observer.

The difficulty is that this response may give away too much. If what exists is relative to a frame, then the ontological distinction between existence and non-existence is also relative to a frame. And if existence is relative to a frame, it is unclear in what sense the view is still presentist — rather than a version of the eternalism it was meant to oppose.

## Sklar's Strategy

Lawrence Sklar has argued that the challenge from special relativity is less decisive than it appears, because the metaphysics of time is not straightforwardly derivable from the kinematics of spacetime. The equations of special relativity describe the transformations between reference frames and the invariances of physical quantities; they do not, by themselves, entail an ontology. Whether one adopts a Minkowski or a Lorentzian interpretation of those equations is a further metaphysical choice (Sklar 1974: 267–271). The physics underdetermines the metaphysics, and there is space — though tight space — for the presentist to maneuver.

## Assessment

The challenge from special relativity is genuine and serious. The neo-Lorentzian response is coherent but requires positing undetectable metaphysical structure. Relativized presentism threatens to collapse into a form of eternalism. Sklar's strategy buys breathing room but does not provide a positive account of the global present.

The state of the debate is this: presentism in its naive form is incompatible with special relativity. Sophisticated versions can be made compatible, at a metaphysical cost. Whether that cost is worth paying depends on how strongly one is committed to the presentist intuitions — and on how seriously one takes the physics.
