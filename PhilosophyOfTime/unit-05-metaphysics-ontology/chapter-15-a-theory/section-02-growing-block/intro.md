# Section 15.2: The Growing Block Universe

---

## Section Introduction

The growing block view occupies a distinctive middle position in the ontology of time. It shares with presentism the intuition that the future is not yet real — that there is something genuinely open about what has not yet happened. It shares with eternalism the recognition that the past, once real, does not simply vanish — that there is something wrong about saying that Caesar and the dinosaurs are just nothing.

The result is a view in which reality grows: what exists is the totality of past and present events, and this totality is always expanding as new events occur. The present is the leading edge of this ever-accumulating reality. What is future does not yet exist — but what is present and past exists permanently, preserved in the growing block.

This section examines Broad's original formulation and the most pressing epistemological objection to the view.
