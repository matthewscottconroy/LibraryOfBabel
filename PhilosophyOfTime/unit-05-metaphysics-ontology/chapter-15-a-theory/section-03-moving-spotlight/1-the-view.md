# 15.3.1 — The Moving Spotlight: Formulation

---

The moving spotlight theory has a long lineage. McTaggart himself seems to have something like it in mind when he formulates the A-series within the context of a complete temporal manifold. Its most rigorous recent defense is Bradford Skow's *Objective Becoming* (2015), which develops the view in careful detail and responds to the major objections.

## The Core Idea

The moving spotlight theorist accepts the four-dimensional block: all times exist, all events are real in the manifold. In this respect, the view agrees with the block universe. But — and this is the essential addition — the moving spotlight theorist also holds that there is an objective, mind-independent fact about which time is *present*. This is not merely the indexical fact that a particular observer is located at a particular time; it is a feature of the temporal structure of the world itself. One time, and only one time at a time, is genuinely, objectively, non-relationally present.

The "spotlight" metaphor captures the idea: imagine the four-dimensional manifold as a darkened theater, and the present as a spotlight moving along the timeline from past to future. Each position is equally there, equally real, equally part of the manifold — but only the position illuminated by the spotlight is *now*. The spotlight moves, advancing through the manifold, and what it illuminates is what is genuinely present.

Bradford Skow describes the core commitment as follows: "On the moving spotlight theory... there is an objective fact about which time is present, and which time is present changes" (Skow 2015: 71). The present is not just a position that each temporal stage occupies relative to itself; it is a primitive property that exactly one time possesses at any given moment of "second-order" time.

## Motivations for the View

The moving spotlight theory is motivated by a specific combination of commitments that neither the block universe nor the pure A-theories can fully satisfy.

**Against presentism:** The moving spotlight theorist is uncomfortable with the apparent ontological discontinuity of presentism — the idea that as the present moves forward, entities literally come into and go out of existence. On the moving spotlight view, nothing comes into or goes out of existence; the four-dimensional manifold is complete. Change consists in the present moving through the manifold, not in the manifold itself growing or shrinking.

**Against the pure block universe:** The moving spotlight theorist is equally uncomfortable with the B-theory's treatment of the present as merely indexical. The sense that there is something objectively special about the present — not just "special for me" but special in itself — is, the moving spotlight theorist insists, tracking a real feature of reality. The block universe, by making the present merely perspectival, misses something genuine.

**For a comprehensive A+B-theory:** The moving spotlight view attempts to have the ontological comprehensiveness of the block universe and the metaphysical dynamism of the A-series. It is, in a sense, the most ambitious A-theory: it says that the A-series is real and adds nothing to the temporal manifold that the A-series requires, while preserving all the advantages of the block universe approach to ontology.

## The Non-Relational Present

A crucial feature of the moving spotlight view is that the property of being present — the property that the spotlight illuminates — is **non-relational** and **intrinsic**. It is not "present relative to me" or "present relative to this utterance." It is just present — a primitive property that the present moment has and no other moment has, independent of any perspective.

This distinguishes the view sharply from the B-theory's indexical treatment of "now." For the moving spotlight theorist, "now" picks out the time that has the objective property of being present. The reference of "now" shifts with the speaker's temporal location not because presentness is relative, but because the speaker is (we hope) at the same time as the objective present, and as the objective present moves, the speaker's temporal location (and hence the reference of their "now") also advances.
