# Chapter 16 Exercises: The Nature of the Present

---

## Comprehension and Analysis

**1.** Explain the difference between the "objective view" and the "perspectival view" of nowness. Which view does the A-theory require? Which does the B-theory require? Are there positions that fall between these options?

**2.** What is the problem of "epistemic presence" — why do we always seem to know we are in the present? How do the B-theory and the A-theory each explain this datum? Which explanation do you find more satisfying?

**3.** Explain Reichenbach's conventionality of simultaneity thesis. What is the parameter ε, and why does its value being variable imply a conventional element in simultaneity? Does this conventionality threaten the objectivity of the present, or only its global extension?

**4.** Explain the difference between extensionalism and retentionalism about the specious present. Give an example — perhaps the experience of hearing a spoken word — and show how each theory accounts for the experience. Which theory seems to you to be a better description of what actually happens phenomenologically?

**5.** Aristotle worried that a durationless present cannot ground the duration of time intervals. Reconstruct his worry precisely. Can the B-theorist resolve the worry, or does the A-theorist need the specious present to answer it?

---

## Synthetic and Argumentative

**6.** John Perry argued that certain indexicals are "essential" — their cognitive role cannot be captured by non-indexical replacements. Is "now" essentially indexical in Perry's sense? If it is, does this favor the B-theory or the A-theory? Argue for a position.

**7.** "The present of physics and the present of experience are two different concepts, and attempting to use one to constrain the other leads to confusion." Evaluate this claim. If physics and phenomenology describe different "presents," what is their relationship? Can metaphysics bridge them?

**8.** Suppose you accept that the specious present is genuinely extended — roughly 2–3 seconds of "thick now." Does this support A-theory over B-theory? Does it support presentism specifically? Or is the specious present compatible with the block universe?

## Lab and Observational Exercises

**9. Lab — Mapping the Specious Present.** Conduct the following informal experiment. Listen to a piece of music (choose something with clear rhythmic structure — a drum solo, a Bach invention, a jazz improvisation). While listening, try to notice where your "now" begins and ends: at what point does a note feel "just gone" (in the immediate past) versus "fully past" (in memory)? At what point does the music feel anticipated rather than current? Try to estimate the duration of your specious present from this exercise. Then look up empirical estimates (approximately 2–3 seconds) and compare your estimate. Write a 200-word reflection on what you found.

**10. Lab — Simultaneity in Everyday Experience.** Stand in a location where you can see and hear something happening at a distance — perhaps a hammer striking something, or a crowd clapping. Notice that you hear the sound slightly after you see the event. The experience of "simultaneous" perception at a distance involves a small but real temporal gap between light and sound signals. What does this suggest about the "simultaneity" of your present experience? Write a brief reflection (150 words) on whether this everyday experience supports the conventionality or the relativity of simultaneity.

**11. Lab — The Experience of Duration.** Sit quietly and try to experience a durationless instant — a moment with no temporal extent. Then try to experience an extended present — a "thick now" of several seconds. What do you find? Is a durationless instant experienceable at all? What happens to experience when you try to make it instantaneous? Write 200 words reflecting on what this suggests about the relationship between the mathematical present and the experienced present.

## Reflection

**12.** Consider the following: "The most immediate, undeniable fact about time is that I am in the present. Everything else — whether the past exists, whether the future is real, whether time flows — is theoretical. But *this* — being here, now — is beyond question." Do you agree? Does the certainty of being in the present provide a foundation for metaphysics of time, or is it just an indexical truism that proves nothing?
