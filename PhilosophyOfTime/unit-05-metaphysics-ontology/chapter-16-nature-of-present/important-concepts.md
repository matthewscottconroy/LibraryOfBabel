# Chapter 16 Important Concepts

---

**Objective Nowness**
The view that "nowness" — the property of being present — is a mind-independent, objective feature of reality that one and only one time possesses at any given moment. Characteristic of A-theory and the moving spotlight view. Contrasted with perspectival nowness.

**Perspectival Nowness**
The view that "nowness" is not a mind-independent feature of reality but is relative to an observer's temporal location — analogous to the way "hereness" is relative to a spatial location. Characteristic of B-theory and the indexical analysis of "now."

**Epistemic Presence**
The phenomenon that conscious agents always seem to know they are in the present: wherever and whenever one is located, one knows that that time is "now." The B-theorist explains this indexically; the A-theorist treats it as evidence for an objective present that consciousness is inherently attuned to.

**Essential Indexical**
An indexical expression whose cognitive and practical role cannot be captured by a non-indexical replacement without loss of content. Associated with John Perry. The question whether "now" is an essential indexical bears on whether tensed knowledge is irreducible to B-theoretic knowledge.

**Specious Present**
William James's term for the brief temporal interval — approximately 2–3 seconds — that is experienced as an extended "now" rather than a durationless instant. The specious present is the unit of temporal integration in perception: temporal sequences within it are experienced as co-present rather than as succession.

**Extensionalism (about temporal experience)**
The view that the specious present is genuinely temporally extended: the experience itself occupies an interval of time, and temporal sequence is directly perceived rather than inferred or retained. Associated with Barry Dainton.

**Retentionalism (about temporal experience)**
The view that the sense of temporal extension in experience is produced by a retention mechanism: the just-past is represented in the present experience as "just past," creating the experiential sense of temporal flow without the experience itself being temporally extended. Associated with Edmund Husserl.

**Retention**
In Husserl's phenomenology of time, a form of immediate consciousness in which the just-past moment is "held on to" in the current experience, not as a memory but as a trace of what has just occurred. Retention is distinguished from memory proper and is what makes the experience of temporal flow possible on the retentionalist account.

**Protention**
In Husserl's phenomenology, the anticipatory component of temporal experience: the "leaning forward" of consciousness toward the immediate future. Protention makes the experience of temporal direction possible — we do not just experience the present as past-plus-now, but as directed toward what is about to occur.

**Conventionality of Simultaneity**
Reichenbach's thesis that the determination of which distant events are simultaneous with a given local event involves a conventional choice — specifically, the choice of the one-way speed of light convention represented by the parameter ε. Different choices of ε give different simultaneity relations, all consistent with the same observable facts.

**Einstein Synchrony Convention**
The standard convention (ε = 1/2) in which the one-way speed of light is assumed to be the same in all directions. This is the conventionidentified with Einstein's own 1905 treatment and is the "natural" choice in Minkowski spacetime geometry.

**Local Present**
The temporal stage of an observer at a given moment — what is happening "here and now" for a particular observer. The local present is less subject to conventionality and frame-relativity than the *global* present (what is present across distant regions). A modest A-theory might claim objectivity for the local present while accepting convention-relativity for its global extension.

**Mathematical Instant**
The durationless point on the real number line that represents a moment of time in mathematical physics. The mathematical instant has zero temporal extent — it is not an interval of any positive duration. Physics uses instantaneous states as the fundamental objects in its equations.

**Thick Present**
A present with positive temporal duration — contrasted with the durationless "thin present" of mathematical physics. The specious present is a "thick" present. Whether the metaphysical present is thick or thin is a question at the intersection of phenomenology and metaphysics.

**Perceptual Binding**
The neural process by which multimodal perceptual inputs (sight, sound, touch) are integrated into a single, unified perceptual event. The time required for perceptual binding — on the order of a few hundred milliseconds — contributes to the specious present's duration and variability.
