# Chapter 17 Important Concepts

---

**Linear Time**
The topological model in which time is isomorphic to the real number line (or a subset of it): totally ordered, with no loops, transitive, asymmetric, and irreflexive. The standard assumption in Western philosophy and classical physics.

**Circular Time**
The topological model in which time is isomorphic to a circle: every event is eventually "after" every other event in the global sense, and the timeline loops back to its own beginning. Raises paradoxes about global temporal order, direction, and identity.

**Branching Time**
The topological model in which time has a tree structure: a unique past and multiple possible future continuations. Used to model indeterminism and the openness of the future. Each branch represents a possible continuation of the world from a given moment.

**Discrete Time**
A temporal ordering in which there are adjacent moments — moments with nothing between them — analogous to the integers. Suggests a minimum temporal unit (chronon). Would dissolve Zeno's paradoxes but requires re-analysis of motion and change as sequences of discrete jumps.

**Dense Time**
A temporal ordering in which between any two distinct moments there is always another, analogous to the rational numbers. Infinitely divisible without being necessarily complete. Standard in classical physics when modeled as the rationals (though physics uses the reals for continuity).

**Continuous Time**
A dense temporal ordering that is also complete (every bounded Cauchy sequence converges to a limit in the ordering), analogous to the real numbers. The standard assumption of classical and relativistic physics. Has no gaps and no adjacent moments.

**Planck Time**
The smallest temporal interval at which current physical theories give coherent predictions, approximately 5.39 × 10⁻⁴⁴ seconds. Theories of quantum gravity suggest that spacetime may be fundamentally discrete at the Planck scale, making continuous time an emergent approximation.

**Zeno's Dichotomy**
Zeno's argument that motion is impossible because to traverse any distance, one must first traverse half of it, then half the remainder, generating an infinite series of sub-journeys. The mathematical response uses convergent infinite series; the discrete time response dissolves the puzzle by eliminating infinite divisibility.

**Zeno's Arrow**
Zeno's argument that a moving arrow is at rest at each instant, and so cannot be in motion at all. The standard response uses the concept of instantaneous velocity (the limit of average velocities). The discrete time response eliminates instantaneous states and re-analyzes motion as transitions between discrete temporal atoms.

**Closed Timelike Curve (CTC)**
A trajectory in spacetime that is always locally future-directed (within the future light cone) but globally returns to its own starting point. A CTC is a physically possible "time machine" trajectory. Gödel's 1949 universe was the first solution to Einstein's equations shown to contain CTCs.

**Gödel Universe**
A solution to Einstein's field equations describing a rotating, homogeneous universe in which every event is in the causal past of every other. Demonstrates that CTCs are consistent with general relativity and that temporal topology is physically contingent.

**Chronology Protection Conjecture**
Hawking's proposal that the laws of physics (including quantum gravitational effects) prevent the formation of CTCs in any physically realistic spacetime. If true, the existence of CTCs is physically forbidden even though it is not forbidden by general relativity alone.

**Kant's Antinomy of Time**
Kant's argument in the *Critique of Pure Reason* that both the thesis "the world had a beginning in time" and the antithesis "the world had no beginning in time" can be supported by apparently valid arguments, suggesting that the question cannot be answered by pure reason and that time is a form of intuition rather than a feature of things-in-themselves.

**No-Boundary Proposal (Hartle-Hawking)**
The quantum cosmological proposal that the Big Bang singularity is replaced by a smooth, closed Euclidean geometry with no boundary. In imaginary time, the universe has no beginning; in real time, the Big Bang appears as a smooth initial boundary. Makes the question "what came before the Big Bang?" ill-formed within the theory.

**Settled Truth**
In branching time semantics, a proposition is settled true at a time if it is true at that time on all future branches — i.e., regardless of how the future develops. Contrasted with historical truth, which may vary across branches.
