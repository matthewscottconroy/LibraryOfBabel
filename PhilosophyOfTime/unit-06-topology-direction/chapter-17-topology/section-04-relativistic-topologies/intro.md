# Section 17.4: Relativistic Topologies — Gödel's Universe

---

## Section Introduction

General relativity is not merely a theory of gravity; it is a theory of the geometry of spacetime. And the geometry of spacetime, according to general relativity, is not fixed in advance but depends on the distribution of matter and energy. This means that, in principle, the *topology* of spacetime — including the topology of its temporal dimension — is a physical question, not just a mathematical or metaphysical one. Different matter distributions produce different spacetime topologies.

The most philosophically startling consequence of this flexibility was demonstrated by Kurt Gödel in 1949: he showed that Einstein's equations admit a solution — a rotating universe — in which every event is in the causal past of every other event. Time, in Gödel's universe, loops back on itself.
