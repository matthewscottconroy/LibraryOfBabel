# Chapter 18 Exercises: The Arrow of Time

---

## Comprehension and Analysis

**1.** Explain, in your own words, why the time-symmetry of the fundamental laws of physics creates a puzzle about temporal asymmetry. What would have to be true for the puzzle not to arise — i.e., what would a fundamentally time-asymmetric physics look like, and why don't we have one?

**2.** Formulate Loschmidt's Reversibility Objection precisely. What exactly does it show about the relationship between the Second Law and the underlying mechanics? Does it show that the Second Law is false, or merely that it cannot be derived from time-symmetric mechanics alone?

**3.** State the Past Hypothesis in Albert's formulation. What are the three ingredients that, combined with the Past Hypothesis, explain the thermodynamic arrow? Does the Past Hypothesis explain the arrow or merely redescribe it?

**4.** Explain the Boltzmann Brain problem. Why does Boltzmann's equilibrium fluctuation explanation generate the problem? How does Albert's Past Hypothesis avoid it?

**5.** Reconstruct Reichenbach's conjunctive fork argument. Why does the fork structure point backward in time (toward common causes) rather than forward (toward common effects)? Is this asymmetry in the causal structure fundamental, or is it derived from the thermodynamic arrow?

---

## Synthetic and Argumentative

**6.** Price argues that the causal theory of the arrow of time is circular: causation already presupposes temporal direction. Evaluate this objection. Can the causal theorist respond without circularity? What would a non-circular causal account of temporal direction look like?

**7.** Lewis argues that the asymmetry of memory — we remember the past, not the future — is explained by the physical asymmetry of overdetermination. Arthur Prior's observation about past versus future suffering suggests that our psychological relationship to time involves something more than mere records. Who has the better argument?

**8.** Consider two possible universes: (a) one identical to ours but with the thermodynamic arrow running in the opposite direction (entropy decreasing toward the future), and (b) one with no thermodynamic arrow at all (entropy is the same in all directions). What would experience be like in each? Could there be beings with memories and anticipations in universe (b)?

## Lab and Observational Exercises

**9. Lab — Documenting the Thermodynamic Arrow.** Choose three everyday examples of the Second Law in action and observe them during the course of a day: ice melting in a drink, a hot cup of coffee cooling, a drop of food coloring spreading in water. For each, describe the process in terms of entropy: the initial low-entropy state, the final high-entropy state, and why the process doesn't run in reverse. Write a 200-word reflection on what it would look like to see these processes run backward, and whether the reversed processes would be *physically* impossible or merely overwhelmingly improbable.

**10. Lab — Memory and Anticipation.** Spend ten minutes writing about a significant event from your past and about a significant anticipated event in your future. Then compare the two descriptions. What is qualitatively different about the two kinds of writing? Does the past event feel more "real" or "fixed"? Does the anticipated event feel more "open" or "uncertain"? Write a 200-word reflection connecting your observations to the debate about the psychological arrow.

**11. Lab — The Arrow in Film and Narrative.** Watch a short film clip or read a scene of a novel that has been artificially time-reversed (or imagine a short "film" of some physical process running backward). Identify all the features of the reversed sequence that feel "wrong" — physically or narratively. Which of the temporal arrows (thermodynamic, causal, psychological) are violated? This is a concrete exercise in identifying the various components of the arrow of time.

## Reflection

**12.** The Past Hypothesis says the universe began in an extraordinarily low-entropy state — a state so special that its probability, by any statistical measure, is effectively zero. Does this specialness of the initial state require a cosmological explanation? An explanatory appeal to God or some form of cosmic purpose? Or is it just a brute fact? Write a half-page reflection on what kinds of explanation (physical, philosophical, theological) you find satisfying in this case.
