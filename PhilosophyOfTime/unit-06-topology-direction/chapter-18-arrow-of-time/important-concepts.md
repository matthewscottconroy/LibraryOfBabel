# Chapter 18 Important Concepts

---

**Arrow of Time**
The directional asymmetry of time: the fact that past and future are not interchangeable, that physical processes are overwhelmingly irreversible, and that our cognitive relationship to the past and future is radically different. The puzzle of the arrow of time is explaining this asymmetry given that the fundamental laws of physics are (nearly) time-symmetric.

**Time-Reversal Symmetry**
A property of a physical law: if a process P is a solution to the law, then the time-reversed process P* (P with all velocities reversed and time parameter reversed) is also a solution. The fundamental laws of classical mechanics, quantum mechanics, electromagnetism, and general relativity are time-reversal symmetric (with the small exception of CP violation in the weak force).

**Second Law of Thermodynamics**
The law that in an isolated system, entropy never decreases. In Clausius's macroscopic formulation: heat spontaneously flows from hot to cold; irreversible processes occur spontaneously in one direction only. In Boltzmann's statistical formulation: entropy (as a measure of microstates) overwhelmingly tends to increase because high-entropy states are vastly more probable.

**Entropy**
In thermodynamics: a measure of the "disorder" or unavailability of energy in a system. In Boltzmann's statistical mechanics: S = k_B log W, where W is the number of microstates compatible with a given macrostate. Higher entropy means more microstates — more "ways to be" disordered — and is therefore the overwhelmingly probable outcome of dynamical evolution.

**Macrostate**
A coarse-grained description of a physical system in terms of macroscopic variables (temperature, pressure, volume). Multiple microstates may correspond to a single macrostate. The number of microstates corresponding to a macrostate determines its entropy.

**Microstate**
A complete specification of the state of a physical system at the level of its individual components (positions and momenta of all molecules). The underlying dynamics governs the evolution of microstates; the macrostate emerges from the microstate description.

**Loschmidt's Reversibility Objection**
Josef Loschmidt's 1876 argument that the Second Law cannot be derived from time-symmetric mechanics, because for every entropy-increasing trajectory there is a time-reversed entropy-decreasing one. Shows that the thermodynamic arrow requires additional input — specifically, an assumption about the initial conditions — beyond the dynamical laws.

**Past Hypothesis**
David Albert's name for the postulate that the universe began in a state of very low entropy. The Past Hypothesis, combined with the dynamical laws and the Statistical Postulate, explains the thermodynamic arrow of time. The postulate is added to the fundamental physics; it is not derived from the dynamical laws.

**Statistical Postulate**
Albert's name for the assumption that physical probabilities are uniform over the phase space of microstates compatible with the current macrostate and the Past Hypothesis. Together with the dynamical laws and the Past Hypothesis, it generates statistical mechanical predictions for the evolution of thermodynamic systems.

**Poincaré Recurrence Theorem**
The theorem that any finite mechanical system with bounded energy will, given enough time, return arbitrarily close to its initial state. This seems to imply that entropy will eventually decrease, contradicting the Second Law. The resolution is that the recurrence time is so astronomically large as to be operationally irrelevant.

**Boltzmann Brain**
A hypothetical minimal observer that pops into existence by random thermal fluctuation, complete with memories and experiences but without a physical history. Because small fluctuations are more probable than large ones, a universe that explains observers by fluctuation from equilibrium predicts Boltzmann Brains are overwhelmingly more probable than observers like us.

**Thermodynamic Arrow**
The macroscopic temporal asymmetry associated with the Second Law: entropy increases toward the future. Explanatorily central among the arrows of time because it appears to ground several others (radiation arrow, psychological arrow, causal arrow).

**Cosmological Arrow**
The temporal asymmetry associated with the expansion of the universe: the universe is larger and more diffuse in the future direction, smaller and denser in the past direction. Related to the thermodynamic arrow via the low-entropy initial condition.

**Psychological Arrow**
The temporal asymmetry in cognitive states: we have memories of the past and anticipations of the future, not vice versa. We feel that the past is fixed and the future is open. Lewis argued this is derived from the physical asymmetry of overdetermination (records of the past are physically ubiquitous; records of the future are not).

**Conjunctive Fork**
Reichenbach's term for the causal structure in which two correlated events are explained by a common cause in their past. The fork opens toward the future (the common cause is behind the correlated events). Reichenbach argued this asymmetric structure constitutes the direction of time.

**Asymmetry of Overdetermination**
Lewis's term for the fact that past events are "overdetermined" by their future traces (many independent physical records of them exist) while future events are not similarly overdetermined by their past causes. This asymmetry explains why we have records of the past but not the future.
