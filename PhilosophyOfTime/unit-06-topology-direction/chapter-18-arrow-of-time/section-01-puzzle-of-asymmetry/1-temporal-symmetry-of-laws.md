# 18.1.1 — Time-Symmetric Laws, Time-Asymmetric World

---

The puzzle of the arrow of time begins with a striking contrast between the laws of physics and the world they govern.

## The Symmetry of Fundamental Laws

The fundamental laws of classical mechanics (Newton's laws), electromagnetism (Maxwell's equations), quantum mechanics (Schrödinger equation), and general relativity are all time-reversal symmetric — or more precisely, they are invariant under time-reversal: if you replace t with -t (run time backward), the equations remain valid. If a physical trajectory is a solution to the equations, then the time-reversed trajectory (every velocity reversed, time running backward) is also a solution.

This means that, at the level of fundamental physics, there is no built-in distinction between past and future. The laws do not prefer one time direction over the other. A collision between two gas molecules, run forward and run backward, is equally consistent with the laws.

There is one known exception: the weak nuclear force exhibits a small time-reversal asymmetry (CP violation). This has been detected in the decay of certain particles (kaons, B-mesons). But this asymmetry is tiny and plays no known role in the macroscopic asymmetries we observe in thermodynamics, psychology, and cosmology. The macroscopic arrows of time are not explained by CP violation.

## The Asymmetry of Experience

Against this background of time-symmetric laws, the world presents us with dramatic and pervasive asymmetries.

**Thermodynamic asymmetry:** Hot objects cool; cold objects do not spontaneously heat up. A drop of ink diffuses through water; it does not spontaneously reassemble. A broken egg does not spontaneously unbreak. These processes happen reliably in one time direction and essentially never in the other.

**Cosmological asymmetry:** The universe is expanding. In the past, it was denser and hotter; in the future, it will be more diffuse and cooler. The cosmological evolution has a definite direction.

**Radiation asymmetry:** Waves spread outward from their sources (ripples from a dropped stone, light from a lamp). We do not observe waves spontaneously contracting inward to a focus. The radiation arrow points outward from causes.

**Psychological asymmetry:** We have memories of the past and anticipations of the future, not vice versa. We feel that the past is fixed and the future is open. Our deliberation and planning are directed toward the future.

**Causal asymmetry:** Causes precede their effects. We identify explanatory factors in the past; we do not routinely identify explanatory factors in the future.

## The Puzzle

The puzzle is the gap between these two observations. The laws are symmetric; the world is asymmetric. How does a symmetric physics produce asymmetric phenomena?

This is not a minor puzzle that can be dissolved by more careful attention to the laws. The laws genuinely are symmetric at the fundamental level; the asymmetry genuinely does characterize macro-level processes. Explaining the transition from symmetric fundamental physics to asymmetric macro-physics is one of the deepest challenges in the foundations of science.

David Albert, one of the most important contemporary workers on this problem, puts it starkly: "The question of the arrow of time is the question of what, if anything, distinguishes the temporal direction from present toward past from the temporal direction from present toward future, given that all of the fundamental dynamical laws are completely time-reversal invariant" (Albert 2000: 5).
