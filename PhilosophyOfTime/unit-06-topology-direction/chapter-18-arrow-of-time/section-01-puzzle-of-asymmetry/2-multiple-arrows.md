# 18.1.2 — The Menagerie of Arrows

---

The phrase "arrow of time" is often used as if there were one thing being named. In fact, there are at least five distinct temporal asymmetries that deserve separate characterization. Whether they are all manifestations of a single underlying arrow, or whether they are partly independent, is itself a deep and contested question.

## The Thermodynamic Arrow

The **thermodynamic arrow** is the asymmetry associated with the Second Law of Thermodynamics: in isolated systems, entropy tends to increase (or at least not decrease) over time. Entropy is a measure of disorder or, in Boltzmann's statistical interpretation, of the number of microstates compatible with a given macrostate. High-entropy states are "typical" in the sense that most microstates correspond to them; low-entropy states are "special."

The thermodynamic arrow is the most physically fundamental of the macroscopic arrows. It explains why broken eggs do not reassemble (the broken state has vastly higher entropy than the unbroken state), why ink diffuses (the mixed state has much higher entropy than the unmixed state), and why heat flows from hot to cold (the equal-temperature state has higher entropy than the temperature-differential state).

## The Cosmological Arrow

The **cosmological arrow** is the direction in which the universe is expanding. The universe began in a very small, dense, low-entropy state (the Big Bang) and has been expanding and evolving toward higher entropy ever since. The cosmological arrow points in the direction of expansion.

The relationship between the cosmological and thermodynamic arrows is non-trivial. Is the thermodynamic arrow caused by the cosmological expansion? Or are both expressions of the same underlying initial condition (the low-entropy Big Bang)? Albert (2000: Ch. 5) argues that the cosmological expansion is not directly responsible for the thermodynamic arrow; what matters is the low entropy of the initial state.

## The Radiation Arrow

The **radiation arrow** is the asymmetry in wave propagation: disturbances propagate *outward* from their sources as diverging waves, not *inward* as converging waves. A stone dropped in water produces outgoing ripples; we do not observe ingoing ripples spontaneously converging on a point where a stone pops out of the water.

The radiation arrow is particularly interesting because it seems to be explicable in terms of the initial conditions (waves spread outward because the source is localized and the surrounding medium is initially undisturbed) rather than any asymmetry in the laws of wave propagation (which are symmetric). This connects the radiation arrow to the Past Hypothesis (Section 18.3).

## The Psychological Arrow

The **psychological arrow** is the asymmetry in our cognitive states: we have memories of the past and anticipations of the future, not vice versa. We feel that the past is fixed and the future is open. Section 18.6 examines whether this arrow is derived from the physical arrows or is independent.

## The Causal Arrow

The **causal arrow** is the asymmetry in causal relations: causes precede their effects. We identify causes in the past, effects in the future. Section 18.5 examines Reichenbach's attempt to ground the causal arrow in the thermodynamic arrow, and Chapter 19 examines whether the causal arrow can ground the temporal arrow.

## Are the Arrows Unified?

A central question in the philosophy of the arrow of time is whether these five arrows are all manifestations of a single underlying asymmetry, or whether some of them are independent.

The standard view (associated with Albert, Penrose, and Carroll) is that all the macroscopic arrows trace back to the thermodynamic arrow, which in turn traces back to the low-entropy initial condition of the universe (the Past Hypothesis). The cosmological arrow, radiation arrow, and psychological arrow are all consequences of the thermodynamic asymmetry, which is itself a consequence of the special initial state.

Huw Price dissents: he argues that the causal arrow cannot be reduced to the thermodynamic arrow without circularity, and that the psychological arrow involves distinctive features not derivable from physics alone (Price 1996: Chs. 6–7).

Whether the arrows are unified is one of the most important open questions in the field.
