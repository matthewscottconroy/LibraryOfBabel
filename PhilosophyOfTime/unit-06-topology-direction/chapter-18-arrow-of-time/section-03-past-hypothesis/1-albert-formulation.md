# 18.3.1 — The Past Hypothesis: Albert's Formulation

---

David Albert's *Time and Chance* (2000) is the most philosophically careful and comprehensive treatment of the thermodynamic arrow of time in recent literature. Albert argues that the arrow of time requires three ingredients: the dynamical laws, a uniform probability distribution over microstates compatible with a given macrostate (the "Statistical Postulate"), and the Past Hypothesis.

## The Past Hypothesis Stated

The **Past Hypothesis** is the claim that the universe began in a state of extremely low entropy. Specifically, Albert formulates it as the postulate that the macrocondition of the world in the distant past — at or near the Big Bang — was one of very low thermodynamic entropy relative to its current state (Albert 2000: 96).

This postulate, combined with the Statistical Postulate (probabilities are uniform over the phase space of microstates compatible with the current macrostate) and the dynamical laws, explains why entropy increases toward the future: we are living in a universe that started in an extraordinarily special (low-entropy) initial state, and the dynamics have been "unwinding" this specialness ever since, with entropy increasing as more and more of the phase space compatible with the macroscopic constraints is accessed.

The Past Hypothesis also explains why we should trust records of the past. Given the low-entropy initial condition, the standard statistical inference works properly: the past really was lower in entropy than the present, and our memories and records really do track what actually happened. Without the Past Hypothesis, time-symmetric statistical reasoning would give the absurd conclusion that our memories are unreliable.

## What the Past Hypothesis Explains

Once the Past Hypothesis is in place, it explains the major macroscopic arrows of time.

**The thermodynamic arrow:** Entropy increases from the Past Hypothesis's initial low-entropy state toward the future. High-entropy states vastly outnumber low-entropy states; once a low-entropy state is "unshackled" by the dynamical evolution, it overwhelmingly tends toward higher entropy.

**The radiation arrow:** Outgoing (diverging) radiation patterns are overwhelmingly more probable than incoming (converging) ones, given a smooth low-entropy past. The radiation arrow follows from the thermodynamic arrow.

**The reliability of records:** Because the past was low-entropy and causally structured, records (memories, traces, fossils, written histories) reliably track past events. This is what makes inference from the present to the past reliable. Without the Past Hypothesis, we would have no reason to trust our records.

## The Quantitative Character of the Low-Entropy Initial State

Roger Penrose has calculated the initial entropy of the universe. The observable universe has a current entropy of roughly 10^(10^80) (a 1 followed by 10^80 zeros) in natural units. The Penrose estimate of the entropy of the initial state is incomparably smaller — something like 10^(10^123) times smaller, in terms of the probability of this state occurring by chance (Penrose 2004: 762–765).

These numbers are difficult to conceptualize, but the point is clear: the initial state of the universe was extraordinarily, unimaginably special. If you randomly selected a microstate consistent with the current macrostate of the universe, the probability of it being one that evolved from a state as low-entropy as the Big Bang state is effectively zero. The universe's initial state is not just "moderately special"; it is special to a degree that strains any ordinary probabilistic intuition.
