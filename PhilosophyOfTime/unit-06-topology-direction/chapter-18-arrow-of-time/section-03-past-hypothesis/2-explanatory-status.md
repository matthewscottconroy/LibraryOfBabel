# 18.3.2 — The Explanatory Status of the Past Hypothesis

---

The Past Hypothesis explains the thermodynamic arrow by invoking the low entropy of the universe's initial state. But does it *explain* the arrow, or merely *describe* it? And even if it is a genuine explanation, the Past Hypothesis itself requires explanation: why was the initial entropy so low?

## Explanation or Redescription?

Albert is explicit that the Past Hypothesis is an additional postulate — not derived from the dynamical laws, not reducible to anything more fundamental within current physics. It is a boundary condition that must be added to the fundamental physical theory to get the right phenomenology.

Is this explanatory? In one sense, yes: the Past Hypothesis, together with the Statistical Postulate and the dynamical laws, generates a complete and accurate description of the thermodynamic arrow. It predicts that entropy increases toward the future, that records are reliable, that the radiation arrow points outward. This is explanatory in the hypothetico-deductive sense.

But in another sense, it is disappointing: the Past Hypothesis itself is not explained. We are told that the universe started in a very low-entropy state, but we are not told *why*. The initial low entropy is simply posited as a brute fact. This is sometimes called the "simple Past Hypothesis": it states the initial condition without explaining it.

Huw Price is skeptical of this approach. He argues that the Past Hypothesis, far from explaining the arrow of time, merely points to where the asymmetry originates — in the initial state — without telling us why the initial state was special. The explanation is incomplete: a full explanation would account for why the initial conditions had the character they did (Price 1996: 29–36).

## The Deeper Question: Why Was the Initial Entropy Low?

The deeper question — why the initial entropy of the universe was so low — is one of the most important unsolved problems in cosmology and philosophy of physics.

Several responses have been proposed.

**Brute fact:** The low initial entropy is simply a brute fact about the universe, not requiring further explanation. Some cosmological parameters are simply what they are. Roger Penrose, who calculated the extraordinarily small probability of the initial state, treats this as a genuine cosmic mystery but does not claim it can be explained within current physics (Penrose 2004: 762–765).

**Cosmological dynamics:** Perhaps there is a deeper dynamical explanation — some feature of the pre-Big Bang physics, or of quantum gravity, that *generates* low-entropy initial conditions. Inflationary cosmology suggests that the universe underwent a period of exponential expansion in its early history, which may have produced the smooth, low-entropy initial state that the Past Hypothesis requires. But whether inflation genuinely explains the low initial entropy or merely shifts the problem back to the initial conditions for inflation is debated.

**Boltzmann's equilibrium explanation:** Boltzmann himself proposed that the low-entropy state of our universe is a fluctuation from a state of thermal equilibrium. In an infinite universe at thermal equilibrium, occasional fluctuations to low-entropy states will occur by chance. Our universe is the result of such a fluctuation. The problem with this explanation is that it generates the Boltzmann Brain worry (Section 18.4).

**Sean Carroll's approach:** Carroll (2010) argues that we should look for a cosmological explanation of the low initial entropy in terms of the dynamics of a very large-scale (perhaps eternal) multiverse, in which universes with various initial conditions are generated, and we happen to inhabit one with an unusually low initial entropy. This is a cosmological anthropic argument.

## Philosophical Assessment

The explanatory status of the Past Hypothesis remains contested. What is clear is that the Past Hypothesis is necessary for a consistent account of the thermodynamic arrow: without it, the time-symmetric dynamics gives the wrong retrodictions about the past. What is unclear is whether the Past Hypothesis is a satisfying explanation or a placeholder for a deeper cosmological account.

The philosophical lesson is that the arrow of time is a cosmological problem, not just a local physical one. Understanding why time has a direction requires understanding why the universe began as it did.
