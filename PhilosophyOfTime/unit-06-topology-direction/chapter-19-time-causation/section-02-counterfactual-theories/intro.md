# Section 19.2: Counterfactual Theories of Causation

---

## Section Introduction

Lewis's counterfactual theory of causation is the most influential contemporary development of the Humean tradition. On this account, causation is analyzed in terms of counterfactual dependence: C causes E iff, had C not occurred, E would not have occurred. This section develops the theory and examines its treatment of the temporal asymmetry of causation, as well as the standard problem cases.
