# Section 19.4: Backward Causation

---

## Section Introduction

If temporal priority is not a conceptual requirement of causation but a contingent feature of the actual causal structure of the world, then backward causation — causes in the future, effects in the past — is at least conceptually coherent. This section examines Dummett's philosophical argument for the coherence of backward causation, the bilking objection, and the Wheeler-Feynman absorber theory as a physical case study.
