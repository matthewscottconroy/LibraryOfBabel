# Important Concepts: Chapter 20

**Absolute Time**
Newton's concept of a time that "flows equably without relation to anything external." Absolute time is independent of matter, motion, and observers; it provides the universal background against which all physical processes are measured. The most fundamental feature of absolute time is its non-dynamical character: nothing in the physical world causes it to flow, speed up, slow down, or stop.

**Relative Time (Common Time)**
Newton's term for the time we actually measure with physical clocks — pendulums, celestial motions, spring mechanisms. Relative time is always an approximation to absolute time, potentially irregular, and always dependent on the physical process used to measure it. Newton regarded relative time as a practical tool that may or may not accurately track the true absolute duration beneath it.

**Non-Dynamical Time**
A time that is not part of the dynamics of a physical system — not determined by the equations of motion, not affected by the distribution of matter, not interacting with the physical degrees of freedom. Newtonian time is non-dynamical: it is the external parameter against which the system evolves, rather than a variable the system determines. Contrasts with the dynamical time of general relativity.

**Time as Parameter**
In Newtonian mechanics, time appears in the equations of motion as the independent variable against which all other variables are measured. It is not a dependent variable determined by the dynamics; it is given externally. This is what it means for time to be a parameter rather than a dynamical variable.

**Time-Reversal Symmetry (T-Symmetry)**
The property of a physical law that the law remains unchanged under the transformation that replaces the time variable *t* with *-t* (combined, in some formulations, with appropriate sign reversals of other quantities). Newton's second law is T-symmetric. T-symmetry means that the fundamental laws of classical mechanics do not distinguish past from future.

**T-Violation**
The failure of time-reversal symmetry. The weak nuclear force violates T-symmetry (and CP-symmetry), meaning that certain decay processes at the particle level are not symmetric under time reversal. T-violation is confirmed experimentally but is far too small in magnitude to account for the macroscopic thermodynamic arrow of time.

**CP Symmetry and the CPT Theorem**
Charge conjugation (C) swaps particles with antiparticles; parity (P) reflects spatial coordinates. The weak force violates CP symmetry. The CPT theorem, a fundamental result in quantum field theory, states that the combination of charge conjugation, parity, and time reversal must always be a symmetry. Therefore, CP violation implies T violation.

**Mach's Principle**
The principle, advocated by Ernst Mach, that the inertial properties of matter — in particular, the resistance to acceleration — should be determined entirely by the distribution of all other matter in the universe, not by absolute space. Mach argued that absolute space and time have no empirical content and should be replaced by purely relational concepts.

**Relational Time**
The view, traceable to Leibniz and developed by Mach and Barbour, that time is not an independent entity but an abstraction from the pattern of change among physical objects. On the relational view, there is no meaningful question about "how much time would pass in an empty universe," because time just *is* the structure of change among actual physical processes.

**Intrinsic Time**
In the Barbour-Bertotti framework, intrinsic time is a measure of duration derived from the dynamical state of the physical system itself, without reference to any external absolute time standard. It is the parameterization of trajectories that makes the relational equations of motion as simple as possible — analogous to astronomical ephemeris time but derived from first principles.

**Extrinsic Time**
The absolute, external time of Newton — a standard imposed from outside the physical system. Extrinsic time is not derived from the system's dynamics but is given as an independent background structure. Barbour contrasts extrinsic time with intrinsic time to motivate the relational program.

**Noether's Theorem**
Emmy Noether's 1918 result that every continuous symmetry of a physical system's action corresponds to a conserved quantity. Time-translation symmetry (the laws are the same at all times) corresponds to conservation of energy; spatial translation symmetry corresponds to conservation of linear momentum; rotational symmetry corresponds to conservation of angular momentum.

**Time-Translation Symmetry**
The symmetry of a physical system or set of laws under shifts of the time coordinate: replacing *t* with *t + τ* for any constant *τ* leaves the equations of motion unchanged. Physically, this means the laws of physics are the same today as they were yesterday. By Noether's theorem, this symmetry corresponds to the conservation of energy.

**Conservation of Energy**
The principle that the total energy of an isolated system remains constant over time. By Noether's theorem, this is a consequence of time-translation symmetry. Importantly, energy is not globally conserved in an expanding universe, because the cosmological background breaks time-translation symmetry.

**Sensorium Dei**
Newton's term, used in private manuscripts and alluded to in the *Principia* and *Opticks*, for absolute space and time understood as God's sensory framework — the medium through which an omnipresent God perceives and is present to all events. The theological grounding for Newton's absolute space and time. Later criticized by Leibniz as making God a spatial being and confusing the divine with the physical.

**Ephemeris Time**
A timekeeping standard used in astronomy that defines the duration of the second in terms of the orbital period of the Earth around the Sun (as computed from celestial mechanics), rather than the rotation of the Earth. Ephemeris time is a practical realization of the Machian idea that time should be derived from actual physical motions rather than a hypothetical absolute standard.
