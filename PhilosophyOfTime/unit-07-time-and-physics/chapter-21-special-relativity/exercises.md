# Chapter 21 Exercises

## Conceptual and Analytical Exercises

**1. The Postulates Unpacked**
(a) State Einstein's two postulates of special relativity in your own words. (b) Which postulate was already recognized (in a version) by Galileo? Which is genuinely new to Einstein? (c) Why did the null result of the Michelson-Morley experiment create a theoretical crisis? What were the options for resolving it, and why did Einstein's choice turn out to be the most fruitful?

**2. The Lorentz Factor**
Calculate the Lorentz factor γ for the following speeds: (a) v = 0.1c (10% of the speed of light), (b) v = 0.5c, (c) v = 0.9c, (d) v = 0.99c, (e) v = 0.999c. Comment on the rate at which γ increases as v approaches c. At what speed does γ = 2? At what speed does γ = 10?

**3. Relativity of Simultaneity**
Two firecrackers explode simultaneously in Alice's frame, one at *x = 0* and one at *x = 3c·s* (three light-seconds apart), both at *t = 0*. Bob is moving relative to Alice at *v = 0.6c*. Using the Lorentz transformation, calculate the time coordinates of each explosion in Bob's frame. Which explosion does Bob say came first? By how much?

**4. The Train-Platform Experiment**
(a) Describe Einstein's train-platform thought experiment in careful detail. (b) Explain why, given Postulate 2, the train observer cannot explain her observation by saying the light from the front reached her first "because she was moving toward it." (c) What is the correct explanation, in terms of the Lorentz transformation?

**5. The Rietdijk-Putnam Argument**
Reconstruct the Rietdijk-Putnam argument in your own words, as carefully as possible. Then evaluate it: (a) Which premises seem most secure? (b) Which is most vulnerable to challenge? (c) Of the three responses discussed (Stein, neo-Lorentzian, quantum gravity), which do you find most compelling, and why?

**6. Proper Time and Coordinate Time**
Distinguish proper time from coordinate time. (a) Which is invariant under Lorentz transformations? (b) Which is what a clock actually measures? (c) Is the time elapsed in a reference frame (coordinate time) or the time registered by a moving clock (proper time) the more fundamental concept in special relativity? Defend your answer with reference to the twin paradox.

**7. The Twin Paradox**
Alice and Bob are twins. Bob travels at 0.8c to a star 4 light-years away (as measured in Alice's frame) and returns at the same speed. (a) How long does the trip take in Alice's frame? (b) How much proper time does Bob accumulate? (c) How much younger than Alice is Bob when he returns? (d) What resolves the apparent symmetry of time dilation in this case?

**8. Minkowski Geometry**
(a) What is the Minkowski spacetime interval, and why is it invariant? (b) Explain the difference between timelike, lightlike, and spacelike intervals. (c) Why is temporal order invariant for timelike-separated events but not for spacelike-separated events? (d) How does the light cone structure of Minkowski spacetime relate to the concept of causation?

**9. Is Time Really Just the Fourth Dimension?**
Minkowski said that space alone and time alone are doomed to fade into mere shadows, and only spacetime preserves reality. Does this mean time is "just a fourth dimension of space"? Write a careful analysis that identifies (a) what is true in this claim, (b) what is misleading, and (c) what distinguishes the time dimension from the spatial dimensions in Minkowski geometry.

**10. Conventionalism and Simultaneity**
Explain Reichenbach's ε-convention. (a) What is the conventional element in Einstein's synchronization procedure? (b) What is the two-way speed of light, and is it conventional? (c) What did Malament's 1977 theorem show about the conventional element? (d) Does the ε-convention undermine metaphysical conclusions about the relativity of simultaneity across frames?

## Observational and Laboratory Exercises

**11. Muon Detection Experiment**
Muons created in the upper atmosphere by cosmic ray impacts travel at about 0.98c and have a proper lifetime of 2.2 μs. (a) Calculate how far a muon should travel before decaying in the absence of time dilation. (b) Calculate the Lorentz factor at 0.98c. (c) Calculate the effective lifetime of the muon in the Earth's frame. (d) How far can the muon travel in this time? (e) Go online and find data from an actual cosmic ray muon detection experiment (e.g., an educational experiment using a muon detector or scintillation counter). Compare the observed muon flux at sea level with what Newtonian mechanics would predict. Write a 400-word analysis of the result as evidence for time dilation.

**12. GPS and Relativistic Corrections**
Research how GPS satellite clocks are corrected for relativistic effects. (a) What is the special relativistic correction (due to satellite speed), and in which direction does it shift the clock? (b) What is the general relativistic correction (due to altitude), and in which direction does it shift the clock? (c) What is the net effect per day, and what would happen to GPS accuracy without the correction? (d) Write a reflection: is GPS a confirmation of special relativity, general relativity, or both? Can these effects be disentangled?

**13. Spacetime Diagram Construction**
Draw a spacetime diagram (with *ct* on the vertical axis and *x* on the horizontal axis) for the following scenario: Alice is at rest at the origin. Bob moves to the right at v = 0.6c. A light signal is emitted from the origin at t = 0. (a) Draw Alice's worldline, Bob's worldline, and the worldline of the light signal. (b) Draw Alice's simultaneity lines (horizontal lines) and Bob's simultaneity lines (tilted by the appropriate angle). (c) Identify a pair of events that are simultaneous for Alice but not for Bob. (d) Identify a pair of events that are timelike separated and one that is spacelike separated. (e) Write a 300-word reflection on what the diagram reveals about the structure of Minkowski spacetime.
