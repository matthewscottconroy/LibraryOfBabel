# Section 3: Time Dilation and the Twin Paradox

Time dilation — the slowing of clocks in motion — is among the most counterintuitive consequences of special relativity, yet it is among the most thoroughly confirmed. It is not an artifact of measurement, a perceptual illusion, or a convenient fiction. Moving clocks genuinely run slow, and the effect has been measured in particle accelerators, GPS satellites, and careful laboratory experiments. The implications for how we think about time are profound.

The twin paradox takes this to its dramatic extreme: two twins begin their lives at the same age; one stays on Earth while the other travels at high speed to a distant star and returns. Upon reunion, the traveling twin is younger than the stay-at-home twin. This is not a paradox in the logical sense — there is no contradiction — but it is deeply puzzling until the resolution is understood. The resolution turns on the difference between inertial and non-inertial motion, and it illuminates one of the most important distinctions in special relativity: the difference between coordinate time and proper time.

This section examines both time dilation and the twin paradox carefully, presenting the experimental evidence for time dilation and then analyzing the twin scenario in detail.
