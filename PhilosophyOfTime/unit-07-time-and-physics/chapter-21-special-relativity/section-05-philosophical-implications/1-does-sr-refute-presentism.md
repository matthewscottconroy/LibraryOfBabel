# Does Special Relativity Refute Presentism?

## The Challenge

Presentism holds that only the present moment is real — that past events no longer exist and future events do not yet exist. This is a natural and widely shared intuition about time. But special relativity's relativity of simultaneity seems to make this position incoherent.

The challenge can be stated simply: if there is no absolute present — no frame-independent fact about which events are happening "now" — then the presentist has no principled way to specify what exists. If "what exists" = "what is present," and "present" is frame-relative, then existence is frame-relative. But surely what exists is not frame-relative. The presentist is therefore forced to either (a) accept a preferred frame (neo-Lorentzian strategy) or (b) drastically revise their concept of the present or of existence.

## Stein's Solution: Causal-Historic Definiteness

Howard Stein's response (1968, 1991) is philosophically sophisticated and avoids positing a preferred frame. Stein argues that what is "real" or "definite" for an observer at spacetime point P should be defined in terms of the causal history of P — specifically, the *past light cone* of P.

Stein's precise proposal: event Q has definite, settled reality for an observer at P if and only if Q is at or within the past light cone of P (i.e., Q is in the causal past of P, or Q = P). This definition is fully covariant: the past light cone of P is an invariant geometrical object, the same for all observers at P regardless of their velocity.

On Stein's view, there is a genuine sense in which the future is "open" and the past is "settled": for any event P, its past light cone is the region of spacetime whose events have already "happened for P," in the sense that their effects could in principle have reached P. The future light cone consists of events that have not yet happened for P but may be influenced by P.

This is a form of *becoming* compatible with special relativity — a relativistic presentism of sorts. It is more modest than the traditional presentism (it relativizes "what is real" not to a global present but to the causal past of each event), but it preserves the intuition that reality "grows" with time rather than being a static four-dimensional block.

**Objections to Stein's view:** Critics, including Putnam (1967), argue that Stein's view implies a strange ontology: two observers at the same spacetime point but with different velocities have identical causal pasts and hence the same "ontological reality," yet two observers at different spacetime points — even if at the same place at different times — have different causal pasts. This is correct, but it is a feature of the causal structure of relativistic spacetime, not an objection. The more serious objection is that Stein's notion of "reality for P" is a relativized notion that may not satisfy the presentist's demand for an *absolute* present.

## The Neo-Lorentzian Strategy

A different response is to insist on the reality of a metaphysically preferred frame — one that is physically undetectable but in which simultaneity is absolute. This position has roots in the work of Hendrik Lorentz (who developed the transformation equations before Einstein but interpreted them within an aether-based framework) and has been defended in various forms by William Lane Craig, Tim Maudlin, and others.

The neo-Lorentzian holds that the Lorentz transformations accurately describe how measurements transform between frames, but that there is nevertheless a fact of the matter about which frame is "really at rest." The preferred frame defines absolute simultaneity; events that are simultaneous in the preferred frame are objectively simultaneous, and that is when they exist. Presentism is preserved.

**The empirical objection:** The preferred frame, by construction, has no observable effects — all experiments yield the same results regardless of our velocity relative to it. This means the neo-Lorentzian is positing an entity that makes no empirical difference. Critics argue that this violates the most basic principle of scientific ontology: do not multiply entities beyond necessity (Occam's razor), and in particular, do not posit entities that are in principle undetectable.

**The structural realist response:** Neo-Lorentzians can respond that empirical undetectability does not entail non-existence. The preferred frame might be as real as the neutrino was before it was directly detected — a posit of theory that has not yet been confirmed by experiment. The question is whether we have theoretical reasons to believe in it. The neo-Lorentzian says yes: we need it to make sense of the presentist metaphysics.

**Assessment:** The neo-Lorentzian strategy is internally consistent but philosophically costly. It preserves a familiar metaphysics of time (presentism) by introducing an unobservable structure. Whether this trade-off is worth making depends on how strongly one is committed to presentism and how much weight one gives to the principle of empirical parsimony.

## The Quantum Gravity Escape Route

A third response, more speculative but intellectually honest about the limits of current physics, notes that special relativity is not our most fundamental physical theory. General relativity supersedes it, and quantum gravity supersedes general relativity. Dean Zimmerman (2008) and others have argued that quantum gravity may provide a preferred temporal structure that special relativity lacks.

In particular, certain approaches to quantum gravity — notably canonical quantum gravity and some formulations of loop quantum gravity — involve a preferred foliation of spacetime into spacelike hypersurfaces. The "time" in these approaches is a preferred global coordinate that plays a special role in the dynamics. If quantum gravity requires such a structure, then at the most fundamental level of physics, there is a privileged notion of simultaneity — and the presentist can identify "the present" with the preferred simultaneity surface.

**The speculative objection:** This argument is conditional: *if* quantum gravity requires a preferred foliation, *then* presentism is physically compatible with our best fundamental physics. But quantum gravity is not yet a complete, empirically confirmed theory. The argument provides no more than a logical possibility, not a physical result.

**The methodological point:** Despite its speculative character, the quantum gravity escape route makes an important logical point: special relativity is not the final word on spacetime structure. Arguments that claim to derive the block universe from special relativity alone are presupposing that special relativity is complete, which it demonstrably is not. Presentism cannot be definitively refuted by appeal to a theory that we know to be incomplete.

## An Assessment

The Rietdijk-Putnam argument puts genuine pressure on presentism, but none of the three responses is obviously untenable. Stein's view is elegant and physically motivated but revises the traditional presentist notion of an absolute global present. The neo-Lorentzian strategy preserves traditional presentism but at the cost of unobservable metaphysical structure. The quantum gravity escape is speculative but logically valid.

Perhaps the most honest conclusion is this: special relativity makes traditional absolute presentism very difficult to defend, but it does not make every form of presentism incoherent. The debate is live, and its resolution may depend on future developments in quantum gravity.
