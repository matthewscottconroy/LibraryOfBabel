# Section 5: Philosophical Implications of Special Relativity

The physics of special relativity is settled. The interpretive and philosophical questions it raises are not. This section examines two of the most important philosophical issues that special relativity forces on us.

The first is the status of presentism — the view that only the present moment is real. The Rietdijk-Putnam argument, introduced in Section 2, pressed hard against presentism. Here we examine three serious responses to that argument in detail: Howard Stein's proposal that "real for an observer at P" should be defined as "in the past light cone of P"; the neo-Lorentzian strategy of positing a metaphysically preferred frame; and the quantum gravity escape route suggested by Zimmerman, according to which future physics may restore a privileged notion of simultaneity.

The second issue is the conventionality of simultaneity, associated with Hans Reichenbach's ε-convention. Within a single inertial reference frame, there is a degree of freedom in how we synchronize spatially separated clocks, and this freedom affects what we call "simultaneous." What are the implications of this conventionality for the metaphysical claims we want to make about simultaneity and simultaneous existence?
