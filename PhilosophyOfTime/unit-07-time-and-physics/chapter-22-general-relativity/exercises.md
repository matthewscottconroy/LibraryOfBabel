# Chapter 22 Exercises

## Conceptual and Analytical Exercises

**1. The Equivalence Principle**
(a) State both the weak and strong equivalence principles in your own words. (b) Describe a thought experiment that illustrates the strong equivalence principle using an accelerating rocket. (c) How does the equivalence principle motivate the claim that light must be gravitationally deflected? (d) How does the equivalence principle imply gravitational time dilation?

**2. Dynamic vs. Fixed Spacetime**
Compare the role of spacetime in Newtonian mechanics, special relativity, and general relativity. (a) In which theory is spacetime fixed (non-dynamical)? In which is it dynamical? (b) What does it mean for spacetime to be "dynamical"? (c) Wheeler said: "Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve." Explain what this means physically and why it represents a departure from both Newtonian mechanics and special relativity.

**3. Gravitational Time Dilation and GPS**
(a) Explain gravitational time dilation: why do clocks in weaker gravitational fields run faster? (b) GPS satellites experience both special and general relativistic time dilation. Calculate the net time offset per day (use: special relativistic effect = -7 μs/day; gravitational effect = +45 μs/day). (c) If this were not corrected, how would GPS position errors accumulate? (d) Is the GPS case a confirmation of GR, or could an alternative theory explain it?

**4. The Singularity Theorems**
(a) What is a trapped surface? (b) State the content of Penrose's 1965 singularity theorem without using mathematical notation. What are its hypotheses and what does it conclude? (c) What role do energy conditions play in the singularity theorems? (d) Why does the violation of the Strong Energy Condition by dark energy complicate the application of the cosmological singularity theorems to the actual universe?

**5. Did Time Begin?**
Describe the three main options for interpreting the Big Bang singularity with respect to the beginning of time: (a) genuine temporal boundary, (b) domain of breakdown of classical GR, and (c) ill-posed question. Which do you find most defensible, and why? How does your answer relate to Kant's First Antinomy?

**6. Closed Timelike Curves**
(a) Define a closed timelike curve. (b) What is the "grandfather paradox," and what does the Novikov self-consistency principle say about it? (c) Why did Gödel present his CTC solution as an argument for the ideality of time, connecting to Kant and Parmenides? (d) Evaluate Gödel's argument: what is its strongest point and its most serious weakness?

**7. The Interior of Black Holes**
(a) What is an event horizon, and why is it a causal boundary rather than a physical barrier? (b) What happens to the roles of the *r* and *t* coordinates inside the Schwarzschild radius? (c) In what sense is it true that inside a black hole you "cannot avoid moving toward the singularity"? Is this a good metaphor, or precisely accurate? (d) What philosophical conclusions, if any, can be drawn from the fact that spatial and temporal directions can exchange roles in extreme spacetime geometries?

**8. The Hole Argument**
(a) Explain the Hole Argument in your own words, without mathematical notation. (b) Why does the argument create a problem specifically for *manifold substantivalism* — the view that spacetime points are genuine individuals? (c) Explain three responses to the argument: relationism, anti-haecceitism, and structural realism. Which do you find most compelling? (d) How does the Hole Argument bear on questions about the reality of temporal instants?

## Comparative Exercises

**9. Gödel's Philosophical Ambition**
Gödel presented his 1949 paper not just as a contribution to physics but as a philosophical argument about the nature of time, connecting to Parmenides and Kant. Write an essay (600–800 words) evaluating the relationship between Gödel's mathematical result (a rotating universe with CTCs) and his philosophical conclusion (the ideality of time). Consider: does the argument succeed? What additional philosophical premises are needed to bridge the gap from the mathematical result to the philosophical conclusion?

**10. Black Holes and Temporal Structure**
Compare the temporal structure inside a Schwarzschild black hole with the temporal structure of ordinary Minkowski spacetime and with the temporal structure of the Gödel universe. In what sense is time "strange" in each case? Are these three cases instances of the same phenomenon (time having an unusual structure), or are they fundamentally different kinds of temporal weirdness?

## Observational and Laboratory Exercises

**11. Gravitational Redshift Measurement**
The Pound-Rebka experiment (1959) measured the gravitational blueshift of gamma rays falling 22.5 meters in Earth's gravitational field. The predicted fractional frequency shift is Δf/f = gh/c², where g is gravitational acceleration, h is height, and c is the speed of light. (a) Calculate the expected fractional frequency shift for the Pound-Rebka setup. (b) How does this compare to the experimental uncertainty they achieved? (c) Design (do not execute) a conceptually similar experiment using modern atomic clocks and a height difference of 1 meter. What precision would be needed?

**12. Black Hole Images and General Relativity**
The Event Horizon Telescope collaboration published the first direct image of a black hole (M87*) in 2019, and the first image of Sgr A* (the Milky Way's central black hole) in 2022. Find and study one of these images (available at eventhorizontelescope.org). (a) What features of the image are directly predicted by general relativity? (b) What is the "photon ring" or "shadow," and what does it tell us about the spacetime geometry near the black hole? (c) Write a 400-word reflection: does the EHT image constitute confirmation of general relativity? What predictions does it test, and how precisely?

**13. Expanding Universe Observation**
Hubble's Law states that the recession velocity of a galaxy is proportional to its distance: v = H₀d, where H₀ ≈ 70 km/s/Mpc. (a) Using Hubble's Law, estimate the recession velocity of a galaxy at a distance of 100 Mpc. (b) How does the expansion of the universe connect to the dynamical character of spacetime in GR? (c) If you have access to a telescope or a computer-based astronomy tool (e.g., SDSS SkyServer, Stellarium), observe a distant galaxy or cluster and look up its redshift. Calculate its recession velocity. Reflect on the fact that you are observing a consequence of general relativistic spacetime dynamics.
