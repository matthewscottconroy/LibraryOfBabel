## Annotated Entries

**Misner, Charles W., Kip S. Thorne, and John Archibald Wheeler. 1973. *Gravitation*. San Francisco: W. H. Freeman.**
The encyclopedic textbook on general relativity, known in the field simply as "MTW." At over 1,200 pages, it covers both the physics and the conceptual foundations of GR in extraordinary depth. Particularly valuable for the philosophy of time are the discussions of spacetime structure, singularities, and the meaning of the metric. Requires substantial mathematical preparation but rewards the effort.

**Hawking, Stephen W., and George F. R. Ellis. 1973. *The Large Scale Structure of Space-Time*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.**
The definitive mathematical treatment of the global structure of general relativistic spacetimes, including the singularity theorems, the causal structure of black holes, and the cosmological theorems. Essential for anyone who wants to understand the Penrose-Hawking singularity theorems in detail. Demanding mathematically but written with philosophical awareness.

**Earman, John. 1995. *Bangs, Crunches, Whimpers, and Shrieks*. New York: Oxford University Press.**
The best philosophical treatment of singularities, time travel, and acausal structures in general relativity. Earman examines the singularity theorems, the implications of the Big Bang singularity for the beginning of time, the physics and metaphysics of closed timelike curves, and the Hole Argument. Written for philosophers of physics, accessible to anyone with basic GR background.

**Yourgrau, Palle. 2005. *A World Without Time: The Forgotten Legacy of Gödel and Einstein*. New York: Basic Books.**
A wonderful account of the intellectual friendship between Gödel and Einstein, and of Gödel's philosophical contributions to physics. Yourgrau provides the best accessible treatment of Gödel's 1949 CTC solution and his argument for the ideality of time. Engaging, well-written, and philosophically serious.

**Earman, John, and John Norton. 1987. "What Price Spacetime Substantivalism? The Hole Story." *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 38: 515–525.**
The paper that revived the Hole Argument and set the agenda for twenty years of debate about spacetime ontology. Accessible to philosophers with basic GR knowledge, and essential reading for understanding the implications of diffeomorphism invariance for the ontology of spacetime and the identity of temporal moments.

**Pooley, Oliver. 2013. "Substantivalist and Relationalist Approaches to Spacetime."**
The best current survey of the debate provoked by the Hole Argument, covering manifold substantivalism, sophisticated substantivalism, relationism, and structural realism. Technical but the conceptual discussion is clear and fair to all positions.

**Penrose, Roger. 2004. *The Road to Reality*. London: Jonathan Cape.**
An ambitious survey of modern physics by one of its greatest practitioners and one of the most philosophically engaged scientists of the age. Penrose's treatment of spacetime singularities, the Weyl curvature hypothesis, and the arrow of time (continued in our Chapter 23) is essential context for the material covered here.

**Carroll, Sean. 2010. *From Eternity to Here*. New York: Dutton.**
A beautifully written popular account of the arrow of time, time in cosmology, and the relationship between the Second Law of Thermodynamics and the large-scale structure of the universe. Carroll's treatment of how GR connects to thermodynamics and the arrow of time is the best accessible introduction to the topic.

**Wald, Robert M. 1984. *General Relativity*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press.**
The most rigorous and conceptually clear graduate-level textbook on GR. Wald's treatment of the global structure of spacetimes, the singularity theorems, and the formulation of GR in terms of the Cauchy problem is technically authoritative. Essential for anyone who wants to understand the mathematical content of the claims made in this chapter.
