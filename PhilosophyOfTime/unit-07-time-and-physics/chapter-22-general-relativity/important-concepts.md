# Important Concepts: Chapter 22

**Equivalence Principle**
The principle that a freely falling reference frame is locally indistinguishable from an inertial frame in empty space, and that a uniformly accelerating frame is locally indistinguishable from a frame at rest in a gravitational field. The word "locally" is crucial: the equivalence holds only in a sufficiently small region where tidal effects are negligible. The equivalence principle is the conceptual foundation of general relativity.

**Gravitational Time Dilation**
The phenomenon whereby clocks deeper in a gravitational field run slower than clocks at higher altitude. Predicted by the equivalence principle and confirmed by experiment (Pound-Rebka 1959, GPS systems, optical atomic clocks). Gravitational time dilation means that proper time accumulation depends on where in a gravitational field an observer is located.

**Einstein's Field Equations**
The central equations of general relativity: G_μν = (8πG/c⁴)T_μν. The left side (Einstein tensor) encodes the curvature of spacetime; the right side (stress-energy tensor) encodes the distribution of matter and energy. The equations say that matter curves spacetime and that the curvature of spacetime determines how matter moves — a mutual, dynamical relationship.

**Dynamical Spacetime**
In general relativity, spacetime is not a fixed background but a dynamical field that changes in response to the distribution of matter and energy. The metric tensor, which encodes how distances and time intervals are measured at each point of spacetime, satisfies differential equations (Einstein's field equations) and evolves over time. This is in contrast to the fixed Minkowski spacetime of special relativity.

**Spacetime Singularity**
A region or boundary of a spacetime where the curvature becomes infinite and the classical description of general relativity breaks down. Singularities are indicated by incomplete geodesics — paths through spacetime that cannot be extended to infinite proper time. The Big Bang is a past singularity; the interior of a black hole contains a future singularity.

**Penrose-Hawking Singularity Theorems**
Results proved by Roger Penrose (1965) and Stephen Hawking (1960s–1970s) showing that under very general conditions, general relativity predicts spacetime singularities. The Penrose theorem shows that gravitational collapse to a trapped surface inevitably leads to a singularity; the Hawking theorems show that an expanding universe consistent with observed matter content must have originated in a past singularity (the Big Bang).

**Trapped Surface**
A closed two-dimensional surface from which both ingoing and outgoing light rays are converging toward the future. The existence of a trapped surface in a spacetime is a key hypothesis of Penrose's singularity theorem. Trapped surfaces form during the gravitational collapse of massive stars and are a precursor to the formation of event horizons.

**Event Horizon**
The boundary of the region of spacetime from which causal signals can reach external observers. Inside the event horizon, nothing — not light, not matter, not any causal signal — can escape. The event horizon is a global property of the spacetime, not a local physical structure; a freely falling observer does not feel anything special when crossing it.

**Closed Timelike Curve (CTC)**
A worldline that is everywhere timelike (traversable by a massive object) but forms a closed loop, returning to its starting spacetime event. A traveler following a CTC would return to their own past. CTCs appear in several exact solutions to Einstein's field equations, including the Gödel metric and the interior of the Kerr black hole.

**Gödel Metric**
An exact solution to Einstein's field equations, found by Kurt Gödel in 1949, describing a rotating universe in which closed timelike curves pass through every point. Not a realistic model of our universe (it does not expand), but philosophically important as a demonstration that GR is consistent with global temporal looping. Gödel used it to argue for the ideality of time.

**Chronology Protection Conjecture**
Stephen Hawking's conjecture (1992) that the laws of physics — including quantum effects — conspire to prevent the formation of closed timelike curves. The physical argument involves the divergence of quantum vacuum energy near a would-be CTC. The conjecture has not been proved in general and remains a subject of research.

**Schwarzschild Radius**
The radius r_S = 2GM/c² at which the Schwarzschild metric has a coordinate singularity. The Schwarzschild sphere defines the event horizon of a non-rotating black hole of mass M. Inside r_S, the roles of the time and radial coordinates effectively exchange: the r-coordinate becomes timelike and the singularity at r = 0 lies in the future of any infalling observer.

**Diffeomorphism Invariance**
The property of general relativity that its equations take the same form in any smooth coordinate system, and that if one spacetime geometry is a solution, any diffeomorphically related geometry (obtained by a smooth relabeling of spacetime points) is also a solution. Diffeomorphism invariance is the mathematical expression of the absence of a preferred coordinate system in GR.

**Hole Argument**
An argument developed by Earman and Norton (1987) showing that manifold substantivalism — the view that spacetime points are genuine individuals — combined with diffeomorphism invariance implies radical indeterminism. The argument uses the freedom to relabel fields within a spacetime region (the "hole") to generate empirically equivalent but, on the substantivalist view, physically distinct models.

**Manifold Substantivalism**
The view that the spacetime manifold is a genuine physical entity and that its points are real individuals with primitive identities (haecceities). Targeted by the Hole Argument as generating radical indeterminism when combined with diffeomorphism invariance.

**Structural Realism (about Spacetime)**
The view that the physical world contains objective structure — relations and patterns — but that individual objects (such as spacetime points) do not have identities over and above their structural role. Applied to the Hole Argument: there are no primitive facts about which spacetime point is which; only structural facts (which metric relations hold) are physically real.
