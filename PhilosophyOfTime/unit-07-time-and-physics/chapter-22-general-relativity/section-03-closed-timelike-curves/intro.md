# Section 3: Closed Timelike Curves

General relativity is extraordinarily permissive. Given the right matter distribution and the right geometry, Einstein's field equations permit solutions with structures that violate almost every commonsense assumption about time. Among the most startling are *closed timelike curves* (CTCs): paths through spacetime that are (a) timelike — traversable by a massive object — and (b) closed — returning to their starting point. A traveler following a CTC would return to the same place and the same moment they departed from, having aged forward the entire time.

The existence of CTC solutions to Einstein's equations has been known since the 1940s and remains a subject of active investigation. This section examines what closed timelike curves are and what they would mean if they existed, and then examines the most celebrated CTC solution: the Gödel universe of 1949, and the philosophical argument that Kurt Gödel built upon it.
