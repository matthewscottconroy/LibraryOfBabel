# Section 5: The Hole Argument

The Hole Argument is one of the most discussed topics in the philosophy of physics of the last four decades. Resurrected by John Earman and John Norton in their influential 1987 paper, it uses a technical feature of general relativity to pose a dilemma: either general relativity is radically indeterministic, or spacetime points are not the fundamental ontological individuals we might have supposed.

The argument is elegant, technical, and philosophically far-reaching. It connects the foundations of spacetime physics to debates about metaphysical haecceitism (the view that individuals have a primitive identity that goes beyond their qualitative properties), structural realism, and the nature of substantivalism about spacetime. Understanding the Hole Argument is essential for anyone who wants to think carefully about what it means to take spacetime seriously as a physical entity.
