# Chapter 23 Exercises

## Conceptual and Analytical Exercises

**1. Formulations of the Second Law**
(a) State Clausius's and Kelvin's formulations of the Second Law in your own words. (b) Prove informally that if one formulation fails, so does the other. (c) What is the statistical mechanical interpretation of entropy? How does *S = k_B ln W* connect macrostates to microstates?

**2. The Reversibility Problem**
Explain in detail why there is a puzzle about the time asymmetry of the Second Law. Your answer should (a) explain what time-symmetry of the microphysical laws means, (b) explain what time-asymmetry of the Second Law means, and (c) articulate precisely why these seem to be in tension.

**3. Loschmidt and Zermelo**
(a) State Loschmidt's reversibility objection. How did Boltzmann respond? (b) State Zermelo's recurrence objection, including what the Poincaré Recurrence Theorem says. How did Boltzmann respond? (c) Are these objections decisive against the Second Law, or do Boltzmann's responses adequately address them? Defend your answer.

**4. The H-Theorem and the Stosszahlansatz**
(a) What does Boltzmann's H-theorem prove, and what does it assume (the Stosszahlansatz)? (b) How does Loschmidt's critique reveal that the Stosszahlansatz is time-asymmetric? (c) Can the H-theorem be reformulated to avoid this time-asymmetry? If so, what does the reformulated theorem say?

**5. Phase Space and Entropy**
(a) Explain what phase space is, and why a "macrostate" corresponds to a region of phase space rather than a point. (b) What is Liouville's theorem, and why does it seem to conflict with entropy increase? (c) How is the conflict resolved by distinguishing fine-grained and coarse-grained entropy?

**6. The Past Hypothesis**
(a) State Albert's Past Hypothesis and Statistical Postulate. (b) Why does Albert need both, not just the Past Hypothesis alone? (c) The Past Hypothesis is a brute posit — it says the universe began in a low-entropy state without explaining why. Is this explanatorily satisfying? What would a better explanation look like?

**7. Carroll and Chen**
(a) Describe Carroll and Chen's proposal for explaining the Past Hypothesis cosmologically. (b) What is the "Boltzmann brain" problem for eternal cosmologies? (c) Does the Boltzmann brain problem undermine Carroll and Chen's account? How have they responded?

**8. Penrose's Weyl Curvature Hypothesis**
(a) What is the Weyl curvature tensor, and why does Penrose think it measures the entropy of the gravitational field? (b) Why was the Big Bang a low-entropy state, given that the matter was at extreme temperature and density? (c) How does Penrose estimate the probability of the initial state as 1 in 10^(10^123), and what implications does this have for the Past Hypothesis?

**9. Maxwell's Demon**
(a) Describe Maxwell's demon thought experiment. (b) Why did Szilard argue that the demon's measurement costs entropy? (c) State Landauer's principle. (d) How does Landauer's principle resolve the apparent paradox of Maxwell's demon, by showing that the demon's cycle does not violate the Second Law?

**10. Information and the Arrow of Time**
Landauer's principle connects information erasure to entropy production. How does this connection illuminate the relationship between: (a) the asymmetry of memory (we remember the past, not the future), and (b) the thermodynamic arrow of time? Is the information-theoretic perspective a reductive explanation of the arrow of time, or merely a redescription of it?

## Observational and Laboratory Exercises

**11. Entropy in Everyday Life**
For each of the following processes, explain whether entropy increases, decreases, or remains constant in the specified system. For each, estimate roughly how large the entropy change is (order-of-magnitude reasoning is fine). (a) A cup of hot coffee cooling to room temperature in an insulated room. (b) A living organism eating and metabolizing food. (c) Water freezing in a freezer. (d) A carbon dioxide molecule being fixed in photosynthesis. For (b) and (c), consider the broader system including the environment.

**12. The Ice Cube Experiment**
Place an ice cube in a glass of warm water in a thermally insulated container. (a) Observe the process over 30 minutes. (b) Use the formula for entropy change *ΔS = Q/T* to estimate the entropy change of the ice (as it melts) and the entropy change of the warm water (as it cools). (c) Show that the total entropy increases. (d) Could this process run in reverse — warm water spontaneously freezing and warming the rest of the water? Why not? How does your answer connect to phase space and the relative sizes of macrostate regions?

**13. Landauer's Principle Calculation**
(a) Calculate the minimum energy dissipated per bit erased at room temperature (300 K) using Landauer's formula: *E_min = k_B T ln 2*, where *k_B = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K*. (b) A modern laptop processor performs approximately 10¹⁰ logic operations per second. If each operation erases one bit, and operates at room temperature, how much heat must the processor dissipate per second at the Landauer limit? Compare this to the actual power consumption of a modern laptop (~30–60 W). What does the comparison tell you about how close modern computers are to the Landauer limit? (c) Write a reflection: what does Landauer's principle tell us about the relationship between information processing, thermodynamics, and the arrow of time?
