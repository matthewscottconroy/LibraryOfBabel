# Important Concepts: Chapter 23

**Second Law of Thermodynamics**
The macroscopic law stating that the entropy of a closed system never decreases over time, and typically increases. Equivalent formulations: heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold (Clausius); no cyclic process can extract work from a single heat reservoir (Kelvin). One of the most robustly confirmed laws in physics, yet derived from the asymmetric initial conditions of the universe rather than from time-asymmetric fundamental laws.

**Entropy**
A macroscopic property of a thermodynamic system that measures the number of microscopic configurations (microstates) compatible with the macroscopic state (macrostate). Defined by Boltzmann as S = k_B ln W, where W is the number of microstates. High entropy corresponds to many compatible microstates; low entropy corresponds to few. Entropy is the quantity whose increase defines the thermodynamic arrow of time.

**Boltzmann's Constant (k_B)**
The fundamental constant connecting macroscopic thermodynamic quantities (entropy, temperature) to microscopic statistical quantities. k_B ≈ 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K. It sets the scale at which thermal fluctuations become comparable to mechanical energies, and appears in the formulas for entropy (S = k_B ln W) and the thermal energy per degree of freedom (k_BT/2).

**H-Theorem**
Boltzmann's 1872 result that the quantity H (proportional to the negative of entropy) always decreases over time for a gas of molecules, given the Stosszahlansatz. The H-theorem purports to derive the Second Law from mechanics, but it does so by implicitly assuming time-asymmetric initial conditions through the molecular chaos assumption.

**Stosszahlansatz (Molecular Chaos Assumption)**
The assumption, central to Boltzmann's H-theorem, that the velocities of two molecules are statistically uncorrelated before they collide. The assumption introduces time asymmetry: it holds before collisions but not after. As Loschmidt showed, this is where the arrow of time enters Boltzmann's derivation.

**Phase Space**
The 6N-dimensional space of all possible states of a system of N particles, where each point specifies the positions and momenta of all particles. Every state of the system corresponds to exactly one point in phase space. The dynamics of the system traces out a path (trajectory) through phase space.

**Liouville's Theorem**
The theorem that Hamiltonian dynamics preserves phase space volume: the dynamics acts as an incompressible flow on phase space. A consequence is that fine-grained entropy cannot increase. The increase of coarse-grained (Boltzmann) entropy is consistent with Liouville's theorem because coarse-grained entropy measures the volume of the macrostate region, not the trajectory's own volume.

**Macrostate and Microstate**
A microstate is a complete specification of all the positions and momenta of all particles — a single point in phase space. A macrostate is a coarse-grained description that specifies only macroscopic properties (temperature, pressure, volume). Many microstates correspond to each macrostate. The Boltzmann entropy of a macrostate is proportional to the logarithm of the number of compatible microstates.

**Past Hypothesis (PH)**
David Albert's term for the postulate that the macrostate of the universe near the beginning of time was one of very low entropy. The Past Hypothesis, combined with the Statistical Postulate, grounds statistical mechanics and explains the thermodynamic arrow of time. It is a brute posit of initial conditions, not derived from the laws of mechanics.

**Statistical Postulate (SP)**
The postulate (Albert 2000) that the probability distribution over microstates of the universe, conditioned on the Past Hypothesis and the present macrostate, is uniform over the compatible phase space region. Together with the Past Hypothesis, the Statistical Postulate gives a complete foundation for statistical mechanical reasoning.

**Boltzmann Brain**
A hypothetical spontaneous fluctuation from a high-entropy state that produces, by chance, a self-aware observer with false memories and a false belief in a long cosmic history. Boltzmann brains are an extreme implication of the statistical approach: in an eternal universe at near-maximum entropy, such fluctuations are vastly more common than real observers who evolved from a low-entropy past. Their ubiquity in some cosmological models is considered a reductio ad absurdum of those models.

**Weyl Curvature Hypothesis**
Penrose's proposal that the Weyl curvature tensor (measuring the tidal/gravitational degrees of freedom of spacetime) was zero at the Big Bang and is large (or infinite) at final singularities (such as the end state of gravitational collapse). The Weyl tensor measures the entropy content of the gravitational field; the hypothesis explains why the Big Bang was a low-entropy state even though matter and radiation were at extreme temperature.

**Conformal Cyclic Cosmology (CCC)**
Penrose's cosmological model in which the universe passes through a sequence of "aeons," with the future conformal boundary of each aeon identified with the past conformal boundary of the next. CCC is proposed as a mechanism for generating successive low-entropy initial states, potentially explaining the Past Hypothesis.

**Maxwell's Demon**
Maxwell's thought experiment in which an intelligent being sorts gas molecules by speed, creating a temperature difference without expending mechanical work — apparently violating the Second Law. The resolution (Szilard 1929; Landauer 1961) is that the demon's measurement and memory operations have thermodynamic costs, and erasing the demon's memory generates entropy that compensates for the apparent violation.

**Landauer's Principle**
The principle that erasing one bit of information in a physical system must generate at least k_B T ln 2 of heat, where T is the temperature of the environment. Landauer's principle resolves Maxwell's demon paradox and reveals a fundamental connection between information processing and thermodynamics. The minimum heat dissipation at room temperature is approximately 2.87 × 10⁻²¹ J per bit erased.

**Thermodynamic Arrow of Time**
The direction of time defined by the increase of entropy: the future is the direction in which entropy increases. The thermodynamic arrow is the most macroscopically prominent arrow of time and is connected to the causal arrow, the epistemic arrow (memory), and the cosmological arrow. Its ultimate grounding, on the contemporary view, is the Past Hypothesis.

**Loschmidt's Reversibility Objection**
Josef Loschmidt's 1876 argument that the H-theorem cannot be derived from time-symmetric mechanics, because for every entropy-increasing trajectory, there is an equally valid reversed trajectory that is entropy-decreasing. The objection correctly identifies that the H-theorem's time asymmetry comes from the initial conditions (molecular chaos assumption), not from the laws of mechanics.

**Poincaré Recurrence Theorem**
Henri Poincaré's 1890 result that any bounded Hamiltonian system will return arbitrarily close to its initial state after a sufficiently long time. Zermelo used this to argue against the H-theorem. Boltzmann's response: the recurrence time for any macroscopic system is astronomically long — vastly longer than the age of the universe — so the recurrence is practically irrelevant.
