# Section 3: The Past Hypothesis

The statistical mechanical explanation of entropy increase requires that the universe began in a low-entropy state. Without this assumption, we cannot explain why entropy has been increasing for the past 13.8 billion years rather than decreasing — since the underlying dynamics is symmetric and both directions are equally lawful. The specific assumption needed was formulated by David Albert as the "Past Hypothesis": the macrostate of the universe near the Big Bang was one of extraordinarily low entropy.

This section examines Albert's precise formulation of the Past Hypothesis and the accompanying Statistical Postulate, which together provide a foundation for statistical mechanics and ground the thermodynamic arrow of time. We then examine the criticisms and alternatives to this approach — including Carroll and Chen's cosmological response and Loewer's defense — to assess how satisfying this foundation really is.
