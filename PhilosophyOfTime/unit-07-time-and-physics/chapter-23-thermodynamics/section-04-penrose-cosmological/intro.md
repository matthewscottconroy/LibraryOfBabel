# Section 4: Penrose's Cosmological Approach

Roger Penrose has developed one of the most ambitious and quantitatively precise approaches to the problem of the cosmological arrow of time: the Weyl curvature hypothesis. Rather than simply positing that the Big Bang was a low-entropy state (as Albert's Past Hypothesis does), Penrose identifies the specific geometrical feature of the initial state that made its entropy so low — the vanishing (or near-vanishing) of the Weyl curvature tensor — and estimates the extraordinary improbability of this condition.

Penrose's approach connects the arrow of time to the structure of general relativity in a way that is quantitatively specific and philosophically provocative. His later cosmological scheme, Conformal Cyclic Cosmology, attempts to explain why successive aeons of the universe can each begin in low-entropy states, offering a potential resolution of the most puzzling aspect of the Past Hypothesis.
