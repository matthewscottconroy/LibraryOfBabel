# Section 5: Maxwell's Demon and the Physics of Information

In 1867, James Clerk Maxwell — the physicist who had unified electricity and magnetism in his famous equations — sent a letter to a friend describing a thought experiment that appeared to violate the Second Law of Thermodynamics. The thought experiment, which Maxwell's contemporary William Thomson would later call "Maxwell's Demon," remains one of the most illuminating puzzles in the history of physics. Its resolution, developed by Leo Szilard in 1929 and given quantitative form by Rolf Landauer in 1961, reveals a deep connection between information and entropy — a connection that has implications for the arrow of time, the nature of irreversibility, and the relationship between the physical and the informational.
