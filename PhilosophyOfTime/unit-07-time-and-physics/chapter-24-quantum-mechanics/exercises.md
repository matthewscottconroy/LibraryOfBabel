# Chapter 24 Exercises

## Conceptual and Analytical Exercises

**1. Pauli's Theorem**
(a) Explain why, in quantum mechanics, observables are represented by self-adjoint operators. (b) State Pauli's theorem: why cannot there be a time operator canonically conjugate to the Hamiltonian? (c) What does Pauli's theorem imply about the status of time in quantum mechanics, relative to position and momentum?

**2. Energy-Time Uncertainty**
(a) How does the energy-time uncertainty relation ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2 differ from the position-momentum uncertainty relation ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2? (b) What does Δt mean in the Mandelstam-Tamm formulation? (c) Apply the energy-time uncertainty relation to explain the natural linewidth of an atomic transition with a lifetime of 10 nanoseconds. Calculate the energy width ΔE.

**3. The Schrödinger Equation and Time Symmetry**
(a) Is the Schrödinger equation time-symmetric? Demonstrate your answer. (b) Is the collapse postulate time-symmetric? Explain. (c) If quantum mechanics includes both the Schrödinger equation and the collapse postulate, is quantum mechanics overall time-symmetric?

**4. The Measurement Problem**
(a) Describe the measurement problem in your own words, without using the technical vocabulary of quantum mechanics. (b) What is the "Schrödinger's cat" thought experiment, and how does it make the measurement problem vivid? (c) Why is the temporal location of collapse a problem — what makes "when does collapse occur?" a difficult question?

**5. Many-Worlds Interpretation and Time**
(a) Explain Everett's many-worlds interpretation. What role does the wavefunction play, and what happens when a measurement is made? (b) What does "branching" mean in MWI? Does time branch in MWI, or does the history of the physical world branch? (c) Does the many-worlds interpretation support a block universe? Justify your answer.

**6. Bohmian Mechanics and Time**
(a) Describe the basic structure of Bohmian mechanics: what are the fundamental ontological elements and what laws govern them? (b) Why does Bohmian mechanics avoid the measurement problem? (c) What conception of time does Bohmian mechanics use, and how does it compare to Newton's absolute time?

**7. Two-State Vector Formalism**
(a) Explain the two-state vector formalism. How does it differ from the standard quantum mechanical description? (b) What is a "weak value," and why can it lie outside the range of eigenvalues? (c) Does the TSVF imply genuine retrocausation — causal influence from the future? Consider both the predictive and ontological interpretations.

**8. The Page-Wootters Mechanism**
(a) Why does the Wheeler-DeWitt equation create a "problem of time" — what is the challenge it poses? (b) Explain the Page-Wootters mechanism in non-technical terms: how does time emerge from entanglement? (c) What does the mechanism imply about whether time is fundamental or emergent? (d) Does the experimental demonstration by Moreva et al. (2014) confirm that time is emergent in our universe?

## Comparative and Synthetic Exercises

**9. Comparing Interpretations**
Compare the many-worlds interpretation and Bohmian mechanics with respect to: (a) their treatment of the measurement problem, (b) their conception of time, (c) their ontological commitments (what do they say fundamentally exists?), and (d) their implications for the arrow of time. Which interpretation do you find most philosophically defensible, and why?

**10. Quantum Mechanics and the Arrow of Time**
The Schrödinger equation is time-symmetric, but the collapse postulate (in standard QM) introduces time asymmetry. (a) Can the collapse asymmetry explain the thermodynamic arrow of time, or is it the other way around? (b) On the many-worlds interpretation, there is no collapse. How does MWI handle the arrow of time? (c) On Bohmian mechanics, how is the arrow of time explained?

## Observational and Laboratory Exercises

**11. Quantum Linewidths**
The natural linewidths of atomic transitions are determined by the energy-time uncertainty principle. The sodium D-line at 589 nm corresponds to an atomic transition with a lifetime of approximately 16 nanoseconds. (a) Calculate the natural linewidth (in frequency units) of the sodium D-line using ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2. (b) Calculate the corresponding wavelength spread. (c) Look up (or observe if possible) the linewidth of the sodium D-line in a laboratory sodium lamp. Is the observed linewidth larger or smaller than the natural linewidth? What other broadening mechanisms are present? (d) Reflect: what does your calculation demonstrate about the physical reality of the energy-time uncertainty relation?

**12. Quantum Eraser Thought Experiment**
Research the "quantum eraser" experiment (Kim et al. 2000, *Physical Review Letters* 84: 1–5). In this experiment, which-path information for a photon interfering with itself can be "erased" by a subsequent measurement on the photon's entangled partner, restoring the interference pattern. (a) Describe the experiment in your own words. (b) Why does this experiment seem to suggest retrocausation — that the later measurement affects what the earlier photon did? (c) What is the conventional quantum mechanical explanation that avoids retrocausation? (d) How would the two-state vector formalism interpret this result?

**13. Entanglement and Clock Correlations**
Using the Page-Wootters mechanism as a framework, design a thought experiment in which an internal "clock" subsystem allows an observer to experience temporal evolution even if the global quantum state of the universe is static. (a) Specify the Hilbert space structure (clock + rest). (b) Describe what it would mean, operationally, for the clock to "read time τ." (c) How does the state of the "rest" system depend on τ? (d) What would happen to the observer's experience of time if the clock subsystem became unentangled from the rest of the universe? (e) Reflect: does this thought experiment suggest that time is a feature of how we are correlated with the rest of the universe, rather than an intrinsic feature of the universe itself?
