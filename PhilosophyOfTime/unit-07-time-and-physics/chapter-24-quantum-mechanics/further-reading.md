## Annotated Entries

**Maudlin, Tim. 2019. *Philosophy of Physics: Quantum Theory*. Princeton: Princeton University Press.**
The best current introduction to the philosophy of quantum mechanics for readers with philosophical training. Maudlin presents the measurement problem with exceptional clarity, evaluates the major interpretations (many-worlds, Bohmian mechanics, and collapse theories) with philosophical rigor, and does not shy away from strong positions. Essential reading for anyone who wants to think seriously about what quantum mechanics says about physical reality.

**Albert, David Z. 1994. *Quantum Mechanics and Experience*. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press.**
An accessible and rigorous introduction to the foundations of quantum mechanics, written for philosophers. Covers the measurement problem, the many-worlds interpretation, Bohmian mechanics, and collapse theories. Albert's treatment is more sympathetic to collapse theories than most, and his discussion of the connection between quantum mechanics and the arrow of time is important.

**Wallace, David. 2012. *The Emergent Multiverse*. Oxford: Oxford University Press.**
The definitive philosophical defense of the many-worlds interpretation. Wallace provides a rigorous derivation of the Born rule from decision theory within MWI, addresses the probability problem, and develops the implications of MWI for the philosophy of physics. Technical but rewards careful reading.

**Saunders, Simon, Jonathan Barrett, Adrian Kent, and David Wallace, eds. 2010. *Many Worlds?* Oxford: Oxford University Press.**
A wide-ranging collection of essays on the many-worlds interpretation, including contributions by physicists and philosophers. The collection represents the state of the art in the debate over MWI and covers all the major objections and responses.

**Page, Don N., and William K. Wootters. 1983. "Evolution Without Evolution." *Physical Review D* 27: 2885–2892.**
The original Page-Wootters paper proposing the mechanism for emergent time. Short and technical but accessible to readers with a basic quantum mechanics background. One of the most philosophically provocative papers in the foundations of quantum mechanics.

**Moreva, Ekaterina, et al. 2014. "Time from Quantum Entanglement." *Physical Review A* 89: 052122.**
The experimental demonstration of the Page-Wootters mechanism using entangled photons. Shows that the mechanism is not merely theoretical but observable in laboratory quantum systems. An important result for understanding time in quantum mechanics.

**Muga, J. G., R. Sala Mayato, and I. L. Egusquiza, eds. 2002. *Time in Quantum Mechanics*. Berlin: Springer.**
A comprehensive scholarly collection on the various notions of time in quantum mechanics, including arrival time, tunneling time, dwell time, and the time-energy uncertainty relation. Technical but invaluable as a reference for the range of temporal concepts that arise in quantum mechanics.

**Aharonov, Yakir, and Lev Vaidman. 1991. "Complete Description of a Quantum System at a Given Time."**
The key paper developing the two-state vector formalism and the concept of weak values. Aharonov and Vaidman argue for a time-symmetric description of quantum reality, with implications for retrocausation and the nature of the quantum past and future. A philosophically rich and technically original contribution.
