# Section 1: Time as Parameter, Not Observable

In classical mechanics, both position and time appear as variables in the equations of motion — but they play different roles. Position is a dynamical variable, determined by the equations; time is an external parameter, given independently. This asymmetry deepened in quantum mechanics, where position became a self-adjoint operator on Hilbert space while time remained an external parameter — the argument of the wavefunction Ψ(x, t).

Could time be promoted to a quantum observable, like position? Wolfgang Pauli's 1933 argument shows that this promotion is impossible, at least in the standard formulation of quantum mechanics. The result, known as Pauli's theorem, has been widely cited and is genuinely important, though its scope and implications are subtler than they are sometimes presented. This section examines Pauli's theorem and the energy-time uncertainty relation, and draws out the philosophical implications.
