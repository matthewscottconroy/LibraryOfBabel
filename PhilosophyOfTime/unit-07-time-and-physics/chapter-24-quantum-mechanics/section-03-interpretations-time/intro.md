# Section 3: Interpretations of Quantum Mechanics and Time

No aspect of quantum mechanics is more contested than its interpretation. The mathematics is settled and extraordinarily successful; what the mathematics means — what picture of physical reality it describes — has been debated since the 1920s and is not settled today. Different interpretations give very different accounts of the nature of time and temporal evolution in quantum mechanics.

This section examines two of the major interpretations with particular attention to how they handle time: Everett's many-worlds interpretation, in which there is no collapse and the universal wavefunction branches into multiple equally real histories; and Bohmian mechanics (the de Broglie-Bohm pilot wave theory), in which particles have definite positions at all times and time is classical and external. Both interpretations avoid the measurement problem in different ways, and both have distinctive implications for the philosophy of time.
