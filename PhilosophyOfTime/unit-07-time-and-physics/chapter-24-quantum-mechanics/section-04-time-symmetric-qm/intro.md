# Section 4: Time-Symmetric Quantum Mechanics

Standard quantum mechanics describes the evolution of a quantum system by a state vector that is propagated forward in time from some initial (past) boundary condition. The state at time t is completely determined by the state at an earlier time and the Hamiltonian. This is a manifestly time-asymmetric picture: the past determines the future, not the other way around.

But the Schrödinger equation is time-symmetric: if |Ψ(t)⟩ is a solution, so is the complex conjugate |Ψ*(-t)⟩. This suggests that a complete description of a quantum system might involve both a forward-evolving state and a backward-evolving state, determined by both past and future boundary conditions.

Yakir Aharonov and Lev Vaidman developed this idea into the "two-state vector formalism" (TSVF), a consistent and empirically equivalent reformulation of quantum mechanics that describes quantum states in terms of both past and future boundary conditions. The formalism has implications for the nature of quantum reality, weak measurements, and the possibility of retrocausation. This section examines the TSVF and its philosophical significance.
