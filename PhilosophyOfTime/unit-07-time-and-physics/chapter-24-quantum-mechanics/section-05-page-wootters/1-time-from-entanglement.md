# Time from Entanglement: The Page-Wootters Mechanism

## The Problem Motivating the Mechanism

As we will see in detail in Chapter 25, the canonical quantization of general relativity leads to the Wheeler-DeWitt equation:

*Ĥ|Ψ⟩ = 0*

where Ĥ is the Hamiltonian constraint operator and |Ψ⟩ is the quantum state of the universe. The striking feature of this equation is that there is no time derivative: the state of the universe does not evolve. The wavefunction is static, frozen, timeless.

This creates a profound puzzle: if the fundamental quantum description of the universe is timeless, where does time come from? We experience time as flowing; physics is built on temporal evolution. If the most fundamental description of the universe has no time, how do we recover the time we observe?

## The Page-Wootters Proposal

Don Page and William Wootters (1983) proposed a mechanism for deriving time from correlations within a timeless quantum universe. Their insight is deceptively simple: even if the global state of the universe is static (no time evolution), it can contain entangled subsystems whose states are correlated with each other. If one subsystem plays the role of a "clock" — if its state changes in a systematic way correlated with the state of other systems — then observers within the universe will experience time as the sequence of clock readings.

The mechanism works as follows:

1. Consider a universe described by a total Hilbert space H = H_clock ⊗ H_rest, where H_clock is the Hilbert space of a "clock" subsystem and H_rest is the Hilbert space of everything else.

2. The global state |Ψ⟩ ∈ H satisfies Ĥ|Ψ⟩ = 0 (timeless, Wheeler-DeWitt-like).

3. Despite the global state being static, the reduced state of H_rest, conditioned on the clock reading τ, can be non-trivial and can evolve as τ changes.

4. Specifically, if we ask "what is the state of the rest of the universe, given that the clock reads τ?" we get a conditional state ρ(τ) that depends on τ. The dependence of ρ on τ is what observers within the universe call "temporal evolution."

In effect, the correlations between the clock subsystem and the rest of the universe encode a history of the universe, which an internal observer (who is part of H_rest) can read off by observing the clock.

## The Formalism

More precisely, in the Page-Wootters mechanism, the conditional state of the rest of the universe given clock reading |τ⟩ is:

*|ψ(τ)⟩_rest = ⟨τ|_clock |Ψ⟩*

This conditional state evolves with τ: as τ changes, |ψ(τ)⟩_rest changes. The evolution turns out to be governed by an effective Schrödinger equation with the Hamiltonian of the "rest" system and the clock's rate variable playing the role of time. The internal observer, who uses the clock as a reference for time, experiences a world that evolves according to the usual quantum mechanical rules.

The key point: from outside the universe (the "God's eye view"), the state is static and timeless. From inside the universe — from the perspective of an observer who uses an internal clock as a temporal reference — the world evolves, and that evolution is the quantum evolution familiar from standard quantum mechanics.

## Experimental Demonstration

The Page-Wootters mechanism was, for decades, a theoretical proposal with no experimental test. In 2014, Ekaterina Moreva and collaborators reported an experimental demonstration of the mechanism using pairs of entangled photons (Moreva et al. 2014).

The experiment involved two entangled photons. One photon was used as a "clock" — its polarization state played the role of the clock reading. The other photon was the "rest of the universe." From an external perspective (using the absolute laboratory time), the total state of the two-photon system was approximately static (in the sense relevant to the experiment). From an "internal" perspective — conditioned on the polarization state of the clock photon — the other photon appeared to evolve.

The experiment demonstrated, in a small-scale quantum system, that the Page-Wootters mechanism is a real physical phenomenon, not just a mathematical trick. Observers who use internal degrees of freedom as clocks genuinely observe time-like evolution, even when the global state is static.

## Philosophical Implications

The Page-Wootters mechanism raises profound philosophical questions about the nature of time.

**Is time fundamental or emergent?** The mechanism suggests that time, as experienced by internal observers, is emergent from correlations between subsystems of a timeless universe. There is no fundamental "flow of time" at the quantum gravitational level; time is what internal clocks measure, and what internal clocks measure arises from entanglement. On this view, time is no more fundamental than temperature: just as temperature is an emergent property of many-particle systems, time is an emergent property of many-subsystem universes.

**The status of the present.** If time is relational — defined by correlations between subsystems — then the "present moment" is also relational: it is defined by the current reading of the internal clock. Different internal clocks within the universe may define different "present moments," just as different observers in relativity define different simultaneity surfaces.

**Temporal experience.** The mechanism suggests an account of why we experience time as flowing: our brains are physical systems, and our experience of time is correlated with the changing state of our neurons and the environment. This changing state is the "temporal evolution" that the Page-Wootters mechanism makes available to internal observers. The "flow of time" is the sequence of correlated states that an observer traverses as the universal state's conditional structure is explored.

**The limits of the mechanism.** The Page-Wootters mechanism, while suggestive, is not a complete theory of time. It does not, by itself, explain the direction of time (why time appears to run in one direction rather than being bidirectional). It does not explain why the correlations take the specific form they do. And it operates within a theoretical framework (quantum gravity) that is not yet complete.

Despite these limitations, the Page-Wootters mechanism is among the most philosophically interesting results in the foundations of quantum mechanics and quantum gravity. It suggests that time — the most fundamental feature of our experience — may be not fundamental at all, but a structure that emerges from the relational properties of entangled subsystems of the universe.
