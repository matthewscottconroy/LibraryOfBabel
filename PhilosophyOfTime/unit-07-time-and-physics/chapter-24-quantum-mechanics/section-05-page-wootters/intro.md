# Section 5: The Page-Wootters Mechanism and Emergent Time

The deepest question raised by quantum mechanics for the philosophy of time is not about the measurement problem or interpretations. It is the question that connects quantum mechanics to quantum gravity: what if there is no external time parameter? What if the universe has no background time against which quantum states evolve?

This is not a purely speculative question. In quantum gravity — particularly the canonical quantization of general relativity — the problem of time (Chapter 25) emerges precisely because the universe as a whole has no external time against which it can evolve. The Wheeler-DeWitt equation, which governs the quantum state of the universe, contains no time derivative: the state is "frozen," static, with no evolution.

Don Page and William Wootters proposed a mechanism in 1983 by which time can be emergent in a timeless quantum universe: time can arise from correlations between entangled subsystems of the universe. One subsystem serves as a "clock" — its quantum state is correlated with the states of other subsystems, and measuring the clock subsystem gives information about what time it is, and hence about the state of the rest of the universe "at that time." This section examines the Page-Wootters mechanism and its recent experimental demonstration.
