# Chapter 25 Exercises

## Conceptual and Analytical Exercises

**1. The Incompatibility**
(a) How does quantum mechanics treat time, and how does general relativity treat time? (b) Why do these two treatments conflict when we attempt to build a quantum theory of gravity? (c) What does it mean to say that "time is in superposition" in quantum gravity? Is this a coherent concept?

**2. The Wheeler-DeWitt Equation**
(a) Explain how the Wheeler-DeWitt equation arises from the canonical quantization of general relativity. Why does it have no time derivative? (b) The equation appears to describe a "frozen" universe. What are the main proposed solutions to this apparent problem? (c) Which solution do you find most philosophically satisfying, and why?

**3. The Problem of Time**
Isham (1992) identified multiple distinct facets of the problem of time in quantum gravity. Choose three of these facets and explain each one clearly. For each, describe whether any of the approaches discussed in this chapter (LQG, CDT, timeless approaches) offers a resolution.

**4. Loop Quantum Gravity and Discrete Space-Time**
(a) What are spin networks, and what do they represent in LQG? (b) What does the discreteness of area and volume in LQG imply about the nature of space? (c) Does LQG imply that time is also discrete? (d) How does loop quantum cosmology replace the Big Bang singularity with a Big Bounce, and what does this imply about whether time had a beginning?

**5. Rovelli's Relational Time**
(a) What does Rovelli mean by "partial observables" and "relational observables"? Why does he think observables in quantum gravity must be relational? (b) State the thermal time hypothesis. (c) Evaluate the thermal time hypothesis: is it circular? Does it recover the right physics?

**6. AdS/CFT and Temporal Structure**
(a) What is the AdS/CFT correspondence? (b) How does time in the bulk gravitational theory correspond to time in the boundary CFT? (c) What does AdS/CFT suggest about whether spacetime (including its temporal structure) is fundamental or emergent? (d) Does AdS/CFT resolve the black hole information paradox? If so, how?

**7. Causal Dynamical Triangulation**
(a) What is the difference between Euclidean dynamical triangulation and causal dynamical triangulation? (b) Why does the causal constraint in CDT lead to better physical results (four-dimensional spacetime)? (c) What does CDT suggest about the relationship between causality and temporal structure?

**8. Barbour's Timeless Universe**
(a) Explain Barbour's concept of "Platonia." (b) On Barbour's view, what is the wavefunction of the universe, and what does it assign amplitudes to? (c) How does Barbour explain our experience of time if there is no fundamental time? (d) What are the two most serious objections to Barbour's position?

**9. Smolin's Real Time**
(a) What is Smolin's "principle of precedence," and what does it imply about the status of the laws of physics? (b) Why does Smolin reject the block universe interpretation of relativity? (c) What does Smolin mean when he says that "even the laws of physics evolve"? Is this a coherent claim?

**10. Barbour vs. Smolin**
Write a structured philosophical essay (800–1,000 words) comparing Barbour's timeless universe to Smolin's real time. Your essay should (a) state each position clearly, (b) identify the best argument for each position, (c) identify the most serious objection to each position, and (d) defend your own view on which position is more defensible, with reasons.

## Observational and Laboratory Exercises

**11. The Planck Scale**
The Planck scale is the energy/length/time scale at which quantum gravitational effects are expected to become important. Calculate: (a) the Planck length l_P = √(ℏG/c³), using ℏ = 1.055 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ m³/(kg·s²), c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s; (b) the Planck time t_P = l_P/c; (c) the Planck energy E_P = ℏ/t_P. Express each in SI units. How does the Planck length compare to the size of a proton (≈10⁻¹⁵ m)? How does the Planck time compare to the timescale of nuclear processes (≈10⁻²³ s)? Reflect on why quantum gravitational effects are currently inaccessible to experiment.

**12. Loop Quantum Cosmology and the Big Bounce**
Research the predictions of loop quantum cosmology for the very early universe. (a) How does the discrete quantum geometry of LQG prevent the divergence of curvature at the Big Bang? (b) What is the "Big Bounce"? How does it differ from the Big Bang in standard GR? (c) Does loop quantum cosmology predict that the universe existed before the Big Bounce? Is this testable? (d) Write a 400-word reflection on what the Big Bounce scenario implies about whether time had a beginning. Compare this with the options discussed in Chapter 22.

**13. Thought Experiment: The Universe's Wavefunction**
Consider the following thought experiment, based on the Page-Wootters mechanism and the Wheeler-DeWitt equation. Suppose the universe is described by a static wavefunction Ψ(q_clock, q_rest), where q_clock is the state of a "clock" subsystem (e.g., a radioactive atom's decay) and q_rest is the state of everything else. (a) Describe what it would mean, from an "internal" perspective (for an observer who is part of q_rest), to experience temporal evolution. (b) What would happen to the observer's experience of time if the clock subsystem became decohered from q_rest — if the entanglement were lost? (c) Now consider: could the experience of temporal direction (past → future) be explained by the Page-Wootters mechanism? What additional structure (analogous to the Past Hypothesis) would be needed to account for the directionality of time in this framework? (d) Write a reflection (400 words) on what this thought experiment suggests about the relationship between quantum entanglement, the observer, and the experience of time.
