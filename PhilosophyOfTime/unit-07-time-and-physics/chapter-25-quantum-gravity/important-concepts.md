# Important Concepts: Chapter 25

**Problem of Time**
The family of conceptual difficulties that arise when attempting to unify quantum mechanics and general relativity. In QM, time is an external classical parameter; in GR, time is a dynamical field. Quantizing gravity makes the metric (including its temporal component) a quantum degree of freedom, creating conflict with QM's requirement for a classical external time. The problem of time encompasses: frozen dynamics, multiple choice of internal time, global time problem, Hilbert space problem, and time-reparametrization invariance.

**Wheeler-DeWitt Equation**
The equation Ĥ|Ψ⟩ = 0 that arises in the canonical quantization of general relativity. The Hamiltonian constraint Ĥ generates time reparametrization, and setting it to zero means the quantum state of the universe has no time evolution. The wavefunction of the universe is static — a solution to a constraint equation, not an evolution equation. This is the central mathematical expression of the problem of time.

**Frozen Dynamics**
The appearance, in the Wheeler-DeWitt equation, that the quantum universe does not evolve. The wavefunction satisfies Ĥ|Ψ⟩ = 0, which has no time derivative. Physical evolution must be recovered as an emergent, approximate, or relational phenomenon rather than being fundamental.

**Internal Time**
A physical degree of freedom within the universe that can serve as a clock, replacing the absent external time parameter. Choosing an internal time variable allows one to write an effective Schrödinger equation for the remaining degrees of freedom, with the internal clock providing the "time." Different choices of internal time may lead to inequivalent quantum theories (the multiple choice problem).

**Semiclassical Approximation**
The approximation scheme in quantum gravity in which the heavy gravitational degrees of freedom are treated classically (as a background metric) and the light matter degrees of freedom are quantized against this background. In the semiclassical approximation, an effective time parameter emerges from the background metric, and ordinary quantum field theory in curved spacetime is recovered. Classical time is therefore an approximate, emergent concept.

**Spin Network**
A quantum state of three-dimensional space in loop quantum gravity. A spin network is a graph with half-integer "spin" labels on edges and nodes, representing quantum excitations of the gravitational field. The area and volume of spatial regions in LQG are determined by the spin network state and take discrete values (the Planck area and volume).

**Spin Foam**
The four-dimensional analogue of a spin network in loop quantum gravity, representing a quantum history of the gravitational field between two spin network states. Spin foams provide a covariant formulation of LQG dynamics, analogous to a path integral over discrete spacetime geometries.

**Loop Quantum Cosmology (LQC)**
The application of loop quantum gravity techniques to cosmology. LQC replaces the Big Bang singularity of classical GR with a "Big Bounce": the universe contracts to a minimum but finite size at the Planck scale, then expands. The quantum discreteness of the geometry prevents the divergence of curvature that produces the classical singularity.

**Thermal Time Hypothesis**
Rovelli and Connes's (1994) proposal that in a generally covariant quantum theory without a preferred time, physical time is defined by the modular flow of the thermal state of the universe. Time is not an external background but an emergent consequence of the thermodynamic state: the thermal time flow is the natural dynamical flow associated with the state the universe is in.

**Partial Observable**
In Rovelli's relational approach to quantum gravity, a partial observable is a quantity that can be measured but whose value is physically meaningful only in relation to another observable. Physical observables in quantum gravity are complete observables: relational correlations between partial observables. The position of a particle at "time t" (where t is an external parameter) is replaced by the correlation between the particle position and the clock reading.

**Platonia**
Julian Barbour's term for the space of all possible instantaneous configurations of the universe — the fundamental ontological landscape of his timeless quantum gravity. Each point in Platonia is a "Now" — a complete three-dimensional configuration. The wavefunction of the universe assigns amplitudes to points in Platonia. Physical existence consists of configurations with high amplitude.

**Holographic Principle**
The principle, suggested by black hole thermodynamics and confirmed in the AdS/CFT correspondence, that the physical degrees of freedom of a (d+1)-dimensional gravitational system can be encoded on a d-dimensional boundary surface. The holographic principle implies that spacetime — including its temporal structure — may be encoded in lower-dimensional, non-gravitational degrees of freedom.

**AdS/CFT Correspondence**
The duality between a (d+1)-dimensional theory of gravity in anti-de Sitter space (the "bulk") and a d-dimensional conformal field theory (CFT) on the boundary of AdS (the "boundary"). Proposed by Maldacena (1997), AdS/CFT is the most concrete realization of the holographic principle and implies that bulk spacetime (including temporal structure) is encoded in boundary entanglement.

**Causal Dynamical Triangulation (CDT)**
A non-perturbative approach to quantum gravity that sums over discrete spacetime geometries (triangulations) with a causal constraint enforcing a consistent time-directed foliation. CDT produces a physically sensible four-dimensional spacetime in its continuum limit, suggesting that the causal structure of time must be imposed fundamentally rather than derived.

**Principle of Precedence**
Lee Smolin and Roberto Unger's principle that the universe follows its own historical regularities: the "laws" of physics are not eternal but emerge from and evolve with the history of the universe. Similar situations in the past generate similar tendencies in the present. Laws are habits, not eternal truths.

**Emergent Time**
The view that time is not a fundamental feature of reality but arises from more basic structures — quantum correlations, entanglement, thermodynamic states, or the geometry of configuration space. Contrasted with fundamental time, which is presupposed as a basic given. Barbour, Rovelli, and the Page-Wootters mechanism all support versions of emergent time.

**Fundamental Time**
The view, associated particularly with Lee Smolin, that time is the most basic feature of physical reality and cannot be reduced to or derived from anything more fundamental. The present moment is real; the past is gone; the future is open. Laws may evolve over time. Contrasted with emergent time.
