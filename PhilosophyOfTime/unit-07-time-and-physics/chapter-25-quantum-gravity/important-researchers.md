# Important Researchers: Chapter 25

**Bryce DeWitt (1923–2004)**
American physicist who made foundational contributions to quantum gravity and quantum field theory in curved spacetime. His 1967 paper "Quantum Theory of Gravity. I. The Canonical Theory" derived the equation bearing his name (the Wheeler-DeWitt equation), which remains the central mathematical expression of the problem of time in canonical quantum gravity. DeWitt also made major contributions to the quantization of gauge theories and the path integral formulation of quantum gravity. He was a champion of the Everett interpretation of quantum mechanics and contributed to its development.

**John Archibald Wheeler (1911–2008)**
American physicist, one of the most visionary and influential figures in twentieth-century theoretical physics. His contributions span nuclear physics, general relativity, and quantum gravity. He coined many of the field's most memorable terms: "black hole," "wormhole," "it from bit," and "quantum foam." His vision of spacetime as subject to quantum fluctuations at the Planck scale, and his insistence that information is at the heart of physics ("it from bit"), shaped the questions that quantum gravity researchers have pursued for decades. His collaborations with DeWitt produced the framework of canonical quantum gravity.

**Carlo Rovelli (1956–)**
Italian theoretical physicist, co-developer of loop quantum gravity with Lee Smolin and Abhay Ashtekar. Rovelli's relational interpretation of quantum gravity, the thermal time hypothesis (developed with Alain Connes), and his popular book *The Order of Time* (2018) have made him one of the most philosophically engaged physicists of his generation. His position — that time does not exist at the fundamental level and emerges from thermodynamics and quantum correlations — is one of the most radical and seriously argued positions in the contemporary philosophy of time.

**Julian Barbour (1937–)**
British independent physicist who has developed the relational program in mechanics and quantum gravity over six decades. His *The End of Time* (1999) argues that time does not exist at the fundamental level: the universe is a static landscape of Nows (Platonia), and temporal experience is an artifact of the structure of the wavefunction. Barbour worked outside academia for most of his career, supporting himself by translation while pursuing his research. His work is technically sophisticated and philosophically serious.

**Lee Smolin (1955–)**
American theoretical physicist at the Perimeter Institute, co-developer of loop quantum gravity and author of *Time Reborn* (2013). Smolin is the most vigorous defender of the view that time is fundamental in contemporary physics. He argues that the laws of physics evolve, that the block universe is wrong, and that eliminating time from fundamental physics is the source of the deepest failures of contemporary physics. His debate with Barbour represents the sharpest contemporary formulation of the question of whether time is fundamental or emergent.

**Abhay Ashtekar (1949–)**
Indian-American physicist who introduced the Ashtekar variables in 1986, which reformulated general relativity in a form amenable to quantization and laid the foundation for loop quantum gravity. His contributions to quantum cosmology, the problem of time, and black hole entropy have been central to the development of canonical quantum gravity. He is the founding director of the Institute for Gravitational Physics and Geometry at Penn State.

**Jan Ambjorn (1952–), Jerzy Jurkiewicz (1946–), and Renate Loll (1962–)**
The three physicists who developed causal dynamical triangulation as a non-perturbative approach to quantum gravity. Their 2000 and 2004 papers demonstrated that imposing a causal constraint on the sum over discrete spacetime geometries produces a physically sensible four-dimensional spacetime in the continuum limit. Their approach gives time a special, irreducible role in quantum gravity — making CDT a distinctive alternative to the timeless approaches.

**Juan Maldacena (1968–)**
Argentine-American theoretical physicist at the Institute for Advanced Study, whose 1997 paper proposing the AdS/CFT correspondence is the most cited paper in the history of string theory (and among the most cited in all of physics). The AdS/CFT correspondence has had far-reaching implications for quantum gravity, black hole physics, and the foundations of spacetime, including its suggestion that temporal structure may be emergent from entanglement.

**Christopher Isham (1944–)**
British theoretical physicist who made foundational contributions to quantum gravity and the problem of time. His 1992 review "Canonical Quantum Gravity and the Problem of Time" is the standard reference for the problem's classification and analysis. Isham combined technical depth with philosophical clarity and is one of the few physicists to have engaged seriously with the philosophical literature on time. His work on topos theory as a mathematical framework for quantum gravity reflects his commitment to foundational questions.

**Karel Kuchař (1936–)**
Czech-American theoretical physicist whose work on the problem of time, canonical quantum gravity, and the interpretation of the Wheeler-DeWitt equation has been foundational. His 1992 review, alongside Isham's, defines the landscape of the problem of time. Kuchař has been a proponent of approaches that take time seriously as a physical observable, rather than eliminating it.
