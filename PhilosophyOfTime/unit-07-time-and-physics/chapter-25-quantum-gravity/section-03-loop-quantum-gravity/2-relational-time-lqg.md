# Relational Time and the Thermal Time Hypothesis

## Rovelli's Relational Interpretation

Carlo Rovelli has developed, over several decades, a relational interpretation of loop quantum gravity that has profound implications for the philosophy of time. His position is articulated in *Quantum Gravity* (2004) and, for a general audience, in *The Order of Time* (2018). The core claim is radical: there is no time at the fundamental level. Time is not a fundamental feature of the universe; it is a relational, emergent phenomenon.

In Rovelli's relational quantum gravity, physical observables are not functions of a time parameter but *partial observables* — functions of both the gravitational and matter degrees of freedom. A physical observable is a relational quantity: it describes a relation between two physical systems, rather than a property of one system at a given time.

For example, instead of asking "what is the position of a particle at time t?" (which requires a background time parameter), we ask "what is the position of the particle when the clock reads value C?" This is a relational observable: the correlation between the particle position and the clock reading. Such observables are well-defined without any reference to external time.

## The "Order of Time" Dissolved

Rovelli's most provocative claim is that many of the features we normally attribute to time — its flow, its directionality, its division into past, present, and future — are not fundamental features of physical reality but emerge from the statistical mechanics of large systems, from thermodynamics, and from our particular position within the universe.

In a universe with few degrees of freedom, Rovelli argues, there is no distinction between past and future. The laws of physics (including the Wheeler-DeWitt equation) are time-symmetric. The direction of time emerges only when we consider large systems and focus on macroscopic, thermodynamic variables — entropy, heat flow, the smearing over of microscopic details. The arrow of time is a consequence of our coarse-grained, macroscopic perspective, not of any fundamental asymmetry in the laws.

This is an elegant view, and in many respects it aligns with the position developed in Chapter 23 (the thermodynamic origin of the arrow of time). Rovelli takes it further, however: he argues that even the *passage* of time — the sense in which time flows — is an effect of the fact that we are thermal systems, embedded in a thermodynamic context. We experience time as flowing because our memories are correlated with entropy increases, because our brains are dissipative systems, and because we are always in partial ignorance about the microstate of our environment.

## The Thermal Time Hypothesis

Rovelli and Connes (1994) proposed the *thermal time hypothesis* as a way of understanding how time arises in a generally covariant, timeless theory. The proposal is: in a generally covariant quantum theory, there is no preferred time evolution. But if we are given a thermal state (a state at thermal equilibrium), we can define a natural time evolution: the one generated by the modular Hamiltonian of the thermal state.

More precisely: given a density matrix ρ, the modular Hamiltonian is K = -ln ρ. The thermal time hypothesis asserts that the physical time flow is the one generated by the flow of K as an automorphism of the observable algebra. Time, on this hypothesis, is the flow generated by the thermal state of the universe — not a background parameter, but an emergent consequence of the thermodynamic state we find ourselves in.

The thermal time hypothesis is controversial. Critics argue that it is circular (you need to know what "time" is to define a thermal state), and that it does not recover the expected dynamics for matter fields in simple cases. Rovelli and collaborators have responded to these objections, but the debate is ongoing.

## Philosophical Assessment

Rovelli's relational and thermal interpretation of time is philosophically serious and scientifically motivated. It takes seriously the implications of the Wheeler-DeWitt equation (timeless), of general relativity (relational), and of thermodynamics (entropy grounds the arrow of time). It connects these in a unified picture in which time at the fundamental level is absent, but emerges at the macroscopic level from thermal correlations.

The picture is not complete. The thermal time hypothesis faces technical challenges. The connection between the relational quantum gravity program and ordinary physics (including quantum field theory in flat spacetime) is not fully established. And the philosophical implications — if time is merely emergent, what are we to make of temporal experience, the reality of the past and future, and the metaphysics of change? — require further development.

But Rovelli's work represents one of the most ambitious and philosophically engaged attempts to grapple with the problem of time in quantum gravity. His insistence that taking physics seriously requires abandoning the naive notion of a fundamental, flowing time is a challenge that philosophy of time must take seriously.
