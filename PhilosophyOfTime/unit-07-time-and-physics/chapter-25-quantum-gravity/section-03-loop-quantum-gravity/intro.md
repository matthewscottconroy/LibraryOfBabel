# Section 3: Loop Quantum Gravity

Loop quantum gravity (LQG) is one of the two leading approaches to quantum gravity, alongside string theory. Developed primarily by Carlo Rovelli and Lee Smolin in the late 1980s and early 1990s, LQG takes a different path from string theory: it attempts to quantize general relativity directly, without adding extra dimensions or new fundamental strings. The result is a theory in which space (and, in certain formulations, time) is discrete at the Planck scale — the fundamental quantum of area is approximately 10⁻⁷⁰ m², and the fundamental quantum of volume is approximately 10⁻¹⁰⁵ m³.

Loop quantum gravity has a distinctive and philosophically rich treatment of time. In its canonical formulation, LQG inherits the problem of time from the Wheeler-DeWitt equation — there is no external time, and dynamics is encoded in the Hamiltonian constraint. But in its covariant formulation (spin foam models), a version of time evolution appears. And Carlo Rovelli's relational interpretation of LQG, developed most fully in his books *Quantum Gravity* (2004) and *The Order of Time* (2018), proposes a thoroughly relational and thermal account of time with far-reaching philosophical implications.
