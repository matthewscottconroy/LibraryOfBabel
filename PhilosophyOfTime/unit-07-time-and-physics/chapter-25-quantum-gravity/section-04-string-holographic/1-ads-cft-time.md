# AdS/CFT and the Nature of Temporal Structure

## The AdS/CFT Correspondence

In 1997, Juan Maldacena proposed a remarkable duality between two seemingly different theories (Maldacena 1997). One theory is a gravitational theory in anti-de Sitter (AdS) space — a spacetime with constant negative curvature, like a hyperbolic space. The other theory is a conformal field theory (CFT) — a quantum field theory without gravity — that lives on the boundary of the AdS space, a space with one fewer dimension.

The duality is holographic: all the physics of the (d+1)-dimensional bulk gravitational theory is encoded in the d-dimensional boundary theory. There is a precise dictionary that translates between the two descriptions. Every physical state in the bulk has a corresponding state in the CFT, and every observable in the bulk corresponds to an observable in the CFT.

The word "holographic" is apt: a hologram encodes three-dimensional information on a two-dimensional surface. The AdS/CFT correspondence suggests that the (d+1)-dimensional spacetime of the bulk — including its temporal dimension — is encoded in the d-dimensional boundary theory, which has no gravitational degrees of freedom.

## Time in AdS/CFT

The relationship between time in the bulk and time in the boundary theory is non-trivial and philosophically revealing.

In the bulk AdS theory, time is one of the (d+1) spacetime dimensions — it is dynamical, subject to quantum gravitational fluctuations (in principle), and affects the geometry of the spacetime. In the boundary CFT, time is also present — it is one of the d dimensions of the boundary spacetime — but it is not gravitational. The boundary CFT is a quantum field theory in a fixed (d-dimensional) spacetime, with no metric fluctuations in the boundary theory itself.

The correspondence between bulk time and boundary time is not a simple identification. In a static background (a time-independent AdS metric), the bulk time coordinate corresponds to the global time coordinate of the boundary CFT. But for time-dependent bulk geometries — including black holes, which form and evaporate — the relationship is more complex. The bulk temporal evolution of the black hole corresponds to the unitary evolution of a thermal state in the boundary CFT.

The most philosophically striking implication: **the formation and evaporation of a black hole in the bulk corresponds to a unitary process in the boundary CFT.** This is the resolution of the black hole information paradox within AdS/CFT: information is not destroyed by the black hole because the boundary CFT is unitary and never destroys information. The bulk time evolution, while appearing to lose information (when viewed from the bulk perspective alone), is in fact a unitary transformation when viewed from the boundary.

## Temporal Structure as Emergent from the Boundary

The AdS/CFT correspondence suggests a striking perspective on temporal structure: the bulk spacetime, including its temporal dimension, may be *emergent* from the boundary CFT. The boundary theory is more fundamental; the bulk spacetime, including its temporal structure, arises from the dynamics of the boundary degrees of freedom.

This is a form of temporal emergence more radical than the Page-Wootters mechanism: it is not just that time emerges from quantum correlations within a timeless universe, but that the entire spacetime geometry — including time — is encoded in the entanglement structure of the boundary theory.

Recent work (Ryu and Takayanagi 2006; Raamsdonk 2010) has suggested that the entanglement entropy of the boundary CFT is directly related to the geometry of the bulk spacetime. In particular, van Raamsdonk argued that the entanglement between different regions of the boundary CFT is what "holds together" the bulk spacetime: as entanglement decreases, the corresponding bulk spacetime splits apart. This suggests that spacetime connectivity — and hence the ability to define temporal structure — is a consequence of quantum entanglement.

## Philosophical Implications

The AdS/CFT correspondence raises philosophical questions about the nature of spacetime and time that are among the deepest in contemporary physics.

**Is spacetime fundamental?** If the bulk spacetime (including its temporal structure) is dual to a non-gravitational boundary theory, in what sense is the bulk spacetime "real"? One could argue that the bulk description is merely a convenient representation of the boundary physics — that the "true" ontology is the boundary CFT, and the bulk spacetime is a derived, emergent description. On this view, spacetime is not fundamental: it is a holographic projection of more fundamental quantum degrees of freedom.

**What is temporal structure?** If time in the bulk emerges from entanglement in the boundary, then temporal structure — the very framework within which we define "past" and "future" — is not primitive but derived from the quantum information structure of the boundary theory. This is perhaps the most radical suggestion about the nature of time that theoretical physics has yet produced.

**The limits of current understanding.** AdS/CFT is the best understood example of holography, but it applies to AdS spacetimes with negative cosmological constant — not to our universe, which has positive cosmological constant (de Sitter space). Extending the holographic principle to de Sitter space is an active and difficult research problem. The philosophical conclusions about emergent time must therefore be understood as tentative, applicable to a class of spacetimes that may not include our own.
