# Section 4: String Theory, Holography, and Time

String theory — the approach to quantum gravity based on replacing point particles with one-dimensional extended objects (strings) — is the other leading candidate for a theory of quantum gravity. String theory has a different relationship to the problem of time than loop quantum gravity: it works in a fixed spacetime background (usually ten-dimensional), treats the metric as a quantum fluctuation around this background, and aims to recover GR as the low-energy limit. In this framework, time is treated as a classical background parameter at the perturbative level, and the problem of time appears only at the non-perturbative level.

The AdS/CFT correspondence (Maldacena 1997), however — the most celebrated result of string theory — suggests something remarkable about the nature of temporal structure: the full quantum gravity theory in a (d+1)-dimensional spacetime (the "bulk") is dual to a non-gravitational field theory in d dimensions (the "boundary"). Time in the bulk corresponds to something different in the boundary theory, and the relationship between them illuminates the holographic nature of spacetime and the emergence of temporal structure.
