# Section 6: Emergent Time vs. Fundamental Time

The debate over the ultimate status of time in physics reaches its sharpest formulation in the contrast between two positions associated with two physicists who are friends, collaborators, and vigorous intellectual opponents: Julian Barbour and Lee Smolin. Both have thought deeply about the problem of time in physics; both have written books for general audiences articulating their views; and they disagree fundamentally.

Barbour holds that time does not exist at the fundamental level: the universe is composed of "nows" — instantaneous configurations — and the appearance of time is an artifact of the structure of the wavefunction over the space of all possible configurations. Smolin holds that time is the most fundamental thing in physics: even the laws of physics evolve, and the attempt to eliminate time from fundamental physics is the deepest mistake of our age.

Their debate is the culmination of the inquiry this unit has pursued. It asks, at the highest level of generality: should physics aim to explain time, or to assume it? Is time a feature of the world that our theories must account for, or the very medium in which all explanation takes place?
