# Chapter 26 Exercises: The Phenomenology of Time-Consciousness

## Analytical Exercises

**1. Brentano vs. Husserl on the Just-Past**
Brentano argues that past tones persist in consciousness in a "modified" mode — as just-now tones with a quality of pastness attached. Husserl argues that this account confuses the content of the present impression with the intentional act directed at the past. In your own words, reconstruct this disagreement as precisely as you can. Which account do you find more persuasive, and why? Your answer should engage with the specific phenomenological datum at issue: the experience of hearing a melody.

**2. The Three Structures of the Living Present**
Husserl distinguishes three phases of the living present: primal impression, retention, and protention. For each phase, explain (a) what it is, (b) what it is directed toward, and (c) why it is necessary for the experience of temporal objects. Then explain Husserl's crucial claim that retention is not memory. What exactly is the difference, and why does it matter?

**3. Interpreting Husserl's Diagram**
Describe Husserl's time-diagram in your own words, explaining what each axis represents and what the diagonal "sinking" of past moments represents. What feature of temporal experience does the diagram capture that could not be captured by a simple horizontal timeline? What, if anything, does the diagram fail to capture?

**4. Heidegger's Ecstases**
Heidegger describes authentic temporality as involving three "ecstases": running ahead toward death (future), taking over thrownness (having-been), and the moment of vision (present). Explain each ecstasis and then explain why Heidegger insists that these three are not three separate things but three moments of a unified structure. How does this differ from simply saying that life involves a past, present, and future?

**5. Vulgar vs. Originary Temporality**
Heidegger argues that "vulgar" time — the ordinary sequence of nows available to anyone — is derived from, and less fundamental than, originary temporality. Is this an empirical claim or a conceptual/ontological one? What would it mean for the claim to be false? Evaluate the argument.

**6. Merleau-Ponty and Embodied Habit**
Merleau-Ponty argues that the body carries the past in its habits, not merely in its memories. Using an example of a specific skill or habit you have developed, explain what Merleau-Ponty would say about the temporal structure of that habit. How does this account differ from Husserl's account of retention? How does it enrich or challenge it?

**7. Ricoeur's Threefold Mimesis**
Explain the three phases of mimesis in Ricoeur's account: prefiguration, configuration, and refiguration. Then apply the framework to a specific narrative you know well — a novel, a film, a historical account, or a story from your own life. What does the analysis reveal about how that narrative configures temporal experience?

**8. The Aporia of Time**
Ricoeur argues that the "aporia of time" — the tension between subjective and objective time — cannot be resolved by phenomenological analysis but can be negotiated through narrative. Is this a satisfying conclusion? Is it a form of philosophical defeat, or is it a genuine philosophical achievement? Defend your view with reference to the specific aporias Ricoeur identifies.

## Comparative Essay Exercises

**9. Husserl and Heidegger on the Present**
Compare Husserl's concept of the "primal impression" with Heidegger's concept of the *Augenblick* (moment of vision). Both are accounts of the present, but they are very different accounts. What does each capture that the other misses? Is there a way to integrate them, or are they fundamentally incompatible?

**10. Embodiment and Temporal Constitution**
Merleau-Ponty argues that temporal experience is grounded in the motor body rather than in pure consciousness. Critically evaluate this claim. Are there aspects of temporal experience — such as the experience of regret, anticipation of death, or narrative self-understanding — that seem to require more than the motor body can provide? What does your assessment imply about the relationship between Merleau-Ponty's account and those of Husserl and Ricoeur?

## Observational and Experiential Lab Exercises

**11. The Melody Experiment (Lab)**
This exercise requires approximately 20 minutes and a recording of a short piece of music — preferably something unfamiliar.

First, listen to the piece once with your eyes closed. After listening, write a paragraph describing your experience. Try to capture: Did you hear the piece as a unified musical object, or as a series of separate sounds? Were you aware, while hearing any given note, of what came before it? Was there a sense of forward momentum, of the music "going somewhere"?

Second, listen again, but this time try to attend specifically to the phenomenological structure Husserl describes: as each note sounds, notice the "trailing" presence of the previous notes (the retention), the freshness of the current note (the primal impression), and the forward lean toward what is coming (the protention). Write a paragraph describing whether you can identify these three structures in your actual experience.

Third, reflect: does Husserl's analysis match your experience? What aspects does it capture well? What aspects does it miss or distort?

**12. Embodied Temporal Habit (Lab)**
Choose a physical skill you have practiced enough to perform habitually — typing, playing an instrument, riding a bicycle, cooking a familiar dish, or any comparable activity. Perform the activity for five to ten minutes. Then answer the following questions:

(a) During the activity, were you consciously recalling how to do it, or was the knowledge "in your hands"? What is the difference?
(b) Merleau-Ponty says that a habit is the past "sedimented" in the body. Can you identify the sense in which your past practice is present in your current performance — not as memory, but as practical disposition?
(c) Was there a sense in which your body was anticipating the next movements before you consciously directed it? Describe this as precisely as you can.
(d) Now compare your experience with Husserl's account of protention. Are these the same phenomenon? Are they related? How?

**13. Temporal Field of a Waiting Experience (Lab)**
Find an occasion when you are genuinely waiting for something — waiting for an important email, waiting in a waiting room, waiting for a result to come in. During the wait, pay close attention to your temporal experience and take notes (or write them up immediately afterward) addressing the following:

(a) Describe the "temporal field" of the waiting experience. Is the future narrowed to a point (the awaited event), or does it have a broader structure?
(b) Is your experience of the past different during the wait than at other times? Do past events seem more or less present? More or less distant?
(c) Merleau-Ponty describes the "field of presence" as a feature of embodied experience. Does your body seem to be involved in the waiting — not just your mind? Describe any bodily dimensions of the temporal experience.
(d) Does the experience of waiting confirm, challenge, or complicate any of the phenomenological analyses in this chapter? Explain.
