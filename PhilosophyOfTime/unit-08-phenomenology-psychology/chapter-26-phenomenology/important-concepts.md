# Important Concepts: Chapter 26

**Absolute Flow** (*absoluter Fluss*): Husserl's term for the self-constituting flow of consciousness that underlies all temporal experience. The absolute flow is not in time in the ordinary sense; it is what constitutes temporal experience from the inside, without requiring a further temporal framework to explain its own temporality. Its self-constituting character makes it the ultimate ground of all time-consciousness.

**Aporia of Time**: Ricoeur's term for the fundamental tension in philosophical accounts of time between the subjective (phenomenological, Augustinian) perspective and the objective (cosmological, Aristotelian) perspective. This tension cannot be definitively resolved by philosophical analysis alone but can be "negotiated" through narrative configuration. The aporia is a permanent feature of the human condition, not a solvable problem.

**Authentic Temporality** (*eigentliche Zeitlichkeit*): Heidegger's term for the originary temporal structure of *Dasein* as it exists in anticipatory resoluteness — running ahead toward death, taking over its thrownness, and making resolute decisions in the moment of vision. Authentic temporality is not a sequence of nows but a unified ecstatic structure in which future, having-been, and present are co-constitutive.

**Augenblick** (Moment of Vision): Heidegger's term for the authentic present — the present as it is constituted within anticipatory resoluteness. The *Augenblick* is not the neutral "now" of public time but the moment in which *Dasein*, clear-eyed about its finitude and resolute about its possibilities, sees its situation and acts. It has weight and significance that ordinary clock-time lacks.

**Care** (*Sorge*): Heidegger's term for the fundamental structure of *Dasein*'s being: being-ahead-of-itself (projection), already-in (thrownness), and alongside (falling). Care is the unified structure that authentic temporality makes possible. The analysis of temporality in Division II of *Being and Time* is an analysis of the ontological ground of care.

**Ecstases of Temporality**: Heidegger's term for the three fundamental "standing-outside-of-oneself" moments that constitute authentic temporality: future (running ahead), having-been (thrownness), and present (moment of vision). "Ecstasis" (literally: standing outside) captures the idea that *Dasein* is always already "outside itself" toward its past and future, not enclosed in a punctual now.

**Emplotment** (*mise en intrigue*): Ricoeur's term for the configurational act at the heart of narrative — the operation by which a narrator weaves together diverse events, characters, and circumstances into a unified, followable story with a beginning, middle, and end. Emplotment transforms a mere sequence of events into a narrative whole. It is Ricoeur's answer to the aporia of time: by configuring events into a plot, narrative gives time a human shape.

**Field of Presence**: Merleau-Ponty's term for the extended temporal field within which embodied experience is always already situated. Rather than being organized around a central "now" that radiates outward through retention and protention, temporal experience is field-like: I am in a temporal context that surrounds me, constituted by my bodily habits and practical orientations. The field of presence is pre-given by embodiment, not constructed by conscious acts.

**Ipse-Identity**: Ricoeur's term for the identity of selfhood — the kind of identity that consists in remaining true to oneself in the sense of keeping commitments and promises. Ipse-identity is distinct from idem-identity (numerical or qualitative sameness) because it is not a static property of an object but a dynamic achievement of a self that persists through time by honoring its commitments.

**Living Present** (*lebendige Gegenwart*): Husserl's term for the experienced now, understood as an extended, three-part structure rather than a punctual instant. The living present encompasses the primal impression of the current moment, the retention of the just-past, and the protention of the about-to-come. It is the most fundamental structure of time-consciousness and the ground of any possible experience of temporal objects.

**Mimesis**: Ricoeur's term (borrowed from Aristotle's *Poetics*) for the threefold process by which narrative mediates between lived temporal experience and its configuration into humanly intelligible form. The three phases — prefiguration (mimesis I), configuration (mimesis II), and refiguration (mimesis III) — describe the circular relationship between lived experience and narrative that gives time its human character.

**Modification** (Brentano): Brentano's term for the transformation that past sensations undergo in consciousness: rather than being recalled by a separate act of memory, just-past sensations persist in the present moment in a modified form — as "just-now" tones with a quality of pastness. Husserl appropriates the concept of modification but argues that the modification must be of the intentional act, not of the perceived content.

**Narrative Identity**: Ricoeur's concept of personal identity over time as constituted by the coherence of a life story. Narrative identity mediates between idem-identity (numerical sameness) and ipse-identity (selfhood as commitment). It is not a fixed essence but an ongoing interpretive achievement — the self is the self that tells and retells this story about this life.

**Originary Temporality**: Heidegger's term for the authentic, ecstatic temporal structure of *Dasein*, as opposed to "vulgar time" (the ordinary sequence of nows). Originary temporality is prior to, and is the source of, vulgar time: public, clock time is a derived and leveled-off version of the existential temporal structure of finite human existence.

**Primal Impression** (*Urimpression*): Husserl's term for the phase of the living present in which something is given as strictly and genuinely now — the source-point of temporal experience. The primal impression is the moment of direct contact with the present, but it never exists in isolation: it is always already surrounded by retention (just-past) and protention (about-to-come).

**Protention**: Husserl's term for the intentional phase of the living present directed toward the about-to-come. Protention is not a conscious expectation or prediction but a pre-reflective forward anticipation built into the structure of every moment of experience. It is the "empty" intentionality that reaches toward what has not yet occurred, and it is fulfilled when the anticipated event arrives and becomes a primal impression.

**Retention**: Husserl's term for the intentional phase of the living present that holds the just-past within the current experience. Retention is not memory: it involves no separate representational act, no re-presentation of a past event. Rather, the just-past is directly given in a retentional mode — present as just-having-been, with a phenomenal quality of pastness that distinguishes it from the primal impression.

**Thrownness** (*Geworfenheit*): Heidegger's term for the factical situation into which *Dasein* has been cast without its choice — including one's historical period, culture, language, body, family, and circumstances. Thrownness is not merely the past; it is the weight of the past as it shapes and constrains current existence. Authentic temporality involves "taking over" one's thrownness — not passively accepting or actively denying it, but appropriating it as genuinely one's own.

**Vulgar Time**: Heidegger's term for the ordinary, public conception of time as an infinite sequence of indifferent "now-points" — the time of clocks, calendars, and schedules available to anyone. Vulgar time is not illusory but derivative: it is what originary temporality looks like when the existential significance of temporal ecstases is stripped away through the leveling-off and publicizing characteristic of inauthentic existence.
