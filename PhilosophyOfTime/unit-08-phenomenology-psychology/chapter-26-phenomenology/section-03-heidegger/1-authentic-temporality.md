# Authentic Temporality: The Three Ecstases

## Care and Its Temporal Meaning

Heidegger's *Being and Time* (1927) is organized around a fundamental question: what is the being of *Dasein* — of the kind of being that we are? In Division I, Heidegger argues that the fundamental structure of *Dasein* is *care* (*Sorge*): we are beings who are always already ahead of ourselves (in projection toward possibilities), always already thrown into a factical situation (a past we did not choose), and always already engaged with things and others in the world (falling into concernful dealings). Care, in Heidegger's technical sense, encompasses this entire structure: *ahead-of-itself-already-in-having-always-already-been-alongside*.

In Division II, Heidegger asks: what makes this structure possible? What is its ultimate ground? His answer is *Zeitlichkeit* — temporality. The three structural moments of care (ahead-of-itself, already-in, alongside) correspond to the three fundamental "ecstases" of temporality: the future (*Zukunft*), the having-been (*Gewesenheit*), and the present (*Gegenwart*). To say that care is grounded in temporality is to say that *Dasein* exists in a manner that is constituted by its orientation toward the future, its inheritance of the past, and its engagement in the present — and that these three orientations form a unified, mutually implicating structure (Heidegger 1962: 374–380).

## Running Ahead Toward Death: The Future Ecstasis

The primary ecstasis of authentic temporality, for Heidegger, is the future — specifically, the future understood as one's ownmost possibility: death. Heidegger argues, notoriously, that *Dasein* is always "being-toward-death" (*Sein-zum-Tode*): death is not merely an event that will eventually occur but a structural possibility that shapes the whole of existence. Being-toward-death means existing in the awareness that one's possibilities are finite, that time is not unlimited, that one cannot indefinitely postpone commitment.

Authentic temporality involves what Heidegger calls "anticipatory resoluteness" (*vorlaufende Entschlossenheit*): running ahead (*Vorlaufen*) to one's own death, not in the sense of obsessing about dying, but in the sense of living with a clear-eyed awareness of one's finitude, and thereby taking ownership of one's possibilities rather than drifting through a life organized by what "one" (*das Man*) — the anonymous, public, average self — expects and does.

When *Dasein* runs ahead to its death, it "individualizes" itself — it becomes aware that its existence is uniquely its own, not exchangeable with anyone else's. This individualizing is also a confrontation with one's thrownness: I am not thrown into possibilities in general but into this particular set of possibilities, this particular situation, this particular life.

## Taking Over Thrownness: The Having-Been Ecstasis

The second ecstasis of authentic temporality is the having-been (*Gewesenheit*). Note that Heidegger uses "having-been" rather than simply "past," and this is deliberate. The past, in ordinary temporal language, is simply what has occurred and is now over. But the having-been, in Heidegger's sense, is not over: it is the thrownness (*Geworfenheit*) that I am, the factical situation into which I have been thrown and which I carry with me.

Authentic existence involves "taking over" one's thrownness — not accepting it passively or denying it, but owning it as one's own. This is what Heidegger means by "authentic repetition" (*Wiederholung*): not nostalgic return to the past, but a resolute appropriation of the possibilities that one's historical situation makes available. I inherit a language, a culture, a set of relationships, a set of limitations and affordances — and authentic existence means taking responsibility for what I make of this inheritance rather than pretending it isn't there or that I could have been otherwise.

The having-been ecstasis also makes clear why Heidegger describes temporality as having "ecstases" — literally, "standing-outside-of-oneself" (*Ekstasen*). I am not simply a point in time that has a past; I am always already "outside myself" toward my past, always shaped by what I have been, always carrying my thrownness with me.

## The Moment of Vision: The Present Ecstasis

The third ecstasis of authentic temporality is the present — but not the present conceived as the "now" of ordinary clock time. Heidegger's term for the authentic present is the *Augenblick* — usually translated as "moment of vision" or "the moment." The *Augenblick* is the present as it appears within anticipatory resoluteness: the moment in which, having run ahead to death and taken over one's thrownness, one makes a resolute decision about how to exist in one's situation.

The *Augenblick* is not the present as a fleeting instant in a series of nows. It is the present as the moment of action and commitment, the present that is constituted by one's having-been and one's running-ahead, the present that has existential weight because it is owned. It is "the moment of vision" because, in it, *Dasein* can see its situation clearly: it can see what genuinely matters and what is merely noise, what possibilities are genuinely its own and what are merely "what one does."

## The Unity of the Ecstases

A crucial feature of Heidegger's account is that the three ecstases are not three separate faculties or three separate times. They are moments of a single, unified temporal structure. Authentic temporality is the "simultaneous" (in a non-temporal sense) holding-together of future, having-been, and present. Running ahead to death is only possible on the basis of taking over one's thrownness, and both together make possible the moment of vision in the present. Neither the future nor the having-been nor the present is primary in isolation; they co-constitute each other (Heidegger 1962: 380–382).

This unity distinguishes Heidegger's account sharply from ordinary conceptions of time as a linear sequence in which future, present, and past succeed one another. For Heidegger, authentic temporality is not a sequence at all; it is a structure. It is the structure of existence as a unified whole that reaches backward into its thrownness and forward into its possibilities, while being concretely engaged in its situation. Time, at this level, is not something that happens to *Dasein* from the outside; it is what *Dasein* is.
