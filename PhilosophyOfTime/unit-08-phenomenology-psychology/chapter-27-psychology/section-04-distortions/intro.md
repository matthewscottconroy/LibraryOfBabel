# Section 4: Temporal Experience in Extreme States and Disorders

The most revealing experiments on any cognitive system are often the natural experiments of pathology and extreme conditions. When a cognitive system breaks down — partially or completely, in specific ways — we learn something about its structure that normal functioning conceals. Temporal experience is no exception. The distortions of time perception that occur in fear, flow, depression, mania, and specific psychiatric conditions like schizophrenia provide a window onto the mechanisms that normally produce our sense of temporal continuity, the "right" pace of time, and the coherent ordering of past, present, and future.

This section examines several classes of temporal distortion, asking in each case both what the mechanisms of distortion are (an empirical question) and what the distortions reveal about the nature of temporal experience more broadly (a philosophical question). The guiding methodological principle is one that Husserl himself endorsed: the analysis of marginal, disturbed, or unusual cases can illuminate the structures of normal experience that are normally too transparent to be noticed.
