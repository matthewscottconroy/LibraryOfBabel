# Important Concepts: Chapter 28

**A-Theory of Time**: The family of views holding that the distinction between past, present, and future is a genuine, irreducible feature of temporal reality — not analyzable in terms of the B-theory's earlier-later relations. A-theories include presentism (only the present exists), the growing block theory (the present and past exist, but the future does not), and the moving spotlight theory (all times exist, but the present has a special property of "NOW"). The A-theory provides natural accommodation for objective temporal passage, which is why defenders of passage typically endorse some version of it.

**B-Theory of Time**: The view that the only genuine temporal facts are facts about the earlier-than, later-than, and simultaneous-with relations between events. The A-series properties (being past, present, or future) are either eliminable (purely eliminativist B-theory) or reducible to facts about the temporal relations events bear to the context of utterance. The B-theory is naturally associated with the block universe picture and with the no-passage view. The "B" stands for "B-series," McTaggart's term for the temporal series of before-and-after relations.

**Block Universe**: The metaphysical picture, suggested by four-dimensional spacetime physics, in which all times are equally real and the temporal dimension is, in this respect, like the spatial dimensions — just another direction in which events are distributed. In the block universe, there is no ontologically privileged "present," no objective "now," and no temporal passage: the universe "just is," spread out across all four dimensions. The block universe is the natural home of the B-theory and is the primary target of A-theory objections.

**Category Mistake**: An error that arises from applying a concept or framework to a domain for which it is inappropriate. Skow's response to the rate-of-passage problem appeals to this notion: asking "at what rate does the present move?" commits a category mistake because the concept of "rate" (requiring a second dimension against which to measure the first) does not apply to the moving of the spotlight, which is a primitive fact rather than a ratio of quantities.

**Cognitive Illusion of Passage**: The view, associated with no-passage theorists like Callender and Prosser, that the sense of temporal passage is not a veridical representation of an objective feature of the world but an artifact of cognitive architecture — specifically, of the asymmetry between how the brain represents the past (via memory traces) and how it represents the future (via anticipation). The illusion is systematic and explicable, not random or anomalous.

**Growing Block Theory**: The A-theory view that the past and present are real (as the "growing block" of already-actualized events) but the future does not yet exist. On this view, the present is the leading edge of the growing block — the moment at which new events are continually being "added" to reality. The growing block theory accommodates both the passage of time (events are continuously becoming part of the block) and the asymmetry between past and future (the past is fixed, the future is open) in a natural way.

**McTaggart's A-Series and B-Series**: J. M. E. McTaggart's distinction (1908) between two ways of ordering events in time: the B-series (ordering by earlier-than, later-than, simultaneous-with) and the A-series (ordering by past, present, and future). McTaggart argued that the A-series is more fundamental because it incorporates the passage of time — events move from future to present to past — but that the A-series is also incoherent, generating a vicious regress. This argument has generated the central debate between A-theorists and B-theorists.

**Moving Spotlight Theory**: The A-theory view, developed by Bradford Skow, that all times exist (as in the block universe) but that a special property — being "NOW" or being present — moves along the temporal sequence. The "spotlight" of the present illuminates each moment in turn, and this movement is what constitutes objective temporal passage. The theory attempts to accommodate both the reality of temporal passage and the four-dimensional structure of spacetime.

**No-Passage View**: The position, defended by Callender (2000, 2017) and Prosser (2016), among others, that there is no objective temporal passage: time does not flow, the present does not move, and the sense of passage is generated by cognitive features of temporally embedded creatures rather than by any objective feature of the temporal manifold. The no-passage view accepts the block universe picture and provides a positive cognitive explanation of why passage seems so vivid and real.

**Nowness**: The phenomenal quality that distinguishes the experienced present from experienced past or future — the quality of "this is happening now," of actuality, of what is occurring as opposed to what has occurred or will occur. The philosophical debate about temporal passage largely concerns whether this quality of nowness corresponds to an objective property of the present moment (as A-theorists hold) or whether it is a feature of the perspective of a temporally located observer (as B-theorists hold).

**Objective Becoming**: Skow's term for the genuine, non-perspectival fact that events come to be present and then recede into the past. Objective becoming is what the moving spotlight theory is designed to capture: the transition from future to present to past is not merely a change in how events are described from different perspectives but a genuine objective change in the temporal status of events.

**Phenomological Passage vs. Objective Passage**: A distinction crucial to the debate. Phenomenological passage is the felt experience of time flowing — the vivid sense that the present is moving, that the future is approaching, that we are being carried along. Objective passage would be an observer-independent feature of the world corresponding to this experience. The no-passage view accepts the reality of phenomenological passage (as a feature of experience) while denying objective passage (as a feature of the world).

**Preferred Foliation**: A specific way of slicing the four-dimensional spacetime manifold into a family of spacelike hyperplanes — a global "present" and its successive "futures." A preferred foliation is required by A-theories that posit an objective present. The problem is that preferred foliations are not detectable by any measurement (since special relativity says there is no preferred reference frame), so positing one amounts to adding undetectable structure to physics.

**Presentism**: The A-theory view that only the present moment exists — the past has ceased to exist and the future has not yet come to exist. Presentism gives the most radical ontological interpretation to temporal passage: at each moment, the present is the totality of what exists, and its passage involves the constant replacement of one moment's entities with the next moment's. Presentism faces serious challenges from special relativity's relativity of simultaneity.

**Rate of Passage**: The supposed rate at which time flows or the present moves — the target of the Williams-Smart objection. If time flows, it should flow at some rate. But "one second per second" is a tautology; a genuine rate requires two dimensions; and providing a second temporal dimension generates a vicious regress. Defenders of passage respond either that the rate is primitive (Skow) or that the question involves a category mistake.

**Two-Level Account**: The position that physics and phenomenology are legitimate autonomous domains that describe different aspects of temporal reality — the physical temporal structure of the world (objectively, without passage) and the structure of temporal experience (with its vivid sense of passage) respectively. On this account, neither level is reducible to the other, and the relationship between them is a subject for further investigation. The two-level account avoids debunking the phenomenology and avoids revising physics, at the cost of leaving the relationship between the two levels underspecified.
