# Section 1: The Phenomenology of Temporal Passage

Before we can assess whether temporal passage is real or illusory, we need to characterize the phenomenon we are assessing. What, exactly, is the experience of temporal passage? What are its features? How vivid is it, and in what conditions does it become especially salient?

These questions are phenomenological questions: they are questions about the character of experience as experienced, and they require the kind of careful, first-person attention that phenomenology cultivates. The analysis in Chapter 26 gave us tools for this: the living present, the ecstases of temporality, the field of presence. Now we need to apply those tools to the specific phenomenon of temporal passage — the sense that time is moving, that the future is approaching, that the present is giving way to the past.

What we will find is that the phenomenology of passage is real and rich, but also complex and somewhat elusive: it resists precise description, and different aspects of the experience seem to point in different philosophical directions.
