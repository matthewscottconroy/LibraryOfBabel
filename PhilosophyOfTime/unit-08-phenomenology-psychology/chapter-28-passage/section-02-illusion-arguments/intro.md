# Section 2: Arguments That Passage Is an Illusion

The arguments that the experience of temporal passage is illusory — or at least that there is no objective temporal passage corresponding to the experience — are formidable. They come in two main varieties. The first targets the coherence of the concept of passage: can we even make sense of time "flowing" at a rate? The second targets the ontological grounding of passage: in a world described by modern physics as a static four-dimensional manifold, where is the passage supposed to be?

These arguments do not show that temporal experience is in some broad sense unreliable. They show, specifically, that the element of temporal experience that represents time as *passing* — as moving, as flowing, as carrying us forward — may have no objective correlate. This is a striking and philosophically important conclusion, and it deserves careful examination.
