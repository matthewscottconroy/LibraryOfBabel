# Skow's Objective Becoming and the Moving Spotlight

## The Moving Spotlight Theory

Bradford Skow, in *Objective Becoming* (2015), develops one of the most careful recent defenses of the view that temporal passage is real and objective. Skow's version of this view is the **moving spotlight theory**: there is a property of being present (or "NOW") that is genuinely instantiated by one and only one time, and this property "moves" along the temporal sequence — what is present now will be past in a moment, and what is future will be present.

The moving spotlight theory is an A-theory in the sense that it takes the distinction between past, present, and future seriously as a genuine metaphysical distinction, not reducible to the B-theory's earlier-later relations. But it differs from presentism (the view that only the present moment exists) in holding that all times exist — the temporal manifold is like the block universe in being a four-dimensional structure — but that one time is distinctively present, in a way that others are not, and that this presentness genuinely "moves."

Skow is explicit that the moving spotlight theory is the best account of what he calls "objective becoming": the genuine fact that events come to be present, and then pass into the past. On this view, the experience of temporal passage is not an illusion generated by cognitive architecture; it is (at least approximately) a perception of a real feature of the world — the moving present (Skow 2015: 80–98).

## The Reply to the Rate-of-Passage Problem

The most challenging part of Skow's project is his response to the rate-of-passage problem. The objection is: if the present moves, at what rate does it move? And "one second per second" is no answer.

Skow's response is that the rate-of-passage question involves a **category mistake**. The rate at which the present moves is not the kind of thing that can be measured in the normal way. Asking "at what rate does the present move?" is like asking what is to the north of the north pole: the question presupposes a framework (a higher-order temporal dimension) that the phenomenon does not require.

More precisely, Skow argues that the "rate of passage" is a primitive quantity — not defined in terms of any ratio of dimensions, but simply the rate at which the present advances (Skow 2015: 103–120). This might seem like a cop-out — just asserting that there is a rate without being able to say what it is. But Skow argues that this is no more problematic than accepting other primitives in physics. The mass of an electron is not defined in terms of anything more fundamental; it is a basic physical constant. Similarly, the rate of the moving spotlight might be a basic temporal constant — not explicable in terms of anything else, but real and determinate for all that.

Skow also argues that the regress argument is not as vicious as it seems. The regress does arise: if the present moves at rate R in the first temporal dimension, we can ask at what rate R' it moves in the second temporal dimension, and so on. But Skow argues that this regress terminates in a primitive fact about the rate at which the spotlight moves, rather than generating a genuinely vicious infinite regress. The terminating fact — the primitive rate — is unexplained but not inexplicable; it is simply what it is (Skow 2015: 110–115).

## Objective Becoming and Physics

One of the challenges for any A-theory is to reconcile objective passage with the physics of relativity. In special relativity, there is no absolute simultaneity: events that are simultaneous in one reference frame are not simultaneous in other reference frames. This seems to undermine the idea of an objective "present" that is shared across the universe — if simultaneity is relative, what determines which events are co-present?

Skow acknowledges this challenge. His response is to accept that the moving spotlight is relativistically well-behaved: it picks out a global "present" that is consistent with the structure of spacetime, but this requires a preferred foliation of spacetime — a preferred way of dividing the four-dimensional manifold into spacelike hyperplanes, one of which is "now." The preference for this foliation cannot be detected empirically (it does not affect any experimental outcome), but it is nonetheless real, on Skow's account.

This is a significant cost: it requires positing a physical structure — a preferred foliation — that is empirically undetectable. Many philosophers and physicists regard this as unacceptable, and it is the most widely cited objection to Skow's view. But Skow argues that empirical undetectability does not entail non-existence, and that the metaphysical benefits of objective becoming — in particular, its ability to account for the phenomenology of temporal passage — justify the ontological posit.

## The Broader Case for Objective Becoming

Skow's defense of objective becoming is more broadly a defense of the intuition that the passage experience is not simply illusory. Even if one finds the specific mechanisms of the moving spotlight theory questionable, the underlying motivation — that something is genuinely happening in time that is not captured by the static four-dimensional manifold of the block universe — remains philosophically powerful.

The phenomenological tradition supports this motivation strongly. Husserl's analysis of the living present, Heidegger's analysis of originary temporality, Merleau-Ponty's account of the bodily field of presence — all of these describe temporal experience as constituted by a genuine orientation from past through present to future that is not merely a representation of a static structure but an active, ongoing engagement with something that is genuinely and dynamically changing. Whether or not these phenomenological analyses provide direct evidence for objective passage in the metaphysical sense, they suggest that the experience of passage is more than a mere byproduct of cognitive architecture — that it is tracking something real about the character of experience and perhaps about the world.
