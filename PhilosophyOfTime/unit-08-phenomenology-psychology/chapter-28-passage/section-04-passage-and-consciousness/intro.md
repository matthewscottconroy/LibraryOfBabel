# Section 4: What Generates the Sense of Passage?

Whether or not temporal passage is objectively real, the sense of passage is undeniably present in conscious experience. This section examines the cognitive mechanisms that generate this sense — asking not whether passage is real, but what processes in the mind-brain system produce the vivid and pervasive experience of flowing time. The question of mechanism is separable from the question of reality: even if passage is real and objective, there must still be a cognitive story about how we detect and represent it; and if passage is illusory, we especially need a cognitive story about what produces the illusion.

Two broad categories of theory have been proposed: asymmetry theories, which ground the sense of passage in the asymmetric cognitive architecture that represents the past differently from the future; and self-model theories, which ground the sense of passage in the specific way that the brain represents its own changing states.
