# Section 5: Reconciling Physics and Experience

The preceding sections have laid out what appears to be a fundamental tension. On one side: physics, and specifically the block universe picture of four-dimensional spacetime, which represents all times as equally real, assigns no special status to any "present," and provides no room for objective temporal passage. On the other side: phenomenology, which finds temporal passage to be vivid, pervasive, and seemingly fundamental to the character of consciousness — part of the very medium of experience, not a detachable belief or representation.

How should we respond to this tension? There are, broadly, three options. We can debunk the phenomenology: accept the physics and explain away the experience as a cognitive illusion. We can revise the physics: adopt an A-theory of time that accommodates objective passage. Or we can accept both on their own terms: maintain that physics and phenomenology are describing different aspects of reality, and develop an account of how they relate.

This section develops the third option — the "two-level" account — in some detail, while acknowledging the trade-offs of all three positions. It is the position the author finds most defensible, but the goal is to present the alternatives fairly enough that you can reach your own considered judgment.
