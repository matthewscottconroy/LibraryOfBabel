## Annotated Entries

**Lewis, David. *On the Plurality of Worlds*. Oxford: Blackwell, 1986.**
The primary source for Lewis's formulation of the problem of temporary intrinsics and his defense of temporal parts. Pages 202–210 are essential for the debate about persistence. Lewis's broader defense of modal realism is developed throughout the book. The persistence discussion is a masterpiece of compact philosophical argument, and engaging with it carefully is an excellent introduction to the style and rigor of analytic metaphysics at its best.

**Sider, Theodore. *Four-Dimensionalism: An Ontology of Persistence and Time*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2001.**
The definitive contemporary defense of four-dimensionalism, covering temporal parts, the metaphysics of persistence, stage theory, and the relationship between four-dimensionalism and physics. Chapter 3 develops arguments for the existence of temporal parts; Chapter 5 develops stage theory. Sider's writing is clear and rigorous, and the book covers the landscape of the debate comprehensively.

**Haslanger, Sally, and Roxanne Marie Kurtz, eds. *Persistence: Contemporary Readings*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2006.**
An excellent anthology that brings together the most important papers in the contemporary debate about persistence. Includes papers by Lewis, Sider, van Inwagen, Haslanger, Thomson, and many others. The editors' introduction is a helpful guide to the terrain. Essential resource for serious engagement with the literature.

**Thomson, Judith Jarvis. "Parthood and Identity Across Time." *Journal of Philosophy* 80, no. 4 (1983): 201–220.**
An important early paper developing the endurantist position in response to the problem of temporary intrinsics and the challenge of four-dimensionalism. Thomson develops the view that persistence should be understood in terms of wholly present objects rather than temporal parts, and examines the implications for cases of partial and gradual change.

**Johnston, Mark. "Is There a Problem About Persistence?" *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 61, suppl. (1987): 107–135.**
One of the clearest and most careful defenses of endurantism in response to Lewis's problem. Johnston argues that the apparent problem with temporary intrinsics arises from a misunderstanding of how intrinsic properties are instantiated by wholly present objects, and that the endurantist can preserve genuine intrinsic properties without accepting temporal parts.

**Wiggins, David. *Sameness and Substance Renewed*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2001.**
A sophisticated treatment of identity, persistence, and their relationship to essential properties and kinds. Wiggins defends an endurantist view on which the identity of objects depends on their belonging to specific natural kinds, with each kind having its own criteria of persistence. A philosophically rich alternative to both standard endurantism and four-dimensionalism.

**van Inwagen, Peter. *Material Beings*. Ithaca: Cornell University Press, 1990.**
Van Inwagen's argument that the only genuine composite material objects are living organisms — with all other apparent composites being mere "fictions" — is both an important contribution to metaphysics and a provocative challenge to the intuitions that drive debates about persistence. His "Special Composition Question" (under what conditions do parts compose a whole?) is one of the central questions of contemporary analytic metaphysics.

**Lowe, E. J. *A Survey of Metaphysics*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2002.**
An accessible and comprehensive introduction to analytic metaphysics, with chapters on persistence, identity, and temporal parts that provide an excellent survey of the main positions and arguments. Lowe's own four-category ontology provides a distinctive perspective on the debate.

**Plutarch. *Life of Theseus*. In *Plutarch's Lives*. Translated by Bernadotte Perrin. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press (Loeb Classical Library), 1914.**
The original source of the Ship of Theseus puzzle. Section 23 contains the relevant passage. Reading it in context — as part of a biography concerned with the historical authenticity of Theseus as a figure — illuminates the philosophical issues by embedding them in questions about historical and cultural identity.
