# Important Concepts: Chapter 29

**Adverbialism**: The view that temporary intrinsic properties should be understood as qualifying the mode of instantiation rather than the property itself. On this view, "I am straight at t₁" means that I instantiate straightness in a t₁-manner, not that I instantiate the relational property "being-straight-at-t₁." Lewis criticizes adverbialism as mysterious and as failing to genuinely analyze what happens when an object has different intrinsic properties at different times.

**Causal-Historical Continuity**: A criterion of persistence for objects: two temporal stages (or two objects at different times) are stages of the same persisting object if and only if they are connected by an appropriate causal-historical chain — the later is causally produced by the earlier in the right way. Causal-historical continuity is a central criterion for the persistence of artifacts (ships, cars, institutions) and is the criterion that most naturally supports the continuously maintained ship in the Ship of Theseus puzzle.

**Counterpart Theory**: Lewis's modal framework, extended by Sider to the temporal domain, on which de re modal (or temporal) claims are made true by the properties of counterparts in other possible worlds (or at other times) rather than by the properties of the original individual at those worlds (or times). A temporal counterpart is a distinct individual — a different temporal stage — that stands in the relevant similarity and continuity relation to the current individual.

**Endurantism (Three-Dimensionalism)**: The view that ordinary objects persist through time by being wholly present at each moment of their existence. Objects are three-dimensional and exist at different times without having temporal parts; the same whole object is present at each time it exists. Endurantism naturally fits ordinary talk and thought about persistence but must respond to the problem of temporary intrinsics.

**Fission**: A case in which one entity divides into two. Fission cases (amoeba fission, brain bisection and transplant, and the like) are philosophically important because they test the transitivity of identity. If I undergo fission and each successor has equal psychological continuity with the original, it seems that I should be identical to each, but the two successors are clearly not identical to each other — contradicting the transitivity of identity. Fission cases are central to Parfit's argument that identity is not what matters.

**Four-Dimensionalism**: The view that objects extend through time as they extend through space, having temporal parts at each time during their existence. The four-dimensional "temporal worm" composed of all the temporal parts is the complete persisting object. Four-dimensionalism aligns naturally with the block universe picture of physics and handles the problem of temporary intrinsics by treating incompatible properties as instantiated by distinct temporal parts.

**Intrinsic Property**: A property an object has in virtue of itself alone, independently of its relations to other things. Being spherical, being bent, being 5 feet tall are intrinsic. Being a sibling, being 3 miles from the Eiffel Tower, being the tallest person in the room are relational (extrinsic). The problem of temporary intrinsics specifically concerns properties that are intrinsic (not relational) but that objects appear to instantiate at some times and not at others.

**Material Identity**: The criterion of persistence based on material composition: two entities are the same if they are made of the same matter arranged in the same way. Material identity generates the intuition that Ship B (the reassembled original planks) is the "real" Ship of Theseus, in tension with the intuition from causal-historical continuity that Ship A (the continuously maintained ship) is the "real" Ship of Theseus.

**Mereology**: The formal theory of parthood and composition, studying the relations between parts and the wholes they compose. Mereological principles govern when parts compose a whole, whether composition is unique, and how parts relate to other parts of the same whole. Lewis's perdurantism employs unrestricted mereological composition, according to which any collection of objects (including scattered temporal stages) has a mereological sum.

**Perdurance (Perdurantism)**: The mode of persistence in which an object persists by having distinct temporal parts at different times. The object "perdures" in the sense that different temporal stages are present at different times; what persists is not any individual stage but the temporal worm composed of all the stages.

**Persistence**: The relation an object bears to itself at different times — what makes the object at t₁ the same object as the object at t₂. Different theories give different accounts of what persistence consists in: endurantism holds that persistence consists in the same whole object being present at both times; perdurantism holds that persistence consists in temporal stages being parts of the same temporal worm; stage theory holds that persistence consists in being connected by the appropriate temporal counterpart relation.

**Ship of Theseus**: The ancient puzzle (reported by Plutarch) about a ship whose planks are gradually replaced, one by one, until all original planks are gone. Is it still the same ship? Hobbes's variant adds the further complication of reassembling the original planks into a second ship, generating two candidates for being the "original" Ship of Theseus. The puzzle reveals the conventional element in identity claims and has been applied to personal identity by Parfit and others.

**Stage Theory**: Sider's view that ordinary object-terms refer to instantaneous temporal stages, not four-dimensional temporal worms. Persistence is analyzed in terms of temporal counterpart relations between stages. Stage theory preserves the ontology of four-dimensionalism while giving ordinary temporal language a more natural semantic treatment.

**Temporary Intrinsics**: Intrinsic properties that an object instantiates at one time but not another. The puzzle of temporary intrinsics is that if the same object instantiates incompatible intrinsic properties at different times, it seems to have contradictory properties. Lewis's three solutions — adverbialism, relativization, temporal parts — are the three main responses to this puzzle.

**Temporal Parts**: The "time-slices" of a four-dimensional object — the portions of the object confined to specific temporal regions. On perdurantism, temporal parts are genuine parts of objects in the same sense as spatial parts. The temporal worm is composed of its temporal parts. Temporal parts are the key ontological posit of four-dimensionalism; endurantists deny their existence.

**Temporal Relativism (Property Relativization)**: The endurantist strategy of treating apparently intrinsic properties as relations to times: "being bent" becomes "being bent at t," a two-place relation between the object and a time. This strategy dissolves the problem of temporary intrinsics by denying that there are genuinely monadic properties that objects both have and lack at different times. Lewis objects that it eliminates genuine intrinsic properties.

**Temporal Worm**: The four-dimensional entity composed of all the temporal stages of a persisting object. On worm theory, ordinary object-terms refer to temporal worms. A temporal worm is to time as a spatial worm (like a snake) is to space: extended across a dimension and composed of parts that differ along that dimension.
