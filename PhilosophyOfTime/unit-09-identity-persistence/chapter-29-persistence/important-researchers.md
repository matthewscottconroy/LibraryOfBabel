# Important Researchers and Thinkers: Chapter 29

**David Lewis (1941–2001)**: American philosopher, widely regarded as one of the most important analytic philosophers of the twentieth century. His work spans modal logic, philosophy of language, philosophy of mind, and metaphysics. His formulation of the problem of temporary intrinsics in *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986) set the terms for the contemporary debate about persistence, and his defense of temporal parts as the solution to that problem is the most influential argument for four-dimensionalism in the literature. Lewis's broader philosophical system — combining modal realism (the view that all possible worlds are real) with perdurantism and a Humean approach to laws and causation — is architecturally unified and philosophically formidable. He died at 60, leaving behind an extraordinary body of work.

**Ted Sider (born 1966)**: American philosopher whose *Four-Dimensionalism: An Ontology of Persistence and Time* (2001) is the most comprehensive and rigorous defense of four-dimensionalism in the contemporary literature. Sider's distinction between worm theory and stage theory clarified the options within the four-dimensionalist framework, and his broader arguments for the existence of temporal parts — including an argument from the possibility of arbitrary temporal fusions — have been widely discussed and debated. His subsequent work on ontology (*Writing the Book of the World*, 2011) connects the metaphysics of persistence to fundamental questions about quantification and the structure of reality.

**Sally Haslanger (born 1955)**: American philosopher whose work on persistence and temporary intrinsics includes one of the most influential defenses of endurantism with time-indexed properties. Her 1989 paper "Endurance and Temporary Intrinsics" develops the endurantist response to Lewis's problem in careful detail, arguing that the time-indexing of properties is not a concession to relationalism but a principled account of how intrinsic properties are instantiated by wholly present objects. Her later work on social ontology, race, and gender has made her one of the most important analytic philosophers currently working.

**Mark Johnston (born 1954)**: Australian philosopher whose work on personal identity and persistence defends endurantism against the four-dimensionalist challenge. His papers on temporary intrinsics, on the relationship between persons and their bodies, and on the metaphysics of constitution have been influential in the debate. Johnston has also made important contributions to the philosophy of religion and to questions about the nature of normativity.

**Peter van Inwagen (born 1942)**: American philosopher who has contributed to the debate about persistence primarily through his work on material constitution and composition. His defense of animalism — the view that we are fundamentally animals, and that personal identity is biological — connects the metaphysics of persistence to the debate about personal identity. His work on the question of when objects compose something (*Material Beings*, 1990) is an important contribution to mereology.

**Plutarch (c. 46–120 CE)**: Greek biographer and essayist whose *Life of Theseus* contains the original statement of the Ship of Theseus puzzle. While Plutarch is not a systematic philosopher of persistence, the puzzle he reports has been used by philosophers ever since to illustrate the deep questions about identity over time that underlie everyday practices of identifying and re-identifying objects.

**Thomas Hobbes (1588–1679)**: English philosopher best known for his political philosophy (*Leviathan*, 1651) but who also contributed importantly to debates about identity and persistence. His formulation of the "rebuilt ship" variant of the Ship of Theseus puzzle in *De Corpore* (1655) sharpens the original puzzle into its most challenging form by generating a genuine conflict between causal-historical and material criteria of identity. Hobbes's variant remains the canonical form of the puzzle in contemporary discussions.

**Judith Jarvis Thomson (1929–2020)**: American philosopher whose work on personal identity and the metaphysics of objects made important contributions to the debate about persistence. Her defense of three-dimensionalism and her careful analysis of the conditions under which objects persist are among the most influential contributions to the endurantist literature.
