# Section 1: Change and Temporary Intrinsics

The problem of persistence can be approached from many angles, but David Lewis's formulation in terms of "temporary intrinsics" is among the sharpest and most illuminating. It focuses our attention on a specific logical difficulty that arises whenever an object has incompatible intrinsic properties at different times, and it forces us to choose between a small number of philosophical options.

The power of Lewis's formulation is that it is not immediately about persons or ships or trees — objects about which we have rich intuitions and competing interests — but about intrinsic properties in the abstract. This level of generality helps to reveal the underlying logical structure of the problem and to evaluate the competing solutions on their merits before bringing them back to the cases that matter most.
