# Endurantism: Objects Wholly Present Through Time

## The Core Endurantist Claim

Endurantism holds that objects persist through time by being **wholly present** at each moment of their existence. There is no sense in which only a part of an object is present at a given time; the whole object is there. This is why endurantism is sometimes called "three-dimensionalism": ordinary objects have three spatial dimensions, and they exist in time, but they do not have a fourth, temporal dimension in the way that perdurantism claims.

This is not merely a claim about everyday appearances — about how objects *seem* to be wholly present. It is a metaphysical claim about the ultimate constitution of objects: there are no temporal parts; there is no "temporal worm" composed of distinct stages; there is simply the object, wholly present whenever it exists.

Consider a simple analogy. Your coffee mug exists in space: it has spatial extent, spatial parts (the handle, the rim, the base), and spatial properties (its height, its width, its volume). But does it also have temporal extent — a "temporal height" or "temporal depth" in the way it has spatial height and depth? The endurantist says no. The coffee mug is wholly present now; it is not merely the present temporal stage of a four-dimensional "temporal mug" that extends from its manufacture to its eventual breaking. The mug simply is, as a fully three-dimensional object, present at each moment it exists.

## Handling Temporary Intrinsics: Relativization

The endurantist must respond to Lewis's problem of temporary intrinsics. The standard endurantist response is some form of relativization — either of properties to times, or of instantiation to times.

The most developed endurantist response holds that properties like "being bent" and "being straight" are not genuinely monadic properties but are what we might call "time-indexed properties" — properties whose instantiation is always relative to a time. When I say "I am bent," what I really mean is "I am bent at this time." Being-bent-at-t is a perfectly coherent property that I can have while also having being-straight-at-t', because these are different properties relativized to different times.

The endurantist must handle Lewis's objection that this relativization eliminates genuine intrinsic properties. The standard response is to distinguish between two notions of intrinsicality. There is "intrinsic" in the narrow sense that Lewis intends: a property an object has independently of all relations to other things, including relations to times. But there is also a broader notion: a property an object has in virtue of how it is in itself, as opposed to how it is in relation to other objects (where "objects" does not include times). Endurantists can maintain that being-bent-at-t is intrinsic in the second sense — it is about how the object is arranged at t — even if it is not intrinsic in Lewis's strict sense (Johnston 1987; Haslanger 1989).

## Advantages of Endurantism

Endurantism has several significant advantages over perdurantism.

**Ordinary talk and thought**: We ordinarily say that it is *you* who was a child, not a temporal stage of you. We hold *you* responsible for what you did last year, not a temporal part of you. We say that *this* tree has grown, not that the current temporal stage of a tree-worm has different height properties from earlier stages. Endurantism's claim that objects are wholly present fits naturally with ordinary discourse about objects, their properties, and their persistence.

**Simpler ontology**: Endurantism does not require positing temporal parts. It acknowledges the temporal dimension in which objects exist without claiming that objects are literally composed of temporal stages in the way they are composed of spatial parts. This parsimony is philosophically attractive: we should not multiply entities beyond what is necessary to account for the phenomena.

**Causal efficacy**: Some philosophers argue that only wholly present objects can be genuine causal agents. If you help a friend move house, it is you — wholly present — who is lifting boxes; not your current temporal stage, which might be causally connected to but is numerically distinct from the stage that agreed to help. Causal responsibility seems to require the causal agent to be wholly present in the relevant situation, not merely to have a present stage there.

## The Persistence Criterion Problem

Endurantism faces a challenge that perdurantism handles more easily: the problem of specifying a criterion of persistence. What makes the object present at t₁ the same object as the object present at t₂? For perdurantism, the answer is clean: they are connected as parts of the same temporal worm. For endurantism, the identity relation holds between the whole object-at-t₁ and the whole object-at-t₂, but what grounds this identity?

The endurantist typically appeals to continuity conditions of various kinds — causal continuity, material continuity, functional continuity — as criteria for persistence. The right criterion varies with the kind of object: the persistence of a river depends on continuity of water source and flow; the persistence of an organism depends on biological continuity; the persistence of a person depends on (according to different views) physical or psychological continuity. These criteria are well-developed in the literature, but they introduce their own complexity into the endurantist picture.
