# Section 2: Endurantism (Three-Dimensionalism)

Endurantism — the view that ordinary objects are wholly present at each moment of their existence, without being composed of temporal parts — is the view that matches most naturally with ordinary, pre-theoretical thinking about objects and their persistence. When you see a friend across the street, you see your whole friend, not a temporal stage or a temporal part. When your friend grows older and changes, it is not the case that different "parts" of your friend exist at different times; it is the very same person who is different. Endurantism takes this common-sense intuition seriously and tries to defend it against Lewis's challenge.

The name "three-dimensionalism" reflects the view that objects have only three spatial dimensions, not four. Objects extend through space (three dimensions) but they do not extend through time in the way that perdurantists hold; they persist through time by being wholly present at each moment, not by having temporal parts.

Defending this view requires responding to the problem of temporary intrinsics, which Lewis presents as a knock-down argument for temporal parts. In this section we examine the endurantist response and assess its strengths and weaknesses.
