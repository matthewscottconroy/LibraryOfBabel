# Section 3: Perdurantism (Four-Dimensionalism)

Perdurantism — the view that objects persist through time by having distinct temporal parts at different times — is a view that initially strikes most people as strange. What does it mean to say that the "you" who is reading this page is a temporal stage of a four-dimensional "worm" that extends from your birth to your death? The picture invites resistance: surely you are a person, not a worm; surely you are here, not a stage.

But the resistance is worth examining. We are, after all, happy to say that you extend through space: you have spatial parts — a left side and a right side, a top and a bottom, a front and a back. Each of these spatial parts is a different part of you at any given moment. Perdurantism simply proposes that you also extend through time: you have temporal parts — an earlier part and a later part, a yesterday-part and a today-part — and these temporal parts are as genuinely distinct as your left hand and your right hand.

This section examines perdurantism in detail: what it claims, how it handles the problem of temporary intrinsics, and what it costs.
