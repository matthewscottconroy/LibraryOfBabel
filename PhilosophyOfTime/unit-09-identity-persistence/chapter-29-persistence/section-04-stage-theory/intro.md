# Section 4: Stage Theory

Stage theory, developed by Ted Sider in *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001), represents a fascinating hybrid position that accepts the ontological framework of four-dimensionalism (temporal stages as the basic entities) but modifies the semantic framework to avoid the counterintuitive consequences of worm theory about ordinary talk and practical concerns.

The key move is to shift what ordinary terms like "I," "this cat," and "the river" refer to. Worm theory says they refer to four-dimensional worms. Stage theory says they refer to instantaneous stages, with persistence talk understood in terms of counterpart relations between stages. This semantic shift preserves the ontological economy of four-dimensionalism while making ordinary temporal language come out literally true in a more natural way.
