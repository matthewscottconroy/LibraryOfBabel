# Section 5: The Ship of Theseus

The Ship of Theseus is one of the oldest and most illuminating philosophical puzzles about identity and persistence. Its enduring fascination lies partly in its simplicity — it can be stated in two sentences — and partly in its depth: it resists easy resolution and reveals something fundamental about the nature of our identity concepts.

The puzzle forces us to ask not just which answer is correct, but what kind of question we are asking. Are questions of identity always determinate? Do they have correct answers independently of our practices and conventions? Or is identity, in at least some cases, a matter of decision rather than discovery? The Ship of Theseus is a perfect vehicle for approaching these meta-level questions, and for applying the theoretical frameworks of endurantism, perdurantism, and stage theory to a concrete and vivid case.
