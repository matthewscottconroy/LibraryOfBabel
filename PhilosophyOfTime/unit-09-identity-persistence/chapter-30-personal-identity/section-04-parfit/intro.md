# Section 4: Parfit and the Unimportance of Identity

Derek Parfit's *Reasons and Persons* (1984), Part III ("Personal Identity"), is arguably the most important contribution to the philosophy of personal identity since Locke. Parfit's central argument is radical and surprising: personal identity is not what matters in survival. What matters is not whether the future person is numerically identical to the present one, but whether there is sufficient psychological continuity and connectedness between them. And since psychological continuity can exist without identity — and can be a matter of degree — identity is not the concept that our deepest practical and moral concerns should track.

This conclusion, if it is right, requires significant revisions to our practices of prudential self-concern, moral responsibility, and the treatment of persons over time. Whether it is right is one of the central questions of practical ethics and philosophy of mind.
