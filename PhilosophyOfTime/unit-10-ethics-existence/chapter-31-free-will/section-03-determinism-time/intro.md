# Section 31.3: Determinism and the Block Universe

---

## Section Introduction

The block universe — the view that past, present, and future events are all equally real, arranged in a four-dimensional spacetime manifold — is one of the most important metaphysical positions in the philosophy of time, and one that many students find immediately threatening to free will. If the future is already "there," already fixed in the four-dimensional structure of spacetime, then in what sense is it open to our choices? Does the block universe entail fatalism?

This section argues that the inference from block universe to fatalism rests on a conflation. The block universe is a thesis about ontology — about what exists — not a thesis about causation or determination. A block universe is compatible with causal determinism (every event is determined by prior events) and equally compatible with causal indeterminism (some events are not so determined). The mere existence of future events does not imply that they were causally inevitable from any earlier time.

Once this is clear, the real question about free will and determinism can be put properly. That question concerns not the ontological status of the future, but the causal structure of the world and its relationship to agency. This is the terrain of the compatibilism debate. Compatibilists argue that free will and determinism are not merely compossible but that free will *requires* a certain kind of deterministic connection — between one's reasons and one's actions. Incompatibilists argue that genuine freedom requires the genuine ability to do otherwise, which determinism forecloses.
