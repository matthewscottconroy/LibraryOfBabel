# Section 32.3: Epicurean Responses to the Deprivation Account

---

## Section Introduction

The deprivation account, developed in Section 32.2, holds that death is bad because it deprives us of goods we would otherwise have enjoyed. This account has been influential and widely accepted, but it faces persistent pushback from those who remain persuaded by the Epicurean tradition.

The core of the Epicurean reply is what Rosenbaum (1986) calls the "existence requirement" for welfare: you can be benefited or harmed only if you exist at the time of the benefit or harm. The deprivation account seems to violate this requirement by attributing harm to a person who no longer exists at the time the harm is supposedly occurring. This section examines whether the Epicurean can successfully deploy the existence requirement against the deprivation account, and what the deprivationist can say in response.
