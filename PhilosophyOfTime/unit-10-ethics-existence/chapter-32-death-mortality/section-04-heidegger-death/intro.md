# Section 32.4: Heidegger on Being-toward-Death

---

## Section Introduction

The analytic debate about whether death is bad, and when it is bad, and for whom — all of this proceeds from a tacit assumption: that death is primarily a future event that we are moving toward, an event whose badness can be assessed by comparing the actual course of events with a counterfactual. Heidegger challenges this assumption at its root. For Heidegger, the question of death is not primarily a question about a future event but a question about the structure of existence itself.

This section examines Heidegger's analysis of death in *Being and Time* (1927), sections 46–53, and then turns to the main criticisms. Heidegger's analysis is demanding — it requires engaging with his technical vocabulary, including Dasein, Being-toward-death, authenticity, and ownmost possibility. But the reward is substantial: a perspective on mortality that is genuinely different from the analytic tradition and that captures aspects of our temporal existence that the analytic debate tends to pass over.
