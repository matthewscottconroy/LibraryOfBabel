# Section 32.5: The Problem of Immortality

---

## Section Introduction

If death is bad — if it deprives us of goods we would otherwise have enjoyed — then it seems to follow that living longer is better, and that immortality would be best of all. Why not more life? Why not indefinitely more life?

This inference, though tempting, has been challenged with remarkable force by Bernard Williams's essay "The Makropulos Case," published in *Problems of the Self* (1973). Williams argues that immortality would not be a gift but a curse — that the very features of a person that make their life worth living are constitutively tied to their finitude, and that an immortal life would eventually become not just tedious but genuinely empty of the conditions for a worthwhile existence.

This section examines Williams's argument, the responses it has attracted, and what the debate about immortality reveals about the deep connections between personal identity, value, and time.
