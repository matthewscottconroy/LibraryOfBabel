# Section 33.2: The Non-Identity Problem

---

## Section Introduction

Among the most disturbing and important philosophical discoveries of the twentieth century is Parfit's non-identity problem. It concerns the relationship between our choices, the identities of future people, and the possibility of harming them. The discovery is disturbing because it threatens to undermine large parts of our ordinary moral reasoning about the future, including much of our concern about climate change, resource depletion, and long-term policy.

The problem arises because our major collective decisions — about energy policy, environmental regulation, population, and economic development — do not merely affect the welfare of future people. They affect *which* people will exist. Different policies lead to different meetings, different conceptions, different births, so that the individuals who will live in 200 years are genuinely different people depending on which policy is in place. This means that the people who will be harmed by a bad policy would not have existed under a good policy. It seems impossible to harm someone by causing them to exist, even in less than ideal conditions.
