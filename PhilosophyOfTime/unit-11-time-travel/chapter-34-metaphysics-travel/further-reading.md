## Annotated Entries

**Lewis, "The Paradoxes of Time Travel" (1976)**
The most important single paper in the philosophical analysis of time travel. Lewis's definition of time travel as a discrepancy between personal and external time, his resolution of the grandfather paradox using context-sensitive ability attributions, and his four-dimensionalist framework for handling temporal stages are all indispensable. Short and beautifully written; essential reading before any other work in this area.

**Nahin, *Time Machines* (1999)**
A comprehensive and accessible survey of time travel in both physics and science fiction, by a physicist who takes the philosophy seriously. Nahin covers the history of time travel fiction, the physics of general relativistic solutions permitting CTCs, the philosophical paradoxes, and the logical constraints on time travel scenarios. An excellent reference for those who want physics and philosophy together.

**Sider, *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001)**
The most rigorous contemporary defense of the perdurantist framework for personal identity and persistence. While not specifically about time travel, Sider's account of temporal parts and the four-dimensional "worm" is the natural framework for Lewis's treatment of time travel personal identity. Chapters 4 and 5 are most directly relevant.

**Sider, "Time Travel, Coincidences, and Counterfactuals" (2002)**
A careful examination of Lewis's resolution of the grandfather paradox, focusing on the ability attributions and their connection to counterfactuals. Sider argues that Lewis's account, while largely correct, faces difficulties in cases of "temporal coincidence" where the traveler and their earlier self are both present. Valuable for understanding the limits of Lewis's approach.

**Smith, "Bananas Enough for Time Travel?" (1997)**
An important paper defending the claim that causal loops are metaphysically impossible, contra Lewis. Smith argues that there is a metaphysical principle — that events require causes not among their effects — that causal loops violate. The title refers to Snoopy's time machine thought experiment. Essential for understanding the philosophical debate about causal loops.

**Wasserman, *Paradoxes of Time Travel* (2018)**
The most comprehensive and up-to-date philosophical treatment of time travel paradoxes. Wasserman examines the grandfather paradox, bootstrap paradoxes, and related issues with great care. He defends a version of Lewis's approach and responds to recent challenges. The best single source for the current state of the debate.
