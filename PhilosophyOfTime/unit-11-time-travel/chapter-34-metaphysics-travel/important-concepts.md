# Chapter 34 Important Concepts: The Metaphysics of Time Travel

---

**Bootstrap Paradox**
A paradox arising in time travel scenarios in which information or objects form a closed causal loop with no external origin — they "bootstrap" themselves into existence. In a bootstrap scenario, the information in the loop (say, a manuscript carried back in time and then published as if original) exists without ever having been created by a cause outside the loop. The loop is logically consistent but explanatorily incomplete: nothing within the loop explains why it has the content it has rather than some other content.

**Branching Timelines**
A response to time travel paradoxes in which the time traveler, upon arriving in the past, creates or arrives in a new causal branch of the timeline, rather than in the unique past of the single original timeline. In this branching scenario, the traveler can do anything in the "new" branch — including things that would produce contradictions in a single-timeline model — because the branch is independent of the original timeline. Lewis objects that this substitutes inter-universe travel for backward time travel, changing the subject.

**Causal Loop**
A sequence of events A → B → C → A, in which each event is (partly) caused by its predecessors and the sequence circles back on itself without any event being external to the loop. Causal loops are logically consistent but raise the question of informational or ontological bootstrapping: if the loop is self-contained, what explains the existence and specific character of the events within it?

**Consistency Constraint**
The requirement, in single-timeline time travel, that all events in a spacetime history be mutually consistent — that no event contradicts any other event. The consistency constraint is responsible for Lewis's "can but won't" resolution of the grandfather paradox: the time traveler will not succeed in killing her grandfather because doing so would produce an inconsistency; ordinary causal factors ensure that she fails without any magical intervention.

**External Time**
Lewis's term for the time measured by clocks, calendars, and all the ordinary instruments we use to locate events in the temporal order of the world. For time travelers, external time and personal time may diverge — either in rate (forward time travel through relativistic dilation) or in direction (backward time travel). External time is the time of physics and history.

**Grandfather Paradox**
The apparent refutation of backward time travel: if a traveler goes back and kills her grandfather before he sires her parent, she is never born, never travels back, and never kills him — but then he lives, she is born, travels back, and kills him. The contradiction seems to prove that backward time travel is impossible. Lewis's response: the consistency constraint shows only that the traveler will not succeed in killing her grandfather, not that backward time travel is impossible.

**Meeting Yourself**
The scenario in which a time traveler is present at an external time at which their earlier (or later) temporal stage is also present. On the single-timeline view, this involves the co-presence of two temporal stages of the same person. The scenario raises deep questions about personal identity: are two temporally co-present stages the same person or two distinct people? Endurantism and perdurantism give different answers.

**Novikov Self-Consistency Principle**
The principle, proposed by Igor Novikov, that for spacetimes with closed timelike curves, only self-consistent solutions to the field equations are physically realized. This principle eliminates paradoxical trajectories (which would produce contradictions) while preserving the possibility of consistent time travel episodes, including causal loops. It implies a holistic model of explanation in which events are explained by the consistency of the entire history, not only by prior causes.

**Ontological Causal Loop**
A specific form of causal loop in which an object or piece of information forms a self-sustaining cycle — its existence is caused by itself (through the loop), without any cause outside the loop. Distinct from an epistemic causal loop (where the loop is in our knowledge) or a structural causal loop (where causal dependencies are mutual without any self-creation). The concept of ontological causal loops is what makes bootstrap paradoxes philosophically puzzling.

**Personal Time**
Lewis's term for the time measured by the traveler's own processes — their biological aging, psychological experience of duration, and the readings of clocks they carry with them. For ordinary travelers, personal time and external time are closely matched. For time travelers, they diverge: either in rate (the traveler ages slowly relative to external time) or in direction (the traveler moves backward in external time while continuing forward in personal time).

**Perdurantism (Four-Dimensionalism)**
The metaphysical view that persisting objects have distinct temporal parts (stages) existing at different times, and that the persisting object is a four-dimensional "worm" extending through time. On this view, the two temporal stages co-present during a meeting-yourself time travel episode are distinct (if related) objects. Perdurantism handles the spatial co-presence of temporally distinct stages without contradiction.

**Temporal Parts**
The distinct stages of a four-dimensional persisting object. On the perdurantist view, a person who lives from 1990 to 2070 has temporal parts at each moment of their life — a 1990-stage, a 1991-stage, and so on. When a time traveler "meets themselves," two temporal parts of the same four-dimensional person-worm are co-present in the same external-time location.

**Time Travel (Lewis's definition)**
Time travel occurs when there is a discrepancy between external time and personal time: the traveler's personal time during the journey does not match the difference in external time between departure and arrival. In forward time travel, personal time runs slower than external time. In backward time travel, personal time runs forward while external time runs backward. The definition is neutral about the physical mechanism and does not require any particular theory of time.
