# Section 34.2: The Grandfather Paradox

---

## Section Introduction

The grandfather paradox is the most famous objection to the possibility of backward time travel. Its logic is simple: if you can travel to the past, and if you can affect what happens there, then you could in principle go back and prevent your own existence — by killing your grandfather before your parent was conceived, for instance. But if you were never born, you could never go back to kill your grandfather. A contradiction follows.

The paradox seems to show that backward time travel is impossible — that the logical structure of causation and self-reference rules out going back to a time before your own birth. Lewis's response challenges this conclusion. The paradox does not prove that backward time travel is impossible; it proves only that if time travel occurs, certain things that are possible in some sense will nevertheless not happen.

This section works through the paradox carefully, examines Lewis's "can but won't" analysis, and assesses whether the resolution is satisfying.
