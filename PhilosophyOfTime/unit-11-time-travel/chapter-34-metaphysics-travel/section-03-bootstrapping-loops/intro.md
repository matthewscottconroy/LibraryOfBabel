# Section 34.3: Bootstrap Paradoxes and Causal Loops

---

## Section Introduction

The grandfather paradox concerns something that *cannot* happen in a consistent time travel scenario. Bootstrap paradoxes concern something that *can* happen — and yet seems metaphysically strange. A causal loop is a sequence of causes and effects that circles back on itself, so that A causes B which causes C which causes A. Such loops are logically consistent — there is no formal contradiction — but they raise a deep question: where did the information (or the object) in the loop *come from*? Who or what is ultimately responsible for it?

Bootstrap paradoxes challenge our intuitions about the origins of information and the explanatory completeness of causal accounts. They reveal a feature of time travel that is different from the grandfather paradox: not the danger of inconsistency, but the strangeness of closed causal loops.
