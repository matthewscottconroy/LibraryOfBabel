# Section 34.4: The Many-Worlds and Branching Solution

---

## Section Introduction

The paradoxes of time travel — both the consistency constraint on grandfather-style cases and the explanatory puzzles of bootstrap loops — arise within the single-timeline view that Lewis adopts. On this view, the past is fixed, and the time traveler's presence in it is part of the actual, unique history of the universe. The paradoxes are features of this single timeline.

A different approach is to deny the single-timeline assumption. Perhaps when you travel back in time, you do not arrive in your own past — you arrive in a *branching* timeline, a new causal branch that diverges from the original at the moment of your arrival. This "many-worlds" or "branching timeline" approach seems to dissolve the paradoxes: you can kill "your grandfather" in the branching timeline without creating any contradiction, because his death occurs in a different branch, not in the branch from which you originate.

This section examines whether this approach genuinely resolves the paradoxes, or whether it merely changes the subject.
