# Section 34.5: Personal Identity and Meeting Yourself

---

## Section Introduction

One of the most philosophically intriguing aspects of backward time travel is the possibility of *meeting yourself* — of an older version of you encountering a younger version, or vice versa. This scenario brings every theory of personal identity into sharp relief. What does it mean for two people who are "the same person" to co-exist in the same place at the same time? How does each theory of persistence through time handle the case where a single person is present at two different temporal stages simultaneously?

The meeting-yourself case is not merely fanciful. If backward time travel is possible on Lewis's single-timeline view, and if a time traveler can return to a time within their own life, then temporal coincidence of this kind is at least conceptually possible. Understanding what it involves requires engaging with the metaphysical theories of personal identity examined in Chapter 25.
