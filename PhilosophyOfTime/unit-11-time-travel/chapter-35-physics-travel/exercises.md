# Chapter 35 Exercises: Time Travel and Physics

---

## Conceptual Exercises

**1. Closed Timelike Curves**
Explain in your own words what a closed timelike curve (CTC) is. Why does the existence of CTC solutions to the Einstein field equations not by itself prove that backward time travel is physically possible in our universe? What additional steps would be needed to establish physical possibility? What would it take to establish physical impossibility?

**2. Gödel's Philosophical Argument**
Gödel argued that his rotating universe solution undermined the concept of an objective global time. Reconstruct his argument as carefully as you can. Does the argument succeed? Does the existence of a CTC-containing solution to the field equations show that time does not "flow" or that there is no objective present, or does it merely show that some spacetimes have exotic causal structures?

**3. The Wormhole Time Machine**
Explain the Morris-Thorne mechanism for converting a traversable wormhole into a time machine. What is the role of special relativistic time dilation in the mechanism? Why can a wormhole time machine only be used to travel to times after the machine was built? Does this limitation affect the philosophical paradoxes discussed in Chapter 34?

**4. Exotic Matter**
The traversable wormhole requires exotic matter — matter with negative energy density violating the weak energy condition. Explain what the weak energy condition is and why its violation is required for traversable wormholes. The Casimir effect demonstrates negative energy density in a quantum context. What would it take to scale up Casimir-like effects to the quantities required for a traversable wormhole?

**5. The Chronology Protection Conjecture**
Explain Hawking's Chronology Protection Conjecture and the mechanism he proposes. What evidence supports it? Why is it a conjecture rather than a theorem? What would it take to prove the conjecture — what physical theory would be required? If the conjecture is false, what follows?

**6. The Novikov Principle and Indeterminism**
The Echeverria et al. billiard ball analysis shows that there may be multiple self-consistent solutions for given initial conditions. Explain this result and its significance. Does the existence of multiple self-consistent solutions mean that the Novikov principle is inadequate? Or does it reveal something important about the nature of determinism in CTC spacetimes?

**7. Deutsch's Model**
Explain Deutsch's fixed-point condition for quantum CTCs. Why does Deutsch's model require mixed states to resolve the grandfather paradox? What is the "information creation from nothing" problem, and why do many physicists find it problematic? How does Lloyd's post-selection model avoid this problem?

## Discussion and Essay Questions

**8. Physics, Metaphysics, and Time Travel**
Compare the philosophical analysis of time travel in Chapter 34 (Lewis's approach) with the physical analysis in Chapter 35. To what extent do the philosophical and physical approaches converge? Where do they diverge? Does the physical analysis vindicate, challenge, or leave unaffected Lewis's philosophical conclusions?

**9. The Information Problem**
The black hole information paradox (Chapter 38) and the Deutsch CTC information problem both involve apparent violations of information conservation. Are these two problems structurally related? What do they tell us about the relationship between quantum mechanics, gravity, and information?

## Lab and Observational Exercises

**10. Gravitational Time Dilation Investigation**
GPS satellites experience both special relativistic time dilation (their motion makes their clocks run slow) and general relativistic time dilation (their distance from Earth's surface makes their clocks run fast). The two effects partially cancel, but the net result requires corrections for GPS accuracy. Research the magnitudes of both effects and calculate: (a) how much time each GPS satellite gains or loses per day relative to Earth-based clocks; (b) how large the GPS positioning error would be if these effects were not corrected. What does this reveal about the practical reality of relativistic time effects?

**11. Research: Current Status of the Chronology Protection Conjecture**
The Chronology Protection Conjecture was proposed in 1992, and work on it has continued. Survey the current research literature (using at least three sources published after 2000) to assess the current status of the conjecture. Has it been strengthened, weakened, or superseded? Are there specific spacetime models for which the conjecture fails? Write a one-page summary of the current state of the question, citing your sources.

**12. The Casimir Effect**
The Casimir effect demonstrates that quantum vacuum fluctuations can produce negative energy density between conducting plates — a real physical phenomenon. Research the experimental verification of the Casimir effect (the landmark paper is Lamoreaux 1997, *Physical Review Letters* 78: 5–8). Explain what was measured and how. What are the theoretical conditions under which Casimir-like effects could, in principle, produce the negative energy required for a traversable wormhole? What scale of effect would be needed?
