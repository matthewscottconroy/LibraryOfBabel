## Annotated Entries

**Deutsch, "Quantum Mechanics near Closed Timelike Lines" (1991)**
The foundational paper on quantum CTCs. Deutsch derives the fixed-point consistency condition, shows how it resolves the quantum grandfather paradox, and identifies the "information from nothing" implication. Dense and technical but essential; Aaronson and Watrous (2009) provide a more accessible follow-up with remarkable computational implications.

**Friedman et al., "Cauchy Problem in Spacetimes with Closed Timelike Curves" (1990)**
The key paper developing the Novikov self-consistency principle in a physical context. Demonstrates the principle for specific classical field theories and lays the groundwork for the billiard ball analysis of Echeverria et al. (1991). Dense physics but accessible introduction and conclusion.

**Gödel, "An Example of a New Type of Cosmological Solutions" (1949)**
The original paper presenting the Gödel rotating universe. Gödel explicitly discusses the philosophical implications for the concept of time in the second half of the paper — this is not merely a technical result but a philosophical argument about the nature of time in general relativity. Accessible to readers with basic GR background.

**Hawking, "Chronology Protection Conjecture" (1992)**
The key paper proposing the conjecture. Hawking's argument for the divergence of quantum vacuum fluctuations near Cauchy horizons is presented clearly, with honest acknowledgment of its limitations. The paper's title and opening quote capture the playful seriousness that characterized Hawking's public scientific writing.

**Lloyd et al., "Quantum Mechanics of Time Travel through Post-Selected Teleportation" (2011)**
The key paper developing the post-selection model of quantum CTCs as an alternative to Deutsch's fixed-point model. Shows that P-CTCs can be implemented using quantum teleportation and post-selection — tools available in real quantum information processing. The comparison with Deutsch's model illuminates both approaches.

**Morris and Thorne, "Wormholes in Spacetime" (1988)**
The paper that launched the theoretical study of traversable wormholes. Written explicitly for pedagogical purposes (published in the *American Journal of Physics*), it is unusually accessible for research physics. The analysis of traversability conditions, exotic matter requirements, and the time machine mechanism is presented with great clarity.

**Thorne, *Black Holes and Time Warps* (1994)**
The best popular account of the physics of black holes, wormholes, and time travel by one of the field's central figures. Thorne covers the relevant physics with exceptional clarity and includes remarkable stories about the development of the field. The most accessible entry point for students without physics backgrounds.

**Visser, *Lorentzian Wormholes* (1995)**
The technical monograph on Lorentzian (traversable) wormholes. Comprehensive and rigorous treatment of the mathematical physics, including exotic matter, energy conditions, chronology protection, and the quantum treatment. Essential reference for those working at the physics end of the subject.
