# Chapter 35 Important Concepts: Time Travel and Physics

---

**Cauchy Horizon**
The boundary in spacetime that marks the onset of the region in which closed timelike curves are present — the "future edge" of the region where the initial data on a Cauchy surface uniquely determines the history. Hawking's Chronology Protection argument focuses on what happens to quantum vacuum fluctuations as spacetime approaches its Cauchy horizon: they diverge, potentially destroying the time machine.

**Chronology Protection Conjecture**
Hawking's (1992) conjecture that the laws of physics conspire to prevent the formation of closed timelike curves at the macroscopic level. The mechanism proposed involves the divergence of quantum vacuum stress-energy near the Cauchy horizon of a prospective time machine. The conjecture is supported by calculations in semiclassical quantum gravity but has not been proved as a theorem, and counterexamples have been proposed for specific spacetime geometries.

**Closed Timelike Curve (CTC)**
A spacetime trajectory that is both timelike (a particle following it would experience proper time) and closed (it returns to its starting point in spacetime). A particle following a CTC would return, at some point in its proper time, to the exact spacetime event at which it started. CTCs are features of several exact solutions to the Einstein field equations, including the Gödel universe, the interior of the Kerr metric, and the van Stockum dust spacetime.

**Density Matrix (Mixed State)**
A mathematical object representing a quantum state that is a statistical mixture of pure states, used when the state of a system is not precisely known or when it is entangled with other systems. In Deutsch's model of quantum CTCs, self-consistency requires that the system entering the CTC be in a specific mixed state — a fixed point of the evolution through the loop. This requirement is what allows the quantum grandfather paradox to be resolved.

**Deutsch's Fixed-Point Condition**
The consistency requirement for quantum CTCs, formulated by Deutsch (1991): the density matrix of a system entering a CTC must equal the density matrix of the system that emerges from the CTC after interaction with its earlier self. This is a fixed-point condition on density matrices. It resolves the quantum grandfather paradox (by allowing mixed states that are fixed points even when no pure state is) but implies that information can apparently be created in CTC spacetimes.

**Exotic Matter**
Matter with negative energy density, violating the weak energy condition. Required to keep the throat of a traversable wormhole open against gravitational collapse. In classical physics, no known form of matter has negative energy density. In quantum field theory, the Casimir effect demonstrates that vacuum fluctuations can produce regions of negative energy density. Whether exotic matter in the quantities required for a traversable wormhole can be realized is an open question.

**Gödel Universe**
An exact solution to the Einstein field equations, presented by Kurt Gödel in 1949, describing a rotating, homogeneous universe in which closed timelike curves pass through every spacetime point. Gödel used this solution to argue that general relativity undermines the concept of an objective global time. The Gödel universe is not thought to represent our actual universe, but its existence demonstrates that the field equations do not categorically forbid CTCs.

**Information Creation (Deutsch CTCs)**
The apparently paradoxical implication of Deutsch's model of quantum CTCs: information (specifically, the mixed state required for self-consistency) can be "created from nothing" in CTC spacetimes, without any external source. This is troubling because it seems to violate the standard quantum mechanical principle that information is conserved in unitary evolution. Aaronson and Watrous (2009) showed that this information creation gives Deutsch CTCs computational power equivalent to PSPACE.

**Kerr Metric**
The exact solution to the Einstein field equations describing the spacetime outside a rotating black hole, found by Roy Kerr in 1963. Astrophysically important because real black holes rotate. Inside the inner horizon of a Kerr black hole, the spacetime contains closed timelike curves. While practically inaccessible (passage through the outer horizon is one-way), the Kerr metric demonstrates CTCs in astrophysically realistic black hole spacetimes.

**Novikov Self-Consistency Principle**
The principle that in spacetimes containing CTCs, only self-consistent solutions to the laws of physics are physically realized. Formulated by Friedman, Novikov, Thorne, and colleagues (1990), the principle automatically eliminates paradoxical trajectories (such as physical analogs of the grandfather paradox) while permitting consistent CTC-based histories. The billiard ball model demonstrates that there can be multiple self-consistent solutions for given initial conditions, implying a form of indeterminism in CTC spacetimes.

**Post-Selected Teleportation (P-CTCs)**
Lloyd et al.'s (2011) alternative model of quantum CTCs, based on post-selection: the self-consistency requirement is implemented by requiring and post-selecting outcomes in which the state at the end of the loop equals the state at the beginning. The P-CTC model can be implemented using quantum teleportation protocols and avoids the information creation problem of Deutsch's model by discarding non-self-consistent outcomes rather than generating mixed states.

**Tipler Cylinder**
A time machine proposed by Frank Tipler (1974) based on an infinite rotating cylinder of sufficiently dense matter. Tipler showed that the spacetime outside such a cylinder would contain CTCs. The requirement for infinite length makes the construction physically unrealizable, and Hawking (1992) argued that quantum effects would prevent the operation of a finite version. Nevertheless, the Tipler cylinder is an important theoretical demonstration that CTCs can arise from apparently mundane matter configurations.

**Traversable Wormhole**
A hypothetical topological structure in spacetime — a tunnel connecting distant regions — through which matter and information can pass without being destroyed. Traversable wormholes (as distinct from Einstein-Rosen bridges, which are not traversable) were analyzed by Morris and Thorne (1988), who showed they require exotic matter to remain open and can in principle be converted into time machines by accelerating one mouth relative to the other, creating a time offset through special relativistic time dilation.

**Weak Energy Condition (WEC)**
The requirement that the energy density of matter, as measured by any observer moving at less than the speed of light, be non-negative. Most known forms of classical matter satisfy the WEC. Quantum effects (like the Casimir effect) can produce violations in localized regions. Traversable wormholes require matter that violates the WEC to keep their throats open — hence the need for "exotic matter."
