# Section 35.1: Closed Timelike Curves in General Relativity

---

## Section Introduction

General relativity describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime. Massive objects curve the spacetime around them, and this curvature affects the trajectories of particles and light. In extreme cases — near singularities, in rapidly rotating spacetimes — the curvature can become so severe that the causal structure of spacetime is dramatically altered.

A *closed timelike curve* (CTC) is a spacetime trajectory that is: (a) *timelike* — a particle following it would be moving at less than the speed of light, experiencing proper time — and (b) *closed* — it returns to its starting point. A person following a CTC would return, at some point in their proper time, to the exact spacetime location from which they started. This is the relativistic realization of backward time travel.

The existence of CTCs in solutions to the Einstein field equations is not in dispute: several such solutions are known. What is disputed is whether any of these solutions represents a physically realistic spacetime — whether our universe could, in principle, contain CTCs.
