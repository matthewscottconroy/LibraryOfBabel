# Section 35.2: Wormholes and Time Machines

---

## Section Introduction

The most extensively studied route to a time machine in contemporary theoretical physics involves traversable wormholes — hypothetical structures that create tunnels through spacetime, connecting distant regions. Morris and Thorne's influential 1988 paper demonstrated both that traversable wormholes are consistent with general relativity (given sufficient exotic matter) and that one can in principle be converted into a time machine.

This section examines the physics of traversable wormholes and the mechanism by which they can produce time travel. It also introduces the key obstacle: exotic matter, matter with negative energy density, which is required to keep the wormhole throat open.
