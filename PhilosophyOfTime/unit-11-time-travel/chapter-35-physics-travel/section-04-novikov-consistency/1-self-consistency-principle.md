# 35.4.1 The Novikov Self-Consistency Principle

---

## Formulation

The Novikov self-consistency principle was developed in a series of papers by Friedman, Morris, Novikov, Echeverria, Klinkhammer, Thorne, and Yurtsever, with the key results published in *Physical Review D* in 1990 (Friedman et al. 1990: 1915–1930). The principle holds:

**For spacetimes that contain closed timelike curves, only self-consistent solutions to the laws of physics are physically realized.**

This is a remarkably strong claim. It means that in a spacetime with CTCs, the initial conditions and the laws of physics together determine a unique, self-consistent history. Any trajectory or sequence of events that would produce a paradox — the physical analog of the grandfather paradox — is simply not a solution to the equations, and therefore does not occur.

The principle does not introduce any new physical law; it is rather a *selection principle* on the solutions of existing laws. Among all the mathematically possible histories consistent with the initial conditions, the ones that are actually realized are those that are self-consistent. Non-self-consistent histories are not physically possible, even if they are locally consistent with the laws at each instant.

## The Billiard Ball Model

Echeverria, Klinkhammer, and Thorne (1991: 1569–1589) demonstrated the principle's operation in a concrete physical model: a billiard ball and a wormhole time machine. The setup is as follows.

A billiard ball approaches the entrance of a wormhole time machine. If it enters the wormhole, it exits from the past mouth at an earlier time and at a different location — specifically, it exits in a position to collide with its earlier self. If the collision is a direct hit, it deflects its earlier self away from the wormhole entrance, so the earlier self never enters, so there is no later self emerging to cause the deflection. This is a physical analog of the grandfather paradox.

The Novikov principle predicts: the only physical trajectory is a self-consistent one. Echeverria et al. showed, by solving the equations of motion, that there exist self-consistent solutions: for example, the ball enters the wormhole at a slightly different angle, emerges from the past exit, hits its earlier self with a glancing blow, and deflects it by exactly the right amount so that it still enters the wormhole — but at the changed angle, so that the whole trajectory is self-consistent.

Crucially, for generic initial conditions (the ball approaching the wormhole), there are *multiple* self-consistent solutions. The physics does not uniquely determine which self-consistent solution occurs. This is a form of indeterminism: the initial conditions do not uniquely fix the future, even with the laws of physics fully known.

## Philosophical Implications

The Novikov principle has three philosophically significant implications.

**1. A new form of explanation.** In normal physics, events are explained by their prior causes: given the initial conditions and the laws, the outcome follows. In Novikov spacetimes, this is not the full story: the outcome is constrained to be self-consistent with the entire history. Explanation becomes *holistic* — the entire self-consistent solution is the explanatory unit, not the local causal history at each moment. This is a fundamentally different explanatory structure from the linear, forward-directed causation we normally assume.

**2. Indeterminism from consistency.** The Echeverria et al. result shows that self-consistency does not determine a unique outcome. When there are multiple self-consistent solutions for given initial conditions, something further must select which one is realized. Is this quantum randomness? Some other factor? The principle is silent on this, suggesting that a full physical treatment of CTCs requires quantum mechanics, not just classical mechanics.

**3. The nature of the constraint.** The self-consistency constraint operates globally: it restricts the space of possible histories to those that are self-consistent as a whole. This is a constraint on what kind of history the universe can have, not on what happens at any particular local moment. In this sense, it resembles the constraint imposed by boundary conditions at the end of spacetime (in some cosmological models) or by global conservation laws. It is a genuinely novel kind of physical constraint with no parallel in ordinary non-CTC physics.

## Connection to Lewis's Philosophical Analysis

The Novikov principle is the physical implementation of Lewis's consistency constraint in the philosophy of time travel. Lewis argued, as a matter of logic, that only self-consistent histories can be actual — the grandfather paradox is not proof of time travel's impossibility but proof that certain events will not occur. Novikov gives this argument a physical form: the laws of physics, applied globally to spacetimes with CTCs, implement the consistency requirement through the equations of motion.

The connection is not merely analogical. Lewis's philosophical analysis and Novikov's physical principle converge on the same structural point: when time travel is possible, the space of physical histories is restricted to self-consistent ones. The tools are different — philosophy uses logical consistency; physics uses solutions to differential equations — but the constraint is the same.
