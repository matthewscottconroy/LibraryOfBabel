## Annotated Entries

**Barbour, *The End of Time* (1999)**
The accessible book-length presentation of Barbour's timeless universe view. Barbour explains the Wheeler-DeWitt equation, the concept of Platonia, and the time capsule account in non-technical language with remarkable clarity. The philosophical implications are engaged seriously. An essential text for the contemporary debate about temporal fundamentality.

**Bombelli et al., "Space-Time as a Causal Set" (1987)**
The foundational paper of causal set theory, proposing that the fundamental structure of spacetime is a locally finite partial order. Short and accessible. Should be read alongside the more recent philosophical interpretations in Huggett and Wüthrich (2018).

**Garfield, *The Fundamental Wisdom of the Middle Way* (1995)**
The standard scholarly English translation of Nāgārjuna's MMK, with full philosophical commentary. Garfield's commentary situates the text within contemporary analytic philosophy as well as the Tibetan Buddhist commentarial tradition. Chapter 19 on time is essential; Chapters 1 (on causation), 2 (on motion), and 24 (on the two truths) provide essential context.

**Huggett and Wüthrich, *Beyond Spacetime* (2018)**
The most comprehensive current collection of philosophical essays on the foundations of quantum gravity, addressing emergent spacetime, the problem of time, causal set theory, loop quantum gravity, and related topics. Essential for anyone working at the intersection of philosophy of physics and philosophy of time.

**McTaggart, "The Unreality of Time" (1908)**
The original paper, required reading. Short enough to read in an hour but dense enough to reward multiple careful readings. Available freely online through JSTOR.

**Price, *Time's Arrow and Archimedes' Point* (1996)**
Excellent discussion of the relationship between temporal asymmetry, the block universe, and the appearance of temporal passage. Price's "Archimedean" approach — assessing temporal asymmetry from outside the temporal perspective — is philosophically innovative and bears directly on the questions of this chapter.

**Sorkin, "Causal Sets: Discrete Gravity" (2005)**
A clear, accessible overview of causal set theory by its principal developer. Explains the mathematical structure, the physical motivation, and the current state of the research program. A good entry point before tackling the more technical primary papers.

**Wüthrich, "The Fate of Presentism in Modern Physics" (2010)**
Examines the tension between presentism and modern physics (special relativity and quantum gravity) and whether any form of presentism can survive. Relevant to the discussion in this chapter of what quantum gravity implies about the reality of time and temporal becoming.
