# Chapter 36 Important Concepts: The Unreality of Time Revisited

---

**Advaita Vedanta**
A non-dualist school of Hindu philosophy, systematized by Ādi Śaṅkara (c. 788–820 CE), holding that ultimate reality is the single, undivided, timeless Brahman. The world of multiplicity, including temporal succession, is the appearance of Brahman through *māyā* (cosmic illusion). Time is conventionally real but ultimately empty of the kind of substantive, intrinsic existence ordinarily attributed to it.

**Brahman**
In Advaita Vedanta, the ultimate, undivided, self-luminous consciousness underlying all apparent reality. Brahman is *nirguna* — without qualities, without temporal determination. It is "eternal" not in the sense of lasting forever through time but in the sense of being timelessly present — outside time altogether. The recognition of one's identity with Brahman is the goal of Advaita practice.

**Causal Set**
A discrete partial order whose elements represent fundamental spacetime events and whose ordering relation represents causal precedence. Proposed as the fundamental structure of spacetime by Bombelli, Sorkin, and colleagues (1987). The smooth spacetime of general relativity is hypothesized to emerge from the collective behavior of many causal set elements. The sequential growth dynamics of causal sets builds in intrinsic becoming as a fundamental feature.

**C-Series (McTaggart)**
McTaggart's term for the underlying permanent order that generates the appearance of both the A-series and B-series. The C-series is a non-temporal ordering of events (not earlier-later, but simply ordered) that, combined with some experiential factor, generates the appearance of temporal succession. McTaggart held that the C-series is the ultimately real structure underlying the illusion of time.

**Discrete Becoming**
The sequential growth of the causal set, element by element, proposed in causal set dynamics (Rideout and Sorkin 2000). Each new element accreted to the causal set represents a genuine coming-to-be — a transition from non-existence to existence. Discrete becoming is a form of objective temporal becoming built into the fundamental dynamics, contrasting with the block universe where all events exist timelessly.

**Emptiness (Śūnyatā)**
The central concept of Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka philosophy: all phenomena, including time, are *śūnya* — empty of intrinsic, self-standing existence. They exist in dependence on other phenomena (dependent origination, *pratītyasamutpāda*), not from their own side. The emptiness of time means that temporal concepts (past, present, future) have no intrinsic nature that survives analysis; they are dependently originated conventions.

**Māyā**
In Advaita Vedanta, the creative power of Brahman through which the world of multiplicity appears. Māyā is neither real (it is not Brahman) nor unreal (it produces genuine appearances); it is "inexplicable" (*anirvacanīya*). The world's temporal character — the apparent flow from past to present to future — is a product of māyā, not a feature of Brahman as it is in itself.

**Nāgārjuna**
Second-century CE Buddhist philosopher, founder of the Madhyamaka school, whose *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* applies the method of *prasaṅga* (consequences analysis) to demonstrate the emptiness of intrinsic existence in all major philosophical categories, including causation, motion, identity, and time. His Chapter 19 on time is a short, dense, philosophically sophisticated argument for the absence of intrinsic temporal existence.

**Platonia**
Julian Barbour's term for the space of all possible instantaneous three-dimensional configurations of the universe — the arena within which the timeless wavefunction of the universe is defined. Each point in Platonia is a "Now" — a complete, instantaneous configuration. The wavefunction assigns amplitudes to Nows but does not order them temporally; the appearance of temporal succession is explained by time capsules.

**Problem of Time (Quantum Gravity)**
The challenge, arising in canonical quantum gravity, that the Wheeler-DeWitt equation governing the wavefunction of the universe contains no time parameter — time has been "factored out" by the diffeomorphism invariance of general relativity. How ordinary temporal structure — continuous time, temporal direction, temporal evolution — emerges from the timeless Wheeler-DeWitt framework is an unsolved fundamental problem.

**Sequential Growth Dynamics**
The preferred dynamical framework for causal set evolution (Rideout and Sorkin 2000), in which the causal set grows by the addition of new elements one at a time according to probability distributions satisfying physically motivated conditions. The growth process is stochastic — which element is added is probabilistic — and constitutes an intrinsic form of temporal becoming at the fundamental level.

**Time Capsule**
Barbour's concept for a Now (an instantaneous configuration) that contains records, memories, and traces that are coherent with the appearance of having evolved from a temporal sequence of earlier configurations. The wavefunction of the universe assigns high amplitude to time capsule Nows — configurations that contain coherent apparent memories. Time capsules explain why inhabitants of these configurations experience temporal succession, even though no actual succession occurs.

**Two Truths Doctrine**
The Madhyamaka distinction between *samvrti-satya* (conventional truth — the level at which ordinary things, including time, operate effectively) and *paramārtha-satya* (ultimate truth — the level at which analysis reveals the emptiness of intrinsic nature). Time is conventionally real (it operates in ordinary experience and practical life) and ultimately empty (it has no intrinsic nature that survives analysis). This doctrine prevents Nāgārjuna's conclusion from collapsing into nihilism.

**Wheeler-DeWitt Equation**
The equation governing the wavefunction of the universe in canonical quantum gravity: Ĥ|Ψ⟩ = 0. Derived by DeWitt from Dirac's quantization of general relativity, the equation has no explicit time parameter — it says that the wavefunction is annihilated by the Hamiltonian constraint, not that it evolves in time. The equation is the primary source of the "problem of time" and the basis for Barbour's timeless universe interpretation.
