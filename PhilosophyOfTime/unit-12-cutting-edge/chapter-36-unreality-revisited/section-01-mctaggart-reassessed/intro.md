# Section 36.1: McTaggart Reassessed

---

## Section Introduction

In Chapter 13, we worked through McTaggart's argument for the unreality of time in detail. Here we return to it from a different vantage point: not as an argument to be assessed in isolation, but as a claim to be compared with what physics and subsequent philosophy have produced. Has McTaggart been vindicated? Has he been superseded? Or has the relationship between his argument and later developments turned out to be more complex than a simple yes or no?

The key is to distinguish three separate claims that might all be packaged under the label "time is unreal": the claim that objective passage (the A-series) is unreal; the claim that the A-series is incoherent; and the claim that time altogether — including the B-series ordering of earlier and later — is unreal. McTaggart held all three. Physics and philosophy offer different verdicts on each.
