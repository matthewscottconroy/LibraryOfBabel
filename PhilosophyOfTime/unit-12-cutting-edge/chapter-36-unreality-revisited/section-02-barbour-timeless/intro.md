# Section 36.2: Barbour's Timeless Universe

---

## Section Introduction

Julian Barbour's *The End of Time* (1999) is the most accessible and philosophically developed presentation of the view that time is fundamentally unreal — not because physics has eliminated the A-series (that is the standard B-theory claim) but because physics has eliminated *time altogether* as a fundamental parameter. Barbour argues that the correct quantum cosmological description of the universe involves no time variable; the wavefunction of the universe is defined over a space of instantaneous configurations he calls "Platonia," and there is no temporal ordering of these configurations at the fundamental level.

This is a bold and philosophically serious position. Its development requires engaging with the Wheeler-DeWitt equation, the problem of time in quantum gravity, and the question of whether the appearance of temporal succession can be explained without a fundamental time parameter.
