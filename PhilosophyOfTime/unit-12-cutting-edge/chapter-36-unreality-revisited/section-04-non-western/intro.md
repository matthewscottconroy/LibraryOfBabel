# Section 36.4: Non-Western Frameworks of Temporal Unreality

---

## Section Introduction

The philosophical literature on the unreality of time in the Western tradition — McTaggart, Barbour, the quantum gravity program — has developed largely in isolation from equally sophisticated non-Western traditions that have arrived at related conclusions through different methods. This section examines two of the most philosophically developed non-Western frameworks: Buddhist philosophy of time, drawing on Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka analysis and the Abhidharma tradition; and Advaita Vedanta's treatment of time as an aspect of *maya* (cosmic illusion).

These traditions are not here deployed as exotic curiosities or as confirmations of Western conclusions. They are treated as genuine philosophical contributions that offer different analyses, different methods, and sometimes different conclusions from the Western traditions examined in this chapter. Engaging with them honestly requires acknowledging both the convergences and the divergences.
