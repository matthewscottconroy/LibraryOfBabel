# Chapter 37 Exercises: The Emergence of Spacetime

---

## Conceptual Exercises

**1. Strong vs. Weak Emergence**
Explain the distinction between strong and weak emergence. Provide an example of each from outside philosophy of time (you may use consciousness, temperature, or any other example). Then apply the distinction to the emergence of spacetime: which kind of emergence does the AdS/CFT correspondence suggest? What would it take to determine whether the emergence is strong or weak?

**2. The AdS/CFT Correspondence**
Explain the AdS/CFT correspondence in your own words to a student who has not studied physics. What are the two theories that are dual? What does "duality" mean here? In what sense is the bulk temporal structure emergent from the boundary theory? What is philosophically significant about the fact that the boundary theory has one fewer spatial dimension than the bulk?

**3. The Ryu-Takayanagi Formula**
The Ryu-Takayanagi formula S_A = Area(γ_A) / 4G_ℏ relates entanglement entropy in the boundary to geometric area in the bulk. What is entanglement entropy? What is geometric area in this context? Explain in conceptual terms why this formula implies that spatial structure in the bulk is encoded in quantum information in the boundary. Does this formula apply to temporal structure as well?

**4. ER = EPR**
Explain the ER=EPR conjecture. What is an Einstein-Rosen bridge? What is EPR entanglement? What is the conjectured relationship between them? What would it mean for the ontology of spacetime if the conjecture is correct? Does the conjecture support the claim that spacetime is "made of" quantum entanglement?

**5. Spin Networks**
Loop quantum gravity proposes that the fundamental structure of spacetime is a spin network — a graph whose nodes and edges carry quantum labels representing volumes and areas. Explain how a spin network differs from a spacetime: in what sense is it non-spatiotemporal? If spin networks are the fundamental structure, how does smooth spacetime emerge from them? What aspects of this emergence remain unsolved?

**6. Spacetime Functionalism**
Explain spacetime functionalism as proposed by Huggett and Wüthrich. What is the analogy with functionalism about mind? What is the "spacetime role" that any substrate must play to count as spacetime? Is spacetime functionalism a satisfying account of what it means for spacetime to be real, or does it deflate the question in a way that loses something important?

**7. Temporal Ontology and Emergence**
If temporal structure is weakly emergent from a non-spatiotemporal substrate, what follows for the main positions in temporal ontology (presentism, eternalism, growing block, moving spotlight)? Can any of these positions be formulated within an emergent spacetime framework? Which is most naturally compatible with the picture of emergent spacetime?

## Discussion and Essay Questions

**8. The Fragility of Time**
The AdS/CFT correspondence suggests that temporal structure in the bulk can "break down" (at singularities) while the boundary theory remains well-defined. What does this imply for the metaphysical status of time? Does it support the view that time is not fundamental, or could it be accommodated within a view on which time is fundamental but sometimes inaccessible?

**9. Information, Entanglement, and Time**
Van Raamsdonk (2010) argued that removing quantum entanglement between two parts of a system causes the corresponding spacetime regions to become disconnected. Write an essay evaluating this claim and its implications. If spatial connectivity is constituted by entanglement, is temporal connectivity also constituted by entanglement? What would this mean for our understanding of temporal relations?

## Lab and Observational Exercises

**10. Entanglement Investigation**
Research the phenomenon of quantum entanglement and the experimental tests of Bell's inequalities (the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded for experimental tests that decisively confirmed entanglement predictions). Prepare a two-page summary covering: (a) what quantum entanglement is; (b) what Bell's theorem shows; (c) how the experimental tests were performed; (d) what the confirmed entanglement implies about non-locality. Reflect: if entanglement is "more fundamental than spacetime" (as ER=EPR suggests), what does the non-locality of entanglement tell us about the status of spatial and temporal relations?

**11. Research: Quantum Gravity Programs**
Research three different approaches to quantum gravity — loop quantum gravity, causal set theory, and CDT (causal dynamical triangulation) — and compare their treatment of time. For each, identify: (a) what the fundamental ontology is; (b) how continuous time emerges (if it does); (c) whether the approach is A-theoretic or B-theoretic in its treatment of temporal becoming. Write a 600-word comparative analysis.

**12. Conceptual Map**
Create a conceptual map showing the relationships between the following concepts discussed in this chapter: weak emergence, strong emergence, AdS/CFT, boundary theory, bulk theory, Ryu-Takayanagi formula, entanglement entropy, ER=EPR, spacetime functionalism, spin networks, CDT. Draw arrows indicating relationships (entails, is evidence for, is an example of, contrasts with) and annotate each arrow. The map should be informative enough to serve as a study guide for the chapter.
