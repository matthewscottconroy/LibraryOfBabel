## Annotated Entries

**Bedau, "Weak Emergence" (1997)**
The classic philosophical analysis of weak emergence. Bedau defines weak emergence in terms of macro-level properties that are in principle derivable from micro-level descriptions through simulation. Provides the conceptual framework needed to assess what kind of emergence applies to spacetime.

**Chalmers, "Strong and Weak Emergence" (2006)**
Chalmers's analysis of the distinction between strong and weak emergence, arguing that consciousness is strongly emergent (not derivable from physical description) while almost all other macro-properties are weakly emergent. Directly relevant to the question of what kind of emergence applies to spacetime.

**Huggett and Wüthrich, *Beyond Spacetime* (2018)**
The most comprehensive philosophical treatment of quantum gravity and emergent spacetime. Covers AdS/CFT, loop quantum gravity, causal set theory, and related programs from a philosophical perspective. The introduction and conclusion are accessible; the individual chapters vary in technical difficulty.

**Huggett and Wüthrich, "Emergent Spacetime and Empirical (In)coherence" (2013)**
A focused paper examining the philosophical challenge of empirical confirmation when the fundamental theory is non-spatiotemporal. How can we confirm or disconfirm theories that describe a substrate without space or time, given that all our observations are spatiotemporal? A rigorous treatment of an important methodological problem.

**Maldacena, "The Large N Limit" (1999)**
The original AdS/CFT paper. Technical but the introduction and conclusion are accessible. Essential to read the original paper before the philosophical secondary literature.

**Maldacena and Susskind, "Cool Horizons" (2013)**
The ER=EPR paper. Written in a relatively accessible style for a physics research paper. The introduction clearly states the conjecture and its motivation. Essential primary source.

**Rovelli, *Quantum Gravity* (2004)**
The most comprehensive textbook treatment of loop quantum gravity by one of its founders. The first few chapters provide an accessible conceptual overview before the technical development. Rovelli's philosophical reflections on time in LQG are particularly valuable.

**Ryu and Takayanagi, "Holographic Derivation of Entanglement Entropy" (2006)**
The paper establishing the Ryu-Takayanagi formula relating boundary entanglement entropy to bulk geometric area. Technically demanding but conceptually important. The formula is the most specific quantitative link between quantum information and spacetime geometry.

**Van Raamsdonk, "Building up Spacetime with Quantum Entanglement" (2010)**
A conceptually clear and influential paper arguing that the spatial connectivity of the bulk spacetime is constituted by entanglement between subsystems of the boundary theory. One of the most important conceptual papers linking entanglement to spacetime geometry.
