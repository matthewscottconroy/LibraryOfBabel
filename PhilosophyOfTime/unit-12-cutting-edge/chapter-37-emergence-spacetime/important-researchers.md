# Chapter 37 Important Researchers: The Emergence of Spacetime

---

**Nick Huggett (b. 1965) and Christian Wüthrich (b. 1973)**
Collaborative philosophical partnership between Huggett (University of Illinois at Chicago) and Wüthrich (University of Geneva) that has produced the most systematic philosophical engagement with quantum gravity and emergent spacetime. Their edited volume *Beyond Spacetime* (2018) is the definitive collection of philosophical essays on the foundations of quantum gravity. Their joint papers on emergent spacetime, empirical coherence in theories without spacetime, and spacetime functionalism represent the current state of the art in philosophy of physics applied to quantum gravity.

**Juan Maldacena (b. 1968)**
Argentine-American theoretical physicist at the Institute for Advanced Study whose 1997 paper proposing the AdS/CFT correspondence became one of the most cited papers in the history of physics. The correspondence has had extraordinary influence on string theory, quantum gravity, condensed matter physics, and quantum information theory. Maldacena's 2013 paper with Susskind proposing ER=EPR is another major contribution. He has received the Breakthrough Prize, the Dirac Medal, and numerous other honors.

**Renate Loll (b. 1962)**
Dutch theoretical physicist at Radboud University Nijmegen who, with Jan Ambjørn and Jerzy Jurkiewicz, developed causal dynamical triangulation (CDT) — an approach to quantum gravity based on summing over causally structured triangulations. CDT has produced some of the most encouraging numerical evidence that a smooth, four-dimensional spacetime can emerge from discrete quantum structures. Loll's work combines technical precision with philosophical reflection on the implications of emergent spacetime.

**Carlo Rovelli (b. 1956)**
Italian theoretical physicist and philosopher of science, one of the principal founders of loop quantum gravity (with Lee Smolin and Abhay Ashtekar). Rovelli's work combines technical contributions to LQG — the development of spin network states, loop representation, discrete quantum geometry — with sustained philosophical reflection on the nature of time, space, and quantum mechanics. His books *Quantum Gravity* (2004) and *The Order of Time* (2018) represent both the technical foundation and the philosophical interpretation of LQG. His philosophical work on time — arguing that time is not fundamental and that temporal experience arises from thermodynamic and quantum processes — is directly relevant to this chapter.

**Shinsei Ryu and Tadashi Takayanagi**
Japanese theoretical physicists who in 2006 derived the formula S_A = Area(γ_A) / 4G_ℏ relating the entanglement entropy of a boundary region in AdS/CFT to the area of a minimal bulk surface. The Ryu-Takayanagi formula is one of the most important specific results within AdS/CFT and provided the most concrete evidence that spacetime geometry is encoded in quantum entanglement. The formula has been extensively developed and extended since 2006 and remains a central tool in holographic approaches to quantum gravity.

**Leonard Susskind (b. 1940)**
American theoretical physicist at Stanford University and one of the founders of string theory. His contributions to the foundations of quantum gravity include the holographic principle (the proposal that the information content of a region of spacetime is bounded by the area of its boundary), the development of string landscape, and numerous contributions to AdS/CFT. His 2013 paper with Maldacena proposing ER=EPR has been highly influential in shaping current thinking about the relationship between quantum entanglement and spacetime geometry.

**Mark Van Raamsdonk (b. 1975)**
Canadian theoretical physicist at the University of British Columbia whose 2010 paper "Building up Spacetime with Quantum Entanglement" argued, using AdS/CFT, that the spatial connectivity of the bulk spacetime is constituted by entanglement between subsystems of the boundary theory. The paper is conceptually clear and has been enormously influential in establishing the idea that "spacetime is made of entanglement." Van Raamsdonk continues to work on the implications of AdS/CFT for the foundations of quantum gravity.
