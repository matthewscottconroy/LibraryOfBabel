# Chapter 37: The Emergence of Spacetime

---

## Chapter Introduction

The idea that spacetime might not be fundamental — that the four-dimensional arena we inhabit might emerge from something more basic — was once the exclusive preserve of speculative metaphysics. It has become, in the first decades of the twenty-first century, a serious and active research program in theoretical physics. Several converging lines of inquiry, associated with string theory, loop quantum gravity, causal set theory (examined in Chapter 36), and holographic duality, all point toward the same possibility: that the spatiotemporal structure of our world is emergent, derived from a substrate that is itself not spatiotemporal.

If this is right, the implications for the philosophy of time are immediate and profound. Time, as a feature of spacetime, would be emergent. The questions explored throughout this course — the direction of time, the passage of time, the metaphysics of temporal ontology — would be questions about an emergent, high-level structure, not about fundamental reality. The fundamental questions would be about the non-spatiotemporal substrate from which spacetime derives.

This chapter examines what "emergence" means in this context, the specific physical proposals that suggest spacetime is emergent, and the philosophical implications. Section 37.1 distinguishes strong and weak emergence and asks which kind is relevant here. Section 37.2 examines the AdS/CFT correspondence — the most mathematically precise and empirically developed example of holographic duality — and its implications for temporal ontology. Section 37.3 turns to the ER=EPR conjecture: the proposal that wormholes and quantum entanglement are equivalent. Section 37.4 considers the philosophical implications of a non-spatiotemporal ontology and introduces the "spacetime functionalism" response.
