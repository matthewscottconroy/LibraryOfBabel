# 37.2.1 The AdS/CFT Correspondence

---

## The Statement of the Duality

Juan Maldacena's 1997 paper "The Large-N Limit of Superconformal Field Theories and Supergravity" (*International Journal of Theoretical Physics* 38, 1999; first circulated as a preprint in 1997) proposed that a specific string theory — Type IIB superstring theory on AdS₅ × S⁵ — is equivalent to a specific conformal field theory — *N* = 4 super Yang-Mills theory — on the four-dimensional boundary of the five-dimensional anti-de Sitter (AdS₅) space.

The two theories are conjectured to be *dual*: they describe the same physical content but using different mathematical frameworks. The partition functions, correlation functions, and all physical observables of one theory can be mapped to corresponding quantities in the other. The correspondence is not an approximate similarity; it is (conjectured to be) an exact equivalence.

The key structural feature: the boundary CFT is a quantum field theory with no gravity. The bulk theory is a string theory with gravity (including a dynamical spacetime metric). The bulk spacetime — the arena where strings propagate and gravity operates — is emergent from the boundary theory, which has no dynamical spacetime of its own.

## The Structure of the Correspondence

The AdS spacetime has a characteristic feature: its spatial sections are negatively curved (like a saddle), and it has a boundary at spatial infinity. The CFT lives on this boundary. The bulk theory describes physics in the interior of the AdS space; the boundary theory describes physics on the (asymptotic) boundary.

A key feature of the correspondence is the relationship between the radial coordinate in the bulk (the "extra dimension") and the energy scale in the boundary theory. Moving into the bulk (toward smaller radial distances from the boundary) corresponds to moving to lower energies in the boundary theory. This means the bulk's spatial structure encodes the renormalization group flow of the boundary theory.

What does this imply for time? The boundary theory has a time coordinate — boundary time — which is a genuine, one-dimensional time of a (d)-dimensional spacetime. The bulk theory has a time coordinate — bulk time — which is a genuine, one-dimensional time of the (d+1)-dimensional bulk spacetime. In the correspondence, boundary time maps to bulk time at the boundary. But the bulk's temporal structure — including the way time is affected by gravity, how it relates to the spatial geometry, how it behaves near singularities — is encoded in the boundary theory in a non-trivial way.

## Time in the Correspondence

From the perspective of the AdS/CFT correspondence, temporal structure in the bulk is emergent from the boundary theory in the following sense: the bulk temporal geometry (how events are connected by timelike relations, how time is affected by the curvature of the bulk metric) is not a primitive input to the boundary theory but is *derived* from the dynamics of the boundary CFT.

This is philosophically striking. The boundary CFT has a time coordinate — but it is the flat-space time of a non-gravitational quantum field theory, without the rich temporal structure (gravitational time dilation, curvature-dependent causal structure) of the bulk. The bulk's rich temporal structure is emergent from the simpler temporal structure of the boundary.

For singularities in particular: if the bulk spacetime contains a black hole singularity — a region where classical GR predicts temporal structure breaks down — the boundary theory is nonetheless perfectly well-defined. The singularity in the bulk corresponds to something (whose precise nature is not fully understood) in the boundary theory; the boundary theory "knows about" the singularity but does not itself become singular. This suggests that the apparent breakdown of temporal structure in the bulk is a failure of the emergent description, not a failure of the underlying boundary theory.
