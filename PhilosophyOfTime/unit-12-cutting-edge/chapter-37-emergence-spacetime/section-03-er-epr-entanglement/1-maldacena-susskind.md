# 37.3.1 ER = EPR: Wormholes and Quantum Entanglement

---

## The Einstein-Rosen Bridge and EPR Entanglement

In 1935, Einstein published two important papers on foundational topics in physics. With Rosen, he published a paper describing a "bridge" in the topology of spacetime connecting two separate regions: what we now call the Einstein-Rosen bridge or wormhole. In the same year, with Podolsky and Rosen (EPR), he published a paper arguing that quantum mechanics, specifically the phenomenon of quantum entanglement between spatially separated particles, was incomplete as a physical theory.

For nearly eighty years, these two papers were treated as addressing entirely separate topics. The 1935 paper on bridges concerned the topology of classical spacetime; the EPR paper concerned quantum correlations between particles. No obvious connection was seen between them.

Maldacena and Susskind (*Fortschritte der Physik*, 2013) proposed a startling unification: the Einstein-Rosen bridge (ER) connecting two entangled black holes is the same physical object as the EPR entanglement (EPR) between them. The formula "ER = EPR" encapsulates the conjecture: you cannot have one without the other. A pair of entangled black holes are connected by a wormhole; a wormhole connecting two black holes is an instance of quantum entanglement.

## The Argument

The argument motivating ER = EPR begins with a well-known result in quantum gravity: the *thermofield double state* (TFD state) of two copies of a quantum field theory (CFT) is dual, via AdS/CFT, to an eternal black hole in the bulk. The eternal black hole has two exterior regions connected by an Einstein-Rosen bridge (the two sides of the Penrose diagram are connected behind the horizons).

The TFD state is a highly entangled state between the two copies of the CFT. The key observation: this entanglement between the two boundary theories corresponds, in the bulk, to the geometric connection provided by the Einstein-Rosen bridge. Remove the entanglement (by, hypothetically, disentangling the two CFTs), and the two bulk spacetime regions become disconnected — the bridge disappears.

Van Raamsdonk (2010: 2323–2329) had already argued, from the Ryu-Takayanagi formula, that the geometric connectivity of the bulk spacetime is encoded in the entanglement structure of the boundary theory. Maldacena and Susskind extended this to the specific case of wormholes and identified the precise sense in which the wormhole and the entanglement are the same thing.

## Philosophical Implications

ER = EPR has several philosophically significant implications for the nature of spacetime and time.

*Entanglement as the glue of spacetime*: if wormholes are entanglement, and if wormholes connect distant spacetime regions, then the topological connectivity of spacetime is determined by the pattern of quantum entanglement. Spacetime topology — and more specifically, the spatial and temporal connectivity of our world — is not a primitive geometric feature but is constituted by quantum correlations. This extends and sharpens the idea that "spacetime is made of entanglement."

*Non-locality and temporal structure*: quantum entanglement is famously "non-local" — entangled particles exhibit correlations that cannot be explained by any locally-mediated influence. The ER=EPR conjecture suggests that this non-locality is not a mysterious feature hovering above spacetime but is instead identical to a geometric feature of spacetime (the wormhole). Non-local correlations are just the boundary description of spatial/temporal connectivity.

*Implications for the direction of time*: if wormholes (which connect spacetime regions across the horizon structure of black holes) are constituted by entanglement, and if the pattern of entanglement has a natural temporal direction (generated by the thermodynamic arrow of time, as discussed in Chapter 38), then the temporal structure of the wormhole — including the direction of time within it — may be inherited from the temporal asymmetry of entanglement dynamics. This is a speculative but potentially deep connection between the thermodynamic arrow and the causal structure of spacetime.

## Caveats

ER = EPR is a conjecture, not a theorem. It is not even as well-established as AdS/CFT — it is a bold proposal based on suggestive evidence from specific cases, whose generality is not yet established. The conjecture faces both technical challenges (specifying precisely what "ER = EPR" means for general entangled systems) and conceptual challenges (explaining the mechanism by which quantum entanglement constitutes the geometry of a wormhole).

Nevertheless, the conjecture has attracted significant attention and has stimulated important research. It represents the cutting edge of attempts to understand the relationship between quantum information and spacetime structure, and its philosophical implications — for the nature of space, time, and their relationship to quantum correlations — deserve serious philosophical attention.
