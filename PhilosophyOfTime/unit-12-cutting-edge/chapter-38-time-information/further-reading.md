## Annotated Entries

**Shannon, Claude E. 1948. "A Mathematical Theory of Communication." *Bell System Technical Journal* 27 (3): 379–423.**
The founding paper of information theory. Introduces the bit, defines entropy as a measure of information content, and establishes the fundamental theorems of data compression and channel capacity. The paper is technically demanding but the conceptual sections (§1–6) are accessible. The formal identity between Shannon entropy and Boltzmann-Gibbs entropy — which Shannon mentions without dwelling on its physical implications — has generated sixty years of philosophical debate.

**Landauer, Rolf. 1961. "Irreversibility and Heat Generation in the Computing Process." *IBM Journal of Research and Development* 5 (3): 183–191.**
The paper establishing Landauer's principle: information erasure generates heat. Essential reading for understanding the information-thermodynamics connection. The argument is physical and conceptual rather than mathematical and is accessible to non-specialists. Landauer argues against the view that computation can be made thermodynamically free in principle — any irreversible logical operation has an irreducible thermodynamic cost.

**Earman, John, and John D. Norton. 1999. "Exorcist XIV: The Wrath of Maxwell's Demon. Part II." *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* 30 (1): 1–40.**
A comprehensive critical examination of the exorcism of Maxwell's demon via Landauer's principle. Earman and Norton argue that standard demonstrations of the demon's thermodynamic defeat are circular — they smuggle in thermodynamic assumptions. The paper is philosophically rigorous and technically careful, and represents the most thorough critical assessment of the information-thermodynamics connection in the philosophical literature.

**Hawking, Stephen W. 1975. "Particle Creation by Black Holes." *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 43 (3): 199–220.**
The original paper deriving Hawking radiation. Technical and requiring advanced knowledge of quantum field theory in curved spacetime, but the physical argument is described in the introduction. This paper generated the information paradox directly: if Hawking radiation is purely thermal, information falls into the black hole and never comes out.

**Wallace, David. 2018. "Why Black Hole Information Loss Is Paradoxical." In *Beyond Spacetime*, edited by Huggett and Wüthrich.**
A clear and rigorous philosophical treatment of why the information paradox is genuinely paradoxical and not easily dissolved by philosophical maneuvers. Wallace argues that information loss is not just a technical problem but a deep incompatibility between the principles of quantum mechanics and the structure of spacetime as described by general relativity. Recommended as the first philosophical reading on the paradox.

**Bennett, Charles H. 1987. "Demons, Engines and the Second Law." *Scientific American* 257 (5): 108–116.**
An accessible survey by one of the founders of quantum information theory, explaining the connection between Maxwell's demon, Szilard's resolution, Landauer's principle, and reversible computation. The article is aimed at general readers and explains clearly why and how information erasure is the key to the demon's defeat. An excellent entry point into the technical literature.

**Wheeler, John Archibald. 1990. "Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for Links."**
Wheeler's "it from bit" essay, arguing that all of physics — every particle, every field, every spacetime event — derives its existence from information: yes-or-no answers to binary questions. Wheeler's vision is speculative but extraordinarily influential in shaping subsequent work on quantum information, quantum gravity, and the foundations of physics. The essay is written in Wheeler's characteristic aphoristic style and rewards multiple readings.

**Penrose, Roger. 2004. *The Road to Reality*. London: Jonathan Cape. Chapter 30.**
A comprehensive but accessible treatment of the black hole information paradox and the status of unitarity in quantum gravity. Penrose defends the view that unitarity may be violated in quantum gravity — that the collapse of the wavefunction involves genuine entropy increase — a minority view that illuminates what is at stake in the paradox.

**Lutz, Éric, and Sergio Ciliberto. 2015. "Information: From Maxwell's Demon to Landauer's Eraser." *Physics Today* 68 (9): 30–35.**
A review article describing the experimental confirmation of Landauer's principle and its connection to Maxwell's demon. The 2012 experiment by Bérut et al. directly measured the heat generated by a single bit erasure and confirmed Landauer's principle. This article is the best starting point for understanding the experimental status of the information-thermodynamics connection.

**Bostrom, Nick. 2003. "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?" *Philosophical Quarterly* 53 (211): 243–255.**
The clearest philosophical formulation of the simulation hypothesis. Bostrom's argument is rigorous — a trilemma with clear logical structure — and illustrates the philosophical implications of computational approaches to physics. The paper does not settle whether we are simulated, but it demonstrates that the question is philosophically non-trivial and that our credence in the simulation hypothesis should depend on our credence in empirical claims about the future of technology.
