# Chapter 38: Time and Information

---

## Chapter Introduction

Among the most fertile and philosophically consequential developments in physics over the past century is the discovery of deep connections between time, entropy, and information. These connections are not merely analogical; they are mathematically precise and physically significant. The arrow of time — the pervasive asymmetry between past and future — is intimately related to the direction of increasing entropy, and entropy is intimately related to information. Information, in turn, has turned out to have physical consequences: erasing information generates heat, and the loss or preservation of information is a central question in quantum gravity.

This chapter traces four dimensions of the time-information connection. Section 38.1 examines the relationship between Shannon information entropy and thermodynamic entropy, and the philosophical question of whether they are "the same thing" or merely formally analogous. Section 38.2 turns to the black hole information paradox — the deepest current problem at the intersection of quantum mechanics and general relativity — and recent proposals for its resolution. Section 38.3 examines Landauer's principle: the discovery that information erasure has physical consequences, demonstrating that information is not merely abstract. Section 38.4 considers digital physics: the radical proposal that the universe is fundamentally a computation and that time is computational steps.

The connections explored here are not only physically important; they are philosophically consequential. If information is physically real — if it has mass and energy consequences — then informational concepts are not mere representations of physical reality but partly constitutive of it. And if the direction of time is a direction in which information is processed, created, and erased, then the arrow of time and the logic of information are facets of the same fundamental structure.
