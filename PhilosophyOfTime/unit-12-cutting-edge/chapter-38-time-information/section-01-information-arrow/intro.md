# Section 38.1: The Information Arrow of Time

---

## Section Introduction

The second law of thermodynamics — entropy always increases — provides the canonical explanation of the arrow of time. But entropy is a concept that lives in two worlds: the physical world of thermodynamic systems and the abstract world of probability and information theory. Claude Shannon's 1948 mathematical theory of communication introduced a measure of information — Shannon entropy — that is formally identical to thermodynamic entropy. The question of whether this formal identity reflects a deep physical connection or a mere mathematical coincidence has been debated since Shannon's work appeared.

This section examines the connection between Shannon information entropy and thermodynamic entropy, Leon Brillouin's development of the connection, and the role of information in understanding the arrow of time.
