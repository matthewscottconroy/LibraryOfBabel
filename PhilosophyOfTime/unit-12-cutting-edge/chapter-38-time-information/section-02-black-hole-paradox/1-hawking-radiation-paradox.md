# 38.2.1 Hawking Radiation and the Information Paradox

---

## Hawking Radiation

Hawking's 1975 discovery (*Communications in Mathematical Physics* 43: 199–220) used quantum field theory in curved spacetime to show that a black hole emits thermal radiation with a characteristic temperature inversely proportional to its mass:

T_H = ℏc³ / (8πGMk_B)

This radiation arises from quantum effects near the event horizon: virtual particle-antiparticle pairs are created near the horizon; one falls in, the other escapes as real radiation. The escaping particle carries energy, gradually reducing the mass of the black hole. Over an enormous time scale, the black hole evaporates completely.

The Hawking radiation is *thermal*: it has the character of blackbody radiation at temperature T_H, with no information content beyond the temperature (and hence the mass, charge, and angular momentum of the black hole). This is the crucial feature for the information paradox.

## The Paradox

When matter falls into a black hole, it carries information: the specific quantum state of the matter (which particles were present, in which quantum states, in what positions and momenta). Quantum mechanics requires that this information is *not destroyed* — quantum evolution is unitary, meaning that the total information content of the universe is preserved. Information can be scrambled and redistributed but not lost.

But if Hawking radiation is purely thermal — if it contains no information about the state of the infalling matter beyond the three macroscopic parameters (mass, charge, angular momentum) — then when the black hole evaporates, the information about all the matter that fell in over the black hole's lifetime is lost. The initial quantum state of the infalling matter cannot be recovered from the outgoing Hawking radiation.

This is a direct conflict with quantum mechanics. Quantum mechanics requires information conservation (unitarity). Hawking's calculation implies information loss. One of them must be wrong, or they must be reconciled through some more fundamental framework.

## The Page Curve

Don Page (1993: 2266–2270) analyzed the expected behavior of the entanglement entropy of the Hawking radiation over the course of the evaporation. He showed that if quantum mechanics is fundamentally preserved (if the evaporation is unitary), the entanglement entropy of the radiation should follow a characteristic curve — the "Page curve" — that first rises and then falls back to zero as the black hole fully evaporates.

The Page curve has a simple physical interpretation. Initially, the radiation is entangled with the interior of the black hole (the information about infalling matter is inside). The entanglement entropy of the radiation grows as more radiation is emitted. But at the "Page time" — roughly the halfway point in the evaporation — the radiation has enough degrees of freedom to start "purifying" the earlier radiation; the entanglement entropy begins to decrease. At the end of the evaporation, the radiation is in a pure state, and all the information has been returned through the Hawking radiation.

Page's prediction was that any truly unitary evaporation must follow this curve. Hawking's original calculation, by contrast, predicts that the entanglement entropy continues to increase monotonically, reaching a maximum at the end of evaporation — which would mean information is lost.
