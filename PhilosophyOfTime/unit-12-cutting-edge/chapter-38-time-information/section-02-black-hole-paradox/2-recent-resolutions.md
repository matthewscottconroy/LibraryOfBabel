# 38.2.2 Recent Progress on the Information Paradox

---

## The Island Formula

A major breakthrough in the black hole information paradox came in 2019–2020 with the development of the "island formula" for entanglement entropy (Penington 2019: 2; Almheiri et al. 2019: 013; Almheiri et al. 2020: 013). The key result: when the formula for the "generalized entropy" of the Hawking radiation is correctly computed using quantum gravity corrections, the result follows the Page curve — information is preserved.

The island formula extends the Ryu-Takayanagi formula (Section 37.2) to dynamical situations involving evaporating black holes. The generalized entropy includes not only the area of the minimal surface but also the quantum field theory contributions in "islands" — regions of the black hole interior that are not directly connected to the exterior but that contribute to the entanglement entropy of the exterior radiation.

The physical picture is striking: at late times (after the Page time), the calculation of the radiation's entanglement entropy must include contributions from the black hole interior — specifically, from the region behind the horizon that is called an "island." The island's contribution to the entropy ensures that the total generalized entropy follows the Page curve.

This is a semiclassical calculation — it uses quantum field theory in a gravitational background, supplemented by quantum gravity corrections captured by the generalized entropy formula. It is not a full quantum gravity calculation, and the precise mechanism by which information escapes the black hole remains not fully understood. But the result strongly suggests that information is preserved, and provides a quantitative formula for how.

## Philosophical Implications

The resolution of the black hole information paradox (if the island formula approach is correct) has several philosophical implications for the nature of time.

*Information and temporal structure*: if information is conserved in the evaporation process — if the outgoing Hawking radiation encodes all the information about the infalling matter — then the temporal evolution of the black hole system is unitary throughout. The apparent information loss would be an artifact of using an incomplete description (Hawking's semiclassical calculation) rather than a genuine violation of unitarity.

*The holographic principle*: the island formula relies on the generalized Ryu-Takayanagi formula, which encodes the relationship between spacetime geometry and quantum information. The resolution of the information paradox thus depends on the same holographic structure that underlies AdS/CFT. This suggests that the relationship between temporal evolution and information is fundamentally holographic: the complete description of the evaporation requires a holographic (boundary) perspective that recovers information that the bulk description loses.

*Complementarity*: Susskind and others developed the concept of "black hole complementarity" — the view that different observers (those outside the horizon and those inside) see complementary but not simultaneously available descriptions of the same physical events. An outside observer sees information encoded in the Hawking radiation; an inside observer sees matter crossing the horizon undisturbed. Both descriptions are consistent; they just cannot be simultaneously confirmed. Complementarity raises deep questions about the relationship between the observer's temporal perspective and the information available to them.

## What Remains Unsolved

The island formula provides strong evidence that black hole evaporation is unitary, but it does not fully explain *how* information gets out of the black hole. The mechanism by which information encoded in the interior becomes accessible in the exterior radiation is not yet fully understood. This is sometimes called the "information-getting-out problem" — distinct from the question of whether information is preserved.

The physics of the "firewall" (Almheiri, Marolf, Polchinski, and Sully 2013) adds another wrinkle: if information is to be encoded in the Hawking radiation, there may be a conflict with the equivalence principle, which predicts that an observer falling into a black hole should experience nothing unusual at the horizon. Some proposals suggest that a high-energy "firewall" exists at the horizon that destroys infalling observers; others argue that the apparent conflict is dissolved by complementarity or by quantum gravitational effects. The debate remains active.
