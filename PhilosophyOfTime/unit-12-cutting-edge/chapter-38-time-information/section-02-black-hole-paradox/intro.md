# Section 38.2: The Black Hole Information Paradox

---

## Section Introduction

In 1975, Stephen Hawking showed that black holes are not entirely black: they emit thermal radiation (Hawking radiation) and, if this process continues, eventually evaporate completely. This beautiful result united quantum mechanics and general relativity in a specific, calculable way. But it also created a profound problem: if a black hole evaporates completely, what happens to the information about the matter that fell in?

The black hole information paradox is the deepest current problem at the intersection of quantum mechanics and general relativity. Its resolution — or lack thereof — has implications for the nature of time, the structure of quantum mechanics, and the relationship between them. This section examines the paradox and discusses recent progress toward its resolution.
