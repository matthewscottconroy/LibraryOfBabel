# Section 38.4: Computational Models of Time

---

## Section Introduction

Digital physics is the radical proposal that the universe is fundamentally a computation — that the physical world is equivalent to, or implemented by, a discrete computational process, and that "time" is nothing other than the sequence of computational steps. The idea has been developed in various forms by Konrad Zuse (1969), Edward Fredkin, and Stephen Wolfram (*A New Kind of Science*, 2002).

This view sits at the extreme end of the information-theoretic approach to physics: it identifies not just entropy or information flow but the entire physical world with computational process. Time, on this view, is not a background parameter but a derived concept — the sequential order of computational states.
