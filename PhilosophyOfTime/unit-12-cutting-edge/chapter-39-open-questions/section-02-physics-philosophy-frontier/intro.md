# Section 39.2: Frontiers at the Physics-Philosophy Interface

---

## Section Introduction

The relationship between physics and philosophy of time is not one of simple deference. Physics does not hand philosophers a set of facts that philosophy then organizes and interprets; the relationship is genuinely bidirectional. Philosophy asks the questions that physics needs to answer, and physics generates results that require philosophical interpretation. The frontier of this relationship — where the two disciplines most intensely engage — is where the most exciting work in the philosophy of time is currently being done.

This section surveys three frontier problems: what quantum gravity will tell us about temporal ontology, the cosmological origin of the arrow of time, and the measurement problem as a problem about temporal structure. Each combines technical physics and philosophical analysis in ways that neither discipline alone can resolve.
