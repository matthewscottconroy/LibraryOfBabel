# Section 39.4: Time in Ethics and Social Philosophy

---

## Section Introduction

The philosophy of time is not only a theoretical enterprise; it has practical implications. How we think about time shapes how we think about our obligations — to future generations, to past victims of injustice, to the long-term flourishing of humanity. The ethical dimensions of temporal thinking have become urgent in the context of climate change, historical injustice, and the emergence of longtermism as a significant moral and political movement.

This section surveys three ethical and social dimensions of open temporal questions: obligations to future generations and the philosophy of longtermism; memory, forgiveness, and political time; and temporal justice as a framework for intergenerational institutions. Each topic connects philosophical analysis of time to urgent questions about how individuals and societies should act.
