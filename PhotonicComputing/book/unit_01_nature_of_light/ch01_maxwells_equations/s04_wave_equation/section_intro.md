# Section 1.4: The Electromagnetic Wave Equation

## Orientation

We have spent three sections establishing Maxwell's four equations. Now we harvest the central consequence: Maxwell's equations predict the existence of electromagnetic waves propagating through empty space at a speed determined entirely by the constants $\varepsilon_0$ and $\mu_0$.

This prediction — that the speed of electromagnetic waves equals the measured speed of light — is what Maxwell called the "most satisfactory coincidence in physics" [1]. It is one of the great moments in the history of science: a purely theoretical prediction, derived from equations that were established for entirely different reasons (static charges and steady currents), turns out to predict the nature of light itself.

The derivation we will perform here is rigorous and elementary. It requires only the vector identity $\nabla \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) - \nabla^2 \mathbf{F}$ and the differential form of Maxwell's equations in free space. It is the same calculation Maxwell performed in 1865.
