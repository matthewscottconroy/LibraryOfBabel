# Section 1.5: Energy and Momentum in Electromagnetic Waves

## Orientation

An electromagnetic wave is not just a mathematical pattern — it carries physical energy and momentum through space. This is not a postulate; it is a consequence of Maxwell's equations. The energy and momentum content of light is what makes photonic computing possible: detectors convert photon energy into electrical signals, and radiation pressure can exert forces on nanoscale objects.

This section derives the Poynting vector (the energy flux of an electromagnetic wave), discusses radiation pressure, and briefly introduces the concept of optical angular momentum — both spin angular momentum (from circular polarization) and orbital angular momentum (from vortex beams).

Understanding the energy transport of electromagnetic waves is also essential for quantifying the efficiency of photonic computing systems: the laser power, the modulation energy, and the noise floor of optical detectors all have their foundations here.
