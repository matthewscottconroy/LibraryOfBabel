# Section 4.1: Population Inversion

## Why This Section Exists

Chapter 3 introduced the Einstein A and B coefficients and showed that stimulated emission has the same rate as stimulated absorption when the populations are equal. For light amplification — for a gain medium — we need stimulated emission to exceed stimulated absorption: we need more atoms in the upper level than the lower level. This is population inversion, and it is the non-equilibrium condition that makes the laser possible.

The question this section answers is not just *what* population inversion is — that was covered in Chapter 3 — but *how* to achieve it in a real system, how to maintain it in the presence of competing processes, and how to quantify it through the rate equations that govern gain media. The rate equation framework developed here is the practical tool used to design every laser that has ever been built.

## What You Will Learn

- Why a two-level system cannot sustain population inversion under any cw illumination, and why this is a consequence of detailed balance rather than engineering limitation
- How three- and four-level systems circumvent this constraint by providing fast non-radiative relaxation pathways
- The rate equations for photon number and carrier density, their steady-state solutions, and what they predict for gain, saturation, and threshold
- The specific rate equations for semiconductor lasers, where "population" refers to electron-hole pairs and the gain is a function of carrier density
