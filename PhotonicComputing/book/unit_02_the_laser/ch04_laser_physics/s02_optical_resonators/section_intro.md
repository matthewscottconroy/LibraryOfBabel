# Section 4.2: Optical Resonators

## From Gain Medium to Laser

A gain medium saturated with population inversion is an optical amplifier — but not a laser. To make a laser, you need two additional things: *feedback* (so the amplified light returns to the gain medium for further amplification) and *mode selection* (so the output is predominantly at one or a few frequencies, not a broad, noisy spectrum).

Both are provided by the optical resonator, or cavity. In its simplest form — the Fabry-Pérot cavity of two parallel mirrors — the resonator does three things simultaneously:

1. Provides optical feedback by reflecting light back through the gain medium on every round trip
2. Selects longitudinal modes by imposing the boundary condition that only integer multiples of a half-wavelength fit between the mirrors (standing wave resonance)
3. Selects transverse modes by diffractive losses that favor the lowest-order Gaussian mode

This section develops the resonator physics in detail, connecting it to the Fabry-Pérot analysis of Chapter 2 and extending it to the Gaussian beam properties of real laser cavities.
