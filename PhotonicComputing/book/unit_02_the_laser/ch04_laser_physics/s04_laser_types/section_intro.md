# Section 4.4: Laser Types for Photonic Computing

## Why Laser Architecture Matters

Understanding laser physics in the abstract is necessary but not sufficient for photonic computing. The engineer also needs to know which laser architecture to choose for a given application — and the choices are consequential. A DFB laser and a VCSEL both emit single-mode light near 1550 nm, but they differ in their linewidth, modulation bandwidth, wall-plug efficiency, coupling geometry, temperature sensitivity, and integration compatibility with silicon photonic platforms.

This section surveys the four laser architectures that matter most for photonic computing:

1. **Distributed Feedback (DFB) and Fabry-Pérot lasers**: The workhorses of photonic computing and optical communications. DFBs provide single-frequency output; FP lasers provide multi-mode output.

2. **Vertical-Cavity Surface-Emitting Lasers (VCSELs)**: Ultra-low threshold, high-efficiency, 2D-array-compatible. Used in short-reach datacom and proposed for neuromorphic photonics.

3. **Heterogeneously integrated III-V-on-silicon lasers**: The current state-of-the-art for on-chip laser sources in silicon photonic computing.

4. **Microresonator frequency combs**: The emerging multi-wavelength source for WDM photonic matrix processing.
