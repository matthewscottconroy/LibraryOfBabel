# Section 5.1: Detection Fundamentals

The detection of light is, at its most fundamental, the absorption of a photon and the liberation of an electron. Everything else — bandwidth, noise, sensitivity — is a consequence of how reliably and quickly this absorption happens, and what happens to the liberated electron.

This section establishes the foundational quantities: quantum efficiency (how many absorbed photons per incident photon), responsivity (current per watt of optical power), and bandwidth (how quickly the detector responds to changes in optical power). We derive the bandwidth-speed tradeoff that governs the design of every high-speed photodetector.
