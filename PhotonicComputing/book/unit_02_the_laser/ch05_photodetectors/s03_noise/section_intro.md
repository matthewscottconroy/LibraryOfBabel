# Section 5.3: Noise in Photodetectors

Noise is not an engineering imperfection. It is a physical reality rooted in the discrete nature of matter and energy. In a photodetector, three noise sources compete: the quantum shot noise of photon arrival, the thermal Johnson noise of the detector resistance and load, and the dark current noise from thermally generated carriers. Understanding their relative magnitudes determines the design strategy for every optical receiver.

This section derives each noise source from first principles and assembles them into a complete receiver noise model, culminating in the sensitivity and SNR formulas that govern photonic computing output precision.
