# Section 6.2: Optical Fiber

Optical fiber is the cylindrical generalization of the planar waveguide: a circular core of higher refractive index surrounded by a lower-index cladding. The modal analysis is more complex (Bessel functions instead of sinusoids and exponentials), but the physics is the same: guidance by total internal reflection, discrete modes, cutoff conditions.

What makes optical fiber historically and practically remarkable is not primarily its mode structure, but its achievable loss. Silica fiber at 1550 nm has propagation loss of ~0.18 dB/km — photons can travel 1000 km before half the power is lost. This is not merely impressive engineering; it represents an approach to the thermodynamic limit for photon propagation in a solid dielectric (the remaining loss is dominated by Rayleigh scattering, a fundamental quantum mechanical process).
