# Section 6.3: Nonlinear Effects in Fiber

The nonlinear optics of Chapter 3 was developed in the context of bulk media and microresonators. Optical fiber provides a third platform: a weakly nonlinear medium ($n_2 \approx 2.6 \times 10^{-20}$ m²/W) compensated by the extremely long interaction lengths available (100 km effective lengths in amplified spans). The result is that nonlinear effects that would require kilowatts to observe in a 1 cm crystal become significant at milliwatt signal powers in 50 km fiber.

For photonic computing, fiber nonlinearities are primarily limits: they constrain the maximum launch power per WDM channel, set the minimum channel spacing, and generate crosstalk between channels. This section quantifies those limits.
