# Chapter 9: Further Reading

## Essential Textbooks

**Shannon, C.E. & Weaver, W. (1949). *The Mathematical Theory of Communication*. University of Illinois Press.**
The original Shannon paper with Weaver's introduction. Short (125 pages), accessible, and irreplaceable.

**Cover, T.M. & Thomas, J.A. (2006). *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. Wiley.**
The standard graduate textbook. Chapter 7 covers channel capacity; Chapter 10 covers rate-distortion theory. Rigorous and complete.

**Agrawal, G.P. (2012). *Fiber-Optic Communication Systems*, 5th ed. Wiley.**
The standard reference for optical communications engineering. Chapters 7–9 cover WDM system design; Chapter 10 covers coherent modulation.

## Primary Literature

**Essiambre, R.-J., Kramer, G., Winzer, P.J., Foschini, G.J., & Goebel, B. (2010). "Capacity limits of optical fiber networks." *Journal of Lightwave Technology*, 28(4), 662–701.**
The definitive treatment of Shannon limits for optical fiber including nonlinear noise.

**Gallager, R.G. (1962). "Low-density parity-check codes." *IRE Transactions on Information Theory*, 8(1), 21–28.**
The foundational LDPC paper.

**Arıkan, E. (2009). "Channel polarization: A method for constructing capacity-achieving codes." *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*, 55(7), 3051–3073.**
The polar codes paper.

**Bocherer, G., Steiner, F., & Schulte, P. (2015). "Bandwidth efficient and rate-matched low-density parity-check coded modulation." *IEEE Transactions on Communications*, 63(12), 4651–4665.**
Probabilistic constellation shaping — the technique that brings DP-64QAM within 2 dB of Shannon capacity.
