# Chapter 9: Important Researchers

## Claude Shannon (1916–2001)

Claude Shannon is, without qualification, the founder of information theory. His 1948 paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication" defined entropy, mutual information, channel capacity, and the channel coding theorem in a single paper — arguably the most impactful single paper in engineering history. Shannon worked at Bell Labs for most of his career, and his theoretical frameworks underlie every digital communication system ever built. He also made foundational contributions to cryptography (Shannon's maxim: "the enemy knows the system") and to the theory of computing (he was among the first to analyze Boolean algebra as a framework for circuit design). Shannon was famously reluctant to accept honors and is said to have ridden a unicycle through the Bell Labs hallways while juggling.

## Robert Gallager (1931–)

Robert Gallager invented LDPC codes in his 1960 MIT PhD thesis, which was largely ignored for 35 years until rediscovered in the turbo codes era. When republished and implemented in the mid-1990s, LDPC codes (and their close relative, turbo codes) were found to approach Shannon capacity within a fraction of a dB — a stunning result. Gallager's thesis is one of the most influential unpublished works in engineering history. He also wrote the textbook *Information Theory and Reliable Communication* (1968), which defined the field's pedagogy.

## Erdal Arıkan (1958–)

Erdal Arıkan at Bilkent University (Turkey) invented polar codes in 2009, the first provably capacity-achieving codes with an explicit, polynomial-time construction. Polar codes were selected for the 5G NR standard for control channels and are now deployed in billions of devices. Arıkan received the Shannon Award (2020), the highest honor in information theory.

## René-Jean Essiambre

René-Jean Essiambre at Nokia Bell Labs is the leading researcher on the capacity limits of optical fiber communications. His 2010 paper "Capacity Limits of Optical Fiber Networks" (*Journal of Lightwave Technology*) provided the first comprehensive analysis of the nonlinear Shannon limit for fiber, accounting for the Kerr nonlinearity, distributed amplifier noise, and WDM channel interactions. This paper redefined how the optical communications community thinks about system design margins.

## Peter Winzer

Peter Winzer (Nokia Bell Labs, later HKUST) is the leading experimentalist in high-capacity optical communications, holding multiple world records in fiber capacity demonstrations. His work on spectral efficiency, modulation formats, and space-division multiplexing has pushed the boundaries of what optical fiber can carry. His review paper "Fiber-Optic Transmission and Networking" (2018) is a comprehensive map of the field.
