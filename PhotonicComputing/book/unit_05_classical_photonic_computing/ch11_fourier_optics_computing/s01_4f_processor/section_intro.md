# Section 11.1: The 4f Optical Processor

## What This Section Is About

This section derives and analyzes the 4f optical processing system — the fundamental architecture of classical optical analog computation. The derivation begins from the Fraunhofer diffraction integral (Chapter 2), shows rigorously why a thin lens computes a Fourier transform, and builds up the 4f system as a cascade of two Fourier transforms. We then examine spatial filtering (the mathematical manipulation of the Fourier transform at the Fourier plane) and the optical correlator.

The treatment here is self-contained given the wave optics of Chapter 2, but connects explicitly to the photonic computing applications in Chapters 12–14: the 4f system is the free-space analog of what MZI meshes and diffractive layers attempt to do in guided-wave or near-field form.

Three subsections:

**11.1.1: Mathematical Foundation** — Lens Fourier transform property; 4f system; isoplanatic approximation; space-bandwidth product.

**11.1.2: Spatial Filtering** — Low-pass, high-pass, and matched filters; the vander Lugt filter; coherent vs. incoherent processing; the phase problem.

**11.1.3: Optical Correlator** — Cross-correlation as matched filtering; Joint Transform Correlator; performance comparison to digital FFT; genuine applications (LADAR, fingerprint matching).
