# Section 17.1: The Postulates of Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics, stripped to its skeleton, is four postulates: states are vectors in a Hilbert space; observables are Hermitian operators; measurement outcomes are governed by the Born rule and change the state; closed systems evolve unitarily. Everything else — interference, entanglement, uncertainty, the no-cloning theorem — is a consequence.

This section develops the postulates with photonic examples throughout. The polarization of a single photon serves as our standing model of a two-level system (a qubit): it is physically real, experimentally accessible with the wave plates and polarizers of Chapter 2, and it is one of the standard encodings for photonic quantum information. Alongside pure states we introduce density matrices from the start, because real photonic experiments — with imperfect sources, lossy channels, and partial detection — live in the world of mixed states.
