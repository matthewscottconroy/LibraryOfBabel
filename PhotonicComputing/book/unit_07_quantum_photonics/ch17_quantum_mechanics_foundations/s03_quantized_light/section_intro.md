# Section 17.3: Quantization of the Electromagnetic Field

This section delivers on the promise of Section 17.2: the electromagnetic field, expanded into modes, is an infinite collection of harmonic oscillators, and quantizing each oscillator quantizes light. The photon emerges not as a postulated particle but as an *excitation of a mode* — one rung on one ladder — and the ladder operators of the previous section become the photon creation and annihilation operators of quantum optics.

With the field quantized, we can finally ask the right question about familiar light sources: *which quantum state* does a laser produce? Not a Fock state. The answer is the coherent state — the eigenstate of the annihilation operator, with Poissonian photon statistics and minimum, symmetric quantum noise. Deforming that noise gives the squeezed states, quieter than vacuum in one quadrature at the lawful price of extra noise in the other. And to see all of these states at a glance, we introduce the Wigner function — a quasi-probability portrait in phase space whose negative regions are the definitive fingerprint of non-classical light.

The four state families met here — vacuum, Fock, coherent, squeezed — are the complete cast of principal characters for Chapters 18 through 22.
