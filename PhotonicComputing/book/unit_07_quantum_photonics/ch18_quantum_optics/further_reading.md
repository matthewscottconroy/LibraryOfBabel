# Chapter 18: Further Reading and References

---

## Reviews and Foundational Theory

**Glauber, R.J. (1963). "The quantum theory of optical coherence." *Physical Review*, 130(6), 2529–2539.**
The paper that defined the normally ordered coherence functions $g^{(n)}$ organizing this whole chapter, and drew the line between classical and quantum light. Its companion, **Glauber, R.J. (1963). "Coherent and incoherent states of the radiation field." *Physical Review*, 131(6), 2766–2788**, introduces the coherent states and the $P$ representation. Together they earned the 2005 Nobel Prize and remain the conceptual bedrock of Sections 18.1–18.3.

**Mandel, L. & Wolf, E. (1995). *Optical Coherence and Quantum Optics*. Cambridge University Press.**
The definitive reference on coherence theory and photon statistics, by two of the field's architects. Encyclopedic on $g^{(1)}$, $g^{(2)}$, the Mandel $Q$ parameter, and the classical-inequality bounds of Section 18.1.1.

---

## Primary Literature: Photon Statistics and Antibunching

**Hanbury Brown, R. & Twiss, R.Q. (1956). "Correlation between photons in two coherent beams of light." *Nature*, 177(4497), 27–29.**
The laboratory demonstration of photon bunching in thermal light — the founding experiment of intensity interferometry (Section 18.1.2). The companion **"A test of a new type of stellar interferometer on Sirius," *Nature*, 178(4541), 1046–1048 (1956)** reports the stellar-diameter measurement that motivated it.

**Kimble, H.J., Dagenais, M., & Mandel, L. (1977). "Photon antibunching in resonance fluorescence." *Physical Review Letters*, 39(11), 691–695.**
The first observation of antibunching, $g^{(2)}(0)<1$ — light shown to be more orderly than a laser, and the birth of single-emitter quantum optics (Section 18.1.3).

**Grangier, P., Roger, G., & Aspect, A. (1986). "Experimental evidence for a photon anticorrelation effect on a beam splitter: a new light on single-photon interferences." *Europhysics Letters*, 1(4), 173–179.**
A single photon on a beam splitter, shown never to trigger both detectors: the cleanest demonstration of the indivisibility of the photon, with the anticorrelation parameter driven well below the classical bound (Section 18.1.3).

---

## Primary Literature: Two-Photon Interference

**Hong, C.K., Ou, Z.Y., & Mandel, L. (1987). "Measurement of subpicosecond time intervals between two photons by interference." *Physical Review Letters*, 59(18), 2044–2046.**
The Hong-Ou-Mandel effect: two indistinguishable photons bunching at a beam splitter, the coincidence dip, and its use to measure femtosecond-scale delays. The single most cited two-photon result in quantum optics and the primitive of Chapter 20 (Section 18.2).

---

## Primary Literature: Squeezing and Parametric Processes

**Slusher, R.E., Hollberg, L.W., Yurke, B., Mertz, J.C., & Valley, J.F. (1985). "Observation of squeezed states generated by four-wave mixing in an optical cavity." *Physical Review Letters*, 55(22), 2409–2412.**
The first observation of squeezed light, via four-wave mixing in atomic sodium (Section 18.3.2).

**Wu, L.-A., Kimble, H.J., Hall, J.L., & Wu, H. (1986). "Generation of squeezed states by parametric down conversion." *Physical Review Letters*, 57(20), 2520–2523.**
The first squeezing from a sub-threshold optical parametric oscillator — the platform that leads to today's records (Section 18.3.2).

**Kwiat, P.G., Mattle, K., Weinfurter, H., Zeilinger, A., Sergienko, A.V., & Shih, Y. (1995). "New high-intensity source of polarization-entangled photon pairs." *Physical Review Letters*, 75(24), 4337–4341.**
Type-II SPDC as a bright, ready-to-use source of polarization-entangled Bell pairs — the workhorse of three decades of quantum optics (Section 18.3.1).

**Vahlbruch, H., Mehmet, M., Danzmann, K., & Schnabel, R. (2016). "Detection of 15 dB squeezed states of light and their application for the absolute calibration of photoelectric quantum efficiency." *Physical Review Letters*, 117(11), 110801.**
The current squeezing record, $15$ dB at $1064$ nm — a decade-long campaign against optical loss (Section 18.3.2).

**Furusawa, A., Sørensen, J.L., Braunstein, S.L., Fuchs, C.A., Kimble, H.J., & Polzik, E.S. (1998). "Unconditional quantum teleportation." *Science*, 282(5389), 706–709.**
Continuous-variable teleportation using two-mode squeezed light as the entangled resource — the CV bridge from Section 18.3.1 to Chapter 21.

## Primary Literature: Squeezed Light in Metrology

**Caves, C.M. (1981). "Quantum-mechanical noise in an interferometer." *Physical Review D*, 23(8), 1693–1708.**
The proposal that squeezed vacuum injected into an interferometer's dark port beats the shot-noise limit — the theoretical seed of squeezed-light gravitational-wave detection (Section 18.3.3).

**Aasi, J., et al. (LIGO Scientific Collaboration) (2013). "Enhanced sensitivity of the LIGO gravitational wave detector by using squeezed states of light." *Nature Photonics*, 7(8), 613–619.**
Squeezing injected into a LIGO interferometer, demonstrating sub-shot-noise sensitivity in a full-scale detector (Section 18.3.3).

**Tse, M., et al. (LIGO Scientific Collaboration) (2019). "Quantum-enhanced Advanced LIGO detectors in the era of gravitational-wave astronomy." *Physical Review Letters*, 123(23), 231107.**
Permanent deployment of squeezing across both Advanced LIGO detectors, with the $\sim15\%$ range and $40$–$50\%$ detection-rate gains quoted in Section 18.3.3.

**Abbott, B.P., et al. (LIGO Scientific Collaboration and Virgo Collaboration) (2016). "Observation of gravitational waves from a binary black hole merger." *Physical Review Letters*, 116(6), 061102.**
GW150914, the first direct detection of gravitational waves — the science that squeezed light now enhances (Section 18.3.3).

## Textbooks

**Loudon, R. (2000). *The Quantum Theory of Light*, 3rd ed. Oxford University Press.**
The standard graduate text on quantized light, photon statistics, and coherence — the closest single-book companion to this chapter.

**Gerry, C.C. & Knight, P.L. (2005). *Introductory Quantum Optics*. Cambridge University Press.**
The gentlest rigorous path from the postulates to beam splitters, coherent and squeezed states, and parametric processes (Sections 18.2–18.3).

**Walls, D.F. & Milburn, G.J. (2008). *Quantum Optics*, 2nd ed. Springer.**
Concise and authoritative on squeezing, parametric amplification, and quantum noise in interferometry — strong on Section 18.3.

**Fox, M. (2006). *Quantum Optics: An Introduction*. Oxford University Press.**
An accessible treatment of photon statistics, HBT, antibunching, and single-photon interference, at the level of Section 18.1.

**Mandel, L. & Wolf, E. (1995). *Optical Coherence and Quantum Optics*. Cambridge University Press.**
Listed again for completeness: the deepest reference on the coherence-function formalism that underpins the entire chapter.
