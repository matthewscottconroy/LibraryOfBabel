# Section 18.1: Photon Statistics and Non-Classical Light

A power meter reports a mean; it says nothing about how the energy is parcelled in time. Yet the *arrival statistics* of photons — bunched, random, or antibunched — carry the sharpest available signature of a light field's quantum character. Two sources can share a wavelength, a spectrum, an intensity, and even a first-order coherence function, and still be told apart the instant one measures whether their photons like to arrive together.

This section builds the tool that reads those statistics: the second-order coherence function $g^{(2)}(\tau)$, and its companion the Mandel $Q$ parameter. We compute their values for the states that matter — coherent light sits at $g^{(2)}(0)=1$, thermal light bunches at $2$, and a single-photon Fock state antibunches to $0$ — and we prove the classical bound $g^{(2)}(0)\ge 1$ that antibunched light violates. That violation is the whole point: $g^{(2)}(0)<1$ is a theorem-protected certificate of non-classicality, the single number by which every single-photon source in Chapter 19 is graded.

We then reconstruct the experiment that made intensity correlations measurable and, in doing so, founded quantum optics as a distinct field.
