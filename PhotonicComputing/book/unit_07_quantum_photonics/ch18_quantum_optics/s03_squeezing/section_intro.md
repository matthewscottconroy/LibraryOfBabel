# Section 18.3: Optical Parametric Processes and Squeezing

Photon statistics and interference (Sections 18.1–18.2) work with photons already in hand. This section builds the machine that *makes* non-classical light from nothing but a laser and a crystal. The mechanism is the $\chi^{(2)}$ parametric interaction: a pump photon splits into two, and the very fact that photons are created *in pairs* is the source of everything that follows.

Split the pair between two modes and you get **two-mode squeezing** — spontaneous parametric down-conversion (18.3.1), the workhorse source of entangled photon pairs and heralded single photons that feeds Chapters 19 through 22. Split the pair within a *single* mode and you get **single-mode squeezing** (18.3.2): light quieter than vacuum in one quadrature, the continuous-variable resource whose decibel scale and merciless loss-sensitivity we quantify here. Both are the squeezing operators of Section 17.3.3, now realized in a nonlinear crystal rather than written on paper.

We close with the payoff that reaches beyond computing: squeezed vacuum injected into the dark port of a kilometer-scale interferometer (18.3.3), the technique by which LIGO now hears gravitational waves below the standard quantum limit. It is the clearest proof in this book that quantum optics is not only a computing substrate but a measurement technology already changing what humanity can observe.
