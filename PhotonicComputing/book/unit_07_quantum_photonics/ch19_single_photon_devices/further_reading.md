# Chapter 19: Further Reading and References

## Reviews: Start Here

**Hadfield, R.H. (2009). "Single-photon detectors for optical quantum information applications." *Nature Photonics*, 3(12), 696–705.**
The canonical review of the single-photon detector landscape — SPADs, PMTs, TESs, SNSPDs — with the figure-of-merit framework (efficiency, dark counts, jitter, dead time) used throughout this chapter. Slightly dated on SNSPD numbers (the field moved fast after 2009), but the conceptual organization has not been bettered.

**Eisaman, M.D., Fan, J., Migdall, A., & Polyakov, S.V. (2011). "Invited Review Article: Single-photon sources and detectors." *Review of Scientific Instruments*, 82(7), 071101.**
The companion review covering both halves of this chapter, with careful attention to measurement methodology — how $g^{(2)}(0)$, heralding efficiency, and detector metrics are actually calibrated.

**Senellart, P., Solomon, G., & White, A. (2017). "High-performance semiconductor quantum-dot single-photon sources." *Nature Nanotechnology*, 12(11), 1026–1039.**
The definitive review of cavity-coupled quantum dot sources: figures of merit, resonant excitation, Purcell engineering, and the state of the art as of the "near-optimal source" era.

**Aharonovich, I., Englund, D., & Toth, M. (2016). "Solid-state single-photon emitters." *Nature Photonics*, 10(10), 631–641.**
Broad survey across quantum dots, diamond color centers, SiC, and 2D materials — the source-platform table of Section 19.1.1 in long form.

**Natarajan, C.M., Tanner, M.G., & Hadfield, R.H. (2012). "Superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors: physics and applications." *Superconductor Science and Technology*, 25(6), 063001.**
The standard technical review of SNSPD device physics: detection mechanism, materials, kinetic inductance, latching, and early integration work.

**Reiserer, A. & Rempe, G. (2015). "Cavity-based quantum networks with single atoms and optical photons." *Reviews of Modern Physics*, 87(4), 1379–1418.**
Authoritative treatment of atomic cavity QED as a device platform — strong coupling, photon generation, atom-photon gates — bridging Section 19.3 to Chapter 22.

## Primary Literature: Sources

**Michler, P., Kiraz, A., Becher, C., Schoenfeld, W.V., Petroff, P.M., Zhang, L., Hu, E., & Imamoğlu, A. (2000). "A quantum dot single-photon turnstile device." *Science*, 290(5500), 2282–2285.**
The first triggered single-photon source based on a quantum dot — the founding experiment of the field.

**Santori, C., Fattal, D., Vučković, J., Solomon, G.S., & Yamamoto, Y. (2002). "Indistinguishable photons from a single-photon device." *Nature*, 419(6907), 594–597.**
Demonstrated HOM interference between successive photons from one quantum dot — the result that made QD sources relevant to quantum computing rather than merely to quantum optics.

**Somaschi, N., Giesz, V., De Santis, L., et al. (2016). "Near-optimal single-photon sources in the solid state." *Nature Photonics*, 10(5), 340–345.**
Electrically tuned QD-micropillar devices achieving high purity, >0.98 indistinguishability, and high brightness simultaneously — the paper that named the modern performance standard.

**Ding, X., He, Y., Duan, Z.-C., et al. (2016). "On-demand single photons with high extraction efficiency and near-unity indistinguishability from a resonantly driven quantum dot in a micropillar." *Physical Review Letters*, 116(2), 020401.**
The USTC counterpart to Somaschi et al. — the source lineage that fed the multiphoton boson sampling program of Chapter 20.

**Tomm, N., Javadi, A., Antoniadis, N.O., et al. (2021). "A bright and fast source of coherent single photons." *Nature Nanotechnology*, 16(4), 399–403.**
A gated quantum dot in an open microcavity delivering 57% end-to-end efficiency into a single-mode fiber — the delivered-brightness record discussed in Section 19.1.2.

**Schweickert, L., Jöns, K.D., Zeuner, K.D., et al. (2018). "On-demand generation of background-free single photons from a solid-state source." *Applied Physics Letters*, 112(9), 093106.**
$g^{(2)}(0) = 7.5\times10^{-5}$: how pure a solid-state single-photon stream can be.

**Kwiat, P.G., Mattle, K., Weinfurter, H., Zeilinger, A., Sergienko, A.V., & Shih, Y. (1995). "New high-intensity source of polarization-entangled photon pairs." *Physical Review Letters*, 75(24), 4337–4341.**
Type-II SPDC as an entangled-pair source — the workhorse configuration for three decades of quantum optics experiments.

**Bhaskar, M.K., Riedinger, R., Machielse, B., et al. (2020). "Experimental demonstration of memory-enhanced quantum communication." *Nature*, 580(7801), 60–64.**
A cavity-coupled SiV center in diamond as a heralded quantum memory — the color-center value proposition (Section 19.1.3) realized.

## Primary Literature: Detectors

**Gol'tsman, G.N., Okunev, O., Chulkova, G., et al. (2001). "Picosecond superconducting single-photon optical detector." *Applied Physics Letters*, 79(6), 705–707.**
The first SNSPD.

**Marsili, F., Verma, V.B., Stern, J.A., et al. (2013). "Detecting single infrared photons with 93% system efficiency." *Nature Photonics*, 7(3), 210–214.**
Amorphous WSi nanowires plus optical-cavity integration — the paper that made >90% SDE routine and established amorphous materials' wide internal-efficiency plateau.

**Reddy, D.V., Nerem, R.R., Nam, S.W., Mirin, R.P., & Verma, V.B. (2020). "Superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors with 98% system detection efficiency at 1550 nm." *Optica*, 7(12), 1649–1653.**
The efficiency benchmark quoted throughout this chapter.

**Korzh, B., Zhao, Q.-Y., Allmaras, J.P., et al. (2020). "Demonstration of sub-3 ps temporal resolution with a superconducting nanowire single-photon detector." *Nature Photonics*, 14(4), 250–255.**
The timing-jitter record, from short straight nanowires with cryogenic readout.

**Pernice, W.H.P., Schuck, C., Minaeva, O., et al. (2012). "High-speed and high-efficiency travelling wave single-photon detectors embedded in nanophotonic circuits." *Nature Communications*, 3, 1325.**
The canonical waveguide-integrated SNSPD: 91% on-chip efficiency and the traveling-wave absorption geometry of Section 19.2.3.

**Lita, A.E., Miller, A.J., & Nam, S.W. (2008). "Counting near-infrared single-photons with 95% efficiency." *Optics Express*, 16(5), 3032–3040.**
The transition-edge sensor as a near-unity-efficiency, photon-number-resolving detector.

**Oripov, B.G., Rampini, D.S., Allmaras, J., et al. (2023). "A superconducting nanowire single-photon camera with 400,000 pixels." *Nature*, 622(7984), 730–734.**
Evidence that SNSPD technology scales to large arrays — the channel-count question of fault-tolerant photonics.

## Primary Literature: Cavity QED

**Purcell, E.M. (1946). "Spontaneous emission probabilities at radio frequencies." *Physical Review*, 69, 681.**
The famous one-paragraph abstract (B10, APS Spring Meeting) in which cavity-modified spontaneous emission was proposed. Possibly the highest citations-per-word document in physics.

**Jaynes, E.T. & Cummings, F.W. (1963). "Comparison of quantum and semiclassical radiation theories with application to the beam maser." *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 51(1), 89–109.**
The original Jaynes-Cummings paper.

**Thompson, R.J., Rempe, G., & Kimble, H.J. (1992). "Observation of normal-mode splitting for an atom in an optical cavity." *Physical Review Letters*, 68(8), 1132–1135.**
Vacuum Rabi splitting with (on average) a single atom — strong coupling reached in optical cavity QED.

**Yoshie, T., Scherer, A., Hendrickson, J., et al. (2004). "Vacuum Rabi splitting with a single quantum dot in a photonic crystal nanocavity." *Nature*, 432(7014), 200–203.**
**Reithmaier, J.P., Sęk, G., Löffler, A., et al. (2004). "Strong coupling in a single quantum dot–semiconductor microcavity system." *Nature*, 432(7014), 197–200.**
The simultaneous demonstrations of solid-state strong coupling.

**Birnbaum, K.M., Boca, A., Miller, R., Boozer, A.D., Northup, T.E., & Kimble, H.J. (2005). "Photon blockade in an optical cavity with one trapped atom." *Nature*, 436(7047), 87–90.**
One atom makes light antibunch: the single-photon nonlinearity of Section 19.3.2.

**Hacker, B., Welte, S., Rempe, G., & Ritter, S. (2016). "A photon–photon quantum gate based on a single atom in an optical resonator." *Nature*, 536(7615), 193–196.**
The deterministic photon-photon gate — cavity QED's answer to the CNOT problem that Chapter 20 solves probabilistically.

## Textbooks

**Fox, M. (2006). *Quantum Optics: An Introduction*. Oxford University Press.**
The gentlest rigorous path through photon statistics, single-photon sources, and cavity QED (Chapters 5–10 map well onto this chapter).

**Haroche, S. & Raimond, J.-M. (2006). *Exploring the Quantum: Atoms, Cavities, and Photons*. Oxford University Press.**
The masterwork on cavity QED by its Nobel-winning practitioners; Chapters 3 and 5 cover the Jaynes-Cummings physics of Section 19.3 in full depth.
