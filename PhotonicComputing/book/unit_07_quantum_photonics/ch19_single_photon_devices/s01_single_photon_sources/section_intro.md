# Section 19.1: Single-Photon Sources

An ideal single-photon source emits exactly one photon, on demand, into a single well-defined optical mode, with every photon identical to the last. No physical source achieves all of this at once. This section defines the figures of merit that quantify how close a real source comes to the ideal, then examines the three device families that dominate the field: semiconductor quantum dots (the brightest and purest on-demand emitters), color centers in diamond and 2D materials (spin-photon interfaces for quantum networks), and heralded spontaneous parametric down-conversion (the workhorse probabilistic source, made quasi-deterministic by multiplexing).

The recurring theme is trade-off: brightness fights purity, purity fights indistinguishability, and every scheme pays for its strengths somewhere else. Understanding these trade-offs quantitatively is what allows an architect to choose the right source for a given quantum photonic system.
