# Section 19.3: Cavity Quantum Electrodynamics

Cavity quantum electrodynamics (cavity QED) is the physics of a single quantum emitter exchanging energy with a single mode of the electromagnetic field. It is the theoretical backbone of this chapter: the Purcell effect explains why a quantum dot in a micropillar is a bright source, the strong-coupling regime explains how a single atom can impose a nonlinearity on light that linear optics cannot, and the Jaynes-Cummings model provides the exactly solvable Hamiltonian underlying both.

The section builds from model to regimes to application: first the Jaynes-Cummings Hamiltonian and its dressed-state ladder, then the competition between coherent coupling $g$ and the dissipation rates $\kappa$ (cavity) and $\gamma$ (emitter) that defines strong coupling and photon blockade, and finally the weak-coupling Purcell regime — less glamorous than strong coupling, but the regime in which every high-performance single-photon source actually operates.
