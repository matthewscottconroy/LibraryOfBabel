# 20.1.3 The CNOT Problem

## What an Entangling Gate Requires

Universal quantum computing needs, beyond SU(2), one entangling two-qubit gate. Take the controlled-Z (CZ), equivalent to CNOT up to Hadamards:

$$CZ = \mathrm{diag}(1, 1, 1, -1) \quad \text{in the basis } \{|00\rangle_L, |01\rangle_L, |10\rangle_L, |11\rangle_L\}.$$

One basis state — $|11\rangle_L$ — must acquire a $\pi$ phase while the other three are untouched. In dual-rail terms (modes $a_0, a_1, b_0, b_1$), the state must change *only when one photon occupies $a_1$ **and** another occupies $b_1$*. The transformation is conditional on the *joint* photon configuration: this is an interaction, by definition.

## Why Linear Optics Cannot Do It

A linear optical network — any arrangement of beam splitters and phase shifters over $N$ modes — acts on creation operators as $\hat{a}_i^\dagger \to \sum_j U_{ji}\hat{a}_j^\dagger$. Apply this to a two-photon input:

$$\hat{a}_i^\dagger \hat{a}_k^\dagger|\text{vac}\rangle \ \to\ \left(\sum_j U_{ji}\hat{a}_j^\dagger\right)\left(\sum_l U_{lk}\hat{a}_l^\dagger\right)|\text{vac}\rangle.$$

Each photon is transformed by the *same* single-particle matrix $U$, independent of the other's presence. The generator of any such network is a quadratic Hamiltonian, $\hat{H} = \sum_{ij}h_{ij}\hat{a}_i^\dagger\hat{a}_j$ — photon-number-conserving hopping terms, nothing more. A CZ on dual-rail qubits requires the two-photon amplitude $|11\rangle_L$ to evolve *differently* from the products of single-photon evolutions, which demands a quartic term in the Hamiltonian:

$$\hat{H}_{Kerr} = \hbar\chi\,\hat{n}_{a_1}\hat{n}_{b_1}$$

— a cross-Kerr nonlinearity, with the interaction strong enough that a *single photon* imposes phase $\chi t = \pi$ on another. No composition of quadratic evolutions generates a quartic one. Within strict linear optics (unitary mode transformations, no measurement), the no-go is exact: two-qubit entangling gates on dual-rail qubits are impossible, deterministically or otherwise.

**Can't we just use a nonlinear crystal?** Do the numbers. The single-photon Kerr phase in a standard silica fiber is $\phi \sim n_2\hbar\omega^2 L/(c\,A_{eff}\,\tau)$ — of order $10^{-8}$ rad for realistic parameters, about eight orders of magnitude short of $\pi$, and attempts to concentrate the interaction (smaller $A_{eff}$, resonators, slow light) drag in loss and multimode distortions faster than they add phase. Worse, Shapiro's analysis (2006) showed that *causal, multimode* cross-Kerr interactions add phase noise that spoils gate fidelity even if the magnitude problem were solved. Single-photon-strength Kerr nonlinearity exists — it is the photon blockade physics of Section 19.3.2, an atom in a high-cooperativity cavity — but at the cost of a cavity-QED system per gate. The mainstream of photonic quantum computing chose a different path.

## The Loophole: Interference Plus Measurement

Linear optics has one genuinely two-photon phenomenon: **Hong-Ou-Mandel interference** (Chapter 18). Two indistinguishable photons entering a 50/50 splitter bunch,

$$\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{b}^\dagger|\text{vac}\rangle \ \to\ \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{(\hat{c}^\dagger)^2 + (\hat{d}^\dagger)^2}{\sqrt{2}}\right)|\text{vac}\rangle,$$

the coincidence amplitude cancelling exactly. No interaction occurred — the effect is bosonic exchange statistics — yet the *output state* depends jointly on both photons. Exchange statistics is a resource that acts only on multi-photon amplitudes.

By itself, HOM interference is not a gate: the evolution is still linear, and the bunched amplitudes ($|20\rangle$, $|02\rangle$) have *left the dual-rail code space*. The escape — the KLM insight — is to add the one nonlinear operation quantum optics has always possessed: **photodetection**. A projective measurement is not generated by any Hamiltonian; it discards amplitudes and renormalizes. The scheme:

1. Mix the computational photons with **ancilla photons** in a linear network, letting exchange statistics entangle the joint amplitudes;
2. **Measure the ancilla modes**;
3. **Condition** on a particular detector pattern (heralding), projecting the computational modes onto a transformed state;
4. **Correct or repeat** based on the outcome (feed-forward).

Conditioned on the herald, the surviving computational state has undergone a nonlinear transformation — including, with the right network, exactly the $|11\rangle_L \to -|11\rangle_L$ phase of a CZ. The price is that the desired pattern occurs only with some probability $p < 1$: the gate is **nondeterministic**. The engineering question of the next section is whether nondeterministic gates can be composed into deterministic computation without exponential overhead. KLM's answer — yes — created this field.

## Three Roads from Here

| Approach | Mechanism | Cost | Where covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deterministic nonlinearity | Cavity QED photon blockade; atom-mediated CZ | One high-cooperativity emitter per gate | §19.3.2 |
| Measurement-induced gates | Ancillas + interference + heralding (KLM) | Probabilistic; ancilla factories, feed-forward | §20.2 |
| Measurement-based computing | Pre-built cluster state consumed by measurements | Offline probabilistic state generation; fusion networks | §20.3 |

The second and third roads are the same physics packaged differently — the third moves all nondeterminism offline, and it is the one that scaled.
