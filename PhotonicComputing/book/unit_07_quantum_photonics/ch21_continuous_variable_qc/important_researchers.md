# Important Researchers — Chapter 21: Continuous-Variable and Xanadu's Quantum Computing

## Samuel L. Braunstein

An Australian-born theorist (long at the University of York), Braunstein co-founded CV quantum information twice over: with H.J. Kimble he devised the CV quantum teleportation protocol whose 1998 realization was the first *unconditional* teleportation experiment, and with Seth Lloyd he wrote the 1999 paper defining universal quantum computation over continuous variables. His 2005 *Reviews of Modern Physics* article with Peter van Loock organized the young field's scattered results into a coherent discipline and remains a standard entry point. Braunstein's recurring theme is that the Gaussian toolbox of quantum optics — squeezers, beam splitters, homodyne detectors — is not merely an analog curiosity but a full-blooded quantum information platform.

## Seth Lloyd (b. 1960)

An MIT professor of mechanical engineering (self-described "quantum mechanic"), Lloyd is among the most inventive theorists in quantum information: early proposals for quantum simulation, quantum machine learning algorithms, and — with Braunstein — the foundational CV computing result that quadratic Hamiltonians generate only the (classically simulable) Gaussian group, while a single cubic interaction unlocks universality. That one algebraic observation still defines the CV field's architecture: every CV machine is a Gaussian machine plus a carefully rationed non-Gaussian resource. Lloyd's later work with Weedbrook on quantum generative adversarial learning seeded the CV quantum machine-learning program.

## Daniel Gottesman (b. 1970), Alexei Kitaev (b. 1963), and John Preskill (b. 1953)

The 2001 GKP paper united three of the architects of quantum fault tolerance: Gottesman (creator of the stabilizer formalism), Kitaev (topological codes, the toric code, Majorana proposals), and Preskill (Caltech; who later coined "NISQ" and framed the quantum-advantage era). Their qubit-in-an-oscillator code looked exotic for two decades — it demanded squeezing and non-Gaussian control nobody had — and then became the load-bearing element of photonic and superconducting bosonic-code roadmaps alike. GKP is a model case of theory built twenty years ahead of its hardware.

## Akira Furusawa (b. 1961)

University of Tokyo experimentalist whose group has repeatedly held the frontier of optical CV experiments: first unconditional quantum teleportation (1998, at Caltech with Kimble), deterministic teleportation-based gates, the one-million-mode time-multiplexed cluster state (2016), the two-dimensional cluster state (2019, with Asavanant), and the first GKP-like logical states of propagating light (2024, with Konno). Furusawa's sustained argument — that time-domain multiplexing makes CV optics the most scalable entanglement technology in existence — is now embedded in every photonic quantum-computing roadmap, including Xanadu's.

## Nicolas Menicucci

RMIT University (Melbourne) theorist who did more than anyone to make CV cluster-state computing rigorous: co-author of the 2006 proposal for universal computation with CV cluster states, inventor (with collaborators) of the temporal-mode cluster constructions the megamode experiments implement, and author of the 2014 proof that fault tolerance is possible with *finite* squeezing (threshold 20.5 dB) once GKP encoding is used. His later work with Baragiola, Pantaleoni, and others showed GKP states make Gaussian operations universal ("all-Gaussian universality"), collapsing the non-Gaussian burden of CV computing into state preparation.

## Christian Weedbrook

Australian theorist turned entrepreneur: first author of the 2012 "Gaussian quantum information" *Reviews of Modern Physics* article, then founder and CEO of Xanadu Quantum Technologies (Toronto, 2016). Weedbrook's bet — that squeezed light on silicon nitride chips, not single photons, is the industrializable path to photonic quantum computing — produced the X-series chips, Borealis's 2022 programmable quantum advantage, the PennyLane/Strawberry Fields software ecosystem, and the 2025 modular Aurora system. Xanadu under Weedbrook is the clearest institutional embodiment of this chapter's ideas.

## Nathan Killoran

Head of Xanadu's software and algorithms effort and the architect of its most influential exports: lead author on Strawberry Fields (2019) and on the CV quantum neural network architecture (2019), and a driving force behind PennyLane and the parameter-shift gradient techniques (with Maria Schuld and Ville Bergholm) that made "differentiable quantum programming" a standard concept across all hardware platforms — arguably Xanadu's broadest impact on quantum computing to date.

## Roman Schnabel

Universität Hamburg experimentalist and squeezed-light record holder: his group's 15 dB squeezing (2016, with Henning Vahlbruch) remains the benchmark, and his earlier squeezers were installed in the GEO 600 gravitational-wave detector in 2010 — the first permanent deployment of a quantum-enhanced measurement technology — with Advanced LIGO following in 2019. Schnabel's career demonstrates the through-line from fundamental quantum optics to both precision measurement and the squeezing budgets of CV quantum computing.
