# Section 21.1: Continuous-Variable Quantum Information

Discrete-variable photonic quantum computing (Chapter 20) encodes information in *which mode* a photon occupies. Continuous-variable (CV) quantum information encodes it in the *field amplitudes themselves*: the position-like and momentum-like quadratures $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{p}$ of each optical mode. Because a mode of the electromagnetic field is mathematically a quantum harmonic oscillator, every mode carries an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, and the natural observables are continuous rather than binary.

This shift of encoding changes everything about the engineering. The workhorse resource state is squeezed vacuum, generated deterministically by parametric amplification. The workhorse measurement is homodyne detection — interference with a strong local oscillator followed by ordinary photodiodes — which reaches quantum efficiencies above 99% at room temperature. Entangling operations are just beam splitters acting on squeezed inputs. No probabilistic gates, no heralding, no single-photon sources. The price is a different failure mode: instead of losing photons outright, CV states suffer *continuous* degradation — finite squeezing behaves like a built-in Gaussian noise floor that contaminates every operation.

This section builds the CV toolbox in three steps. First, the quadrature formalism: phase space, vacuum noise, squeezing, and the homodyne/heterodyne measurements that read quadratures out. Second, the Gaussian world: states described entirely by first and second moments, operations that act as symplectic matrices, the CV cluster states that hold the world record for entangled-state size — and the no-go theorem showing that this entire Gaussian world is efficiently classically simulable, so that non-Gaussianity is the true quantum fuel. Third, the Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill encoding, which plants a qubit inside an oscillator as a grid state and converts CV noise into correctable qubit errors, providing the leading route from squeezed light to fault-tolerant photonic computation.

## Subsections

- **21.1.1 — Quadrature Variables**: Quadrature operators and their commutator; phase-space pictures; vacuum noise and the uncertainty relation; coherent and squeezed states; squeezing in decibels and the 15 dB record; homodyne and heterodyne detection.
- **21.1.2 — Gaussian States and Operations**: Covariance-matrix formalism; symplectic transformations; displacement, phase rotation, squeezing, beam splitters; two-mode squeezing and EPR entanglement; CV cluster states; the classical-simulability no-go and the necessity of non-Gaussian elements.
- **21.1.3 — GKP Encoding**: The qubit-in-an-oscillator code; grid states and stabilizer displacements; correcting small displacement errors with homodyne measurement; finite-energy GKP states; squeezing thresholds for fault tolerance and the state of experimental GKP generation.
