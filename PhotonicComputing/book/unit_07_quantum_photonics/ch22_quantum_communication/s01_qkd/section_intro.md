# Section 22.1: Quantum Key Distribution

All of modern cryptography rests on a single primitive: two parties who share a secret random key can communicate with provable secrecy (the one-time pad) or practical security (AES). The entire difficulty is *establishing* that shared key when every channel between the parties is tapped. Classical solutions — RSA, Diffie-Hellman, elliptic curves — rest on unproven computational assumptions, and the largest of them fall to a sufficiently capable quantum computer running Shor's algorithm. Quantum key distribution (QKD) replaces computational assumptions with physics: encode key bits on quantum states of light, and any eavesdropper's measurement necessarily disturbs those states in a way the legitimate parties can detect and quantify. Security holds against an adversary with unlimited computing power — including a quantum computer.

Two ideas make this work. First, *non-orthogonal states cannot be reliably distinguished*: a photon prepared randomly in one of two conjugate polarization bases carries information that no measurement can extract completely. Second, *information gain implies disturbance*: whatever partial information an eavesdropper obtains leaves fingerprints as errors in the legitimate receiver's data. QKD protocols are machines for turning these theorems into keys — transmit, measure, compare a sample, bound the eavesdropper's knowledge from the observed error rate, and distill a shorter but provably secret key.

The gap between the four-line protocol and a deployed system is where the photonics of this book earns its keep. Real systems use attenuated lasers rather than single photons (opening the photon-number-splitting attack, closed by decoy states), real detectors with dark counts and exploitable side channels (closed by measurement-device-independent protocols), and real fiber whose 0.2 dB/km attenuation compounds to a hard exponential wall (softened by twin-field protocols, bypassed by satellites). This section builds from the ideal protocol (22.1.1), through the implementation battles and record key rates (22.1.2), to QKD from orbit (22.1.3).
