# Chapter 26: Further Reading and References

## Field Reviews and Perspectives

**Shastri, B.J., Tait, A.N., Ferreira de Lima, T., Pernice, W.H.P., Bhaskaran, H., Wright, C.D., & Prucnal, P.R. (2021). "Photonics for artificial intelligence and neuromorphic computing." *Nature Photonics*, 15, 102–114.**
The standard review of the photonic AI hardware field, co-authored across its leading academic groups; the best single map of the research base behind the companies in Section 26.1.

**McMahon, P.L. (2023). "The physics of optical computing." *Nature Reviews Physics*, 5, 717–734.**
A rigorous analysis of where optical computing can and cannot win; indispensable for evaluating company claims.

**Margalit, N., Xiang, C., Bowers, S.M., Bjorlin, A., Blum, R., & Bowers, J.E. (2021). "Perspective on the future of silicon photonics and electronics." *Applied Physics Letters*, 118, 220501.**
An industry-and-academia perspective on the silicon photonics roadmap — transceivers, co-packaging, and integration — written by authors spanning UCSB and major industry.

**Rudolph, T. (2017). "Why I am optimistic about the silicon-photonic route to quantum computing." *APL Photonics*, 2, 030901.**
The PsiQuantum thesis in essay form, by a co-founder; opinionated, quantitative, and unusually candid about what must go right.

## Primary Literature: The Founding Papers of the Companies

**Shen, Y., Harris, N.C., Skirlo, S., et al. (2017). "Deep learning with coherent nanophotonic circuits." *Nature Photonics*, 11, 441–446.**
The MZI-mesh ONN demonstration from which both Lightmatter and Lightelligence descend.

**Sun, C., Wade, M.T., Lee, Y., et al. (2015). "Single-chip microprocessor that communicates directly using light." *Nature*, 528, 534–538.**
The zero-change CMOS photonics milestone behind Ayar Labs.

**Atabaki, A.H., Moazeni, S., Pavanello, F., et al. (2018). "Integrating photonics with silicon nanoelectronics for the next generation of systems on a chip." *Nature*, 556, 349–354.**
Monolithic photonics in a 45 nm CMOS platform — the deep integration route later industrialized as GlobalFoundries' monolithic photonics offerings.

**Wade, M., et al. (2020). "TeraPHY: A chiplet technology for low-power, high-bandwidth in-package optical I/O." *IEEE Micro*, 40(2), 63–71.**
The optical I/O chiplet architecture paper; read together with the UCIe standard to understand how photonics docks to commercial ASICs.

**Ramey, C. (2020). "Silicon photonics for artificial intelligence acceleration." *IEEE Hot Chips 32 Symposium.***
Lightmatter's first public system disclosure; a model of the conference-talk genre through which this industry communicates system results.

**Demirkiran, C., Eris, F., Wang, G., et al. (2023). "An electro-photonic system for accelerating deep neural networks." *ACM Journal on Emerging Technologies in Computing Systems*, 19(4).**
A careful academic-industrial study (with Lightmatter co-authors) of full-system electro-photonic accelerator performance — the end-to-end accounting Chapter 25 demands.

## Primary Literature: Quantum Photonics Companies

**PsiQuantum team — Alexander, K., et al. (2025). "A manufacturable platform for photonic quantum computing." *Nature* (arXiv:2404.17570).**
Foundry-fabricated sources, interferometers, integrated SNSPDs, and BaTiO₃ switches: the company's loss-budget scorecard, in the peer-reviewed record.

**Bartolucci, S., Birchall, P., Bombín, H., et al. (2023). "Fusion-based quantum computation." *Nature Communications*, 14, 912.**
The FBQC architecture paper — the theoretical backbone of PsiQuantum's machine and of Section 26.2.1's loss-threshold discussion.

**Arrazola, J.M., Bergholm, V., Brádler, K., et al. (2021). "Quantum circuits with many photons on a programmable nanophotonic chip." *Nature*, 591, 54–60.**
Xanadu's X8 chip: programmable squeezed-light quantum circuits delivered over the cloud.

**Madsen, L.S., Laudenbach, F., Askarani, M.F., et al. (2022). "Quantum computational advantage with a programmable photonic processor." *Nature*, 606, 75–81.**
Borealis: 216-mode time-multiplexed Gaussian boson sampling.

**Aghaee Rad, H., et al. (2025). "Scaling and networking a modular photonic quantum computer." *Nature*, 638, 912–919.**
Aurora: Xanadu's modular, fiber-networked, rack-scale architecture demonstration.

**Bourassa, J.E., Alexander, R.N., Vasmer, M., et al. (2021). "Blueprint for a scalable photonic fault-tolerant quantum computer." *Quantum*, 5, 392.**
Xanadu's GKP-based fault-tolerance architecture.

**Somaschi, N., Giesz, V., De Santis, L., et al. (2016). "Near-optimal single-photon sources in the solid state." *Nature Photonics*, 10, 340–345.**
The quantum-dot micropillar source performance that founded Quandela.

**Maring, N., et al. (2024). "A versatile single-photon-based quantum computing platform." *Nature Photonics*, 18, 603–609.**
Quandela's full-stack platform paper (sources to cloud processor).

**Taballione, C., et al. (2023). "20-mode universal quantum photonic processor." *Quantum*, 7, 1071.**
QuiX Quantum's Si₃N₄ programmable interferometer.

## Ecosystem, Interconnect, and Roadmaps

**Cheng, Q., Bahadori, M., Glick, M., Rumley, S., & Bergman, K. (2018). "Recent advances in optical technologies for data centers: a review." *Optica*, 5, 1354–1370.**
The data-center optics landscape from the Columbia systems group — background for the CPO discussion.

**Minkenberg, C., Krishnaswamy, R., Zilkie, A., & Nelson, D. (2021). "Co-packaged datacenter optics: opportunities and challenges." *IET Optoelectronics*, 15, 77–91.**
A sober engineering treatment of CPO: what it saves, what it complicates (serviceability, laser location, test).

**Chrostowski, L. & Hochberg, M. (2015). *Silicon Photonics Design: From Devices to Systems*. Cambridge University Press.**
The PDK-era design textbook; Chapter 26's foundry ecosystem, seen from the designer's chair.

**AIM Photonics & the Integrated Photonic Systems Roadmap (IPSR-I).**
The US manufacturing institute's public roadmap documents; alongside imec's and JePPIX's public MPW schedules, the primary sources on foundry capability. (Consult the current editions — these are living documents.)

## A Note on Company Sources

Company whitepapers, product briefs, and press releases are legitimate objects of study but not evidence of performance. Where this chapter cites a company capability, prefer the peer-reviewed or refereed-conference source listed above; where none exists, the capability should be regarded as announced rather than demonstrated.
