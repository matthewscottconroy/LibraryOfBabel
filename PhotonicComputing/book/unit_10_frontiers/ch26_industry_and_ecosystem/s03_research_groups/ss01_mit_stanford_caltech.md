# 26.3.1 The Americas: MIT, Stanford, Caltech, and Their Network

## MIT and the Boston Corridor

MIT is the single most consequential source of photonic computing companies and results. **Dirk Englund's** group spans quantum photonics (diamond color centers, programmable nanophotonic processors) and photonic deep learning — the 2017 coherent ONN [Shen et al., 2017], later system concepts such as delocalized "Netcast" edge inference [Sludds et al., *Science*, 2022]. **Marin Soljačić's** group co-produced the 2017 ONN and pursues inverse design, photonic band structure, and AI-for-physics; its alumni founded both Lightmatter and Lightelligence. **Karl Berggren** leads superconducting nanowire single-photon detector (SNSPD) development — the detector technology on which all of Unit VII leans. **Juejun Hu** advances phase-change and chalcogenide photonic materials. **Ryan Hamerly** (MIT/NTT Research) is a central theorist of large-scale ONN energetics [Hamerly et al., *PRX*, 2019] and coherent Ising machines. The corridor extends through Boston's startups and to nearby Yale (**Hong Tang**: integrated lithium niobate, optomechanics, SNSPDs).

## Stanford

Stanford hosts the field's deepest bench of theory-plus-device groups. **Jelena Vučković** (photonic crystal cavities, inverse design — the origin of much of Chapter 23's adjoint-method toolchain), **Shanhui Fan** (theory: synthetic dimensions, topological photonics, photonic computing architectures, in situ training [Hughes et al., *Optica*, 2018; Pai et al., *Science*, 2023]), **David A. B. Miller** (the field's foremost analyst of interconnect energetics and self-configuring optics — Chapters 25 and 28 owe him their framing), **Gordon Wetzstein** (computational imaging and optics-for-inference [Wetzstein et al., *Nature*, 2020]), **Martin Fejer** (PPLN nonlinear optics underlying OPO-based Ising machines), and **Hideo Mabuchi** (coherent nonlinear dynamics and the theory of optical Ising machines). The Stanford-NTT coherent Ising machine program (Section 27.1) grew from the **Yoshihisa Yamamoto** era; Yamamoto now directs NTT Research's Physics & Informatics (PHI) Lab in Sunnyvale — an industrial basic-research lab that functions as an honorary member of this academic list.

## Caltech

**Alireza Marandi** (ultrafast nonlinear photonics: femtosecond OPO networks, all-optical computing primitives; co-inventor of the time-multiplexed CIM [Marandi et al., *Nature Photonics*, 2014]), **Kerry Vahala** (microresonator frequency combs and soliton physics — the foundation of Section 27.2), **Andrei Faraon** (rare-earth quantum memories, meta-optics), and **Oskar Painter** (optomechanics and, via AWS, superconducting-photonic quantum interfaces).

## The Wider Network

- **Columbia**: **Michal Lipson** (silicon photonics device physics; Chapter 7) and **Alexander Gaeta** (chip-scale nonlinear optics and combs), plus **Keren Bergman**, the leading architect of photonic networks-on-chip and optically connected computing systems [Cheng et al., *Optica*, 2018] — the academic center of gravity of the interconnect-first thesis.
- **Princeton / Queen's**: **Paul Prucnal** (neuromorphic photonics, broadcast-and-weight — Unit VI) and his alumnus **Bhavin Shastri** (Queen's University, Canada), co-authors of the field's standard review [Shastri et al., *Nature Photonics*, 2021].
- **UCSB**: **John Bowers** — heterogeneous III-V-on-silicon lasers and comb integration; the supply chain for every "where does the light come from?" question in this book.
- **UCLA**: **Aydogan Ozcan** (diffractive deep networks, computational imaging [Lin et al., *Science*, 2018]) and **Bahram Jalali** (time-stretch analog processing, photonic ADCs).
- **Berkeley**: **Ming Wu** (MEMS-actuated photonic switch fabrics and optical I/O).
- **University of Washington**: **Arka Majumdar** (meta-optics for computation and imaging).
- **NIST (Boulder/Gaithersburg)**: **Scott Papp** and **Kartik Srinivasan** (microcombs, integrated frequency metrology), plus the SNSPD groups — the national-metrology backbone for Chapters 4, 18, and 27.
- **Toronto/Max Planck**: **Joyce Poon** (electronic-photonic integration, neurophotonics), bridging to the European list.

The unifying observation: nearly every US photonic computing company in Section 26.1–26.2 can be traced to two or three of these groups, and the co-authorship graph among them is dense. For a student choosing where to work, the graph matters more than any single node.
