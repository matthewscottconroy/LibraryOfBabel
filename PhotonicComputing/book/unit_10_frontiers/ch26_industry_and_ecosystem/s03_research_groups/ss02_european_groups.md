# 26.3.2 Europe and Israel: From Ghent to the Technion

Europe's strength in this field is structural: the continent hosts the dominant silicon photonics prototyping route (imec), the leading low-loss nitride platforms, the deepest quantum optics tradition, and coordinated public funding (the EU Quantum Flagship; national programs such as the Netherlands' PhotonDelta, which committed over a billion euros to integrated photonics in 2022).

## The Low Countries and France

- **Ghent University / imec (Belgium)**: the academic-foundry symbiosis that defined European silicon photonics. **Roel Baets** built the platform; **Wim Bogaerts** leads *programmable photonics* — general-purpose reconfigurable waveguide meshes [Bogaerts et al., *Nature*, 2020] — and **Peter Bienstman** pioneered integrated photonic reservoir computing [Vandoorne et al., *Nature Communications*, 2014].
- **University of Twente / LioniX (Netherlands)**: ultra-low-loss Si₃N₄ (TriPleX), the substrate for QuiX Quantum's processors; **Pepijn Pinkse** (quantum optics in complex media). **TU Eindhoven** carries the InP integration tradition of **Meint Smit** (COBRA/JePPIX ecosystem; SMART Photonics spin-off) — the platform where lasers, amplifiers, and modulators live on one chip.
- **Universitat Politècnica de València (Spain)**: **José Capmany** and **Daniel Pérez-López** — microwave photonics and the hexagonal programmable mesh [Pérez et al., *Nature Communications*, 2017]; spin-off iPronics sells general-purpose photonic processors.
- **France**: **C2N/CNRS (Paris-Saclay)** — **Pascale Senellart** (quantum-dot sources; Quandela) and **Jacqueline Bloch** (polariton lattices as analog simulators). **Sorbonne/LKB** — **Sylvain Gigan** (computing with multiple scattering: optical random projections at 10⁶-dimensional scale [Rafayelyan et al., *PRX*, 2020]; spin-off LightOn). **FEMTO-ST (Besançon)** — **Laurent Larger** and **Daniel Brunner**, the European home of photonic reservoir computing [Larger et al., *Optics Express*, 2012; Brunner et al., *Nature Communications*, 2013].

## Switzerland, Germany, Austria, Nordics

- **EPFL (Lausanne)**: **Tobias Kippenberg** — dissipative Kerr soliton microcombs and the Damascene Si₃N₄ process (Section 27.2's foundation); **Camille Brès** (nonlinear nitride photonics); and the optical computing heritage of **Demetri Psaltis**, whose holographic neural network work [Psaltis et al., *Nature*, 1990] anticipated this entire book.
- **Karlsruhe Institute of Technology**: **Christian Koos** — comb-based terabit communications [Marin-Palomo et al., *Nature*, 2017] and photonic wire bonding / 3D nanoprinted micro-optics for heterogeneous integration (Section 27's 3D frontier).
- **Münster / Heidelberg**: **Wolfram Pernice** — phase-change photonic memory and in-memory optical computing, with Oxford's **Harish Bhaskaran** [Feldmann et al., *Nature*, 2019; 2021]. The Bhaskaran-Pernice axis is Europe's flagship photonic-AI collaboration.
- **Paderborn**: **Christine Silberhorn** — engineered PPLN sources, time-multiplexed quantum walks, and pulsed squeezed light.
- **Jena / Rostock**: **Alexander Szameit** — laser-written waveguide arrays; photonic Floquet topological insulators [Rechtsman et al., *Nature*, 2013].
- **Max Planck Institute for the Science of Light (Erlangen)**: **Florian Marquardt** (machine learning meets wave physics; neuromorphic computing theory [Wanjura & Marquardt, *Nature Physics*, 2024]) alongside deep quantum optics roots.
- **Vienna**: **Philip Walther** — photonic quantum computation and quantum machine learning experiments.
- **Copenhagen (Niels Bohr Institute)**: **Peter Lodahl** — photonic-crystal waveguide quantum-dot sources (spin-off Sparrow Quantum).
- **UK**: **Oxford** — Bhaskaran (above) and the ultrafast quantum optics school of **Ian Walmsley** (now Imperial College), which seeded ORCA; **Bristol** — the integrated quantum photonics group (O'Brien-Thompson legacy; **Anthony Laing**) whose alumni populate PsiQuantum and PsiQuantum-adjacent startups; **Southampton** — **Graham Reed's** silicon modulator school and the Optoelectronics Research Centre's fiber heritage.

## Israel

- **Technion (Haifa)**: **Mordechai Segev** — photonic topological insulators [Rechtsman et al., 2013], topological insulator lasers [Bandres et al., *Science*, 2018], and photonic lattice physics generally; one of the most influential figures of Section 27.4.
- **Weizmann, Hebrew University, Tel Aviv**: nonlinear and quantum optics groups plus an applied cluster (e.g., laser-based Ising solver startup LightSolver) connecting to Section 27.1.

The European pattern, in one sentence: platform stewardship (imec, LioniX, JePPIX) plus quantum optics depth produce component companies and programmable-photonics concepts, while the US pattern produces venture-scale system companies — a complementarity visible throughout Sections 26.1 and 26.2.
