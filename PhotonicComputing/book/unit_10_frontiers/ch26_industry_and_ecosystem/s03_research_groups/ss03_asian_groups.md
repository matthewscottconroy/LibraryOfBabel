# 26.3.3 Asia-Pacific: NTT, USTC, Tsinghua, and the Australian Photonics Cluster

## Japan: NTT and the University of Tokyo

**NTT Basic Research Laboratories** is arguably the most important industrial photonics laboratory in the world. Its contributions span this whole book: **Masaya Notomi's** photonic crystal nanocavities and femtojoule-class all-optical switches [Nozaki et al., *Nature Photonics*, 2010] (Chapter 28's transistor question), and **Hiroki Takesue's** and **Takahiro Inagaki's** measurement-feedback coherent Ising machines, scaled to 100,000 spins [Inagaki et al., *Science*, 2016; Honjo et al., *Science Advances*, 2021] (Section 27.1). NTT's corporate IOWN program (an "all-photonics network" vision for computing and communications infrastructure) and its Silicon-Valley-based NTT Research PHI Lab (directed by **Yoshihisa Yamamoto**) make NTT simultaneously a research lab, an infrastructure operator, and a long-horizon investor in photonic computing.

At the **University of Tokyo**, **Akira Furusawa's** group leads continuous-variable optical quantum computing: deterministic quantum teleportation, time-domain-multiplexed cluster states of enormous size, and a program (and spin-off, OptQC) toward measurement-based CV quantum computers — the academic wellspring of the approach Xanadu commercializes.

## China: Scale and Speed

- **USTC (Hefei)**: **Jian-Wei Pan** and **Chao-Yang Lu** — the Jiuzhang Gaussian boson sampling experiments [Zhong et al., *Science*, 2020, and successors with hundreds of detected photons], quantum communication at continental scale, and a pipeline of records in multi-photon entanglement. For sampling-advantage claims and their classical-algorithm rebuttals, this group defines the frontier's pace.
- **Tsinghua (Beijing)**: **Qionghai Dai** and **Lu Fang** — large-scale optoelectronic computing: the ACCEL all-analog photonic-electronic vision chip [Chen et al., *Nature*, 2023], the Taichi diffractive-interference chiplet reporting energy efficiency orders of magnitude beyond contemporary GPUs on its benchmarks [Xu et al., *Science*, 2024], and fully forward on-chip training [Xue et al., *Nature*, 2024]. Apply Chapter 25's accounting discipline to these results — and note that they are peer-reviewed system demonstrations, which is precisely what the field needs more of.
- **Peking University**: **Jianwei Wang** — very-large-scale integrated quantum photonics (multidimensional entanglement, graph photonics [Wang et al., *Science*, 2018]).
- **Shanghai Jiao Tong**: **Xianmin Jin** — photonic chips for quantum walks and analog photonic computing; spin-off TuringQ.
- **Zhejiang University**: **Daoxin Dai** — one of the most prolific silicon photonics device groups (polarization handling, multimode photonics).

China's national investment in photonic integration and quantum technology, plus vertically integrated foundry ambitions, make it the ecosystem most likely to pursue photonic computing at industrial scale independent of Western supply chains.

## Singapore, Korea

- **Singapore**: A*STAR's IME and the AMF foundry (Section 26's supply chain), plus university groups at NUS/NTU (e.g., **Ai Qun Liu**) in optofluidics, quantum photonics, and photonic processors; **Ping Koy Lam** (CV quantum optics) bridges the Australian and Singaporean communities.
- **Korea**: KAIST and Seoul National University contribute microcomb (e.g., **Hansuek Lee**, ultra-high-Q wedge resonators) and silicon photonics device research within a semiconductor-dominant national ecosystem.

## Australia: The Microcomb Computing Cluster

Australia hosts a distinctive concentration in nonlinear and microcomb-based processing: **Ben Eggleton** (Sydney; nonlinear and Brillouin photonics, topological quantum optics collaborations [Blanco-Redondo et al., *Science*, 2018]), **David Moss** (Swinburne) and **Arnan Mitchell** (RMIT), whose soliton-crystal microcomb convolutional accelerator [Xu et al., *Nature*, 2021] is one of the two landmark comb-computing demonstrations of Section 27.2, and **Bill Corcoran** / **Arthur Lowery** (Monash; single-chip-source ultrahigh-capacity transmission [Corcoran et al., *Nature Communications*, 2020]).

## The Pattern

Three features distinguish Asia-Pacific: industrial laboratories doing decade-scale basic research (NTT — a model largely extinct in the West outside NTT and a few hyperscalers), state-scale coordinated programs (China), and specialized regional clusters (Australian microcombs). A literature search that stops at US and European groups misses, among other things, the largest Ising machines, the largest boson samplers, and the most aggressive photonic-AI system demonstrations in the published record.
