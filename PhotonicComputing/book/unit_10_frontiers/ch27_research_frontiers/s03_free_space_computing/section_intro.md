# Section 27.3: Free-Space Photonic Computing

Units V through VII built their processors from confined light: modes shepherded through waveguides, split and recombined at directional couplers, phase-shifted in the arms of a Mach–Zehnder interferometer (Chapter 11). Free-space photonic computing abandons the waveguide. Its computational medium is light propagating through open space — or through a few centimeters of bulk glass — sculpted by spatial light modulators (SLMs), lenses, and fabricated diffractive layers, and read out on a camera. The information is carried not by a handful of guided channels but by the transverse profile of an optical wavefront, sampled across a two-dimensional grid of $10^5$ to $10^6$ pixels.

That pixel count is the entire argument. An integrated MZI mesh that realizes an $N \times N$ unitary needs $O(N^2)$ interferometers and, at $N \sim 100$, already strains the reticle and the calibration budget (Chapters 11, 25). A commodity SLM presents a megapixel array in a single aperture; a lens couples every input pixel to every output pixel in one optical transit. Free space thus offers the largest raw parallelism available in any computing substrate — a matrix dimension one to two orders of magnitude beyond what integrated meshes reach, obtained not by fabricating more devices but by using more of the space-bandwidth product that a wavefront already carries.

The trade is equally stark, and it sets the tone for the whole section. The propagation is effectively free: a wavefront crosses a tabletop processor in nanoseconds, and a passive diffractive stack computes at the speed of light. But nothing else is free. The system's throughput is gated not by the optics but by the *frame rate of its interfaces* — the rate at which an SLM can be reloaded (tens of hertz for phase liquid-crystal panels, kilohertz for the fastest micromirror devices) and a camera can be read out — and its accuracy is gated by the alignment and calibration of a bulk optical train. As everywhere in this chapter, the celebrated physics is not where the budget is spent; the interfaces are. The two subsections develop first the elementary operation and then its composition into layered processors, keeping that accounting in view throughout.
