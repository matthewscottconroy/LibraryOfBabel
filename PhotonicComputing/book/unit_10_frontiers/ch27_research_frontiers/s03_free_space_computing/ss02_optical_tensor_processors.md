# 27.3.2 Optical Tensor Processors and Diffractive Networks

A single matrix–vector product (Section 27.3.1) is one linear layer. A useful processor stacks many of them, interleaves nonlinearity, and thereby computes a deep function. Free-space optics composes layers in the most literal way imaginable: by putting one optical plane after another along the propagation axis, so that "depth" in the network is depth in physical space. Three families have emerged, and they differ precisely in *what is reconfigurable* and *where the nonlinearity lives* — the two axes along which the honest evaluation turns.

## Diffractive Deep Neural Networks

The purest expression of the idea is the diffractive deep neural network (D2NN) [Lin et al., *Science*, 2018]. One trains, in a computer, the transmission and phase profiles of a stack of diffractive layers — plates whose every point is a learnable complex-valued pixel — modeling each layer as a modulation and each gap as free-space (Fresnel) diffraction that connects every point on one layer to every point on the next. The connectivity is dense and enormous: with $10^5$–$10^6$ neurons per layer, the diffraction between two layers is an all-to-all linear map that no integrated mesh could host. Once trained, the plates are *fabricated* — 3D-printed for terahertz demonstrations, lithographically patterned for shorter wavelengths — and thereafter the network is passive and fixed. Inference is performed by illuminating the input and letting light propagate through the stack to a detector array that reads the class scores, all-optically and at the speed of light, dissipating no dynamic power in the layers themselves.

The discipline to apply is to separate what this demonstrates from what it does not. DEMONSTRATED: an all-optical, multi-layer classifier whose forward pass costs essentially only the illumination and the final detection, with real experimental accuracy on tasks such as handwritten-digit recognition. STRUCTURALLY EXCLUDED: any *in situ* learning. The weights are frozen in glass; retraining means refabrication. A D2NN is therefore not a general processor but a manufactured, special-purpose front end — superb where the task is fixed and volume is high (a dedicated classifier, a physical-layer preprocessor), irrelevant where the workload changes. It is also strictly linear unless a nonlinear material or an optoelectronic readout is inserted between stacks; the celebrated versions lean on the final detector and a softmax done in software for their nonlinearity, a fact worth keeping in view when the system is called a "neural network."

## Reconfigurable and Optoelectronic Processors

To recover trainability one replaces the fabricated plates with spatial light modulators, so that each weight layer becomes a reprogrammable page, and interleaves an optoelectronic nonlinearity — detect, apply a pointwise nonlinear function in electronics or in a nonlinear medium, re-emit — between diffractive stages. The reconfigurable diffractive processing unit of Zhou et al. is the canonical large-scale realization: a programmable optoelectronic building block that can be reconfigured layer by layer and, crucially, *trained in situ* against its own physical imperfections rather than against an idealized model, so that fabrication and alignment errors are absorbed into the learned weights [Zhou et al., *Nature Photonics*, 2021]. This is a genuinely different regime from the D2NN: dynamic power is now spent (every SLM page and every optoelectronic conversion costs energy), reconfiguration is possible but rate-limited by the SLM, and the system can adapt. A complementary design point is the hybrid optical–electronic convolutional network, in which a single learned diffractive element forms an optical front end — typically the first convolutional layer, computed for free during imaging — feeding a conventional electronic CNN that does the rest [Chang et al., *Scientific Reports*, 2018]. The lesson of that hybrid is the correct default for the whole family: use optics for the one operation whose parallelism it wins — a large, fixed, dense linear map at the sensor — and hand everything else to electronics.

## Scattering Media as Million-Mode Processors

The third family dispenses with fabricated layers altogether and uses a *disordered* medium as a fixed, high-dimensional linear map. A multiply-scattering slab implements a dense random transformation of the input wavefront across up to $\sim 10^6$ modes; combined with a nonlinear readout it forms an optical *reservoir* or random-feature engine whose internal weights are never trained — only the linear output layer is (Unit VI). Rafayelyan et al. demonstrated a large-scale optical reservoir on this principle, predicting the evolution of spatiotemporal chaotic systems with a reservoir of dimension far beyond what a comparably fast electronic implementation could update per step [Rafayelyan et al., *Physical Review X*, 2020]. Here the physics supplies enormous, essentially free dimensionality; the computing claim is correspondingly modest and honest — the machine is powerful exactly because reservoir computing asks so little of the fixed internal map, and the burden of accuracy falls on the trained readout and on the interfaces that load inputs and collect states.

## Free Space Versus the Mesh, Honestly

Set these against the integrated MZI mesh of Chapter 11 on the axes that matter. On raw channel count free space wins outright, by two to four orders of magnitude: a megapixel SLM or a scattering slab hosts a linear map no reticle-limited mesh can approach. On reconfiguration speed and integration the mesh wins: a thermo-optic or electro-optic mesh reweights in microseconds and lives on a chip, whereas an SLM reweights in milliseconds and lives on an optical table that must be aligned and held stable. The diffractive route sharpens the trade to its limit — by freezing the weights in fabricated glass it achieves *zero dynamic power in the linear layers*, at the total sacrifice of reconfigurability. There is no universally correct choice here; there is only a workload. A fixed, high-throughput classifier rewards the diffractive extreme; an adaptive accelerator rewards the reconfigurable-but-slower SLM system, or the integrated mesh.

## End-to-End Accounting

The accounting is the same as in Section 27.3.1 and must be stated for any of these processors. The propagation through the stack is effectively instantaneous and nearly energy-free; the system's latency is set by the *frame rate* of its slowest interface, and its power by the illumination source, the SLM control electronics, and the camera readout — quantities measured in watts for the electronics and gated by tens-of-hertz-to-kilohertz reload and readout, not by the picoseconds of transit. A D2NN moves almost all of that cost to the input laser and the output detector; a reconfigurable optoelectronic processor adds the per-page SLM drive and per-stage conversion; a scattering reservoir adds only the input and state-readout interfaces. In every case the correct figure of merit is the end-to-end one — energy and latency including sources, converters, and calibration — and the correct framing is the field's own: these are co-designed optical-and-electronic inference systems, powerful where a large fixed linear map at the sensor is exactly what the task needs, and unremarkable where it is not [Wetzstein et al., *Nature*, 2020].
