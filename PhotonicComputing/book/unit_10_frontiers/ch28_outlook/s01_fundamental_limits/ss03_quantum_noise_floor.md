# 28.1.3 The Quantum Noise Floor

## Poissonian Light and the Standard Quantum Limit

Beneath the precision–energy trade-off lies its physical cause: the quantum granularity of light. A coherent state — the output of an ideal laser, the closest optical analog of a classical wave — carries photons that arrive as independent random events, Poisson-distributed in number. A measurement detecting a mean of $\bar n$ photons therefore has variance $\bar n$ and standard deviation $\sqrt{\bar n}$, giving an amplitude signal-to-noise ratio

$$\mathrm{SNR}\sim\frac{\bar n}{\sqrt{\bar n}}=\sqrt{\bar n}.$$

This is the *standard quantum limit* (SQL), also called the shot-noise limit: the best precision obtainable with classical (coherent) light, improving only as the square root of the optical energy. It is the same $\sqrt{\bar n}$ that fixed the $4^B$ photon cost of the previous subsection — the number of distinguishable amplitude levels, $\sqrt{\bar n}=2^B$, is precisely this amplitude SNR. The precision–energy trade-off is nothing more than the SQL rewritten in bits.

## Why It Is Quantum, Not Thermal

It is worth being precise about the character of this noise, because it is often confused with the thermal (Johnson–Nyquist) noise familiar from electronics. Shot noise is not thermal. It does not vanish as the temperature falls toward absolute zero; it is a consequence of the quantization of the electromagnetic field, present even for a perfectly cooled, perfectly coherent beam. In the quantum-optical picture it originates in the vacuum fluctuations that accompany any measurement of a field quadrature — the irreducible uncertainty of the electromagnetic vacuum, not the thermal agitation of charges. A photodetector cooled to millikelvin temperatures still registers full shot noise on a coherent beam. This is why the SQL is a genuinely *fundamental* floor for classical light rather than an engineering nuisance to be refrigerated away: it is imposed by quantum mechanics, and a receiver that reaches it — a shot-noise-limited, or "quantum-limited," receiver — has extracted all the information the coherent field contains.

## Squeezing: Beating the SQL by Redistributing Noise

The SQL is the floor for *coherent* light, and that qualifier is the opening. Quantum mechanics constrains only the *product* of the uncertainties in a field's two conjugate quadratures — amplitude and phase — through a Heisenberg relation; a coherent state distributes the minimum uncertainty equally between them, but a *squeezed* state redistributes it, suppressing the noise in one quadrature below the vacuum level at the cost of amplifying it in the other. Measuring the quiet quadrature then beats the standard quantum limit. Caves showed in 1981 that injecting squeezed vacuum into the dark port of an interferometer lowers its phase noise below the shot-noise limit [Caves, *Physical Review D*, 1981] — the principle now used to enhance gravitational-wave detectors, and the same continuous-variable resource that underlies photonic quantum computing (Unit VII). Squeezing is the one physically sanctioned route past the floor that governs everything else in this section.

## Does Squeezing Help a Computer?

Whether that route helps a *computer* is an open and honestly contested question, and it should not be oversold. Squeezed light is expensive to generate and fragile to preserve: the degree of squeezing is degraded by every optical loss in the system — precisely the losses that Section 28.1.1 identified as photonic computing's binding constraint — so that several decibels of squeezing can be undone by a fraction of a decibel of insertion loss. In a precision interferometer, where the measurement is the entire purpose and the optics can be built to extraordinary loss standards, the trade pays. In a computer, whose signals must traverse modulators, meshes, and detectors that each contribute loss, the overhead of generating, routing, and preserving squeezed states has generally exceeded the modest SNR improvement it buys. The honest statement is that squeezing offers a *demonstrated* advantage in metrology and a *speculative* one in computing; whether a squeezing-enhanced classical optical processor ever repays its overhead is unresolved, and the burden of proof — as always in this book — rests on the full end-to-end accounting, not on the quadrature variance in isolation.

## The Floor Beneath the Trade-off

Step back and the three floors of this section resolve into a single layered picture. Landauer's $k_B T\ln 2$ lies at the very bottom, a thermodynamic floor so far beneath any real machine as to be irrelevant to photonics (Section 28.1.1). Far above it, and genuinely operative, is the precision–energy trade-off, which taxes every bit of analog precision by a factor of four in photons (Section 28.1.2). And the physical origin of *that* tax is the standard quantum limit examined here: the shot-noise floor is the precision–energy trade-off expressed in the language of quantum optics, and it is the fundamental ceiling on classical photonic computing. No arrangement of coherent light does better; a designer who has minimized loss, maximized detector efficiency, and reached the shot-noise limit has arrived at this floor and can descend no further without changing the quantum state of the light itself. This is where the map of classical photonic computing ends — and, at exactly this boundary, where quantum resources become relevant, because squeezing and entanglement are the only physics that reaches beneath the SQL (Unit VII). Whether reaching beneath it buys a computational advantage worth its cost is the question that separates classical photonic computing, the subject of most of this book, from photonic *quantum* computing, its more speculative and more discontinuous cousin. The precision–energy trade-off is at once the ceiling of the one and the doorway to the other [McMahon, *Nature Reviews Physics*, 2023; Hamerly et al., *Physical Review X*, 2019].
