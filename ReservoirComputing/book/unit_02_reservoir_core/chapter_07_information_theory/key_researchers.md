# Chapter 7: Key Researchers

## Joni Dambre

Joni Dambre is a professor at Ghent University (Belgium) in the Electronics and Information Systems department. Her work on reservoir computing spans both the theoretical foundations and practical applications of the framework. The 2012 paper "Information processing capacity of dynamical systems" [Dambre2012], co-authored with Verstraeten, Schrauwen, and Massar, established the capacity decomposition framework that is the centerpiece of this chapter. This work provided the field with its first rigorous, quantitative language for asking what a reservoir computes — not just whether it computes well on a specific benchmark, but how its finite degrees of freedom are allocated across the full space of possible target functions. Dambre's group has also made significant contributions to photonic reservoir computing, implementing the theoretical framework in physical substrates.

## Jan Dambre

Jan Dambre (note: a distinct researcher from Joni Dambre, though both are at Ghent University) has contributed to the hardware and implementation side of reservoir computing. His work emphasizes the connection between the theoretical capacity framework and physical implementations, where the degrees of freedom of the reservoir are physically constrained resources. The interplay between Joni and Jan Dambre's perspectives — theoretical capacity analysis and physical implementation — represents a productive coupling of abstraction and engineering that characterizes Ghent University's approach to the field.

## Herbert Jaeger

Herbert Jaeger introduced the echo state network and, with it, the concept of memory capacity in his 2002 technical report [Jaeger2002memory] from the German National Research Center for Information Technology. This report posed the question of reservoir memory quantitatively and gave the first proof that memory capacity is bounded by the number of neurons — a result that remains central to the field's understanding of reservoir computational limits. Jaeger's broader career is one of the most influential in recurrent neural network research: his 2001 Science paper on echo state networks, his later work on conceptors, and his persistent advocacy for reservoir computing as a window into the computational principles of biological neural circuits have shaped the field at every level. He is currently a professor at Jacobs University Bremen (now Constructor University).

## David Verstraeten

David Verstraeten was a PhD student and postdoc at Ghent University who played a pivotal role in the early development of reservoir computing theory and software. He is a co-author of the 2012 capacity paper and was one of the developers of the Oger framework, an early open-source toolkit for reservoir computing experiments. His doctoral work systematically compared different reservoir implementations and began the quantitative study of how reservoir properties translate to task performance. Verstraeten's contribution to the capacity framework was in formalizing the computational experiments and connecting the theoretical bounds to empirically observable quantities.
