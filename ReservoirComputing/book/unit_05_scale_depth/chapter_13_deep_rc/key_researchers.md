# Chapter 13 — Key Researchers

---

## Claudio Gallicchio

**Affiliation:** University of Pisa, Department of Computer Science

**Contributions:** Gallicchio is the primary architect of deep reservoir computing as a coherent theoretical and empirical program. His 2017 papers with Micheli established both the foundational architecture of DeepESNs [Gallicchio2017a, Gallicchio2017b] and the key theoretical results: the layerwise echo state property, the timescale hierarchy, and the empirical case for concatenated readouts. Gallicchio has subsequently extended this work to Graph ESNs [Gallicchio2010, Gallicchio2020], stochastic deep reservoirs, and architectural design principles. His work has substantially shifted the community's understanding of what depth buys in the reservoir setting.

**Selected publications:**
- Gallicchio, C. & Micheli, A. (2017). Echo state property of deep reservoir computing networks. *Cognitive Computation*, 9(3), 337–350.
- Gallicchio, C., Micheli, A., & Pedrelli, L. (2017). Deep reservoir computing: A critical experimental analysis. *Neurocomputing*, 268, 87–99.
- Gallicchio, C. & Micheli, A. (2020). Fast and deep graph neural networks. *Proceedings of AAAI*, 34(04), 3898–3905.

## Alessio Micheli

**Affiliation:** University of Pisa, Department of Computer Science

**Contributions:** Micheli is Gallicchio's long-term collaborator and co-originator of the deep ESN framework. He has also made foundational contributions to the theory of graph neural networks, having proposed one of the earliest GNN architectures [Micheli2009]. The combination of deep reservoir computing and graph-structured learning is a synthesis of these two research threads. Micheli's broader research program encompasses structured-domain learning, recursive neural networks, and the application of RC methods to bioinformatics and chemoinformatics.

**Selected publications:**
- Micheli, A. (2009). Neural network for graphs: A contextual constructive approach. *IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks*, 20(3), 498–511.
- Gallicchio, C. & Micheli, A. (2017). Echo state property of deep reservoir computing networks. *Cognitive Computation*, 9(3), 337–350.

## Herbert Jaeger

**Affiliation:** Constructor University Bremen (formerly Jacobs University Bremen)

**Contributions to this chapter:** Although Jaeger's foundational work on ESNs (Chapter 5) predates deep reservoir computing, his development of the echo state property and its conditions [Jaeger2001] provides the single-layer building block on which the entire deep architecture rests. The sufficient conditions for the deep ESP in Section 13.1 are direct extensions of Jaeger's original analysis. Jaeger's research on complex-valued reservoirs and structured connectivity also bears on the design of inter-layer weight matrices.

**Selected publications:**
- Jaeger, H. (2001). The echo state approach to analysing and training recurrent neural networks. GMD Technical Report 148.
- Jaeger, H. & Haas, H. (2004). Harnessing nonlinearity: Predicting chaotic systems and saving energy in wireless communication. *Science*, 304(5667), 78–80.

## Luca Pedrelli

**Affiliation:** University of Pisa, Department of Computer Science (PhD student and postdoctoral researcher)

**Contributions:** Co-author of the foundational 2017 deep reservoir computing paper [Gallicchio2017b], which provided the critical experimental analysis validating the timescale hierarchy and the value of multi-layer architectures. Pedrelli has continued to contribute to the empirical evaluation of deep ESNs across speech, sequential image, and medical time-series tasks.

## Wolfgang Maass

**Affiliation:** Graz University of Technology, Institute of Theoretical Computer Science

**Contributions to this chapter context:** Maass's work on liquid state machines [Maass2002] provides an important parallel theoretical framework to the deep ESN. His analysis of the separation property and the approximation capacity of liquid state machines informs the theoretical understanding of what reservoir depth buys in terms of expressivity. The temporal kernel theory of Maass and colleagues directly motivates the timescale hierarchy analysis of Section 13.2.

**Selected publications:**
- Maass, W., Natschläger, T., & Markram, H. (2002). Real-time computing without stable states: A new framework for neural computation based on perturbations. *Neural Computation*, 14(11), 2531–2560.
