## The Single-Layer Ceiling

A single-layer reservoir, trained with a linear readout, is a powerful machine. But it has limits. Its memory time constant is determined by a single spectral radius parameter. Its representation is formed in one step — one pass through the nonlinearity. Its effective feature space, while high-dimensional, is generated by a single layer of temporal mixing.

Deep learning's great empirical discovery was that depth helps. Not because more parameters are always better, but because hierarchical composition of nonlinearities can represent certain functions exponentially more efficiently than shallow architectures. The question for reservoir computing is natural: can we go deep, without losing the simplicity that makes reservoir computing attractive in the first place?

The answer is yes — and the theory of deep reservoirs reveals something genuinely new: a **timescale hierarchy**. Deep reservoirs naturally develop multiple effective time constants, with lower layers tracking fast dynamics and higher layers tracking slow ones. This is not an artifact of training; it emerges from the architecture itself.

## The Two Chapters

**Chapter 13** develops deep Echo State Networks fully, from architecture to theory to practice. We prove the deep echo state property, analyze the timescale hierarchy analytically, and examine how depth changes the effective expressiveness of the reservoir. We also introduce graph echo state networks — reservoirs that operate on structured, graph-organized data — which extend the deep RC framework beyond sequences.

**Chapter 14** covers ensemble methods: committees of reservoirs that combine their predictions to reduce variance and improve robustness. We derive the bias-variance tradeoff for reservoir ensembles, examine diversity promotion as a design principle, and build up to mixture-of-experts architectures where a learned gate selects which reservoir is active based on the input.
