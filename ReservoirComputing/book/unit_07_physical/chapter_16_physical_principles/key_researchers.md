# Chapter 16 — Key Researchers

---

## Ingo Fischer

**Affiliation:** Institute for Cross-Disciplinary Physics and Complex Systems (IFISC), UIB-CSIC, Palma de Mallorca, Spain

**Contributions:** Fischer is one of the founding figures of physical reservoir computing. He was a co-author of the seminal Appeltant et al. 2011 paper [Appeltant2011] and has led the IFISC group's extensive experimental program in photonic and optoelectronic reservoir computing. Fischer's research combines deep expertise in nonlinear photonics, laser dynamics, and machine learning. He co-edited the definitive reference book on physical RC [Nakajima2021] and has been a central organizer of the physical RC community. His group has demonstrated time-multiplexed photonic reservoirs, spatially parallel implementations, and signal processing applications at gigahertz rates.

---

## Guy Van der Sande

**Affiliation:** Vrije Universiteit Brussel (VUB), Applied Physics and Photonics department

**Contributions:** Van der Sande co-authored the Appeltant et al. 2011 paper and has been a central figure in photonic RC since. His research spans theoretical analysis of delay-feedback systems, experimental optoelectronic implementations, and the application of physical reservoirs to real-world communication tasks. His group has contributed extensively to understanding the memory-nonlinearity tradeoff in time-multiplexed systems.

---

## Miguel C. Soriano

**Affiliation:** IFISC, UIB-CSIC, Palma de Mallorca, Spain

**Contributions:** Soriano is another co-author of Appeltant et al. 2011 and has contributed extensively to the experimental and theoretical development of delay-feedback reservoir computers. His work on the characterization of reservoir performance, the role of the mask in virtual node diversity, and the benchmarking of physical RC systems has been particularly influential.

---

## Laurent Larger

**Affiliation:** FEMTO-ST Institute, Université de Franche-Comté, Besançon, France

**Contributions:** Larger's group developed the first optoelectronic reservoir computers operating at gigahertz rates [Larger2012], demonstrating that the time-multiplexed architecture can achieve speeds far beyond digital silicon implementations. His research focuses on high-speed nonlinear optical systems, delay-differential equations, and their applications to information processing.

**Selected publications:**
- Larger, L., Soriano, M.C., Brunner, D., et al. (2012). Photonic information processing beyond Turing: An optoelectronic implementation of reservoir computing. *Optics Express*, 20(3), 3241–3249.

---

## Daniel Brunner

**Affiliation:** FEMTO-ST Institute, Université de Franche-Comté; previously IFISC

**Contributions:** Brunner demonstrated the first parallel (spatially multiplexed, rather than time-multiplexed) photonic reservoir using a spatial light modulator, achieving gigabyte-per-second processing rates [Brunner2013]. His work represents the frontier of fast physical RC and has driven much of the interest in hardware neural network acceleration.

**Selected publications:**
- Brunner, D., Soriano, M.C., Mirasso, C.R., & Fischer, I. (2013). Parallel photonic information processing at gigabyte per second data rates. *Nature Communications*, 4, 1364.

---

## Kohei Nakajima

**Affiliation:** The University of Tokyo, Graduate School of Information Science and Technology

**Contributions:** Nakajima has made foundational contributions to the theory and practice of physical RC, with particular emphasis on soft robotics reservoirs, quantum reservoir computing, and the formalization of physical RC principles. He co-edited the book *Reservoir Computing: Theory, Physical Implementations, and Applications* (Springer, 2021) [Nakajima2021], the most comprehensive reference in the field. His work on the "computational power of morphology" — the idea that the physical body of a robot can serve as a reservoir — opened the field of embodied RC.
