# Chapter 17 — Key Researchers

## Daniel Brunner

**Affiliation:** FEMTO-ST Institute, Université de Franche-Comté, Besançon, France

**Contributions:** Brunner is one of the most prolific experimentalists in photonic reservoir computing. His 2013 *Nature Communications* paper demonstrated parallel (spatial rather than temporal) photonic reservoir computing at gigabyte-per-second data rates using a spatial light modulator and a single-pass architecture [Brunner2013]. He has subsequently developed chip-scale photonic RC, semiconductor laser arrays as reservoirs, and methods for characterizing photonic RC performance. Brunner serves as a bridge between the photonics and machine learning communities and has contributed to making physical RC results quantitatively comparable through standardized benchmarks.

**Selected publications:**
- Brunner, D., Soriano, M.C., Mirasso, C.R., & Fischer, I. (2013). Parallel photonic information processing at gigabyte per second data rates. *Nature Communications*, 4, 1364.
- Brunner, D. & Fischer, I. (2015). Reconfigurable semiconductor laser networks based on diffractive coupling. *Optics Letters*, 40(16), 3854–3857.

## Peter Bienstman

**Affiliation:** Ghent University, Department of Information Technology (INTEC)

**Contributions:** Bienstman led the Ghent University team (with Vandoorne, Dambre, Schrauwen) that designed, fabricated, and characterized the silicon photonic chip reservoir [Vandoorne2014]. His group developed the coupled mode theory modeling of silicon ring resonators for reservoir computing and contributed the experimental validation. Bienstman's broader research encompasses silicon photonics, nanophotonic circuits, and the intersection of photonics with neuromorphic computing.

**Selected publications:**
- Vandoorne, K. et al. (2014). Experimental demonstration of reservoir computing on a silicon photonic chip. *Nature Communications*, 5, 3541.
- Bienstman, P. et al. (2017). Integrated nanophotonic waveguide-based devices for IR and THz sensing. *Nanophotonics*, 6(3), 627–645.

## Kristof Vandoorne

**Affiliation:** Ghent University (PhD); subsequently Intel, Thinfilm Electronics

**Contributions:** Vandoorne is the lead author of the landmark silicon photonic reservoir paper [Vandoorne2014]. His doctoral work at Ghent (under Bienstman) developed the theory, simulation, and experimental demonstration of passive photonic reservoirs. The coupled mode equations and TPA model used in Section 17.3 are largely from his dissertation and the associated publications.

## Jan Danckaert

**Affiliation:** Vrije Universiteit Brussel (VUB), Applied Physics and Photonics

**Contributions:** Co-author of Appeltant et al. 2011. Danckaert's group contributed the delay-differential equation analysis and the stability characterization of the Ikeda DDE used for optoelectronic reservoir computing. His expertise in nonlinear photonics and semiconductor laser dynamics provided the physical foundation for the virtual node architecture.

## Claudio R. Mirasso

**Affiliation:** IFISC, UIB-CSIC, Palma de Mallorca, Spain

**Contributions:** Co-author of Appeltant et al. 2011 and a long-term collaborator of Fischer at IFISC. Mirasso's research spans nonlinear laser dynamics, synchronization of coupled lasers, and photonic reservoir computing. His contributions to the theoretical analysis of delay-feedback photonic systems underlie much of the formalism in Section 17.1.
