# Chapter 18: Further Reading

## Foundational Papers

**Hauser, H., Ijspeert, A. J., Füchslin, R. M., Pfeifer, R., & Maass, W. (2011).** Towards a theoretical foundation for morphological computation with compliant bodies. *Biological Cybernetics*, 105(5–6), 355–370.
The foundational paper establishing compliant robot arms as physical reservoirs. Provides the formal proof of the echo state property for damped mechanical systems and the first systematic demonstration of nonlinear function approximation from joint angle readout. Essential reading.

**Pfeifer, R., & Iida, F. (2004).** Embodied artificial intelligence: Trends and challenges. In *Embodied Artificial Intelligence*, Lecture Notes in Computer Science (Vol. 3139), pp. 1–26. Springer.
Introduces the morphological computation concept and surveys the experimental evidence for body-based computation in both biological and artificial systems. The conceptual foundation for the chapter.

**Nakajima, K., Hauser, H., Li, T., & Pfeifer, R. (2015).** Information processing via physical soft body. *Scientific Reports*, 5, 10487.
Demonstrates that a simulated octopus-arm-inspired soft body can perform the NARMA-10 task purely through its mechanical dynamics. Introduces the concept of "computational octopus arm" and analyzes the role of body geometry in reservoir capacity.

**Nakajima, K., Hauser, H., Li, T., & Pfeifer, R. (2015).** Exploiting short-term memory in soft body dynamics as a computational resource. *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, 12(112), 20150330.
Analyzes granular media as reservoirs, providing quantitative comparisons with software ESNs and establishing the role of particle contact nonlinearity in task performance.

## Books and Book Chapters

**Pfeifer, R., & Bongard, J. (2006).** *How the Body Shapes the Way We Think: A New View of Intelligence*. MIT Press.
The definitive monograph on embodied cognition and morphological computation. Chapter 5 (Morphological Computation) is directly relevant; the entire book provides essential context.

**Nakajima, K., & Fischer, I. (Eds.) (2021).** *Reservoir Computing: Theory, Physical Implementations, and Applications*. Springer.
A comprehensive edited volume covering both theoretical foundations and physical implementations. Part III (Physical Implementations) includes chapters on mechanical, optical, and electronic reservoirs, with detailed experimental methodology.

**Skelton, R. E., & de Oliveira, M. C. (2009).** *Tensegrity Systems*. Springer.
The standard reference for tensegrity mechanics, covering static analysis, dynamic modeling, and control. Provides the mathematical tools needed for the tensegrity reservoir analysis in Section 18.2.5.

## Soft Robotics and Compliant Systems

**Rus, D., & Tolley, M. T. (2015).** Design, fabrication and control of soft robots. *Nature*, 521(7553), 467–475.
A survey of soft robotic systems and their unique computational properties. Discusses how compliance enables embodied computation and provides an overview of fabrication methods for soft reservoirs.

**Kim, S., Laschi, C., & Trimmer, B. (2013).** Soft robotics: A bioinspired evolution in robotics. *Trends in Biotechnology*, 31(5), 287–294.
Situates soft robotics in the biological context and discusses the information-processing advantages of compliant bodies, with connections to the reservoir computing interpretation.

**Hauser, H., Ijspeert, A. J., Füchslin, R. M., Pfeifer, R., & Maass, W. (2012).** The role of feedback in morphological computation with compliant bodies. *Biological Cybernetics*, 106(10), 595–613.
Extends the 2011 paper to include proprioceptive feedback, showing how feedback further enriches the reservoir state and enables closed-loop control.

## Granular Media

**Jaeger, H. M., Nagel, S. R., & Behringer, R. P. (1996).** Granular solids, liquids, and gases. *Reviews of Modern Physics*, 68(4), 1259.
The standard physics reference for granular media, covering contact mechanics, force chains, and collective dynamics. Provides the physical foundation for understanding granular reservoirs.

**Nakajima, K., Hauser, H., Kang, R., Guglielmino, E., Caldwell, D. G., & Pfeifer, R. (2013).** Computing with a muscular-hydrostat system. In *Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 1504–1511.
Early work demonstrating reservoir computing in a tentacle-like soft robotic arm, a precursor to the granular medium results.

## Tensegrity Computing

**Caluwaerts, K., D'Haene, M., Verstraeten, D., & Schrauwen, B. (2013).** Locomotion without a brain: Physical reservoir computing in tensegrity structures. *Artificial Life*, 19(1), 35–66.
The definitive paper on tensegrity reservoir computing, with detailed analysis of how structural topology affects computational capacity and locomotion control.

**Rieffel, J., Valero-Cuevas, F., & Lipson, H. (2010).** Morphological communication: Exploiting coupled dynamics in a complex mechanical structure to achieve locomotion. *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, 7(45), 613–621.
Explores how tensegrity and other compliant structures can communicate information through mechanical coupling, with direct relevance to distributed sensing strategies.

## Theoretical Foundations

**Dambre, J., Verstraeten, D., Schrauwen, B., & Massar, S. (2012).** Information processing capacity of dynamical systems. *Scientific Reports*, 2, 514.
Provides the theoretical framework for measuring computational capacity of reservoirs, applicable to physical systems. The nonlinear capacity measure used in Section 18.2.3 is developed here.

**Jaeger, H. (2002).** Short term memory in echo state networks. *Technical Report GMD-148*, German National Research Center for Information Technology.
Introduces the memory capacity measure used throughout the chapter. Though focused on software ESNs, the framework applies directly to physical reservoirs.
