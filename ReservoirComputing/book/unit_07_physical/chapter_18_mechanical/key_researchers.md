# Chapter 18: Key Researchers

## Rolf Pfeifer (University of Zurich / Shanghai Jiao Tong University)

Rolf Pfeifer is the principal architect of the morphological computation research program. His book *How the Body Shapes the Way We Think* (co-authored with Josh Bongard) [PfeiferBongard2006] articulated the theoretical framework within which mechanical reservoir computing became meaningful. Pfeifer's empirical work on self-organizing robots and compliant actuators laid the experimental groundwork for later reservoir computing demonstrations. His earlier paper with Iida [PfeiferIida2004] coined the term "morphological computation" and defined it in terms that directly anticipated the reservoir computing formalization.

**Representative works**: [PfeiferIida2004], [PfeiferBongard2006], [PfeiferEtAl2007]

## Fumiya Iida (University of Cambridge)

Fumiya Iida was co-founder of the morphological computation concept and has been one of its most active experimental proponents. His work on compliant, bio-inspired robots demonstrated concrete cases in which body morphology reduces the computational burden on the controller. Iida's research group has studied locomotion, manipulation, and perception in soft robotic systems, and the reservoir computing interpretation has been a recurring theme.

**Representative works**: [PfeiferIida2004], [IidaEtAl2008], [IidaEtAl2010]

## Kohei Nakajima (University of Tokyo)

Kohei Nakajima is the central figure in the theoretical and experimental development of physical reservoir computing in the mechanical domain. His work has established the quantitative framework for measuring reservoir capacity in physical systems, demonstrated granular media and octopus-arm-inspired soft robots as reservoirs, and developed the theory of "computational tensegrity." Nakajima's book chapter [NakajimaEtAl2018Chapter] provides the most systematic treatment of physical reservoir computing available.

**Representative works**: [NakajimaEtAl2013], [NakajimaEtAl2015Granular], [NakajimaEtAl2015Octopus], [NakajimaEtAl2018Chapter]

## Helmut Hauser (University of Bristol)

Helmut Hauser led the team that produced the foundational theoretical paper on compliant robot arms as reservoirs [HauserEtAl2011]. This work provided the first formal proof that mechanical compliance satisfies the echo state property and that linear readout from joint angle measurements can approximate nonlinear functions of input history. Hauser's subsequent work has developed the theory of morphological computation in the RC framework and explored soft robotic applications.

**Representative works**: [HauserEtAl2011], [HauserEtAl2012], [HauserEtAl2014]

## Auke Jan Ijspeert (EPFL)

Auke Jan Ijspeert is a pioneer in bio-inspired robotics, particularly in locomotion and the use of central pattern generators. His contribution to [HauserEtAl2011] brought expertise in robot dynamics and the connection between mechanical oscillators and neural computation. Ijspeert's broader research on CPGs (see Chapter 23) provides the bridge between mechanical reservoir computing and rhythmic motor control.

**Representative works**: [IjspeertEtAl2007], [HauserEtAl2011], [IjspeertEtAl2014]

## Wolfgang Maass (Graz University of Technology)

Wolfgang Maass co-authored the Hauser et al. paper [HauserEtAl2011] as theorist, providing the connection to the liquid state machine framework and the formal mathematical analysis of fading memory in physical systems. Maass's earlier theoretical work on LSMs (Chapter 2) established the foundations that made the mechanical reservoir formalization possible.

**Representative works**: [MaassEtAl2002], [MaassEtAl2004], [HauserEtAl2011]

## Randall Beer (Case Western Reserve University)

Randall Beer's work on minimal cognitive agents and the dynamics of locomotion in compliant mechanical bodies anticipated many themes of morphological computation. His studies of insect locomotion models demonstrated how neural-mechanical coupling distributes computation between the nervous system and the body mechanics.

**Representative works**: [BeerEtAl1999], [BeerEtAl2014]

## Matthew Botvinick (DeepMind / Princeton)

While primarily a computational neuroscientist, Botvinick's theoretical analysis of hierarchical temporal processing in biological systems has influenced thinking about why compliant bodies, which naturally implement multiple timescales, may be well-suited as reservoirs for biological motor control.
