# Chapter 22: Key Researchers

## George Moody and Roger Mark (MIT Laboratory for Computational Physiology)

George Moody and Roger Mark created the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database and the PhysioNet platform [MoodyMark1982, GoldbergerEtAl2000], which has been central to biomedical signal processing research for four decades. Their work on standardized evaluation protocols (ANSI/AAMI EC57) established the rigorous benchmarking framework within which ECG classifiers — including reservoir systems — are evaluated.

**Representative works**: [MoodyMark1982], [MoodyMark2001], [GoldbergerEtAl2000]

## Ali Shoeb (MIT / Harvard)

Ali Shoeb developed the CHB-MIT Scalp EEG Database as part of his PhD work on personalized seizure detection algorithms [ShocklockEtAl2010]. His patient-specific SVM approach to seizure detection established the standard for subject-specific EEG classification and is the primary baseline against which RC-based EEG systems are compared.

**Representative works**: [ShocklockEtAl2010], [ShoebGuttag2010]

## Claudio Gallicchio (University of Pisa)

Claudio Gallicchio developed the Deep Echo State Network (DeepESN) architecture [GallicchioMicheli2017] with Alessio Micheli. The hierarchical multi-timescale reservoir is particularly relevant to biomedical signals, and Gallicchio's subsequent work has examined DeepESN performance on physiological time series.

**Representative works**: [GallicchioMicheli2017], [GallicchioEtAl2018], [GallicchioMicheli2020]

## Cecilia Laschi (Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna / National University Singapore)

Cecilia Laschi's work on bioinspired soft robotics and physiological signal processing has connected the physical reservoir computing framework to biomedical applications, particularly in wearable sensing systems where the reservoir is physically implemented in the sensor material.

## Simon Haykin (McMaster University)

Simon Haykin's foundational work on adaptive signal processing [Haykin2001] and neural networks for signal processing provides the theoretical framework within which reservoir-based time series methods are evaluated. His analysis of RNN-based signal processing defines the theoretical performance bounds that RC approaches approach.

**Representative works**: [Haykin2001], [HaykinPrincipe2011]

## Mantas Lukoševičius (Constructor University, Bremen)

Lukoševičius's practical guide to ESNs [Lukosevičius2012] contains the most accessible treatment of reservoir computing for time series analysis and includes worked examples on biomedical and financial data. His guide is the standard starting point for practitioners.

**Representative works**: [Lukosevičius2012], [LukoseviciusJaeger2009]

## Patrik Thiran / Nicolas Brunel

Patrik Thiran and Nicolas Brunel (separately) contributed theoretical analyses of reservoir computing in the context of neural modeling that are directly relevant to EEG-based reservoir systems, connecting the mathematical theory of recurrent neural dynamics to experimental EEG observations.
