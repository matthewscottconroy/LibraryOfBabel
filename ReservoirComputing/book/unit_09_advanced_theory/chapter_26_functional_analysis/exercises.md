# Chapter 26 Exercises

## Section 26.1: Stone-Weierstrass

**Exercise 26.1.** *(Proof completion)* The Stone-Weierstrass theorem as stated assumes the algebra $\mathcal{A}$ is real-valued. State and prove the complex-valued version. Where exactly does the proof break down without the conjugation-closure assumption? Provide a specific counterexample.

**Exercise 26.2.** *(Counterexamples)* For each of the following, either prove the algebra is dense in $C(K)$ or provide a specific function it cannot approximate:

(a) The even polynomials $\{p(x^2) : p \text{ polynomial}\}$ on $[-1, 1]$.

(b) The algebra generated by $\{e^{-nx} : n \geq 1\}$ on $[0, \infty)$ (with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets).

(c) The algebra of polynomials on $[0,1]^2$ that vanish on the diagonal $\{(x,x) : x \in [0,1]\}$.

**Exercise 26.3.** *(Lattice version)* Prove the *lattice version* of Stone-Weierstrass: a sublattice $\mathcal{L} \subseteq C(K)$ that separates points and contains the constants is dense in $C(K)$. (A sublattice is closed under $\max$ and $\min$ but not necessarily under products.)

**Exercise 26.4.** *(Bernstein polynomials)* Let $f: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuously differentiable. Show that $B_n(f)' = n(B_{n-1}(f') )$ — the derivative of the Bernstein polynomial is the Bernstein polynomial of the derivative (at the next degree). Use this to give a rate of convergence: show $\|B_n(f) - f\|_\infty = O(n^{-1/2})$ for $f \in C^1([0,1])$.

**Exercise 26.5.** *(Neural networks)* Consider the activation function $\sigma(t) = \text{ReLU}(t) = \max(t, 0)$. Explain why the argument for density based on Stone-Weierstrass (as used for sigmoidal $\sigma$) does *not* directly apply. However, ReLU networks are still universal approximators. Sketch a direct proof using the fact that ReLU networks can approximate any continuous piecewise-linear function, and CPL functions are dense in $C([0,1]^d)$.

## Section 26.3: Boyd-Chua Theorem

**Exercise 26.6.** *(Weight sequences)* Let $w_k = e^{\alpha k}$ for $\alpha > 0$. Show that the corresponding space $X_w$ contains all bounded sequences with exponentially decaying values in the distant past. Compute the set of all sequences $\mathbf{u}$ such that $\|\mathbf{u}\|_w \leq 1$.

**Exercise 26.7.** *(Compact sets in $X_w$)* Let $w_k = k+1$. Show that the unit ball $\{\mathbf{u} : \|\mathbf{u}\|_w \leq 1\}$ is *not* compact in the $\|\cdot\|_w$ topology. (Hint: construct a sequence with no convergent subsequence.) Now show that the set $\{\mathbf{u} : \sup_{k \geq 0} (k+1)^2 |u_{-k}| \leq 1\}$ *is* compact.

**Exercise 26.8.** *(Polynomial degree sufficiency)* Let $H: X_w \to \mathbb{R}$ be a Lipschitz functional. Prove that $H$ can be approximated by degree-1 (linear) polynomial functionals if and only if $H$ itself is a continuous linear functional. Give an example of a fading-memory functional that requires degree $\geq 2$ for approximation.

**Exercise 26.9.** *(Delay-line reservoir realization)* Let $P(\mathbf{u}) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} a_k u_{-k} + \sum_{k,\ell=0}^{N-1} b_{k\ell} u_{-k} u_{-\ell}$ be a degree-2 polynomial functional depending on $N$ time steps. Write down explicitly a reservoir computer (specifying $W_{\text{res}}, W_{\text{in}}, W_{\text{out}}$, and the activation function) that exactly computes $P$. How many reservoir units are required?

**Exercise 26.10.** *(Fading memory and stability)* Suppose a reservoir is driven by input $\mathbf{u}$ and has state $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$. The functional $H(\mathbf{u}) =$ (the asymptotic state $x(0)$ as a function of the past inputs) has the fading memory property if and only if the reservoir is globally uniformly asymptotically stable (the state forgets its initial conditions). Make this equivalence precise and prove one direction.

## Section 26.5: Approximation Rates

**Exercise 26.11.** *(Curse of dimensionality)* Consider functionals of the form $H(\mathbf{u}) = h(u_0, u_{-1}, \ldots, u_{-(d-1)})$ where $h: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ is a function with bounded Sobolev norm $\|h\|_{W^{s,2}} \leq R$. Using the classical approximation theory for multivariate functions, derive the approximation rate for polynomial functionals of degree $s$ depending on $N$ time steps, as a function of $N$, $s$, and $d$.

**Exercise 26.12.** *(Ridge regression bounds)* Let the reservoir have $N$ units and suppose the optimal readout is $W^* \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N}$. Show that the ridge regression estimator $\hat{W} = Y X^\top (XX^\top + \lambda I)^{-1}$ (where $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T}$ and $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times T}$) satisfies
$$\mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{W} - W^*\|^2\right] \leq \frac{N \sigma^2}{\lambda T} + \lambda^2 \frac{\|W^*\|^2}{\lambda^2} \cdot \text{(bias term)}$$
and find the optimal $\lambda$ that minimizes the total expected error.

**Exercise 26.13.** *(RKHS perspective)* A reservoir with $\tanh$ activations and weights drawn from $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ induces (in the infinite-width limit) a kernel $K(u, v) = \mathbb{E}_w[\sigma(w^\top u)\sigma(w^\top v)]$. Compute this kernel explicitly for $d=1$ and show that it is related to the arc-cosine kernel of Cho and Saul [ChoSaul2009]. What smoothness class of functions is approximated by the corresponding RKHS?

**Exercise 26.14.** *(Minimax lower bound)* Prove that for the class of Lipschitz functionals $\mathcal{F}_L = \{H : |H(\mathbf{u}) - H(\mathbf{v})| \leq L\|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|_w\}$ on a compact set $K \subseteq X_w$ of metric dimension $d$, any estimator using $N$ evaluations has worst-case error at least $c \cdot N^{-1/d}$ for a universal constant $c$ depending on $L$. (This is a minimax lower bound.) What does this say about the fundamental limits of reservoir computing for Lipschitz functionals?

**Exercise 26.15.** *(Research problem)* *[Open-ended]* The bounds in Section 26.5 apply to the approximation of single functionals. In practice, a reservoir is trained on a finite time series of length $T$, not $T$ independent samples from a distribution. The temporal correlations in the training data affect both the estimation error (through reduced effective sample size) and the approximation error (through the induced bias). Formulate a precise version of this problem and sketch what an optimal bound might look like. What additional assumptions on the input process would you need?
