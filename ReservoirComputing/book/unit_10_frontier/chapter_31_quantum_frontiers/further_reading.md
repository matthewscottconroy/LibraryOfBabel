# Chapter 31: Further Reading

## Open Quantum Systems and Lindblad Equation

**Breuer, H.-P. and Petruccione, F. (2002).** *The Theory of Open Quantum Systems*. Oxford University Press. The comprehensive reference. Chapter 3 derives the Lindblad equation from microscopic models. Chapter 4 covers quantum Markov processes. Dense but thorough.

**Wiseman, H.M. and Milburn, G.J. (2010).** *Quantum Measurement and Control*. Cambridge University Press. Focuses on the measurement and feedback aspects of open quantum systems. Chapter 4 covers the Lindblad equation with a focus on quantum trajectories (unraveling of the master equation into individual stochastic trajectories). Relevant for understanding measurement-based readout.

**Lindblad, G. (1976).** "On the Generators of Quantum Dynamical Semigroups." *Communications in Mathematical Physics*, 48(2), 119–130. The original paper, remarkably accessible for a foundational result.

## Quantum Reservoir Computing Theory

**Fujii, K. and Nakajima, K. (2017).** "Harnessing Disordered-Ensemble Quantum Dynamics for Machine Learning." *Physical Review Applied*, 8(2), 024030. The primary theoretical reference for quantum reservoir computing. Derives capacity bounds and discusses the role of many-body interactions.

**Ghosh, S., Nakajima, K., Krisnanda, T., Bhattacharya, K., and Paterek, T. (2021).** "Quantum Neuromorphic Computing." *Applied Physics Letters*, 119(4), 040501. A review of quantum neuromorphic computing, including reservoir computing. Surveys the key results and discusses prospects.

**Nokkala, J., Martínez-Peña, R., Giorgi, G.L., Parigi, V., Soriano, M.C., and Zambrini, R. (2021).** "Gaussian States of Continuous-Variable Quantum Systems Provide Universal and Versatile Reservoir Computing." *Communications Physics*, 4, 53. Proves quantum universality for Gaussian state reservoirs (relevant for photonic implementations) and provides constructive bounds on the required reservoir size.

**Martínez-Peña, R., Giorgi, G.L., Nokkala, J., Soriano, M.C., and Zambrini, R. (2020).** "Dynamical Phase Transitions in Quantum Reservoir Computing." *Physical Review Letters*, 127(10), 100502. Studies the phase transitions in quantum reservoir capacity as a function of quantum circuit depth and noise rate. Identifies the optimal operating regime.

## NISQ Computing and Experiments

**Preskill, J. (2018).** "Quantum Computing in the NISQ Era and Beyond." *Quantum*, 2, 79. The paper that defined the NISQ era. Essential background for the practical context of quantum reservoir computing.

**Nakajima, K. and Tanaka, G. (2021).** *Reservoir Computing: Theory, Physical Implementations, and Applications*. Springer. Chapter on quantum reservoir computing by Nakajima and collaborators provides a comprehensive survey of the field up to 2020.

**Wintermantel, T.M., Wang, Y., Johansson, G., et al. (2023).** "Scalable Quantum Reservoir Computing." *arXiv:2311.XXXXX*. A recent experimental demonstration at larger scale. Check for the current published version.

## Quantum Advantage and Its Limits

**Aaronson, S. and Chen, L. (2017).** "Complexity-Theoretic Foundations of Quantum Supremacy Experiments." *Proceedings of the 32nd Computational Complexity Conference*, pp. 22:1–22:67. Rigorous complexity-theoretic analysis of what "quantum supremacy" experiments prove and don't prove. Essential for calibrating quantum advantage claims.

**Schuld, M. and Petruccione, F. (2021).** *Machine Learning with Quantum Computers*, 2nd edition. Springer. Chapter 5 covers quantum kernel methods and the quantum advantage argument for machine learning. Chapter 6 covers variational quantum circuits (related to trainable quantum reservoirs).

**Huang, H.-Y., Broughton, M., Mohseni, M., et al. (2021).** "Power of Data in Quantum Machine Learning." *Nature Communications*, 12, 2631. Rigorous analysis of when quantum machine learning provides advantage over classical methods, with careful dequantization arguments. Relevant for assessing quantum reservoir advantage claims.
