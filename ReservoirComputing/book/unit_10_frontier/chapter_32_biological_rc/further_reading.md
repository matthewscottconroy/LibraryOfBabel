# Chapter 32: Further Reading

## The DishBrain Paper and Direct Responses

**Kagan, B.J., Kitchen, A.C., Tran, N.T., et al. (2022).** "In vitro neurons learn and exhibit sentience when embodied in a simulated game-world." *Neuron*, 115(6), 973–990. The primary source for Section 32.3. Read the full paper, including supplementary materials, before forming judgments. The experimental design, controls, and quantitative results are in the supplementary.

**Response commentaries in *Neuron* (2022).** Several commentaries were published alongside the DishBrain paper. These provide perspectives from neuroscientists and philosophers that contextualize the claims and identify the key scientific and philosophical issues.

## Ethics of Neural Organoid Research

**Trujillo, C.A., Gao, R., Negraes, P.D., et al. (2019).** "Complex Oscillatory Waves Emerging from Cortical Organoids Model Early Human Brain Network Development." *Cell Stem Cell*, 25(4), 558–569. Demonstrates that human cortical organoids can exhibit EEG-like oscillations. One of the papers that raised serious ethical questions about organoid research.

**Munsie, M., de Wert, G., Dondossola, E., et al. (2022).** "An Ethics Framework for the Use of Stem Cell-Based Embryo Models for Research." *EMBO Reports*, 23(3), e54299. Discusses frameworks for ethical oversight of stem cell-derived neural tissue, with application to organoids. The most practical ethics guidance available.

**Lavazza, A. and Sani, L. (2023).** "Cerebral Organoids and the Moral Status of the Mind: Between Functionality and Phenomenology." *AJOB Neuroscience*, 14(2), 150–162. A philosophical analysis of the moral status of organoids, engaging with IIT, functionalism, and the hard problem. Recommended for Section 32.4.

## Philosophy of Mind and Consciousness

**Chalmers, D.J. (1995).** "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness." *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 2(3), 200–219. The paper that introduced the "hard problem" terminology. Accessible and clear.

**Chalmers, D.J. (1996).** *The Conscious Mind: In Search of a Fundamental Theory*. Oxford University Press. The book-length treatment of the hard problem and the case for non-reductive approaches. Chapters 1-3 are most relevant.

**Tononi, G., Boly, M., Massimini, M., and Koch, C. (2016).** "Integrated Information Theory: From Consciousness to its Physical Substrate." *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 17(7), 450–461. The most accessible presentation of IIT. Explains $\Phi$, gives examples, and discusses empirical tests.

**Dennett, D.C. (1991).** *Consciousness Explained*. Little, Brown and Company. The leading defense of functionalism, arguing that consciousness can be fully explained by functional organization. A classic that should be read alongside Chalmers for the full debate.

**Searle, J.R. (1980).** "Minds, Brains, and Programs." *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 3(3), 417–424. The Chinese Room argument against functionalism and for biological naturalism. Short and influential.

## Biological Reservoir Design

**Natschläger, T., Maass, W., and Zador, A. (2001).** "Efficient Temporal Processing with Biologically Realistic Dynamic Synapses." *Network: Computation in Neural Systems*, 12(1), 75–87. Shows that short-term synaptic plasticity dramatically expands the temporal feature set of neural circuits. The primary reference for Section 32.5.3.

**Maass, W., Natschläger, T., and Markram, H. (2002).** "Real-Time Computing Without Stable States: A New Framework for Neural Computation Based on Perturbations." *Neural Computation*, 14(11), 2531–2560. The original Liquid State Machine paper. Essential for understanding the biological motivation of reservoir computing.

**London, M. and Häusser, M. (2005).** "Dendritic Computation." *Annual Review of Neuroscience*, 28, 503–532. Reviews the computational role of dendritic morphology and ion channels, showing that individual neurons implement sophisticated nonlinear computations. Motivates dendritic models of reservoir units.

**Watts, D.J. and Strogatz, S.H. (1998).** "Collective Dynamics of 'Small-World' Networks." *Nature*, 393, 440–442. The seminal small-world networks paper. Essential for Section 32.5.4.

## Neuromorphic Computing

**Mead, C. (1990).** "Neuromorphic Electronic Systems." *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 78(10), 1629–1636. The founding paper of neuromorphic engineering. Explains the biological inspiration and engineering approach.

**Davies, M., Srinivasa, N., Lin, T.-H., et al. (2018).** "Loihi: A Neuromorphic Manycore Processor with On-Chip Learning." *IEEE Micro*, 38(1), 82–99. Description of Intel's Loihi chip, which implements spike-based computing with on-chip learning. The primary reference for Intel neuromorphic hardware.
