# Chapter 34: Open Problems in Reservoir Computing

## Introduction

Every mature scientific field has a set of central open problems that define its frontier. For reservoir computing, we are now far enough from the pioneering papers of 2000–2004 to identify the questions that genuinely remain open — not merely computationally hard but conceptually unresolved — and to characterize what a solution would require.

This chapter is different from the others. Rather than presenting established knowledge, we present the boundaries of established knowledge. We try to be precise about what is unknown, honest about why current approaches fall short, and concrete about what progress would look like. Our goal is to equip the reader not just to understand the current state of the field but to contribute to its future.

Section 34.1 presents five theoretical open problems with precision: what is currently known, what is not known, and what a solution would require. We resist the temptation to list every open question in the field; instead we focus on the five problems that seem most fundamental.

Section 34.4 steps back to offer a research program for the next decade. This section is necessarily more speculative — research programs are bets, not guarantees — but we try to make the bets explicitly and defend them.
