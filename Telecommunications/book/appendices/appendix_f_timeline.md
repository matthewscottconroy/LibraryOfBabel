## What the timeline shows

Three patterns worth noticing, because each is an argument the book has made.

**Theory precedes practice by decades.** Shannon's capacity theorem (1948) was
approached by practical codes in 1993. Gallager's LDPC codes (1960) waited 35 years for
hardware. Ring's cellular concept (1947) waited 32 years for a product. Kao's fibre
argument (1966) waited four years for glass and twenty for amplifiers.

**Infrastructure changes on decade timescales, regardless of merit.** IPv6 was
standardised in 1995 and is at roughly 45% adoption in 2026. NAT, proposed in 1994 as a
short-term measure, is universal. The reason in both cases is incremental adoptability,
not technical quality.

**Security arrives after deployment, always.** ARP (1982), BGP (1989), DNS (1983) and
SS7 (1975) were all designed for a trusted environment, and every one has required
retrofitted defences — DAI, RPKI, DNSSEC, and SS7 firewalls — that remain partially
deployed. The pattern is consistent enough that it should inform how you design
anything intended to last.
