# Truth Bearers: What Can Be True or False?

## Overview
The question of what *kind* of thing is true or false — sentences, utterances, beliefs, or
abstract propositions — has been debated extensively in philosophy of language and logic.
Understanding this helps clarify what formal logic is actually about.

## Learning Objectives
- List the main candidates for truth-bearers in the philosophical literature
- Explain the problem of context-sensitivity for sentence-based accounts
- Understand why formal logic abstracts away from this debate

## Candidates for Truth-Bearers
1. **Sentences (types)**: "Snow is white" is true — a sentence type
2. **Utterances (tokens)**: a particular use of a sentence at a time and place
3. **Statements**: what is said on a particular occasion
4. **Beliefs**: mental states with propositional content
5. **Abstract propositions**: platonic entities expressed by sentences

## The Indexicality Problem
Sentences containing "I," "here," "now," or tense are **indexical** — their truth depends on
the context of utterance. This motivates moving to propositions (context-free truth-bearers)
for logic.

## Formal Logic's Abstraction
Formal logic sidesteps the metaphysical debate by treating propositional variables (p, q, r)
as standing for whatever we decide are truth-bearers. The logical machinery works the same
regardless of whether you think propositions are abstract objects or sentence types.
