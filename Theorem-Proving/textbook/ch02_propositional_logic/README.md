# Chapter 2: Propositional Logic

Reasoning about statements combined by "and," "or," "not," and "if ... then" — the simplest non-trivial formal system, and the only logic in this book whose every question is settled by an algorithm.

## Overview

Propositional logic studies the language $\mathcal{L}_{prop}$: propositional variables $p, q, r, \ldots$, the constants $\top$ and $\bot$, and the connectives $\neg, \land, \lor, \to, \leftrightarrow$. Syntax comes first: **well-formed formulas** are generated by a recursive BNF grammar, each with a unique parse tree and a **main connective** (the Unique Readability Theorem again); $\{\neg, \land\}$ — indeed NAND alone — is **functionally complete**. The **material conditional** ($\phi \to \psi$ is false only when $\phi$ is true and $\psi$ false, hence vacuously true on a false antecedent) receives special attention as the connective students most often misread.

Semantics: a **truth valuation** $v: \mathrm{Var} \to \{0,1\}$ extends compositionally through recursive truth clauses, so a truth table with $2^n$ rows decides any question about a formula with $n$ variables — propositional logic is **decidable**, though SAT is NP-complete (Cook 1971). Every formula is a **tautology** ($\vDash \phi$; e.g. the law of excluded middle $p \lor \neg p$), a **contradiction** (unsatisfiable, e.g. $p \land \neg p$), or a **contingency**; and $\Gamma \vDash \phi$ iff $(\bigwedge \Gamma) \to \phi$ is a tautology. The algebra of **logical equivalences** — De Morgan's laws, double negation, commutativity, associativity, distributivity, absorption, contrapositive, material implication $\phi \to \psi \equiv \neg\phi \lor \psi$ — licenses formula rewriting; notably, the direction $\neg(P \land Q) \to (\neg P \lor \neg Q)$ of De Morgan requires classical LEM, while its converse is intuitionistically valid.

Rewriting culminates in **normal forms**: negation normal form, **CNF** (a conjunction of clauses, each a disjunction of literals) and **DNF**, the four-step conversion algorithm, and the **Tseitin transformation**, which reaches CNF in linear size at the price of mere satisfiability-equivalence. The named theorems: every formula has CNF and DNF equivalents; **compactness** ($\Gamma$ is satisfiable iff every finite subset is); and **resolution completeness** — a clause set is unsatisfiable iff the empty clause $\square$ is derivable by the resolution rule. The chapter closes by proving the classic tautologies in Lean 4 and Coq.

## Why It Matters

Propositional logic is implemented in hardware (Boolean circuits, NAND gates), in SAT solvers — the industrial workhorse of formal verification — and in the `Prop` of Lean 4 and Coq. Chapter 1's syntax/semantics distinction is instantiated here in full detail; Chapter 4 builds proof systems (natural deduction, resolution, tableaux) over exactly this language; Chapter 3 extends it with quantifiers; and Chapter 13's SAT-based verification rests on the CNF and resolution material developed here.
