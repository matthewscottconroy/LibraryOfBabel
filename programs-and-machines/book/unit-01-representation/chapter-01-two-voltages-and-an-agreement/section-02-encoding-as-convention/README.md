# Encoding as Convention

This is the half of the chapter the book is built on.

Having established that a machine holds patterns, we ask what makes a pattern
*mean* something. The answer — that meaning comes from an agreement stated in
advance, and from nowhere else — sounds almost too simple to be worth three
lessons. Its consequences are not simple at all: they include why encoding bugs
are silent, why the width of a value has to be decided before you know what will
go in it, and why the same thirty-two bits can be six different things without
any of the six being wrong.

Read the last lesson with a pencil. It asks you to do the readings yourself, and
the surprise only lands if you have.
