# Living with Approximation

The format is settled. Now the consequences, which is where the working
programmer actually lives.

Three lessons. The first derives `0.1 + 0.2` all the way down, so the famous
result stops being folklore and becomes something you could have predicted. The
second deals with the two operations that go wrong most often — comparing two
floating-point values, and adding up a long list of them — and gives you
something better to do in each case.

The third is the one I care most about. Every other page of this chapter teaches
you to use floating point well; that lesson teaches you to recognize when the
right move is not to use it at all. Knowing which tool to refuse is a larger part
of engineering than most introductions admit.
