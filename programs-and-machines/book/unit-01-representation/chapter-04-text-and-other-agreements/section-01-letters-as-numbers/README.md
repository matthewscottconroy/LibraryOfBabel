# Letters as Numbers

Three lessons, arranged historically, because in this subject the history *is*
the explanation.

We begin with ASCII: what it decided, why those decisions were sensible in 1963,
and which of them turned out to be assumptions rather than facts. Then Unicode,
whose key contribution is a distinction rather than a table — the separation of
"which character is this" from "how is it stored". Then the encodings themselves,
UTF-8 chief among them, and what happens when two programs disagree about which
one is in force.

By the end you should be able to explain why an emoji has a length of 2 in Java,
and why that is not a bug.
