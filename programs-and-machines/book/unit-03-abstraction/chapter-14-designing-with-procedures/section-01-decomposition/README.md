# Decomposition

You can now write a method, and the next question is where to stop.

A hundred-line method that works is a real thing that real people write, and it is
not wrong exactly — it is just a program that has not been divided yet, and
dividing it is a skill with recognisable rules rather than a matter of taste.

Two lessons.

The first is about finding the seams: the signals that tell you a computation
wants to be divided in a particular place. Comments, repetition, indentation
depth, and the difficulty of naming are all evidence, and learning to read them is
most of the skill.

The second is the principle underneath — one job per method — together with what
"one job" means, which is less obvious than it sounds.
