# Parsing and Formatting

Text enters a program from files, users, and networks, and must become structured
data. Structured data must become text for display and storage.

Both directions are conversions between representations, and Chapter 1's warning
applies to both: a conversion is where information is lost, and where an assumption
that was never stated becomes visible.

Three lessons. Splitting and scanning, which is how text becomes fields. Regular
expressions, which are a small language for describing patterns and which are worth
meeting even though this chapter cannot teach them properly. And formatting, which
is the other direction and where the argument is that you should say what the
output looks like rather than assembling it.
