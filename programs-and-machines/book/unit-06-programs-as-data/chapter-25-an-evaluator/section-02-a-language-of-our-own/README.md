# A Language of Our Own

What you have so far evaluates one expression. That is a calculator.

Three additions turn it into a language: a way to remember things, a way to name a
computation, and a way to *not* do something. The third sounds the least important
and is the one that makes the other two worth having — without it, nothing you
define can ever stop.

Three lessons, each adding something, and the additions are cumulative — by the
end the language computes factorials.

Names and definitions first, which requires statements as well as expressions.
Then procedures, which is where `apply` earns its place, and conditionals, without
which recursion cannot stop. Then an accounting: what we built, what we left out,
and what separates this from a real implementation.
