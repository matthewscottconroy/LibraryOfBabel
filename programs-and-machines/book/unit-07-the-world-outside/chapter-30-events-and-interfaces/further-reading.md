# Further Reading

**Trygve Reenskaug, "Models-Views-Controllers" (1979).** Two pages, from the
Xerox PARC archive. Read it to see how small the original claim was, and how much
of what is now argued about was never in it.

**Martin Fowler, "GUI Architectures" (2006).** The clearest available account of
how MVC, MVP, MVVM and their relatives differ and why each appeared. It also
explains the specific historical confusion in which "MVC" came to mean something
quite different on the web than it did in Smalltalk.

**Josh Bloch and others, "Swing Threading Policy"** — the `javax.swing` package
documentation and the JDK's threading articles. Short, authoritative, and the
reference for the single-thread rule when someone tells you it can be worked
around.

**Ivan Sutherland, "Sketchpad: A Man-Machine Graphical Communication System"
(1963).** His thesis. Read the introduction and look at the figures; the films are
easier to find and more striking. It is genuinely humbling to see how much was
already there in 1963.

**Jef Raskin, *The Humane Interface* (2000).** Not about programming. About what
makes an interface good, from the person who started the Macintosh project.
Opinionated, sometimes wrong, and the best short introduction to thinking of an
interface as something designed rather than assembled.

**Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*.** If Raskin is the theory, this is the
practice, and it takes an evening. The most useful book on this list for anyone
who will build interfaces occasionally rather than professionally.

**The JavaFX documentation, particularly on properties and bindings.** Worth
reading after Section 30.2.3 even if you never use JavaFX, because its binding
model is MVVM made explicit and it will show you what the separation looks like
when a toolkit is designed around it rather than allowing it.
