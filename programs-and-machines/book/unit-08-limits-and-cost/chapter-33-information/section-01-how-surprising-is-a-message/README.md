# How Surprising Is a Message?

"How much information is in this message" sounds like a question that could not
have a number for an answer.

It has an exact one. Somebody worked it out in 1948, in a single paper with almost
no predecessors, and the answer turns out to explain why files compress, how much,
and why some of them cannot.

Two lessons.

Information as surprise: why a message you could have predicted tells you nothing,
and why the amount an unlikely message tells you can be given a number. Then
entropy, which averages that over a source and is the exact answer to how many
bits per symbol are needed.
