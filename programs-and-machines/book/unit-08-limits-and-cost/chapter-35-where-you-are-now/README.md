# Where You Are Now

Thirty-four chapters ago you were told that a computer holds patterns and changes
them, and that everything else is an agreement layered on top. That claim was
supposed to recur.

This chapter checks whether it did, and then points somewhere.

Section 35.1 traces the two ideas the book was actually built around —
representation and abstraction — through every unit, in order. Not a summary: a
demonstration that the same argument was being made eight times in different
registers, which is easier to see now than it was while it was happening.

Section 35.2 is about what comes next. What to read, what to build, and what the
honest state of your knowledge is — which is a more useful thing to be told than
that you have finished something.

There are no exercises for this chapter in the usual sense, and there is one
question worth carrying instead.

The preface said that almost every genuinely confusing bug comes from a moment
where you forgot which agreement you were operating under: you thought you had a
number and you had a pattern, you thought you had a copy and you had a second name
for one thing, you thought you had text and you had bytes somebody else read
differently.

You have now met all three of those, plus about forty more. **The question to keep
asking is not "what does this code do" but "what am I assuming, and who agreed to
it".** That habit is the thing this book was trying to install, and the Java was
the place where it could be made concrete enough to check.
