# The Through-Line

Thirty-four chapters ago the preface made a claim and said to watch for it
recurring. This section checks whether it did.

Not a summary — you can reread chapters. What is worth doing here is showing that
the same argument was being made eight times in different registers, which is much
easier to see now than it was while it was happening.

Two lessons, one for each of the book's two ideas.

Representation first: the claim that meaning is an agreement, traced from voltage
through to undecidability. Then abstraction: the claim that a boundary is worth
more than what is behind it, traced from a named method through to an interpreter.

They are not separate ideas, and the second lesson ends by saying why.
